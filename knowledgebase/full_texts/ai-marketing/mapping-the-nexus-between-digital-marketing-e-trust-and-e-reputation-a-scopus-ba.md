ABSTRACT
This study maps the nexus between digital marketing, e -trust, and e -reputation through a Scopus -based
bibliometric analysis of 229 articles published between 2016 and 2025. Using PRISMA -guided screening and
bibliometric techniques, the study examines annual scientific production, citation impact, keyword co -
occurrence, thematic evolution, and international collaboration. The findings reveal rapid growth, particularly
after 2022, with an annual growth rate of 37.18%, confirming the increasing relevance of trust and reputation in
digital environments. The results show that consumer trust is a central and well -established construct, whereas
e-reputation remains less consolidated but strategically important. Thematic mapping highlights digital
marketing, social media marketing, artificial intelligence, e -commerce, and consumer behavior as key research
areas. The study contributes by clarifying the intellectual structure of the field and identifying future research
opportunities related to AI -driven marketing, online reviews, influencer credibility, platform trust, and
sustainable digital marketing. It also offers practical insights for trust -based digital strategy development
globally.
Keywords: Digital Marketing; E-Trust; E-Reputation; Bibliometric Analysis; Scopus.
1. Introduction
1.1. Contextual Background
The rapid digital transformation of markets has reshaped how firms communicate with customers, build
relationships, and create value. Digital platforms have become central spaces for interaction, persuasion,
engagement, and customer experience management, particularly as firms increasingly rely on social media,
online content, and data -driven communication strategies. Prior studies show that social media marketing has
expanded the scope of firm –customer interaction by raising new questions about ethics, privacy, transparency,

contemporary marketing systems [2]. Online storytelling has also emerged as a mechanism for strengthening
emotional and relational connections between firms and customers in digital environments [3]. More recently,
artificial intelligence and marketing automation have intensified this transformation by enabling personalization,
predictive analytics, and more sophisticated customer targeting practices [4].
1.2. Digital Marketing as a Core Research Domain
Digital marketing has evolved into a multidimensional research domain that covers online advertising, social
media marketing, mobile marketing, content marketing, search engine optimization, e -commerce
communication, and digital customer engagement. Empirical evidence suggests that digital marketing tools can
support market penetration and sales enhancement, particularly in contexts where firms need to adapt quickly to
changing market conditions [5]. In family business contexts, digital technologies have also been linked to
marketing strategy renewal, business model innovation, and technology -enabled competitiveness [6]. The
digitalization of marketing communication has further created new challenges for branding, advertising, and
customer relationship management [7]. In small and medium -sized enterprises, digitalization and social media
engagement have been shown to enhance brand presence and customer engagement, reinforcing the strategic
importance of digital marketing across firm sizes and sectors [8].
1.3. Importance of E-Trust
E-trust is a critical construct in digital environments because online transactions often involve uncertainty,
perceived risk, information asymmetry, and limited physical interaction. In online shopping contexts, e -service
quality has been found to influence e -trust, e -satisfaction, and customers’ behavioral intentions [9]. Trust in
online advertising is also shaped by credibility, informativeness, and technological mechanisms that reduce
consumer uncertainty [10]. In social media environments, e-trust has been conceptualized as a dynamic outcome
of customer satisfaction and digital interaction experiences [11]. In tourism and online travel agencies,
consumer trust can be strengthened by authenticity, parasocial interaction, and self -congruity, demonstrating the
relational nature of trust formation online [12]. Moreover, consumer trust has been identified as a key
antecedent of online purchase decisions, confirming its role as a central driver of digital consumer behavior
[13].
1.4. Importance of E-Reputation
E-reputation is equally important because consumers increasingly rely on online signals, reviews, ratings, brand
visibility, and digital word -of-mouth when evaluating firms and brands. Payment provider reputation and third -
party internet seals, for instance, can influence millennials’ online transaction behavior by reducing perceived
risk and increasing confidence in digital payments [14]. In the banking sector, online corporate reputation has
been examined through reputation indices and panel data approaches, highlighting its measurable and strategic
value in digital markets [15]. Strategic online reputation management has also been linked to brand equity and
customer relationship management, particularly when firms use online tools to monitor and influence
reputational outcomes [16]. Reputation risk in digital marketing further requires systematic monitoring because
brand-related controversies can spread quickly across online channels [17]. Recent evidence also shows that

1.5. Interrelationship between Digital Marketing, E-Trust, and E-Reputation
The relationship between digital marketing, e -trust, and e -reputation is increasingly relevant because digital
marketing activities do not merely promote products; they also shape perceptions of credibility, reliability,
reputation, and relational value. In banking services, digital marketing has been shown to influence purchase
intention through the roles of trust and engagement [19]. In e -marketing contexts, the adoption of digital
payment systems is moderated by consumer trust, confirming that trust conditions the effectiveness of digital
marketing strategies [20]. In tourism, social media marketing efforts, e -reputation, and destination image jointly
influence tourists’ intention to visit, supporting the idea that trust - and reputation -related mechanisms interact
within digital persuasion processes [21]. Corporate reputation can also be built through social media marketing
efforts when mediated by corporate brand credibility [22]. In digital advertising, corporate reputation and
consumer trust jointly shape consumer responses to sustainability-oriented branding messages [23].
1.6. Growth of Scientific Production
The growth of research at the intersection of digital marketing, trust, and reputation reflects the increasing
complexity of digital consumer behavior. Recent studies have examined artificial intelligence in digital
marketing as a mechanism for enhancing consumer engagement and supporting sustainable behavior through
social and mobile networks [24]. The rise of AI tools and human -like digital influencers has also generated new
debates about consumer decision -making and trust in technologically mediated persuasion [25]. In hospitality,
live-streaming characteristics and motivational factors have been connected to customer engagement, trust, and
impulsive booking intention, showing the growing importance of real -time digital interaction [26]. Social media
advertising research has also expanded toward credibility, authenticity, sustainability, and their influence on
consumer behavior, indicating the broadening thematic scope of digital marketing scholarship [27].
1.7. Research Gap and Previous Review Studies
Previous review studies provide valuable knowledge about digital marketing, online consumer behavior, trust,
and reputation, but they do not fully map the combined intellectual structure of digital marketing, e -trust, and e-
reputation. Early review work on internet marketing identified the need for future research directions in online
marketing scholarship, but it did not specifically integrate trust and reputation as a combined analytical lens
[28]. Later, a systematic literature review on online presence, visibility, and reputation clarified the relevance of
online reputation in management studies, yet its focus remained primarily on reputation rather than its
bibliometric connection with digital marketing and e -trust [29]. Review research on big data analytics and
consumer behavior in digital marketing has emphasized data -driven insights and customer experience, but with
limited attention to reputation as a parallel construct to trust [30]. Similarly, systematic review evidence on
digital marketing and sustainable consumption highlights consumer trust and ethical marketing, while leaving
the broader trust –reputation nexus underexplored [31]. Recent review work on virtual influencers has focused
on authenticity, ethics, transparency, consumer trust, and engagement, but does not provide a comprehensive
bibliometric mapping of e -reputation [32]. Generative AI research in digital marketing has also examined
opportunities and challenges related to personalization, content generation, and ethical AI, yet the combined
knowledge structure of e -trust and e -reputation remains insufficiently synthesized [33]. Broader reviews of

1.8. Justification for Bibliometric Analysis
Bibliometric analysis is particularly appropriate for this topic because it enables the systematic mapping of
publication trends, influential authors, journals, countries, keywords, and thematic structures within a growing
research domain. Recent bibliometric work in adjacent areas, such as AI bias in marketing, demonstrates the
value of bibliometric techniques for identifying field evolution and future research agendas [36]. In addition,
computational approaches such as sentiment analysis have been used to simplify the interpretation of social
media data for marketing decisions, reinforcing the importance of structured analytical methods in digital
marketing research [37]. Network -based decision methods have also been applied to understand consumer
decision-making in e-marketplaces, showing that complex digital marketing phenomena benefit from systematic
mapping approaches [38].
1.9. Scopus Database Justification
Scopus provides a suitable source for this bibliometric study because it indexes a wide range of peer -reviewed
literature across business, management, computer science, social sciences, economics, and decision sciences.
The interdisciplinary nature of the topic requires a database capable of capturing research on marketing,
technology, consumer behavior, branding, and digital platforms. Studies on blockchain -enabled brand –
consumer relationships, for example, show how digital marketing research intersects with transparency, trust,
privacy, and online brand relationships [39]. Similarly, research on responsible AI in social media marketing for
digital health illustrates how digital marketing scholarship increasingly crosses disciplinary boundaries between
marketing, technology, ethics, and consumer trust [40].
1.10. Study Aim
The aim of this study is to map the scientific and intellectual structure of research connecting digital marketing
with e -trust and e -reputation in the Scopus database. This objective is justified by evidence that digital
marketing and online trust influence online purchase intention through customer relationship management
mechanisms [41]. It is also supported by findings that e -service quality and e -reputation affect e -customer
satisfaction [42]. Furthermore, the impact of digital marketing on the reputation through service quality and
brand trust, confirms the empirical relevance of studying trust and reputation together within digital marketing
environments [43].
1.11. Research Questions
This study is guided by research questions concerned with the evolution of scientific production, the dominant

work showing that social media marketing communication can influence brand trust, brand affect, and brand

2. Methodology
This study adopted a bibliometric research design to map the scientific production on the nexus between digital
marketing, e -trust, and e -reputation. Bibliometric analysis is appropriate for examining large volumes of
scientific publications, identifying publication trends, influential sources, thematic structures, and the
intellectual development of a research field [51]. In line with this objective, the study relied on the Scopus
database as the sole source of bibliographic data because of its broad multidisciplinary coverage and its
extensive indexing of peer -reviewed research in business, management, computer science, social sciences,
economics, and decision sciences.
The data collection and screening process followed a PRISMA -based logic adapted for bibliometric studies.
Although PRISMA was originally developed to improve transparency in systematic reviews and meta -analyses,
its flow-diagram structure is widely useful for reporting the identification, screening, eligibility, and inclusion
stages of literature selection [52]. Therefore, the present study used a PRISMA flow diagram to ensure
transparency and reproducibility in documenting how the final bibliometric dataset was obtained.

The search was conducted in Scopus using the TITLE -ABS-KEY field, which covers titles, abstracts, and
keywords. The search strategy combined three main conceptual blocks: digital marketing -related terms, e-trust-
related terms, and e-reputation-related terms.
The initial search identified 573 records from Scopus. These records represented the broad body of literature
related to digital marketing, trust, and reputation in digital environments. In the screening phase, a time -span
filter was applied to retain only publications issued between 2016 and 2025, which reduced the dataset to 411
records. This period was selected to capture the most recent decade of research development and to reflect the
contemporary expansion of digital marketing practices, platform -based interactions, and trust -related online
consumer behavior.
A document-type filter was then applied to include only articles, as journal articles are generally considered the
most rigorous and standardized form of scholarly output for bibliometric analysis. This step reduced the dataset
from 411 to 237 records, excluding 174 records that did not meet the document -type criterion. Subsequently, a
language filter was applied to retain only publications written in English, ensuring consistency in textual
analysis, keyword interpretation, and bibliometric processing. This final filter excluded 8 records, resulting in a
final dataset of 229 articles.
Thus, the final sample included 229 Scopus -indexed articles published between 2016 and 2025. These records
constituted the corpus used for the bibliometric analysis. The bibliographic data were exported from Scopus
with the relevant metadata, including authors, titles, publication years, source titles, abstracts, author keywords,
index keywords, citations, affiliations, references, and DOI information. These fields are essential for
conducting descriptive bibliometric analysis, citation analysis, co -authorship analysis, source analysis, keyword
co-occurrence analysis, and thematic mapping.
The bibliometric analysis was guided by established methodological recommendations for science mapping and
bibliometric studies. Bibliometric techniques allow researchers to examine both the performance dimension of a
field, such as publication output and citation impact, and the structural dimension, such as conceptual
relationships, collaboration patterns, and thematic clusters. In addition, tools such as bibliometrix and
Biblioshiny are widely used for comprehensive science mapping because they support data conversion,
descriptive analysis, network analysis, and thematic visualization [53].
Accordingly, the methodological process of this study consisted of four main stages. First, the relevant literature
was identified through a structured Scopus search. Second, the records were screened using time -span,
document-type, and language criteria. Third, the final dataset was prepared and cleaned for bibliometric
analysis. Fourth, bibliometric indicators and science mapping techniques were applied to explore the evolution,
structure, and thematic orientation of research linking digital marketing, e -trust, and e-reputation.
Overall, the use of a PRISMA -based selection process strengthens the transparency of the study, while
bibliometric analysis provides a systematic and replicable approach for mapping the intellectual and thematic
structure of the field. The final dataset of 229 articles offers an appropriate empirical basis for examining how
scholarly research has addressed the intersection of digital marketing, e -trust, and e -reputation over the last
decade.

4. Discussion
The findings of this study show that research on the nexus between digital marketing, e -trust, and e -reputation
has become an increasingly visible and interdisciplinary field during 2016 –2025. The analysis of 229 Scopus -
indexed articles published across 177 sources indicates that this topic is no longer limited to traditional
marketing research, but extends across e -commerce, consumer behavior, information systems, social media
studies, digital communication, and emerging technology contexts. This confirms that trust and reputation have
become central concerns in understanding how firms and consumers interact in digital environments.
The strong increase in annual scientific production demonstrates the growing academic relevance of this
research area. Although publication activity was modest in the early years, the field expanded notably after

2022, reaching its highest level in 2025 with 86 articles. This growth reflects the increasing importance of
platform-based marketing, artificial intelligence, social media communication, online consumer engagement,
and data-driven personalization. The annual growth rate of 37.18% further suggests that digital trust and online
reputation are becoming key analytical dimensions in contemporary digital marketing scholarship.
The citation results provide a more nuanced interpretation of the field’s development. While publication volume
increased sharply in recent years, citation impact was stronger in earlier periods, particularly in 2018 and 2020,
when articles had more time to accumulate citations. The peak of total citations in 2023 indicates that the field
has achieved measurable scholarly influence, but the lower citation averages in 2024 and 2025 should be
interpreted cautiously due to the limited citation window. This confirms that the field is both expanding in
productivity and gradually consolidating its academic impact.
The keyword co -occurrence network shows that the intellectual structure of the field is centered on digital
marketing, consumer trust, social media marketing, e -commerce, consumer behavior, and online reputation.
These connections suggest that digital marketing is increasingly studied not only as a promotional activity, but
also as a relational process that shapes credibility, reduces perceived risk, strengthens customer engagement, and
influences reputational evaluations. The presence of multiple thematic clusters also reflects the conceptual
diversity of the field and its connection to several research streams.
The thematic map further clarifies the maturity of the research domain. Digital marketing, social media
marketing, and consumer trust appear as basic themes, indicating that they are highly relevant but still require
deeper theoretical development. In contrast, marketing, electronic commerce, artificial intelligence, consumer
behavior, decision making, and online advertising appear as motor themes, showing that these topics are both
central and well developed. This suggests that the future development of the field will likely be shaped by AI -
enabled marketing, e-commerce platforms, algorithmic personalization, and digital consumer decision -making.
A particularly important finding is that e -trust appears more mature and central than e -reputation. While trust -
related constructs are strongly embedded in digital marketing research, reputation -related themes such as brand
reputation, social media advertising, and online reputation appear less consolidated. This does not reduce their
importance; rather, it highlights a research gap. E -reputation remains a fragmented but promising area that
requires stronger integration with trust -building mechanisms, online reviews, influencer marketing, brand
communities, digital advertising credibility, and AI-generated content.
The country collaboration map shows that the field has a clear international dimension, with visible
contributions from North America, Europe, Asia, the Middle East, Africa, and Oceania. Countries such as the
United States, China, India, Indonesia, Malaysia, and Australia appear as important contributors. However,
collaboration remains concentrated within specific regional and cross -regional networks. This indicates that
broader international cooperation is still needed, especially with underrepresented regions, to capture different
cultural, technological, and institutional perspectives on digital trust and online reputation.
Theoretically, this study contributes by showing that digital marketing research is shifting from a focus on
communication efficiency and promotional performance toward relational and perceptual constructs such as
trust, reputation, credibility, engagement, and risk reduction. It also demonstrates the need for more integrated

From a practical perspective, the findings suggest that firms should not treat digital marketing merely as a tool
for visibility or sales conversion. Instead, digital marketing strategies should be designed to build trust and
protect online reputation through transparency, credibility, consistency, responsiveness, and ethical data use. In

communication, but also by user-generated content, influencer credibility, online reviews, privacy practices, and

Overall, the study confirms that the nexus between digital marketing, e -trust, and e -reputation represents a
growing and strategically significant research domain. The findings demonstrate strong publication growth,
expanding thematic diversity, and increasing international relevance. At the same time, they reveal that e -

research. Further studies should explore how digital marketing practices simultaneously build trust and shape
reputation in emerging contexts such as artificial intelligence, social commerce, influencer marketing, digital
platforms, online reviews, and sustainable consumption.
5. Conclusion
This study conducted a Scopus -based bibliometric analysis of research on the nexus between digital marketing,
e-trust, and e -reputation during 2016 –2025, based on 229 articles. The findings show that this field has grown
substantially, especially after 2022, reflecting the increasing importance of trust and reputation in digital
marketing environments.
The results indicate that digital marketing research has moved beyond promotional tools toward relational and
perceptual dimensions, including consumer trust, online reputation, credibility, engagement, artificial
intelligence, e -commerce, and social media marketing. The annual growth rate of 37.18% confirms the rapid
development of the field, while citation patterns show stronger impact for earlier publications due to longer
citation windows.
The keyword co -occurrence network and thematic map reveal that consumer trust is a central and well -
established construct, whereas e -reputation remains less consolidated but highly promising. This highlights a
clear research gap and supports the need for more integrated frameworks linking digital marketing, e -trust, and
e-reputation.
The study contributes theoretically by clarifying the intellectual and thematic structure of this emerging field.
Practically, it suggests that firms should design digital marketing strategies not only to increase visibility and
sales, but also to strengthen transparency, credibility, consumer confidence, and reputational value. Future
research should expand the analysis to other databases and explore emerging topics such as AI -generated
marketing, influencer credibility, online reviews, social commerce, platform trust, privacy, algorithmic
transparency, and sustainable digital marketing.
