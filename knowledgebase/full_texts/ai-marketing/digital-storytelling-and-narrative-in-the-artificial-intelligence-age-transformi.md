But the connection between AI and creativity is more nuanced, as AI isn't about making people
more creative, rather it is about making creativity more efficient. Empirical research has been done
recently, but with mixed results. In a pre -registered lab experiment with 302 university students,
access to ChatGPT led to better performance on creative picture -idea generation tasks. The effect
was not consistent among participants, though, and the researchers did not see any evidence that
human–AI teams are nece ssarily complementary to ChatGPT working independently. The
research, however, revealed some evidence of how the participants' interactions with the AI affected
their work's creativity. Similarly, Medeiros et al. (2025) examined whether exposure to ideas
attributed to ChatGPT influenced divergent thinking. Their experiment found no general
improvement in divergent thinking based simply on whether participants received human - or AI-
generated creative prompts. This finding is important because it challenges t he assumption that
exposure to generative AI automatically enhances creativity. Instead, the effectiveness of AI
appears to depend partly on how the technology is incorporated into the creative process.
Other experimental evidence provides further support for the importance of human participation.
Research involving two experiments on poetry writing found that participants were more creative
when writing independently than when placed in an editing role f ollowing an AI-generated poem.
However, the observed creativity deficit disappeared when participants were positioned as co-
creators with AI rather than merely editors of AI-produced material. The researchers also identified
creative self-efficacy as an important mechanism in this relationship. Recent research involving 225
university students similarly found that ChatGPT -assisted creative writing improved aspects of
creative output and reduced the time and effort required to complete the task. At the same time,
participants reported that the AI -assisted activity was less enjoyable and less intellectually
demanding. These findings demon strate that improvements in output quality do not necessarily
correspond to improvements in the subjective experience of creativity.  The issue is particularly
important for digital storytelling because storytelling is not simply an exercise in producing
grammatically correct text or visually attractive media. Narratives involve cultural meanings,

OBJECTIVES OF THE STUDY
The objectives of this study are to:
1. Examine the evolution of digital storytelling in the age of artificial intelligence.
2. Investigate how AI transforms creativity in storytelling.
3. Explore the influence of AI on communication and audience engagement.
4. Identify the opportunities presented by AI-assisted storytelling.
5. Examine ethical issues associated with AI-generated narratives.
6. Discuss the future of storytelling through human-AI collaboration.

Research Questions
The study is guided by the following research questions:
1. How does artificial intelligence influence creativity in digital storytelling and narrative
production?
2. How is artificial intelligence transforming communication through digital storytelling and
narrative practices?
3. How does AI -driven digital storytelling influence human experience, engagement, and
interaction?

5. What ethical, cultural, and creative challenges are associated with the use of artificial
intelligence in digital storytelling?

Artificial Intelligence and Generative Narrative
In the past, AI was known for its problem-solving capabilities, pattern recognition, predictions, and
automation. New developments in large language models and multimodal generative systems have
ushered in new possibilities in fields previously dominated by creative production.
Generative AI can generate text, images, music, speech and more art from user instructions. This
helps producers in the storytelling process to try out other characters, locations, conversations, story
forms, ideas for images and perspectives. But content creation doesn't necessarily mean human
creativity. However, Medeiros et al. (2025) did not generally find that being exposed to AI-generated
creative prompts automatically led to an increase in divergent thinking. There is further evidence
in a meta -analysis that was published in 2025, which encompassed 28 studies and 8214
participants. The researchers found that humans who used GenAI were significantly better on
creative tasks than humans who didn't use GenAI, and that the diversity of the ideas generated  in
the human–AI collaboration were negatively affected. The difference of creative performance and
creative diversity is significant for storytelling. Use of AI can enable a creator to craft an efficient
and polished narrative, but overreliance on similar AI-generated patterns can foster convergence in
narratives. This may lead to stories that are technically well done, but are becoming more similar
in structure, language, imagery, and/or characterization.

Human–AI Co-Creation
Human–AI co-creation provides a useful framework for understanding contemporary AI -assisted
storytelling. Under this approach, AI is not regarded as an autonomous author but as a tool or
collaborator that interacts with a human creator. Empirical evidence supports the importance of
this distinction. In two experimental studies of poetry writing, researchers found that participants
placed in an editing role after receiving AI-generated material performed less creatively than those
writing independently. Howe ver, when participants were encouraged to  co-create with AI, this
disadvantage disappeared. Creative self -efficacy played an important role in explaining the
difference. Another experiment involving 202 German participants compared human –human,
human–Internet, and human –AI creative collaboration. The human –human condition produced
higher creativity scores on divergent-thinking tasks than the other conditions.
These findings suggest that AI should not be conceptualized as automatically superior to human
creativity. Instead, the value of AI depends on how it is integrated into the creative process. For

AI and Communication
Digital storytelling is fundamentally a communication practice. AI has expanded communication by
enabling automated translation, speech synthesis, content personalization, conversational
interaction, and adaptive narrative systems. AI-supported communication can potentially increase
accessibility by allowing stories to be translated into different languages or converted between
textual, visual, and auditory formats. This is particularly significant for education and cultural
communication. However, UNESCO ( 2023) emphasizes that GenAI also creates risks involving
privacy, misinformation, cultural and linguistic diversity, equity, and human agency. Its guidance
therefore advocates a human -centred approach rather than unrestricted technological adoption.
Communication through AI-generated narratives must therefore be assessed not only according to
speed and convenience but also according to accuracy, cultural sensitivity, transparency, and the
preservation of human agency.

Cultural Representation, and Human Experience
Narratives contribute to the formation and transmission of cultural identity. Digital storytelling
can preserve folklore, oral histories, indigenous knowledge, family histories, and community
experiences. AI has the potential to support these activities th rough transcription, translation,
digitization, image generation, and interactive presentation. Nevertheless, AI systems can
reproduce biases contained within their training data. This is particularly important when
representing cultures that have historic ally been underrepresented in digital datasets. AI -
generated narratives may simplify complex traditions or reproduce stereotypes if they are not
carefully reviewed by culturally knowledgeable humans.
Beguš's (2024) comparative analysis of human and AI -generated narratives illustrates the
usefulness of examining storytelling systems for cultural and social patterns rather than evaluating
them only according to linguistic fluency.  Thus, meaningful AI -assisted storytelling requires
cultural awareness, human editorial judgment, and mechanisms for identifying problematic
representations.

Theoretical Framework
This study is anchored on three complementary theories:

Narrative Theory
Narrative Theory explains how stories influence human understanding, identity, memory, and
communication. AI expands narrative possibilities but still depends on human cultural knowledge
for meaningful storytelling.

Media Ecology Theory
Marshall McLuhan's Media Ecology Theory suggests that communication technologies reshape
human interaction. AI represents a new media environment influencing both storytellers and
audiences.

Social Constructivism
Learning occurs through interaction and shared experiences. AI -powered storytelling supports
collaborative knowledge creation while maintaining human participation.

Artificial Intelligence and the Transformation of Digital Storytelling
AI has revolutionized the process of story ideas, story building, and storytelling. In contrast to the
traditional narrative mode, where all creative aspects rely on the human imagination, AI provides
computational creativity that can support human work. T here are systems available using AI
technology that can review millions of stories and come up with an idea of a coherent plot in just a
few seconds. Today, writers can use generative AI to get ideas for dialogue, character development,
descriptions of sce nes, and even alternative endings. These systems are not intended to supplant
authors, but can serve as creative assistants to save time and generate new ideas. Another notable
change is in the storytelling medium, specifically, the use of multimedia. The use of AI can help
create illustrations, animations, background music, voice narration, and visual effects with minimal
technical skills. The democratization of creative tools enables people, teachers and small businesses
to make professional -level digital  stories using comparatively a small amount of resources.
Additionally, AI can improve collaboration and co -creativeness among writers, designers,
filmmakers, and programmers, as well as artists. In today's digital era, AI systems run in the cloud
enable practitioners to collaborate in real -time, and storytelling becomes a mind -bending, artistic
endeavor that marries technology with creativity.

Challenges and Ethical Issues in AI-Driven Storytelling
While artificial intelligence has revolutionized digital storytelling, its integration into creative
practices presents several ethical, legal, and social challenges that require careful consideration.

Copyright and Intellectual Property
Ownership of AI-generated content is one of the most subject to debate issues. Questions that arise
with AI systems include originality, authorship, and copyright infringement, as they are built from
large amounts of existing text, images, music, and videos. Clearness of ownership between the user,
the AI developer, and/or the organization that created the AI system is often lacking. Policies are
still being developed to respond to these concerns, by governments and legal institutions.

Authenticity and Originality
Human storytelling reflects lived experiences, emotions, cultural identity, and personal
imagination. Although AI can generate coherent narratives, it does not possess consciousness,
emotions, or real-life experiences. Excessive reliance on AI may therefore produce stories that lack
emotional depth, originality, or authentic cultural representation.

Algorithmic Bias
AI systems learn from existing datasets, which may contain historical, cultural, or social biases. As
a result, AI -generated narratives may unintentionally reinforce stereotypes, exclude minority
voices, or misrepresent certain communities. Ensuring fairne ss, diversity, and inclusivity in AI -
generated storytelling requires continuous monitoring and responsible dataset development.

Misinformation and Deep fakes
Generative AI can create highly realistic text, audio, images, and videos that are difficult to
distinguish from authentic content. While these technologies have legitimate creative applications,

data protection regulations and respect users' privacy.

Future of Digital Storytelling in the AI Era
The future of digital storytelling will likely be characterized by collaboration rather than
competition between humans and artificial intelligence. Emerging technologies are expected to
transform storytelling in several ways.

Human–AI Co-Creation
Rather than replacing authors, AI will increasingly function as a creative partner, assisting with
research, idea generation, editing, visualization, and multimedia production while human creators
provide vision, emotional intelligence, ethical judgment, and cultural context.

Immersive Storytelling
Advancements in virtual reality (VR), augmented reality (AR), and mixed reality (MR) will enable
audiences to experience stories in highly immersive environments. AI will dynamically adapt
narratives based on users' actions, emotions, and preferences.

Intelligent Educational Narratives
Educational storytelling will become more adaptive, offering personalized learning experiences that
respond to each learner's strengths, weaknesses, and interests. AI tutors may guide students
through interactive stories that enhance engagement and knowledge retention.

Preservation of Cultural Heritage
AI technologies will assist in documenting endangered languages, oral traditions, folklore, and
historical archives, ensuring that cultural narratives are preserved for future generations in digital
formats.

Emotionally Responsive Narratives
Advances in affective computing may allow AI systems to recognize users' emotional states through
speech, facial expressions, or physiological signals, enabling stories to adapt in real time to enhance
empathy and emotional engagement.

Transformation of Digital Storytelling
The study found that artificial intelligence has significantly transformed the process of digital
storytelling. Traditional storytelling generally followed a relatively linear structure in which the
storyteller controlled the development and delivery of th e narrative. AI -supported storytelling,
however, introduces adaptive, interactive, multimedia, and personalized approaches. AI
technologies can assist with generating story ideas, developing characters, producing dialogue,
creating images, generating audio , editing content, and developing interactive experiences. The
finding indicates that AI has expanded the possibilities available to storytellers by allowing
different forms of media to be integrated into a single narrative experience. Digital stories can now
combine written text, images, animation, music, voice narration, virtual environments, and
interactive elements. This development has made storytelling more flexible and accessible to
creators and audiences.

AI-Assisted Creativity and Narrative Development
The findings reveal that artificial intelligence can serve as a creative assistant in the development
of narratives. Generative AI can produce multiple ideas, character profiles, plot structures, dialogue
options, visual concepts, and alternative narrative directions within a short period. This capability
can assist writers, artists, filmmakers, designers, educators, and other creative professionals during
the early and developmental stages of production. However, the findings also show that AI-assisted
creativity does not eliminate the importance of human creativity. Human creators remain
responsible for selecting, modifying, organizing, and interpreting AI -generated materials. Human
imagination, emotional experience, cultural knowledge, intuition, and ethical judgment continue to
play an important role in determining whether a narrative is meaningful and appropriate.

Influence on Communication and Audience Engagement
The study found that AI has improved the ability of digital storytelling systems to communicate
with audiences in personalized and interactive ways. AI -powered recommendation systems can
identify patterns in audience preferences, while interactive storytelling systems can respond to user
choices and behavior. This development changes the audience from a passive receiver of information
into an active participant in the narrative process. Interactive games, educational simulations,
conversational systems, and personalized digital stories demonstrate how AI can create experiences
that respond to individual users. The findings further indicate that AI -supported translation,
speech recognition, speech synthesis, and automated subtitles can improve accessibility a nd allow
stories to reach audiences across linguistic and communication barriers.

Benefits of AI-Assisted Storytelling
The study identified several major benefits associated with the integration of AI into digital
storytelling. These include increased productivity, enhanced creative exploration, personalization,
accessibility, reduced production costs, global reach, and co ntinuous innovation. AI can reduce the
amount of time required for certain creative and technical tasks. It can also provide creators with
multiple alternatives, enabling them to experiment with different narrative possibilities. For small
organizations, independent creators, educators, and students, AI-assisted tools may reduce some of
the technical and financial barriers associated with multimedia storytelling.

Ethical and Professional Challenges
The findings indicate that the benefits of AI -assisted storytelling are accompanied by significant
ethical, legal, and professional challenges. Copyright and intellectual property remain major
concerns because questions of ownership and authorship can aris e when AI systems generate
creative materials. The study also found that AI-generated narratives may reproduce biases present
in training data. This can lead to stereotypes, exclusion, or inaccurate representation of particular
communities. Another concern  is misinformation, particularly because AI can generate realistic
text, images, audio, and video that may be difficult to distinguish from authentic material. Privacy
is another important issue because personalized storytelling systems may rely on information about
users' preferences and behavior. In addition, the increasing use of automation may change the roles
of writers, journalists, designers, illustrators, voice actors, and other creative professionals.

Human–AI Collaboration
A major finding of the study is that the most appropriate model for the future of digital storytelling
is human –AI collaboration. AI provides speed, computational capacity, personalization, and
multimedia production capabilities, while human creators provi de emotional intelligence, cultural
understanding, ethical reasoning, lived experience, and creative direction. The findings therefore
suggest that AI should not be viewed solely as a replacement for human storytellers. Instead, it can
function as a creati ve partner that extends human capabilities. The quality and meaning of AI -
assisted storytelling depend substantially on how effectively human creators direct, evaluate, edit,
and contextualize AI-generated materials.

Future Opportunities
The study found that emerging technologies such as virtual reality, augmented reality, mixed
reality, adaptive learning systems, and emotionally responsive technologies are likely to expand
digital storytelling further. AI may enable narratives to respond dynamically to audience choices,
preferences, and interactions. There are also opportunities for cultural preservation through the
documentation and digitization of oral traditions, indigenous languages, folklore, historical records,
and other forms of cultural heritage. These developments may help preserve narratives that could
otherwise be lost while making them accessible to future generations.

DISCUSSION OF FINDINGS
The thematic analysis of the reviewed literature produced five major findings : AI enhances
certain aspects of creative production; the effects of AI depend on the mode of human participation;
AI improves opportunities for adaptive and personalized storytelling; AI can simultaneously create
risks to originality and diversity; and hu man judgment remains central to ethical and culturally
meaningful narratives.

AI Enhances Creative Productivity but Does Not Automatically Produce Creativity
The first finding is that AI can improve creative productivity and performance under particular
conditions. The 2026 experimental study involving 302 university students found that access to
ChatGPT improved performance on creative picture -idea generation tasks. Similarly, a 2025
experiment involving 225 university students found that ChatGPT -assisted creative writing
produced better performance on several measures while reducing the time and effort required.
These findings support the argument that AI can  function as a creative accelerator. For a digital
storyteller, AI can rapidly generate alternative plot ideas, character concepts, visual directions, and
narrative structures. This can reduce the amount of time spent on preliminary ideation. However,
the evidence does not support the concl usion that AI automatically makes every creator more
creative. Medeiros et al. (2025) found no general effect of the source of creative prompts human or
ChatGPT on divergent thinking. Therefore, AI should be regarded as a resource that can facilitate
creativity rather than as an independent source of human creativity.

The Role of Human Participation Determines Creative Outcomes
The second finding concerns the importance of how humans interact with AI.
Research on poetry writing found that participants were less creative when they merely edited AI -
generated content but recovered this disadvantage when they actively co -created with AI.  This
finding has direct implications for digital storytelling. If cre ators simply accept AI -generated
narratives without substantial intervention, the resulting stories may reflect the dominant patterns
learned by the model. By contrast, when creators use AI as a brainstorming and experimentation
partner, they retain greate r control over narrative intention. The evidence therefore supports a
human-led collaborative model in which AI generates possibilities and the human creator evaluates,
modifies, contextualizes, and integrates those possibilities.

AI Supports Adaptive and Personalized Storytelling
The third finding is that AI can increase narrative adaptability and audience engagement.
The 2026 study on GenAI -based digital storytelling found that students exposed to adaptive AI
storytelling demonstrated higher learning engagement and cross -cultural learning outcomes than
students in the comparison conditions. Engagement also partially me diated the relationship
between AI storytelling and learning outcomes.  This finding demonstrates the potential of AI to
transform storytelling from a fixed narrative into an adaptive communication experience. Instead

AI May Increase Creative Output While Reducing Diversity
The fourth finding is more cautionary. Although AI can improve aspects of creative performance,
evidence suggests that extensive AI assistance can reduce diversity or originality. A 2025 meta -
analysis of 28 studies involving 8,214 participants found that h umans working with GenAI
performed better creatively than humans without AI assistance, but human –AI collaboration was
also associated with reduced diversity of ideas.  This finding is particularly relevant to narrative
production. If large numbers of creators rely on similar generative systems and prompts, there is a
possibility that stories will increasingly share common linguistic structures, plot conventions, visual
styles, or character patterns. Recent research on Chinese creative writing provides anot her
example. The study found that limited AI assistance could enhance creativity, while extensive AI
co-writing was associated with reduced originality despite increased elaboration.  The implication
is not that AI should be excluded from creative practice . Rather, creators should maintain active
authorship and use AI selectively to expand rather than standardize their creative possibilities.

Human Experience, Ethics, and Cultural Context Remain Essential
The fifth finding is that technological efficiency cannot replace the human dimensions of
storytelling. AI can generate narratives rapidly, but meaningful storytelling requires decisions
concerning identity, cultural representation, emotional significance,  ethics, and social
consequences. UNESCO's human -centred framework emphasizes privacy, inclusion, equity,
cultural and linguistic diversity, and human agency in the development and use of GenAI.  The
reviewed evidence also suggests that the subjective experience of creativity can differ from the
quality of the final product. In the 225 -student creative -writing experiment, participants using
ChatGPT produced improved outputs but reported lower enjoyment and perceived intellectual
demand.
This distinction is significant. Storytelling is not merely about producing an acceptable final
product; it is also a human process through which individuals explore identity, emotion,
imagination, memory, and meaning. Excessive automation could therefore improve efficiency while
reducing some of the experiential dimensions associated with creative practice.

Overall Interpretation of the Findings
Taken together, the reviewed empirical evidence indicates that the relationship between artificial
intelligence and digital storytelling is conditional rather than deterministic . AI can improve
productivity, support ideation, enhance adaptive storytelling, and expand access to creative
technologies. However, these benefits are influenced by the user's level of participation, the design
of the AI system, the nature of the creative  task, and the extent of AI intervention. The evidence
therefore supports a human–AI co -creative model rather than a replacement model. AI is most
useful when it expands the creative possibilities available to human storytellers while humans
retain responsibility for narrative purpose, cultural interpretation, ethical judgment, and final
creative decisions. This conclusion is consistent with the broader movement toward human-centred
AI identified by UNESCO and with recent experimental research showing that the structure of
human–AI collaboration significantly influences creative outcomes.

CONCLUSION
Many people believe that artificial intelligence is a very great technical creation in the long history
of storytelling. Because artificial intelligence possesses the power to create materials and make
tales personal, the way storytelling is done in educat ional institutions and news centers has
changed. The way people see tales in medical professional areas and business has been modified
because artificial intelligence handles information well. It has been observed that storytelling across
many areas changed when artificial intelligence began to help with making and sharing these tales.
Experts claim that storytelling stays as a thing for humans because real tales come from heart
feelings and the way people live together. Although technology is strong, the h uman nature of
storytelling is kept because feelings belong to people. While artificial intelligence is very good at
organizing facts and showing new paths, human makers must provide the true meaning and the
right choices for the tales. It has been observed that artificial intelligence lacks the moral heart that
a person provides. Many people believe that the use of artificial intelligence in digital storytelling
needs to be a work of helping each other rather than taking the place of human makers. Because
artificial intelligence and human dreams are joined, the world can use artificial intelligence to find
new ways to make art. The values that make storytelling important are saved when people use their
own minds alongside the machine. It is often argued tha t artificial intelligence serves the creator
when the creator keeps the control.

RECOMMENDATIONS
Based on the findings of this study, the following recommendations are proposed:
1. Educational institutions should integrate AI-assisted storytelling into media, communication,
art, and design curricula while emphasizing ethical and creative responsibility.
2. Governments should establish clear legal frameworks addressing copyright, authorship, and
ownership of AI-generated content.
3. Technology companies should prioritize transparency, fairness, and accountability in AI
systems used for storytelling.
4. Creative professionals should develop AI literacy and continuously update their digital skills
to remain competitive in evolving creative industries.
5. Researchers should conduct empirical studies examining audience perceptions of AI-generated
narratives across different cultural contexts.
6. Organizations should adopt ethical guidelines that protect privacy, promote inclusivity, and
reduce algorithmic bias in AI-assisted storytelling.
7. Human-centered design principles should remain central to AI development, ensuring that
technological innovation enhances rather than diminishes authentic human communication.
