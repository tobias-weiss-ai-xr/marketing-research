2.THE RISE OF AI: SETTING THE CONTEXT
Artificial Intelligence (AI) is not a new concept. Its roots can be traced back to the mid -20th
century, when computer scientists began exploring the potential of machines that could mimic
human intelligence. But it wasn ’t until recent years, with the convergence of vast data and
advanced computational power, that AI began to show its real potential.
In the context of digital marketing, AI emerged as a game -changer. No longer did marketers
have to  rely solely on human -driven analytics and insights. AI -powered algorithms began
analysing vast amounts of data, deriving patterns that were previously invisible to the human
eye.
Chatbots became the new customer service representatives, available 24/7 and  capable of
handling a multitude of queries simultaneously. Predictive analytics, powered by AI, allowed
businesses to forecast market trends and customer behavio urs with unprecedented accuracy.
Personalization reached new heights, with AI -driven content r ecommendations making user
experiences more tailored than ever.
But why did AI gain so much traction in the world of digital marketing? The answer lies in its
ability to operate at scale and in real-time. With billions of internet users generating quintillions
of bytes of data daily, human analysis, no matter how expert, simply couldn't keep up. AI
stepped in to fill this gap, sifting through massive data sets, learning from them, and delivering
actionable insights.

 4.2 Understanding Customer Behaviour
AI can help businesses understand what their customers are interested in. It can look at
activities such as searches, product views, clicks, purchases and products added to the cart.
For example, if many customers search for a particular type of shoe but do not buy it, a
company can try to understand why this is happening. Maybe the price is too high, the product
is not available in the right size, or customers are simply comparing it with other products.
This helps marketers understand customers better instead of only looking at the final sales.
4.3 Targeted Advertising
AI can also help businesses show advertisements to people who may be interested in a
particular product. Different people  may see different advertisements because their interests
and online activities can be different.
For example, a person who searches for laptops may see advertisements related to laptops,
while another person searching for travel products may see advertisements related to travel.
This can help companies avoid showing completely unrelated advertisements to their
customers.
4.4 Chatbots and Customer Service
AI is also used in customer service through chatbots. Many websites and apps use chatbots to
answer simple questions from customers.
For example, a customer may ask about the status of an order, return policy or product details.

employee.
However, I think human help i s still important for complicated problems where the customer

similar or less personal.

9.CHALLENGES OF AI DRIVEN MARKETING

After understanding the benefits of AI in marketing, I also realised that it is not perfect. The
same technology that makes marketing more personal can also create some problems.
9.1 Privacy
If companies use customer activities to understand their interests, it raises a question about how
much information should be collected and used.
9.2 Wrong Predictions
AI can sometimes misunderstand a customer's behaviour. For example, if I search for a product
only once because I am curious, it does not mean that I actually want to buy it. Showing me
advertisements for the same product again and again can become irritating.
9.3 Too Much Personalization
Personalization can be helpful, but sometimes it can feel uncomfortable when advertisements
become too specific. Customers may feel that the company knows too much about them.
9.4 Bias and Unfair Results
AI learns from information and data. If the data has unfair patterns or problems, the result
produced by AI can also be unfair. This is why AI-based marketing decisions should not always
be accepted without checking them.
9.5 Too Much Dependence on AI
AI can help marketers make decisions, but it should not completely replace human thinking.
Marketing also needs creativity, understanding of people and human judgment.
What I understood
I think the main challenge is not whether AI should be used in marketing or not. The real
challenge is ‘using AI in a useful way while also respecting customers, their privacy and their
choices.’
