Artificial intelligence (AI) -generated influencers have transformed influencer
marketing by introducing virtual personalities that interact with consumers on
digital channels. Brands are using AI -generated influencers more often because
they are scalable, consistent, and easy to control. However, there are still concerns
about how authentic and trustworthy these influencers seem compared to human
influencers. This study examines how consumers respond to both AI-generated and
human influencers, drawing on So urce Credibility Theory, Parasocial Interaction
Theory, and the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM). It explores how the type
of influencer affects trust, authenticity, engagement, and purchase intention. The
study also considers how AI literacy and consumer innovativeness might influence
these effects. This study uses a quantitative research design and collects survey
data from social media users in India. Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) is used
to test the framework. The results are expected to show that  human influencers
build greater trust and perceived authenticity, while AI-generated influencers may
be more attractive to technology -literate consumers. Trust, authenticity, and
engagement are expected to explain how influencer type affects purchase intention.
This study adds to research on AI -enabled marketing by applying traditional
influencer marketing theories to virtual environments. The findings deliver
practical advice for marketers seeking to improve their influencer strategies in a
digital world increasingly reliant on AI.

influencer marketing a key way to engage people online. Influencer marketing uses people with large
online followings to shape consumer attitudes, brand images, and buying decisions through content
that feels real and authentic (Lou & Yuan, 2019). Unlike celebrity endorsements, social media
influencers interact directly with their followers, building stronger trust and engagement that affect
consumer actions (Schouten et  al., 2020). As a result, companies are spending more on influencer

The emergence of social media has greatly altered traditional marketing communications, permitting
organizations to interact with consumers through increasingly personalized and interactive channels.
Influencer marketing has become a dominant strategy with in digital marketing because consumers
increasingly depend on peer recommendations and online communities when making purchasing
decisions (Lou & Yuan, 2019). Unlike classic celebrity endorsements, social media influencers

Influencers are generally defined as individuals who can influence followers' attitudes, beliefs, and
purchase decisions through content shared on social media platforms (Freberg et al., 2011). Their
influence derives from perceived expertise, trustworthin ess, attractiveness, and social
connectedness. Previous studies have demonstrated that influencer endorsements can markedly
affect brand awareness, customer engagement, purchase intentions, and brand loyalty (Sokolova &

More recently, technological developments have facilitated the emergence of AI-created influencers,
introducing a new paradigm within influencer marketing. This evolution mirrors wider trends in
digital transforma tion, in which artificial intelligence is increasingly integrated within customer
engagement and communication tactics (Huang & Rust, 2021).
2.2 AI-Generated Influencers: Definition and Characteristics
AI-generated influencers, also referred to as virtual influencers, are computer -generated
personalities designed to simulate human appearance, behavior, and communication on social media
platforms (Thomas & Fowler, 2024). These influencers are created usin g cutting-edge technologies
such as artificial intelligence, computer -generated imagery (CGI), machine learning, and natural
language processing. Different from human influencers, virtual influencers lack a physical presence;
instead, they are managed by o rganizations, agencies, or technology firms that control their
appearance, personality, and content creation (Djafarova & Trofimenko, 2023).
Several virtual influencers have gained significant global popularity. For example, Lil Miquela has
accumulated millions of followers on Instagram and collaborated with luxury brands including Prada
and Calvin Klein. Similarly, Shudu, often described as th e world's first digital supermodel, has
participated in fashion campaigns for globally recognized brands. These developments illustrate the
growing commercial potential of AI -produced influencers within contemporary marketing
ecosystems (Jayasingh & Sivakumar, 2025).
Organizations increasingly adopt AI-generated influencers because they offer multiple benefits over
human influencers. First, virtual influencers provide complete control over brand communication,
reducing the risk of scandals, controversial behavior, or r eputational damage (Thomas & Fowler,
2024). Second, AI influencers can generate content continuously without geographical or temporal
limitations. Third, they facilitate highly personalized buyer interactions through AI -driven
communication capabilities. F inally, virtual influencers may offer cost efficiencies across long -term
marketing campaigns by eliminating expenses associated with traditional influencer contracts (Huang
& Rust, 2021). Despite these advantages, concerns remain about transparency, ethics, and consumer

2.3 Source Credibility and Consumer Trust
Trust is widely recognized as one of the most influential determinants of consumer buying behavior.
Source Credibility Theory claims that message effectiveness depends on the perceived
trustworthiness, expertise, and attractiveness of the message source (H ovland & Weiss, 1951).
Within influencer marketing contexts, source credibility directly affects consumers' willingness to
accept recommendations and engage with promoted products (Lou & Yuan, 2019).  Trustworthiness
refers to the degree to which consumers perceive a source as honest, reliable, and sincere. Expertise
reflects perceptions regarding knowledge and competence, while attractiveness includes physical
appearance, social appeal, and likability (Ohanian, 1990). Research consistently demonstrates that
highly credible influencers generate stronger brand attitudes, higher engagement, and increased
purchase intentions (Sokolova & Kefi, 2020).
Human influencers frequently establish trust through personal experiences, self-disclosure, and long-
term interactions with followers. These activities contribute to perceptions of honesty and sincerity,
which enhance influencer credibility (Audrezet et al ., 2020). In contrast, AI -generated influencers
could experience obstacles in establishing trust because consumers recognize them as artificial
entities lacking authentic experiences. Choung et al. (2022) argue that trust in AI technologies differs
essentially from interpersonal trust because it depends on perceptions of technological competence,
transparency, and reliability rather than emotional authenticity.  Recent studies examining virtual
influencers have produced mixed findings regarding trust formation. While some researchers suggest
that AI-generated influencers can achieve credibility through consistency and professionalism, others
report that consumers continue to perceive human influencers as more trustworthy due to their
authenticity and relatabi lity (Djafarova & Trofimenko, 2023; Jayasingh & Sivakumar, 2025). These
conflicting findings show the need for additional investigation into how influencer type affects buyer
trust.
2.4 Authenticity in Influencer Marketing
Authenticity has emerged as one of the most important constructs in influencer marketing research.
Consumers increasingly seek genuine and transparent communication from influencers, particularly
as concerns regarding sponsored content and commercial motiv ations continue to grow (Audrezet
et al., 2020). Authenticity refers to the extent to which an influencer is perceived as real, honest,
consistent, and true to their identity (Moulard et al., 2021).  Authenticity influences consumer
behavior through several  mechanisms. First, authentic influencers foster stronger emotional
connections with followers. Second, authenticity enhances trustworthiness because consumers
perceive authentic influencers as less likely to engage in deceptive promotional practices. Thir d,
authentic communication reduces consumer skepticism and increases message acceptance
(Schouten et al., 2020).

Human influencers often establish authenticity through personal storytelling, emotional self -

2.5 Parasocial Interaction and Consumer Engagement
Parasocial Interaction Theory explains how audiences develop one -sided emotional relationships
with media personalities (Horton & Wohl, 1956). Social media environments have strengthened
parasocial relationships by enabling direct communication and continuous intera ction between
influencers and followers (Labrecque, 2014).  Parasocial relationships are particularly relevant in
influencer marketing because consumers often perceive influencers as friends, mentors, or trusted
advisors. Strong parasocial bonds increase en gagement behaviors such as liking, commenting,
sharing, and recommending influencer content (Kim & Song, 2023). Moreover, parasocial
relationships contribute to the formation of trust and positively influence purchase intentions
(Sokolova & Kefi, 2020).
Human influencers typically establish stronger parasocial relationships because followers perceive
them as authentic individuals with relatable experiences. Through storytelling and personal
disclosure, influencers create perceptions of intimacy and social  connection that enhance
engagement (Jin & Ryu, 2024). Although AI -generated influencers increasingly simulate human
communication patterns, questions remain regarding whether consumers develop similar emotional
attachments toward virtual personalities. Emerging research suggests that anthropomorphism—the
attribution of human characteristics to non -human entities—may facilitate parasocial interactions
with AI-generated influencers (Ki et al., 2023). Nevertheless, the strength and effectiveness of such
relationships compared with those formed with human influencers remain unclear, highlighting an
important research gap.
2.6 Purchase Intention and Influencer Effectiveness
Purchase intention represents a consumer's conscious plan or willingness to purchase a product or
service in the future (Ajzen, 1991). In influencer marketing contexts, purchase intention is a key
outcome variable because influencer campaigns ultimately ai m to shape consumer purchasing
behavior. Previous research consistently identifies trust, authenticity, and engagement as important
antecedents of purchase intention (Lou & Yuan, 2019; Sokolova & Kefi, 2020). Consumers who trust

Recent studies investigating virtual influencers suggest that purchase intention may depend on
multiple factors, including perceived human -likeness, credibility, technological innovativeness, and

2.7 Research Gap
Despite growing scholarly interest in AI-generated influencers, several important gaps remain in the
literature. First, existing research has largely examined virtual influencers in isolation rather than
making direct comparisons with human influencers. Se cond, limited studies have simultaneously
investigated trust, authenticity, engagement, and purchase intention within a single integrated
framework. Third, the moderating effects of AI literacy and consumer innovativeness remain
underexplored despite their  theoretical relevance to technology acceptance. Finally, empirical
evidence from emerging markets such as India remains scarce, limiting the generalizability of existing
findings. Addressing these gaps is important because AI -generated influencers are becoming
increasingly prevalent in marketing practice. Understanding the psychological mechanisms
underlying consumer responses to virtual influencers will contribute to influencer mar keting theory
and provide actionable insights for organizations seeking to leverage AI -enabled communication
strategies. Consequently, the present study develops an integrated theoretical framework that
combines Source Credibility Theory, Parasocial Interaction Theory, and the Technology Acceptance
Model to explain consumer responses toward AI-generated and human influencers.
3. Theoretical Framework and Hypotheses Development
3.1 Theoretical Foundation
The present study integrates three complementary theoretical perspectives —Source Credibility
Theory (SCT), Parasocial Interaction Theory (PIT), and the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) —to
explain consumer responses toward AI -generated and human influencer s. The integration of these
theories provides a comprehensive framework for understanding how influencer type affects trust,
authenticity, engagement, and purchase intention in digital marketing environments.
3.1.1 Source Credibility Theory
Source Credibility Theory, originally proposed by Hovland and Weiss (1951), suggests that the
effectiveness of persuasive communication depends largely on the perceived credibility of the
message source. Ohanian (1990) further conceptualized source credibi lity as comprising
trustworthiness, expertise, and attractiveness. In influencer marketing, these dimensions shape

consumers’ acceptance of product recommendations and their willingness to act on endorsed
messages (Lou & Yuan, 2019).
Human influencers often establish credibility through lived experiences, domain expertise, and long-
term interactions with followers. Their ability to share personal stories and experiences contributes
to perceptions of authenticity and trustworthiness (Schouten et al., 2020). In contrast, AI-generated
influencers rely primarily on technological sophistication, visual attractiveness, and algorithmically
generated interactions. Although virtual influencers may demonstrate consistency and
professionalism, con sumers may question their sincerity because they lack genuine human
experiences (Djafarova & Trofimenko, 2023). Recent studies indicate that source credibility remains
a critical determinant of consumer attitudes toward AI -generated influencers. Jayasingh and
Sivakumar (2025) found that perceived credibility significantly affects consumer engagement and
purchase int ention in virtual influencer contexts. Similarly, Jin and Ryu (2024) reported that
trustworthiness remains one of the strongest predictors of in fluencer effectiveness regardless of
influencer type. Consequently, Source Credibility Theory provides an appropriate foundation for
understanding differences in consumer responses toward AI-generated and human influencers.
3.1.2 Parasocial Interaction Theory
Parasocial Interaction Theory (Horton & Wohl, 1956) explains the one-sided emotional relationships
audiences develop with media personalities. Unlike traditional interpersonal relationships, parasocial
interactions occur when consumers perceive emotional i ntimacy and social connection despite the
absence of reciprocal communication.  Social media environments have significantly strengthened
parasocial interactions because influencers engage in continuous communication, self -disclosure,
and the creation of pe rsonalized content (Labrecque, 2014). Through these mechanisms, followers
often perceive influencers as friends, mentors, or trusted advisors, resulting in stronger emotional
attachment and engagement (Kim & Song, 2023).
Human influencers are particularly effective at developing parasocial relationships because
consumers perceive them as authentic individuals with relatable experiences (Sokolova & Kefi, 2020).
AI-generated influencers, however, present an interesting theor etical challenge. Although virtual
influencers can simulate social interaction and emotional expression, consumers remain aware that
these personalities are artificial creations. Consequently, the extent to which AI -generated
influencers can foster meaning ful parasocial relationships remains uncertain.  Recent research
suggests that anthropomorphism may facilitate parasocial interactions with virtual influencers by
encouraging consumers to attribute human characteristics to artificial entities (Ki et al., 2023).
Nevertheless, empirical evidence indicates that human influencers generally generate stronger
emotional connections than virtual influencers, which may influence engagement and purchase
behavior (Lee, 2025).
3.1.3 Technology Acceptance Model
The Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), developed by Davis (1989), explains individuals’
acceptance of technological innovations through perceptions of usefulness and ease of use. TAM has
been widely applied to examine consumer adoption of AI -enabled technologies, digital platforms,

3.2 Conceptual Framework
Based on the preceding theoretical discussion, this study proposes a conceptual framework in which
the type of influencer (AI-generated versus human) is the primary independent variable influencing
consumer trust, perceived authenticity, and engagement. Th ese variables subsequently influence
purchase intention.
Trust and authenticity are proposed as mediating mechanisms because previous studies consistently
identify them as central drivers of influencer effectiveness (Audrezet et al., 2020; Lou & Yuan, 2019).
Engagement is also proposed as a mediator because cons umers who interact more frequently with
influencer content are more likely to develop favorable attitudes toward endorsed products (Kim &
Song, 2023).  Additionally, AI literacy and consumer innovativeness are proposed as moderating
variables that influence  the strength of relationships between influencer type and consumer
responses. Consumers with higher levels of AI literacy may be more accepting of AI -generated
influencers, while innovative consumers may perceive virtual influencers as technologically appealing
rather than artificial or deceptive.

3.3 Hypotheses Development
H1: Influencer Type and Consumer Trust
Trust is a fundamental determinant of persuasive communication and consumer decision -making.
According to Source Credibility Theory, consumers are more likely to accept recommendations from
sources perceived as credible, knowledgeable, and trustworthy (Hov land & Weiss, 1951). In
influencer marketing, trust develops through consistent communication, perceived expertise, and
meaningful interactions with followers (Lou & Yuan, 2019). Human influencers often establish trust
by sharing personal experiences and authentic narratives, whereas AI-generated influencers rely on
technologically mediated interactions. These differences suggest that influencer type plays an
important role in shaping consumers' perceptions of trust.
H1: Influencer type significantly influences consumer trust.
H2: Influencer Type and Perceived Authenticity
Perceived authenticity reflects the extent to which consumers believe that an influencer
communicates in a genuine, sincere, and transparent manner. Authenticity has become an essential
attribute in influencer marketing because consumers increasingly value  influencers who appear
relatable and truthful (Audrezet et al., 2020). Human influencers generally communicate real -life
experiences that strengthen perceptions of authenticity, whereas AI -generated influencers create
digitally constructed identities that  consumers may evaluate differently (Moulard et al., 2021).
Therefore, influencer type is expected to influence perceived authenticity.
H2: Influencer type significantly influences perceived authenticity.
H3: Influencer Type and Consumer Engagement
Consumer engagement refers to the cognitive, emotional, and behavioral interactions that
consumers develop with influencer -generated content. Parasocial Interaction Theory suggests that
repeated interactions and emotional attachment encourage consumers to engage more actively with
media personalities (Horton & Wohl, 1956). Since AI-generated and human influencers differ in their
communication styles, realism, and interpersonal characteristics, consumers may exhibit different
levels of engagement depending on the type of influencer.
H3: Influencer type significantly influences consumer engagement.
H4: Trust and Purchase Intention
Trust reduces perceived uncertainty and enhances consumers' confidence in influencer
recommendations. Previous studies have consistently demonstrated that trust strengthens
consumers' willingness to purchase endorsed products because trustworthy influencer s are viewed
as reliable information sources (Lou & Yuan, 2019; Sokolova & Kefi, 2020). Consequently, trust is
expected to play an important role in shaping purchase intentions.
H4: Consumer trust significantly influences purchase intention.
H5: Perceived Authenticity and Purchase Intention

Quantitative research is appropriate for testing theoretical relationships among latent constructs and
validating conceptual models using multivariate statistical techniques (Hair et al., 2022). A cross -
sectional survey was adopted because it enables the collection of standardized responses from a

4.4 Questionnaire Development
A structured questionnaire was developed based on previously validated measurement scales
reported in the marketing and information systems literature. The questionnaire consisted of three
sections. The first section collected demographic information, including gender, age, education,
occupation, monthly household income, preferred social media platform, and average daily social
media usage.
The second section measured respondents' perceptions of influencers. Prior to responding,
participants were randomly exposed to either a human influencer profile  or an AI-generated
influencer profile that was comparable in terms of product category, number of followers, content
style, and endorsement format. Random assignment minimizes selection bias and allows differences
in consumer responses to be attributed to the type of influencer.  The third section measured trust,
perceived authenticity, consumer eng agement, purchase intention, AI literacy, and consumer
innovativeness using multi -item scales.  All measurement items were evaluated using a five -point
Likert scale ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). Five-point Likert scales are widely
used in consumer behavior research because they are easily understood by respondents while
providing sufficient variance for multivariate statistical analysis (Hair et al., 2022). All constructs were
measured using previously validated scales with minor wording modifications to fit the context of AI-
generated and human influencers.
Influencer Type was operationalized as a categorical variable representing the source of the product
endorsement. Respondents were randomly assigned to one of two experimental conditions: (1) AI-
generated influencer  and (2) Human influencer . Each participant was exposed to a standardized
influencer profile with comparable content, product category, and endorsement message. The only
manipulated element was the influencer type (AI -generated versus human), allowing differences in
consumer responses to be attributed to the endorsement source. The variable was coded as 0 = AI-
generated influencer  and 1 = Human influencer  for statistical analysis. This operationalization is
consistent with previous experimental studies comparing virtual and human influencers (e.g.,
Djafarova & Trofimenko, 2023; Lee, 2025; Thomas & Fowler, 2024).

7. Conclusion
This study developed and evaluated an integrated framework to explain consumer responses toward
AI-generated and human influencers by drawing upon Source Credibility Theory, Parasocial
Interaction Theory, and the Technology Acceptance Model. The proposed f ramework demonstrates
that influencer type influences purchase intention both directly and indirectly through trust,
perceived authenticity, and consumer engagement. In addition, AI literacy and consumer
innovativeness strengthen the relationship between i nfluencer type and purchase intention by

intention. Consumers are more likely to respond favourably to influencer recommendations when
they perceive the influencer as credible, authentic, and capable of establishing meaningful
engagement. These findings reinforce the continuing importance of relationship quality and
credibility in digital marketing despite rapid technological advances.  The study also demonstrates
that consumer characteristics influence the effectiveness of AI -generated influencers. Individuals
with greater AI literacy and stronger innovativeness appear more receptive to AI-enabled marketing
communication, suggesting that  future influencer strategies should be tailored according to
consumer technological readiness and innovation orientation. Organisations should therefore
consider audience characteristics  when selecting between AI -generated and human influencers or
when designing hybrid influencer campaigns.  From a theoretical perspective, the study contributes
to the influencer marketing literature by integrating multiple behavioural theories into a unified
framework explaining consumer responses to emerging AI technologies. The proposed model
expands existin g knowledge by simultaneously examining direct, mediating, and moderating
relationships within a single analytical framework. From a practical perspective, the findings suggest

8. Future Scope of the Study
The rapid advancement of artificial intelligence and generative technologies presents numerous
opportunities for extending research on AI-generated influencers and consumer behavior. While this
study provides a comprehensive framework for understanding the  relationships among influencer
type, trust, perceived authenticity, consumer engagement, AI literacy, consumer innovativeness, and
purchase intention, several promising avenues remain for future investigation.  First, future studies
should validate the proposed framework across different countries and cultural contexts. Consumer
perceptions of AI -generated influencers may vary according to cultural values, technological
readiness, and social media usage patterns. Comparative studies involving developed and emerging
economies would provide deeper insights into cross -cultural differences in consumer acceptance of
AI-generated influencers and enhance the generalizability of the proposed model.  Second, future
research may extend the proposed framework by incorpo rating additional psychological and
behavioral constructs. Variables such as anthropomorphism, perceived intelligence, emotional
attachment, perceived enjoyment, social presence, perceived risk, privacy concerns, transparency,

electronics, healthcare, tourism, financial services, education, and lux ury brands. Industry -specific

than human influencers and vice versa.  Fourth, future studies should employ longitudinal research
designs to examine how consumer perceptions evolve over time. As consumers become increasingly
familiar with artificial intelligence and virtual influencers, their trust, authenticity perceptions, and
purchase intentions may change. Longitudinal studies would provide valuable insights into the long-

research may adopt experimental and mixed -method approaches to complement survey -based
investigations. Controlled experiments can establish causal relationships between influencer type
and consumer responses, while qualitative methods such as interviews and focus groups can provide
richer insights into consumers' experiences, motivations, and concerns regarding AI -generated
influencers. Finally, advances in generative artificial intelligence, virtual reality, augmented reality,
and the metaverse are expected to reshape digital marketing and influencer ecosystems. Future
