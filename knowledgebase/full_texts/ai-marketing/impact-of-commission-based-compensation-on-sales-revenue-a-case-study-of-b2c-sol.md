Abstract

 B2C Solar Pay -As-You-Go (PAYGO) organizations in Zambia face challenges related to sales force costs,
customer affordability, and delayed revenue realization. This study assessed the impact of commission -based
compensation on sales revenue in B2C Solar PAY GO organizations in Zambia, focusing on commission
structures, sales agent productivity, and organizational benefits. A quantitative cross-sectional survey, guided by
a positivist philosophy, was conducted among 114 sales agents and 25 managers from four o rganizations:
SunKing, MySol/Engie, Vitalite, and RDG, operating in Lusaka and Kitwe. Data were analyzed using descriptive
statistics, independent-samples t -tests, one-way ANOVA, Pearson correlation, and cross -tabulation in SPSS.
Salary plus commission was  the dominant compensation structure (55.3%), followed by commission -only
arrangements (29.8%). Agents reported modest understanding of commission systems (mean = 3.28), while
perceptions of payment timeliness (2.69) and fairness (2.74) were below the neut ral midpoint. The mean
motivation score was 3.52. No significant differences in motivation were found across compensation structures
(F = 1.388, p = 0.251) or gender (t = 0.620, p = 0.537). However, a very strong positive relationship was observed
between motivation and sales performance (r = 0.861, p < 0.001). In addition, 84% of managers agreed that
commissions improve organizational performance. The findings indicate that commission-based compensation
is positively associated with sales agent motivation and performance, although its effectiveness is influenced by
agents' understanding, perceptions of fairness, payment timeliness, and age. The study recommends transparent
commission communication, timely payments, equitable compensation structures, and tar geted support
strategies to enhance sales outcomes.

1. Introduction
Sales force compensation is a critical component of organizational strategy because it directly influences employee motivatio n, productivity, and revenue
generation. In competitive markets, organizations increasingly adopt performance -based compensation systems to align employee effort with organizational
objectives and improve financial performance (Dessler, 2008; Goel, 2025). Among these systems, commission -based compensation has become one of the
widely used approaches for motivating sales personnel by rewarding them according to their sales achievements (Ahearne & Ahearne, 2025). Research suggests
that well-designed commission structures can increase employee commitment, improve sales performance, and contribute to sustainable organizational growth,
although their effectiveness depends on factors such as fairness, transparency, and timely payment (Goel, 2025; Perros et al., 2024).
In Zambia, Business-to-Consumer (B2C) Solar Pay-As-You-Go (PAYGO) organizations operate in a unique business environment characterized by installment-
based payment models, customer affordability constraints, and delayed revenue realization. These organizations depend heavily on field sales agents to educate
customers, acquire new clients, facilitate financing arrangements, and support continued customer repayments. Consequently, c ommission-based
compensation has become an important mechanism for motivating sales agents and supporting organizational revenue growth (Schneider & Anderie, 2025).
However, the effectiveness of different compensation structures may be affected by issues such as payment delays and perceive d fairness (Perros et al. 2024:
Yadav et al., 2019).
Despite the use of commission -based compensation in the sector, limited empirical evidence exists on its relationship with sales revenue and sales agent
productivity within Zambia's B2C Solar PAYGO organizations. Previous studies have largely focused on so lar energy access, affordability, financing, and
sustainability, with comparatively limited attention to sales force compensation and its relationship with organizational performance (Perros et al. (2024; Yadav
et al., 2019). This study therefore examines the impact of commission-based compensation on sales revenue in selected B2C Solar PAYGO organizations in
Zambia by assessing compensation structures, sales agent productivity, and organizational benefits. The findings provide evidence to support the development
of effective compensation systems that may enhance motivation, productivity, and revenue generation.

1.1 Statement of the problem
The cost of doing business in Zambia remains persistently high, with human resource and sales force costs constituting a major component of operational
expenditure, particularly in sales -driven sectors such as B2C solar Pay -As-You-Go (PAYGO) organizations. These firms rely heavily on large, field -based
sales teams to acquire customers, manage installment payments, and provide after-sales support. For example, solar PAYGO companies operating in low-
income and peri-urban communities often incur substantial co sts related to recruitment, training, supervision, and remuneration of sales agents, while
revenue realization is delayed due to installment-based payment models.
To manage these costs and improve revenue performance, many organizations have adopted commission-based compensation systems that link pay directly
to sales outcomes. Recent evidence suggests that performance-linked and data-driven incentive systems can help organizations align employee effort with
revenue objectives and optimize compensation efficiency (Goel, 2025). However, the effectiveness of such systems in cost cont ainment and sustained
revenue growth is highly context dependent. In PAYGO business mod els, where customer affordability constraints and income volatility are prevalent,
commission-based pay may not always translate into predictable or sustainable revenue outcomes (Perros et al., 2024).
Despite the widespread use of commission-based compensation, empirical evidence on its impact on sales revenue in Zambia’s B2C solar PAYGO sector
remains limited. Existing studies on Zambia’s solar energy industry focus primarily on affordability, access t o clean energy, financing challenges, and
sustainability, with little attention given to sales compensation structures and their cost-revenue implications (Perros et al. 2024). This lack of sector-specific
evidence constitutes a clear research gap. If this gap is not addressed, this could weaken the sector’s ability to expand access to clean energy and contribute
to broader climate and development goals.

1.2 Aim of the study
The aim of the study is to assess the impact of commission-based compensation on sales revenue in B2C PAYGO solar organizations in Zambia.

1.3 Objectives
• Established how commission-based compensation is implemented.
• Examined the effect of commission-based compensation on productivity.
• Assessed the benefits of commission-based compensation to the organization.

2 Literature Review and Theoretical Framework
The section reviewed literature on the impact of commission-based compensation on sales revenue, focusing on its effect on productivity, implementation,
and organizational benefits within Business-to-Consumer (B2C) Solar Pay-As-You-Go (PAYGO) organizations. The review establishes the theoretical and
empirical foundation for the study while identifying research gaps that justify the investigation. Commission-based compensation is widely recognized as
a strategic tool for motivating sales personnel and aligning employee effort with organizational objectives, particularly revenue growth and market expansion
(Goel, 2025; Ahearne & Ahearne, 2025). In Solar PAYGO businesses, where organizations depend on field-based agents to acquire customers and manage
installment payments, effective compensation systems are essential for sustaining sales performance.

2.1 Effect of Commission-Based Compensation on Productivity
Globally, commission-based compensation is associated with improved sales productivity and organizational performance. Goel (2025) found that data -
driven commission structures significantly improve sales productivity and revenue forecasting by encouraging employees to exceed performance targets.
Similarly, Ahearne and Ahearne (2025) argue that commission incentives positively influence sales behaviour when aligned with organizational goals, while
Akuffo-Aduamah (2025) reports that employees perform better when rewards are directly linked to effort. However, excessive dependence on commissions
may encourage unethical selling practices, increase job stress, and contribute to employee turnover if poorly designed (Eloma ri, 2024; Jayaratne &
Rathnayake, 2025; Mendes, 2025). These findings suggest that commission -based compensation enhances productivity when supported by transparent
and balanced incentive systems.
Across Africa, commission-based compensation has become common because it provides organizations with flexibility while motivating sales personnel.
Studies conducted in Nigeria and other African countries show that commission -based employees consistently outperform those receiving fixed salaries
(Adegoroye & Ojo, 2025; Obiora et al., 2025). Within renewable energy markets, Yadav et al. (2025) and Schneider and Anderie (2025) highlight that
commission-driven sales models have been instrumental in expanding a ccess to solar energy among low -income households. Nevertheless, African
organizations continue to face challenges including weak performance monitoring systems, labour informality, and employee burnout, which may reduce
the effectiveness of commission-based compensation (Kansiime et al., 2025; Anusi, 2025).
Research on commission-based compensation in Zambia remains limited, particularly within the Solar PAYGO industry. Existing studies mainly focus on
renewable energy adoption, affordability, and financing rather than compensation practices. Zambia's PAYGO s ector operates under unique conditions
characterized by geographically dispersed customers, low purchasing power, and installment-based repayments (da Corta & Bwalya, 2025). These conditions
place considerable responsibility on sales agents, making effective commission structures essential for improving productivity and sales revenue. However,
little empirical evidence exists on how commission-based compensation influences revenue performance within the sector.

2.2 Implementation of Commission-Based Compensation
Organizations implement commission -based compensation using different structures, including straight commission, salary plus commission, and
commission with bonuses. Hybrid systems combining salary and commission are increasingly preferred because they pro vide income security while
maintaining performance incentives (Goel, 2025; Ahearne & Ahearne, 2025). Advances in data analytics have further improved compensation design by
enabling organizations to establish transparent, equitable, and performance-oriented commission structures (Marlizan & Rajasegaram, 2025).

Within Solar PAYGO organizations, implementation is more complex because sales agents are responsible not only for customer acquisition but also for
repayment monitoring and customer retention. Consequently, commission structures often reward both sales an d repayment performance (Yadav et al.,
2019; Schneider & Anderie, 2025). However, ineffective implementation, delayed commission payments, and poor transparency can  reduce employee
motivation and weaken organizational performance (Elomari, 2024; Jayaratne & Rathnayake, 2025).

2.3 Organizational Benefits of Commission-Based Compensation
Commission-based compensation offers several organizational advantages. The most important benefit is improved sales revenue through inc reased
employee motivation and productivity (Goel, 2025). Organizations also benefit from lower fixed labour costs becau se part of employee compensation
depends on actual sales performance rather than fixed salaries (Perros et al., 2024). Additional benefits include greater acc ountability, improved goal
alignment, and enhanced operational efficiency (Aziz et al., 2025). For  Solar PAYGO organizations, commission-based compensation facilitates market
expansion by encouraging agents to reach underserved rural and peri-urban communities while supporting customer repayment compliance (Yadav et al.,
2025). Nevertheless, organizations must carefully manage commission systems to avoid inequities, administrative complexity, and employee burnout that
could undermine long-term performance (Marlizan & Rajasegaram, 2025; Anusi, 2025).
The reviewed literature consistently demonstrates that commission -based compensation positively influences employee motivation, productivity, and
organizational performance. Global studies emphasize sophisticated performance management systems and data -driven compensation models, while
African research confirms the importance of commission -based incentives in improving customer acquisition and market expansion, particularly in
renewable energy sectors (Goel, 2025; Adegoroye & Ojo, 2025; Yadav et al., 2025).  Despite these findings, important gaps remain. First, most studies
concentrate on employee motivation and productivity rather than directly examining sales revenue outcomes. Second, limited re search has focused
specifically on B2C Solar PAYGO organizations, despite their growing role in expanding renewable energy access. Third, empirical evidence from Zambia
remains scarce, with existing studies concentrating mainly on energy access, financing, and affordability rather than compensation systems.
This study addresses these gaps by examining the impact of commission-based compensation on sales revenue in Zambia's B2C Solar PAYGO organizations.
Unlike previous studies, it investigates how commission structures are implemented, their influence on sales agent productivity, and their contribution to
organizational revenue within the Zambian context.
The literature shows that commission-based compensation is an effective strategy for improving sales performance when incentive systems are transparent,
fair, and aligned with organizational objectives. Hybrid compensation models combining salary and commi ssion appear more sustainable because they
balance employee motivation with income security. Successful implementation also requires ethical oversight, effective monito ring, timely commission
payments, and performance measurement systems. Although international and African studies provide valuable insights, limited empirical evidence exists
for Zambia's Solar PAYGO sector. This study therefore contributes context -specific evidence on how commission -based compensation influences sales
revenue, providing prac tical guidance for managers, policymakers, and researchers seeking to strengthen organizational performance within Zambia's
renewable energy industry.

2.4 Theoretical Framework
This study is anchored on Expectancy Theory (Vroom, 1964), Agency Theory (Jensen & Meckling, 1976), and Equity Theory (Adams, 1963), which provide
a foundation for understanding how commission-based compensation influences sales performance and revenue out comes. Expectancy Theory explains
that sales agents are motivated when they believe that increased effort will lead to improved performance and attractive rewa rds. In B2C Solar PAYGO
organizations, commission payments create a direct link between sales act ivities and financial rewards, encouraging agents to increase productivity and
generate higher sales revenue.
Agency Theory explains how commission -based compensation aligns the interests of sales agents with organizational objectives by reducing conflicts
between managers and employees. By linking earnings to sales outcomes, organizations can improve accountabili ty and reduce monitoring challenges.
Equity Theory further highlights the importance of fairness, suggesting that employees are more motivated when they perceive their commission rewards
as proportional to their efforts compared with others. Together, these theories explain how compensation design, motivation, fairness, and performance
relationships influence sales revenue in B2C Solar PAYGO organizations.

3 Research Methodology
The study adopted a quantitative research approach, guided by a positivist philosophy, to examine the relationship between co mmission-based
compensation, sales agent motivation, productivity, and sales performance. A deductive approach and cross-sectional survey design were employed. Data
were collected using structured questionnaires from 114 sales agents across four B2C Solar PAYGO organizations: SunKing, MySol/ENGIE, Vitalite, and
RDG, operating in Lusaka and Kitwe, Zambia. Stratified random sampling was used to facilitate representation across the participating organizations. The
questionnaire comprised closed-ended and Likert-scale questions covering compensation structures, commission-system implementation, motivation, and
sales performance. Data were analyzed using SPSS, employing descriptive statistics, independent samples t -tests, one-way ANOVA, Pearson correlation,
and crosstabulation. Measures were undertaken to ensure the validity and reliability of the research instrument, while ethical principles, including informed
participation, confidentiality, and appropriate handling of respondent information, were observed throughout the study.

4 Presentation of Findings
The 114 sales agents were predominantly young, with moderate educational attainment and relatively limited work experience. Female agents accounted
for 54.4% (n = 62) of the sample, compared with 45.6% (n = 52) male agents. Although the distribution was re latively balanced, females constituted a
slight majority. Regarding age, agents aged 25–34 years formed the largest group at 54.4% (n = 62), followed by those below 25 years at 34.2% (n = 39).
Only 11.4% of the agents were aged 35 years and above. Educatio nal attainment was concentrated at the secondary level. GCE certificate holders
represented 80.7% (n = 92) of the sample, while 7.9% (n = 9) held a Grade 9 certificate and another 7.9% (n = 9) held a diploma. Only 3.6% (n = 4) had
bachelor's or master's degrees. In terms of work experience, 63.2% (n = 72) had between one and three years of experience, 26.3% (n = 30) had less than
one year, and 10.5% (n = 12) had four or more years of experience.

distribution demonstrates that B2C Solar PAYGO organizations in Zambia predominantly favor hybrid compensation models that balance income stability
with performance incentives, though a substantial minority of agents operate under pure commission structures that offer higher earning potential at the
cost of income predictability. The minimal use of Salary Only and Salary + Bonus arrangements suggests that fixed compensatio n without performance
linkage is largely discouraged in this sales-driven sector.

4.2 Discussions of Findings
The study found that Salary and Commission was the most common compensation structure among sales agents (55.3%), followed by Commission Only
(29.8%). This finding supports Ahearne and Ahearne (2025), who argued that hybrid compensation models are increasingly preferred because they provide
income stability while maintaining performance incentives. Similarly, Goel (2025) emphasized that organizations are shifting toward performance-based
systems to improve sales effectiveness.
However, the significant proportion of agents working under Commission Only arrangements raises concerns regarding income sec urity, employee
retention, and long -term sustainability. Although commission -only systems reduce fixed labour costs and encourage s ales effort, previous studies have
highlighted risks such as income instability, short -term sales behaviour, and employee stress (Elomari , 2024; Jayaratne & Rathnayake, 2025). These
concerns are relevant in Zambia’s Solar PAYGO sector, where agents operate in challenging markets characterized by customer a ffordability constraints
and delayed revenue collection.
The findings also revealed gender differences in compensation structures, with male agents more likely to work under Commissi on Only arrangements
while female agents were more represented in Salary + Commission structures. This raises possible equity concerns, as explained by Equity Theory (Adams,
1963), which suggests that employees compare their rewards with those of others. Additionally, agents reported only moderate understanding of
commission systems (mean = 3.28) and low perceptions of payment timelin ess (2.69) and fairness (2.74). These findings suggest that unclear
communication and delayed payments may weaken the motivational impact of commission systems, supporting the argument by Goel (2025) and Perros
et al. (2024) that transparency and fairness are essential for effective incentive systems.
The findings also demonstrated that commission -based compensation positively influences sales agent productivity, with an overall motivation score of
3.52. More than half of agents agreed that commissions improve time management (54.4%) and encourage custo mer follow-ups (56.2%). These results
support previous studies showing that performance-based rewards increase employee effort, goal orientation, and sales behaviour (Goel, 2025; Ahearne &
Ahearne, 2025).
However, the ANOVA results showed no statistically significant differences in motivation across compensation structures (p = 0.251). This suggests that
while commissions contribute to motivation, the specific pay structure alone may not determine employee performance. Other factors, including
organizational culture, supervision, market conditions, and individual characteristics, may also influence productivity. This  aligns with Kansiime et al.
(2025), who noted that productivity outcomes in African markets depend on broader organizational and environmental factors.
The gender analysis further showed no significant difference in motivation between male and female agents (p = 0.537). This i ndicates that commission-
based compensation can motivate employees regardless of gender. However, the earlier differences in pay structure allocation highlight the importance of
ensuring equal access to compensation opportunities.
The study further found that commission -based compensation provides important organizational benefits, particularly in improving sales performance
and revenue outcomes. The strong positive relationship between motivation and sales performance (r = 0.861, p  < 0.001) demonstrates that motivated
agents achieve better sales results. This finding strongly supports Expectancy Theory, which argues that employees perform better when they believe their
effort will lead to valuable rewards.
Managers also expressed strong support for commission -based compensation, with 84% agreeing that commissions improve organizational performance
(mean = 3.81). This supports Agency Theory, which explains that organizations use incentive systems to align emp loyee behaviour with organizational
objectives. By linking compensation to sales outcomes, commission systems encourage accountability and reduce monitoring challenges.
Despite these benefits, some concerns were identified. Poorly designed commission structures may encourage unethical selling practices, excessive pressure,
and unhealthy competition, as highlighted by Elomari (2024) and Mendes (2025). Therefore, organizations must balance revenue targets with ethical sales
practices and customer relationship management.

The findings therefore provide support for the three theoretical frameworks guiding the study. Expectancy Theory is supported by the strong relationship

Agency Theory is supported by the dominance of Salary and Commission structures and strong managerial support for commission systems. The findings

Equity Theory is reflected in agents’ concerns regarding fairness and payment transparency. Perceptions of unequal treatment or unclear commission

opportunities. The findings confirm that commission-based compensation can improve motivation, productivity, and sales revenue in B2C Solar PAYGO

incentives and organizational goals.

5 Conclusion and Recommendations
It can therefore be concluded that the commission-based compensation implemented within B2C Solar PAYGO organizations in Zambia is the dominant
pay structure employed by 55.3% of sales agents. However, the findings also revealed that agent understanding of commission systems is only modest and
perceptions of payment timeliness fall below acceptable levels, indicating significant implementation challenges. The effect of commission -based
compensation on productivity, was achieved by demonstrating that commiss ions positively influence agent motivation, with over half of agents agreeing
that commissions improve their time management and encourage customer follow-ups. The ANOVA results further confirmed that motivation levels do
not differ significantly across pay structures (p = 0.251), suggesting that the presence of commission incentives matters more than the specific structure in
driving productivity. The organizational benefits of commission-based compensation, was achieved by revealing a very strong positive correlation between
motivation and sales performance (r = 0.861, p < 0.001), confirming that motivated agents generate higher revenue outcomes. A dditionally, the finding
that 84% of managers agree that commissions improve organizational performance, coupl ed with the age -based performance pattern showing peak
effectiveness among agents aged 35 -44 years, validates that commission -based compensation delivers meaningful organizational benefits when properly
implemented.
The study therefore recommends the following:
• Solar PAYGO organizations should implement regular training sessions and transparent payment schedules to address the low com mission
understanding, poor payment timeliness, and weak fairness perceptions identified in this study.
• Solar PAYGO organizations should develop age-specific commission support strategies, including mentorship for younger agents, flexible targets for
agents aged 45+ who recorded the lowest performance, and tiered commission structures that accommodate varying career stages and risk tolerances.
• The Ministry of Labour and Energy Regulation Board should establish minimum standards for sales compensation in the renewable energy sector,
including written commission agreements, maximum allowable payment delays, and formal dispute resolution mechanisms to protec t agents under
Commission Only structures.
• Solar PAYGO organizations should audit their recruitment and role assignment practices to address the gender pay structure di sparity identified in
this study, where male agents are more frequently placed in Commission Only roles compared to female agents.
• Solar PAYGO organizations should establish formal feedback mechanisms, including monthly commission review meetings and anonymous reporting
channels, to address the substantial variability in motivation scores and identify agents who are not responding pos itively to current commission
structures before they become disengaged or leave the organization.
• Solar PAYGO organizations should pilot hybrid commission models that incorporate non -financial recognition elements, such as public
acknowledgment, awards, or career advancement opportunities, to complement monetary incentives, particularly for agents who reported neutrality
or disagreement with commission effectiveness.
