  The banking industry's shift towards digitalization, particularly Web 2.0 banking,
signifies a profound change in the bank -client relationship. This transformation
integrates digital elements into consumers' lives, digitising everyday activities and
highlighting the importance of online social platforms and digital business operations.
AI's growing interest in academia and commerce underscores its significant role in
customer service. The study examines AI strategies in the banking industry, focusing
on service areas and adaptation to the evolving technological landscape, particularly
in customer service in developing economies . By adopting an interpretivist
philosophy emphasising practicality, a qualitative approach was employed through a
systematic literat ure review of 90 articles using thematic analysis. The research
highlights AI's growing use in process automation, customer experience, stock market
trend prediction, credit risk management, online banking, marketing management, and
auditing. It concludes AI's positive impact on banking operational efficiency and
customer service by recommending ethical AI usage, stakeholder collaboration, trust
in data handling, and robust internal control systems. Additionally, it suggests
exploring the association betwee n organisational culture, leadership , and AI
integration in developing economies. The research contributes to the theoretical and
empirical frameworks that explain how AI is optimally integrated into banking
operations, guiding policymakers and practitioners in leveraging AI to foster financial
inclusion and economic growth. Future researchers should include non -profit
institutions and non -financial performance metrics to gain insights into AI’s
sustainability implications in banks beyond financial metrics.

1. Introduction
The rise of Web 2.0 has ushered in an era where Artificial Intelligence (AI) significantly impacts the
financial sector, especially banking (Agarwal et al., 2024; Al-Baity, 2023). AI advancements are essential for
financial institutions to meet evolving customer expectations and stay competitive. AI is transforming banking
by enhancing customer services, like chatbots and voice recognition, and improving backend operations, such
as credit underwriting and risk monitoring (Almatrafi et al., 2024). This integration is reshaping the industry,
boosting customer interactions, and streamlining financial processes, with substantial cost savings anticipated,
including over $447 billion in 2023 for banks in the UK and USA (Jameaba, 2022). Despite the benefits, AI
adoption in ban king presents challenges, such as developing efficient customer relationship management
systems and automating processes like credit scoring and loan approval (Andoni et al., 2019; Borges et al.,
2021). AI's expanding capabilities support various internal banking systems, particularly those related to
consumer behavio ur. However, the digital divide, limited access to advanced technologies, inadequate
infrastructure, and low digital literacy hamper AI implementation. The World Bank reported that only 43% of
the population in low -income countries had internet access, compared to 87% in high -income countries
(Mpofu, 2024; Mhlanga, 2021 ), hindering banks' ability to implement AI solutions requiring robust
connectivity and infrastructure. Consumer scepticism and mistrust towards AI-driven services in developing
economies pose barriers, with a PwC study revealing 38% of consumers are hesitant due to data privacy and
security concerns (Rabby, Chimhundu, & Hassan, 2022; Aiello et al., 2020; Abidin, Nawawi, & Salin, 2019).
Regulatory frameworks also struggle to ensure ethical AI use and data protection. A 2020 McKinsey report
noted that 60% of banks in sub -Saharan Africa operate on outdated sys tems, impeding AI integration
(Jayadatta & Majeed, 2024; Amankwah-Amoah, & Lu, 2022). Although, 25% of banks in developing regions
have implemented AI in customer service compared to 45% in developed regions  (Sheth et al., 2022) .
Operational inefficiencies are prevalent, with 45% of banks in developing economies reporting high costs
associated with manual processes and customer support (Zuhair et al., 2024; Charoenwong et al., 2024). These
challenges highlight the need for a strategic approach to AI adoption in the banking sector of developing
economies to unlock AI's full potential in revolutionizing customer service journeys and banking service areas.
 AI's influence in banking, as outlined by research from Allied Market Research (AMR) and Grand
View Research (GVR), is profound. In 2020, the market for AI in banking was valued at $12.9 billion, soaring
to an impressive $196.63 billion in 2023, and is expected to continue rising, potentially reaching new heights
by 2030. This growth is propelled by a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 37.3% from 2023 to 2030
(AMR, 2021; GVR, 2022). AI is a game -changer, empowering banks to stay competitive in the digital age
against FinTech companies. According to a 2020 study by the National Business Research Institute and
Narrative Science, approximately 32% of banks have embr aced AI technologies, particularly through
predictive analytics and voice recognition, to gain a competitive edge. The implementation of AI has the
potential to significantly boost revenue in various banking segments, with corporate and retail banking
anticipated to reap the most benefits. Successful AI implementations could add an estimated $321 billion and
$306 billion in value to these sectors, respectively (Statista, 2024a). In investment banking, machine learning
leads as the most prevalent AI application, with a usage rate of 60% to 80%, followed by predictive analytics
and virtual assistant technologies (Statista, 2022). Reflecting on the banking sector's business landscape,
Generative AI (GenAI) emerges as a significant player, potentially contributing $200 to $340 billion in value
to the industry. This figure translates to 3% to 5% of total industry revenue, and successful GenAI deployment
has been linked to a profit increase of 9% to 15% for the banking sector (Statista, 2024b).
 Research on AI in the banking industry has undergone significant evolution  (Calderon-Monge &
Ribeiro-Soriano, 2024), particularly concerning service areas and customer service journeys in developing
economies. Initially, studies focused on theoretical applications, highligh ting benefits like cost reduction,
efficiency improvement, and enhanced customer experiences  (Di-Vaio et al., 2023) . As AI technologies
became more accessible, research shifted towards practical aspects, examining its actual impact on banking
operations an d customer interactions  (Gupta & Ramachandran, 2021) . Currently, its key evolution is the
increasing emphasis on specific service areas that revolve customer complaints  and satisfaction (Gurtu &
Johny, 2019). Early research broadly discussed AI's potential  across various banking functions, but recent
studies delve deeper, exploring nuances of AI implementation  (Debrah et al., 2022) . Research now

the customer experience but identified a gap in understanding its effects on the customer service journey. These
studies underscore the potential of AI in improving business performance but also reveal the need for further
investigation into its long-term implications and integration within the customer domain. This is particularly
relevant in developing economies, where there is a notable scarcity of literature specifically addressing the
consumer aspect of AI in the banking industry, despite the growing presence of AI in this sector. Mor eover,

dynamics and explore their effect on marketing performance,

2. Literature Review and Theoretical Framework
2.1. Stakeholder and Agency Theory
According to Garcia et al. (2023), AI can help mitigate information asymmetries and ethical concerns in
the banking industry. This capability is attributed to features such as transparency, precise dissemination of
information, un-changeability, and computational reasoning, facilitated by technologies like smart contracts
and automation. These are excellently fostered by the collaboration of the service areas offered by the manager
and stakeholder. Consequently, theories from managerial or stakeholder perspectives provide insights into how
AI shape the customer’s service journey through provision of excellent service areas that has impact on their
purchase decision cycle (Lakhchini et al., 2022; Leung et al., 2013).
According to the agency theory coined in 1976 by Meckling, Michael and Jensen, AI technology has the
potential to improve the ac curacy of accounting data through features anchored on smart contracts and
meticulous record-keeping (Sidorova & Rafiee, 2019). AI enhances the verification of data by establishing
agreement among multiple stakeholders, making it harder for managers to alter information. Additionally, AI
simplifies tasks such as account reconciliation by using shared block-chain ledgers in triple-entry accounting
(Agerfalk, 2020). The integration of block -chain technology and AI is expected to enhance the management
and oversight of accounting data, making financial fraud more difficult to conceal. This synergy allows for the
automation of various tasks such as bill payments, expense tracking, audit sampling, and compliance processes
through the use of smart contracts. Addit ionally, AI's ability to detect anomalies, including the real -time
identification of suspicious transactions, is anticipated to improve the detection of financial irregularities.
However, it is important to note that while AI can reduce information asymmetries and address principal-agent
problems, the system remains susceptible to manipulation if individuals tamper with the initial or raw data on
the block-chain (Fritz et al., 2020). Even when the potential rewards are significant, companies may still be
inclined to manipulate original data through deceitful actions. As a result, there is a risk that if managers
acquire more than half of the computing power (51%), they might be able to manipulate a block-chain's ledger
by adding incorrect information or modifying/deleting previous records (Han et al., 2023).
Rooted in R. Edward Freeman's stakeholder theory from the 1980s, AI is a powerful tool that is
transforming the banking sector into a more open and inclusive environment (Mhlanga & Moloi, 2020). This
technology allows various participants, including accountants, business partners, and investors, to actively
engage in block-chain ecosystems. These participants work together to oversee, update, or verify transactions
based on their specific access rights  (Miller, 2022). This collaborative model enhances stakeholder
involvement and opens up new business opportunities within block-chain networks. As a result, the adoption
of an event-centric accounting model, where real-time data is captured on block -chains, caters to the diverse
needs and goals of different accounting information users (Bitskkovskaya et al., 2022). AI-driven applications
play a pivotal role in identifying patterns and predicting trends. Within the block -chain network, authorized
users can m onitor transaction data in real -time. The implementation of triple -entry accounting introduces a
shared ledger, which acts as a single source of truth for authorized users (Gungor, 2022). Continuous auditing
practices enhance assurance mechanisms, thereby increasing trust. However, it is crucial to address and

3. Material and Methods
The interpretivist philosophy, emphasising the researcher's core beliefs regarding the creation of
knowledge, is often hailed as a cornerstone of systematic literature review in behavioural research. According
to Morgan, (2014), its value stem s from its ability to integrate qualitative reasoning while highlighting the
significance of grounding ideas and actions in practicality and real -world applicability as a measure of their
validity and importance. Adopting an interpretivist approach prioritizes actions and strategies that lead to
tangible and positive outcomes. It emphasizes the need for adaptability and flexibility in thinking, encouraging
openness to experimental eff orts that adjust strategies based on feedback and insights from trends in the
banking industry (Simpson, 2018; Maarouf, 2019). Interpretivist reasoning allows banks to quickly adapt to
unexpected challenges and seize new opportunities, enabling essential a djustments to their AI strategies. By
focusing on concrete results, this philosophy can effectively reduce the risks associated with sticking to
ineffective AI strategies, ensuring that banks remain grounded in market realities. This ensures that AI-driven
decisions are based on solid data rather than vague assumptions, leading to enhanced performance in the
banking sector. This research adopts the systematic literature survey methodology outlined by Zhai, (2023)
and Wolfswinkel et al., (2013), which is str uctured into five distinct stages. Namely: scope of research
inclusion and exclusion criteria, search query of literature survey, sample selection, thematic analysis and
result presentation. These stages are necessary and designed to facilitate a thorough exploration and evaluation
of the collected articles from various databases.

3.2 Keywords’ Search Strategy

In January 2023, an extensive literature review was conducted using prominent academic databases such
as ProQuest, IEEE, Scopus, Google Scholar, Sciendo, Scimago, Science Direct, and Web of Science to ensure
a comprehensive examination of AI in behavioural science research. These databases were selected due to
their relevance to the research topic, while providing a broad range of data and serving as a secondary source
for additional materials (Boonstra & Laven, 2022). According to the search framework outlined by Zawacki-
Richter et al., (2019), the search strategy e mployed specific keywords related to the study's focus, including
"AI (AI)," "AI in organizational performance," "Consumer Behaviour," "Banking Industry," "Customer
Service Journeys," "Service Areas," "Digital Banking in Developing Economy," "Customer Expe rience,"
"Banking’s Applications of AI," "Technology Adoption in Banking," “Banking Sector in Developing
Economy," "AI applications," "Performance enhancement through AI," "Adoption of technology in the
banking sector," "Financial sector in Developing economy," "Thematic analysis of AI’s usage," "Efficiency in
organizations," and "Sustainability in business"," combined with Boolean operators "OR"; “BETWEEN”
“NEXUS” and "AND" to refine the results. However, the snowball method was utilized to broaden the search
scope and exclude irrelevant materials by meticulously reviewing reference lists in pertinent review papers
and related studies. This ensure s the inclusion of a diverse range of empirical research that foster s the
accumulation of a comprehensive body of knowledge to support the methodological review (Wohlin, 2014).
This approach was deemed ideal for providing effortless access to a wide range of information from multiple
sources, such as industry analyses, financial statistics, and scholarly research.

3.4 Thematic Analysis
A thematic analysis was conducted using Nvivo version 11.0 and MS Excel to systematically identify and
construct themes from the literature. This analytical method is ideal as it allows for a systematic examination
and documentation of prevalent patterns within the data, providing deeper insights into various fact ors
influencing AI's impact on consumer behaviour (Harper & Thompson, 2011; Fryer, 2014). Analysing the
interplay between AI technology, customer service experiences, and financial management tactics provides a
thorough examination of the challenges and op portunities that banks face in adopting AI in developing
economies to enhance their operational efficiency and competitiveness. As Clarke and Braun, (2017) suggest,
identifying key themes is appropriate for this research, as it allows for an in -depth exami nation of the
complexities involved in integrating AI technologies into a highly regulated and competitive sector. Through
a systematic review of various sources, including industry reports, academic research, and expert opinions, the
researcher can gain valuable insights that can guide strategic decisions and enhance the performance of banks
by effectively incorporating AI solutions in developing economies.
 The research follows a six-step procedure outlined by Zhai, (2023) for thematic analysis namely: data
familiarization, thematic coding, identifying and reviewing sub-dimensions of the themes, compiling recurring
concepts, and analysing and interpretation of the data according to the study's objectives aim to maintain the
quality and relevance of the lit erature. The PRISMA method, which includes a 90 -item checklist and a four-
phase flow diagram, was employed for screening in systematic reviews and meta -analyses to ensure this
standard, as depicted in Figure 2. This technique guarantees a meticulous and me thodical examination of the
literature, leading to an in -depth comprehension of the impact of AI on the efficiency of banks, while
conforming to a prescribed set of guidelines for reporting systematic reviews and meta-analyses (Zhai, 2023).

4. RESULT PRESENTATION
4.1 Description of Article Profile
As AI becomes part of the banking sector daily routine, the study objective anchored on service areas and
customer service journey as reflected in Figure 3 and Figure 4, focusing primarily on AI’s application within
the banking sector. Consequently, a thorough examination of the selected 90 articles revealed that 71% (64
articles) of the articles explored the various aspects of AI’s usage in banking sector, including “Equity Markets
and Investment, Risk and Credit Management, Retail Financing and Banking, Consumer Decision Making and
Marketing Segmentation, Financial Management and Strategy, Financial Accounting and  Auditing and
Assurance, and Technology and Automation”. However, 81% of these articles adopted a qualitative research
approach, of which content research strategy becomes their most common data collection method. However,
10 articles followed a document analysis. Moreover, the research predominantly covered developing countries,
with a particular emphasis on the Australia, USA, China, Japan, UK, and Saudi Arabia. Although, limited
studies focus on Nigeria as a central realm, implying that research on AI’s usage in consumer behaviour is at
early stage of life in Nigeria. Kao et al., (2012) argue the performance of hedge funds during the financial
crisis of 2008, by employ ing a comprehensive dataset of hedge fund returns and us ing various statistical
techniques to analyse how several factors influencing hedge fund performance during the crisis. They suggest
that AI play critical roles in hedge funds by facilitat ing higher liquidity and lower leverage through block -
chain that adequately withstand the market turmoil. This highlights the importance of AI in helping fund
managers' skills in navigating challenging market conditions. Furthermore, Han et al., (2023) argue that there
is extensive use of AI in accounting and auditing for transparency and faithful reporting, specifically the
application of block -chain technology for real -time audit trails in developing economy. This innovation is
praised for its ability to increase audit efficiency and reduce human errors, leading to enhanced marketing
performance by streamlining manual data extraction and audit preparation processes. Finally, Khandani et al.,
(2010) examine the role of AI in analysing the trading data of individual stocks showing that stocks with higher
liquidity risk experienced larger price declines during the crash. This evidence that liquidity risk was a
significant factor in the market downturn and emphasizes the need for investors to consider liquidity risk in
their investment strategies. Therefore, the identified studies contribute to the understanding of AI’s usage in
financial market dynamics during periods of stress and offer valuable insights for investors and policymakers
in managing risk and enhancing market stability.

4.2 Presentation of Research Themes
To investigate the application of AI in the banking sector, the research approach began with a deduct ive
method, organizing articles into predefined themes to identify key themes. An inductive method was then used
to identify sub-themes and provide context to the main themes (see Figure 3). Borges et al., (2020), Bavaresco
et al., (2020), Loureiro et al.,  (2020), and Verma et al., (2021) describe a methodological approach for
identifying key themes that involve a comprehensive review of prior systematic literature reviews, the
extraction of keywords, and the development of codes (themes) from selected articles. This process also entails
a thorough examination of titles, abstracts, and full texts, as required, to ensure precise categorization within
these identified themes. Through this process, four main themes were identified: Business Strategy, Business
Process, Business Challenge, and Customer Service Blueprint, as shown in Figure 4.

4.2.2 Business Process
AI became a reality and gained traction in the global banking sector after the dot com bubble and the

beyond credit analysis. Therefore, the sub-theme "AI and Services," which includes twenty articles, explores
the role of AI in improving and optimizing processes. It focuses on how technology can enhance internal
service processes in financial institutions. Soltani et al., (2019) explore the application of machine learning to
optimize appointment scheduling and decrease service durations. The central idea underscores the capability
of AI to improve banking procedures and functions. Nonetheless, there is a lack of empirical behavioural
studies on the incorporation of techno logy into the banking infrastructure. Although there is a wealth of
research on credit risk, the examination of additional financial products like cryptocurrency, block-chain, and
black market currency is still restricted.

5.1 Research Implication, Limitation and Gap for Further Enquiries
The research findings reveal practical implications for the banking sector in these regions. These
implications span across operational efficiency, customer experience, financial inclusion, and regulatory
challenges. First ly, AI's integration into banking operations significantly enhances operational efficiency
(Hübenbecker, 2023; Milian et al., 2019) . By automating routine tasks such as transaction processing, fraud
detection, and customer service inquiries, banks can redu ce costs and minimize human error. This leads to
improved accuracy and faster processing times, which are crucial for customer satisfaction and competitive
advantage. The adoption of AI-driven predictive analytics allows banks to better manage risk, optimi ze their
credit scoring processes, and offer personalized financial products  (Kumar et al., 2021; Kusal et al., 2022) .
Consequently, banks can operate more efficiently and allocate resources to more strategic initiatives, fostering

customer relationships and loyalty. In developing economies, where access to traditional banking services may
be limited (Mariani et al., 2023), AI-driven mobile banking solutions can bridge the gap, offering convenient
and accessible financial services to under served populations. Furthermore, the research highlights AI's
potential to promote financial inclusion in developing economies. By leveraging AI, banks can reach unbanked
and underbanked populations with tailored financial products and services. AI algorit hms can assess
creditworthiness using alternative data sources, such as mobile phone usage and social media activity

impact the acceptance and effectiveness of AI solutions in these regions. Lastly, the interdisciplinary nature of

6. CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATION
The role of AI in the banking sector is crucial, as it significantly influences consumer decisions,
operational efficiency, and overall firm performance. Although there has been a recent decline in short -term
investments in AI technologies by banks in developing economies , banks still continue to recognize the
importance of leveraging AI for both financial and non-financial services. Consistent with the findings of Wu
and Olson (2020), this study emphasizes the necessity of continuous investment in AI to mi tigate potential
risks and improve the integration of online and offline banking channels. The study firmly believes in the
inherent advantages of AI, particularly its positive impact on customer journey metrics such as loyalty and
transaction ease. This underscores the crucial role of AI in improving client interactions in the banking sector.
Moreover, the research recognizes the contribution of AI to enhancing the operational effectiveness of banking
services, which ultimately results in superior organizational performance. By effectively utilizing AI in areas
such as consumer behaviour analysis, financial reporting, auditing, stock market forecasting, and credit risk
assessment, banks can improve service quality and achieve commercial success. Therefore, AI is increasingly
becoming an essential part of banking operations, and in today's business environment, adopting AI is crucial
for enhancing organizational efficiency and ensuring long -term growth. This statement emphasizes the
importance of AI as a fundamental factor in achieving success within the dynamic banking industry. Therefore,
the below action plans are recommended:
I. Ethics in AI’s usage of consumer behaviou r: Financial institutions should develop and adhere to
ethical guidelines for AI usage, foc using on data privacy, information integrity, and security. Transparent
communication about AI processes is essential to foster customer trust. Thus, implementing robust security
measures will protect customer data, ensuring compliance with regulatory stan dards and fostering long-term
customer relationships.
II. Internal control system: As unethical withdraw al of customer funds become s widespread, internal
control accountants should establish continuous monitoring and improvement mechanisms for AI applications.
This includes implementing AI-driven continuous auditing to detect financial anomalies and regularly updating
AI models to meet changing business needs. Such practices will optimize AI systems, reduce error risks, and
