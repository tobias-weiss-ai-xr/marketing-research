ABSTRACT

The introduction of innovative products is a critical driver of competitive advantage
in highly turbulent technological environments. The objective of this article is to
systematically analyze the recent literature on the marketing strategies that optimize
the acceptance and performance of such innovations. Using a systematic -narrative
methodology, a final corpus of 20 scientific articles published between 2020 and
2025 in high-impact databases such as Scopus and Web of Science was selected.
The results reveal that commercial success does not reside exclusively in technical
superiority, but in the creative configuration of an integrated marketing mix, where
packaging design, dynamic pricing, and the diversification of channels toward e -
commerce reduce adoption barriers. A strategic shift is identified, moving from
traditional launch models toward agile, iterative, and niche approaches that prioritize
user co-creation to mitigate perceived risk. Furthermore, functional communication
and educational pre-announcements emerge as essential cognitive mechanisms for
reducing consumer resist ance to change in the face of radical innovations. It is
concluded that dynamic organizational capabilities, enhanced by Marketing 4.0 and
artificial intelligence, are crucial for personalizing the value proposition and ensuring
product sustainability in global markets.

Keywords: Marketing strategies, business strategies, commercialization, innovative

RESUMEN

La introducción de productos innovadores constituye un factor determinante para la
generación de ventajas competitivas en entornos tecnológicos altamente dinámicos. El
objetivo de este artículo es analizar sistemáticamente la literatura reciente sobre las
estrategias de marketing que optimizan la aceptación y el desempeño de dichas
innovaciones. Mediante una metodología de revisión sistemático -narrativa, se seleccionó
un corpus final de 20 artículos científicos publicados entre 2020 y 2025, indexados en bases
de datos de alto impacto como Scopus y Web of Science. Los resultados revelan que el
éxito comercial no depende exclusivamente de la superioridad técnica, sino de la
configuración creativa de una mezcla de marketing integrada, en la que el diseño del
empaque, la fijación dinámica de precios y la diversificación de los canales hacia el
comercio electrónico contribuyen a reducir las barreras de adopción. Se identifica un cambio
estratégico desde los modelos tradicionales de lanzamiento hacia enfoques ágile s,
iterativos y orientados a nichos de mercado, que priorizan la cocreación con los usuarios
para mitigar el riesgo percibido. Asimismo, la comunicación funcional y los anuncios
educativos previos al lanzamiento emergen como mecanismos cognitivos esenciale s para
reducir la resistencia del consumidor al cambio frente a innovaciones radicales. Se concluye
que las capacidades organizacionales dinámicas, potenciadas por el Marketing 4.0 y la
inteligencia artificial, resultan fundamentales para personalizar la p ropuesta de valor y
garantizar la sostenibilidad de los productos en los mercados globales.

INTRODUCCIÓN
The introduction of innovative products is now one of the main mechanisms through
which companies build and sustain competitive advantages in environments characterized
by high technological turbulence, fragmentation of demand, and shorter product life cycles
(Purchase & Volery, 2020). In this context, marketing strategies play a decisive role in
translating the technical potential of innovation into market acceptance, sustained adoption,
and economic performance (Edeh et al., 2020). Recent literature on innovation, marketing,
and strategic management agrees that the success of innovations does not depend solely
on their technological characteristics, but also on the ability of organizations to design and
implement novel combinations of product, price, distribution, and communication, as well as
customer-oriented relational and digital models (Wichmann et al., 2021; Vlačić et al., 2021).
In recent years, a specific field has emerged around the concept of innovation in
marketing, understood as the implementation of new marketing practices that invo lve
significant changes in the design of the offering, channels, promotion, or pricing (Purchase
& Volery, 2020). This literature shows a gradual shift from a focus on technological
innovation to a broader perspective that incorporates innovations in distr ibution, branding,
communication, and pricing mechanisms, in many cases driven by digitization and the rise
of big data (Figueiredo et al., 2021). At the same time, studies on market innovation analyze
how new markets are created or existing ones are trans formed, highlighting the emerging,
distributed, and non -linear nature of these processes, and the relevance of marketing
practices in shaping new spaces for exchange and use (Sprong et al., 2020).
Recent evidence indicates that marketing strategies influen ce the performance of
innovations in both domestic and international markets. In developing economies, the

performance of SMEs, highlighting the positive impact of m arketing innovation on the

Customer resistance is recognized as one of the greatest risks to the introduction of
innovations, especially in services and tourism, where consumers may show skepticism,
attachment to the status quo, or low willingness to change (Tuominen et al., 2022). Recent
qualitative studies show that the main  sources of resistance are associated with risk
perception and a lack of understanding of the value of innovation, which makes
communication and market education strategies critical tools for reducing uncertainty and
building trust (Sánchez et al., 2019). This reinforces the role of marketing communication
not only as a persuasive vehicle, but also as a cognitive mechanism for giving meaning to
innovations (Edeh et al., 2020).
The literature also highlights the procedural nature of commercializing highly
uncertain innovations, especially those that are radical or disruptive in nature (Putra et al.,
2023). Several systematic reviews show that marketing is no longer conceived as a final
stage in the development of new products, but rather as an iterative pro cess that begins in
the early stages, integrating market exploration, learning, ecosystem configuration, and
niche creation (Timoumi et al., 2022). In this context, the choice between mass launch
strategies, niche introduction, or phased approaches depends on technological, institutional,
and market conditions, as well as the state of the technological innovation system
surrounding the new offering (Li et al., 2022).
Another central theme in recent literature is the role of customer insights and
customer he terogeneity in the generation and success of innovations (Wijekoon et al.,
2021). Frameworks have been proposed that systematize the processes of generating,
disseminating, and applying customer insights for innovation, encompassing practices such
as co -creation, observation, testing, interpretation, and monitoring, and pointing out that
these practices are high priority for the research agenda and for innovation management in
marketing (Stremersch et al., 2024). At the same time, research on customer heterogeneity
and innovation-based competitive strategy indicates that differences in needs, knowledge,
and relational preferences can be sources of competitive advantage if the firm develops
dynamic capabilities to manage them in the design of its offering and  in its market entry
strategies (Wu & Monfort, 2022).
Digital transformation has amplified the possibilities of marketing strategies for
introducing innovative products (Figueiredo et al., 2021). Advances in technologies
associated with Industry 4.0, big data, and artificial intelligence have given rise to what has
been termed marketing 4.0, where large -scale personalization, advanced analytics, and
digital platforms enable the design of messages, experiences, and value propositions that
are highly tailored to specific segments and contexts (Kaur et al., 2022). Recent reviews on
the adoption of information technologies in digital marketing and on the role of artificial
intelligence in marketing highlight that strategic decisions are increasingly based on
predictive models of consumer behavior, automation of interactions, and continuous
optimization of the marketing mix, which reconfigures the traditional logic of launching and
disseminating innovations (Kaur et al., 2022; Vlačić et al., 2021).

Likewise, the literature on the marketing mix from a global perspective shows that
decisions about product, price, place, and promotion are influenced by major structural
forces—technological changes, socioeconomic and geopolitical transformations, and
environmental challenges—that force us to rethink who participates in marketing decisions,
what instruments are considered, how they are implemented, and where they are deployed
(Purnomo, 2023). There is greater integration of instruments, customization of actions, and
recognition of the specificities of emerging markets, which has direct implications for
strategies for introducing innovative products in diverse contexts (Malshe et al., 2021).
Despite the growing number of studies and reviews on marketing innovation, market
innovation, new product development, and the commercialization of disruptive innovations,
the evidence remains fragmented and scattered across different disciplines, sectors, and
methodological approaches (Bohórquez et al., 2023; Sanbella et al., 2024). Gaps have been
identified, for example, in relation to the articulation between the different dimensions of the
marketing mix, the role of organizational and relational capabilities, and the interaction
between digital and face-to-face strategies in the early stages of introduction (Sutaguna et
al., 2023). This justifies the need for literature reviews that synthesize findings and offer
integrative frameworks specifically geared toward marketing strategies for introducing
innovative products to the market (Ali et al., 2024).
In this context, the objective of this article is to systematically and critically analyze
recent literature on marketing strategies used to introduce innovative products to the market,
identifying approaches, marketing mix components, org anizational capabilities, and
relational practices that are associated with greater acceptance, adoption, and performance
of such innovations.

METHODOLOGY
This study constitutes a systematic -narrative literature review, aimed at identifying,
analyzing, and synthesizing the main marketing strategies employed for the introduction of
innovative products into the market. A systematic procedure for searching and selecting
studies was combined with a narrative and interpretive analysis of the findings. This
approach enabled the integration of recent and diverse evidence while maintaining a
rigorous and transparent focus throughout the review process.
Academic databases with broad coverage and international recognition were
consulted, including: Scopus, Web of Science, ScienceDirect, EBSCOhost, and ProQuest.
Additionally, Google Scholar was used to locate potentially relevant supplementary articles
and to track citations of key works.
The search strategy combined terms in English and Spanish, incorporating Boolean
operators (AND, OR, NOT) to refine the results. For illustrative purposes, search strings
such as the following were employed:

To ensure the relevance and quality of the compiled evidence, clearly defined
eligibility criteria were established in advance.

Inclusion Criteria
 Empirical research articles (qualitative, quantitative, or mixed -methods), literature
reviews, meta-analyses, and case studies.
 Published in peer-reviewed scientific journals.
 Explicit focus on marketing strategies associated with the introduction, launch,
commercialization, or diffusion of innovative products (technological, consumer,
industrial/B2B, or service-based).
 Published between 2020 and 2025.
 Available in full-text digital format.
 Written in English or Spanish.

Exclusion Criteria
 Studies that address innovation solely from a technical or R&D perspective (e.g.,
prototype design, engineering developments, patents) without clearly linking it to
marketing strategies or decisions.
  Books, book chapters, theses, technical reports, working papers, and non -peer-
reviewed conference proceedings, unless identified as seminal works clearly
referenced in multiple articles (a scenario that did not require inclusion in this review).
 Duplicate articles across different databases.
 Studies for which the full text was not available or presented significant access issues.
  Research focused exclusively on non-innovative products or low-impact incremental
improvements, lacking a clear component of novelty or disruption relative to the
existing market offering.

In the initial phase, all records retrieved from the searches in each database were
downloaded, including title, authors, publication year, abstract, and journal details.
Subsequently, an automated and manual deduplication process was carried out to ensure
each study appeared only once in the initial dataset.
In the second phase, a preliminary scr eening based on titles and abstracts was
conducted. At this stage, the inclusion and exclusion criteria were strictly applied, discarding
those papers whose topic or approach did not have a direct connection to marketing
strategies for launching innovative  products. Potentially relevant articles were retained for
more detailed evaluation.
In the third phase, a full-text review of the shortlisted studies was performed. During
this stage, the comprehensive fulfillment of the eligibility criteria was verified. The relevance
of the objectives and conceptual framework to the research question was assessed, along

The data analysis was conducted using a thematic content analysis approach. In the
first stage, categories and subcategories related to the marketing strategies used for
introducing innovative products were identified through a detailed reading of each article and
the extraction matrix. These categories emerged from both the explicit theoretical
frameworks in the studies and the empirically described practices and tactics.
Subsequently, the findings were grouped into broader thematic dimensions, such as:
consumer-focused and user experience strategies, digital and content-based strategies, risk
and uncertainty management strategies for launch, pricing strategies for innovations, and
collaboration and innovation ecosystem strategies, among others. This organization allowed
for the comparison of approaches across different contexts, sectors, and product types.
Finally, an integrative narrative synthesis was developed, articulating the results of
the reviewed studies and fostering a  dialogue between different theoretical and empirical
perspectives. To facilitate understanding and comparison between research studies, the
information was supported by summary tables and comparative descriptions, highlighting
convergencies, divergences, and gaps in the literature. This approach not only enabled the
description of the most recurrent marketing strategies but also identified areas where
evidence is still limited and future research is required.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
After applying the systematic search strategy described in the methodology, an initial
total of 47 potentially relevant studies were identified. The screening phase was carried out
through the rigorous application of predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria that prioritized
empirical research, reviews, and case studies from the last five years, explicitly focused on
marketing strategies for innovative products. This process allowed the sample to be refined,
excluding 27 studies that did not meet the established requirement s, mainly because they
did not address the strategic marketing component, were outside the defined time period, or
corresponded to ineligible document types. Consequently, the critical synthesis and analysis
of this work are based on a final corpus of 20 scientific articles, which constitute the basis of
the evidence presented in the following sections.
Table 1 summarizes the 20 articles selected based on criteria of topicality, thematic
relevance, and academic quality, considering studies published between 2020 and 2025 in
high-impact indexed journals. These works address, from different perspectives, marketing
strategies linked to the introduction of products and innovations in the market, including both
approaches focused on the marketing mix and customer orientation, as well as approaches
linked to digitization, artificial intelligence, and digital platforms.

Marketing mix strategies for innovative products
The introduction of innovative products requires creatively redesigning the 4Ps of
marketing. The l iterature on innovation marketing shows that many successes can be
explained by significant changes in design and packaging, distribution channels, types of
communication, and pricing schemes, even when the product technology does not change
substantially (Grimpe et al., 2017; Purchase & Volery, 2020; Scafuto & Nassif, 2025). Table
2 shows how companies that innovate simultaneously in product, price, place, and
promotion achieve better new product performance than those that only modify a single
variable (Grimpe et al., 2017; Scafuto & Nassif, 2025). This integrated view of the mix is
consistent with the logic that marketing innovation is itself a source of sustainable
competitive advantage (Muhamedjanova & Ivanovich, 2020; Purchase & Volery, 2020).
Product design and presentation take on a central role when it comes to innovations
perceived as risky by consumers. Changes in packaging, interactivity (e.g., QR codes), and
elements of sustainability or authenticity help reduce uncertainty and build trust around the
new product (Larina, 2017; Muhamedjanova & Ivanovich, 2020). Companies that
incorporate visible attributes of innovation in their design achieve greater brand recall and
initial trial rates (Table 1). This is particularly relevant in mass consumer ma rkets, such as
innovative foods or fast -moving consumer goods, where decisions are quick and highly
influenced by visual and symbolic factors (Larina, 2017). Consistency between design,
positioning, and value proposition becomes a key indicator.
The pricin g policy for the launch of innovative products combines profitability
objectives with penetration strategies and quality signaling. Recent studies show the
growing use of schemes such as dynamic pricing, flat rates, packages, and sign-up bonuses
to reduce barriers to adoption while maintaining acceptable margins (Grimpe et al., 2017;
Purchase & Volery, 2020). The most successful launches combine an attractive entry price
with elements of perceived added value, rather than competing solely on low cost (Table 2).
In regulated or high-tech sectors, price also communicates sophistication and reliability, so
excessive discounting can be counterproductive (Lee & O’Connor, 2023; Schiavone &
Simoni, 2019). Calibrating the price according to the degree of novelty of the product is
decisive.
Distribution channels for innovative products tend to diversify toward direct and digital
formats, reducing intermediaries and allowing greater control over the introduction
experience (Purchase & Volery, 2020; Yatsenko, 2025). Evidence indicates that the
combination of e -commerce, marketplaces, and selective partners facilitates market
education and early feedback collection (Schiavone & Simoni, 2019; Yatsenko, 2025). In
regulated industrial markets, the creation of specific market access units and alliances with
key players (scientific societies, patient associations) is critical to overcoming institutional
barriers (Schiavone & Simoni, 2019).
In terms of communication, studies highlight the role of integrated strategies that
combine advertising, public relations, educational content, and social media presence
(Akbar & Sugianingsih, 2024; Hadzhi et al., 2024). For highly innovative products, customer

Market entry strategies and launch models
Market entry strategies for innovative products range from traditional sequential
approaches to agile, user -centered models. Recent literature cl assifies launch models as
waterfall, phased launches, agile approaches, user -centered design, crowdfunding, pre -
sales, and “big bang” launches, among others (Zozulov, 2025). These models differ in
speed, risk, degree of customer involvement, and payment st ructure. Table 3 shows how
companies select the entry model based on technological complexity, market maturity, and
available resources, reinforcing the idea that there is no universally optimal model
(Tokhtamysh & Sheptukha, 2024).
Iterative models, such as Agile and Design Thinking, have established themselves
as particularly well suited to contexts of high uncertainty and dynamic markets. By
incorporating user feedback and real-time data early on, they allow the value proposition to
be adjusted before a mass rollout (Tokhtamysh & Sheptukha, 2024; Zozulov, 2025). These
models show lower failure rates and greater product -market fit in incremental or digital
innovations. However, its implementation requires sophisticated organizational capabilities
and a cu lture of experimentation, which limits its adoption in more traditional or highly
regulated companies (Schiavone & Simoni, 2019; Tokhtamysh & Sheptukha, 2024).
In contrast, “big bang” launches and frontal attack strategies continue to be used in
revolutionary technological innovations or when seeking a strong competitive impact.

launches, beta programs, targeted campaigns) with these segments achieve more stable
growth trajectories (Debruyne et al., 2022; Tokhtamysh & Sheptukha, 2024).
There is also a growing trend toward nic he-oriented strategies for innovative
products, which reduce the likelihood of direct competitive reaction. An analysis of more
than 500 industrial launches shows that competitors respond less to radically new products
or those that employ a niche strategy, while they react more frequently (especially in terms
of price) to clear attacks in existing categories (Debruyne et al., 2022). Table 3 shows this
difference, highlighting that the choice of target segment and initial positioning modulate not
only customer adoption but also the intensity of competition (Beard & Easingwood, 2019;
Debruyne et al., 2022).
Finally, the literature on go-to-market model classification emphasizes the need for
hybrid approaches that combine flexibility, data-intensive methods, and active collaboration
with the customer (Krasnov, 2023). In practice, this translates into strategies that begin with
niche testing, use digital channels for rapid learning, and, once the concept is validated,
scale through partnerships and broader campaigns (Yatsenko, 2025). Successful launches
integrate multiple models (e.g., online pre-sales followed by phased rollout), demonstrating
that launch architecture sophistication is a critical success factor for innovative products
(Yatsenko, 2025; Nazara, 2025; Krasnov, 2023).

Market Communication and Education for Highly Innovative Products
Communication plays a strategic role in the commercialization of highly innovative

The pre -announcement strateg y has been identified as a key component in the
launch of major innovations. Empirical studies distinguish between messages oriented
toward customer education, anticipation building, and market pre-emption (Krasnov, 2023).
Educational pre-announcements are particularly effective when the degree of technological
novelty is high, as they help reduce uncertainty and facilitate understanding of product use.
In contrast, pre-announcements focused on generating expectations or blocking competition
may be more sui table for incremental innovations or highly competitive markets, where
reaction speed is crucial (Yatsenko, 2025; Nazara, 2025; Krasnov, 2023).
The type of advertising appeal (emotional vs. functional) is also intricately related to
the level of innovation . Evidence suggests that for highly innovative and technologically
complex products, functional appeals that detail attributes, concrete benefits, and modes of
use tend to improve new product performance (Yatsenko, 2025). Predominantly emotional
campaigns work better for innovations with lower perceived risk, where the main challenge
is to differentiate in saturated categories rather than explaining entirely new concepts
(Nazara, 2025; Krasnov, 2023). This distinction forces companies to segment their
messaging according to the target audience and the type of innovation.
In regulated and industrial markets, communication extends beyond the end-user to
include multiple stakeholders: regulators, institutional decision -makers, professional
associations, and, in some cases, patient organizations (Schiavone & Simoni, 2019). Case
studies in healthcare show that innovative companies resort to educational activities with
highly reputable partners, simulations of the innovation's systemic impact, and specific
market a ccess units (Schiavone & Simoni, 2019; Nazara, 2025; Krasnov, 2023). These
multi-stakeholder communication strategies improve the odds of institutional adoption,
despite regulatory constraints. This contrasts with consumer markets, where communication
is m ore oriented toward branding and community building (Schiavone & Simoni, 2019;
Nazara, 2025; Krasnov, 2023).
Digital communication and social media have taken on a central role in the
commercialization of innovations, especially for startups and SMEs. Rece nt cases of
technology and service companies show that the combined use of crowdsourcing, pitching,
networking, and social media increases sales and customer engagement during the
commercialization process (Schiavone & Simoni, 2019; Nazara, 2025; Krasnov, 2023).
Companies that integrate digital channels with more traditional communications achieve
greater reach and better feedback, although they face challenges such as credibility, patent
management, and resource scarcity (Nazara, 2025; Krasnov, 2023). The design of
omnichannel communication strategies thus becomes a requirement for success.
Finally, the relationship between communication intensity and competitive reaction
warrants attention. Research with large samples of industrial launches shows that
competitors are more likely to react (especially with price adjustments) when the new
product is supported by extensive communication (Lee & O’Connor, 2023). Table 4
illustrates this correlation, suggesting the need to balance the desire for visibility with
strategic considerations regarding market response. In summary, communication in the
launch of innovative products should be understood as a system that articulates education,
brand building, expectation management, and anticipation of competitive reactions (Sary et
al., 2024).

Customer focus, co-creation, and performance of innovative products
The market performance of innovative products increasingly depends on companies'
ability to integrate the customer into the innovation and commercialization process. Recent
systematic reviews highlight the trend towards co-creation, service-dominant logic, and user
communities as key sources of marketing innovation (Sary et al., 2024). Table 5 shows that
companies with greater customer orientation, measured by their ability to identify and satisfy
current and future needs, achieve better new product outcome s (Larina, 2017). This
orientation translates into practices such as continuous market research, advanced
segmentation, and the development of customized value propositions.
Co-creation with customers and early adopters helps reduce uncertainty and
improve product-market fit. In sectors such as innovative foods, IT, and digital products,
consumer participation in concept testing, beta versions, or online communities has been
shown to improve acceptance and accelerate diffusion (Larina, 2017; Sary et al., 20 24).
Companies that intensively use these mechanisms exhibit lower launch failure rates and
higher levels of customer satisfaction. However, this openness also entails managing
expectations, intellectual property, and the potential divergence between the preferences of
advanced users and the mass market (Sary et al., 2024).
Another relevant dimension is product life -cycle management based on market
response. Studies on innovation strategies in industrial and consumer goods indicate that
firms must adjust their marketing mix, product development, and portfolio decisions as the
product moves through the stages of introduction, growth, maturity, and decline (Larina,
2017; Sary et al., 2024). Companies that systematically monitor indicators of efficiency,
sales, and satisfaction at each stage are able to extend the lifespan of their innovations
through incremental improvements, product variations, and diversification strategies (Polous
et al., 2025).
In the context of SMEs, educational institutions, and local mar kets, the literature
emphasizes the importance of relatively simple but well -executed marketing strategies.
Studies of innovative products from polytechnic institutes and local SMEs show that
marketing activities concentrate on promotion via social network s and websites, with less
emphasis on formal market research or strategic alliances (Larina, 2017; Sary et al., 2024).
Although these basic digital strategies increase visibility, their impact on performance is
limited if not complemented by market research, competitive pricing, channel selection, and
strong brand building (Larina, 2017).
The integration of sustainability and cultural differentiation into marketing strategy
also appears as an emerging factor. In the case of local products seeking to compete

CONCLUSIONES
The results show that the successful introduction of innovative products does not
depend on a single tactic, but on the coherent integration of several marketing strategies :
precise segmentation, a clear value proposition, communication focused on the benefits of
the innovation, and an appropriate selection of channels. When these dimensions are
managed in an integrated way, the likelihood of early adoption and progressive diffusion of
the product in the target market increases significantly.
The reviewed literature and the study's findings agree that the main obstacle to the
adoption of innovative products is the perceived risk by consumers. Companies that actively
incorporate mechanisms to reduce this risk—such as free trials, warranties, demonstrations,
and educational communication—succeed in accelerating the adoption curve. Likewise, a
customer orientation based on researching needs, expectations, and cognitive barriers
allows for the design of more persuasive messages and user experiences.
The most effective marketing strategies are not limited to the initial launch, but are
sustained over time by building trust-based relationships with distributors, early users, and
opinion leaders. The co-creation of value through continuous feedback, product adaptation,
and customer involvement in the innovation process reinforces loyalty, generates positive
