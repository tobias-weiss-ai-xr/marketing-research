ABSTRACT
This paper presents a bibliometric analysis of Artificial Intelligence in Marketing (AIM), summarizing research
patterns, thematic trends, and collaboration networks in this rapidly evolving field. Drawing on 1,128 Scopus-
indexed documents published between 1992 and 2024, the study examines publication growth, journal impact,
key-word co-occurrences, geographic distributions, institutional affiliations, and influential authors. The results
highlight a steep increase in AIM research after 2018, mirroring the rise of AI applications in customer segmentation,
personalization, predictive analytics, and strategic decision-making. Despite a wide dispersion of publications, a
core group of high-impact outlets, such as Lecture Notes in Networks and Systems and the Journal of Business
Research, contributes a substantial portion of the literature. The analysis also reveals China, India, and the United
States as the top contributors, with leading institutions including Lovely Professional University and Chandigarh
University. A keyword-based clustering points to five major themes, underscoring the multifaceted scope of AIM,
spanning digital marketing, data mining, big data, and social media. Based on a content review, 10 research
opportunities are identified, covering areas such as ethical governance, hyper-personalization, and
collaborative intelligence. The study concludes by emphasizing the necessity of balancing technological
advancements with ethical considerations to promote sustainable development in AI-driven marketing.

1. INTRODUCTION
Since its origin at the Second Dartmouth Conference
in 1956, artificial intelligence (AI) has evolved from
a theoretical concept into a foundational driver
of technological transformation in fields including
marketing, consumer behavior, and psychology
(Mariani et al., 2021). Advances in big data, machine
learning, and computational power have facilitated
the transformation of marketing decision-making by
supporting real-time personalized marketing through
tools including recommendation systems, automated
service bots, and content generation in fields such
as journalism and music production (Marshall, 2018;
Belanche et al., 2020). Yet, academic research on
Artificial Intelligence in Marketing (AIM) remains
fragmented. However, the studies either take a narrow
view on the core processes, such as algorithmic
processes, consumer behavior, or technological
implementation, and thus do not have an integrated
framework to interconnect these dimensions (Huang
& Rust, 2017; Davenport et al., 2020). This fragmented
approach limits theory building and its application in
marketing contexts.
While previous bibliometric studies (Bansal et al., 2024;
Mustak et al., 2021; Vla čić et al., 2021) have offered
functional insights, they are also fairly limited in what
they can look at due to either small datasets or search
strategies that are not able to capture more recent
developments (such as generative AI). However, many
of these past studies are informative, yet do not integrate
emerging developments in AI as comprehensively,
nor do they dive deeply enough into these topics
methodologically. The present study provides a
comprehensive bibliometric analysis of 32 years of AIM
research to address these research gaps. Following
methods such as co-citation and keyword analysis, the

2. LITERATURE REVIEW
2.1 Artificial Intelligence in Marketing (AIM)
Artificial intelligence (AI) has evolved from its early
conceptualization as “intelligent agents”, systems
capable of perceiving and interacting with their
environment (Poole & Mackworth, 2010), into a
transformative force in marketing. From machine
learning, deep learning, big data analytics, and
automation (Wang et al., 2025) to LLMs and generative
AI exemplified by tools such as ChatGPT (Grewal et
al., 2025), core AI components have expanded their
applications and their impact on marketing practice.
Traditionally, the insights derived from qualitative
research with small, limited datasets marked the basis
for traditional marketing. However, with the advent of
AI-driven tools, marketing increasingly integrates data-
intensive tasks such as the prediction of future consumer
behavior, market segmentation, and personalized
customer experiences (Huang & Rust, 2017). These
include predictive modeling, dynamic pricing, product
recommendation engines, and campaign optimization
(Dzyabura & Huser, 2019; Davenport et al., 2020; Verma
et al., 2021). It has also redefined customer engagement
with technologies such as chatbots, virtual assistants,
and intelligent social media algorithms that have
customized the content delivery and automated routine
interactions, leading to better efficiency and user
experience (Aulia et al., 2021; Prentice & Nguyen, 2021;
Ngai et al., 2021). Moreover, immersive technologies
like augmented reality (AR) and virtual reality (VR) are
facilitating innovative AI-driven brand engagements
in virtual spaces (Arya et al., 2024; Enyejo et al., 2024).
Although these advances have been made in AIM, the
literature still falls far short. Research typically breaks
AI down into algorithmic, behavioral, or managerial
facets without considering how AI affects marketing
ecosystems (Huang & Rust, 2017; Davenport et al.,
2020). This lack of integration hinders both practical
application and theoretical development.
Furthermore, there are geographic disparities that
make the landscape even more complicated. However,
despite the strong infrastructure and investment power,
as well as their lead in this topic of study, leading
countries like China, the U.S., and India face ethical
concerns, regulatory challenges, and resource gaps
that hamper innovative adoption in emerging markets
(Singal, 2025). This digital divide is bridged by taking into
account local cultural, regulatory, and socioeconomic
contexts (Dwivedi et al, 2021). To advance the field, future
AIM research requires a cross-disciplinary approach
that synergizes technical innovation with ethical
factors such as fairness, transparency, trust, and data
privacy (Adanyin, 2024). Considering how important
such efforts will be for the business performance of AI-
driven marketing strategies, it is also essential that these
strategies contribute to societal well-being.
2.2 Bibliometric Analysis of AIM Research
A bibliometric analysis, a systematized and objective
investigation, is presented of the large amount of
Artificial Intelligence in Marketing (AIM) research.
Bibliometrics, as Pritchard (1969) defined it, is the use of
statistical and mathematical methods to bibliographic
records to evaluate scientific output quantitatively.
Compared to traditional literature reviews, which may
be subjectively or locally scoped, the use of bibliometric
methods involves the application of computational
tools in mapping the knowledge structures, discovering
influential authors and institutions, as well as tracing
scholarly developments across areas and regions
(Osareh, 1996; Okubo, 1997; Zupic & Čater, 2015).
The main bibliometric techniques used are co-citation
analysis, bibliographic coupling, co-authorship
analysis, and co-keyword analysis (Donthu et al., 2021).
This study emphasizes co-keyword analysis, which
effectively uncovers conceptual linkages, foundational
theories, and thematic trends in AIM. Furthermore,
the results guide scholars toward seminal research
while highlighting underexplored areas that merit
future investigation. Thakur and Kushwaha (2023) have
identified emerging clusters in AIM scholarship, such as
generative AI, data mining, deep learning, AI-enabled
commerce, and chatbot technologies. Trending topics
like natural language processing (NLP), Internet of Things
(IoT), and robotic services suggest that the research
landscape is broadening and dynamic. Academic and
industry interest has been escalating, as evidenced by
the exponential growth in AIM publications between
1990 and 2022.
Furthermore, AI’s strategic value in consumer behavior

3. MATERIALS AND METHODS
3.1 Methods
The aim of this study is to conduct a systematic,
bibliometric analysis of scholarly contributions to
Artificial Intelligence in Marketing (AIM). Bibliometric
analysis is represented as a rigorous and data-driven
method to provide transparency and objectivity through
quantification of publication trends, identification of
influential authors and institutions, and mapping out
of geographic distributions, emerging themes, and
collaborative networks (Donthu et al., 2021; Hu et
al., 2023). It is used widely across disciplines such as
marketing, management, and the social sciences, and
has been effective as a tool to uncover the intellectual
structure of research fields (Ishak et al., 2023; Thakur
& Kushwaha, 2023). Bibliometric methods have been
employed by recent studies to examine AIM’s thematic
evolution and scholarly impact. For example, Thakur and
Kushwaha (2023) found some major research clusters,
for example, generative AI, data mining, robo-advisors,
and AI-enabled commerce, with the key contributors
and patterns of citation. This foundation is then used to
build upon in the present study, with the provision of a
comprehensive bibliometric mapping of the intellectual
and thematic development of AIM.
3.2 Data Collection
Scopus was selected as the primary data source
due to its extensive journal coverage, standardized
bibliographic metadata, and established history in
bibliometric research (Alam et al., 2023; Hu et al.,
2023). In terms of data quality, Scopus is generally
more consistent in quality of data than the alternatives
of Google Scholar and Web of Science (Burnham,
2006; Ahmi et al., 2019; Elsevier, 2023), and is usually
the best bet for conducting robust analyses and to
be able to reproduce the analyses. Data collection
proceeded as per a modified PRISMA protocol (Moher
et al., 2009) for a systematic, transparent review (see
Figure 1). This framework enhances reproducibility and
reduces selection bias through four structured phases:
Identification (database search via Scopus), Screening
(filtering based on document type, language, and
search field), Eligibility (evaluation of topical relevance
based on titles and abstracts), and Inclusion (final
selection of documents for bibliometric analysis).
Records were extracted on December 10, 2024, using
the keywords “marketing” and “artificial intelligence”
in document titles. Adaptive keyword strategies were
based on bibliometric studies using keywords in previous
work (Mustak et al., 2021; Anayat & Rasool, 2024; Bansal
et al., 2024). The inclusion criteria were restricted to
peer-reviewed journal articles, books, and conference
papers published in English. A process on this yielded
1,139 documents totaling a pretty good, robust, and
relevant dataset for analysis.
3.3 Data Screening and Cleaning
Although Scopus is a curated and structured database,
duplicates may still arise due to metadata inconsistencies
such as variations in author names (e.g., initials versus
full names), institutional affiliations (e.g., “University
Utara Malaysia” vs “UUM”), or multiple representations
of the same publication across early-access and
final formats. Ahmi (2023a) suggests discrepancies in
naming conventions and spelling variances, resulting
in redundant entries that need to be harmonized
before bibliometric analysis. To resolve this, we
employed careful data cleaning via OpenRefine and
biblioMagika to tackle the issues of standardizing
authors' names, institutional affiliations, and keywords.
This removes analytical accuracy and prevents
distortions by repeating the metadata (Ahmi, 2023a;
Ahmi, 2023b). Non-relevant documents were excluded
through a structured review of titles and abstracts.
Only publications focusing specifically on artificial
intelligence applications in marketing were included.
Studies were excluded if they focused on other fields
such as AI in logistics, healthcare, or manufacturing,
discussed marketing only tangentially, or lacked
substantive methodological content. These steps also
resolved issues related to spelling, formatting, and
terminology that could affect the analysis. Following
these procedures, 1,128 documents remained in the
final dataset for analysis.

4. DATA ANALYSIS
4.1 Subsection Publications by Year
Concerning the index, the first article identified in
Scopus was written in 1992. It appeared in the Journal
of the Japan Research Association for Textile End-Uses
under the title “Marketing Analysis on Apparel Based
on Neural Network” by Watada et al. (1992). The paper
introduced a neural network-based clustering method
for analyzing consumer behavior in apparel. It modeled
consumer preferences and demonstrated that the
network could match the decision-making abilities of
fashion experts. Regular publications on AIM began in
2013, with 15 articles published that year. The number of
publications grew steadily, particularly after 2018 (see
Figure 2). Between 1992 and 2012, the annual number of
articles mostly remained below 10, with no publications
in 1994. Eight articles were published in 1998, followed by
minor increases in 2009 (12 articles) and 2011 (11 articles).
The publication rate surged after 2018, reaching 19
articles that year, and 48 in 2019. It continued to climb
in 2020 (67 articles), 2021 (97), 2022 (171), and 2023 (228),
peaking at 329 articles in 2024.
4.2 Publications by Journal
The dataset comprised 1,128 articles published across
639 distinct journals and conferences. Among these,
73.2% of the journals and conferences published only
one article on AIM. This suggests that most of them are
not specialized in this field. Another 13.3% (85 journals
and conferences) published two articles, while 4.9% (31
journals and conferences) published three. Only 1.9%
(12 journals and conferences) published four articles. As
shown in Table 1, we examined journals that published
10 or more articles related to the topic of AIM. This group
includes 11 journals that published a total of 152 articles,
representing 23.8% of the sample. Lecture Notes in
Networks and Systems leads with 25 articles, accounting
for 2.2% of the total. The ACM International Conference
Proceedings Series follows with 20 articles (1.8%). Smart
Innovation, Systems, and Technologies and the Journal
of Business Research both published 14 articles, each

However, despite AI being a globally trending topic,
citation counts for many recent AIM publications
(post-2022) remain relatively modest. This discrepancy
can be explained by several factors. Firstly, recent
articles are subject to a "citation lag effect," wherein
it typically takes one to two years post-publication for
papers to accumulate significant citations due to the
inherent delays in academic publishing cycles and
database indexing (Ismagiloiva et al., 2020; Donthu
et al., 2021). Secondly, as AIM research has grown
rapidly, citing articles have spread to many studies
with correspondingly lower average citations per
article. Fourth, the recent literature on AIMs presents
final thematic convergence and methodological
homogeneity that may have discouraged scholars' swift
visibility and understanding of their contribution, which
may have resulted in fewer citations.
Knowledge of these citation dynamics is thus key for
researchers wishing to navigate the literature as they
seek out influential works that serve as critical theoretical
benchmarks and practical breakthroughs. Recognizing
citation patterns also aids in identifying new research
gaps, emphasizing the originality and methodological
innovation in future AIM studies. Understanding these
citation dynamics is essential for researchers to navigate
the literature effectively, highlighting influential works
that represent critical theoretical milestones and
practical advancements. Additionally, recognizing
citation patterns helps identify emerging research gaps
and emphasizes the importance of originality and
methodological innovation in future AIM studies.

5. DISCUSSION
This study used a bibliometric approach to analyze the
knowledge regarding Artificial Intelligence in Marketing
(AIM). The bibliometric analysis demonstrates how this
method can systematically uncover key themes, major
publication outlets, and research chronology in this
rapidly evolving field of research (Kushairi & Ahmi, 2021).
5.1 Temporal Evolution and External Catalysts
However, this research shows that the volume of AIM-
related publications was sporadic in the 1990s, but
increased rapidly after 2019. This increase in investment
has occurred based on the widespread adoption
of AI applications, the development of big data
infrastructure, and the increase in corporate interest in
data-driven marketing (Duan et al., 2019).

5.2 Geographic Distribution and International
Collaboration
A total of 93 countries have contributed to AIM research,
with China, India, the US, and the UK making the most
significant contributions. This finding also corresponds
to the conclusion of Thayyib et al. (2023) that AI is
becoming more global. However, China is leading
extensively in patent filings and has strong R&D support
for each filing, alongside major tech companies such
as Baidu. Private AI investments, some of the leading
companies in AI, such as Google and OpenAI, and a
focus on responsibly using AI, ensure that it remains in
the US for the time being. The UK stands out for its top
universities, institutions like DeepMind, and leadership in
AI safety. India shows rapid growth due to its expanding
AI research community, growing investments, and
active public discussions on AI (O’Brien, 2024). These
global connections enable cross-market comparisons
and cross-disciplinary collaborations. However,
cultural differences and regulatory challenges remain,
highlighting the need for frameworks that address ethics
and compliance (Jobin et al., 2019).
5.3 Keyword Insights and Ethical Considerations
Recent keyword analyses confirm that AI is becoming
central to marketing. This growth is driven by machine
learning, big data, and natural language processing.
In digital and social media, algorithms enable precise
customer segmentation, personalization, and predictive
capabilities (Khneyzer et al., 2024). E-commerce
platforms increasingly use neural networks and
fuzzy logic for real-time product recommendations
(Karthik & Ganapathy, 2021). At the same time,
ethical concerns have gained importance. Issues
like algorithmic bias, data privacy, and fairness are
now key topics (Dwivedi et al., 2021). Scholars call for
stronger governance to ensure AI tools such as hyper-
personalized pricing and collaborative intelligence do
not harm specific consumer groups. Looking ahead,
addressing bias, exploring artificial empathy, and
fostering interdisciplinary collaborations will be essential.
These steps will help ensure that AI-driven marketing
remains innovative, ethical, and socially responsible.
5.4 Methodological Reflections and Study Limitations
This research relied primarily on Scopus, using title-
based searches to create a focused dataset. This
approach ensured that the studies were closely aligned
with AIM. However, it may not have covered articles
that discuss AI marketing from a broader perspective
and articles that do not contain an exact keyword
match in their titles (Fayad et al., 2023). This indicates
the typical bibliometric analysis trade-off between
specificity and comprehensiveness (Sweileh et al.,
2017). Another limitation is the use of a single database.
While Scopus is known for its wide coverage (Kushairi &
Ahmi, 2021), depending on Scopus alone will not lead
to relevant studies being found. To minimize this risk,
cross-verification with databases like Web of Science,
IEEE Xplore, and Google Scholar can be performed.
Future research should expand the dataset to include
data from other databases, combining several of them,
and search titles, abstracts, and keywords.
5.5 Theoretical and Managerial Implications
Theoretically, this study verifies that AIM is an
interdisciplinary area. It is a combination of the insights
of marketing, information systems, data science, and
psychology. As AI becomes more incorporated into
marketing, traditional theories and ideas, such as
consumer decision-making and segmentation, may
have to be revisited or made sense in the light of new
computational abilities. For practitioners, AI offers many
opportunities. For example, it might improve predictive
analytics, real-time customizations, and targeting
accuracy. These prospects have the potential to
change customer relationship management. However,
to gain from these gains, one needs to pay attention
to data governance, privacy regulations, and the
potential social impact of going too far with automation.
Technology can be viewed with ethical safeguards.
Those organizations that can position themselves at
favorable points in this balance will achieve sustainable
competitive advantage.
6. CONCLUSION
This bibliometric analysis offers a comprehensive
and structured overview of scholarly contributions to
Artificial Intelligence in Marketing (AIM), addressing the
fragmented nature of prior research. By examining 32
years of literature, the study consolidates key research

while uniquely incorporating recent advances in
generative AI and natural language processing to
provide a timely perspective on the evolution of the
field.
The findings show a string of publications related to AIM
since 2019, highlighting the growing importance of AI
in marketing strategy and customer engagement. By
examining geographic distribution, research is worldwide
and increasingly globalized, with contributions
concentrated in China, India, the United States, and
the United Kingdom, as well as increasing amounts of
international collaboration. Such dynamics show that
