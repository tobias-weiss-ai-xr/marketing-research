This study analyzes information asymmetry and fragmentation of
responsibility in vehicle insurance offerings by sales agents and their
implications for consumer protection in Indonesia. The issue arises from
discrepancies between the information provided b y sales agents and the actual
insurance benefits received by consumers, indicating the presence of
information asymmetry  in transactions. This research employs a normative
juridical method combined with a qualitative approach using a socio -legal
design and  embedded case study. The findings reveal that information
asymmetry has become institutionalized within vehicle sales systems
integrating insurance products through financing institutions. Furthermore, the
involvement of multiple parties sales agents, dea lers, financing com panies,
and insurance providers creates fragmentation of responsibility, resulting in
unclear accountability in consumer protection. From a legal perspective, this
condition may constitute a violation of consumer protection obligations, as
well as breach of contract or tort under civil law. This study recommends
strengthening regulatory frameworks, clarifying stakeholder responsibilities,
and establishing effective ethical standards to ensure transparency, fairness,
and accountability.

Keywords: Information Asymmetry, Responsibility Fragmentation, Vehicle
Insurance, Consumer Protection, Sales Agents

1. Introduction
The complexity of derivative products offered to consumers is reflected, among other things, in motor
vehicle insurance products. In practice, the offering of vehicle insurance is not carried out solely by insurance
companies directly, but also through intermediaries such as vehicle salespersons who constitute part of the
broader sales ecosystem. This condition gi ves rise to new dynamics in the legal relationship between business
actors and consumers, particularly with respect to the obligation to provide accurate, clear, and honest
information in every transaction (Sulfitri, 2022).
Nevertheless, practice in the fi eld frequently reveals discrepancies between the information conveyed by
salespersons and the actual insurance product ultimately received by consumers.  According to (Hidayah, 2024)
notes that consumers are often provided with incomplete, and at times misleading, explanations regarding the
benefits, risks, and scope of insurance coverage. This circumstance indicates the existence of information
asymmetry between salespersons, as parties possessing greater knowledge of the product, and consumers, who
occupy a comparatively weaker position. Such asymmetry has the potential to cause losses to consumers and
suggests practices that are inconsistent with the fundamental principles of consumer protection.
From a legal perspective, this condition is relevant to be examined within the framework of consumer
protection law, which affirms the obligation of business actors to provide accurate, clear, and honest information
concerning the goods and/or services being traded. In addition, this issue may also be analyzed on the basis of
the Indonesian Civil Code, particularly in relation to the possibility of breach of contract or tort arising from the
non-fulfillment of promises or from acts that cause harm to another party (Nasution et al., 2025). Accordingly,
the practi ce of offering vehicle insurance through salespersons constitutes not merely a matter of business
ethics, but also one with significant legal implications.
The issue becomes increasingly complex when viewed in light of the legal relationship structure
involving multiple parties, including salespersons, dealers, financing companies, and insurance companies. In
practice, the interrelationships among these parties are often not fully understood by consumers, thereby

demonstrates the fragmentation of liability within the vehicle sales and insurance ecosystem, which in turn may

Furthermore, from the standpoint of profe ssional ethics, the code of ethics applicable to vehicle

The information asymmetry examined in this study is not merely individual in nature, but is
institutionalized within financing-based vehicle sales patterns that integrate insurance products, thereby creating
a systemic fragmentation of responsibility.

2. Methodology
This study employs a mixed normative –qualitative legal research design with a socio -legal approach,
aimed at integratively analyzing information asymmetry and the fragmentation of responsibility in the practice
of vehicle insurance offerings by salesperson s. According to (Djatmiko, 2022), this approach not only examines
legal norms at the level of law in the books, but also explores their implementation in practice (law in action),
thereby enabling the identification of gaps between legal norms, ethics, and  business realities in the field.
Methodologically, this study adopts an embedded case study design, in which a principal case concerning
the practice of vehicle insurance offerings by salespersons is examined in depth as the main unit of analysis,
with su b-units of analysis consisting of the legal relationships among salespersons, dealers, financing
companies, and insurance companies. This design enables the researcher to identify patterns of information
asymmetry as well as the distribution of legal respo nsibility within a complex transaction ecosystem, while at
the same time testing the relevance of the applicable legal norms to such practices.
The approaches employed in this study include the statute approach, conceptual approach, and case
approach. The statute approach is carried out by examining the provisions of the Consumer Protection Law and
the Indonesian Civil Code, particularly those relating to the obligations of business actors, breach of contract,
and unlawful acts. The conceptual approach is u sed to construct an analytical framework based on information
asymmetry theory, liability theory, and professional ethics in business practice. Meanwhile, the case approach is
used to examine empirical facts as a reflection of the implementation of legal n orms in practice (Pamungkas et
al., 2025).
The data used in this study consist of legal materials and qualitative empirical data. The legal materials
include primary legal materials in the form of statutory regulations, as well as secondary legal materials  in the
form of scientific literature, indexed journals, and expert doctrines (Ahmad et al., 2024). The empirical data,
meanwhile, are obtained through case reconstruction and transaction document analysis, which are used to
identify forms of information a symmetry, patterns of communication by salespersons, and potential legal
violations occurring in the practice of offering vehicle insurance.
Data collection techniques are conducted through library research, document analysis, and case
reconstruction, with  a focus on gathering relevant data to build a comprehensive legal narrative. Furthermore,
the data are analyzed using qualitative legal analysis within the framework of analytical triangulation, namely
by integrating normative analysis (regulations), conc eptual analysis (theories), and empirical analysis (cases) to
produce a holistic understanding (Firmanto et al., 2024). The analytical process is carried out through stages of
categorizing legal issues, constructing relationships among variables (informati on–responsibility–loss), and
drawing normative-critical conclusions.
Through this methodological design, the study not only produces a descriptive analysis of the
phenomenon under examination, but is also able to offer normative criticism and conceptual re construction of
the system of legal responsibility and ethics in the practice of vehicle insurance offerings, thereby contributing
to the strengthening of consumer protection in Indonesia.

3. Result and Discussion
3.1 Information Asymmetry in the Offering of Motor Vehicle Insurance by Salespersons
In the practice of offering motor vehicle insurance, there is an information asymmetry between
salespersons and consumers. Salespersons, as parties who possess a greater understanding of insurance product s,
tend to become the primary source of information, while consumers are placed in a position of dependence on
such explanations. In the case analyzed, consumers received information regarding the benefits and scope of
insurance coverage in general terms, with an emphasis on the aspect of protection, without being accompanied
by adequate explanations concerning the limitations and exclusions of the policy.

This information asymmetry is evident from the discrepancy between the promises made at the time of
the offer and the actual realization of the insurance benefits after the transaction was completed. Initially,
consumers understood that the insurance would provide protection as explained by the salesperson; however, in
practice, it was found that the cover age received did not fully correspond to what had been represented.
According to (Ganie, 2023), this condition indicates that the offering process was not carried out transparently
and reflects an imbalance in the position between business actors and consumers in the transaction.

3.2 Misinformation as a Source of Consumer Loss
The discrepancy between the information provided and the actual insurance product has direct
implications for the losses suffered by consumers. According to (Kriswandaru, 2024), such losses are not only
material in nature, but also include non-material losses in the form of the loss of consumer trust in the protection
system that had been promised. In this context, incomplete or inaccurate information becomes the primary factor
influencing consumers’ decisions in entering into the transaction.
Moreover, it was found that consumers only came to understand the limitations of the motor vehicle
insurance product when filing a claim or when facing certain circumstances not covered by t he policy. This
indicates that information which should have been conveyed at the initial stage was, in fact, not adequately
disclosed. Therefore, misinformation is not merely a matter of communication, but also a factor that directly
triggers consumer loss.

3.3 The Legal Relationship among Salespersons, Dealers, MyBank, and Insurance Companies
The offering of motor vehicle insurance does not involve only salespersons and consumers, but is also
connected to dealers, financing companies such as MyBank, and insurance companies as product providers.
Salespersons act as the parties who communicate information and offer the product, dealers serve as vehicle
providers, MyBank acts as the financing institution that integrates insurance products into the credit sch eme, and
insurance companies function as the parties bearing the insured risk.
However, in practice, there is no clear delineation regarding the limits of responsibility of each party
throughout the overall transaction process. When losses occur due to ina ccurate information, each party tends to
assume a different position in determining its responsibility. According to (Pratama and Viana, 2025), this
condition demonstrates the existence of a complex legal relationship structure that has the potential to cr eate
uncertainty for consumers in determining the party that should be held accountable.

3.4 Information Asymmetry as a Legal Violation from the Perspective of Consumer Protection
According to (Romadh lon, 2019), information asymmetry in the offering of motor vehicle insurance by
salespersons constitutes a violation of the principle of transparency, which forms the basis of consumer
protection. From the perspective of Consumer Protection Law, business actors are obliged to provide accurate,
clear, and honest information regarding the products they offer. When the information conveyed does not reflect
the actual condition of the insurance product, such conduct may be qualified as misleading information that
violates consumer rights.
In this cont ext, information asymmetry is no longer passive in nature, but rather the result of a
communicative construction carried out by salespersons in order to encourage the occurrence of a transaction.
Therefore, such practice cannot merely be regarded as an adm inistrative error, but rather as a form of violation
of the legal obligations of business actors (Heriawan, 2025). This demonstrates that consumer protection does
not depend solely on regulation, but also on how such regulation is implemented in practice.

3.5 Misinformation from the Perspective of Breach of Contract and Tort
From the perspective of the Indonesian Civil Code, the information provided by salespersons at the
offering stage occupies an important position in the formation of agreement between the parties. Such
information becomes the basis upon which consumers make their decisions; therefore, when the information
conveyed does not correspond to the actual realization of insurance benefits, the matter may be categorized as a
breach of co ntract. The discrepancy between promise and performance demonstrates a failure to fulfill the
promised obligation (Marpi & Kom, 2020). This is in line with Article 1239 of the Indonesian Civil Code,
which provides that every obligation to do something or n ot to do something, if not fulfilled, gives rise to the
obligation to compensate costs, losses, and interest.
In addition, in the context of contract formation, inaccurate or incomplete information may also affect the
validity of consent. Article 1320 of t he Indonesian Civil Code requires the consent of the parties as one of the
essential requirements for the validity of an agreement. If such consent arises from misleading information, then
substantively there is a defect of will that may serve as grounds f or annulling the agreement (Chang et al.,
2024). If such misinformation causes loss and is accompanied by an element of fault, then the practice may also
be qualified as a tort. According to Mantili (2019 ), this refers to Article 1365 of the Indonesian Civ il Code,
which provides that every unlawful act causing harm to another obliges the wrongdoer to compensate for such

loss. In this regard, the act of a salesperson in providing incomplete or misleading information may be regarded
as a form of negligence or intentional conduct that harms consumers.
Furthermore, within the framework of consumer protection law, the obligation to provide accurate, clear,
and honest information is also expressly stipulated in Law Number 8 of 1999 concerning Consumer Protection,
particularly Article 7 letter b, which obliges business actors to provide accurate, clear, and honest information
regarding the condition and guarantee of goods and/or services (Indonesia, 1999). A violation of this obligation
may strengthen the legal basis for assessing the existence of both breach of contract and tort.
Thus, the legal relationship in motor vehicle insurance transactions is not merely contractual in nature,
but also encompasses a broader dimension of legal responsibility, both within the f ramework of civil law and
consumer protection law.

3.6  Fragmentation of Responsibility in the Vehicle and Insurance Sales Ecosystem
The involvement of various parties such as salespersons, dealers, MyBank, and insurance companies in
the offering of motor  vehicle insurance creates a fragmentation of responsibility that weakens consumer
protection. The role of financing companies such as MyBank is not limited to that of a transaction facilitator, but
also extends to an entity that indirectly reinforces the distribution of insurance products through integrated
financing schemes, thereby contributing to the formation of information asymmetry. Each party has an
interrelated role, yet this is not followed by a clear allocation of responsibility (Mahardika et al. , 2025). This
condition enables the shifting of responsibility among parties when losses arise, making it difficult for
consumers to determine which party should be held accountable.
This phenomenon demonstrates a weakness in the legal liability system, wh ich has not yet been able to
accommodate the complexity of modern business relationships. In the context of consumer protection, such
condition is contrary to the principles of legal certainty and justice. Therefore, there is a need to strengthen
regulations capable of integrating responsibility among the parties, as well as to establish clearer ethical
standards for vehicle salespersons, so that the practice of offering insurance no longer harms consumers and can
be carried out in a transparent and accountable manner.

4. Conclusion and Suggestion
Based on the research findings, it can be affirmed that the practice of offering vehicle insurance by
salespersons not only demonstrates the existence of information asymmetry, but also reflects a systemic failure
to ensure transparency and accountability in transactions. Such information asymmetry is no longer incidental in
nature, but has become institutionalized within financing -based vehicle sales patterns that integrate insurance
products in a non-transparent manner. From a legal perspective, this condition normatively satisfies the elements
of a violation of the obligations of business actors as stipulated in Law Number 8 of 1999 concerning Consumer
Protection, particularly with regard to the obligation  to provide accurate, clear, and honest information.
Furthermore, within the framework of civil law, such practice may potentially be qualified as both breach of
contract and tort, especially when inaccurate information forms the basis of an agreement that  is detrimental to
consumers. In addition, the involvement of multiple actors within the sales ecosystem, including salespersons,
dealers, financing companies, and insurance companies, has created a structural fragmentation of responsibility,
thereby blurr ing the lines of legal accountability and weakening the position of consumers in claiming their
rights. This condition shows that the problem does not merely lie at the level of business practice, but also
reflects weaknesses in regulatory design in anticipating the complexity of modern legal relationships.
In line with these findings, strategic measures of both a normative and implementative nature are required
to strengthen consumer protection. The government needs to reformulate regulations in a more int egrative
manner in order to ensure a clear allocation of responsibilities among the parties within the vehicle sales and
insurance ecosystem, so that no loopholes exist for the transfer of responsibility to the detriment of consumers.
In addition, there mu st be a clearer affirmation of legal responsibility among the parties, including the possible
application of the principle of joint liability in cases of consumer loss. On the other hand, the formulation and
enforcement of a professional code of ethics for  vehicle salespersons has become an urgent necessity, with
nationally applicable standards supported by effective monitoring and sanction mechanisms in order to prevent
misleading information practices. Efforts to improve consumer literacy must also be car ried out continuously so
that consumers have an adequate understanding of insurance products and financing schemes, while at the same
time being supported by more accessible and responsive complaint -handling and dispute -resolution
mechanisms. Moreover, the  role of supervisory authorities such as the Financial Services Authority must be
strengthened in overseeing the marketing practices of insurance products integrated with financing, particularly
in ensuring transparency of information from the offering sta ge onward. Thus, consumer protection should not
