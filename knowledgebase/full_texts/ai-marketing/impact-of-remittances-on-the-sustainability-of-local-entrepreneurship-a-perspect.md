ABSTRACT
Objective: To examine the relationship between remittance inflows and the development, impact, and
sustainability of local entrepreneurship.
Design/Methodology/Approach: Using a qualitative approach, a structured interview guide titled
“Migration and Remittances” was designed and conducted with five key informants (municipal president,
head of local commerce, migrant, tourist, and resident) directly involved in migration, remittances, and local
entrepreneurship. The responses were analyzed through the identification of frequent terms in the vocabulary
of the five informants.
Results: Through content analysis, recurrent terms were identified, including: the municipality of residence
(Salinas), “businesses,” “remittances,” “migrants,” “increase in sales,” “investment,” “family,” “economy,”
“construction,” and “livelihood.” These terms are directly associated with the local development of
entrepreneurial activities and the subsistence dynamics within the territory.
Study limitations/Implications: A more comprehensive understanding of remittance investment would be
achieved by interviewing persons from the more than 100 localities that make up the territory.
Findings/Conclusions: Migration boosts the local economy through remittance-supported entrepreneurship.
The migrant guarantees family livelihood, acts as an economic agent, and plays a key role in the sustainability
of local enterprises.
Keywords: migration, financing, interviews, local development.

(Herrera-Tapia, 2013). This type of development involves a productive and institutional
transformation within a defined space, aimed at reducing poverty (Schejtman & Berdegué,
2004). This process comprises the different forms of capital within communities, including
social, human, financial, and physical capital (Green & Haines, 2015). It implies a dynamic

enhancement of productive capacities among the various economic agents within the

 A new rurality has redefined the role of rural environments by promoting local economic
diversification beyond traditional agriculture (Ramos-Millán et al., 2024). Consequently,
the specific conditions of the rural spaces where such processes take place must be taken
into account (Cerón-Aparicio, 2015).
 In its study, ¿Qué hay en las localidades rurales de México? (What Exists in Rural Localities
in Mexico?), the National Institute of Statistics and Geography (INEGI, 2024) determined
that a rural area has less than 2,500 inhabitants, while urban areas have a population that
exceeds this threshold.
 Arellano (2013) and other authors classify territorial occupation into four categories: 1)
rural, which includes populations of up to 5,000 inhabitants; 2) mixed (rural-urban), with
up to 50,000 inhabitants; 3) urban, with up to 500,000 inhabitants; and 4) dense urban,
which comprises populations exceeding 500,000 inhabitants.
 Rural and urban populations require the same types of services; however, urban
localities demand them in greater quantity.
 In rural-urban spaces, most inhabitants usually know each other; families live in houses
or apartments, with more open spaces (INEGI, 2020). These spaces are characterized by the
diversity of their economies (Berdegué & Favareto, 2019), including the fundamental tools
needed to meet individual socioeconomic needs, which can promote local development
and microfinance (Valentin-Mballa, 2017). However, people migrate when the lack of
employment opportunities do not allow them the opportunity to sustain a more fulfilling
life project (Eguía Peña & Murua Múgica, 2016). The migration process aims to improve
community welfare (Chávez et al., 2023), and remittances, or money transfers, impact
the local economy. Remittances have a clear effect on consumption and on investments
in new and existing entrepreneurial ventures, which can strengthen income-multiplier
mechanisms, such as employment and productivity (Urciaga-García, 2006).
 According to the Yearbook of Migration and Remittances Mexico (CONAPO-BBVA,
2024), the Mexican migrant population residing in the United States amounted to 12
million. Additionally, based on data from INEGI (2020), migration flows from the state of
San Luis Potosí recorded a total of 25,569 individuals who decided to move abroad, 92%
of whom migrated to the United States.
 The municipality of Salinas recorded the 8th most intense migratory flow in the state of
San Luis Potosí 2020. A total of 7,926 households were recorded as having international
migrants residing in the United States (CONAPO, 2023). This phenomenon is replicated
across different regions of Mexico, resulting in an all-time high in remittance inflows from
migration by 2024 (Torres & de Villa, 2025).
 Migration is a persistent phenomenon in populations of the Altiplano Potosino. It
impacts the inflow of financial resources from abroad (remittances), which have theoretically
contributed to the consolidation of local entrepreneurial ventures or to the strengthening
the sustainability of existing ventures. Therefore, evaluating the relationship between
remittance inflows and the development and impact on the creation and sustainability of

MATERIALS AND METHODS
Study Area
 The study was conducted in the municipality of Salinas de Hidalgo, San Luis Potosí,
Mexico (23° 11ʼ-22° 27ʼ N and 101° 22ʼ-101° 57ʼ W, at 1900 and 2800 m.a.s.l.) (INEGI,
2020). Salinas has a population of 31,107 inhabitants and comprises 122 localities. Its
climate is classified as temperate dry (74.74%) and temperate semi-dry (25.26%) (INEGI,
2010); this distribution belongs to the dry climate (B) group, based on the Köppen climate
classification, adapted for the Mexican Republic by García (2004). Salinas de Hidalgo
has the largest population in the municipality: 18,586 inhabitants (INEGI, 2014). This
locality (and its 20 neighborhoods) was selected as the study area due to its high migratory
intensity.
Type of Study
 A descriptive and mixed-method study was conducted (Hernández Sampieri et al.,
2014) under a quantitative approach that involves data collection to test hypotheses,
analyze data, refine research questions, or reveal new inquiries. Hernández-Sampieri and
Mendoza-Torres (2018) explained that descriptive research seeks to specify the variables
that determine a social phenomenon within a given context, such as migration, remittances,
and local entrepreneurship.
Interview Guide Design
 A structured interview guide with open-ended questions, titled “Migration and
Remittances,” was developed, comprising four dimensions (Table 1). These dimensions
were classified according to the forms of capital available in the territory that drive
development (Green & Haines, 2015) and were subsequently operationalized in order to
methodologically transform the abstract or theoretical constructs of the hypothesis into
concrete, observable, and measurable variables.
Interview
 Five key informants from the locality of Salinas de Hidalgo were interviewed. The
interviewees were identified by residents, based on their perceived involvement and
knowledge of migration, remittances, and local entrepreneurship. They included the
municipal president, who has made efforts to promote entrepreneurial financing; the
director of local commerce, who understands the dynamics of business formalization; an
irregular migrant; a permanent resident; and a tourist who sought a long-term employment
opportunity. The participants were provided an informed consent form, outlining the
confidentiality and conditions of the interview, including the right to withdraw due to
discomfort and the secure handling of the information provided. Informants with direct
links to the migration process, public policy, and local development were included.
Interviews were conducted in person, recorded, and transcribed verbatim (Table 2).

Interview Processing and Analysis
 Each interview was initially transcribed using the Good Tape software (2026). The
completed transcriptions were subject to a standardized text format and the five interviews
were compiled into a single document. Each interview was given a title that clearly
identified it as a distinct interview. After obtaining the coded interview document (Word
format), a uniform format was applied to all interviews, removing the questions and only
keeping the responses. Empty spaces were replaced and only paragraph markers for each
line were kept. This process facilitated readability and resulted in a clean, complete text
(corpus).
 The interviews were analyzed using the text analysis code developed by Schweinberger
(2026) at The Language Technology and Data Analysis Laboratory (LADAL). The
analysis was conducted using the R programming language (version 4.5.1) (2026), within
the RStudio development environment (version 2026.05.0218) (2026). The interview
analysis is detailed below (Figure 1).
 As seen above, the qualitative data obtained from the interviews enabled a quantitative
analysis.
RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Interview Coding and Cleaning
 A corpus of 1,536 words used by the five interviewees was developed. The resident used
482 terms, while both the migrant and the tourist used 212 terms each. The municipal
president used 364 words, while the head of local commerce used 266 terms.
 Key informants used 39% meaningful terms to directly refer to the sustainability of
local entrepreneurial ventures financed by remittances; the remaining 61% terms did not
provide relevant information and were considered stopwords (Table 3).
Corpus cleaning
 Once the corpus was ready, punctuation marks (quotation marks, commas, periods)
were eliminated. Additionally, linking words, prepositions, conjunctions, and adverbs
(stopwords) were eliminated in order to exclude empty words and enable a more in-depth
analysis of the text. Table 4 shows the frequency of words used based on 302 terms.

Concordance Analysis (KWIC: Key Word in Context)
 This analysis enables the identification of keywords within the corpus through the
evaluation of their preceding and following context. This context shows the number of
words to be analyzed, in order to locate and conceptualize the meaning of each line,
according to its surrounding environment (Schweinberger, 2026).
 Key Words
 The terms with the highest concordance were identified in this analysis. The words
selected for analysis were “remittances,” “businesses,” and “migrants.” Key words were
found within a five-word window, before and after the key search term (Table 7).
 In the study locality, remittances are predominantly used for family maintenance,
construction, land investment, and business activities. The key informant from the local
commerce registry pointed out that the highest remittance inflows in the municipality are
received in December, while the municipal president stated that grocery stores (retail trade)
are largely dependent on remittances. Hanusch and Vaaler (2015) found that remittances
are primarily spent on meeting household needs, including food, clothing, education, and
health. However, remittance recipients also use them for house repairs and construction.
Finally, remittances are also commonly used for productive investments in commerce and
for savings (Ochoa Lupián et al., 2018).
 The second term analyzed was “businesses.” Three key informants (the head of local
commerce, the migrant, and the resident) indicated that remittances increase sales and
sustain most ventures, strengthening local entrepreneurship (Table 8). According to García
Zamora (2008), the use of remittances is optimized in the consolidation of entrepreneurial
activities and, at the same time, becomes a factor that helps reduce poverty in these
territories.
 Key informants believe that Salinas is a strong recipient of remittances, pointing out
that households that receive these monetary flows usually spend more. In their turn, the
purchases made by relatives of migrants increase sales in local businesses, contributing
to the sustainability of local enterprises and the growth of the local economy (Table 5).
The findings of Rodríguez-Ramírez and Chávez-Elorza (2019) match the results of this
study. They consider that the impact of remittances on communities of origin is essential,

CONCLUSIONS
 Key informants believe that remittances foster the continuity of local entrepreneurial
ventures. Migration drives local development within the community. Migrants, motivated
by the need to secure better living opportunities, decide to emigrate. In the study area,
migration impacts the local economy, through entrepreneurial activities, which benefit
from remittance inflows. Migrants not only guarantee family livelihood, but also act as
economic agents and key contributors to the sustainability of local enterprises. Furthermore,
they invest their financial resources in construction and trade-related activities. These
dynamics generate employment and, as a whole, strengthen the social fabric, enabling
sustained territorial growth, through the promotion of the local economy.
