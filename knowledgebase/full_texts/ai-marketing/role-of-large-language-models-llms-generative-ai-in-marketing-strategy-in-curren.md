ABSTRACT
This paper introduces the pervasive influence of LLMs and GenAI on modern marketing paradigms, tracing the origins of this technology as a major driver of
change in the marketing landscape by presenting unprecedented opportunities to reshape customer segmentation, targeting and engagement, whereby marketers
are now equipped with tools to read the minds of buyers, and ensuring individual experiences that meet evolved personal expectations and preferences inside a
digital and an omnichannel universe, in the wake of explosions in the natural language processing over the past few years; it emphasizes the unobstructed
pathways towards massive personalization, automation, and efficiency across numerous marketing functions such as AI-based social media churning, blogs and
emails creation, intelligent chatbots and other types of instant customer services, recommendation engines, and predictive analytics solutions for campaigns to
discover the optimal strategies for success, thereby revealing the long-lasting cost-savings, shortened response times, and precision of sending messages that are
percent relevant and ensuring near hundred percent success rates; From a broader perspective, with an emphasis on real-world applications and recent trends
including case studies of top global brands successfully integrating LLMs and GenAI into their marketing strategies to refine customer journeys in a
fundamentally new way, the paper explains how these tools spur creative revolutions inside ad copywriting, video productions, and other forms of interactive
media, and accelerate persuasive creativity by extracting actionable insights from unstructured text narratives, unorganized numbers, and other types of
undocumented feedback to locate opportunities for growth in entire consumer interactions cycles; it illustrates how LLM and GenAI are transforming
conventionalbusinessmodelsbyboostingcustomerengagement, streamliningcontentcreation,andenablingdata-drivendecision-makingsuchthatorganizations
can implement agile marketing, scalable personalization, and fact-based decision-making that leads to an increase in conversion rates, customer loyalty, and
overall brandvalue forautomatic extraction ofactionable insights from human records that are largely inaccurate, unstructured, and prone tohuman bias, posing
a psychological challenge to marketers; It further elaborates the necessity for marketers to adapt Consequently, it is obvious that innovative technologies provide
massive opportunities for marketers to quickly reduce the cost of operations, control responses among target segments, and increase closeness of marketing
communications, however, along with the promise, it reveals the interaction of the technology with human judgment that success from the integration of AI
requires; such as, challenges of compliance with regulatory frameworks such as GDPR, mitigating risks of algorithmic bias, and maintain transparency in AI
communications; and summarize overall recommendations for the successful integration such as, failings to maintain compliance, failure to embrace the novel
technology, advocating for speedy assimilation of rapidly evolving processes in risk-averse company cultures, necessitating an urge for marketers to embrace
some additional emerging creative solutions that deserve furthercaution aswell asresponsible implementation in companies to maintainconsumer confidence in
atrustfulecosystemforsustainablegrowthindigitalmarketenvironments;andconcludingwithastatementthatbusinessesthatactearlyinintegratingLLMsand
GenAI into their marketing ecosystems will emerge as the winners by serving hyper-personalized experiences to the consumers, increasing marketing efficiency
ofoperations,anddevelopinglong-termrelationshipswithbrandsinanincreasinglydata-driven,anddigital-centricglobalmarket.

Discussion related to the study
The implications of LLMs and generative AI on marketing strategy are broad: theoretically, scholars must broaden established paradigms—extending
Resource-Based View (RBV) to assess intangible capabilities such as AI model literacy, prompt engineering, data stewardship; augmenting Service-
Dominant Logic (SDL) to define AI as content co-creator facilitating value-in-use through interactive, adaptive dialogic experiences; enriching
Institutional Theory through examination of norms, regulatory landscapes, and ethical standards shaping firm adoption of generative AI such that
theorymustnowconsiderhowAI-drivencreativityandintelligence(e.g.automatedcontentgeneration,conversationalcommerce)disruptconventional
separations between creator and audience, and how trust, fairness, and transparency mediate value derived from AI outputs (Arce-Urriza, 2025; Li &
Aral, 2025; Kertai, 2025); managerially, trends indicate that to deploy LLMs strategically, firms will need to invest in AI-enabled personalization
systems and creative contentgeneration pipelines capable of producinghigh qualitycontent at-scale (e.g. firms report 40-70%reduction inturnaround
timeusinggenerativedesigntools—BrandTechInsights,2025),ensuringefficiencygains donotdilutebrandvoiceor authenticity,embeddinghuman
oversightandbiasauditing,adoptingbestpracticesinpromptengineering,monitoringmodelperformanceanduserfeedbacktocontinuouslyiterateon
AIoutputs,andbalancinginnovationwithcostoptimization—automatingrepetitivecontentorstandardizedmessaging,andreservinghumancreativity
for high-touch or brand-defining interactions; moreover, from a policy perspective, the rise of generative AI in marketing raises the stakes for robust
frameworks around data privacy, model transparency, ethical deployment, IP/copyright regulation—whereby regulators should for international
alignmentinestablishingclearresponsibilitiesandaccountabilityalongthelinesofthecontroversialdraftEU'sAIAct(2025)mandatingdisclosuresof
AI-generated content, explaining limitations of algorithms, managing algorithmic bias, and creating guidelines for protecting personal data fed into
models under GDPR, CCPA, and the proposed EU AI Act, while enabling innovation by granting safe-harbor provisions or regulatory sandboxes to
enable experimentation with generative AI under supervision—as emerging evidence from regulatory initiatives such as EU AI Act draft (Regulatory
EngagementNetwork,2025),FTC guidance(FTC, 2025),andDigitalMarketsAct(2025)shows thattransparentregulatorysignals promptcompanies
to invest more in ethical governance, while consumer surveys (KPMG, 2024) indicate that consumers are likely to engage more with brands that
disclose how AI is used, how data are secured, and that ensure authenticity in content they produce; together, these implications suggest that the
promiseof sustainable competitive advantage from LLMs andgenerative AI requires synergisticmarketing frameworks thatintegrate capability, trust,
and institutional norms; managerial practices that optimize for personalization, creativity, efficiency, and cost while upholding ethics; and policy
environments that protect consumer interests without stifling innovation, enabling generative AI be leveraged as a strategic differentiator in the post-
cookieerainasustainablemanner.
Conclusion
In conclusion, we submit that this conceptual article advances scholarship in marketing strategy by providing a cohesive framework that positions
LLMs & Generative AI not simply as additional/marginal tools but rather as essential strategic differentiators (delivering AI-powered personalization,
creative generation, enhanced customer experience, effectiveness gains and competitive benefit) while also clarifying the moderating influence of
consumer trust/privacy and the enabling role of ethical governance, and drawing on the RBV, Service-Dominant Logic, Institutional Theory and
PrivacyCalculustoexplainhowfirmswithstronginternaldataassetsandcapabilitiestosenseandshapetheirenvironmentsanda strongcommitment
to governance can translate GenAI adoption into desirable performance outcomes (brand equity, loyalty, CLV and ROI) (Teece, 2018); the
convergenceoffindingsfrom industry(e.g., GenAIadoptionmoved from55%in2023to75%in2024withROIresultsacross industriesaccordingto
IDC) and productivity boosts in marketing functions projected at 5-15% of total marketing spend (McKinsey, 2024; 2025)—illustrate how LLMs are
transitioning from technology experiment to enterprise-wide infrastructure and thus how relevant is this framework for marketers in a post-cookie
world; nevertheless, to move from conceptual clarity to actionable insight requires empirical testing of the framework for example, but not limited to,
across different sectors (for example, retail, financial services, hospitality, B2B), geographies (mature and comparatively emerging markets) and
functionally across types of content and touchpoints, using mixed-methods, longitudinal designs, field experiments and panel data to validate
hypothesized relationships (for example, AI adoption → personalization → performance, moderated by trust/privacy and conditional on ethical
