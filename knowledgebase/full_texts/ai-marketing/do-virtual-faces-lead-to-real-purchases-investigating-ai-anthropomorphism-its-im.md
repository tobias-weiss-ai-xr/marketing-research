Abstract
The research paper focuses on the impact of AI
anthropomorphism on purchase intention, with the mediating
role of marketing personalization, entertainment value and
perceived homophily in the context of AI influencers/models.
Brands around the world are adopting AI marketing tools, in
which AI influencers/models are a relatively newer domain. The
aim of the research is to fill the gap of research done on AI
influencers/models in the Pakistani consumer market,
discussing its practical implications on the fashion industry in
particular. The study involved a sample size of 220 respondents,
Pakistani residents aged 16 to 45 years, who had either seen or
interacted with AI influencers/models before in any media type,
using convenience and snowball sampling technique for data
collection, which was then run through SPSS using Process
Hayes Mediation Model 4. The results of the mediation analysis
represented that the three proposed hypotheses in the study
were accepted. The hypotheses were; marketing
personalization mediates the positive relationship between AI
anthropomorphism and purchase Intention, entertainment
value mediates the positive relationship between AI
anthropomorphism and purchase intention, and lastly,
entertainment value mediates the positive relationship between
AI anthropomorphism and purchase intention. The proven
hypotheses signify the importance of the practical implications
of the research on the Pakistani fashion industry, as big global
brands are moving towards and investing in newer technologies
like AI marketing tools.
Keywords: AI Anthropomorphism, Purchase Intention,
Marketing Personalization, Entertainment Value, Perceived
Homophily, AI Influencers
1. Introduction
In the past decade, social media has evolved into a powerful marketing tool, fundamentally
transforming the way businesses connect with consumers. Consequently, influencer marketing has emerged
as a key strategy for brands (Varsamis, 2018), who shape public sentiment and purchasing decisions through
the content they share with their audiences (Yodel, 2017). AI has been introduced in this domain, emerging
as AI influencers. Zhang and Wei (2021) suggest that AI influencers lead to better control over branding as
they continuously learn and process data of interactions with customers and can be used by brands to enhance
marketing. Parallel to that, AI is used to create human-like models for advertisements. Huang (2025) notes
that AI has the ability to allow the creation of realistic faces that have a high degree of resemblance with
actual human faces, and these faces can often be photorealistic and must be carefully curated to adhere to the
psychology of the consumers, such that they perceive it as authentic and not touch the boundaries of
hyperrealism. In these ways, AI models can be used in advertisements on different media platforms for

The origins of virtual influencers could be traced back to the mid-2010s, with Lil Miquela, a computer-
generated imagery character released in 2016, then celebrities like Shudu, Imma, and Noonouri gained
millions of followers and collaborations with premium labels such as Prada, Calvin Klein, Balmain, and Dior
(Jayasingh et al., 2025). The virtual influencer market was seen to be valued at around USD 4.6 billion in
2024 and is expected to rise indicating global brands' growing faith in AI-generated personalities as marketing
assets (Jayasingh et al., 2025). Anthropomorphism, or the attribution of human-like features, feelings, and
behaviours to non-human entities, is a crucial factor propelling this adoption. It has been found to be a crucial
factor in determining consumer engagement and trust in AI-driven marketing scenarios (Gomes et al., 2025).
The Pakistani market has yet to fully embrace AI influencers and virtual models. A few local fashion brands,
like Saya, Nishat Linen, Meme Retail, Engine, and ZERO, have started experimenting with AI-generated
models in their ads and campaigns indicating the trend's early emergence in Pakistan (Rana et al., 2024).
However, research on consumer responses to AI anthropomorphism of AI influencers/models in
Pakistan is limited, particularly in terms of understanding the psychological mechanisms such as marketing
personalization, entertainment value, and perceived homophily that mediate the relationship between
anthropomorphism and purchase intention. This study therefore situates itself at the crossroads of AI
marketing innovation and an underexplored consumer market, with the goal of contributing both theoretical
depth and practical insight to a fast-expanding field. In the contemporary era, there is an increased focus to
create anthropomorphic qualities in AI-based marketing tools, as AI without anthropomorphism leads to lower
trust (Chi & Hoang Vu, 2023). Personalized recommendations positively lead to increased purchase intention
(Yin & Qiu, 2021). Moreover, since anthropomorphism is related to human likeness, another aspect to
consider is perceived homophily, which strengthens the relationship between anthropomorphism and virtual
influencers' parasocial interactions (Lee et al., 2025). AI influencers/models also have an entertainment value
component attached to them like human influencers (Cao, 2025). So, there is a gap in the contemporary
research to prove how AI anthropomorphism leads to purchase intention via these above-stated variables, that
are technical, social and hedonic in nature respectively, when AI influencers/models are considered. This is
necessary to draw out a significant comparison on how AI anthropomorphism can be effectively utilized by
discussing the three distinct features that the AI influencers/models can be built on. Moreover, there is a gap
in research on AI influencer/models, considering a multi-factor approach, particularly because the concept is
rare in the Pakistani market (Rana et al., 2024). This research aims to target those gaps.
2. Literature Review
Artificial Intelligence has created a revolution in the way a firm interacts with their customer (Beyari
& Hashem, 2025). AI influencers/models are now being used for marketing, which are personas designed and
generated by computers to not just look but also behave like humans on social networking sites, are a
noteworthy innovation (Jayasingh et al., 2025).
2.1. Underpinning Theories
The Uses and Gratification Theory (UGT), developed by Katz, Blumler, and Gurevitch in 1974, would
serve as a major theoretical pillar in this study. With regard to entertainment value, UGT's hedonic pleasure
dimension is immediately applicable. In the context of AI influencer marketing, perceived homophily satisfies
specific social and identity-based demands (Toyib & Paramita, 2024). The theoretical foundation for
marketing personalisation is UGT's cognitive pleasure dimension, which includes the preference for content
that is accurate and personally meaningful to each particular consumer (Katz et al., 1974). Previous UGT
applications in influencer marketing research have primarily focused on human influencers (Lou & Yuan,
2019; Lee & Watkins, 2016). This discovery broadens the scope of UGT by demonstrating that the theory's
explanatory power is not dependent on the biological humanity of the media actor, but rather on the perceived
social and hedonic features of the media stimulus.

Anthropomorphism Theory was first articulated in the consumer behaviour literature by Epley, Waytz,
and Cacioppo (2007). This study introduces an important theoretical refinement, the nonlinear, U-shaped
relationship between AI influencer anthropomorphism and purchase intention, as demonstrated by Pan (2024),
which is compatible with Mori's original uncanny valley theory. The uncanny valley effect predicts that mid-
level anthropomorphism sufficiently realistic to resemble human but falling short of full perceptual
convincingness induces feelings of eeriness, uneasiness, or distrust in observers, suppressing positive
behavioural intentions.
2.2. AI Anthropomorphism and Purchase Intention
Recent literature increasingly suggests a relatively positive and direct relationship between AI
anthropomorphism and purchase intention. When AI influencers depict anthropomorphic characteristics such
as humanlike features, especially visage, personalities, and interactions, consumers react to them in ways
comparable to human endorsers (Chi et al., 2020). Epley et al. (2008) found that anthropomorphism stems
from two motivations: the desire to interact with the unknown environment in a natural and understanding
way, and the desire to form social bonds and relationships. This is especially true when a person lacks real-
life social encounters and consequently relies on a human-non-human relationship.
Empirical studies strengthen this connection. According to various studies anthropomorphic AI
influencers improve perceived warmth and credibility, which boosts consumers' readiness to purchase. For
instance, a study by Gerlich & Michael (2023) suggests that AI influencers with human-like ways of
interacting and personalities are found to considerably influence consumers' sentiments regarding marketed
products, hence increasing buying intention. In the fashion industry in particular, realistic visuals and human-
like aesthetics are particularly important. A study by Wang et al. (2025) states that consumers have a higher
probability to buy clothing recommended by AI influencers who are in accordance with the cultural beauty
standards and human aesthetics of the society. However, Pan (2024) presented a contrasting study that
demonstrated that the relationship between anthropomorphism and purchase intention frequently follows a U-
shaped pattern, with either low (clearly animated) or high (highly realistic) anthropomorphism being effective,
while mid-level anthropomorphism can elicit the uncanny valley effect and reduce purchase intention.
Overall, the research backs up the claim that AI anthropomorphism directly increases buying intention,
making it a crucial precondition for influencer marketing techniques powered by AI.
2.3. AI Anthropomorphism and Purchase Intention: A Mediated Process by Marketing Personalization
AI influencers/models anthropomorphism plays a significant role to ensure the purchase intention of
consumers. Previous research reveals a U-Shape trend for the relationship between AI influencers/models
anthropomorphism and purchase intention, i.e., the AI influencers/models should either be animated (low
anthropomorphism) or very realistic (high anthropomorphism), but never have realistic imagery with bad
graphics as it reduces purchase intention. So, if the right amount of anthropomorphism is chosen, the purchase
intention of consumers would be higher (Pan, 2024). Moreover, consumers willingly pay higher prices when
AI tools are more anthropomorphic (Sidlauskiene et al., 2023).
Recent research shows that consumers perceive personalization positively when they come in contact
with AI-based technology, and those AI tools have more anthropomorphic features integrated in them. This
creates familiarity with the human-likeliness of the AI, showing that AI anthropomorphism positively
influences marketing personalization (Laban & Araujo, 2020). This analogy is used on the variables AI
influencers/models anthropomorphism, leading to AI marketing personalization, as AI influencers/models are
also an AI tool. In the context of AI influencers/models, AI Marketing Personalization makes people more
likely to buy and be attracted to such platforms that gauge consumer interests better (Liu et al., 2023). So
looking at the previous studies, it can be hypothesized that if AI Anthropomorphism leads to marketing
personalization, and AI Marketing Personalization leads to Purchase Intention, all whilst the relation between
AI Influencer/Models Anthropomorphism and Purchase Intention is positive, marketing personalization
mediates this relation. Hence, the studies formulated the following hypothesis:
Hypothesis 1:  Marketing Personalization mediates the positive relationship between AI
Anthropomorphism and Purchase Intention.

2.4. AI Anthropomorphism and Purchase Intention: A Mediated Process by Entertainment Value
Research indicates that entertainment value holds strong significance to inculcate emotional
attachment with virtual influencers. If the close and personal interactions of consumers with them are
interesting, they result in cognitive consistency and emotional attachment. These anthropomorphic results
make the consumers build virtual influencer ecosystems, driving brand recognition and purchase intention
(Liu et al., 2025). The aforementioned features are parallel to anthropomorphism, such that consumers when
interacting with AI influencers/models, feel a level of cognitive similarity and emotionality with it due to
perception of entertainment. Studies note that in the online ecosystems, consumer experience is shaped by the
entertainment component and perceived enjoyment instilled by the anthropomorphic cues of AI. Increased
anthropomorphism due to entertainment leads to greater purchase intention of customers (Klein & Martinez,
2023).
According to a recent study, the higher the consumer engagement is with AI influencers/models, the
more is the entertainment perception of consumers. If AI influencers/models have the characteristic of being
entertaining or amusing, that leads to enjoyment or escapism. These features are important to divert the
attention of the consumers via the perceived entertainment that they provide. The entertainment component
of AI influencers/models also enhance user satisfaction. So, interactions with AI influencers/models lead to
increased entertainment, user satisfaction and then in turn these increase purchase intention (Cao et al., 2025).
One research reports via five studies that entertainment value itself leads to higher purchase intention in social
media marketing. Entertainment leads to enjoyment and pleasure that increase the probability that the
followers would be more likely to be attached to the influencer (Ao et al., 2023). Like for human
models/influencers, consumers form a perception about AI models/influencers that is significantly dependent
on the entertainment value that they provide, in turn influencing their purchase actions and intentions. These
studies help us formulate our hypothesis:
Hypothesis 2: Entertainment Value mediates the positive relationship between AI Anthropomorphism
and Purchase Intention.
2.5. Anthropomorphism and Purchase Intention: A Mediated Process by Perceived Homophily
Previous studies reveal that AI Influencer/Models Anthropomorphism leads to enhanced purchase
intention by consumers and sometimes even lead to consumer's willingness to pay more, as their human-
likeliness enhances their purchase decisions (Pan et al., 2024; Sidlauskiene et al., 2023). It's because
consumers are more likely to relate to high or low anthropomorphism, i.e. animated or hyper-realistic AI
influencers/Models; medium range anthropomorphism in AI influencers/models ads looks uncanny, diffusing
consumers' reliability and decreased buying interest. Perceived homophily plays an integral role in forming
emotional bonds between consumers and AI influencers/models. When consumers perceive shared values or
preferences, they become more likely to develop a sense of identification and psychological closeness (Lee
& Watkins, 2016). The perceived similarity strengthens message credibility and also boosts persuasion
effectiveness, specifically in social media environments (McPherson et al., 2001). A study by Cottrell (2023)
proves that positive anthropomorphism results in homophily. Also, homophily has positive correlation with
purchase intention, particularly in the aspects of physical appearance and perceived similarity of virtual
influencers/models. Just like for human influencers and actors in ads, if virtual influencers/models are
authentic, then it will lead to purchase intention (Dondapati & Dehury, 2024). Hence, the studies formulated
the following hypothesis:
Hypothesis 3:  Perceived Homophily mediates the positive relationship between AI
Anthropomorphism and Purchase Intention.

3. Methodology
3.1. Procedures and Participants
The target population for this study consists of consumers in Pakistan, both males and females, aged
16-45, who have interacted with AI influencers/models of Pakistani and International brands on digital
platforms, that are also the unit of analysis; individuals who didn't fit said criteria of know-how of AI
Influencers were filtered out, yet adhering to sample size requirements. This study is an exploratory,
quantitative research at a cross-sectional timeframe, using convenience and snowball sampling with a sample
size of 220, using Nunnally's (1978) i.e., 20 items of questionnaire (22×10 = 220). Pakistani consumers are
selected because it represents an under-researched population regarding AI influencers/models as a marketing
tool. The age bracket largely represents Gen-Z and Millennials, including people who are, or will be a large
buyer group in Pakistan.
Questionnaire was uploaded via Google forms and distributed via online channels, using a five-point
Likert scale for each variable. The software and techniques of data collection have been derived from surveys
(Clark et al., 2021). The statistical tool used for data analysis was SPSS, and Hayes Model 4 for testing the
hypotheses in accordance with our research framework. Using SPSS, demographic analysis of participants'
age and gender distribution was done, along with finding the correlation and reliability of the data. All initial
analysis deemed the data set to be adequate for research, further mediation analysis was conducted using
Hayes Process Model 4.

5.2. Implications
5.2.1. Theoretical Implications. The empirical findings of this study yield four unique, novel
theoretical contributions that expand existing knowledge in AI influencer marketing research.
A Novel Tri-path Mediation Model.  The study's most significant contribution is the simultaneous
empirical validation of three theoretically independent mediating routes, marketing personalization,
entertainment value, and perceived homophily, through which AI influencers' anthropomorphism increases
purchase intention. Prior research has looked at each pathway separately (Kumar et al., 2019; Ao et al., 2023;
Lou & Yuan, 2019), and no study has included all three into a single model. This tri-pathway design indicates
that anthropomorphism stimulates a technical, hedonic, and social psychological process in customers, each
of which separately drives purchase intention. This necessitates a more architecturally complicated approach
to AI anthropomorphism in future consumer research.
A Differential Mediation Pattern: Full vs. Partial Mediation.  A theoretically refined finding is that
the three pathways function asymmetrically. Marketing personalisation results in full mediation, which means
that anthropomorphism only influences purchase intention through the personalisation process; once
personalisation is implemented, the direct effect becomes unimportant. This builds on the study of Beyari and
Hashem (2025), confirming personalisation as the sole channel through which anthropomorphism influences
buying behaviour. In contrast, entertainment value and perceived homophily result in partial mediation,
implying that these pathways accentuate rather than totally explain the anthropomorphism-purchase intention
association. This full vs partial asymmetry has not been proposed in previous AI influencer literature, and it
is a precise empirical refinement of how anthropomorphism acts differently across technical, hedonic, and
social channels.
Cross-Cultural Validation in an Emerging Market. This is one of the first studies to evaluate the AI
anthropomorphism-purchase intention concept in the Pakistani market, addressing a gap in research in South
Asian and Muslim-majority markets. The confirmation of all three mediated pathways in this collectivist,
digitally emerging consumer context, where brands like Saya and Nishat Linen are just starting to use AI
influencer marketing, broadens the model's theoretical generalisability beyond Western and East Asian
settings (Jayasingh et al., 2025). Notably, marketing personalisation appeared as the highest predictor of
purchase intention (r = 0.715), possibly reflecting Pakistani consumers' increased receptiveness to
individualised brand communication, a culturally specific dynamic that merits additional theoretical
exploration.
Uncanny Valley: An Empirically Operative Boundary Condition.  Chapter 2 examined the uncanny
valley hypothesis (Mori, 1970) and the U-shaped anthropomorphism-purchase intention link (Pan, 2024) as
theoretical concepts. This work provides real-world empirical evidence for these within a Pakistani consumer
sample (β = 0.410, p < 0.001), indicating that the uncanny valley is not only a laboratory-based perceptual
phenomenon but also an operational boundary condition in live AI influencer marketing. Furthermore, the
full mediation finding for personalisation implies that mid-level anthropomorphism fails to activate
personalisation perception, relating the uncanny valley effect to a particular mediating mechanism that was
not previously specified in the literature.

caution. Mid-level anthropomorphism, which is realistic enough to look human but not quite convincing, runs

5.3. Limitations
1. The research consisted of a convenience sampling technique, with a sample consisting of easily
accessible respondents within Pakistan. Although the sample size (n = 220) might be suitable for
statistical analysis, it may not fully represent the diverse population of consumers across different
regions and cultural backgrounds in Pakistan or other parts of the world. Hence, the generalisability
of the findings is limited.
2. Data was collected only at a single point in time due to the time limit of the research under study, but
realistically consumer perceptions are ever changing and evolve over time especially after repeated
exposure or the multiple types of marketing techniques employed on them might generate different
viewpoints over time. Hence cross-sectional design is a limitation of this study.
3. The data was gathered through questionnaires based on questions taken from different research papers
with authentic and adequate item scales. However, the respondents might be biased, especially in terms
of expressing their need for social disability as the survey only included individuals with prior
knowledge of AI influencers/models. So, inaccurate self-assessment is possible.
4. The research made conclusions about the fashion industry utilizing the data, but that makes the
practical application non-generalized. Other growing Pakistani industries induce food, finance and
health, hence responses of customers may differ based on product types and the need for industry
specific marketing techniques.
5. This research prioritised only three mediating variables, such as AI marketing personalization,
entertainment value, and perceived homophily. Although these variables are theoretically significant,
the research excludes other important factors such as trust, credibility, perceived authenticity, and
ethical concerns regarding AI influencers/models, which may also influence purchase intention.

5.4. Future Directions
1. The future studies can explore the relation of AI anthropomorphism and purchase intention in the
context of AI influencers/models, with reference to different industries with specific characteristics
e.g the finance industry, which is more dependent on factors like trust other than visual features and
entertainment. In the same way, it can also analyse the application according to specific
product/service types.
2. The future studies can explore the concept of AI influencers/models in rural areas, where there is lack
of advancement in technology. This would assist marketers to derive a better understanding of how to
apply this particular marketing tool in different social contexts.
3. Future research can be longitudinal, spread over a longer time frame to garner a more accurate
understanding of the impact of continued exposure of AI influencers/models on the consumers and
how their acceptability, reactions and purchase intentions change over time. AI anthropomorphism
could then be concluded on the basis of longitudinal analysis (Asim et al., 2024).
4. Researchers can dwell on the ethical considerations that may arise due to widespread adoption of AI
influencers/models as a marketing tool, considering customer consent, privacy of their data and
transparency of AI influencers/models expressed towards consumers. Ethical considerations may also
include the impact on the livelihood of newer and less established human influencers/models.
5. Researchers can also focus on comparing human vs virtual models/influencers in the same settings
across multiple industries, in order to predict the effectiveness of each in separate contexts. They can
also include clips, images and other visuals for a better understanding of the consumers' perceptions
about this technology.
5.5. Conclusion
In conclusion, marketing personalization as a technical feature, entertainment value as a hedonic
characteristic and perceived homophily as a social aspect are significant to derive and increase Pakistani
consumers' purchase intention, all while taking into account the human likeliness of said AI
influencers/models in the form of AI Anthropomorphism. about it in this contemporary era of the 5.0 industry,
where AI is making its way into the industry especially as a tool to be utilized in a multifaceted way, i.e.
marketing in this context. It has also been established by previous literature and other factors like high
dependability of visuals and better levels of trust gained, that its best applicability is in the fashion industry,
as many global fashion brands are also adopting this tool at an increased rate. This research gives scholars
and the fashion industry alike, an insight into the fact that which aspects and why, matter for the consumers
in AI influencer/model-based marketing; Marketing Personalization is crucial as it allows brands to target
consumers at an enhanced data-based level and achieve better conversion rates, impacting their profitability
and an adequate returns on marketing efforts. However, at the same time, Entertainment Value and Perceived
Homophily are also important, as for consumers, they still expect that the AI models/influencers are relatable
like real life influencers/models, and that they are entertaining like them as well. In the case of AI
influencers/models, AI Anthropomorphism facilitates these consumer expectations by the inherent ability of
AI to be more responsive and fulfill their social need for homophily, while data collected over time could be
utilized to train the AI to be more flexible and specific in its entertainment component. Hence, the insights
garnered from this research can help industry practitioners to leverage the emerging technology of AI
influencers/models and become industry leaders in doing so, leading to capitalization of the opportunity first
hand when this area of marketing is not saturated in the Pakistani market yet.
