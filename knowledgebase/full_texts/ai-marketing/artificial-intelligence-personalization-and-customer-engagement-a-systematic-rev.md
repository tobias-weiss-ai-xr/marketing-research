Abstract
The emergence of artificial intelligence (AI) has become a vocal strategy in digital marketing and
has no doubt been receiving huge attention and adoption. Firms now channel individualized
services and marketing communication to its customers using machine  learning algorithms,
predictive analytics, and large -scale consumer data from purchase or engagement history to
provide highly personalized offerings. Despite this, the relationship between AI personalization
and customer engagement is inconsistent across  different digital environment. Thus, the need to
evaluate existing literatures. This study addresses this gap by conducting a systematic review of
literatures from year 2022 to 2026, synthesizing evidence from 84 peer reviewed studies on AI
personalization and customer engagement in  the e-commerce environment. It found out th e
dominant research themes , patterns, methodological approaches, and key determinants
influencing engagement outcomes. The result established that engagement comes from perceived
usefulness and relevance of a firms’ offering, however, concerns regarding privacy, algorithmic
transparency and intrusiveness remains key challenges affecting consumers which may undermine
trust and limits the effectiveness of personalization strategies.  The use of AI personalization
reduces consumers’ information overload and improves its overall digital experiences.  From the
managerial perspective, the adoption of ethical frameworks and customer-centric personalization
will enable firms to enjoy high engagement leading to positive responses  and the policy aspect
employs government to step in and tackle the growing concern of privacy and intrusiveness from
the use of personalization by establishing clear regulatory frameworks and data protection laws
for its citizens because the use of AI personalization is shaping the business environment.

1. Introduction
Advances in digital technology have fundamentally transformed how firms interact with customers
in the digital environment. The rapid evolution of digital platforms has enabled business to move
beyond the traditional mass marketing approaches toward more personalized and data driven
customer experiences. AI personalization allows a firm to deliver tailored products, services and
communications to individual consumers based on behavioural data and predictive analytics

AI personalization enables businesses to track consumer preferences, bro wsing history, purchase

Customer engagement, a behavioural outcome reflects the cognitive and emotional interactions
customers have with brands across digital platforms  expressed in the digital space as product
enquiry, reviews (online), social media participation and continuous interactions with contents of
brands. Thus, utilizing AI personalization plays an important role in enhancing these interactions
by delivering relevant content that aligns with consumer interests and preferences (Etuk, Akpan,
& Awah, 2025).
When customers receive personalized recommendations or targeted marketing messages that
matches their needs, they tend to more likely interact with the brands and develop stronger
relationships, thus, personalization is a key driver of customer engagement in the digital market
(Okwudiri et al., 2025, Etuk et.al., 2025).
As AI personalization in the digital environment is becoming widespread, so also its challenges .
First is the growing concern of privacy and security of consumer data , and perceive misuse or
inadequate protection of its information or personal data . These are relevant because
personalization relies heavily on the collection and analysis of large volumes of consumer data,
(Moqaddem, 2025; Madane & Azeroual, 2025).
The rise of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in  the digital space/ world  has transformed businesses
interactions with customers, particularly through personalized recommendations, targeted

engagement, satisfaction, and loyalty. However, despite the growing body of literature on AI
personalization and customer engagement , existing  research and landscapes tends to be
inconsistent and requires evaluation.
First, studies on AI personalization are context based which cuts  across multiple disciplines . A

2. Methodology
Through a comprehensive and organized summary that adheres to accepted standards, systematic
literature reviews give readers a deep understanding of th e literature i n an area by identifying
themes and techniques that offers sufficient transparency and reproducibility (Tandon, Dhir,
IIslam, & Mantymaki, 2020).

2.1 Search Strategy
The literature search was conducted using the Google Scholar database on Harzing’s Publish and
Perish with the following Boolean search query: (“AI personalization” OR “artificial intelligence
personalization”) AND (“consumer engagement” OR “customer engagement” OR “consumer
responses”). The initial search produced 231 records from Scopus, Google Scholar, IEEE and
Springer databases. When screened to produce results from year 2022 to 2026 resulted to 209
articles. The inclusion and exclusion criteria were carried out on Zotero.

2.2 Inclusion Criteria
i. Peer-reviewed journal articles pertaining to AI personalization and customer engagement;
ii. Articles that were published from 2022 to 2026;
iii. Journal articles written in English; and
iv. Articles conducted in e-commerce platforms.

2.3 Exclusion Criteria
i. Articles that do not fall between 2022 and 2026;
ii. Conference papers and books that served as a tutorial or course material;
iii. Papers that were duplicated and articles that did not examine  personalization and
engagement outcomes.

3.0 Discussion of Findings

3.1 Artificial Intelligence Personalization in E-commerce Environment
Artificial Intelligence (AI) Personalization is a data driven and algorithmic process that enables
firms to analyze large volumes of consumer data, predict individual preferences and behaviour and
deliver tailored marketing messages across digital platform s. With AI personalization, firms can
now utilize digital technologies such as chatbots, recommendation systems and predictive
analytics to offer personalized experiences to consumers. According to Maqaddem (2025), AI
personalization involves the use of ad vanced data analytics, machine learning and predictive
modelling to deliver hyper -personalized experiences and real time engagement. That is, AI
personalization is used to generate large volumes of structured and unstructured consumer data,
including browsing history, purchase records, clickstream data and social media interactions. This
aids in anticipation of consumer needs, behaviour and present tailored content.
AI personalization uses predictive intelligence to anticipate consumer preferences and behaviour
by utilizing machine learning algorithms and big data that continuously learn from previous
purchase and also evolve. Predictive intelligence operates through learning models, behavioural
clustering and segmentation and recommendation algorithms based on similarity and relevance
(Mathur & Tripathi, 2025, Kumar , Gupta, & Sharma, 2025). This shifts marketing from the
reactive point of view to proactive where firms  not only respond to user behaviour but anticipate
and shape the behaviour.
AI personalization has been seen as transformative because it moved marketing from mass
communication to individualized interactions, enabling firms to deliver highly tailored contents
that aligned with user preferences. This transformation allows organizations to optimize customer
targeting and marketing efficiency (Oke, et. al, 2024).
Across the reviewed studies,  leveraging on predictive analytics, recommender systems, and
automated decision -making processes  showed strong consensus (Moqaddem, 2025; Mathur &
Tripathi, 2025). These technologies , enables organizations (e -commerce) to deliver tailored
content, thereby improving customer interaction and engagement across digital platforms.
However, the focus is largely on capabilities  of the technologies  rather than customer -centric
outcomes (satisfaction and loyalty).

3.2.    AI Personalization and Customer Engagement
From the systematic review, AI personalization and customer satisfaction is a  dominant theme
across the literature. Available empirical studies have demonstrated that personalization through
the use of recommendation systems, targeted advertising, and AI interfaces significantly enhance
interaction, browsing behaviour, and purchase intentions (Ahmed et al., 2025; Brown et al., 2023;
Chen et al., 2023; Kumar et al., 2023; Gupta et al., 2025). A similar study according to Yin et al.,
(2025) on experimental and data-driven studies show ed that recommendation systems improve
click-through rates and consumer immersion . Despite the convergence, outstanding divergence
exists vis-à-vis the nature of this relationship. Though some studies reported a direct positive effect
of personalization on engagement, others argued that the relationship is indirect and facilitated by
psychological constructs such as trust and perceived usefulness (Teepapal, 2024; Jayapal, 2025).
Teepapal (2024), in particular, found that AI persona lization does not have a direct influence on
engagement without the presence of mediating variables. Suggesting that, prior models may have
oversimplified the linkage.

Moreover, w hile engagement reflect s short -term interaction, satisfaction represents long -term

existing research.

3.3      Mediating Mechanisms: Trust, Perceived Usefulness, and Experience
The mediating role of cognitive and affective factors is widely acknowledged in the literature.
Trust emerges as a critical mediator influencing how consumers respond to AI -driven
personalization, particularly in contexts involving data sharing and algorit hmic decision-making
(Chen et al., 2022; Garcia et al., 2024). Similarly, perceived usefulness has been shown to
significantly shape user acceptance and engagement with personalized systems (Teepapal, 2024).
In addition, customer experience is frequently identified as an intermediary variable linking
personalization to satisfaction and loyalty outcomes (Yeriko & Caesar, 2024; Kim et al., 2023).
These findings suggest that AI personalization operates through a multi-stage process , where
technological inputs influence psychological perceptions, which in turn drive behavioral
outcomes.
Nevertheless, the literature lacks a unified framework integrating these mediators simultaneously.
Most studies examine trust, usefulness, or experience in isolation, resulting in fragmented
theoretical development.

3.4  Privacy, Ethical Concerns, and Perceived Intrusiveness
While AI personalization offers substantial benefits, it also raises concerns related to privacy and
perceived intrusiveness. Several studies acknowledge that excessive personalization can lead to
negative consumer reactions, particularly when users percei ve their data as being misused
(Jayapal, 2025; Zhao et al., 2025; Singh et al., 2024). These concerns can weaken trust and reduce
the effectiveness of personalization strategies.
Interestingly, the literature presents contradictory findings in this area. Some studies suggest that
personalization enhances trust by delivering relevant and valuabl e content (Garcia et al., 2024) ,
while others argue that it undermines trust due to privacy risks. This contradiction indicates that
the impact of personalization on trust is  context-dependent, influenced by factors such as
transparency, data governance, and cultural expectations.

3.5 Contextual and Platform Differences
Majority of studies has confirmed the effectiveness of AI personalization across various digital
contexts, including e-commerce, social media, and online advertising platforms, there is limited
agreement on whether its impact varies significantly by context (Turki, 2024; Torres et al., 2022;
Zhou et al., 2024). Some scholars are of the opinion that  personalization is more effective in e -
commerce environments due to direct purchase incentives, while others highlight the importance
of social interaction in so cial media platforms. However, the lack of comparative cross -platform
analyses makes it difficult for the findings to be generalized.

3.6 Key Themes in AI Personalization and Customer Engagement
i. Targeted advertisements and recommendation systems are dominant and well-established
artificial intelligence driven personalization theme that enhances: Click-through rates; Purchase
intention; and Time on site and immersion.

ii. Mediated Relationship (Not Always Direct): The theoretical theme of personalization from
the study moves from trust to perceived usefulness to experience to engagement. Some studies
reject a direct effect and emphasized psychological mediation. This aligns with Technology
Acceptance Model (TAM) and Stimulus-Organism-Response Framework (S-O-R).

iii. Role of Customer Experience and Satisfaction: Engagement is widely studied while
customer satisfaction is under represented. Research prioritizes short-term behavioural metrics
over long-term relational outcomes.

iv. Privacy, Ethics, and Intrusiveness: This is a recurring theme that states that personalization
can be beneficial or intrusive:
a. Personalization builds trust (relevance)
b. Personalization erodes trust (privacy concerns)
This explains the Privacy Paradox and depends on the perception of the user.

4.0 Conclusion and Recommendations
This study examined the effect of AI personalization on customer engagement in e -commerce
environment through a comprehensive systematic review, synthesizing evidence from 84 peer -
reviewed studies published between 2022 and 2026. The study revealed that, personalization has
become a transformative drive in e-commerce platforms, enabling organizations to deliver tailored
and highly relevant, data driven, and individualized experiences that results to engagement.
Secondly, the synthesizes showed that the relationship between both constructs is not direct or
uniformed. It showed a rather complex, context dependent, and psychological themes: perceived
usefulness, trust, customer experience, and immersion . These, consistently emerge d as key
mechanisms. It also confirms that, engagement is perception and experience  driven rather than a
direct result of technology. In addition, the study highlighted the presence of critical moderating
and constraining variables and factors, particularly privacy concerns, perceived intrusiveness, and
algorithmic transparency, which can weaken or even negate the positive effects of AI
personalization. The evidence also points to a growing dual effect phenomenon, where AI
personalization simul taneously generates benefits (improved decision making, reduced
information overload, and enhanced user experience) and risks (privacy threats, overreliance on
technology, and loss of consumer autonomy).  These findings underscore the need for a balanced
and ethical approach to AI personalization, where technological innovation is aligned with user
trust, transparency, and data protection. Overall, the study contributes to the literature by
advancing a process-based and integrative understanding of AI personalization, emphasizing that
its effectiveness depends on how users perceive, interpret, and experience personalized
interactions. Based on the findings, the following recommendation emerged. Organizations should
adopt a customer-centric personalization strategy that prioritizes user experience, relevance, and
value creation, rather than solely focusing on algorithmic sophistication. Organisations must invest
in artificial intelligence systems that leads to real-time responsiveness, predictive accuracy, and
usability, while ensuring that personalization contributes meaningfully to customer decision -
making and interaction quality. It can also engage in building and maintaining customers trust as
a central objective. This can be achieved through transparent processes, consistent system
performance, and clear communication regarding data usage, as trust has been identified as a key
driver of both engagement and long-term loyalty. Finally, organizations must proactively address
privacy and ethical concerns by implementing data governance frameworks, user consent
mechanisms, and privacy protection measures .  Policymakers and regulatory bodies should
establish clear guidelines and data protection laws to safeguard consumer interests and ensure
responsible use of AI technologies. Future research should focus on developing integrated models
