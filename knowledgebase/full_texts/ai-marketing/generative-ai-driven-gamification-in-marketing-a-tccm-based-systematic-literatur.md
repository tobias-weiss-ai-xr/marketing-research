Abstract:
The rapid advancement of Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) has increased the potential to create,
customise, and deploy gamification effectively in different sectors, including educa tion, healthcare, software
creation, and online engagement. Many studies are exploring the use of GenAI, and the literature rema ins
fragmented and conceptually weak, even though gamification is on the rise.  This article provides a review of the
literature through a the systematic literature review intends to integrate the research undertaken. Research gaps
that need to be addressed to expand the field using The Theory-Context-Characteristics of Gami fication & GenAI
Methodology (TCCM) framework. Research articles were systematically searched and filtered using the PRISMA
protocol. From the Scopus database and additional literature sources, including publications f rom Notable
publishing houses such as Taylor & Francis, Emerald, and Springer. The first search in th e Scopus database
found 2,515 papers and 79 from other sources. A thorough analysis resulted in the inclusion o f 52 research
articles.  An analysis based on TCCM indicates that there is a dominance of study of theories of motivation and
learning, education, serious games, healthcare rehabilitation, programming education and game des ign. AI-
Generated Game Content, Adaptive Difficulty Modifications are the characteristics in the study. The Feedback is
automatic, and learning is tailored and adaptable.  A range of methodological techniques co mprises experimental,
and mixed-methods investigations. Studies with longitudinal and cross-contextual are rare. Id entifying the gap
through this literature review develops a structured research agenda for future s tudies in the context of theory
integration, contextualization, human-centred design considerations and Improved Methodologica l Rigor based
on the TCCM framework.

4. DISCUSSSION
4.1 Theoretical frameworks employed in earlier research
Earlier research on AI-based gamification has taken on various theoretical perspect ives to understand
student motivation, engagement, and learning Outcomes. Self-Determination Theory claims that learners
have higher motivation when they experience competence, relatedness, and autonomy. When people feel
that their needs for autonomy, competence and relatedness are satisfied. Using r ewards, feedback, and
challenges in gamified learning environments increases intrinsic motivation that is consistent with Self-
Determination theory.  Another theory often used is Constructivist Learning Th eory, which states that
learners efficiently construct knowledge through interaction, exploration, and experience rather than
instructional information (Piaget, 1972). The ability for learners to do, experi ment, receive feedback and
information and make it compatible using constructivist learning principles. In addition, studies have
employed Adaptive Learning theory, flow theory, and the technological acceptance model t o show
learner engagement and implementation of technology in gaming systems. Nonetheless , these sorts of
theories are used. Research on the integration of motivational factors has been l imited. Constructivism
describes the adaptive characteristics and learner-centred environments that have been analysed in
previous research.
4.2 Characteristics examined in previous research
The existing literature on AI-based gamification has examined several constructs related to learner
behaviour and learning outcomes. Commonly studied constructs include motivation, engag ement,
learning performance, usability, personalisation, satisfaction, and learning effectiveness. Many studies
focus on adaptive difficulty levels, intelligent feedback, and personalised lear ning paths as factors
influencing learner performance. These constructs are frequently analysed in digi tal learning
environments, intelligent tutoring systems, and online education platforms. Howeve r, constructs related
to developmental learning characteristics are less frequently examined. In early chi ldhood education,
learning depends on factors such as individual pace of learning, sensory interacti on, and response to the
environment. These constructs are particularly important for young learners and children wit h
disabilities, who require flexible learning environments that adapt to their a bilities and needs. The
existing literature gives limited attention to these developmental constructs, indicating the need for
further investigation.
4.3 Contexts studied in previous research
Most studies on AI-driven gamification have been conducted in higher education, online l earning
platforms, corporate training, and general school education. Research frequently ex amines university-
level learners and digital classrooms, where gamification is used to increase engagem ent and
performance. Some studies also investigate gamification in healthcare education and professional
training. In contrast, early childhood education has received limited attention in the literature.
Montessori education represents a learner-centred pedagogical approach that emphasises sel f-paced
learning, sensory interaction, and independent exploration. These characteristics make Monte ssori
learning compatible with adaptive and personalised gamified systems. The need for  flexible learning
environments is even greater for children with disabilities, who often r equire individualised pacing and

4.4 Methods Employed in Prior Investigations
The methodological underpinnings of the TCCM framework indicate that the preponderance of res earch
on generative AI-enhanced gamification predominantly employs quantitative research desi gns. These
studies largely depend on survey-based methodologies, which are then subjected to stat istical analyses
such as regression analysis, ANOVA, and structural equation modeling. Moreover, a number of
investigations integrate experimental designs to enable comparisons between gamified and non -
gamified, or adaptive and non-adaptive, learning systems, especially within educati onal contexts.
Conversely, a relatively small proportion of studies adopt qualitative or mixed-method approaches,
which are primarily utilized to gain insights into user experience and syst em design.
In summation, the existing literature is significantly characterized by quantitat ive and experimental
research, whereas qualitative, mixed-method, and longitudinal studies are infrequently enc ountered,
thereby highlighting a methodological deficiency. Future research should adopt more d iverse research
designs to better understand the effectiveness of generative AI-driven gamificati on in different learning
contexts.
4.5 Antecedents–Mediators–Consequences Framework
The effect of the antecedents is often explained through mediators, such as engagement, moti vation,
satisfaction, flow experience, perceived usefulness, and interaction level. These medi ating variables
describe the internal processes through which users respond to gamified and AI-supported systems.
The literature shows that the main antecedents are related to technological and system design factors,
including AI-based personalization, adaptive game mechanics, feedback systems, rewards and badges,
intelligent tutoring, and user interface design. These antecedents represent the key inputs that determine
the effectiveness of gamified AI environments.The most frequently cited outcomes e ncompass learning
performance, learning effectiveness, skill acquisition, behavioral intention, tec hnology acceptance,
participation levels, and user satisfaction. These observed consequences collectively suggest that the
fundamental objective of gamification, particularly when driven by generative AI, is to enhance
engagement, improve learning efficiency, and boost user performance.

5. RESEARCH GAP
A review of existing research shows several areas that need more study. First, most of t he current
research on gamification using AI focuses on higher education and digital learning. In contrast, early
childhood education and special education settings are not studied as much.
Moreover, previous studies often use Self-Determination Theory and Constructivist Learni ng Theory
separately. There's not much research on how these two theories can be combined to explain  learning
that adapts to the learner's needs.
Third, the rapid development of generative artificial intelligence after 2023 has resulted in new research
that has not been fully synthesised in earlier reviews. Fourth important constructs r elevant to early
education such as pace of learning and response to environment have received limited attention in the
existing literature.
These limitations indicate the need for a structured systematic review to synthesis e the literature on
generative AI-driven gamification and to identify theoretical, contextual, and m ethodological gaps,
particularly in the context of early childhood and special education.

6. ACADEMIC AND PRACTICAL IMPLICATIONS
Our study provides both academic and practical implications for research on gene rative AI-driven
gamification. From an academic perspective, the study contributes to the literat ure by synthesising prior
research using the TCCM framework, which provides a structured understanding of  the theories,
contexts, characteristics, and methods employed in existing studies. The review shows that m ost prior
research is concentrated in educational environments, while marketing and business appli cations of AI-
driven gamification remain limited, indicating the need for broader contextual exploration. By
incorporating the Antecedent –Mediator–Consequence (AMC) perspective, the study also provides a
clearer conceptual explanation of how gamification elements and AI-based featu res influence user
responses, such as motivation, engagement, and satisfaction, which, in turn, affe ct performance and
behavioural outcomes. The findings further highlight the need for stronger theoretica l integration and
more diverse methodological approaches, including qualitative and mixed-method research, to better
understand the effectiveness of AI-based gamified systems.
From a practical perspective, the results of this review suggest that integra ting generative artificial
intelligence with gamification can significantly improve user engagement, experience, and performance
through personalisation, adaptive feedback, and intelligent game mechanics. For educators, A I-driven
gamification can create personalised, interactive learning environments that enhance stu dent
participation and learning outcomes. For marketers and business practitioners , gamification combined
with AI technologies can be used to enhance customer engagement, brand interaction, and use r retention
through adaptive, interactive digital platforms. In addition, system designers can use gamification
features such as rewards, feedback, and adaptive difficulty to create more user-cent red and effective
applications. Overall, the study reveals the importance of designing AI-based gamif ied systems that are
helpful in achieving better outcomes across educational, marketing, and digital environments.

7. LIMITATIONS OF THE STUDY
Our study has several limitations that should be considered while interpreting t he findings. First, the
review was limited to articles retrieved from the Scopus database and selected academ ic sources, which
may have excluded relevant studies indexed in other databases. Although Scopus is widely recognised
for its high-quality peer-reviewed publications, restricting the search to a limit ed number of databases
may reduce the comprehensiveness of the review.
Second, only articles published in the English language were included in the analysi s. As a result,
relevant studies published in other languages were not considered, which may limit the global
representation of research on generative AI-driven gamification. Third, the study focused m ainly on
journal articles, and conference papers, book chapters, and grey literature were excluded to m aintain the
quality of the review. This restriction may have resulted in the omission of some rece nt or emerging
studies, particularly in the rapidly developing field of artificial intelligence.
Another limitation relates to the contextual distribution of the reviewed studies. Mos t of the selected
articles were conducted in educational and digital learning environments, while fewer studies examined
marketing, business, and customer engagement contexts. Therefore, the findings may not fu lly represent
the application of AI-driven gamification across all domains. In addition, the majority of previous
studies used quantitative or experimental research designs, and fewer studies adopted qual itative or
longitudinal approaches, which limits the understanding of the long-term effects of gamification.

8. CONCLUSION
Our study conducted a systematic literature review to analyse the existi ng research on generative AI-
driven gamification using the PRISMA method and the TCCM framework. The review shows that
research on artificial intelligence and gamification has increased significantl y in recent years,
particularly after the development of generative AI technologies. The findings indicate that most
previous studies focus on educational and digital learning environments rather than pure marketing,
where gamification is used to improve engagement, motivation, and performance. Theoretic al analysis
reveals that Self-Determination Theory, Constructivist Learning Theory, and Techno logy Acceptance
Model are the most frequently used frameworks, although these theories are often applied independently
rather than in an integrated manner.
The context analysis shows that higher education and online learning platforms dominat e the literature,
while marketing, business, and customer engagement contexts remain less explored. The characteris tics
examined in previous studies mainly include gamification elements, adapti ve feedback, personalisation,
and intelligent tutoring systems, which are found to influence user motivation, satisfaction, and
behavioural outcomes. The methodological analysis indicates that most studies use quantitative and
experimental approaches, with limited use of qualitative and longitudinal research designs.
Overall, the review highlights the growing importance of generative AI in enha ncing gamified systems
and provides a structured understanding of the current state of research. By applying the TCCM and
AMC perspectives, the study offers a comprehensive overview of theories, contexts, variabl es, and
methods used in AI-driven gamification research.
