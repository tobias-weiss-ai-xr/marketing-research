Abstract: In an increasingly competitive world, retail companies are implementing marketing strategies aimed at
gaining customer preference by building lasting emotional relationships. This facilitates the conversion of s o-called
followers into loyal customers and brand ambassadors, thereby increasing return on investment (ROI), facilitating direct
feedback to improve the product mix, and reducing customer acquisition costs over time, generating competitive
advantages. The  purpose of this systematic review is to analyze recent scientific production (2020 -2025) on the
application of Artificial Intelligence (AI) for the personalization of engagement in B2C business models, in order to
analyze the predominant techniques, evalu ate their impact on the consumer and determine the critical factors that
condition long-term customer loyalty; the methodology used consisted of a systematic review in the main scientific
databases in publications of high-impact journals. The main findings suggest that personalization tends to be dominated
by machine learning models focused on algorithmic efficiency; however, a critical asymmetry was observed: the
literature shows a hyper-focus on quantitative studies and functional efficiency, while studies on trust and ethics are the
least relevant. Additionally, knowledge is biased by Asian dominance (53%) and the "novelty effect," limiting the
applicability of strategies in markets with high demands for transparency, such as the American market. Furtherm ore,
an emerging trend toward an Ethical -Relational Model has been observed in the literature. It is concluded that moving
beyond the functional efficiency-centric approach and integrating Ethical AI as a strategic consideration may be key to
fostering sustainable engagement, as evidenced by the reviewed literature.

Index Terms: Artificial Intelligence, Recommendation Systems, Customer Engagement, Customer Experience, Digital
Marketing, Consumer Behavior, B2C (Business to Consumer), E-commerce.

1. Introduction
In the last decade, digitization has radically transformed the architecture of consumer markets, consolidating e -
commerce and B2C (Business -to-Consumer) business models as scenarios where competition is no longer defined
solely by price, but by the ability to capture and retain the user's attention. As a result, numerous tools have emerged to
boost competitiveness; however, the most disruptive in recent years is undoubtedly Artificial Intelligence (AI), which
has evolved from a purely operational tool to one capable of redefining modern marketing management [1]. This has led
to the development of AI -based tools such as machine learning and deep recommendation systems, enabling

organizations to process massive volumes of data to predict consumer behavior and personalize offers with
unprecedented accuracy. This has become the primary driver of innovation in today's retail value chain [2].
However, despite these advances, technological implementation alone is not enough to guarantee sustainable
success if it does not generate a meaningful connection with the consumer. Therefore, current efforts are focused on
driving the evolution of AI from a utilitarian and efficiency -driven function to a source of relational value capable of
fostering Customer Engagement [3], which can be defined as a proactive and emotional connection that acts as a critical
mediator between technology and customer loyalty [4]. Evidence shows that, while AI functionality and responsiveness
are essential, it is the affective dimens ion of personalization that ultimately triggers repurchase intention and long -term
loyalty in digital environments [5].
Despite the benefits described above, several ethical challenges and barriers exist to the full adoption of AI. These
include the opacity of algorithms and concerns about data privacy, which have generated a climate of distrust. The lack
of transparency and the perception of compromised autonomy can erode consumer trust, becoming a difficult asset to
regain [6,7]. Furthermore, increased machine efficiency has not replaced human empathy; therefore, consumers tend to
penalize AI agents more severely than humans for service failures [8,9].
In this context, it is essential to systematize recent evidence to understand a strategy that balances te chnological
efficiency with human experience. Analyzing the sustainability of engagement is key, since, while AI generates an
initial competitive advantage, companies have discovered that this differentiating factor tends to diminish over time,
shifting from a novelty to a standard expected by the customer [10]. Added to this is the latent uncertainty about the
future reach of the technology, which acts as a psychological barrier to full adoption. Therefore, the challenge lies in
preventing innovation from losing value if it violates ethical expectations, making trust the only asset that guarantees
the long-term relationship [6,7].
Therefore, given the identified gap in the study of trust factors, the main objective of this research is to analyze the
academic literature published between 2020 and 2025 on the application of Artificial Intelligence for personalized
engagement in B2C business models. Through a Systematic Literature Review (SLR) based on the PRISMA protocol,
the study is structured around three s pecific objectives: first, to analyze the predominant AI techniques and tools;
second, to evaluate the impact of these technologies on consumer response metrics; and finally, to determine the critical
factors and information gaps that influence long-term customer loyalty.
2. Literature Review
Companies today use various digital tools to improve the performance of their processes and generate competitive
advantages as quickly as possible. These factors have driven the integration of tools like artificial intelligence (AI) into
their business processes with the goal of transforming relationships with customers and employees [11].  However,
unlike in the past, these relationships are now based on personalization, operational efficiency, and anticipating needs .
Regarding customer engagement, the use of AI has driven hyper -personalization by analyzing large volumes of data to
offer personalized experiences and precise recommendations based on user behavior, providing continuous support and
reducing purchase abandonment rates. This has transformed processes from reactive to proactive [12].
2.1. Artificial Intelligence as a Strategic Imperative in B2C Marketing.
Recent studies mark a turning point in the conceptualization of technology in retail. While traditiona l approaches
perceived technology as merely operational support, current theories maintain that Artificial Intelligence (AI) is a
strategic resource that drives business sustainability across all processes, including commercial, operational, strategic,
and support processes [1]; [2]. The current debate is not about whether or not to adopt this tool, but rather about its
capacity to process large volumes of data and transform them into predictive knowledge to improve short -term
performance. This predictive a bility is the basis of Recommendation Systems, which contribute to generating
competitive advantages in modern B2C by setting aside product characteristics that remain important, but focusing on
algorithmic accuracy to anticipate consumer needs before they are made explicit [13].
2.2. Personalization as a Mediator of Customer Engagement.
One of the aspects addressed by various studies today is the debate on the causal relationship between technology
and consumer behavior. Currently, the main research theory  considers that AI alone does not generate loyalty and that
its effect is indirect, being mediated by personalization [3,5]. From this theoretical perspective, personalization acts as
the value mechanism: by adapting the digital environment to individual p references through machine learning, it
reduces the user's cognitive load and increases perceived relevance. It is precisely this relevance that triggers customer
engagement, understood here as a multidimensional psychological state encompassing cognitive,  emotional, and
behavioral components [4]. Therefore, the theoretical model suggests that AI is the technical enabler, but
personalization is the psychological driver of engagement.

2.3. The Theoretical Tension Between Privacy and Trust.
The implementa tion of engagement strategies using AI is no longer solely analyzed from the perspective of
economic performance. Currently, the benefits obtained are weighed against the risks incurred; this is known as the
"Privacy Calculus." This concept focuses on expl aining the inherent resistance to the use of AI: consumers constantly
perform a cost -benefit analysis, meaning they are only willing to share their data if they perceive the value of the
personalization received as greater than the risk [6,7]. However, when customers perceive AI as opaque or intrusive (the
"Black Box" phenomenon), the perceived risk outweighs the benefit, activating rejection mechanisms [14]. Thus, trust
is theoretically established as the indispensable moderating variable for the sustainability of the model.
2.4. Ethical Considerations of Using Artificial Intelligence (AI) to Boost Engagement
While AI offers significant opportunities for businesses, it also presents critical ethical challenges that must be
addressed to protect consumer trus t and rights. These ethical considerations include data privacy, transparency,
algorithmic fairness, and preventing manipulation. Respect for data privacy is a crucial aspect for customers,
particularly the protection of personal information throughout the AI lifecycle, which must comply with existing data
protection regulations [15].
Data Privacy and Security: This is a fundamental aspect to consider, since AI requires massive volumes of data to
personalize engagement and generate appropriate results. However, this creates risks of surveillance and privacy
breaches. Therefore, informed consent from users is required to authorize the collection of their information, as they are
often unaware of it. Furthermore, responsible management by organizations is required to ensure the development and
management of robust technical infrastructures to prevent security breaches and identity theft at all times [16].
A second aspect to consider is transparency and explainability; this is the right of consumers to be informed when
they interact with an AI system or to understand the decisions generated by algorithms in order to ensure that the
interactions are fair and comprehensible [17].
A third aspect to consider is algorithmic bias and the perception of fairness. This occurs because algorithms can
reinforce existing prejudices if trained with deficient or biased data, leading to discriminatory treatment in segmentation
or customer service. Therefore, companies must develop processes that ensure their AI systems treat all demographic
groups (race, gender, socioeconomic status) fairly and impartially [15].
3. Methodology
This research was conducted using a Systematic Literature Review (SLR) approach, following the guidelines of
the PRISMA 2020 protocol (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta -Analyses). This
methodological design facilitated the identification, evaluation, and analysis of the available scientific evidence on the
objective of this research, which is the application of Artificial Intelligence in the personalization of engagement within
B2C business models.
3.1. Information Sources
The search for research and review articles was conducted considering the period between 2020 and 2025, in three
high-impact academic databases: (1) Scopus, (2) Web of Science (WoS), and (3) IEEE Xplore. The selection of these
sources is based on the quality of the research, the peer -review process they employ, and their coverage, allowing for
the integration of the strategic management and consumer behavior perspective with research related to the
development and application of Artificial Intelligence solutions in digital environments, thus ensuring a comprehensive
approach aligned with the object of study.

Keywords and Boolean operators were used in English to select the scientific articles and ensure the study's
comprehensiveness. This process resulted in the following equations:

• Artificial Intelligence AND Recommender Systems AND E-commerce OR B2C.

The following complementary search strings were added to capture ethical, relational, and conver sational AI

3.3. Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria
To ensure a thorough literature review, inclusion and exclusion criteria aligned with the research objectives were
defined.

Inclusion Criteria.

The inclusion criteria were defined as follows

• Document Type: Only research articles and systematic reviews published in peer -reviewed journals were
included.
• Time Frame: The search was restricted to the period between 2020 and 2025.
• Subject: Studies related to the application of Artificial Intelligence to boost engagement within B2C business
models.
• Language: Documents published in English and Spanish were considered.
• Access: Open Access documents with full text availability were considered.

Exclusion Criteria.

The exclusion criteria defined in this review were as follows:

• Document Type: Theses, conference proceedings, book chapters, editorials, and documents not published in
indexed journals were excluded.
• Thematic Incongruity: Articles focused on B2B models, sectors outside the commercial sphere (such as
medicine), and purely computer science or algorithmic research lacking practical application or analysis within business
management were eliminated.
• Language: D ocuments written in languages other than English or Spanish (e.g., Turkish, Portuguese) were
excluded.
• Duplication: Repeated records resulting from the intersection of searches across different databases were
excluded.

3.5. Article Classification Criteria
To ensure transparency in the coding process, the following classification criteria were applied systematically to all
60 articles:

(a) Thematic category: Each article was assigned to one of four categories (Marketing Stra tegies and Customer
Experience; Recommendation and Personalization Tools; Virtual Assistants and Service Quality; Critical Trust and
Acceptance Factors) based on the primary research question and main findings as stated in the abstract, introduction,
and conclusion. When an article addressed multiple themes, the dominant focus determined classification.
(b) Methodological approach: Articles were classified as Quantitative (surveys, experiments, statistical modeling),
Qualitative (interviews, case studies, c onceptual frameworks), or Mixed based on explicit statements in the
methodology section.
(c) Continental affiliation: Determined by the institutional affiliation of the first (lead) author as listed in the article
metadata.
(d) Journal quartile: Assigned based on the SCImago Journal Rankings (SJR) quartile for the specific journal in the
year of publication, cross-referenced with the Journal Citation Reports (JCR) where available.

The authors interpret the data as suggesting a hierarchy in the priorities of current Artificial Intelligence research.
First, the predominant category is Marketing Strategy and Customer Experience, which accounts for 42% of the

"predicting better" to increase commercial performance [19].
As we move down the percentage distribution, the category of Virtual Assistants and Service Quality (17%)
emerges, highlighting the trend toward interaction automation. While systematic reviews indicate that chatbots improve
operational efficiency, their success depends critically on functional and anthropomorphic factors [20]. However, it is at
this point that the literature begins to show cracks: recent research warns that technology alone, being excessively direct
and algorithmic, does not guarantee a satisfactory experience if it lacks relational components that emulate human
warmth [21].
However, the most crit ical and revealing finding of this analysis lies in the minority category: Critical Trust and
Acceptance Factors, which represents only 7% of the studies. This deficit suggests a potential disconnect in the state of
the art; despite the technological matur ity described above, some authors argue that user personality and privacy
concerns are barriers that are not being addressed with the same intensity as software development. Similarly, it is
emphasized that the lack of transparency and ethics in AI -driven marketing can irreversibly damage the long -term
relationship, since current models operate with a transactional logic that neglects the ethical dimension necessary to
mitigate the consumer's perception of risk [6,7]. This asymmetry suggests that, although AI has managed to optimize
processes, it still faces the major challenge of humanizing its interaction to generate genuine trust.

The authors interpret this temporal pattern as suggesting that interest in the technology has matured in the business

This scenario has clearly changed in the last two years. The growth in  2024 (13%) and the strong increase in 2025
(48%) are not coincidental, but rather reflect the current operational reality of the market. Some research indicates that
AI has ceased to be an experiment and has become a strategic tool that companies use toda y to compete [1]. The most
recent research suggests that technologies such as live streaming and automated content creation are increasingly
common practices for connecting with the digital consumer, suggesting that the current literature increasingly describes
the tools that the market demands [22,23].
However, the fact that most of the information is so recent carries a natural limitation. By focusing on current mass
adoption, studies tend to measure the immediate impact on the customer, which could be ca pturing the so -called
"novelty effect." While the evidence reports high levels of short -term satisfaction, long-term evidence is still lacking to
confirm whether these results will hold once the initial fascination with the technology fades and it becomes part of
everyday life. This opens up a key research opportunity: validating whether AI truly builds relationships [30,31].
Analysis of the temporal evolution by thematic categories reveals a transition from a foundational approach to a
tactical overload in  business logic. The field of study began with a balanced interest in its technical and conceptual
pillars, with research focused on recommendation tools and marketing strategy in the period between 2020 and 2021
[12,19]. However, this uniformity breaks down in the maturity phase: growth is uneven, driven primarily by Marketing
Strategy and Recommendation Tools, which went from being foundational areas to accounting for the largest share of
research in 2025 (11 and 10 articles,  respectively). This pattern suggests that the focus of academia and industry may
have shifted from "understanding the value of AI" to "applying it tactically at high volume," though this interpretation
requires longitudinal validation. This massive shift toward execution reveals a "Tactical Myopia" in managerial

neglecting the pillars of long-term relationships. The Critical Trust Factors category, which should have seen increased
research given the sector's maturity, remains at the lowest volume, suggesting that the urgency for novelty has prevailed
over ethical consolidation. Therefore, basing the marketing strategy exclusively on the abi lity to surprise without
consolidating a policy of transparency should be avoided, as this puts the brand's reputation and future customer loyalty
at risk [7].

The corpus data reveal a predominantly quantitative approach, prioritizing financial aspects over abstract theory.
Evidence indicates that 58% of the studies (35 articles) employ a quantitative approach, demonstrating a clear need

business variables, such as the direct impact on sales and return on investment (ROI), providing managers with precise

Additionally, the authors identify an interpretive pattern termed the "Efficiency Trap" — operationalized as the
disproportionate concentration of research investment in short -term performance metrics at the expense of long -term
relational factors — and the "Deep Insights Gap" — defined as the insufficient volume of qualitative research needed to
explain consumer psychological responses to AI. These analytical heuristics, derived from the observed patterns in the
corpus, highlight a fundamental issue for decision -making: the sector exhibits a tendency toward the "Efficiency Trap"
by focusing the majority of research efforts ( 58%) on validating short-term performance metrics and KPIs—as seen in
the predictive purchasing models — meaning companies are managing what they can measure (transactions) while
neglecting what they need to understand (emotions) [26]. Evidence suggests that executives have dashboa rds full of
data on "how much" AI sells, but lack the qualitative depth needed to explain "why" customers sometimes feel
manipulated or rejected. Currently, studies are being developed to close the aforementioned gap with in -depth studies
on human interaction, warning that managing the business solely with quantitative spreadsheets can cause brands to lose
the real emotional connection with the consumer, becoming efficient but irrelevant [27].
The analysis of thematic categories and methodological approaches reveals a structural fracture in how industry
knowledge is constructed. The data shows that research is not homogeneous, but rather exhibits methodological silos
that skew the understanding of the business. The most striking finding is in the Re commendation and Personalization
Tools category, which exhibits a radical hegemony of the quantitative approach (82%). In this area, the numerical
measurement of algorithm accuracy and conversion rate is prioritized almost exclusively, relegating qualitative research
to 18% [13,26]. This implies that the most powerful sales engine in e -commerce is being optimized under a purely
transactional logic, with little in-depth exploration of how these recommendations affect the consumer's psyche or sense
of autonomy.
In contrast, the Virtual Assistants and Service Quality category shows an opposite profile, being predominantly
Qualitative (42%); here,  some research uses reviews and conceptual frameworks to understand the nature of human -
machine interaction [3]. This  disparity is consistent with what the authors term "Design Myopia" — an interpretive
label for the observed pattern: while customer service areas are studied from the perspective of empathy and human
understanding (Qualitative), sales and recommendation a reas are studied from the cold perspective of data
(Quantitative). For management, this represents a strategic risk: the tools designed to sell (Recommenders) are
technically perfect, but "humanly blind," which explains why, despite their high numerical ef ficiency, they often
generate rejection or distrust in the end user due to a lack of relational tact.

5. Discussion
Note: Section 5 represents the authors' interpretive synthesis of the bibliometric patterns identified in Section 4.
The conceptual labels and strategic conclusions presented here constitute scholarly inference from the corpus data and
are not d irect empirical findings; they should be evaluated as theory -building contributions rather than data -verified
conclusions. A systematic analysis of the literature reveals asymmetries that may compromise the sustainability of
engagement in B2C models. By comparing research patterns with conceptual models (Ethical AI, Engagement Theory),
the ethical and operational challenges that may condition the implementation of AI are discussed.
a) Theoretical Tension: The Divide between Functional Efficiency and Loyalty.
The authors identify a theoretical tension in the corpus: the concentration of quantitative studies (58%)  and
recommendation tools (35%) appears consistent with a prioritization of functional efficiency over relational depth,
making perceived utility the dominant pattern in the reviewed literature [5,13 ,19]. However, this concentration has led
to tactical myopia: the literature focuses on adoption, neglecting post-adoption loyalty. The research deficit on trust (7%)
suggests that functionality alone may b e insufficient for sustainable commitment, a key point demanded by
Commitment Theory.
b) New Theoretical Contribution: Ethical Debt and Positivism Bias.
The combination of the low volume of trust -related studies (7%) with the high concentration of literatu re in elite
quartiles (88% in Q1/Q2) constitutes the new theoretical contribution of this review. A Positivism Bias is identified
within the academic elite, where prioritizing efficiency successes creates a hyper-optimistic view.
The review determines that  the implementation of AI faces two critical barriers that limit its effectiveness
compared to traditional human interaction.
In the ethical sphere, the central challenge is the high perceived risk for consumers. For consumers, the massive
collection of data by AI generates a sense of vulnerability that acts as a direct deterrent to purchase intent, leading users
to reject personalization when they feel that the brand is invading their privacy without explicit permission [7,29]. This
creates a business para dox: the more AI tries to personalize without transparency, the more distrust it generates in the
customer [6].
As a result, the concept of "Ethical Debt" has emerged as an analytical construct in this review, defined as the
cumulative gap between the transparency and ethical governance that consumers expect from AI systems and what
current implementations actually provide. This ethical debt acts as a negative multiplier in interactions, where perceived
risk negates the benefit of personalization. This validates concerns about transparency in AI and the increased demand
for managing perceived consumer control, as the se factors limit AI's advancement to higher levels in interaction
processes [3,25].
c) Practical and Business Policy Implications: Geopolitical Decoupling:
The finding of observed Asian concentration (53% in this corpus) presents a challenge for business policy and
practical application. Asian dominance defines Recommendation tools (59%) with a logic of rapid conversion, such as
Live Streaming [22].
This pattern creates a strategic decoupling from Western markets, which demand an ethical approach. Therefor e,
the evidence suggests that corporate policy may benefit from evolving toward greater accountability, prioritizing the
management of explainability (XAI) to mitigate reputational risk [7].
6. Conclusions
A systematic literature review of 60 peer -reviewed articles suggests that Artificial Intelligence is playing an
increasingly strategic role in B2C business models, with observable asymmetries in the literature that may limit the
long-term sustainability of Customer Engagement (CE). These conclusions are l imited by the scope of the review
method and should be interpreted as evidence -based observations rather than definitive empirical findings. The
conclusions are structured according to the main objectives of this review:
Predominant Tools: The review ident ifies a clear evolution in Artificial Intelligence capabilities. Initially, the
predominant tools were based on traditional AI algorithms, such as Collaborative Filtering and standard Matrix
Factorization, which reduce their performance when data is scarce . A market evolution towards the implementation of
Deep Learning architectures has been identified, with advanced models such as Deep Matrix Factorization (DeepMF)
and Neural Networks consolidating as the new standards of accuracy. Furthermore, cutting -edge technology
incorporates Generative AI (GAI) tools for the dynamic creation of visual content, Graph -Based Systems for managing
complex relationships, and Federated Learning for the secure and decentralized processing of data.
Impact of Technologies and Engagement: The reviewed studies provide quantitative evidence on AI performance:
some report that AI-powered visual personalization is associated with a 22.45% increase in conversion rate compared to
standard interfaces. In terms of customer expe rience, th e quality of the AI is crucial, explaining almost 70% of

The reviewed literature ind icates that the implementation of AI appears to face ethical barriers that may limit its

On an operational level, an apparent gap in the quality of experience is observed across studies. While human staff
excel at the empathetic resolution of complex problems, evidence from the reviewed studies suggests that current
chatbots tend to generate dissatisfaction by delivering generic and repetitive responses. This functional limitation leads
consumers to prefer human interaction in situations of uncertainty to avoid the frustration of not being understood by
the technology.
Therefore, technology alone is ins ufficient to guarantee commercial success in today's market. For this reason,
evidence from the reviewed literature is consistent with the need for companies to consider an Ethical and Relational AI
strategy, where technology acts as a support for scalability, but never as a substitute for the human trust and authenticity
that underpin long-term loyalty.
7. Limitations and Future Research Directions
7.1. Study Limitations (PRISMA Phases)
• Identification (Search): The restriction to Scopus, WoS, and IEEE Xplore (Open Access only) and the exclusion
of Asian languages limit access to primary sources, which is critical given the identification of an "Eastern Hegemony"
with 53% of the research originating from Asia.
• Screening (Selection): The exclusion of theses and conference papers resulted in a sample where 88% are Q1/Q2
articles. This generates a "Positivism Bias," obscuring reports of failures or null results that are not typically published
in elite journals.
• Analysis: There is a "Novelty Effect" since 48% of the studies are from 2025, preventing the evaluation of long-
term sustainability. Furthermore, quantitative predominance (58%)  limits a deeper understanding of the user's
psychological resistance.
7.2. Future Research Directions
1. Longitudinal Post-Adoption Validation: Investigate whether retention is sustained over time, given that current
evidence is very recent (48% published in 2025) and may only reflect the initial fascination with the technology.
2. Impact of AI on Ethics and Transparency: Empirically study risk perception, as the Trust and Ethics category is
the least studied (only 7%) compared to the saturation of studies on operational efficiency.
3. Cross -Cultural Studies: Evaluate the acceptance of aggressive conversion tools in the West  to measure
"Strategic Decoupling," given that 53% of the technology analyzed comes from an Asian cultural context.
