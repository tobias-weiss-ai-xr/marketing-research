# &lt;p&gt;Decoding Consumer Responses to Personalized Marketing: An Empirical Study within the Stimulus–Organism–Response Framework&lt;/p&gt;

Decoding Consumer Responses to Personalized Marketing: An
Empirical Study within the Stimulus–Organism–Response Framework
Anirudh K. Murty   , Ashutosh Mohan
1. Institute of Management Studies, Banaras Hindu University, Varanasi, IND
Abstract
Personalized marketing is widely used to engage consumers in digital commerce environments, supported by advances
in artificial intelligence and data analytics that allow firms to create communication aligned with individual preferences.
At the same time, concerns around the handling of individuals’ personal information continue to grow, and the
psychological processes through which such communication aﬀects purchase intentions are not yet clearly understood.
This study uses the Stimulus-Organism-Response (S-O-R) framework to examine how personalized marketing messages
influence online purchase intentions through consumers’ internal states. Perceived personalization and consumer trust
are considered as mediators, while privacy concerns are examined as a moderator. A quantitative approach was adopted
using data from 195 online shoppers in India with prior exposure to personalized marketing. The proposed relationships
were tested using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling with SmartPLS 4. The results show that personalized
marketing messages significantly improve perceived personalization and trust, which in turn increase purchase
intentions. Both direct and indirect eﬀects are observed, indicating multiple pathways through which personalization
shapes consumer behavior, while the moderating role of privacy concerns is not significant. The study oﬀers a clearer
understanding of personalization eﬀects in digital settings and provides useful insights for designing eﬀective and
trustworthy marketing strategies, especially within developing markets such as India.

Introduction
The expansion of digital commerce has fundamentally changed how firms interact with consumers (Ratchford, 2009). The
rapid growth of digital commerce further underscores the importance of personalized marketing in contemporary
business environments. Recent global estimates project e-commerce sales to exceed USD 8 trillion by 2027, with a
significant share of this growth driven by data-driven and AI-enabled personalization strategies (Statista, 2025). In India,
the digital commerce market is also expanding rapidly due to increasing internet penetration, smartphone adoption, and
growing digital engagement across online platforms. As digital platforms become more integrated into consumers’ daily
lives, firms are increasingly relying on personalized marketing strategies to improve customer engagement and
strengthen online relationships. Studies indicate that more than 70% of consumers expect personalized experiences from
brands, while nearly 80% are more likely to purchase when exposed to personalized content and recommendations
(McKinsey and Company, 2021). These developments highlight the growing role of personalization in shaping consumer
behavior and influencing online purchase decisions. At the same time, concerns related to data privacy, security, and the
eﬀectiveness of personalization continue to shape consumer responses toward personalized marketing eﬀorts. Therefore,
understanding how personalized marketing influences consumer purchase intentions has become increasingly
important in contemporary digital environments.

Online platforms collect large volumes of behavioral data, enabling firms to move beyond traditional mass
communication and develop marketing messages tailored to individual users (Duch-Brown et al., 2017) (Fulgoni, 2018).
Consumers interact with brands across multiple digital touchpoints, including e-commerce websites, mobile apps, and
social media platforms. In these environments, marketing communication is increasingly shaped by browsing behavior,
prior purchases, and expressed preferences (Tam and Ho, 2006). As a result, standardized promotional messages are
becoming less eﬀective, while personalized communication is gaining importance for capturing attention and improving
relevance (Bleier et al., 2020). Firms rely on data analytics to determine the content, timing, and delivery of marketing
messages (Bhavsar and Tiwari, 2024). Personalization is therefore emerging as a key mechanism through which
organizations influence consumer responses and maintain relationships in digital marketplaces (Arora et al., 2008).
Advances in technologies such as artificial intelligence (AI), machine learning, big data analytics, and IoT (Internet of
Things) have strengthened firms’ ability to analyze consumer behavior and anticipate individual preferences
(Theodorakopoulos and Theodoropoulou, 2024). These technologies enable marketers to adjust communication in real
time, creating adaptive, context-specific marketing messages (Deligiannis et al., 2020). Through automated systems,
organizations can optimize not only who receives a message but also how it is delivered across digital platforms (An and
Ngo, 2025). However, technological capability alone does not guarantee eﬀectiveness, as consumer responses depend on
how personalization is perceived and interpreted.
Existing research suggests that personalized marketing influences purchase intentions through both cognitive and
aﬀective mechanisms. Personalized recommendation systems enhance perceived value and usefulness, which in turn
increase purchase willingness (Kim et al., 2024). High-quality personalization can also elicit positive emotional responses
that support persuasion and decision-making (Pappas et al., 2017). In addition, customized digital interfaces and tailored
content shape cognitive evaluations that guide consumer behavior (Bleier and Eisenbeiss, 2015). These findings indicate
that personalization operates through perceived relevance rather than mere exposure (Xia and Bechwati, 2008). The
Stimulus-Organism-Response (S-O-R) framework, proposed by Mehrabian and Russell (1974), provides a structured
theoretical basis for explaining how external stimuli influence internal psychological states, which subsequently guide
behavioral responses. In digital commerce contexts, personalized marketing messages serve as stimuli, while perceived
personalization and trust constitute organismic states that influence purchase intention. Prior studies applying the S-O-R
framework show that personalized messages enhance perceived relevance, satisfaction, and trust, which in turn increase
purchase likelihood (Pappas et al., 2017). At the same time, the eﬀectiveness of personalized marketing is shaped by
contextual factors. Perceived personalization and trust often mediate the relationship between personalized messages
and purchase intention, while privacy concerns influence how consumers interpret personalization eﬀorts (Xu et al.,
2011) (Awad and Krishnan, 2006). When personalization is perceived as intrusive or lacking transparency, consumers may
respond with skepticism or resistance. This perspective can also be understood through the lens of Privacy Calculus
Theory, which holds that consumers weigh the benefits of personalization against potential privacy risks when engaging
with digital platforms (Xu et al., 2011). 
Although prior studies confirm that personalized marketing influences purchase intentions through internal processes
(Pappas et al., 2017), the literature remains fragmented (Chandra et al., 2022). Existing research often examines perceived
personalization and trust separately or focuses primarily on direct eﬀects, without integrating these mechanisms into a
unified framework (Nobile and Cantoni, 2023). This limits the ability to fully understand how personalization translates
into behavioral outcomes in integrated models. In addition, privacy concerns are frequently discussed conceptually but
are less often tested as moderating factors in comprehensive empirical models (Awad and Krishnan, 2006) (Xu et al.,
2011). Evidence from emerging digital markets also remains limited (Bhavsar and Tiwari, 2024). To address these gaps,
this study proposes and empirically tests an integrated S-O-R-based model that examines how personalized marketing
messages influence online purchase intention through perceived personalization and trust, while incorporating privacy
concerns as a moderator. This study makes three specific contributions. First, it extends S-O-R-based personalization
research by integrating perceived personalization and trust into a unified framework, providing a more comprehensive
explanation of consumer responses (Pappas et al., 2014). Second, it examines both direct and mediated relationships,

Literature review
Research on personalized marketing, trust formation, privacy concerns, and online purchase behavior provides an
important foundation for understanding consumer responses in digital commerce environments (Chandra et al., 2022)
(Nobile and Cantoni, 2023). The Stimulus-Organism-Response framework oﬀers a structured basis for examining how
external marketing factors influence internal psychological states and subsequent behavioral responses (Mehrabian and
Russell, 1974). The framework posits that environmental stimuli shape cognitive and aﬀective evaluations, which in turn
guide approach or avoidance behaviors. Its ability to integrate evaluative and emotional processes has led to its
extensive use in consumer behavior and digital marketing research (Pappas et al., 2017). In online shopping contexts,
marketing communication, platform design, and digital interfaces serve as external stimuli that shape consumer
perceptions (Pappas et al., 2014). These perceptions, which represent organismic states, include constructs such as
perceived personalization and trust. Behavioral intentions, including purchase intention, constitute the response
component. Empirical research demonstrates that the Stimulus-Organism-Response framework eﬀectively explains how
online marketing environments influence consumer engagement and decision-making (Eroglu et al., 2001) (Pappas et al.,
2017) (Mehrabian and Russell, 1974). Recent studies further suggest that contextual variables, including privacy-related
concerns, influence how consumers interpret personalized digital communication (Xu et al., 2011) (Awad and Krishnan,
2006).
Personalized marketing messages are a central driver of contemporary digital commerce. These messages are generated
from information on individual preferences, browsing patterns, prior purchases, and contextual data (Tam and Ho, 2006)
(Arora et al., 2008). Recent advances in AI  and data analytics have enabled firms to deliver tailored content at scale,
making personalization a core strategic capability in online marketing (Bhavsar and Tiwari, 2024) (Deligiannis et al., 2020).
Empirical evidence indicates that personalized communication attracts more attention and is evaluated more positively
than non-personalized messages (An and Ngo, 2025). Research across online retail and digital advertising contexts shows
that personalization enhances perceived relevance, reduces information-processing eﬀort, and increases engagement
(Tam and Ho, 2006) (Sundar and Marathe, 2010). Recent research highlights that personalized marketing is increasingly
driven by AI and predictive analytics, enabling firms to deliver hyper-personalized experiences tailored to individual
consumer behavior (Kumar et al., 2019) (Davenport et al., 2020) (Huang and Rust, 2021). AI-driven personalization enables
real-time adaptation of marketing content, significantly enhancing customer engagement and conversion outcomes
compared with traditional approaches (Lemon and Verhoef, 2016). However, growing reliance on data-driven
personalization has also raised concerns about algorithmic bias, ethical data use, and consumer trust (Martin et al., 2017)
(Culnan and Bies, 2003). These technological developments further strengthen the role of personalized marketing as a
key element within the S-O-R framework.
Despite these advantages, consumers often evaluate personalization through a cost-benefit assessment of perceived
benefits and privacy risks, as explained by Privacy Calculus Theory (Dinev and Hart, 2006) (Awad and Krishnan, 2006). The
theory holds that individuals weigh the perceived benefits of sharing personal information against potential privacy risks
when engaging with digital platforms (Culnan and Armstrong, 1999). In personalized marketing, this trade-oﬀ shapes
how consumers interpret and respond to tailored communication. This perspective complements the S-O-R framework
by explaining how consumers cognitively evaluate personalized stimuli before forming behavioral responses. Within the
Stimulus-Organism-Response framework, personalized marketing messages prompt internal evaluations of relevance
and fit. When consumers perceive communication as tailored to their preferences, they are more likely to feel recognized
and aligned with the brand (Sundar and Kim, 2005) (Komiak and Benbasat, 2006) (Adomavicius et al., 2005). This internal
assessment is reflected in perceived personalization, defined as the extent to which consumers believe marketing
communication aligns with their individual needs and interests (Tam and Ho, 2006). Although prior studies emphasize
the importance of relevance in digital persuasion, fewer investigations integrate personalized marketing messages and
perceived personalization within a unified theoretical structure.

H1. Personalized marketing messages positively influence perceived personalization.
Personalized communication also serves as a relational signal. In digital environments marked by uncertainty and
information asymmetry, signals of attentiveness and competence help build trust. When marketing messages seem
relevant and appropriately tailored, consumers may view them as evidence that the firm understands their preferences
and can deliver satisfactory service. Trust in online retailers has consistently been identified as a key determinant of
purchasing behavior (Gefen et al., 2003). Research further indicates that non-intrusive personalization strengthens trust
perceptions by enhancing perceived competence and benevolence (Pappas et al., 2017). Positioning trust as an
organismic response allows examination of how personalized stimuli influence relational confidence. 
H2. Personalized marketing messages positively influence consumer trust.
Perceived personalization reflects consumers’ subjective evaluation of how well marketing messages align with their
personal interests and situational needs (Sundar and Marathe, 2010) (Xu et al., 2008). In online shopping environments,
perceived personalization enhances the usefulness of information and reduces decision eﬀort. Consumers exposed to
relevant, tailored content report greater engagement and higher satisfaction, which positively influence behavioral
intentions (Pappas et al., 2014) (Cavdar Aksoy et al., 2021). From a Stimulus-Organism-Response perspective, perceived
personalization serves as a cognitive mechanism that translates external stimuli into behavioral outcomes. Empirical
evidence supports a positive association between perceived personalization and purchase intention in digital retail
settings (Bhavsar and Tiwari, 2024) (Chandra et al., 2022). 
H3. Perceived personalization positively influences purchase intentions.
Trust is another critical psychological mechanism that influences online consumer behavior. In the absence of physical
interaction, trust reduces perceived uncertainty and increases confidence in transaction outcomes (Gefen et al., 2003)
(McKnight et al., 2002). Consumers who perceive an online platform as reliable and responsible are more likely to make
purchases. Within the Stimulus-Organism-Response structure, trust functions as an organismic state that facilitates the
translation of marketing stimuli into approach behavior. 
H4. Trust positively influences purchase intentions.
Although the Stimulus-Organism-Response framework emphasizes mediated pathways, some stimuli can also elicit
direct behavioral responses. In digital contexts, highly relevant, personalized cues delivered at decisive moments may
prompt immediate action without extensive cognitive processing. Evidence from online advertising research indicates
that personalized messages can directly increase click-through rates and purchasing behavior. This suggests that
personalization may influence behavior even without extensive cognitive processing.
H5. Personalized marketing messages positively influence purchase intentions.
Despite the advantages of personalization, concerns about data privacy set important boundary conditions. Privacy
concerns refer to individuals’ worries about the collection, storage, and use of their personal information. The
personalization-privacy paradox captures the tension between consumers’ desire for tailored experiences and their
concerns about losing control over personal data (Awad and Krishnan, 2006). Individuals with higher levels of privacy
concern may approach personalized communication with caution, potentially weakening relational trust and behavioral
intentions (Xu et al., 2011). Within the Stimulus-Organism-Response framework, privacy concerns serve as contextual
conditions that shape how consumers interpret personalized marketing stimuli and translate trust into action. This
underscores the conditional nature of personalization eﬀectiveness, as consumer sensitivity to privacy can alter how
marketing stimuli are interpreted. Empirical evidence suggests that privacy sensitivity can moderate the relationship
between trust and purchase intention in digital settings (Grimmer and Woolley, 2014) (Cavdar Aksoy et al., 2021).
Examining privacy concerns as a moderator rather than a direct predictor oﬀers a more nuanced understanding of
personalization eﬀectiveness.

H7. Privacy concerns moderate the relationship between trust and purchase intentions.

Research Method
Study framework and approach
This study adopted a quantitative, explanatory, cross-sectional research design to examine the relationships among
personalized marketing messages, perceived personalization, trust, privacy concerns, and purchase intention in online
shopping environments. A survey-based approach was employed to test theoretically derived relationships among
multiple latent constructs, consistent with prior research in digital marketing and consumer behavior (Hair et al., 2019)
(Sarstedt et al., 2022) (Bryman, 2016). The study was grounded in the Stimulus-Organism-Response (S-O-R) framework,
which conceptualizes personalized marketing messages as external stimuli that influence internal psychological states
and subsequent behavioral responses (Mehrabian and Russell, 1974). Accordingly, the research design was intended to
examine and test theoretically derived relationships among the study constructs rather than merely describe observed
patterns. The empirical context focuses on online shopping platforms in India, where consumers are regularly exposed to
AI-enabled personalization across e-commerce websites, mobile applications, and social media advertising. India

Sampling and data collection
Primary data were collected using a structured questionnaire administered via Google Forms. The survey was distributed
through digital channels, including WhatsApp, Twitter, LinkedIn, and email, to reach respondents with relevant online
shopping experience in India. A purposive sampling approach was used to ensure that participants had prior exposure to
personalized digital marketing messages. Initially, 205 responses were collected. After screening for incomplete
responses, patterned answers, and inconsistencies, responses that did not meet the study criteria were excluded. The
final dataset included 195 valid responses. The adequacy of the sample size is supported by the widely used “10-times
rule” (Hair et al., 2017) (Barclay et al., 1995), which recommends that the minimum sample size be at least ten times the
maximum number of structural paths directed at any construct. Based on this criterion, the sample size used in this study
is suﬃcient for PLS-SEM analysis. The use of purposive sampling is appropriate because the study focuses on respondents
with prior exposure to personalized marketing, ensuring that participants can meaningfully evaluate the constructs
under investigation. All subsequent analyses, including descriptive statistics, measurement model assessment, and
structural model evaluation, were conducted using the final dataset of 195 valid responses.

Measurement instruments
All constructs were measured using multi-item scales adapted from prior studies in the fields of personalized marketing,
online consumer behavior, and information systems research to ensure content validity and construct reliability
(Churchill, 1979) (DeVellis and Thorpe, 2021). The measurement items were modified to fit the context of online shopping
and personalized digital marketing. Personalized Marketing Messages (PMM) were measured using items adapted and
contextualized from the personalization literature, particularly Tam and Ho (Tam and Ho, 2006), Xu et al. (Xu et al., 2008),
and Pappas et al. (Pappas et al., 2017). These studies emphasize key dimensions of personalization, including content
relevance, tailored communication, personalized recommendations, and alignment with users’ interests and preferences
in digital environments. Accordingly, the PMM items used in this study assess the extent to which consumers perceive
online marketing messages, recommendations, and content as personalized, relevant, and aligned with their interests
and prior online behavior.
Perceived Personalization (PP) was assessed using scales adapted from Tam and Ho (Tam and Ho, 2006) and Pappas et al.
(Pappas et al., 2017). These items reflect consumers’ perceptions regarding the degree to which online platforms
recognize their individual preferences and provide personalized experiences based on prior interactions and behavioral
patterns. Trust (TR) was measured using items adapted from Gefen et al. (Gefen et al., 2003) and Pappas et al. (Pappas et
al., 2014). These scales have been widely used in e-commerce research to measure consumers’ confidence in online
platforms, reliability of personalized communication, and belief that firms handle personal information responsibly.
Privacy Concerns (PC) were measured using items adapted from Xu et al. (Xu et al., 2011) and Awad and Krishnan (Awad
and Krishnan, 2006). Xu et al. (Xu et al., 2011) examined the personalization-privacy paradox in location-aware marketing
and demonstrated how increasing personalization may simultaneously increase perceived benefits and privacy risks. The
items used in the present study assess consumers’ concerns regarding data collection, tracking, monitoring, and potential

Data screening and preliminary analysis
The dataset was screened for missing values, straight-lining responses, and unusual response patterns (Sarstedt et al.,
2022). Observations with identical responses across all items were removed. Reverse-coded indicators were checked and
recoded for consistency. Multicollinearity was assessed using Variance Inflation Factor (VIF) values, which remained
within acceptable limits, indicating that multicollinearity is not a concern (Hair et al., 2017) (Kock, 2015). Common
method bias was examined using both procedural and statistical methods. Respondents were assured of anonymity to
minimize response bias. Harman’s single-factor test showed that the first factor accounted for 28.601% of the total
variance, below the recommended threshold of 50% (Podsakoﬀ et al., 2003). These results suggest that common method
bias is not a serious issue in this study. Full collinearity VIF values were below the recommended threshold of 3.3, further
indicating that common method bias does not significantly aﬀect the results (Kock, 2015).
Analytical approach
The study used PLS-SEM in SmartPLS 4 for data analysis. This method is well suited for analyzing complex models with
multiple constructs and for explaining variation in key dependent variables, even with a relatively small sample size. PLS-
SEM is appropriate for this study because the model includes both mediation and moderation relationships, and the
method does not impose strict distributional assumptions. This makes it suitable for behavioral research settings where
data may not follow normal distributions.
The analysis was conducted in two stages. In the first stage, the measurement model was evaluated for indicator
reliability, internal consistency, convergent validity, and discriminant validity. In the second stage, the structural model
was assessed to test the hypothesized relationships, including path coeﬃcients, explanatory power (R²), eﬀect sizes,
predictive relevance, and moderation eﬀects.
Results And Discussion
Measurement model
The measurement model was evaluated to assess the reliability and validity of the constructs before proceeding to the
structural analysis. Indicator reliability was assessed using outer loadings, and most indicators exceeded the
recommended threshold of 0.70, indicating acceptable reliability (Hair et al., 2019), as shown in Figure 2. During
measurement model refinement, indicators with relatively low outer loadings were removed to improve construct
reliability and convergent validity while maintaining theoretical consistency. Specifically, PMM3, PP2, PP4, TR4, PC4, and
PI3 were excluded from the final measurement model because their outer loading values did not meet the
recommended criteria. Although two retained indicators (PC2 and PMM2) exhibited outer loadings below the
recommended threshold of 0.70, they were retained after careful evaluation. According to Hair et al. (2019), indicators
with outer loadings between 0.40 and 0.70 may be retained when their removal does not lead to a meaningful
improvement in Composite Reliability (CR) or Average Variance Extracted (AVE), provided they are theoretically justified.
In the present study, the PC construct achieved a Composite Reliability of 0.791 and an AVE of 0.564, while the PMM
construct achieved a CR of 0.811 and an AVE of 0.592, both exceeding the recommended thresholds. Therefore, the
inclusion of PC2 and PMM2 did not compromise the reliability or convergent validity of the measurement model, and
these indicators were retained for subsequent structural model analysis.

Hypothesis testing
The results indicate that most proposed relationships are statistically significant, with five out of seven hypotheses
supported. The structural model was evaluated using the bootstrapping procedure in SmartPLS 4, which generated
standardized path coeﬃcients (β), t-values, p-values, and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for all relationships. Path
coeﬃcients indicate the strength and direction of the relationships, while p-values confirm their statistical significance.
PMM have a strong positive eﬀect on PP (β = 0.584, t = 11.506, p < 0.001), supporting H1 and indicating that personalized
communication plays a central role in shaping how consumers evaluate the relevance of marketing messages. A positive
relationship is also observed between personalized marketing messages and TR  (β = 0.217, t = 4.177, p < 0.001),
supporting H2 and suggesting that personalization contributes to the development of trust in online platforms.
Perceived personalization has a significant positive eﬀect on PI  (β = 0.259, t = 4.088, p < 0.001), supporting H3 and
indicating that consumers are more likely to consider purchasing when marketing messages are perceived as relevant
and tailored. Trust also shows a positive relationship with purchase intention (β = 0.287, t = 3.886, p < 0.001), supporting
H4 and highlighting its role in reducing uncertainty in online transactions.
Alongside these indirect eﬀects, personalized marketing messages also have a direct eﬀect on purchase intention (β =
0.159, t = 2.676, p = 0.007), supporting H5 and indicating that personalization influences decision-making both directly
and through mediating variables. The moderating eﬀects of privacy concerns are not supported. The interaction between
privacy concerns and personalized marketing messages on trust is not significant (β = 0.019, t = 0.460, p = 0.645), leading
to the rejection of H6. The interaction between privacy concerns and trust on purchase intention is also not significant (β
= −0.023, t = 0.634, p = 0.526), leading to the rejection of H7. In addition, the confidence intervals for both moderating
eﬀects include zero, further confirming the absence of significant moderation eﬀects. These findings suggest that privacy
concerns did not significantly alter the relationships in the present sample, indicating that consumers responded to
personalized marketing in a relatively consistent manner across diﬀerent levels of privacy concern. The detailed

Discussion
This study examined how personalized marketing messages influence consumers' purchase intentions in online
shopping environments using the S-O-R framework. The findings provide empirical support for the proposed model and
oﬀer insights into the psychological mechanisms through which personalization shapes consumer behavior. The results
indicate that personalized marketing messages influence purchase intention through both indirect pathways involving
internal evaluations and a direct pathway reflecting an immediate behavioral response. The strong positive eﬀect of
personalized marketing messages on perceived personalization indicates that consumers actively evaluate the relevance
of marketing content. When communication aligns with prior behavior and individual preferences, it is perceived as
personally meaningful rather than generic, consistent with prior research (Tam and Ho, 2006) (Pappas et al., 2014). The
magnitude of this relationship highlights the importance of personalization quality in shaping consumers' evaluations of
digital marketing stimuli. Personalized marketing messages also show a significant positive eﬀect on trust, suggesting
that personalization functions as a relational signal in uncertain digital environments. When communication appears
tailored and appropriate, consumers may infer competence and responsiveness from the firm, which aligns with existing
research emphasizing the role of trust in online transactions (Gefen et al., 2003) (Pappas et al., 2017). The positive eﬀects
of perceived personalization and trust on purchase intention further confirm their role as key internal mechanisms within
the S-O-R framework, supporting earlier findings that relevance and trust reduce perceived risk and cognitive eﬀort,
thereby encouraging purchase behavior (Pappas et al., 2014) (Chandra et al., 2022).
The presence of a statistically significant but relatively modest direct eﬀect of personalized marketing messages on
purchase intention indicates partial mediation, suggesting that personalization operates through dual pathways. In one
pathway, consumers engage in deliberate evaluation through perceived personalization and trust, while in another,
highly relevant stimuli may trigger more immediate behavioral responses. This pattern aligns with dual-process
perspectives in consumer decision-making, in which both reflective and intuitive processes influence behavior (Evans,

2008). The model has substantial explanatory power, with 62.4% of the variance in purchase intention explained by the
proposed predictors, indicating that integrating personalization, perceived personalization, and trust provides a strong
framework for understanding consumer decision-making in digital environments. The moderating role of privacy
concerns is not supported, as the interaction eﬀects are statistically insignificant. This finding contrasts with prior
research on the personalization-privacy paradox, which suggests that privacy concerns weaken the eﬀectiveness of
personalization (Awad and Krishnan, 2006). Instead, the results indicate that personalization continues to influence
consumer responses even when privacy concerns are present. This pattern can be interpreted through Privacy Calculus
Theory, which explains how consumers evaluate the trade-oﬀ between perceived benefits and potential privacy risks (Xu
et al., 2011). The perceived benefits of relevance and convenience may outweigh concerns related to data usage,
consistent with recent findings (Nobile and Cantoni, 2023).
Theoretically, this study extends the S-O-R framework by showing that personalized marketing operates through both
mediated and direct pathways. While prior research has largely focused on internal psychological mechanisms, the
present findings show that personalized stimuli can also produce direct behavioral eﬀects. By integrating cognitive
(perceived personalization) and relational (trust) mechanisms within a single model, the study oﬀers a more
comprehensive explanation of how personalization influences consumer decision-making in digital environments. The
findings also support the relevance of combining the S-O-R framework with dual-process perspectives, oﬀering a more
nuanced understanding of consumer responses to personalization.
Implications
The findings of this study have both theoretical and practical implications for research on personalized marketing in
digital environments. The study strengthens the application of the S-O-R  framework by showing how personalized
marketing messages influence consumer behavior through both internal evaluations and direct behavioral responses
(Mehrabian and Russell, 1974) (Pappas et al., 2017). The results highlight perceived personalization and trust as key
mechanisms linking marketing stimuli to purchase intention. The presence of both direct and indirect eﬀects also
underscores the relevance of dual-process perspectives for explaining consumer decision-making. The findings also
contribute to the literature on the personalization-privacy relationship. The absence of significant moderation eﬀects
suggests that privacy concerns do not consistently undermine personalization outcomes. Instead, consumers appear to
evaluate personalization by weighing perceived benefits against potential risks, consistent with the logic of Privacy
Calculus Theory (Awad and Krishnan, 2006). This suggests that privacy concerns may operate in a more context-
dependent manner rather than acting as a uniform barrier.
From a practical perspective, the results underscore the importance of delivering meaningful, relevant personalization.
The strong link between personalized marketing messages and perceived personalization suggests that firms should
prioritize improving the quality and contextual relevance of communication rather than increasing the volume of
targeted content. The positive eﬀect of personalization on trust indicates that well-executed personalization can
strengthen consumer confidence in digital platforms (Gefen et al., 2003). This, in turn, requires transparency in how
personalization is implemented, including clear communication about data usage and recommendation processes. The
presence of both direct and indirect eﬀects suggests that personalized marketing can influence consumer decisions at
multiple stages of the decision process. Timely, relevant personalized stimuli may prompt quicker decisions in routine
contexts, whereas more complex decisions may rely more on perceived personalization and trust. For policymakers, the
findings underscore the need to balance personalization with consumer protection through transparent data practices,
informed consent, and accountability mechanisms.
Limitations and future research
Despite providing important insights into consumer responses toward personalized marketing, the present study has
certain limitations that should be acknowledged. First, the study adopted a cross-sectional research design, which
captures consumer perceptions at a single point in time. Consumer attitudes toward personalization and privacy may
evolve with technological advancements and changing digital experiences. Future studies may therefore adopt
longitudinal approaches to examine how responses toward personalized marketing change over time. Second, the study

was conducted in the Indian online shopping context using purposive sampling. Although this context is relevant due to
the rapid growth of digital commerce and AI-driven personalization, the findings may not be fully generalizable across
diﬀerent countries, cultures, or consumer groups. Future research may conduct cross-country comparative studies or
apply probability-based sampling approaches to improve generalizability.
The study also relied on self-reported questionnaire responses, which may be influenced by response bias or socially
desirable answers. Future studies may combine survey-based methods with behavioral or experimental data to better
examine actual consumer responses to personalized marketing practices. The study applied a prediction-oriented PLS-
SEM approach to examine mediation and moderation relationships within the proposed framework. Although this
method is appropriate for exploratory and prediction-focused research, future studies may compare the findings using
covariance-based structural equation modeling approaches to assess the stability of the observed relationships across
diﬀerent analytical techniques. The non-significant moderating eﬀects of privacy concerns suggest that additional
contextual variables may influence consumer responses toward personalized marketing. Future research may therefore
examine variables such as perceived transparency, personalization intrusiveness, digital literacy, algorithmic awareness,
or platform trust to develop a deeper understanding of how consumers interpret AI-driven personalization practices in
digital commerce environments.
Conclusions
This study examined the eﬀect of personalized marketing messages on consumers’ purchase intentions in online
shopping environments using the S-O-R framework. The findings show that personalized marketing influences consumer
behavior through both indirect and direct pathways. Perceived personalization and trust serve as key mechanisms
through which consumers interpret and respond to personalized communication, while the presence of a direct eﬀect
indicates that behavioral responses may also occur without extensive cognitive processing. The results underscore the
importance of relevance and trust in shaping consumer decisions in digital contexts. Consumers are more likely to make
purchases when marketing messages align with their preferences and when they perceive the platform as reliable. The
model demonstrates substantial explanatory and predictive power, indicating that integrating personalization, perceived
personalization, and trust provides a strong framework for understanding consumer behavior in online shopping
environments. The findings further indicate that privacy concerns do not significantly moderate the relationships
examined in the model. This suggests that consumers may continue to respond positively to personalized marketing
despite awareness of potential privacy risks, particularly when the perceived benefits of personalization outweigh these
concerns. The study contributes to the literature on personalized marketing by providing empirical evidence on the
mechanisms through which personalization influences consumer decision-making. It oﬀers a structured framework that
integrates cognitive evaluation, trust formation, and behavioral response into a single model. Future research can build
on this work by examining additional contextual factors, such as product categories, cultural settings, or levels of
consumer involvement. Longitudinal approaches may also oﬀer deeper insights into how consumers' responses to
personalization evolve over time.
