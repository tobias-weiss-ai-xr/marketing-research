ABSTRACT: The present review is a systematic literature review -cum-bibliometric analysis of the applications of
analytics in social commerce. Through this review, the authors have tried to find the dominant themes in the field, its
most relevant authors  and sources, and how  research on the topic has grown over time. The data for the review is
procured from Elsevier’s Scopus database using the keywords “Social Commerce AND Analytics”. The final database
used for this review comprises 415 documents published between 2016 and 2026. It was found that social media
platforms, influencer marketing and customer engagement were the most dominant themes, while artificial
intelligence, chatbots and natural language processing remain less developed.

KEYWORDS: Social Commerce; Analytics

1. INTRODUCTION

Buying has always been a social act. Long before the internet, people asked friends, family and fellow customers for
advice before they spent their money, and the internet has simply widened that circle. Social commerce refers to the
delivery of e-commerce activities and transactions through the social media environment, using Web 2.0 technologies
to support online interactions and user contributions that help people acquire products and services (Liang & Turban,
2011). It brings together two spaces that were once separate: the social networks where people talk, share and
recommend, and the commercial platforms where they buy (Huang & Benyoucef, 2013).

Every interaction on these platforms, whether a like, a share, a review or a product search, leaves behind data. Big data
analytics covers a range of data -intensive approaches capable of analysing such large -scale data, and it adds
considerable value to decision -making (Khanra  et al., 2020). Within this literature, social media analytics and
sentiment analysis form a distinct theme centred on targeting consumers, alongside marketing analytics and predictive
analytics (Khanra et al., 2020). In social commerce, analytics is used to personalise recommendations and content
(Chandra et al., 2022), to understand the effect of social media influencers, who are now a significant element of the
marketing mix (Kim et al., 2021), and to detect spam reviews written to promote or demote produ cts (Hussain et al.,
2019).

Research on social commerce has grown alongside the market, and earlier reviews have helped organise it. Zhang and
Benyoucef (2016) reviewed the literature on consumer behaviour in social commerce, while Busalim and Hussin
(2016) conducted a systematic literature review of  the field and set out directions for further research. These reviews,
however, were published a decade ago and do not focus specifically on how analytics is applied in social commerce. The
literature on this subject is also spread across Computer Science, Engineering, Business, Management  and

2. LITERATURE REVIEW

The literature on social commerce has been reviewed, summarized, and analyzed using previously conducted
bibliometric reviews. Social commerce is the blend of social media and e -commerce. Essentially, social commerce
allows for consumers to search for, discuss, and purchase products on platforms connected socially. The approach
contrasts with traditional e -commerce which  focuses on sellers, buyers, products, and websites and adds a social
element where consumers share experiences about products, sellers, and each other (Wang & Zhang, 2012). According
to reviewers, many publications have been written on consumer behavior, adoption, trust, and engagement (Busalim &
Hussin, 2016; Zhang & Benyoucef, 2016; Ng, 2018) and the field itself is multidimensional/intersdisciplinary
covering areas such as marketing, consumer  psychology, information systems, and management (Busalim &  Hussin,
2016). The earliest paper that was included in the analysis is the work of Wang and Zhang (2012) while the latest
papers looked at more specialized cases like community group buying and visual social media platforms (Tingli et al.,
2024; Wang & Xin, 2026). The results of the latest bibliometric analysis are based on the evaluation of the findings
published in Web Science  only until 2022 (Herzallah et al., 2025) which proves the need for an updated summary of

3. RESEARCH OBJECTIVE

The objective of this review is to describe how analytics is being used in social commerce research  by analysing
publication trends, the most productive authors and journals, and the key themes. This may be achieved by setting
smaller goals as follows:
• To find the trend of publishing the research work related to analytics in social commerce from 2016 to
2026 in years, journals, and authors.
• To find out the most relevant authors and journals that publish articles of this study area.
• To identify key themes prevalent in the field and how its keywords are interconnected.
• To analyse if the earlier works are biased towards certain platforms, topics, or technologies.
• To identify gaps in the research and suggest future research avenues.

4. RESEARCH METHODOLOGY
This study adopts a systematic literature review (SLR) design combined with bibliometric analysis. An SLR follows a
predefined, transparent and replicable protocol for identifying, selecting and evaluating the literature on a topic, which
reduces researcher bias in the choice of studies (  Martina K Linnenluecke et al., 2020). Bibliometric reviews are
themselves recognised as one form of SLR (Paul et al., 2021). In this study, the systematic component governs how
documents were searched, screened and selected, reported using the PRISMA framework (Moher et al., 2010), while
the bibliometric component is used to analyse the resulting body of work.
Bibliometric analysis is a popular and rigorous method for exploring and analysing large volumes  of scientific data,
and it allows researchers to trace how a field has evolved and where it is heading (Donthu et al., 2021). It draws on two
sets of techniques: performance analysis, which  accounts for the contributions of authors, journals, institutions and
countries, and science mapping, which examines the relationships between them through citation, co -citation,
bibliographic coupling, co-word and co-authorship analysis (Donthu et al., 2021). As these measures are quantitative,
they bring a degree of objectivity to the evaluation of a body of literature (Zupic & Čater, 2015). To keep the review
replicable and transparent, the search terms, database and filtering criteria were defined and documented before the
data was analysed ( Martina K Linnenluecke et al., 2020).
Data was retrieved from Elsevier's Scopus database, which, along with Web of Science, is generally  accepted as one
of the two most comprehensive bibliographic databases (Pranckutė, 2021). The search was run on 10th September 2026
using the query "Social Commerce AND Analytics" across article titles, abstracts and keywords.
The selection of documents followed the four phases of the PRISMA flow diagram: identification, screening,
eligibility and inclusion (Moher et al., 2010). The search identified 1,451  documents. In the screening stage, 131
documents published before 2016 were removed, leaving 1,320. A further 900 book chapters, conference papers,
conference reviews and proceedings were excluded so that only peer -reviewed articles and review papers remained,
which brought the count to 420. In the eligibility stage, 2 non -English documents were removed, leaving 418, and 3
documents not published in journals were excluded. The final sample  for the review therefore consists of 415
documents. Figure 1 shows the search, inclusion and exclusion flow chart.
The records of the 415 documents were  exported from Scopus in RIS format to Mendeley for reference management,
and in BibTeX format for the bibliometric analysis. The analysis was carried  out in Biblioshiny, the web interface of
the bibliometrix R package, an open-source tool for comprehensive science mapping (Aria & Cuccurullo, 2017).

5. DATA ANALYSIS AND RESULTS

This study examines the structure and development of research on social media, digital marketing and related
technologies through seven bibliometric perspectives: (1) the thematic map  and strategic positioning of research
themes; (2) the co-occurrence network of keywords;
(3) authors’ production over time; (4) the most relevant authors; (5) the evolution of publication sources; (6) the most
relevant sources; and (7) annual scientific production. Taken together, these perspectives identify the themes that are
most developed and central to the field, the topics  that remain specialised or emerging, the main intellectual
connections among keywords, and the growth of the literature over time.

The Thematic Map
Figure 2: Thematic Map

Figure 2 presents a strategic diagram in which the horizontal axis represents relevance or centrality, while the vertical
axis represents development or density. The four quadrants distinguish motor themes, niche themes, basic themes, and
emerging or declining themes. Larger  bubbles indicate themes with greater keyword occurrence and therefore greater
research attention.

The motor -theme quadrant is led by social -media platforms and digital engagement topics. TikTok, Instagram and
Facebook form a prominent, highly developed social-media cluster, while influencer marketing, customer engagement
and virtual influencers also occupy the upper -right area. This indicates that platform -based engagement and
influencer-led communication are both well-developed and strongly connected to the broader research field.
Digital transformation, technology and transparency are also positioned close to the centrality boundary, showing their
growing importance in the wider discussion.

The niche-theme quadrant contains social media analytics, disaster management and machine learning (ML), together
with system dynamics and value co-creation. These topics show comparatively high development but lower centrality,
suggesting specialised research streams that have developed internally without becoming as broadly connected as the
dominant social-media and engagement themes.

Integrated Findings
The bibliometric analysis shows a growing and diversified number of publications, in terms of themes, authors and
publication sources, in this field. The growth rate for scientific publications per year increased from approximately 10 in
2016 to 82 publications in 2026. Although the number of publications decreased slightly in 2021, the number has
increased steadily since then, suggesting that the field is becoming more popular among academics at this time.

The themes that are most prevalent in the motor category in the thematic maps are social media platforms, influencer
marketing, customer engagement, virtual influencers, social -commerce, digital marketing and consumer behaviour.
Platform communications (TikTok, Instagram and Facebook) reflect the increasing importance of platform
communication, and influencer marketing and Consumer engagement highlight consumer interaction. Technology and
transparency are also becoming more and more associated with the wider research landscape and the digital
transformation.

6. FUTURE RESEARCH AGENDA

The bibliometric study indicates that the emerging discipline represents a rapidly developing, technology -driven area
based on social media and digital marketing/consumer engagement. Yearly  publication rate increased from about 10
papers in 2016 to some 82 papers in 2026, and the thematic map, keywords’ network, authors’ patterns  and sources'
patterns demonstrate the strengths and weaknesses of this discipline in terms of development. Social media, influencer
marketing and customer engagement have been developed and are central topics; social commerce,  digital marketing
and consumer behavior are central but not very developed topics; artificial intelligence an d related technologies are
peripheral topics. This situation suggests several areas for further investigation as follows.

Developing the Basic Themes Further
Digital marketing, social commerce and consumer behavior are located in the basic theme cluster. Their high
centrality means that these concepts are significant components of the academic field, while their low density indicates
that they are still being shaped from multiple perspectives. Further research should be conducted in order to develop
theory-based integrated models explaining the way in which consumers move from platform usage to purchase
decisions and loyalty, rather than treat them as general background for the technical or descriptive research. Examples
of such research questions could be: what are the engagement behaviors (e.g., likes, shares, comments, follows) that
best predict purchase on social media platforms,  how much trust in influencers and brands transfers into purchase
behavior and how does that transfer depend on consumer group? The inclusion of platform data together with
surveys/experiments will let the researchers not only observe but also test the correlations..

Bringing AI, Chatbots, Big Data and NLP into the Core
AI, chatbots, big data and NLP are present in either the emerging or declining zone of the map. It must be noted that the
map itself does not give any information about whether the emerging or declining zone is the right one – it can say
only that these themes have now become less important compared to others. Future studies should track the
development of these themes over  time (for example, by using thematic evolution analysis) and test their applicability
within consumer research. Relevant topics may include influence of chatbots, personalization by means of AI and
NLP analysis of user-generated content on engagement, trust and buying behavior of consumers, as well as consumer
reactions to AI application in marketing communication. Technology and transparency are located close to the zone of
centrality and further research on transparent and explainable AI in marketing is natural.

Connecting Niche Streams to the Core
Social media analytics, machine learning, disaster management, system dynamics, and value co -creation are in the
niche quadrant as they are well established internally but are not so important  in the field. The co-occurrence network
also demonstrates the same trend, where the purple and orange clusters are separate from the central cluster. Further
research is required to integrate these topics into the core streams. Social media analytics and machine learning can be
used for analysis of outcomes like engagement and social commerce results, such as conversion  and retention. Value
co-creation can be researched as a process that is associated with brands, influencers, and consumers. Disaster
management and system dynamics are more specific areas, and further investigation on their connection to the social
commerce topic is required prior to including them into the core.

Comparing Platforms and Influencer Formats
TikTok, Instagram, and Facebook represent the most visible social -media cluster, and influencer marketing, customer
engagement, and virtual influencers are also part of the motor quadrant, while Twitter is located in another basic cluster
along with social media and machine learning. Such positioning demonstrates that platform -specific research is quite
popular, although cross-platforms comparisons are not as frequent. In future studies, one can focus on how  the format
of content, audience, and characteristics of the platform affect engagement and purchase behavior, for instance, short
videos vs. images vs. textual platforms. Another important  topic is virtual influencers, who represent a relatively new
phenomenon of influencer marketing. It is worth exploring how consumers perceive credibility of such influencers and
how the information about their artificiality influences their perceptions. Such themes are relevant for  the current
discussion of future research on the marketing in the age of social and digital media (Appel et al., 2020; Dwivedi et al.,
2021). At the same time, the issue of influencer marketing is rather well -researched (Abhishek & Srivastava, 2021;
Tanwar et al., 2021).

Linking to Sustainability and Business Performance
Most relevant is Sustainability (Switzerland) with 9 documents, demonstrating the most recent growth, while the source
analysis suggests the trend of switching to sustainability, open-access, science and operations management journals.

Methodological Note
The results for 2026 should be treated with caution because the data may have been extracted before  the end of the

where possible, add a thematic evolution analysis so that the movement of themes between quadrants can be seen
directly. Even so, the pattern is  consistent: the field has grown quickly, and the main opportunity is to connect its
technology-driven, platform-driven and consumer-driven strands.

7. CONCLUSION AND LIMITATIONS

This study intends to be a useful piece of work for faculty members, researchers and students of  operations research,
and for anyone who wants to understand how analytics is used in social commerce. It shows that research in this field
has grown quickly, from about 10 articles in 2016 to 82 articles in 2026. Most of this research focuses on social media
platforms such as TikTok, Instagram and Facebook, influencer marketing and customer engagement. Analytics tools
such as artificial intelligence, chatbots, big data and natural language processing (NLP) are still at the edges of the
field.
As more people discover, discuss and buy products on social media, businesses are  collecting more data than ever on
what customers like, share and buy. More research is needed on how this data can be used, especially through AI,
chatbots and NLP, so that businesses can make better decisions and customers can trust the platforms they buy from.
Therefore, there is a huge scope for further research in this field. The main limitation of this study is that it uses only
one database, Elsevier’s Scopus, and one search term, “Social Commerce AND Analytics”, which limits the number
of documents that could be analysed. Conference papers and non -English articles were also left out, and the data for
2026 covers only part of the year.
