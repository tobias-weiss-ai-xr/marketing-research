Abstract
Background: Artificial intelligence (AI) is playing an increasingly important
role in supply chain management by improving demand-forecasting accuracy
and supporting better inventory decisions. Traditional forecasting techniques
can struggle to accommodate demand uncertainty, which may lead to stockouts,
overstocking, and higher operating costs. AI-powered forecasting models and
intelligent inventory-optimization technologies can help organizations improve
efficiency, reduce waste, and become more resilient to supply chain disruptions.
However, empirical evidence linking forecasting capability to cost outcomes
through inventory decisions remains limited.
Aim: This study explores the relationships among AI-driven demand
forecasting, inventory optimization, supply chain efficiency, and operational
cost reduction. It also examines whether inventory optimization acts as a bridge
between AI-driven forecasting and operational cost reduction.
Method: This study used a quantitative, cross-sectional explanatory survey. A
structured questionnaire was administered to 300 supply chain respondents
from manufacturing (n = 156) and retail (n = 144) organizations, including
supply chain professionals, logistics managers, inventory managers, and
procurement specialists. Data were analyzed using descriptive statistics and
PLS-SEM with bootstrapping to test the relationships among AI-driven demand
forecasting, inventory optimization, supply chain efficiency, and operational
cost reduction, with inventory optimization examined as a mediator.
Results: Respondents reported a 29.4% improvement in forecasting accuracy,
a 22.7% decrease in inventory holding costs, and a 31.5% decrease in stockout
incidents. Overall supply chain efficiency increased by 27.9%; 84.2% of
respondents agreed that AI-based forecasting enhanced inventory planning and
decision-making, and operational costs decreased by 19.8%. In the PLS-SEM
model, AI-driven demand forecasting was positively associated with inventory
optimization (β = 0.71, p < 0.001) and supply chain efficiency (β = 0.63, p <
0.001). Inventory optimization was positively associated with supply chain
efficiency (β = 0.58, p < 0.001) and operational cost reduction (β = 0.34, p <
0.001). Inventory optimization partially mediated the relationship between AI-
driven demand forecasting and operational cost reduction (indirect effect =
0.24).
Conclusion: AI-driven demand forecasting and inventory optimization were
significantly and positively associated with improved supply chain
performance, including better forecasting, more effective inventory utilization,
and lower operational costs. Given the cross-sectional design and perceptual
measures, these findings should be interpreted as associations rather than
evidence of cause and effect.
Keywords: Artificial Intelligence; Demand Forecasting; Inventory
Optimization; Supply Chain Management; Operational Cost Reduction

COVID-19 pandemic demonstrated how changes in requirements and availability at one organization can
propagate through interconnected supply networks (Ivanov, 2020; Ivanov & Dolgui, 2020). The flow of
information from one stage of a supply chain to another can also contribute to demand volatility. When each
stage forecasts demand based on orders from the preceding stage, fluctuations can become progressively
amplified upstream, a phenomenon known as the bullwhip effect (Lee et al., 1997).
Forecast quality is important if forecasts are to improve inventory decisions. When forecasts are too
low, firms risk stockouts, lost sales, and reduced service levels. When forecasts are too high, firms may carry
excess inventory, tying up working capital and increasing storage, handling, and obsolescence costs. Classical
inventory theory suggests that the safety inventory required to achieve a target service level increases with
forecast error and lead-time variability (Silver et al., 2017). Empirical retail studies have linked inventory
turnover to differences between actual and predicted sales (Gaur et al., 2005), while research on retail
forecasting highlights the difficulty and importance of obtaining accurate product-level forecasts for
replenishment (Fildes et al., 2022). Traditional forecasting techniques, which often rely on relatively
straightforward statistical models, may fall short when demand is intermittent, affected by many external
factors, or strongly influenced by promotions.
Artificial intelligence (AI) has therefore attracted substantial attention in supply chain management.
AI, particularly machine learning, is viewed as a potential solution to complex planning challenges such as
forecasting, inventory management, and logistics decision-making (Baryannis et al., 2019; Min, 2010; Riahi
et al., 2021; Toorajipour et al., 2021). AI-based demand forecasting uses machine-learning and related
modeling techniques to identify patterns in large and diverse datasets, including sales, prices, promotions, and
supply chain information, and to update those patterns as new data become available. Empirical applications
have shown benefits in some settings. Feizabadi (2022) reported statistically significant supply chain
performance improvements from machine-learning-based forecasting in a steel-manufacturing case study,
while Zhu et al. (2021) found improved forecast quality when comparing machine-learning and traditional
models in a pharmaceutical supply chain. However, the superiority of AI is not guaranteed. Machine-learning
methods did not clearly outperform statistical counterparts in the M4 competition, whereas they performed
more strongly in the retail-sales-focused M5 competition (Makridakis et al., 2020, 2022).
Better forecasts add value only when they lead to better decisions. AI-enabled inventory optimization
applies data-driven and learning-based techniques to determine order quantities, safety stocks, and other
replenishment decisions. Recent studies suggest that inventory decisions can be learned directly from data
and demand-related features rather than solely from a demand distribution estimated in a separate step (Ban
& Rudin, 2019; Bertsimas & Kallus, 2020; Oroojlooyjadid et al., 2020). Research on deep reinforcement
learning for inventory control also shows promising, although not yet conclusive, results (Boute et al., 2022;
Gijsbrechts et al., 2022).
Better forecasting and inventory decision-making can potentially affect two organizational outcomes.
Supply chain efficiency refers to a supply chain's ability to use resources effectively, including inventory,
capacity, and time, while providing the required service level to customers. Operational cost reduction refers
to decreases in inventory-related ordering, holding, handling, and expediting costs. AI-powered decision-
making may also improve responsiveness and resilience by enabling organizations to respond more quickly
to shifts in demand. Empirical evidence has linked AI and analytics capabilities to resilience and operational
performance (Belhadi et al., 2024; Chae et al., 2014; Dubey et al., 2020, 2021).
1.2 Research Problem and Gap
Although interest in AI-based forecasting is high, three gaps remain.
First, much of the forecasting literature evaluates accuracy. Competitions and algorithm comparisons
rank methods by error measures (Makridakis et al., 2020, 2022), but forecast accuracy is not in itself an
organizational outcome. Fewer studies examine whether organizations that rely on AI-driven forecasting also
report better inventory decisions, higher supply chain efficiency, and lower costs.
Second, the mechanism is not well established. Forecasting research and inventory-control research
have developed largely as separate streams. If AI-driven forecasting is related to costs, one plausible route is

1.3 Rationale
Examining AI-driven demand forecasting together with inventory optimization provides a more
comprehensive view of how forecasting capability may translate into operational outcomes. Supply chain
efficiency and operational cost reduction indicate whether these potential benefits are realized through
improved resource utilization, lower costs, or both. The mediation test examines whether inventory
optimization represents an intermediate mechanism linking AI-driven forecasting to reduced operational
costs. The findings may help managers make better-informed investments in forecasting technology and
identify the inventory-planning changes needed to realize its value. The study also contributes to research on
information processing, resources, and dynamic capabilities in supply chains (Teece, 2007; Tushman &
Nadler, 1978).
1.4 Research Questions
1. To what extent is AI-driven demand forecasting associated with inventory optimization?
2. How is AI-driven demand forecasting related to supply chain efficiency?
3. How is inventory optimization related to supply chain efficiency?
4. To what extent is inventory optimization associated with operational cost reduction?
5. Does inventory optimization mediate the relationship between AI-driven demand forecasting and
operational cost reduction?
1.5 Objectives
 To measure the level of AI-driven demand forecasting and inventory optimization among the
participating organizations.
 To examine the relationship between AI-driven demand forecasting and inventory optimization.
 To analyze the relationship between AI-driven demand forecasting and supply chain efficiency.
 To examine the relationship between inventory optimization and operational cost reduction.
 To determine whether inventory optimization mediates the relationship between AI-driven demand
forecasting and operational cost reduction.
1.6 Hypotheses
 H1: AI-driven demand forecasting has a significant positive effect on inventory optimization.
 H2: AI-driven demand forecasting has a significant positive effect on supply chain efficiency.
 H3: Inventory optimization has a significant positive effect on supply chain efficiency.
 H4: Inventory optimization has a significant positive effect on operational cost reduction.
 H5: Inventory optimization significantly mediates the relationship between AI-driven demand
forecasting and operational cost reduction.

processing capacity by turning large volumes of data into timely demand signals. This logic supports H1,

resource use and lower costs (H3 to H5).

3. Materials and Methods
3.1 Research Design and Participants
This study used a quantitative, cross-sectional explanatory design to examine the relationships among
AI-driven demand forecasting, inventory optimization, supply chain efficiency, and operational cost
reduction. Primary data were collected using a structured questionnaire from 300 respondents working in
manufacturing and retail organizations, with the individual supply chain professional as the unit of analysis.
The sample comprised 75 supply chain professionals (25.0%), 78 logistics managers (26.0%), 72 inventory
managers (24.0%), and 75 procurement specialists (25.0%), all of whom had responsibilities directly related
to demand planning, inventory management, procurement, logistics, replenishment, or supply chain
operations. By sector, 156 respondents (52.0%) worked in manufacturing organizations and 144 (48.0%) in

3.2 Measures
The questionnaire measured four constructs. AI-driven demand forecasting was assessed through
respondents' perceptions of the use of artificial intelligence and data-driven forecasting technologies, the
integration of relevant demand information, the ability to identify changing demand patterns, and the
improvement of forecasting decisions. Inventory optimization covered inventory-level management,
replenishment decisions, safety-stock management, product availability, and control of inventory-related
costs. Supply chain efficiency covered resource utilization, inventory management, order fulfillment,
operational responsiveness, and the overall effectiveness of supply chain activities. Operational cost reduction
covered reductions in inventory holding, ordering, handling, and other supply chain operating costs. The
questionnaire also included operational indicators of perceived change (forecasting accuracy, inventory
holding costs, stockout incidents, overall supply chain efficiency, and operational costs) and an item asking
whether respondents agreed that AI-based forecasting enhanced inventory planning and decision-making.
These percentages are respondents' perceptions, not independently audited organizational measurements.
3.3 Data Analysis
Data were analyzed using descriptive statistics and PLS-SEM (Hair et al., 2019, 2022) to estimate the
relationships among the constructs and the proposed mediation. The measurement model was evaluated for
indicator reliability, internal consistency reliability, convergent validity, and discriminant validity. Indicator
loadings, Cronbach's alpha, and composite reliability values of approximately 0.70 or higher, and average
variance extracted (AVE) values of 0.50 or higher, were treated as acceptable. Discriminant validity was
assessed using the Fornell-Larcker criterion (Fornell & Larcker, 1981) and the heterotrait-monotrait (HTMT)
ratio (Henseler et al., 2015). The structural model was evaluated using standardized path coefficients (β),
bootstrapped significance tests, and coefficients of determination (R²), with p < 0.05 as the significance
criterion. Mediation was examined by estimating the indirect effect of AI-driven demand forecasting on
operational cost reduction through inventory optimization.

5. Discussion
5.1 Summary of Main Findings
The results provide a coherent pattern linking AI-driven demand forecasting, inventory optimization,
supply chain efficiency, and operational cost reduction. AI-driven demand forecasting was positively and
significantly associated with inventory optimization (β = 0.71, p < 0.001) and supply chain efficiency (β =
0.63, p < 0.001). Inventory optimization was also positively and significantly associated with supply chain
efficiency (β = 0.58, p < 0.001) and operational cost reduction (β = 0.34, p < 0.001). The model explained
50% of the variance in inventory optimization, 62% of the variance in supply chain efficiency, and 41% of
the variance in operational cost reduction. In addition, inventory optimization partially mediated the
relationship between AI-driven demand forecasting and operational cost reduction: the indirect effect was
0.24 with a 95% confidence interval of [0.17, 0.32], while the remaining direct effect was 0.29 (p < 0.001).
These results support all five hypotheses and indicate that forecasting capability and inventory decision
quality operate as related, but distinct, components of supply chain performance.
The descriptive findings reinforce the structural results. Respondents reported a 29.4% improvement
in forecasting accuracy, a 22.7% decrease in inventory holding costs, a 31.5% decrease in stockout incidents,
a 27.9% increase in overall supply chain efficiency, and a 19.8% decrease in operational costs; 84.2% also
agreed that AI-based forecasting enhanced inventory planning and decision-making. Taken together, the
findings suggest that the organizational value of AI forecasting is not limited to prediction accuracy. Its value
appears to be strongest when demand information is translated into concrete inventory decisions and broader
operational coordination. At the same time, these percentages are perceptual reports rather than audited
organizational measures, and the cross-sectional design supports interpretation in terms of association rather
than cause and effect.
5.2 AI-Driven Demand Forecasting and Inventory Optimization
The association between AI-driven demand forecasting and inventory optimization was the
numerically largest path in the model (β = 0.71, p < 0.001). This finding is consistent with organizational
information processing theory, which argues that organizations facing uncertainty require information-
processing capacity that matches the complexity of their decision environment (Galbraith, 1974; Tushman &
Nadler, 1978). Demand forecasting based on richer and more timely information can reduce uncertainty
around expected sales patterns, promotions, and changes in market conditions. In inventory terms, improved
demand information can support more appropriate safety-stock levels, reorder points, and replenishment
timing. This interpretation is also consistent with inventory theory, in which forecast error and lead-time
variability are central inputs to stock decisions (Silver et al., 2017).
The result also aligns with prior evidence showing that machine-learning forecasting can improve
forecasting and supply chain performance in particular organizational settings (Feizabadi, 2022; Zhu et al.,
2021), as well as research that directly links predictive information to inventory decisions (Ban & Rudin,
2019; Bertsimas & Kallus, 2020). The 84.2% agreement that AI-based forecasting enhanced inventory
planning provides additional descriptive support for this link. However, the finding should not be interpreted
to mean that any AI method will automatically produce superior inventory outcomes. Forecasting
competitions have shown that machine-learning methods do not consistently outperform well-designed
statistical or hybrid approaches across all data environments (Makridakis et al., 2020, 2022). The practical
implication is therefore not simply to adopt AI, but to ensure that forecasting systems generate reliable signals
that can be incorporated into inventory policies and reviewed against existing planning methods.
5.3 AI-Driven Demand Forecasting and Supply Chain Efficiency
AI-driven demand forecasting was positively associated with supply chain efficiency (β = 0.63, p <
0.001), supporting H2. From the resource-based view, this relationship suggests that forecasting capability
can function as an organizational resource when it helps firms allocate inventory, capacity, time, and
managerial attention more effectively (Barney, 1991; Chae et al., 2014). The result is consistent with research

5.4 Inventory Optimization and Supply Chain Efficiency
Inventory optimization was positively and significantly associated with supply chain efficiency (β =
0.58, p < 0.001), supporting H3. This result is important because it identifies inventory decisions as an
operational mechanism through which demand information can be converted into improved resource use.
Dynamic capabilities theory provides a useful explanation: forecasting supports the sensing of demand
changes, whereas inventory optimization represents the reconfiguration of stock levels, safety stock, and
replenishment policies in response to those signals (Teece, 2007; Teece et al., 1997). The finding therefore
shifts attention from information generation to information use. An accurate forecast has limited operational
value if purchasing, replenishment, or stock-allocation decisions are not adjusted accordingly.
The result also supports the view that inventory performance should not be equated with simply
holding less stock. Inventory that is too low can increase stockout risk, while inventory that is too high ties
up working capital and raises handling and obsolescence costs. Prior evidence likewise suggests that the
inventory-performance relationship depends on how well inventory is matched to operating requirements
(Eroglu & Hofer, 2011). In this study, the simultaneous findings of fewer reported stockouts and lower
reported holding costs are consistent with the idea of better alignment rather than indiscriminate inventory
reduction.
5.5 Inventory Optimization and Operational Cost Reduction
Inventory optimization was positively and significantly associated with operational cost reduction (β
= 0.34, p < 0.001), supporting H4. This relationship is theoretically plausible because stock levels and
replenishment decisions affect holding, ordering, handling, expediting, and shortage-related costs (Silver et
al., 2017). The reported 22.7% decrease in inventory holding costs and 19.8% decrease in overall operational
costs are directionally consistent with the structural result. The coefficient for this path was numerically
smaller than the other direct paths in the model, but no formal coefficient-comparison test was conducted;
therefore, the difference should not be interpreted as statistically established. One possible explanation for the
more modest coefficient is that operational costs are influenced by a broader set of factors than inventory
decisions alone, including labor, transportation, production, supplier conditions, and facility utilization. These
factors were not modeled here. The result nevertheless indicates that inventory optimization is meaningfully
associated with cost outcomes while also highlighting that cost reduction is likely to require coordinated
improvements across several operating functions.
5.6 The Mediating Role of Inventory Optimization
The mediation analysis provides the central mechanism tested in this study. AI-driven demand
forecasting was associated with inventory optimization through path a (0.71, p < 0.001), and inventory
optimization was associated with operational cost reduction through path b (0.34, p < 0.001). Their product
yielded an indirect effect of 0.24, with a bootstrapped 95% confidence interval of [0.17, 0.32]. The direct
effect of AI-driven demand forecasting on operational cost reduction remained significant at 0.29 (p < 0.001),
indicating partial mediation. This pattern supports the argument that part of the forecasting-cost relationship

From a dynamic capabilities perspective, the mediation result links sensing and reconfiguration: AI-

sequence. Establishing causal mediation would require stronger temporal designs and preferably objective
operational data.
5.7 Theoretical Implications
The study contributes to the supply chain and AI literature in three related ways. First, it extends
organizational information processing theory by showing that stronger AI-driven forecasting is associated not
only with better perceived information outcomes, but also with the quality of inventory decisions. This
supports the idea that information-processing capacity creates value when it is aligned with operational
decision requirements (Galbraith, 1974; Tushman & Nadler, 1978; Srinivasan & Swink, 2018). Second, the
results connect the resource-based view with an explicit operational mechanism. Rather than treating AI
adoption as a resource that directly produces performance, the model shows that part of its relationship with
cost outcomes runs through inventory optimization. This provides a more process-oriented explanation of
how an analytics capability may become valuable (Barney, 1991; Chae et al., 2014).
Third, the findings strengthen the dynamic capabilities interpretation of AI-enabled planning.
Forecasting represents a sensing capability, while inventory optimization represents the operational
reconfiguration needed to respond to sensed changes. The significant paths to supply chain efficiency and
operational cost reduction indicate that this conversion from information to action is central to the model. By
testing forecasting, inventory optimization, efficiency, and cost reduction within one framework, the study
also helps connect research streams that have often been examined separately. The contribution should
nevertheless be framed as evidence of theoretically consistent associations across manufacturing and retail
respondents. Replication with longitudinal, multi-source, and objective data is needed before stronger claims
about capability development or causal performance effects can be made.
6. Practical Implications
The findings offer the following implications for practice, all of which should be read as associations
rather than guaranteed results.
 Supply chain managers: Treat AI-driven forecasting as part of a decision system rather than a stand-
alone tool. The strong association between forecasting and inventory optimization suggests that
benefits are more likely when forecasts feed directly into inventory decisions. Track efficiency and
cost outcomes together with forecast accuracy.
 Inventory managers: Link forecast outputs to safety-stock, reorder, and replenishment settings, and
review those settings when forecast quality changes. Aim for inventory levels that match demand
rather than simply minimizing stock, because extremely lean inventory can create its own risks (Eroglu
& Hofer, 2011).
 Procurement managers: Share forecast information with purchasing planning so that order timing
and quantities reflect expected demand. Better demand visibility can support fewer emergency
purchases and lower holding costs.
 Logistics managers: Use improved demand visibility to plan warehousing and transport capacity, and
monitor stockout incidents as an indicator of whether forecasting and replenishment are working
together.
 Manufacturing organizations:  Connect AI-driven forecasts to production and materials planning,
since forecast errors can be passed upstream and cause variability (Lee et al., 1997; Feizabadi, 2022).

7. Strengths and Limitations
The study has several strengths. It draws on professionals who work directly with forecasting,
inventory, procurement, and logistics in manufacturing and retail organizations. It examines forecasting and
inventory optimization together in one model, with both supply chain efficiency and operational cost reduction
as outcomes. It also uses PLS-SEM to estimate the measurement and structural models and to test mediation
within the same analysis.
Several limitations should be considered. First, the cross-sectional design does not allow causal
inference, and reverse or reciprocal relationships are possible. For example, organizations with stronger
inventory practices may be more likely to adopt AI-driven forecasting. Second, the data are self-reported,
which raises the possibility of common-method bias and perceptual error (Podsakoff et al., 2003). Third, the
outcomes, including the percentage improvements, are perceptual and were not checked against objective
inventory or cost records. Fourth, the sample may not represent all organizations, so the findings may not
generalize beyond the participating manufacturing and retail organizations. Fifth, manufacturing and retail
differ in demand patterns and inventory structures, and possible differences between the two sectors were not
examined.
7.1 Future Research
Future research should first strengthen the temporal and measurement basis of the model.
Longitudinal, multi-wave, or panel designs could establish whether changes in AI-driven forecasting precede
changes in inventory practices and operational outcomes. Survey measures should also be linked, where
possible, to objective organizational indicators such as forecast error, inventory turnover, service levels,
stockout rates, holding costs, and total operating costs. Matching responses from several managers within the
same organization would reduce reliance on a single source and help address common-method bias (Podsakoff
et al., 2003). Quasi-experimental or before-and-after designs around the implementation of forecasting
technologies could provide particularly useful evidence on whether observed performance changes are
attributable to the technology and associated process changes rather than to pre-existing organizational
differences.
Second, future studies should test the boundary conditions of the relationships identified here.
Manufacturing and retail differ in demand variability, product life cycles, lead times, service requirements,
and replenishment structures, yet sector differences were not examined in the present analysis. Larger samples
would allow multi-group comparisons across sectors, firm sizes, and countries. Moderating variables such as
demand volatility, lead-time uncertainty, product type, data quality, organizational size, digital maturity, and
staff analytical capability could also be evaluated. Such analysis would help determine when AI-driven
forecasting is most strongly associated with inventory optimization and when improvements in inventory
decisions are most likely to translate into efficiency and cost benefits.
Third, future research should extend the mechanism itself. The remaining direct effect of AI-driven
demand forecasting on operational cost reduction suggests that additional pathways may exist beyond
inventory optimization. Production planning, procurement synchronization, supplier coordination,
warehousing, transportation planning, and supply chain resilience are plausible mediating processes that can
be tested in broader models. Researchers could also compare machine-learning, deep-learning, traditional
statistical, and hybrid forecasting approaches under similar operating conditions (Makridakis et al., 2020,
2022; Zhu et al., 2021), and examine reinforcement-learning or data-driven inventory control approaches as
downstream decision tools (Boute et al., 2022; Gijsbrechts et al., 2022; Oroojlooyjadid et al., 2020). Future
SEM and PLS-SEM studies should report complete measurement and structural evidence, including direct,

8. Conclusion
This study examined an integrated pathway through which AI-driven demand forecasting is associated
with supply chain performance. Across 300 manufacturing and retail respondents, AI-driven demand
forecasting was positively and significantly associated with inventory optimization (β = 0.71, p < 0.001) and
supply chain efficiency (β = 0.63, p < 0.001). Inventory optimization was, in turn, positively associated with
supply chain efficiency (β = 0.58, p < 0.001) and operational cost reduction (β = 0.34, p < 0.001). The
mediation analysis further showed a significant indirect effect of 0.24, 95% CI [0.17, 0.32], alongside a
significant direct effect of 0.29, indicating partial mediation. These findings support the central proposition
of the study: the organizational value of AI-based forecasting is closely connected to how effectively forecast
information is converted into inventory decisions.
The study therefore contributes a decision-centered perspective to the discussion of AI in supply chain
management. Forecasting accuracy is important, but it is not an operational outcome by itself. The reported
improvements in forecasting accuracy, inventory holding costs, stockout incidents, supply chain efficiency,
and operational costs suggest that forecasting capability and inventory optimization should be managed as
connected elements of the same planning system. For managers, this means that investment in AI forecasting
should be accompanied by clear links to safety-stock policies, replenishment rules, procurement planning,
and wider operational coordination, while performance should be evaluated using both forecasting and
business outcomes.
These conclusions remain subject to the study design. The data are cross-sectional and self-reported,
the percentage improvements are perceptual rather than audited measures, and the sample does not establish
generalizability to all industries or countries. Accordingly, the results demonstrate statistically significant
associations rather than causal effects. Future longitudinal, multi-source, and objective-data studies can test
the proposed pathway more rigorously and examine additional mechanisms and boundary conditions. Within
these limits, the evidence consistently indicates that AI-driven demand forecasting is most meaningful when
it becomes actionable information for inventory and supply chain decisions, and that this integration is
associated with greater efficiency and lower perceived operational costs.
