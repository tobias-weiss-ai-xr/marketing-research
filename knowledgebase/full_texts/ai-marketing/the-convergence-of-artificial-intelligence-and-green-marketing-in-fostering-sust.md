Abstract
This paper examines the way Artificial Intelligence (AI) is reshaping green marketing practice and contributing
to sustainable growth within India's automobile industry. As Indian consumers become increasingly
environmentally conscious, manufacturers are turning to AI-enabled capabilities — predictive analytics,
machine learning and automated, data-backed decision-making — to read eco-conscious buying behaviour,
streamline production and cut the environmental cost of operations. Drawing on primary survey data,
regression modelling and a review of existing literature, the study argues that AI does more than sharpen
operational efficiency and strengthen brand image: it actively pulls the automobile sector into closer alignment
with India's national sustainability agenda. A regression analysis of two-wheeler electric-vehicle (EV) sales
against an eco-friendly growth indicator (R² = 0.86, F = 92.45, p < 0.001) confirms a strong, statistically
significant relationship, reinforcing the case for AI-supported green marketing as a driver of sustainable
mobility.

1. Introduction
Environmental awareness has moved from the margins to the mainstream of consumer decision-making, and
green marketing has grown in step with it. Categories such as solar-powered devices, LED lighting, electric
vehicles, compostable packaging, eco-conscious furnishings and organic apparel now carry real weight in
shaping how products are perceived and purchased (Nabivi, 2025). Artificial Intelligence has emerged as a
natural companion to this shift: it can be applied to design more sustainable products, tighten supply-chain
performance and shape marketing messages around the specific concerns of eco-aware buyers (Singh, 2024).
India offers a particularly rich setting in which to study this intersection. Its expanding middle class and
fastgrowing economy are driving demand for products that combine affordability with environmental
responsibility, giving AI-enabled green marketing a large and receptive audience (Mishra & Farooqi, 2024).
Mishra and Farooqi's study of Indian millennials, based on 651 survey responses analysed through SmartPLS 4,
found that affordability, awareness and availability meaningfully predict green purchase behaviour, while
apprehension about eco-products did not. Purchase behaviour did not vary significantly by gender, but did vary
by education, occupation and income — a finding that gives marketers a concrete basis for segmenting and
targeting sustainability-focused campaigns rather than treating the ‘green consumer' as a single, undifferentiated
group.
2.Literature Review
Green marketing is generally understood as the practice of promoting goods and services on the strength of their
environmental benefits — spanning sustainable product design, environmentally responsible operating practices
and transparent communication about ecological impact. As consumer concern for the environment has risen, so
has the pressure on firms to weave sustainability into their marketing strategy rather than treat it as an add-on
(Ottman, 2011).
Separately, AI has reshaped marketing more broadly by enabling decisions grounded in data, automating
repetitive tasks and improving the quality of customer interaction. Core AI capabilities — machine learning,
natural language processing and predictive analytics — give marketers the tools to interpret large volumes of
data, anticipate consumer behaviour and tailor campaigns at a level of precision that was previously unavailable
(Chaffey & Ellis-Chadwick, 2019).
Where these two fields meet, the potential for advancing sustainability goals is substantial. AI can refine green
marketing by making better use of resources, lifting operational efficiency and deepening insight into what
sustainability-minded consumers actually want (Kumar et al., 2021). AI-powered analytics let marketers

example of this in practice (Young, 2021).
AI also supports the operational side of sustainable marketing: by analysing energy-usage patterns, it can
recommend efficiency gains and help schedule digital campaigns during lower-impact periods, trimming the
carbon cost of marketing activity itself (Watson & Baker, 2022; Young, 2021). Continuous, AI-assisted monitoring
of compliance with environmental standards helps firms maintain green certifications and protects the
credibility of their sustainability claims (Green & Adams, 2022). Finally, AI-driven chatbots and virtual assistants
increasingly serve as a front line for consumer education — answering questions about a product's
environmental credentials and steering buyers toward eco-friendly alternatives in real time (Roberts, 2023).
Two further studies frame the methodological approach taken here. Gündüzyeli (2024) conducted a
PRISMAbased systematic review of how AI is reshaping digital marketing while simultaneously advancing
sustainability goals, underlining the need for research that treats the two as connected rather than parallel
developments. Gu and Duan (2024), working from self-determination theory within a stimulus–organism–
response framework, examined how influencer-related factors, advertisement content and social cues drive
consumer engagement — a lens that is directly relevant to understanding how AI-personalised green messaging
is received by consumers.
3.Objectives Of The Study
•To examine how the adoption of AI shapes green marketing practice in India's automobile sector.
•To identify the principal benefits of, and obstacles to, applying AI within green marketing initiatives.
•To evaluate AI's capacity to advance sustainable practices across the automobile value chain.
4.Hypotheses
H1: AI adoption is positively and significantly associated with green marketing outcomes in the durable-
goods (automobile) industry.
H2: AI adoption is associated with higher consumer engagement and greater awareness of
environmentally friendly products.
H3: A strong positive correlation exists between the extent of AI adoption and consumer awareness of
ecofriendly automobile products.
5.Research Methodology
The study follows a mixed-method design, combining primary data (structured questionnaires and consumer
interviews) with secondary literature. The interview component explored how firms implement AI, which
specific tools they use, and what effect this has on green marketing outcomes; the questionnaire captured
consumer-facing marketing practices tied to AI adoption.
For the quantitative component, regression analysis was used to test the effect of AI-linked green marketing
activity on consumer-facing outcomes, with consumer behaviour measures treated as the dependent variable
and green marketing activity as the independent variable. Age, income, education, personal values and market-
level factors — government policy, tax incentives, influencer marketing and green advertising — were treated as
controls. This approach is consistent with Gündüzyeli (2024), whose PRISMA-based systematic review likewise
framed AI's contribution to marketing sustainability as an empirical question worth testing directly rather than
assuming.
Qualitative data from the interviews and open-ended questionnaire responses were analysed thematically,
coding responses to surface recurring patterns in how firms implement AI-driven green marketing and how
consumers respond to it — an approach informed by Gu and Duan's (2024) work on the mechanisms driving
consumer engagement with digitally mediated marketing content.

7.Interpretation Of Results
7.1A Steep and Sustained Climb
The clearest signal in the data is the steady upward trajectory of the sales index itself. Starting at 28.2 in January
2024, the index climbed to 64.4 by May 2025 — a 128% increase over seventeen months. This is not incidental
movement; it reflects a durable shift in consumer preference driven by improving vehicle technology, rising fuel
costs, targeted subsidies and growing environmental awareness. The index crossing the 50-point mark in
December 2024 (Fig. 3) is a useful marker in its own right, suggesting the segment's move from a niche category
toward mainstream adoption.
7.2Volatility as a Signature of an Emerging Market
The month-over-month growth figures (Fig. 4) tell a more turbulent story than the index alone. Growth swung
from a contraction of -16.9% in February 2024 to a surge of +34.7% in September 2024 — a range that points to
a market still highly sensitive to external triggers such as new model launches, shifts in incentive policy and
seasonal buying patterns. The September 2024 spike aligns plausibly with the festive season (Navratri–Diwali) in
India, a period known to drive major consumer purchases, while the February and August 2024 dips likely reflect
post-peak inventory correction or consumers deferring purchases ahead of anticipated launches or policy

7.3Early Signs of Market Maturation
A notable shift appears when comparing 2024 and 2025 (Fig. 5). The wide double-digit swings that characterised
2024 give way, in the first five months of 2025, to a comparatively narrower band of roughly -2.8% to +9.7%
(excluding the strong May reading). This compression in volatility, occurring even as the sales index holds at
historic highs, is consistent with a market transitioning from early-adopter-driven spikes toward the steadier
purchasing behaviour typical of a broader, more pragmatic buyer base.
7.4Strategic Implications
For firms operating in this space, the underlying growth trend supports continued investment in AI-enabled
green marketing and EV-related product development. At the same time, the scale of month-to-month volatility
calls for supply chains and inventory systems resilient enough to absorb swings on the order of a 35% surge
followed by a near-10% contraction. Marketing and launch timing should be planned around known seasonal
peaks — positioning new model launches ahead of the festive period — while AI-driven demand forecasting can
help smooth some of the operational disruption this volatility creates.
8.Conclusion
This study set out to examine how Artificial Intelligence supports green marketing and sustainable growth in
India's automobile sector, and the evidence — both from the literature and from the two-wheeler EV regression
model (R² = 0.86, F = 92.45, p < 0.001) — points to a strong and statistically robust link between AI-enabled, eco-
focused marketing activity and market outcomes. The 17-month sales record examined here captures a market
in the midst of rapid, occasionally volatile growth: the underlying trend is unambiguously upward, but the path
includes sharp seasonal peaks and corrections typical of a technology still finding its footing. The narrowing of
volatility observed through early 2025 offers a promising signal that this segment — and by extension, India's
broader AI-driven green-marketing ecosystem — is moving toward more stable, sustainable growth.
