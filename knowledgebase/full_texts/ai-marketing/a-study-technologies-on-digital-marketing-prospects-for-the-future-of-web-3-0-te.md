1. Introduction

Digital transformation has profoundly changed how organizations engage with customers, develop brands, and achieve
competitive advantages. The evolution of the internet from the static structure of Web 1.0 to the interactive environment of
Web 2.0 has revolutionized marketing by shifting communication from a unidirectional flow of information to a more engaging,
collaborative, and customer-focused model. Social media platforms such as Facebook, Instagram, YouTube, LinkedIn, TikTok,
and X have become vital marketing channels through which organizations establish connections with consumers, promote
user-generated content, engage in influencer marketing, and offer personalized experiences. These developments have
enabled businesses to broaden their market reach while fostering stronger customer loyalty and brand advocacy.
The growing integration of artificial intelligence, big data analytics, blockchain technology, augmented reality (AR),
virtual reality (VR), and the metaverse is currently driving another technological transition towards Web 3.0. Unlike Web 2.0,
which relies heavily on centralized platforms that control user-generated data, Web 3.0 emphasizes decentralization, semantic
intelligence, interoperability, digital ownership, and enhanced privacy. This evolution has the potential to reshape digital
marketing by enabling transparent transactions, intelligent personalization, decentralized commerce, and immersive customer
experiences. Nevertheless, organizations continue to face considerable challenges related to technological complexity,
infrastructure requirements, regulatory uncertainty, data governance, cybersecurity, and consumer acceptance.
Despite the considerable expansion of literature related to social media marketing, the majority of existing reviews
primarily concentrate on social media platforms, influencer marketing, artificial intelligence, blockchain, or Web 3.0
technologies in a fragmented manner. There is a notable lack of studies that critically integrate the entire transition from Web
2.0 to Web 3.0, while also encompassing technological innovation, consumer engagement, decentralized governance, and
intelligent marketing systems within a unified conceptual framework. Consequently, the theoretical transition between these
two internet generations is not adequately articulated, which impedes the comprehension of how emerging technologies
collectively reshape digital marketing strategies.
Abstract The swift advancement of internet technologies has fundamentally altered the landscape of digital marketing,
transitioning from static web communication to interactive, consumer -focused, and increasingly decentralized marketing
frameworks. Social media pla tforms, driven by Web 2.0 technologies, have transformed customer engagement by
facilitating real -time communication, user -generated content, influencer marketing, and tailored brand experiences.
Despite extensive investigations into social media marketing and Web 2.0, there is a scarcity of comprehensive studies that
explore the shift towards Web 3.0 technologies and their potential impacts on future digital marketing strategies. This
review aims to fill this research void by synthesizing existing literature on the progression of digital marketing from Web 2.0
to Web 3.0, while also pinpointing emerging opportunities and challenges. A structured narrative review methodology was
employed, utilizing peer-reviewed articles published mainly between 1996 and 202 6. Literature was sourced from Scopus,
Web of Science, ScienceDirect, Emerald Insight, SpringerLink, Wiley Online Library, Taylor & Francis, Google Scholar, and
IEEE Xplore through predefined search strategies and thematic synthesis. The findings indicate that Web 2.0 technologies
have significantly improved customer participation, brand engagement, and data -driven marketing practices, while Web
3.0 introduces decentralized platforms, blockchain technology, artificial intelligence, semantic web capabilities , and
immersive virtual environments that offer enhanced transparency, personalization, and consumer control over data.
Nevertheless, challenges related to technological complexity, privacy concerns, interoperability, and organizational
preparedness contin ue to pose significant obstacles. The review suggests an integrated conceptual framework that
connects the marketing paradigms of Web 2.0 and Web 3.0 and outlines future research avenues for the development of
intelligent, decentralized, and ethically responsible digital marketing ecosystems.

This review aims to fill these gaps by conducting a structured thematic synthesis of current research on social media

the role of social media and Web 2.0 technologies in modern digital marketing, (ii) identify the opportunities and challenges
posed by Web 3.0 technologies, (iii) compare centralized and decentralized marketing ecosystems, and (iv) propose an
integrated conceptual framework that demonstrates the evolution of digital marketing from interactive Web 2.0 environments

2. Methodology

2.1. Research Design

This study employs a structured narrative literature review, supported by a systematic search strategy, to evaluate the
impact of social media and Web 2.0 technologies on digital marketing, while also exploring the new possibilities introduced by
Web 3.0 technologies. The choice of a structured narrative review is based on its objective to synthesize theoretical
developments, identify research trends, compare previous findings, and establish an integrated conceptual framework
regarding the transition from Web 2.0 to Web 3.0, rather than simply aggregating empirical data statistically. The review follows
well-defined and replicable methods to ensure comprehensive coverage of relevant literature and to minimize selection bias.

2.2. Search Strategy and Source Selection

An extensive literature search was conducted using globally recognized academic databases, including Scopus, Web of
Science, ScienceDirect, Emerald Insight, SpringerLink, Wiley Online Library, Taylor & Francis Online, Google Scholar, and IEEE
Xplore. These databases were selected for their broad coverage of peer-reviewed research in the domains of digital marketing,
information systems, management, computer science, and emerging internet technologies. The search employed various
combinations of keywords and Boolean operators, such as "Social Media Marketing," "Web 2.0," "Web 3.0," "Digital
Marketing," "Blockchain," "Artificial Intelligence," "Metaverse," "Semantic Web," "Influencer Marketing," "Consumer
Engagement," and "Decentralized Marketing." Significant search strings included ("Social Media Marketing" AND "Web 2.0"),
("Web 3.0" AND "Digital Marketing"), and ("Blockchain" AND Marketing). The review primarily concentrated on publications
from 1996 to 2026, incorporating foundational studies that established the basis for Web technologies, as well as recent
research that emphasizes advancements in artificial intelligence, blockchain, and decentralized digital ecosystems.

2.3. Inclusion, Screening, and Thematic Analysis

The review included peer-reviewed journal articles, high-quality conference proceedings, and scholarly books published
in English that concentrated on digital marketing, social media, Web 2.0, Web 3.0, blockchain, artificial intelligence, semantic
web technologies, metaverse applications, influencer marketing, customer engagement, and decentralized commerce.
Excluded from the review were editorials, news articles, blogs, dissertations, duplicate records, and studies that did not directly
pertain to the research objectives. Following the retrieval of data from databases, duplicate records were removed, and titles
and abstracts were assessed against predefined eligibility criteria. Full-text articles that met the inclusion criteria were
subsequently analysed in detail.
The selected studies were synthesized through thematic analysis, organizing the literature into the following sections:
Evolution of Web Technologies and Digital Marketing (1996–2005), Emergence of Web 2.0 and Social Media Marketing (2008–
2014), Expansion of Social Media Marketing and Customer Engagement (2015–2020), Artificial Intelligence, Blockchain, and
the Emergence of Web 3.0 (2021–2026), Topic-wise Synthesis of the Literature, Theme 1: Social Media Marketing and Customer
Engagement, Theme 2: Web 2.0 Technologies in Digital Marketing, Theme 3: Artificial Intelligence and Data-Driven Marketing,
Theme 4: Web 3.0 Technologies and Future Marketing Ecosystem, and Research Gaps and Conceptual Synthesis. This thematic
approach enabled a critical comparison of previous studies, the identification of commonalities and differences, and the
development of an integrated conceptual framework that links the marketing paradigms of Web 2.0 and Web 3.0.

3. Thematic Literature Review

The progression of digital marketing has closely mirrored the advancements in internet technologies, evolving from
static Web 1.0 sites to interactive Web 2.0 platforms, and more recently, to intelligent and decentralized Web 3.0 ecosystems.
The literature reviewed indicates that research has transitioned from focusing on website functionality and online
communication to exploring social media marketing, artificial intelligence (AI), blockchain, metaverse applications, and
decentralized digital commerce. To ensure a thorough synthesis, the literature is organized in both chronological and thematic
manners.

3.1. Evolution of Web Technologies and Digital Marketing (1996–2005)

The initial studies laid the groundwork for internet-based marketing by highlighting websites as platforms for
communication and information dissemination. Morgan (1996) recognized the Internet as a strategic asset capable of
revolutionizing traditional marketing practices by broadening market reach and enhancing customer communication.
Srirojanant et al. (1998) contended that internet technologies promote relationship marketing by improving customer
interaction. Likewise, Siegel (2000), Coyle and Thorson (2001), Gilbert and Powell-Perry (2001), Lau et al. (2001), and Kucuk et
al. (2001) illustrated that website design, online advertising, and interactive interfaces had a positive impact on customer
satisfaction and online purchasing behaviour. Tsang and Tse (2005) further underscored that website interactivity significantly
improved advertising effectiveness. Collectively, these studies established that digital marketing was transitioning from a
model of one-way information delivery to a framework of interactive customer communication.

3.2. Emergence of Web 2.0 and Social Media Marketing (2008–2014)

The emergence of Web 2.0 marked a pivotal transformation in digital marketing by enabling user-generated content,
collaboration, and social networking. Constantinides and Fountain (2008) provided a conceptual framework for Web 2.0,
demonstrating how collaborative technologies influenced customer behaviour and marketing strategies. Thackeray et al.
(2008) illustrated that Web 2.0 applications significantly improved public communication and social marketing initiatives, while
Cooke and Buckley (2008) highlighted the growing importance of social networks in marketing research.
Research conducted between 2009 and 2014 focused on the practical applications of Web 2.0. Cronin (2009) and
Caravella et al. (2009) stressed its educational and managerial applications, while Jones (2010) showed that Web 2.0
technologies encouraged entrepreneurial marketing and innovation. Kaplan (2011), Chen et al. (2011), Akar and Topçu (2011),
and Chan and Guillet (2011) consistently found that social networking platforms enhanced customer engagement, electronic
word-of-mouth (eWOM), and online brand communication. Ribeiro Soriano et al. (2012) introduced the emerging concept of
Web 3.0 and its implications for organizational management, while Palacios-Marques et al. (2013) revealed that Web 2.0
technologies improved innovation capabilities and market orientation. Together, these studies demonstrated that Web 2.0
shifted digital marketing from an organization-centred communication model to one focused on customer engagement.

3.3. Expansion of Social Media Marketing and Customer Engagement (2015–2020)

The literature published between 2015 and 2020 concentrated on themes such as customer engagement, branding,
influencer marketing, and digital relationship management. Ashley and Tuten (2015) demonstrated that branded content on
social media significantly enhances consumer interaction and cultivates brand loyalty. Habibi et al. (2015) examined the
opportunities and challenges related to the use of social media in business-to-business marketing, while Fisher (2015), Brocato
et al. (2015), and Crittenden and Crittenden (2015) acknowledged social media as an essential component of contemporary
marketing strategies.
In the years following 2016, there was a notable increase in research focused on consumer behaviour and the
effectiveness of digital marketing. Alves et al. (2016) discovered that social media has a considerable effect on consumer
perceptions and their purchasing choices. Stephen (2016) emphasized that digital consumer behaviour has emerged as a
central theme in modern marketing research. Duffett (2017), Alalwan et al. (2017), Ramanathan et al. (2017), Assimakopoulos
et al. (2017), and Saura et al. (2017) consistently found that social media marketing positively affects customer engagement,
satisfaction, organizational performance, and brand loyalty. Additionally, Tafesse et al. (2018), Buratti et al. (2018), and Tuten
and Mintu-Wimsatt (2018) illustrated that digital engagement and social media analytics improve marketing effectiveness by
enabling organizations to customize customer interactions and enhance strategic decision-making.

3.4. Artificial Intelligence, Blockchain, and the Emergence of Web 3.0 (2021–2026)

Recent studies reveal a gradual transition from Web 2.0 to intelligent and decentralized digital marketing ecosystems.
Mukherjee (2020), Drummond et al. (2020), Mason et al. (2021), Cheung et al. (2021), He et al. (2021), Rohde and Mau (2021),
and Zahay (2021) have demonstrated that customer engagement increasingly depends on interactive digital content, influencer
marketing, artificial intelligence, and value co-creation through social media platforms. Starting in 2022, research has expanded
its scope beyond conventional social media marketing to include blockchain, semantic web technologies, metaverse
applications, and decentralized commerce. Hamid et al. (2022) found that social media is vital for improving organizational
trust and reputation, while Boegershausen et al. (2022) highlighted the growing importance of digital analytics and web data
in marketing decision-making. Tan et al. (2023) and Ramadan (2023) proposed that metaverse technologies offer immersive
consumer experiences that could transform digital commerce.
Recent insights from Murtas et al. (2024), Feng and Xie (2024), Zhang and Lu (2025), Li et al. (2025), Joshi et al. (2025),
Bernovskis and Sceulovs (2026), Abate et al. (2026), and Arghashi et al. (2026) indicate that Web 3.0 technologies including
blockchain, NFTs, decentralized applications, semantic web technologies, and agentic commerce represent the next
evolutionary stage of digital marketing. These technologies improve personalization, transparency, consumer data ownership,

3.5. Topic-wise Synthesis of the Literature

3.5.1. Theme 1: Social Media Marketing and Customer Engagement

A common finding across the reviewed studies is that social media marketing significantly boosts customer engagement,
electronic word-of-mouth (eWOM), brand awareness, customer loyalty, and purchase intentions. By utilizing interactive
communication and user-generated content, organizations can establish stronger connections with consumers while improving
marketing effectiveness. However, many studies caution that challenges such as misinformation, fraudulent reviews,
algorithmic bias, and excessive dependence on centralized platforms present considerable managerial and ethical challenges.

3.5.2. Theme 2: Web 2.0 Technologies in Digital Marketing

The current body of literature consistently demonstrates that Web 2.0 has transformed digital marketing through
collaboration, knowledge sharing, online communities, blogs, and social networking platforms. These technologies have shifted
marketing from a one-way communication approach to one that promotes customer involvement and value co-creation.
Nonetheless, researchers are increasingly expressing concerns that the centralized management of user data raises significant
issues regarding privacy, governance, platform dependency, and the rise of digital monopolies.

3.5.3. Theme 3: Artificial Intelligence and Data-Driven Marketing

Artificial intelligence has emerged as a crucial driver of marketing innovation by enabling predictive analytics,
recommendation systems, customer segmentation, sentiment analysis, chatbots, and personalized advertising. Data-driven
decision-making enhances organizational efficiency and enriches customer experiences. However, despite these advantages,
ethical issues surrounding algorithmic transparency, consumer privacy, and responsible AI practices remain insufficiently
addressed in the existing marketing literature.

3.5.4. Theme 4: Web 3.0 Technologies and Future Marketing Ecosystems

The reviewed literature suggests that Web 3.0 represents the next phase in digital marketing. Technologies such as
blockchain, the semantic web, decentralized applications, NFTs, metaverse platforms, and agentic commerce work together to
enable secure transactions, digital ownership, intelligent automation, and immersive customer experiences. While these
technologies offer significant opportunities for innovation, there remains a lack of empirical data concerning organizational
implementation and ongoing consumer adoption.

3.6. Research Gaps and Conceptual Synthesis

The thematic synthesis reveals several notable research gaps. First, existing studies often examine social media
marketing, artificial intelligence, blockchain, influencer marketing, and Web 3.0 in isolation, rather than integrating them into
a unified theoretical framework. Second, there is a deficiency of comparative research that investigates the transition from
centralized Web 2.0 platforms to decentralized Web 3.0 ecosystems. Third, there are relatively few empirical studies addressing
governance mechanisms, consumer trust, organizational readiness, interoperability, ethical AI, and regulatory compliance
within the context of Web 3.0 marketing. In light of these observations, this review posits that Web 3.0 should be viewed as
an evolutionary advancement of Web 2.0, rather than a total replacement. Future digital marketing ecosystems are anticipated
to combine social media engagement, artificial intelligence, blockchain, semantic technologies, and immersive virtual
environments to create intelligent, decentralized, transparent, and customer-centric marketing systems. This integrated
perspective represents the primary theoretical contribution of the current review and establishes a foundation for future
empirical research in the domain of digital marketing.

5. Discussion

The findings suggest that digital marketing has evolved from static web communication to dynamic and intelligent
ecosystems. Consistent with previous studies, Web 2.0 technologies have transformed the interactions between businesses
and consumers by promoting customer engagement, online communities, and collaborative value creation. Social media has
become the primary channel for customer interaction, enabling organizations to build stronger relationships, improve brand
reputation, and acquire real-time market insights. Additionally, the review highlights the growing importance of artificial
intelligence in digital marketing. AI-powered recommendation systems, customer analytics, and personalized communication
have significantly improved marketing efficiency and customer satisfaction. Furthermore, blockchain technology provides
enhanced transparency, secure transactions, and decentralized management of customer data, effectively addressing various
issues associated with centralized Web 2.0 platforms.
The analysis indicates that Web 3.0 ought to be regarded as an evolutionary progression rather than a substitute for
Web 2.0. While Web 2.0 remains the cornerstone for customer engagement via social media, Web 3.0 incorporates blockchain,
semantic technologies, artificial intelligence, and immersive virtual environments to establish intelligent, decentralized, and
consumer-focused marketing ecosystems. A review of the current literature shows that the majority of studies concentrate on
individual technologies instead of exploring their collective impact on digital marketing. This fragmented perspective hinders
both theoretical advancement and practical implementation. The current review adds value by merging these technologies
into a cohesive conceptual framework that connects customer engagement, intelligent automation, digital trust, and
decentralized governance. However, several limitations persist. As a structured narrative review, this research relies on
published literature and does not encompass primary empirical data or meta-analytic statistical findings. Furthermore, the
rapidly changing landscape of Web 3.0 technologies implies that many existing insights are still in the exploratory phase, and
there is a deficiency of long-term evidence concerning organizational performance and consumer adoption.

6. Conclusion and Future Directions

This review demonstrates that social media and Web 2.0 technologies have significantly transformed the digital
marketing landscape by enhancing customer engagement, promoting relationship marketing, facilitating personalized
communication, and encouraging collaborative value creation. Emerging Web 3.0 technologies, such as blockchain, artificial
intelligence, semantic web technologies, decentralized applications, NFTs, and metaverse platforms, are further enhancing
these capabilities by increasing transparency, enabling intelligent automation, ensuring digital ownership, and protecting
customer interactions. The review identifies a significant theoretical gap in the existing literature. Much of the previous
research has concentrated on social media, artificial intelligence, blockchain, influencer marketing, or metaverse applications
in isolation, with insufficient focus on their integration within a comprehensive digital marketing framework. By integrating
these diverse research streams, the current study provides a unified conceptual understanding of the transition from Web 2.0
to Web 3.0 marketing ecosystems.
The integration of social media, artificial intelligence, blockchain, and immersive digital technologies is expected to
revolutionize digital marketing over the next decade. Organizations that effectively combine these technologies while
maintaining consumer trust, transparency, and privacy are likely to gain a sustainable competitive advantage in the evolving
Web 3.0 economy. Future research should prioritize longitudinal and cross-country empirical studies to evaluate the
effectiveness of decentralized marketing strategies across different sectors. Further exploration is required to investigate
consumer trust, ethical AI, privacy safeguards, blockchain governance, interoperability, and regulatory frameworks. Moreover,
researchers should examine the synergy between AI, blockchain, immersive virtual environments, and semantic web
technologies in developing sustainable, customer-centric, and intelligent marketing ecosystems that can support the next
generation of digital commerce.
