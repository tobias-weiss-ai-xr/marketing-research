 ABSTRACT Artificial intelligence (AI) -driven personalization has increasingly transformed digital
marketing by enabling firms to deliver individualized recommendations, content, and offers
based on consumer data and behavioral patterns. However, the growing use of personalization
also raises important questions regarding consumer trust, engagement, and purchase behavior.
This study aims to conduct a systematic literature review (SLR) of research on AI -driven
personalization in digital marketing to examine its implic ations for consumer trust,
engagement, and purchase behavior. Using Scopus as the primary database, the search process
identified 2,906 records and, through successive filtering and screening procedures, resulted
in 51 eligible studies. Bibliometric analys is using VOSviewer and thematic synthesis were
employed to identify publication trends, intellectual structures, and key research themes. The
findings indicate a shift from technology -oriented research toward consumer -centered
perspectives, with increasing  attention to consumer experience, trust, engagement, privacy,
transparency, purchase intention, and purchase behavior. The review further demonstrates that
the effectiveness of AI-driven personalization is context-dependent and influenced by consumer
perceptions and data -related concerns. These findings provide an integrated basis for
understanding the evolving role of AI personalization in contemporary digital marketing.

INTRODUCTION
The rapid diffusion of artificial intelligence (AI) has fundamentally reshaped digital marketing
by enabling firms to collect, process, and interpret large volumes of consumer data to deliver
increasingly individualized marketing experiences (Al-Ababneh & Al Muala, 2025; Rane et al., 2024;
Zhuk & Yatskyi, 2024). Among these developments, AI-driven personalization has emerged as a critical
mechanism through which digital platforms tailor product recommendations, advertising content,
promotional offers, and customer interactions according to consumers' interests, preferences, and
behavioral histories (Sathya et al., 2024; C. Wang et al., 2025; Xia et al., 2024). Recent literature
indicates that AI-powered recommendation systems, predictive analytics, and personalized marketing
tools are increasingly embedded in e -commerce and social media environment s, transforming
conventional customer targeting into more adaptive and real-time decision-making processes (Alsulami
et al., 2025; Diao et al., 2025; Peruchini et al., 2024; L. Wang et al., 2025). In particular, AI
personalization enables marketers to move  beyond demographic segmentation toward individualized
interactions based on continuously generated behavioral data (Krasnov & Kurushin, 2025; Kumar et
al., 2025; Youbi et al., 2025).
The growing importance of AI-driven personalization is closely associated with its potential to
improve consumer experiences and influence purchasing outcomes (Lakshmi et al., 2025; Sasanuma &
Yang, 2024). AI -based personalization can facilitate more relevant product discovery, reduce
information-search costs, and provide consumers with timely recommendations (El Youbi et al., 2025;
Henriques & Pinto, 2023; Korosi & Vinkó, 2021). Empirical evidence suggests that personalized digital
experiences can contribute to customer retention and relationship development, although the magnitude
of these effects depends on technological and social factors (Panya et al., 2025). Similarly, AI -driven
personalization has been found to contribute significantly to purchase intention among Generation Z
consumers, with personalized applications, bra nding, and social commerce features collectively
strengthening digital purchase processes (Hasim & Nazri, 2025). AI can also predict purchase intention
during an ongoing online session, allowing firms to provide real-time offers before consumers leave the
platform (Esmeli et al., 2021). These developments demonstrate the increasing convergence between
personalization capabilities and consumer decision -making (Li et al., 2025; Louis et al., 2024; Ma &
Ye, 2025).
However, personalization does not inherently p roduce positive consumer responses. The
effectiveness of AI-driven marketing depends substantially on how consumers perceive the technology,
particularly in terms of trust, privacy, transparency, control, and perceived risk (Hoa, 2025; von Böhlen
& Simberová, 2025). Dixit et al. (2025), for example, found that trust in AI was a critical determinant
of behavioral intention toward AI -enabled online shopping, whereas perceived risk had a negative,
although comparatively marginal, influence. Panya et al. (2025)  similarly emphasized the importance
of communicating data -security and privacy protections to strengthen trust in AI -powered
personalization. This tension creates a fundamental paradox in personalized digital marketing: greater
access to consumer data can  enhance relevance while simultaneously increasing concerns about
surveillance, privacy, and inappropriate data usage (Han et al., 2023; Syahraeni et al., 2025). Privacy -
preserving approaches have therefore become increasingly important, with emerging fram eworks
seeking to enable personalized targeting while maintaining consumer control over personal data (Han
et al., 2023).
Beyond trust and purchase behavior, consumer engagement and customer experience represent
important mechanisms through which personali zation may generate marketing outcomes. AI -based
personalization, chatbots, AI -generated content, and data analytics have been shown to enhance

customer experience and engagement in digital marketing (Rane et al., 2024; Peruchini et al., 2024).
However, th e relationship between AI personalization and consumer responses is not always
straightforward (Shajrawi, 2025). Nevertheless, existing research remains fragmented across
technological, behavioral, psychological, and marketing perspectives (El Koufi & Bela ngour, 2024;

diversity and potentially produce homogenization effects (Molai & Lee, 2022).
Accordingly, a systematic synth esis is needed to integrate the fragmented evidence and clarify
how AI-driven personalization influences consumer trust, engagement, and purchase behavior, as well
as how these constructs interact across digital marketing contexts. Therefore, this systematic literature
review aims to unpack the mechanisms, antecedents, outcomes, and contextual conditions surrounding
AI-driven personalization in digital marketing. By synthesizing existing evidence, this study seeks to
develop a more integrated understanding of the relationship between AI personalization and consumer
responses while identifying theoretical, methodological, and empirical gaps that can guide future
research.

conceptualization, and implications of AI-driven personalization in digital marketing. The review was
guided by four research questions (RQs) covering publication trends a nd intellectual structures,
conceptualization and application, consumer trust and engagement, and purchase intention and purchase
behavior. The research questions and corresponding objectives are presented in Table 1.

Data from the 51 included studies were systematically extracted according to publication
characteristics, research context, AI-driven personalization applications, consumer -related constructs,
research methods, and major findings. The methodological quality of the included studies was also
assessed using the Mixed Methods Appraisal Tool (MMAT) to evaluate the appropriateness of the study
designs, sampling strategies, measurements, data collection procedures, and analytical approaches. The
quality appraisal was conducted to identify potential methodological weaknesses and assess the risk of
bias across the included evidence. The extracted evidence was subsequently synthesized thematically
according to RQ1 –RQ4, with par ticular attention given to the relationships among AI -driven
personalization, consumer trust, engagement, purchase intention, and purchase behavior, including
reported mediating and moderating factors.
To address RQ1, bibliometric analysis was conducted using VOSviewer. Keyword co-occurrence
analysis was employed to identify the major research themes and intellectual structures within the
literature. Network visualization was used to examine relationships among frequently occurring
keywords, while overlay v isualization was applied to observe the temporal development of research
themes. Density visualization was further used to identify dominant and emerging areas of research.
The bibliometric mapping was complemented by thematic synthesis to provide a broader interpretation
of how AI-driven personalization research has evolved and how its technological, psychological, and
behavioral dimensions are interconnected.

RESULT AND DISCUSSION
Result
Overview of the Selected Studies
The final dataset comprised 51 studies published between 2021 and 2025, reflecting the growing
academic interest in AI -driven personalization in digital marketing. The number of publications
increased substantially over the period, from 3 studies in 2021 and 2 in 2022 to 7 in 2023, 9 in 2024,
and 30 in 2025, indicating a rapid expansion of research in this field.
The selected studies cover diverse digital marketing contexts, including e -commerce, online
retailing, social commerce, digital advertising, and recommendation syst ems. The technologies
examined range from machine learning and predictive analytics to AI -based recommendation and
personalization systems. Earlier studies primarily focused on purchase prediction and recommendation
applications (Esmeli et al., 2021; Korösi & Vinkó, 2021) , while later research increasingly addressed
customer experience, privacy, personalization, retention, and consumer decision -making (Han et al.,
2023; Sasanuma & Yang, 2024).
Recent studies further demonstrate a shift toward consumer -centered outcomes, particularly
customer retention, purchase decisions, and brand -related responses (Panya et al., 2025; Sh ajrawi,
2025). Overall, the literature indicates an evolution from technical applications of AI toward broader
investigations of personalization and consumer behavioral outcomes.

Thematic Findings
The thematic synthesis of the 51 selected studies reveals five interconnected themes that
characterize the development of AI -driven personalization in digital marke ting: AI -enabled
recommendation and personalization, data -driven consumer profiling and prediction, customer
experience and engagement, consumer trust and responsible personalization, and purchase intention and
behavioral outcomes. These themes demonstrate  the evolution of AI research from technological
optimization toward increasingly consumer-centered applications.

AI-Enabled Recommendation and Personalization
The first dominant theme concerns the use of AI and machine learning to provide personalized
recommendations and marketing interventions. Recommendation systems represent one of the most
established applications, using user –item interactions and behavioral data to identify consumer
preferences and improve product recommendations  (Gao et al., 2021; Xia et al., 2024) . Machine -
learning-based systems have also been applied to targeted sales promotion and real -time marketing,
enabling firms to identify potential buyers and deliver personalized offers during ongoing shopping
sessions (Esmeli et al., 2021; Korösi & Vinkó, 2021; Seo & Yoo, 2023) . More recent studies extend
these capabilities through de ep learning, multimodal information, and adaptive recommendation
architectures to address dynamic consumer preferences and information overload (Diao et al., 2025;
Sathya et al., 2024; Z. Wei et al., 2025). This indicates that personalization is increasingly moving from
static recommendation toward dynamic, context-aware, and behavior-responsive marketing.

Data-Driven Consumer Profiling and Behavioral Prediction
A second theme focuses on the use of AI to understand, segment, and predict consumer behavior.
Customer segmentation has been implemented using clustering techniques based on behavioral and
transactional characteristics, enabling marketers to identify distinct customer groups and develop more
targeted strategies (Hussein et al., 2024; Ullah et al., 2023) . Other studies employ predictive analytics
and deep learning to anticipate purchasing trends, click-through rates, and consumer responses (Ma &
Ye, 2025; Yang et al., 2022; Zh ang et al., 2023) . This stream suggests that AI -driven personalization
relies heavily on the ability to transform large-scale behavioral data into actionable consumer profiles.
Consequently, personalization increasingly functions not only as a recommend ation mechanism but
also as a predictive marketing capability.

Customer Experience and Consumer Engagement
The third theme emphasizes the relationship between AI personalization, customer experience,
and engagement. AI -enabled tools such as personalized recommendations, chatbots, predictive
analytics, and intelligent customer -service systems can provide more re levant and efficient digital
interactions (Rane et al., 2024). Research specifically examining recommendation engines shows that
their effectiveness depends partly on how consumers interact with them throughout the customer
journey (Sasanuma & Yang, 2024) . The broader literature further connects AI innovation and
personalization with enhanced customer experience and engagement, with customer experience
potentially translating technological capabilities into stronger brand rel ationships (Peruchini et al.,
2024; Shajrawi, 2025). Similarly, AI-supported personalization in live-streaming and social-commerce
environments has been associated with increased consumer interaction and purchase intention (Mei et
al., 2025) . These findings position customer experience and engagement as important behavioral
pathways through which personalization can generate marketing value.

Consumer Trust, Privacy, and Responsible Personalization
The fourth theme highlights the trust –personalization tension. Although AI personalization can
increase relevance and convenience, the extensive use of consumer data raises concerns regarding
privacy, transparency, and perceived control. Privacy -preserving digital marketing frameworks have
therefore emerged to enable marketers to utilize consumer information while maintaining greater user
control over personal data (Han et al., 2023) . Recent evidence further indicates that algorithmic
transparency and platform governance can strengthen trust and engagement, particularly when
consumers interact with intelligent recommendation systems. Trust also appears to be an important
determinant of consumer acceptance of AI -enabled shopping, while percei ved risk may negatively
influence behavioral intentions (Dixit et al., 2025). Other research demonstrates that trust can be shaped
by contextual and consumer -specific factors, including cultural or value -based considerat ions in
personalized e-commerce (Syahraeni et al., 2025). Thus, effective personalization depends not only on
technological accuracy but also on whether consumers perceive AI systems as transparent, secure,
controllable, and trustworthy.

The fifth them e concerns the behavioral consequences of AI -driven personalization. Several
studies demonstrate the use of AI to predict purchase intention and identify consumers with a high
probability of conversion, enabling real-time offers and targeted marketing (Esmeli et al., 2021; Seo &
Yoo, 2023) . Empirical studies further associate AI personalization with purchase decisions, repeat

Discussion
Synthesis of Key Findings
The findings suggest that AI -driven personalization should not be understood simply as a
technological tool for improving marketing precision. Although the literature demonstrates the growing
use of AI for recommendation, prediction, segmentation, and targ eted marketing, the effectiveness of
personalization ultimately depends on how consumers interpret and respond to these practices. Earlier
studies largely emphasize the predictive and computational capabilities of AI, particularly in identifying
purchase intentions and optimizing recommendations (Esmeli et al., 2021; Korösi & Vinkó, 2021) .
More recent research, however, increasingly focuses on customer experience, trust, personalization, and
behavioral responses. This shift indicates a broader transformation in the field from algorithm-centered
personalization toward consumer-centered personalization.
Importantly, the findings reveal a tension between personalization a nd consumer autonomy.
More extensive use of consumer data can increase the relevance of recommendations, but it can
simultaneously intensify concerns regarding privacy, transparency, and perceived surveillance. Thus,
greater personalization should not auto matically be interpreted as greater marketing effectiveness.
Research on privacy -preserving digital marketing demonstrates that personalization increasingly
requires mechanisms that allow firms to utilize consumer information while maintaining greater
consumer control. Similarly, trust has emerged as an important consideration in AI-enabled purchasing
environments, suggesting that consumers evaluate not only the usefulness of personalized
recommendations but also whether the underlying AI system is trustworthy (Dixit et al., 2025).
This finding challenges a purely technology -driven interpretation of AI personalization. The
critical issue is therefore not whether firms can personalize, but under what conditions personalization
is perceived as valuable and acceptable by consumers. Customer experience and engagement may
improve when personalization is relevant and useful, but these benefits can be weakened when
consumers perceive personalization as intrusive or insufficiently transparent. The literature therefore
points to a conditional rather than universally positive relationship between AI personalization and
consumer outcomes.

Theoretical Implications
The review extends existing perspectives on digital marketing by demonst rating that
technological sophistication alone is insufficient to explain consumer responses to AI personalization.
The prominence of trust, customer experience, privacy, and perceived risk in the reviewed literature
indicates that consumer responses are shaped by both functional benefits and psychological evaluations.
A particularly important theoretical implication is the need to distinguish between personalization
capability and personalization effectiveness. AI may possess sophisticated predictive capabilities, yet
consumers may not respond positively if the recommendations are perceived as irrelevant, intrusive, or
opaque. This distinction helps explain why findings across studies are not always consistent. For

Practical Implications for Digital Marketers
The findings suggest that marketers should reconsider the assumption that more personalization
is inherently better. Effective AI personalization requires an appropriate balance between relevance and
consumer privacy. Firms should therefore prioritize  transparency regarding data collection, provide
meaningful personalization, and avoid excessive targeting that may be perceived as intrusive.
The findings also imply that marketing performance should not be evaluated exclusively through
immediate conversion or purchase intention. Customer experience, trust, engagement, and retention
provide important indicators of whether personalization creates sustainable value. This is particularly
relevant because AI-driven personalization can strengthen customer retention when consumers perceive
the technology as useful and trustworthy (Panya et al., 2025). Consequently, personalization strategies
should be evaluated not only according to their ability to increase short-term sales but also according to
their effects on longer-term consumer relationships.

Integrated Interpretation of Consumer Outcomes
The literature also suggests that the relationship between personalization and consumer behavior
is more complex than a simple positive effect. Personalization can facilitate product discovery and
improve relevance, but recommendation systems may  also narrow consumers' exposure to alternative
products and influence the diversity of their consumption choices (Molaie & Lee, 2022). This raises an
important issue: personalization may simultaneously improve marketing efficiency and constrain
consumer choice.
This tension is particularly important for digital marketing research because successful
personalization from the firm's perspective may not always correspond to  a positive consumer
experience. Consequently, future research should examine both the benefits and unintended
consequences of AI personalization, particularly concerning consumer autonomy, privacy, algorithmic
transparency, and diversity of choice.

Limitations and Future Research
The reviewed literature remains limited by its strong emphasis on purchase intention and short -
term consumer responses. More research is needed to determine whether these intentions translate into
actual purchasing behavior and sustained customer relationships. Longitudinal and experimental studies
could provide stronger evidence regarding the durability and causal mechanisms of AI personalization.
Future research should also investigate emerging technologies such as generative AI  and large
language models, as their ability to generate highly individualized content introduces new questions
concerning authenticity, transparency, privacy, and consumer trust. Cross-cultural research is likewise
needed because consumer acceptance of pe rsonalization may vary according to cultural norms,
technological familiarity, and privacy expectations.

 CONCLUSION
This systematic literature review provides an overview of the development and growing
importance of AI-driven personalization in digital m arketing. The findings indicate that the field has
expanded from technology -focused applications toward broader consumer -centered concerns,
particularly trust, engagement, customer experience, and purchasing responses. The review also
highlights that the effectiveness of AI personalization is shaped not only by technological capabilities
but also by consumer perceptions of relevance, transparency, privacy, and value.
Overall, the reviewed literature demonstrates that AI -driven personalization represents an
increasingly important component of contemporary digital marketing, while its consumer effects remain
context-dependent. The study therefore highlights the need for future research to move beyond
technological performance and examine actual consumer behavior, emerging AI technologies, and the
conditions that shape consumer acceptance of personalized marketing. This broader perspective can
support both theoretical development and more responsible implementation of AI -driven
personalization in digital marketing.
