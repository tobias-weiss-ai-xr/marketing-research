Abstract. With the rapid development of the digital economy, live-stream e-commerce has
become the mainstream form of online consumption. Big data and artificial intelligence (AI)
serve as the core driving force for marketing transforamtion, shifting marketing from an
extensive mode to more accurate user profiling and personalized recommendations. Existing
studies mostly focus on the direct relationship between traditional marketing factors and
consumer behavior, while few explore how AI-enabled personalized recommendation
influences impulse buying through internal psychological mechanisms. Based on the S-O-R
(Stimulus-Organism-Response) theoretical framework, this study takes big data and AI-
enabled personalized recommendation as the independent variable, impulse buying behavior
as the dependent variable, and emotional arousal as the mediating variable. Adopting the
quantitative research method, a questionnaire survey was conducted among 200 valid
respondents with live-stream shopping experience. SPSS 26.0 was used to conduct
reliability and validity tests, correlation analysis, regression analysis and mediating effect
tests. The results show that AI-enabled personalized recommendation has a significant
positive impact on impulse buying, and emotional arousal plays a partial mediating role in
their relationship. This study clarifies the psychological mechanism of AI-driven consumer
impulse buying and provides practical implications for platforms and merchants to optimize
data-driven precision marketing strategies.
Keywords: Artificial intelligence, Personalized recommendation, Live-stream e-commerce,
Impulse buying behavior, Emotional arousal

2. Theoretical foundation and research hypotheses
2.1. Theoretical foundation: the S-O-R framework
The S-O-R (Stimulus-Organism-Response) theory was proposed by Mehrabian & Russell in 1974 as
a classic theoretical framework in environmental psychology and consumer behavior research [3].
The theory posits that individual behavior is not directly triggered by external stimuli. Instead,
external environmental stimuli (S) act on an individual's internal psychological states (O), which in
turn drive the final behavioral responses (R), forming a complete transmission mechanism of
"stimulus – psychology – behavior". Given its ability to clearly explain how external environmental
factors influence consumption decisions through internal psychological pathways, the S-O-R theory
has been widely applied in behavioral mechanism studies in fields such as online consumption, live-
stream e-commerce, and precision marketing [4].
2.2. Definition of research variables
Based on the S-O-R theory and combined with the scenarios of live-stream e-commerce and AI
marketing, this study defines the stimulus, organism and response variables, and clarifies their
specific roles in the research. Corresponding respectively to Stimulus (S), Organism (O) and
Response (R) in the S-O-R theory, the three variables jointly form a complete psychological
transmission mechanism.
2.2.1. Stimulus variable: AI-enabled personalized recommendation (independent variable X)
As the independent variable of this study, AI-enabled personalized recommendation refers to a
technology-driven marketing method. Relying on multi-dimensional user behavioral data including
historical browsing, interaction, cart-adding and purchase records, live-stream e-commerce
platforms construct accurate user portraits through AI algorithms such as collaborative filtering and
deep learning so as to push commodities and content in real time that match users' interest

2.2.2. Organism variable: emotional arousal (mediating variable M)
Emotional arousal serves as the mediating variable of this study. It refers to consumers' aroused
emotional states and psychological involvement, such as anticipation and pleasure, thrills triggered
by external stimuli including AI-enabled personalized recommendations. As a crucial internal
psychological link connecting external stimuli and subsequent behavioral responses, emotional
arousal can significantly promote consumers' immediate consumption decisions.
Emotional arousal was operationalized through consumers' emotional excitement and
psychological engagement during live-stream shopping.
2.2.3. Response variable: impulse buying behavior (dependent variable Y)
Impulse buying behavior is the dependent variable of this study. It refers to consumers' unplanned,
immediate and less deliberative purchase decisions induced by external marketing stimuli in the
live-stream e-commerce context. It is the core behavioral outcome focused on in this research, as
well as a key conversion indicator concerned by live-stream e-commerce platforms and merchants.
Impulse buying behavior is operationalized as consumers' unplanned and immediate purchase
tendencies in live-stream shopping scenarios.
2.3. Theoretical model construction
Based on a clear understanding of the connotation of the S-O-R theory and the definitions of
relevant variables, this study constructs a theoretical model with mediation effects as follows: AI-
enabled personalized recommendation (X) → emotional arousal (M) → impulse buying behavior
(Y).
This model indicates that AI-enabled personalized recommendation may not only directly affect
consumers' impulse buying behavior but also exert an indirect effect via emotional arousal as a
psychological mediating pathway.
2.4. Research hypotheses development
Combining the aforementioned theoretical framework with the classic S-O-R theoretical paradigm,
this paper puts forward a core proposition: AI-driven personalized recommendation algorithms
generate positive effects on customers' impulsive purchasing within live streaming commerce
scenarios. Meanwhile, such intelligent recommendation services can trigger consumers' emotional
excitation, and this emotional state will further encourage impulsive consumption. It is thus inferred
that emotional excitation acts as a mediator variable linking AI personalized recommendations and
impulsive buying. On this basis, four research hypotheses are raised as follows:
H1: AI-based personalized recommendations exert a notably positive effect on consumers'
impulsive purchase intentions.
H2: AI-based personalized recommendations can significantly enhance consumers' emotional
arousal.
H3: Higher emotional arousal level positively predicts consumers' impulsive buying behaviors.

2.5. Questionnaire design and data collection
Based on the research variables and live-stream e-commerce context, this study developed a
questionnaire by adopting and revising mature scales from domestic and international literature. All
measurement items used a 5-point Likert scale [5], ranging from 1 = "strongly disagree" to 5 =
"strongly agree". The questionnaire mainly included demographic information and measurement
items for three core variables: AI-enabled personalized recommendations, emotional arousal, and
impulse purchase behavior.
The measurement items of the three core variables were adapted from well-established scales in
prior studies and modified to fit the live-stream e-commerce context. Specifically, AI-enabled
personalized recommendations were measured with 5 items, mainly reflecting recommendation
accuracy, personalization, and timeliness. Emotional arousal was measured with 4 items, covering
consumers' emotional excitement, pleasure, and psychological engagement during live-stream
shopping. Impulse buying behavior was measured with 5 items, assessing unplanned and immediate
purchase tendencies in live-stream scenarios. To ensure applicability, some expressions were
moderately revised according to the features of AI-enabled recommendations and live-stream
platforms.
The questionnaire was distributed online via the Wenjuanxing mini-program, targeting consumers
with live-stream shopping experience. A total of 220 questionnaires were distributed. After
screening, invalid questionnaires with incomplete information, excessively short response times, or
logical contradictions were excluded. Finally, 200 valid questionnaires were obtained, with an
effective recovery rate of 90.91%.
Among the 200 valid respondents, females accounted for 73.0% and males for 27.0%. In terms of
age, respondents aged 18–25 accounted for the largest proportion (68.0%), followed by those aged
26–35 (20.0%). Regarding educational background, most respondents held a bachelor's degree
(74.0%), while 16.0% had a master's degree or above. Regarding monthly income, the largest group
earned 3,000–6,000 RMB (42.0%). Overall, the sample was dominated by young female consumers
with live-stream shopping experience, which is in line with the mainstream user profile of live-
stream e-commerce in China.
2.6. Reliability and validity test
This study first conducted reliability and validity analysis on the adopted scales. The data analysis
results showed that the Cronbach's α coefficient of each variable was higher than 0.7, indicating that
the overall reliability of the scale used in this study was satisfactory. Meanwhile, the KMO value
was 0.876 and Bartlett's sphericity test yielded significant results. The cumulative variance
explanation rate of the scale reached 71.82%, which was well suited for factor analysis. The overall
structural validity met the standard, laying a valid foundation for subsequent empirical research.

3.1. Main results
The results indicate that AI-enabled personalized recommendations in the live-stream e-commerce
scenarios can positively promote consumers' impulse buying behavior. The more the recommended
content matches user preferences and the more timely the delivery is, the more likely consumers are

AI recommendations can further indirectly stimulate impulse buying by activating individuals'
emotional states. Overall, the internal transmission logic of AI recommendations affecting consumer
behavior is verified, forming a complete path where AI recommendations trigger emotional arousal
and further influence impulse buying.
3.2. Practical implications
In terms of core operational logic, live streaming platforms are able to integrate diversified data
including user browsing tracks and consumption tendencies. By iteratively optimizing
recommendation algorithms, platforms make the recommended content more consistent with users'
real demands and lift the matching degree between audiences and recommended materials [7].
Supported by such technical foundations, sellers can prioritize customers' emotional perceptions.
With the aid of flash promotions, live atmosphere building and scenario-oriented commodity
presentation, merchants stimulate shoppers' shopping sentiments, which lowers users' rational
judgment and ultimately increases transaction conversion rates [8].
When looking at the complete business framework, brand vendors and offline shops need to
abandon duplicated marketing modes. They can implement refined operational plans according to
segmented user groups, deliver customized products and content to target audiences, cut
unnecessary resource consumption and optimize overall marketing returns [9]. To guarantee long-
term steady growth, platforms need to moderately deploy personalized recommendation functions.
Operators need to strike a balance between precise traffic positioning and user experience, strictly
manage content push frequency and strengthen users' data privacy protection to lessen user
weariness, so as to retain stable customer groups and realize sustainable business development [10].
4. Conclusion
Based on the S-O-R theory, this study explores the influence mechanism of AI-enabled personalized
recommendations on consumers' impulse buying behavior in the live-stream e-commerce context.
The results show that AI-enabled personalized recommendations can significantly promote
consumers' impulse buying behavior, and emotional arousal plays a partial mediating role in this
process, which clearly reveals the psychological path of algorithm-driven impulse consumption
behavior. These findings provide practical references for platforms and merchants to optimize their
operation strategies.
However, this study has certain limitations. The sample group is dominated by young females,
resulting in limited representativeness; the cross-sectional data used in the analysis make it difficult
to capture the dynamic changes between variables; in addition, only one mediating path (emotional
arousal) is examined, while other potential influencing factors are not included. Future research can
further expand the sample scope, introduce moderating variables such as platform trust and
consumer personal traits, and conduct verification combined with longitudinal tracking or
experimental design to improve the universality and accuracy of the research conclusions.
