Introduction. Contemporary digital marketing is undergoing profound structural
changes driven by the intensive development of artificial intelligence and machine
learning technologies that underlie the recommendation systems of social media
platforms. Whereas a decade ago influencer marketing was based primarily on a linear
model of content distribution through a chronological subscriber feed, today
personalization algorithms (TikTok’s “ For You,” Instagram Explore, and YouTube’s
recommendation system) determine which content is shown to which users, regardless
of whether those users subscribe to a creator’s page [9; 10; 15]. This fundamentally
reshapes the logic of brand- influencer engagement strategies: the effectiveness of a
publication no longer depends exclusively on the size of a blogger’s audience but is
instead determined by a complex of behavioral signals, including user response speed,
depth of engagement, and relevance to the interests of a specific viewer [12; 15].
The phenomenon of algorithmic content personalization has been examined in the
works of both international [3; 9; 10; 14; 15] and domestic [1; 2] scholars, who
emphasize that the recommendation systems of social networks are forming a
fundamentally new type of media environment, termed “algorithmic publicity, ” in
which brands lose direct control over audience access and are compelled to adapt their
content strategies to platform logic [10; 15]. At the same time, questions concerning
the transformation of influencer selection criteria, shifts in communication formats,
and the emergence of new risks, including algorithmic bias, filter bubbles, and
competition from virtual influencers, remain insufficiently systematized, which
underscores the relevance of this study.
Research Objective and Tasks.  The objective of this study is to identify the
primary directions of transformation in influencer marketing strategies under the
influence of content personalization algorithms on social media platforms. To achieve
this objective, the following tasks were established:
1. to determine the operating principles of contemporary recommendation
algorithms on leading social platforms;

Research Results and Discussion
1. Changes in the Logic of Content Selection and Distribution. The traditional
model of influencer marketing was based on a user’s direct subscription to a blogger:
content appeared in the feed in chronological order, and campaign reach was forecast
primarily by the number of subscribers. The recommendation algorithm s of
contemporary social media platforms employ hybrid models, namely collaborative
filtering (based on the behavior of similar users) and content-based filtering (based on
the characteristics of the content itself, such as hashtags, viewing duration, and speech
tempo) [15]. The TikTok algorithm, in particular, is capable of showing videos to users
who have never subscribed to a creator, relying solely on engagement signals during
the first seconds of viewing [9; 15]. This means that an influencer marketing content
strategy can no longer be built around an “accumulated” audience; the ability of content
to hold a viewer’s attention in real time becomes critically important.

As the diagram in Figure 1 illustrates, the transition from a content distribution
model oriented toward subscriber count to one driven by behavioral engagement
signals constitutes a cross -cutting factor that simultaneously affects influencer
selection criteria, content formats, campaign effectiveness assessment methodology,
and the emergence of new risks associated with the algorithmic environment. This
confirms that the transformation occurring in influencer marketing under the influence
of recommendation algorithms is systemic in nature rather than localized.
2. Transformation of Influencer Selection Criteria and Campaign
Effectiveness Assessment. Meta-analytic studies synthesizing more than one thousand
effects drawn from hundreds of scholarly works demonstrate that the key determinants
of influencer marketing effectiveness are not the overall number of subscribers but
rather publication character istics (the informational and hedonic value of content, as
well as disclosure of its promotional nature) and subscriber characteristics (social
identity and level of trust) [7]. As a result, brands are increasingly selecting micro- and
nano-influencers, whose content platform algorithms promote more actively due to
higher audience engagement rates compared to mega -influencers [7; 10]. Systematic
literature reviews likewise confirm a shift in research focus from descriptive models of
“influenceability” toward computational methods of influencer identification
employing deep learning techniques and network graph analysis [9; 10].
3. New Interaction Formats Under Conditions of Algorithmic
Personalization. Adaptation to the logic of recommendation systems has prompted the
emergence of new influencer marketing formats. First, the share of short -form video
content optimized for the “infinite feed” (TikTok, Reels, and Shorts) is growing, as the
algorithm adjusts content delivery in real time in accordance with audience responses
[14; 15]. Second, the direction of AI -generated virtual influencers is actively
developing, with their personas and content created and adapted algorithmically to the
cultural and behavioral characteristics of target audiences across different regions [11;
13]. Research indicates that virtual influencers are capable of forming emotional
connections with younger audiences (Generation Z and millennials) through
personalized content and consistent communication strategies [11; 13], while
simultaneously raising questions regarding their impact on users’ psychological
perception of appearance (Table 1) [4].
4. Risks and Limitations of the Algorithmic Environment.  Despite the
advantages of personalization, researchers identify a number of systemic risks. First,
recommendation algorithms may reproduce and amplify bias in the selection of content
and influencers, thereby reducing the diversity of information presented to audiences [3].
Second, excessive personalization produces a “filter bubble” effect and content "over-
specialization," limiting the organic reach of new audience segments [15]. Third, the
opacity of algorithmic operations complicates the predictability of marketing outcomes
and necessitates continuous monitoring and flexible adaptation of content strategies [9;
15]. A synthesis of domestic experience indicates that for Ukrainian enterprises, the
primary challenge remains the frequent modification of social network algorithm rules,
which directly affects the visibility of both promotional and organic publications [1; 2].

Conclusions. This study demonstrates that content personalization algorithms on
social media platforms have substantially transformed the logic of influencer
marketing: communication effectiveness is now determined not by the scale of an
influencer's audience but by the quality of behavioral engagement signals, the
relevance of content to the interests of a specific user, and the speed of adaptation to
algorithmic changes. This drives the growing role of micro- and nano-influencers, the
proliferation of short-form video formats, and the emergence of AI -generated virtual
personas as instruments of personalized communication with target audiences.
At the same time, the algorithmic environment generates new risks, including bias
in recommendation systems, the filter bubble effect, the loss of direct brand- to-
audience contact, and the opacity of content ranking criteria. Enterprises are advised to
develop flexible, adaptive content strategies grounded in continuous monitoring of
behavioral metrics, diversification of formats and platforms, and the ethical application
of artificial intelligence technologies in audience communication. A promising
direction for further research is the analysis of regulatory frameworks governing the
transparency of recommendation algorithms and the assessment of the long- term
impact of virtual influencers on consumer trust.
