Abstract
Purpose—Despite a broad consensus on firm internationalization through digital platforms, it
still appears to be a black box mechanism through which emerging market small and medium-
sized enterprises (ESMEs) upgrade their global value chain position.  This study aims to
investigate how artificial intelligence (AI) adoption and strategic agility coupling shape
ESMEs' global value chain positioning through the lens of international marketing capabilities
(IMCs), differentiating international advantage into two dimensi ons: product innovation and
brand upgrading.  Furthermore, it investigates how the depth and breadth of platform
embeddedness moderate this relationship.
Design/methodology/approach—A quantitative approach was employed to analyze data from
322 small and medium -sized enterprises  in China utilizing cross -border platforms. The
research model was tested using structural equation modelling, with data analysis conducted
through SPSS 27, AMOS 24, and STATA to evaluate both the measurement and structural
models.
Findings—The study demonstrates that the coupling of AI adoption and strategic agility
positively influences IMCs, product innovation, and brand upgrading, while IMCs serve as a
mediating mechanism. Additionally, platform embedding characteristics exhibit a nonline ar,
inverted U -shaped moderating effect: a medium level of embedding depth maximizes the
coupling’s positive impact on product innovation, whereas a moderate level of embedding
breadth optimizes its positive effect on brand upgrading.
Originality/value—The study proposes a  holistic framework examining  how platforms'
affordances facilitate identifying and forecasting international market information, and
redeploying resources among platform ecosystems, ultimately driving product and brand value
creation. It advances the dynamic capabilities hierarchy view in international business research
by integrating cross-border platforms and the ESMEs perspective, elucidating how platforms'
higher-order strategic agility shapes embedded ESMEs' lower -order IMCs, fostering
international competitive advantage. Additionally, it examines AI -enabled marketing
capabilities via a broader international advantage lens.

2. Theoretical background
2.1 Coupling AI adoption and strategic agility
AI adoption is defined as the incorporation of technology into an organization’s identity,
integrating machines' ability to identify, interpret, infer, and learn from data to create flexible
and accurate decisions (Mikalef and Gupta, 2021; Dahlke et al., 2 024). In the context of
ESMEs’ internationalization via digital platforms, cross -border platforms’  investment in AI
infrastructure enables ESMEs to overcome traditional infrastructure constraints (Luo  and Bu,
2018; Del Giudice et al., 2019; Wei and Pardo, 2022). For example, Ahi et al. (2022) highlight
that advanced technologies have important implications for firms that govern the structure of
their value chains through digital platform networks. By adopting AI, platforms can generate,
analyze, and act upon real-time data at unprecedented speed and scale (Massa et al., 2023; Liu
et al., 2025). Thus, the cross-border platform not only provides ESMEs with access to foreign
networks for global expansion but also offers a range of advantages through AI adoption ,
supporting their international competitiveness (Hossain et al., 2022; Manis  and Madhavaram,
2023; Dahlke  et al.,  2024). For example, platforms’ AI adoption allows more value to be
created by offering cognitive analyses of global consumer behavior, mining global user profiles
through online footprints and user feedback, facilitating real-time multilingual communication,
and streamlining logistics and payment systems to its participants (Wei and Pardo, 2022; Magni
et al., 2023). Accordingly, ESMEs embedded in the platform constantly reshape their
international competitive advantages by mitigating challenges, such as language
barriers, temporal and spatial limitations  in gathering international market insights, cross -
border payment issues, and logistical complexities.

2.2 International marketing capabilities (IMCs)
IMCs are defined as a firm’s ability to deliver products that meet the needs of target
ed market segments and to deploy marketing resources effectively in turbulent foreign
markets (Eng and Spickett -Jones, 2009; Fang and Zou, 2009; Zahoor and Lew, 2023).
 IMCs are conceptually linked to shifts in international marketing strategies (Fang and
 Zou, 2009). Accordingly, for resource -limited ESMEs, cultivating robust IMCs is criti
cal for securing competitive positions in global value chains. Since AI analytics prov i
de data -driven knowledge, they offer accurate and reliable market information, enablin
g firms to flexibly respond to international market opportunities that might be overloo
ked by humans.  AI-enabled cross-border platforms  thus enhance the ESME’s ability to
 marketing research, strategy, and action stages —key tenets of the IMCs framework
(Huang and Rust, 2021 ). For example, at the marketing research stage, AI can be eff
ectively employed for  data collection,  in-depth market analysis, and customer understa
nding; Progressing to the marketing strategy phase,  AI facilitates precise segmentation
recognition, targeting, and positioning; At the marketing action stage, AI contributes t
o standardization, personalization, and relationalization. This structured applicati on of
AI across different marketing stages underscores its pivotal role in optimizing marketi
ng efficacy and strategic alignment. Despite emerging research on AI -enabled marketin
g capabilities (Huang and Rust, 2021; Manis and Madhavaram, 2023; Zahoor and Le
w, 2023), international marketing scholarship has inadequately examined their role in
global international contexts.
From a hierarchical view of dynamic capability,  successful international development
necessitates higher-order dynamic capabilities for adapting  to the global market environment
and adjusting resource bases. AI operations, in practice, function synergistically with other
resources to enhance ESMEs' capabilities, including diversified marketing tools that optimize
market capabilities (Dahlke et al., 2024).  IMCs serve as vital lower -order operational
capabilities that support various aspects  of core marketing  activities (Winter, 2003). In this
setting, ordinary capabilities are the ESME's international marketing routines (operationalized

10
in product or brand), and we study the processes through which such routines change affected
by platforms’ coupling technical infrastructure and higher -order dynamic capabilities. In this
regard, ESMEs need to utilize platforms’  higher-order dynamic capabi lities for developing
lower-order IMCs that address market-specific demands, such as market conditions, consumer
preferences, cultural nuances, regulatory environments, and distribution channels embedded in
market practices, thereby effectively delivering value in global value chains (Teece et al., 2016;
Ahammad et al., 2021; Schulze and Brusoni, 2022).  Platforms exhibiting superior strategic
agility can co-create value with participants, and highlighting the importance of AI adoption
for ESMEs to identify market demands and emerging opportunities (Pfajfar et al., 2024b).
Thereby, the coupling of AI adoption and strategic agility allows ESMEs to extend, modify, or
create ordinary capabilities, allowing them to respond faster to shifting market demands and
capitalize on emerging opportunities  (Winter, 2003). Accordingly, as lower-order operational
capabilities, IMCs translate customer knowledge into tactical marketing applications, enabling
product innovation and brand development.
Empirical studies have consistently revealed a well-supported relationship between IMCs
and firms’ international performance outcomes, such as financial metrics, export results, and
innovation performance (e.g. Eng and Spickett-Jones, 2009; Chatterjee et al., 2022). However,
the results can vary across different contexts and samples. Nonetheless, the international
marketing literature still lacks more granular advantages research, including new product
innovation and global brand upgrading (Pfajfar et al., 2024b). Furthermore, research has
typically examined IMCs in isolation without adequately accounting for their mediating roles.
This lack of integration into the interaction perspective between digital platforms and ESMEs
limits the explanatory power of ex isting theories in explaining international competitive
advantage heterogeneity among ESMEs via cross -border platforms. Addressing these
questions could help build a richer theoretical understanding of how coupling AI adoption and
strategic agility enables ESMEs to upgrade their global value chain position.

2.3 Global value chains upgrading of ESMEs as a research context
Rapid shifts in technological innovation, market upgrading, global value chain restructuring,
and geopolitical tensions are causing traditional emerging economies small and medium-sized
enterprises (ESMEs) to lose their competitive edge in low -cost, large -scale production
(Adomako et al., 2019; Gölgeci et al.,  2023). ESMEs now face both opportunities and
challenges in upgrading their global value chain to reshape their international advantages.

11
Global value chains consist of interconnected processes in the production, distribution, and
consumption of goods and services worldwide; thus, product innovation and brand upgrading
are critical for enhancing value chain positioning ( Caputo et al., 2022; Orland et al., 2022;
Gölgeci et al., 2023). Unlike small and medium -sized enterprises in developed economies,
ESMEs often suffer from institutional voids and limited resources (Ko et al., 2021; Singh et al.,
2022), which can trap them in a ‘low-end lock-in’, resulting in low-profit margins within
global value chains (Adomako et al., 2019). The development of cross-border platforms should
not only foster growth points and new momentum for foreign trade but also foster independent
brands and innovative development momentum for enterpris es. Consequently,  ESMEs
compete not based emphatically on the institutional and policy environment and resources
shortage, etc. in which they may be disadvantageous, but on the creative utilization and
combination of internal and external resources of the cross-border platforms in which they are
embedded to enhance their international value-added activities, such as producing high-quality,
branded products.
As one of the largest cross-border e-commerce markets, China’s cross-border e-commerce
exports exceeded 1. 9 trillion yuan in 202 4, marking a 15.2% increase and  underscoring the
sector’s pivotal role in emerging economies and global trade growth . The dynamic nature of
the Chinese market presents a valuable context for investigating how cross-border platforms
contribute to support the potential of product innovation and brand upgrades for ESMEs.
However, current research predominantly focuses on the impact of  digital platforms on
international performance and scope, with the literature largely centred on firms from
developed countries. This narrow focus offers limited insights into how ESMEs can leverage
their limited resources and weak formal institutions to strengthen their international standing
(Chandra et al., 2020; Da Rocha et al., 2024; V o et al., 2025). We believe that AI adoption and
strategic agility coupling models have great potential to explain why and how some ESMEs
without possessing traditionally defined monopolistic advantages such as global market power,
brand images, organizational reputation, customer loyalty, and proprietary technologies can
upgrade position in the global value chain. Addressing this gap holds both theoretical and
practical significance in understanding how cross-border platforms can reposition ESMEs from
low-end participants to key innovators and brand leaders (Gölgeci et al., 2023).

3.1 Coupling of AI adoption and strategic agility for international competitive advanta
ge
We propose that coupling AI adoption  with strategic agility creates significant opportunities
for ESMEs to achieve international competitive advantage.  AI adoption includes routines for
data collection and analysis, supporting data -driven decision-making and crafting innovative
scenarios (Verganti et al., 2020; Garbuio and Lin, 2021). Specifically, AI’ s capabilities
for sensing, interpreting and evaluating market data can act as powerful tools for product
creativity and brand enhancement through automation and augmentation (Raisch et al., 2021;
Ameen et al., 2024). For example, generative AI technologies like Open AI’s system facilitate
the expansion of both problem and solution spaces, driving product and brand advantages in
alignment with design thinking principles.
Product innovation has long been a primary avenue for establishing international
competitive advantage, particularly by developing or enhancing products that offer unique
benefits and superior quality compared to those of competitors (Cui and Wu, 2017; Hur tado-
Torres et al., 2018). Cross-border digital platforms deploy AI to provide real-time data across
the value chains; this facilitates collaboration among participants, shortens experimentation
cycles and speeds up international market entry (Mariani and Nambisan, 2021; Gama and
Magistretti, 2023; Del Giudice et al., 2023 ). AI on these platforms also helps aggregate and
identify diverse consumer demands, enhancing the accuracy of market predictions and
empowering ESMEs to bridge cultural and geographical divides, thereby fueling product
innovation (Garbuio et al., 2021; Verganti et al., 2020; Bodhi et al., 2023). Meanwhile, brand
upgrading refers to the ability of export firms to achieve higher customer cognition, satisfaction
and loyalty towards their products compared to foreign competitors (Lang et al., 2023; Zahoor
et al., 2024). When brands operate across different cultures, managing value perceptions
becomes more complex (Spyropoulou et al., 2011). Here, AI assists ESMEs by supporting
targeted advertising and automatically personalised content that resonates with specific cultural
and institutional contexts. This approach facilitates the precise communication of brand image,
improves the international exposure  and dynamic stakeholders ’ interactions, reduces the
difficulty of cross -cultural content adaptation,  and ultimately increases international brand
recognition and customer loyalty (Spyropoulou et al., 2011; Mandler et al., 2020). For example,
AI-driven chatbots offer real-time engagement across time zones, which Mandler et al. (2020)
argue builds trust and credibility, strengthening brands’ market positions abroad.
Strategic agility is particularly crucial for ESMEs’ internationalisation efforts due to the
variable institutional support and rapid, unpredictable changes in global markets (Adomako et

13
al., 2019; Ahammad et al., 2021). It entails the timely sensing of opportunities, rapid decision-
making and adaptive strategic direction adjustments, which are essential for managing
disruptive innovations and navigating unpredictable market conditions (Fo urné et al., 2014;
Zahoor et al., 2022). Given that ESMEs often lack strong brand recognition or cutting -edge
technology, we argue that platforms’ strategic agility allows these ESMEs to leverage platform
resources to reconfigure their existing resource ba se, enabling swift responses to evolving
market trends (Ahammad et al., 2021). This flexibility empowers ESMEs to bridge competitive
gaps, particularly by swiftly aligning resources with international market demands through
digital platforms to enable new product development and enhance brand recognition  (Ameen
et al., 2024). The literature has addressed the role of strategic agility in international business
or international performance (e.g. Fourné et al., 2014; Demir et al., 2021), though mainly
focusing on its isolated effects rather than examining its potential synergy with AI. AI supports
this agility by enabling automated and data -driven decision -making processes, which are
particularly valuable for product innovation and brand positioning (Raisch and  Krakowski,
2021; Gama and Magistretti, 2023). Recent studies comparing optimal profits with and without
AI have found that manufacturers’ optimal profits in both modes consistently increase with
platform power when AI is integrated (Xu et al., 2023). Desp ite these advancements, limited
research has explored the combined impact of AI adoption and strategic agility on driving
product innovation and brand advantage. Thus, we formulate the following hypothesis:
H1: The coupling of AI adoption and strategic agility has a significant positive effect
on (a) product innovation advantage and (b) brand upgrading advantage.

IMCs refer to the ability of ESMEs  to reconfigure marketing resources, effectively ide
ntify foreign marketing needs, introduce new products to meet these needs and deliver
 value in foreign markets, ultimately helping ESMEs create value in the export marke
t (Papa et al., 2022; Zahoor and Lew, 2023). Innovation performance has been extensi
vely observed  by studies on a sample of SMEs operating in various foreign markets;
evidently, SMEs with IMCs are becoming more innovative in international markets (Pf
ajfar e t al., 2024 b). For example,  early on, Eng and Spickett -Jones (2009) indicate tha
t marketing capabilities help Chinese companies manufacture upgrades in international
value chains. A similar mechanism recently revealed indicates that the focal marketing
 capabilities de veloped by born -global firms help in addressing a global market gap o

r being first to market with radical, patented product innovations  (Zahoor and Lew, 2
023; Pfajfar et al., 2024 b). Thus, we maintain that IMCs can engender product innova
tion advantages  for ESMEs.
Moreover, ESMEs that possess  IMCs can realise brand advantage, transitioning from
original equipment manufacturing (OEM) to original design manufacturing (ODM) and,
ultimately, to own-brand manufacturing (OBM) (Eng and Spickett-Jones, 2009). In the area of
international branding, IMCs  encompass specific categories, such as marketing
communications, brand management,  market information acquisition,  interpretation and
dissemination, all of which are expected to influence foreign customers’ mental
responses towards the brand (Chatterjee et al., 2022; Pfajfar et al., 2024b). For example, market
and process knowledge, along with export market  experience, enables firms to effectively
adapt their brands to local characteristics ( Philsoophian et al., 2022; Zahoor et al.,
2024). Notably, managing customers’ brand perceptions is particularly complex, requiring
brands to tailor their positioning to fit local market characteristics.  Spyropoulou et al. (2011)
indicate that an exporting firm’s communication capabi lity, through its market sensing and
linking customer attitudes and behaviour, significantly  enhances its branding
advantage. Collectively, ESMEs with superior IMCs may strengthen their brands’ global
positioning. On these grounds, we hypothesise:
H2: IMCs have a significant positive effect on (a) product innovation advant
age and (b) brand upgrading advantage.

Drawing further on the hierarchical view of dynamic capability (Winter, 2003), we aim to
elucidate that the coupling of AI adoption and strategic agility directly influences IMCs, which,
in turn, mediate the impact of this coupling on product innovation an d brand upgrading
advantages. According to Winter (2003), ordinary capabilities enable short -term operational
success, while dynamic capabilities modify, extend or create these ordinary capabilities.
Previous research argues that higher -order dynamic capab ilities, like strategic agility, do not
directly enhance competitive advantage but do so by shaping lower -order operational
capabilities (Fourné et al., 2014; Ahammad et al., 2021). In this framework, strategic agility
acts as a higher -order dynamic capabi lity, coupling with AI adoption to facilitate the
development of lower-order IMCs, which then strengthen  ESMEs’ international competitive
advantage.

The coupling of AI adoption with strategic agility offers significant potential for
enhancing IMCs. AI can reinforce IMCs by enabling strategic agility to combine or integrate
with customers, partners, global supply chain  and other resources, regardless of geographical
boundaries (Hossain et al., 2022; Manis and Madhavaram, 2023). AI adoption are also
becoming standard for cross -border platforms , m arketing functions and activities that can
benefit from AI, due to its ability to recognize patterns from data (e.g., text mining, speech
recognition, facial recognition) (Huang and Rust, 2021). Specifically, AI can collect and
synthesise vast amounts of global user data from various international markets, while advanced
cognitive computing transforms tacit knowledge into segmentation insights, enabling ESMEs
to uncover latent market positioning, and gain a more comprehensive understanding of market
dynamics within their industry  (Manis and Madhavaram, 2023). For instance, Alibaba
International Station ’s AI -powered risk control agent can swiftly identify market risks in
response to rapidly evolving global trade policies and product standards. Notably, when cross-
border platforms demonstrate strategic flexibility, they can flexibly reallocate and reconfigure
global resources and competencies (Zahoor et al., 2024). In this regard, ESMEs enable leverage
platforms’ powerful resource advantages, such as intelligent supply chains, to expand market
coverage and enhance IMCs, allowing them to quickly adapt to var ying market conditions
across different countries and regions (Zahoor and Lew, 2023). Thus, although, being resource-
constrained, ESMEs may not have the market resources needed to engage in global value -
creation activities, the cross -border platform’s agility enables them to a realignment of any
available resources and the exploitation of opportunities presented by changing global market
conditions (Zahoor et al., 2024). Accordingly, the benefits of AI adoption, coupled with their
agile nature, can en able E SMEs to attain market orientation, marketing planning, and new
product development advantages suited to improving IMCs (Manis and Madhavaram, 2023).
Mainstream studies in the international business domain have extensively discussed the
relevance of strategic agility and proposed its primary aspects  for international performance
and success (Ahammad et al., 2021; Demir et al., 2021; Zahoor et al., 2024). Still, more studies
are needed to connect marketing to agile strategies, particularly in the context of ESMEs,
distinguishing the different underlining capabilities that lead to international advantage (Shams
et al., 2021).  More importantly,  despite the emergence of AI -enabled marketing
capabilities (Manis and Madhavaram, 2023), limited attention has been paid to what impact
combining AI adoption and strategic agility may have on IMCs. Therefore, it is expected that
AI adoption, when coupled with strategic agility, will enhance IMCs, we hypothesize:

H3: Coupling AI adoption with strategic agility has a significant positive effect on
IMCs.

In addition to the direct effects, we anticipate that IMCs mediate the effects of coupling
AI adoption and strategic agility on product innovation and brand upgrading advantage in our
proposed conceptual model.  An enterprise that effectively integrates its IMCs is better
positioned to leverage the AI and strategic agility of cross -border platforms to enhance
creativity in new products and brand development. It is more likely to generate added value in
global value chains because it can fulfill marketing -related needs. IMCs facilitate customer
knowledge acquisition, price adjustments, product and brand development, and the strategic
adaptation of marketing tactics, enabling ESMEs to target foreign customers with differentiated
offerings (Zahoor and Lew, 2023).
Product innovation is mainly reflected in tangible goods R&D and production, and service
innovation and process (Cui and Wu, 2017). Product innovation decisions typically involve
utilizing AI-enabled marketing capabilities to determine the optimal product attributes for
development and employing test markets to decide whether and to what degree the product will
be accepted (Huang and Rust, 2021). For instance, AI can assist ESMEs in analyzing foreign
user feedback from social and e-commerce platforms to identify new product features or design
directions; AI can also support ESMEs in simulation testing, thereby reducing R&D costs. One
especially important research area concerns how well prediction AI-driven algorithms forecast
demand for really new markets, develop innovative products, and expedite the overall product
development process (Davenport et al., 2020). Furthermore, the strategic flexibility offered by
platforms allows ESMEs to adapt more effectively to AI -driven predictions (Shams et al.,
2021), for example, flexible global supply chain models enable ESMEs to adjust production
plans based on market feedback swiftly, accelerating new product iteration.
Moreover, the absence of brand effects not only diminishes the added value of products
but also constrains the long -term international competitiveness of ESMEs. B uilding strong
brands requires ESMEs to employ positioning and marketing  mix strategies to  shape foreign
customers’ perceptions (Pfajfar et al., 2024b). AI-driven insights can tailor advertisements for
foreign markets by classifying customers and evaluating potential advertisements based on
their effectiveness in eliciting desired responses, thereb y enhancing brand influence (Manis
and Madhavaram, 2023).  Besides, c oupling AI adoption and strategic agility  provides a
foundation for IMCs to achieve effective allocation of advertising resources and manage cross-
border customer relationships, thereby mitigating the constraints imposed by ESMEs ’ limited

investments in marketing and brand building . IMCs facilitate brand differentiation, drive
innovative branding strategies and align with international customers’  preferences, thereby
enhancing brand image and perceived value (Zahoor  et al., 2024). This enhanced positioning
elevates ESMEs in global val ue chains by fostering customer loyalty and enabling premium
pricing (Eng and Spickett-Jones, 2009). Thus, we propose the following hypotheses:
H4: IMCs positively mediate the effects of coupling AI adoption and strategic agility
on (a) product innovation advantage and (b) brand upgrading advantage.

3.3 Moderating effects of ESMEs’ platform embedding characteristics
We refer to embedding breadth  as the number of digital platforms in which ESMEs
participate, encompassing various types, such as e-commerce, social and innovation platforms
(Kobarg et al., 2019). Below,  we will discuss how embedding breadth is associated w ith the
effects of coupling AI adoption  and strategic agility  on ESMEs’ international advantage,
specifically in terms of product innovation and brand upgrading.
More specifically, when embedding breadth shifts from low to medium, ESMEs can
access a wider variety of data through diverse platforms, enhancing their potential for creative
recombination, complementary alignment within digital ecosystems and differentia ted
platform positioning, ultimately facilitating product innovation and brand upgrading (Hurtado-
Torres et al., 2018). In such conditions, the coupling of AI adoption and strategic agility enables
the integration and analysis of diverse international market information, resulting in knowledge
spillovers and unique product innovation advantages derived from heterogeneous resources,
distributed innovation and cooperation among participants (Li et al., 2019; Hagedoorn et al.,
2018). For example, interaction and reach platforms mostly comprise social media, facilitating
product exchanges and providing access to essential data on prices, location and reputation.
Additionally, different platforms allow ESMEs to tailor branding strategies based on platform
positioning and target demographics, ultimately advancing brand equity. For example, AI -
driven rankings and review scores on e-commerce platforms can communicate product quality
to foreign consumers, while social platforms facilitate interactions that build brand  trust
(Cutolo and Kenney, 2020). Consequently, increasing embedding breadth amplifies the effects
of coupling AI adoption and strategic agility on ESMEs’ international advantages.
By contrast, when embedding breadth shifts from medium to high, ESMEs may encounter
a ‘learning trap’, increase in information processing, resource dispersion and attention
allocation challenges (Hagedoorn et al., 2018; Kobarg et al., 2019). As platform types increase,

18
the degree of complexity of the portfolio and the difficulty of information processing,
coordination, and integration across platforms also escalate (Mariani and Nambisan, 2021; Li
et al., 2022).  In such conditions, even with the adoption of AI and high st rategic agility, the
limited attention ESMEs can allocate across multiple platforms  may restrict resources for
innovation activities (Kobarg et al., 2019 ; Shen et al., 2025 ). Additionally, various platforms
can lead to inconsistent brand messaging, hinderi ng consumers’ ability to form a clear brand
identity and negatively impacting brand perception (Hagedoorn et al., 2018). For example, Li
et al.  (2022) discover that multinational enterprises with more diversified digital platform
attention scope experience  constrained international sales.  In short, initially, ESMEs benefit
from embedded variety in digital platforms; however, excessive diversification can diminish
these advantages due to rising coordination costs and declining information quality  (Hurtado-
Torres et al., 2018; Ko et al.,  2021). Combining the discussions above, we formally propose
that:
H5: The breadth of platform embedding has an inverted U-shaped moderating effect
on the positive relationship between coupling AI adoption and strategic agility and (a)
product innovation advantage and (b) brand upgrading advantage.

Embedding depth reflects the resource investment ESMEs dedicate to cross-border digital
platform activities and the intensity of their interactions with platform owners and participants
(Li et al., 2019; Kobarg et al., 2019). We also anticipate that embedd ing depth moderates the
effect of coupling AI adoption and strategic agility on international advantage.
More precisely, as embedding depth shifts from low to medium, ESMEs invest targeted
resources and relationships into digital platforms, fostering close collaboration and tacit
knowledge transfer (Li et al., 2019). Dahlke et al. (2024) argue that the effectiveness of direct
transmission of AI knowledge relies on the strength of links and the depth of shared knowledge
among partners. By deeply engaging with digital platforms, ESMEs can establish AI -based
organisational learning routines and extract AI-driven international marketing insights (Manis
and Madhavaram, 2023). This engagement promotes resource transfer and integration,
allowing ESMEs to leverage platform agility to shorten R&D cycles (Hagedoorn et al., 2018).
In addition, AI -powered, strategically ag ile organisations establish access mechanisms and
bolster brand credibility.  ESMEs grow alongside these platforms, resulting in a bundling of
their brand images.  The global influence of large digital platforms enhances ESMEs’
international visibility, particularly when consumers are cautious about new brands, as platform
credibility fosters trust (Dahlke et al., 2024). Furthermore, frequent interactions with global

19
consumers also sustain a strong brand community atmosphere (Spyropoulou et al., 2011).
Hence, increased embedding depth positively moderates the effect of coupling AI adoption and
strategic agility on product innovation and brand upgrading.
However, this positive moderating effect may diminish as embedding  depth rises. A
higher embedding depth is similar to a fairly closed system for AI adopters  that may limit
ESMEs’ abilities to value and process distant and novel information  (Hagedoorn et a l.,
2018). This may lead to a ‘familiarity trap’, where knowledge integration restricts the flow of
new insights and reduces the novelty of the platform’s knowledge base (Cutolo and Kenney,
2021; Shen et al., 2025). Embedding depth can also result in relational inertia, making ESMEs
rigid in their platform type preferences. Over -dependence on specific platforms restricts
strategic flexibility and hinders resource reallocation for relevant product
innovation (Hagedoorn et al., 2018). In addition, ESMEs employing a single-platform strategy
may encounter power imbalances,  as platform control limits their ability to implement
independent branding strategies. Furthermore, a lack of marketing channel diversification can
impede effective brand promotion (Cutolo and  Kenney, 2021). Considering the discussion
above, we postulate that varying levels of platform embedding depth affect ESMEs’ ability to
develop new products and upgrade brands in the global markets:
H6: The depth of platform embedding has an inverted U-shaped moderating effect
on the positive relationship between coupling AI adoption and strategic agility and (a)
product innovation advantage and (b) brand upgrading advantage.

4. Research methods
4.1 Study setting and data
To test the hypotheses, this study employed a questionnaire -based survey to explore how
Chinese SMEs build international competitive advantage through cross -border platforms,
focusing on their embedding of B2B and B2C platforms. The sample data was collect ed from
Chinese SMEs for the following reasons, justifying our selection as the research background.
First, China is a key player in the global value chain, with a vast number of export SMEs and
cross-border platforms. By 2024, China had over 120,000 cross-border e-commerce firms, and
the number of independent cross -border e-commerce sites had surpassed 200,000, marking a
significant advancement in the internationalization of SMEs through cross -border platforms.
Second, Chinese SMEs are advancing from the middle and low to higher ends of the global
value chain, enhancing the added value of export products to meet the global demand for
personalized, high-quality, and branded goods. For example, Alibaba International Station has
introduced a range of AI tools for SMEs, including text translation, keyword extraction, product
page generation, and market analysis. These tools create new opportunities for product
innovation and brand upgrading of SMEs. Currently, over 30,000 SMEs globally use Alibaba’s
AI business assistant. Therefore, the pursuit of upgrading the global value chain by Chinese
SMEs through applying cross -border platforms’ AI and strategic agility provides an ideal
context to test our research model.
To ensure accuracy and cultural appropriateness, the survey was translated into Chinese
using standard translation and back -translation procedures (Brislin, 1980). Moreover, b efore
conducting the actual survey and following discussions on the proposed questionnaire, we pre-
tested it with five business professionals to ensure the questions were clear, easily answerable,
and relevant to the industry. Their feedback led to revisions that improved clarity and finalized
the questionnaire design.
After finalizing the questionnaire, we engaged a third -party independent survey agency
from the Wen-Juan-Xing database, one of China’s largest data collection platforms, which has
over 620,000 registered managers and has completed 21.9 billion surveys by 2024. We selected
SMEs in China, where classification is based on companies employing fewer than 2,000 staff
typically defined as SMEs (Tian et al., 2018). In the contract, we clearly stated our data
collection requirements, including the definition of SMEs, the random sampling of SMEs from
different industrial sectors, and the inclusion of multinational companies, foreign trade firms,
and businesses engaged in cross-border operations. In addition, our respondents had extensive

21
experience in business analytics and operational management, with the ability to apply these
skills across various business functions.
They met three key criteria: (i) employment in foreign trade firms or departments engaged
in international business; (ii) involvement with cross-border digital platform-related activities;
and (iii) holding a managerial position. This process led to the id entification of 6,600 eligible
managers, to whom the online link to the final questionnaire was emailed for participation in
the survey to test our hypotheses. Meanwhile, the questionnaire included a dummy variable for
participation in cross -border platforms and a multiple -choice question regarding the specific
platforms used for international business. The platforms listed included Alibaba International
Station, Global Express, Amazon, TikTok, Temu, SHEIN, and others. We asked SME managers
to choose at lea st one cross -border platform through which their firms had internationalized
their activities and to answer the questions from this perspective.
Ultimately, a total of 949 questionnaires were collected, yielding a 14.38% response rate,
with 322 valid responses, representing a 33.94% effective response rate. The established
criteria confirmed that the sample of Chinese SMEs were actively utilizing digital platforms to
gain a competitive edge in the global value chain. Anonymity was fully maintained throughout
the data collection process. Table 1 summarizes the sample demographics, shows that a

embed one platform but combine several platforms. All sample respondents are aware of the
benefits of internationalization by embedding cross-border platforms and ongoing product and
brand pilot initiatives around the world.

5.4 Common method bias
To address potential common method bias (CMB) from single-informant data, we implemented
several preventive approaches. During data collection, we included redundant questions in the
questionnaire to minimise simultaneity bias and the effects of common method variance (CMV)
(Chang et al., 2010). Additionally, respondents were kept unaware of the study’s objectives to
reduce evaluation concerns and social desirability bias and maintain the integrity of their
responses. To further assess CMB, we conducted Har man’s single-factor test, which revealed
that a single factor accounted for only 33.08% of the total variance —well below the 50%
threshold (Podsakoff et al., 2003), indicating minimal CMB risk. Moreover, the results of the
single latent factor analysis  (χ2/df = 6.151; RMSEA = 0.127; GFI = 0.571;  CFI = 0.516; TLI
= 0.483; IFI = 0.519) differed from those of the six -factor confirmatory model ( χ2/df=
1.361; RMSEA = 0.034; GFI = 0.900; CFI = 0.967; TLI = 0.964; IFI = 0.968).
We employ Kock’s (2015) full collinearity test to complement the previously suggested
collinearity test. According to Kock and Lynn (2012), collinearity is typically indicated when
the variance inflation factor (VIF) exceeds 3.3, suggesting the presence of common method
variance (CMV). In our analysis, all VIF values remain below this threshold  (VIF<1.101),
confirming that any potential effects of CMV are negligible.  In addition, to establish the
validity and reliability of the higher -order construct of cou pling of AI adoption and strategic
agility, we first assessed the weights of the formative lower -order constructs on their
corresponding higher -order constructs (comprising two first -order constructs). All weights
were significant and positively associated with their assigned higher-order construct. Next, we
examined potential multicollinearity among the first -order constructs. All variance inflation
factor (VIF) values for the first -order constructs were below the 3.3  threshold, indicating that
multicollinearity was not a concern within our sample (see Table 9).

6.Discussion
6.1 Discussion of results
Digital technologies have significantly influenced international business, and SMEs have
increasingly leveraged digital platforms to expand internationally in recent years  (Li et al.,
2019). These platforms, often multi -sided or two -sided, connect foreign customers with
independent SME providers. Beyond becoming important conduits for international
transactions, cross-border platforms serve as vital marketing channels for resource-constrained
ESMEs, enabling them to secure a favourable global position in bo th product and brand. Our
study investigates the role of AI adoption and strategic agility of platforms in promoting the
international competitive advantage of ESMEs. We found that the coupling of AI adoption and
strategic agility allows ESMEs to identify target foreign international opportunities and access
market-specific insights and resources for product innovation and brand upgrading. Moreover,
our results indicate that IMCs mediate the relationship between this coupling  and two key
international advantages. ESMEs can develop their IMCs through digital platforms to fulfil
foreign market customers’ expressed and latent needs, thus promoting international
competitiveness.
More importantly, the results highlight that platform embedding characteristics are crucial
moderating factors.  Specifically, embedding breadth has an inverted U -shaped moderating
effect on the relationship between the coupling of AI with strategic agility and brand upgrading

30
advantage. That is, embedding breadth improves the effect of coupling AI with strategic agility
on brand upgrading up to an optimal point (at a medium level). We also find that
embedding depth has an inverted U-shaped moderating effect on the relationship between the
coupling of AI with strategic agility and product innovation advantage. In other words, the
effect of embedding depth on product innovation advantage becomes stronger when the depth
of enterprise embeddedness in the platform is from low to medi um, while when the depth of
platform embeddedness is from medium to high, the impact of coupling on brand upgrading
advantages weakens.  These findings imply that while network engagement is beneficial,
excessive breadth or depth does not always yield superior performance.
6.2 Theoretical implications
Building on the body of research that examines internationalization through digital plat
forms, this study's objective was to try to understand if and under what conditions cr
oss-border platforms can lead to the upgrading of global value chains for ESMEs.   T
o address this question, we consider cross -border platforms from the standpoint of key
 resources and capabilities  that may provide ESMEs embedded within these platforms
with a competitive advantage.  This study makes three significant theoretical contribut io
ns.
First, to the best of our knowledge, our study is one of the first to explore how
 ESMEs can enhance product innovation and brand upgrading by coupling AI adoptio
n and strategic agility into platform embedding strategies,  which is crucial for overco
ming the "low -end lock -in" in global value chains  and achieving global sustainable de
velopment goals.  As a second -order aggregate concept where the effects of coupling o
f AI adoption  and strategic agility are considered in totality. Such a dualistic conceptu
alization helps move the field of internationalization through digital platforms forward
as it integrates previous independent perspectives. Specifically, this study extends the i
mpact of AI adoption to international markets, expanding existing research on the r ela
tionship between digitalization and SME internationalization and responding to calls fo
r deeper exploration of the intersection of digital platforms and SME internationalizati
on (Coviello et al., 2017).  Although previous  international business literatur e examined
 ESMEs’ use of digital platforms for more cost -effective market entry (Li et al., 201
9), it is still necessary to combines the emerging technological resources and higher -or
der dynamic capabilities perspectives, extending the higher -level impacts  (Coviello et a
l., 2017).   Digital platforms can accelerate and expand internationalization for ESMEs,
 but they may also fall into a “virtuality trap,”  raising concerns about the sustainabilit

31
y of competitive advantage (Da Rocha et al., 2024).  Our research demonstrates that A
I adoption, as an advanced digital technology, when coupled with strategic agility, pla
ys a crucial role in driving product innovation and brand upgrading.  This coupling all
ows ESMEs to predict international market trends more accurately, and the value of
AI itself needs to be derived by pushing for utilization and combination, in different
ways, of the ecosystem ’s actors.
Second, this study contributes to the dynamic capability hierarchy view literature
by emphasizing the pivotal role of robust IMCs in mediating the relationship between
 AI adoption, strategic agility, and international competitive advantage (Zollo and Wint
er, 2002 ; Winter, 2003; Schulze and Brusoni, 2022). By providing empirical insight in
to higher -order dynamic capabilities that drive “regular” operational capabilities for glo
bal value chain upgrading, this research offers a significant opportunity to advan ce stu
dies on cross -border platforms and SME interactive relationships (Eng and Spickett -Jo
nes, 2009). Previous research has examined the antecedents of digital platform use in
internationalization, focusing on internal factors like firm resources and capa bilities, as
well as organizational factors such as marketing and international orientation (e.g., Li
et al., 2019; Da Rocha et al., 2024; Gong et al., 2024). Whereas, the crucial perspect
ive of the dynamic interaction between digital platforms and SMEs re mains underexpl
ored. While not all marketing capabilities have uniformly positive consequences on int
ernational competitive advantage, theoretically, coupling the appropriate platform's AI a
doption with strategic agility can enhance our understanding of IM Cs and their potenti
al for product innovation and brand upgrading.
Additionally, this study also responds to Pfajfar et al. (2024b), who advocate for
the application of IMCs within intricate international value chains by highlighting the
critical role of robust IMCs in enabling product innovation and brand upgrading withi
n the context of emerging economies  (Eng and Spickett -Jones, 2009).  Existing IMC re
search has predominantly focused on developed economies (e.g., Zahoor and Lew, 202
3), studies in emerging economies provide unique insights by exploring the direct rela
tionship between focal capabilities and outcomes, such as company adaptiveness, expor
t performance, and financial performance (Pfajfar et al., 2024a). Our findings, which f
ocus on the specific effects of AI -enabled marketing, reveal the role of ESMEs advan
cing in internationalization without yet attaining global corporate status.
Third, our study examines the nonlinear moderating role of platform embedding b
readth and depth in the direct relationship between coupling AI adoption and strategic

32
 agility and two distinct outcomes: product innovation advantage and brand upgrading
advantage. While existing research has pointed out that the boundary conditions of int
ernationalization were mostly focused on  macro-level contingencies, such as institution
al pressures, foreign market uncertainty, and country -specific environmental factors (e.
g. Ado mako et al., 2019; Ko et al.,  2021). However, little is known about how platfo
rm embedding characteristics  shape the effectiveness of AI adoption and strategic agili
ty. This nuanced understanding provides a more refined model for theoretical predictio
ns and enriches the academic discourse on the embedding breadth and depth and its r
ole in shaping ESMEs’ product innovation and brand upgrading  advantage.
We observed embedding depth has an inverted U-shaped moderating effect on the positive
relationship between coupling AI adoption and strategic agility and product innovation
advantage, however, it does not moderate the relationship between coupling AI adoption with
strategic agility  and brand upgrading. The findings suggest that greater depth in platform
embeddedness facilitates the transfer of tacit knowledge generated by AI, thus reducing the risk
of product innovation, and enhancing  the exploratory level for innovation. However, over -
reliance on specific platforms limits access to diverse market information, creating a
‘familiarity trap’ that hinders the reallocation of resources necessary for product innovation.
This pattern consistent with Kobarg et al.'s (2019) observation of a similar inverted U-shaped
relationship between collaboration depth and radical innovation.  Similarly, the impact of
embedding breadth on brand upgrading follows an inverted U-shape. ESMEs embedded across
multiple platforms enable SMEs  to harness platform -specific differences, tailor brand
strategies, and deliver targeted brand messages underpinned by platform positioning and user
preferences, driving overall brand exposure. However, excessive embedding risks a  ‘learning
trap’, where limited attention allocation hinders the integration of fragmented multi -platform
information, potentially  leading to inconsistent brand messages  received by users, thus
damaging the brand image.  This finding extends the research of Xiao et al.  (2020), who
highlighted the significance of international network breadth in enhancing global performance,
our findings cautioned that an excessive network scope may dilute brand focus, potentially
undermining brand equity.

6.3 Practical implications
The findings of this study carry several crucial implications for managers striving to global
value chain upgrade  via digital platforms . First, owners of cross -border platforms should
recognize AI adoption and strategic agility as critical drivers of participants' product innovation

33
and brand upgrading. AI adoption significantly enhances the platform's potential for identifying
international market opportunities by collecting, integrating, analyzing, and leveraging user
and international market knowledge. This enables efficiently and effectively redeploying their
resources to value -creating and value -capturing higher -yield activities as AI -driven insight
warrants. However, AI adoption can be costly and needs to be married to a sound strategy
agility. AI provides direction and goals for  agility, while agility supports the effective
implementation of AI -driven insight. Without considering such a coupling relationship, the
value and necessity of AI adoption cannot be properly presented. Therefore, incorporating
strategic agility throughout  AI adoption processes is crucial, ensuring a smooth creation and
protecting the value of new digital tools.
Second, IMC is a key mechanism through which the coupling of AI adoption and strategic
agility affects product innovation and brand strategies. ESMEs  should build efficient
capabilities to cater to international customers’ requirements, develop cross -functional
marketing processes specific to foreign markets, innovate routines, and appeal to local clients
while maintaining global brand identity. This approach enables them to effectively translate
market-specific insights gained through digital platforms int o actionable global product and
brand strategies. For example, this can be achieved through platform data assetization,
intelligent marketing systems, and modular product platforms. Consequently, ESMEs should
optimize their IMCs to develop a set of dynamic  marketing skills and competencies through
responsive and efficient cross -functional market processes to adapt to the changing global
market environments, competing products and distribution channels, and customer needs.
Third, managers should carefully consider product attributes and brand positioning needs
when selecting cross-border platforms that align with their target foreign markets. In particular,
striking a balance between platform embedding breadth and depth is essentia l. Platforms are
called “two-sided markets” or “multi -sided markets”, equipped with mature marketing tools
and global resource networks to expand ESMEs’ international marketing channels and product
international visibility through search and promoti on functions. However, excessive
diversification across multiple platforms complicates market information management, while
over-reliance on a single platform constrains strategic autonomy. To optimize international
competitiveness, our results indicate that when embedding breadth at a medium level, coupling
AI with strategic agility significantly benefits brand upgrading. This finding suggests
that ESMEs aiming to  enhance their brand advantages should appropriately maintain
cooperative relationships with multiple cross-border platforms. If they prefer to pursue product
innovation advantages, ESMEs should prioritize establishing close collaborative relationships

34
over-diversification, as economies of scope provide limited applicability in platform-based new
product development context.

6.4 L imitations and future research
While this study provides an initial step in understanding how AI coupling with strategic agility
should be organized within platforms and how it drives international value creation, this
research has certain limitations that offer avenues for future resea rch. Our findings are based
on data from SMEs in China. While this region provides a relevant and timely context due to
its proactive stance on digital platform use in internationalization, the results may not be fully
generalizable to other geographical s ettings or international models. Future studies could test
the robustness of our framework and compare cross-national contexts, to assess how regulatory,
cultural, and infrastructural variations influence the effects of AI-enabled strategic agility and
SMEs’ international competitive advantage.
In addition, while we recognized platform embedding characteristics by studying the
moderating role of embedding breadth and depth, other characteristics of digital platforms may
also affect their outcomes in internationalization. This suggests fruitful op portunities to
examine additional platform characteristics, particularly typological variations such as sales,
interaction, and innovation platforms operating in domestic versus foreign contexts.
Specifically, future work should therefore investigate how S MEs strategically deploy distinct
platform types to achieve specific objectives, particularly investigating how interaction
platforms drive brand upgrading while sales platforms facilitate product innovation.
Finally, self-reported data from SME managers regarding platform AI adoption and agility
perceptions risks introducing bias. Future studies should incorporate secondary data for
objective measures to strengthen the robustness and reliability of the results . Addressing this
limitation would advance understanding of how cross -border platforms enhance SMEs' post -
entry competitive advantage across diverse contexts and conditions. Furthermore, the evolving
nature of AI technologies introduces temporal limitation s, as current platform tools and
implementation strategies may soon become obsolete. Future scholars might investigate
longitudinal studies to track the evolution of AI infrastructures and their strategic integration
over time, assessing whether their impa cts on marketing and innovation remain stable or
fluctuate in response to technological advancements and market developments.

7.Conclusion
To conclude, our research investigates how cross -border platforms serve as catalysts for
ESMEs' upgrading within global value chains. We address the underexplored coupling role of

35
AI adoption and strategic agility and its impact on product innovation and brand upgrading.
These effects vary across platform embedding characteristics, depending on the breadth and
depth of ESMEs embedded in the platform. Additionally, drawing on the dyn amic capability
hierarchy as a lens for interaction between platforms and ESMEs, we establish IMCs' mediating
function in how the coupling of  AI adoption and strategic agility shapes product innovation
and brand upgrading advantage. Our arguments and findi ngs, therefore, suggest that the
deployment of AI infrastructure and incorporation strategy agility can be valuable instruments
for platform owners to strengthen complementary international competitiveness, including
product innovation and brand upgrading.  Overall, as digital platforms become increasingly
prominent viable channels for ESMEs participating in the global value chain, we hope this
study will stimulate more future research on the relationship between digital platform use in
internationalization and international marketing.
