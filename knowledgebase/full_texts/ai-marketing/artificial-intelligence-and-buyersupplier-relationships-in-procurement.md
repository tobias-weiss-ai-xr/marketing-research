Abstract
This study investigates the influence of Artificial Intelligence (AI) applications on buyer–supplier
relationship quality in Nigeria's mobile manufacturing sector, with specific reference to TECNO
Mobile Nigeria. Drawing on the Commitment-Trust Theory of Relationship Marketing (Morgan
& Hunt, 1994), the study operationalises AI through three proxies: Predictive Analytics (PA),
Automation of Processes (AP), and AI Risk Management (AIR). A survey research design was
adopted, and structured questionnaires were administered to 250 employees and supply chain
partners purposively selected from TECNO Mobile's procurement, logistics, IT, and supplier
management functions. Data were analysed using descriptive statistics (frequencies, means,
standard deviations) and one-sample t-tests benchmarked against the scale's neutral midpoint
(μ₀ = 3.00). All three null hypotheses were rejected at p < 0.001. Predictive Analytics recorded an
aggregate mean of 3.88 (SD = 0.34; t(249) = 40.53), Automation of Processes 3.91 (SD = 0.36;
t(249) = 40.05), and AI Risk Management 3.92 (SD = 0.36; t(249) = 40.47), all significantly above
the neutral midpoint. Findings confirm that respondents perceive each AI dimension as positively
and meaningfully contributing to trust, efficiency, transparency, and long-term relational stability
with suppliers. The study contributes empirical evidence from an under-studied domain in the
AI-supply chain literature, Nigeria's mobile manufacturing sector, and offers practical guidance
for practitioners and policymakers pursuing AI-enabled supply chain transformation in emerging
economies.

Introduction
The integration of Artificial Intelligence (AI) into supply chain management (SCM) has
emerged as one of the most consequential developments in contemporary operations
management. By enabling real-time data processing, predictive decision-making, and

intelligent automation, AI has transformed traditional supply chains into adaptive,
resilience-oriented ecosystems (Wamba et al., 2023Wamba et al., 2020; Ivanov, 2023). A
particularly salient outcome of AI -enabled SCM is the reconfiguration of buyer –supplier
relationships (BSRs), the relational and operational interactions between purchasing
organisations and their supply partners that determine supply chain performance, trust,
and long-term stability (Lambert, 2019).
Globally, the business case for AI in SCM is well established. Gartner (2024) reports that a
majority of manufacturers now deploy AI-driven solutions for demand forecasting, supplier
performance analysis, and inventory optimisation. Major multinationals have embedded AI
into their procurement and logistics systems to monitor supplier compliance, reduce costs,
and enhance operational resilience (Ivanov & Dolgui, 2022; Riad et al., 2024). The
disruptions of the past decade also exposed the limits of reactive, linear supply models, and
this accelerated the adoption of AI -enabled resilience frameworks that anticipate
disruptions and optimise alternative sourcing (Baryannis, Validi, Dani, & Antoniou, 2023).
In Africa, AI adoption  in SCM has gained momentum, supported by the continent's
expanding digital economy (World Bank, 2024). Studies from Kenya, South Africa, Ghana,
and Morocco increasingly demonstrate that AI-driven forecasting, supplier evaluation, and
resource-efficiency t ools can improve supply chain visibility and resilience even under
significant infrastructural constraints (Daraojimba et al., 2024; Manu et al., 2024; Peprah et
al., 2025). Nigeria, Africa's largest economy, has articulated this ambition through its
National Artificial Intelligence Strategy (NITDA, 2023). Yet empirical evidence linking AI
adoption to BSR quality in the Nigerian manufacturing context remains sparse.
Nigeria's mobile manufacturing sector, anchored by brands such as TECNO, Infinix, and itel,
serves a large and fast -growing smartphone user base and is among the fastest -growing
consumer electronics markets in Africa (GSMA, 2024). The sector's supply chains are,
however, constrained by currency instability, logistical bottlenecks, and infrastruct ural
deficits (Ifere et al., 2022). These conditions make efficient, trust -based buyer –supplier
interactions not merely desirable but operationally critical. TECNO Mobile Nigeria, as the
sector leader, presents an important empirical site for examining whether AI adoption can
meaningfully enhance BSR quality under such conditions.
Existing studies on AI and BSRs have largely focused on technologically advanced
economies, with limited integration of multiple AI dimensions within a single relational
model applicable to emerging markets (Al -Shboul, 2024; Osewe et al., 2026). This study
addresses that gap by empirically examining three AI proxies, Predictive Analytics,
Automation of Processes, and AI Risk Management, and their perceived influence on BSR
outcomes at TECNO Mobile Nigeria. It is grounded in the Commitment -Trust Theory of
Relationship Marketing (Morgan & Hunt, 1994), which explains how relational mechanisms
of trust and commitment mediate the exchange behaviours this study measures.

Statement of the Problem
Despite the well -documented global business case for AI -enabled supply chain
management, Nigerian manufacturing firms, and mobile handset manufacturers in
particular, continue to operate buyer –supplier relationships under conditions of
information asymmetry, delayed communication, inconsistent quality assurance, and
limited algorithmic governance. Where AI tools such as predictive analytics, workflow
automation, and risk -monitoring systems have been introduced, their perceived
contribution to the relational dimensions of supply chain performance (trust, transparency,
confidence, and long -term stability) has rarely been examined using a theory -driven,
quantitative approach specific to the Nigerian mobile manufacturing context.
Furthermore, much of  the existing AI -supply chain literature either treats AI as a single
undifferentiated construct or restricts its theoretical grounding to adoption -oriented
frameworks such as the Technology –Organisation–Environment (TOE) model, which
explain why firms ado pt AI but not how that adoption translates into relational outcomes
between buyers and suppliers. This leaves a conceptual and empirical gap: it is not clear,
from a relationship-marketing standpoint, how strongly Nigerian supply chain practitioners
perceive specific AI capabilities as building the trust and commitment on which durable
buyer–supplier relationships depend. This study was designed to close that gap by
empirically testing, through the lens of the Commitment-Trust Theory, whether Predictive
Analytics, Automation of Processes, and AI Risk Management are perceived to significantly
and positively influence buyer–supplier relationship quality at TECNO Mobile Nigeria.

Objectives of the Study
The general objective of this study is to investigate t he influence of Artificial Intelligence
applications on buyer –supplier relationship quality in Nigeria's mobile manufacturing
sector, with specific reference to TECNO Mobile Nigeria. The specific objectives are to:
i. examine the effect of Predictive Analytics on trust and reliability in TECNO Mobile
Nigeria's buyer–supplier relationships;
ii. assess the ways in which Automation of Processes improves efficiency,
transparency, and coordination between TECNO Mobile Nigeria and its suppliers;
and
iii. determine how AI Risk Management contributes to confidence and long -term
stability in TECNO Mobile Nigeria's buyer–supplier relationships.

Research Questions
In line with the objectives above, the study addresses the following research questions:
i. How does P redictive Analytics influence trust and reliability in TECNO Mobile
Nigeria's buyer–supplier relationships?
ii. In what ways does Automation of Processes improve efficiency, transparency, and
coordination between TECNO Mobile Nigeria and its suppliers?

Significance of the Study
This study is  relevant to several groups of stakeholders. For practitioners and managers
within TECNO Mobile Nigeria and comparable mobile manufacturing firms, the findings
provide an evidence base for prioritising investment across predictive analytics, process
automation, and AI governance as levers for strengthening supplier relationships. For
Nigerian policymakers, particularly the National Information Technology Development
Agency (NITDA), the study offers empirical grounding for sector -specific AI governance
guidelines that can support trustworthy AI adoption in manufacturing supply chains. For
suppliers and supply chain partners, the study points to the relational value of transparent,
well-governed AI systems, which may inform how they engage with AI-enabled buyers. For
academics, the study extends the Commitment -Trust Theory into an AI -enabled,
emerging-economy manufacturing context, offering a replicable empirical framework
linking specific AI capabilities to relationship -quality outcomes that can be extended to
other sectors, firms, and African country contexts.

Scope of the Study

Theoretical Framework and Literature Review
Conceptual Review
Artificial Intelligence in Supply Chain Management
Artificial Intelligence (AI) in supply chain management refers to the application of machine
learning, natural language processing, and intelligent automation technologies to support
data-driven decision -making, forecasting, and process execution across procurement,
logistics, and supplier -facing functions (Ivanov, 2023; Daios et al., 2025). Rather than a
single technology, AI in SCM is best understood as a portfolio  of capabilities, spanning
predictive modelling, robotic process automation, and governance and risk -monitoring
tools, that collectively reconfigure how buying organisations plan, communicate with, and
evaluate their suppliers (Riad et al., 2024). This con ceptualisation underpins the present
study's decision to operationalise AI through three distinct but complementary proxies
rather than as an undifferentiated construct.

Predictive Analytics
Predictive Analytics (PA) is the use of historical and real -time data, statistical algorithms,
and machine learning techniques to forecast future events, supplier behaviour, and
potential supply chain disruptions (Schoenherr & Speier -Pero, 2022). In a buyer –supplier
context, PA is typically applied to demand forec asting, supplier performance scoring, and
early-warning detection of delivery or quality risks, enabling buying organisations to shift
from reactive to proactive supplier management (Nzeako et al., 2024; Nweje et al., 2025).

Automation of Processes
Automation of Processes (AP) refers to the deployment of robotic process automation
(RPA), intelligent workflow engines, and autonomous transaction systems to execute
routine procurement and logistics tasks with minimal human intervention (Zdravković et
al., 2021). Within SCM, process automation is associated with reduced cycle times, fewer
manual errors, and improved consistency in supplier -facing transactions such as purchase
order processing, invoice matching, and shipment tracking (Lodhi et al., 2024).

AI Risk Management
AI Risk Management (AIR) encompasses the governance frameworks, algorithmic
transparency mechanisms, data-privacy safeguards, and continuous monitoring processes
that organisations put in place to ensure AI -driven decisions remain fair, explainable, and
accountable (Baryannis, Validi, Dani, & Antoniou, 2023; Cobbe, Veale, & Singh, 2023). In
supplier-facing contexts specifically, AI risk governance addresses concerns such as
algorithmic bias in supplier scoring, the trustworth iness of automated decisions, and the
protection of shared operational data (Ahangar et al., 2025).

Buyer–Supplier Relationships
Buyer–supplier relationships (BSRs) are the relational and operational interactions between
purchasing organisations and their supply partners that determine trust, coordination, and
long-term supply chain performance (Lambert, 2019). Contemporary supply chain
scholarship increasingly frames BSR quality along relational dimensions, including trust,
commitment, satisfaction, and collaborative behaviour, rather than purely transactional or
contractual terms, since collaborative activities such as information sharing and joint effort
have been shown to generate the trust and commitment that ultimately drive relationship
performance (Nyaga, Whipple, & Lynch, 2010). This relational conceptualisation of BSR
quality is the outcome construct that the present study measures across its three AI proxies.

Theoretical Framework
Commitment-Trust Theory of Relationship Marketing
This study is anchored on the Commitment -Trust Theory (CTT) of relationship marketing,
originally advanced by Morgan and Hunt (1994) in their seminal Journal of Marketing
article. CTT was selected over adoption -oriented frameworks because the outcome
variables thi s study measures (trust and reliability, efficiency and transparency, and
confidence and long -term stability) are, definitionally, the relational constructs at the
centre of CTT itself. This makes it a substantially closer theoretical fit to the study's research
questions than a technology -adoption lens such as the Technology –Organisation–
Environment framework, which explains why firms adopt a technology but is silent on the
relational mechanisms through which that technology then shapes ongoing exchange
behaviour.
Morgan and Hunt (1994) theorised that successful relationship marketing, the establishing,
developing, and maintaining of successful relational exchanges, depends fundamentally on
two mediating variables: relationship commitment and trust. Their Key Mediating Variable
(KMV) model proposes that five antecedent conditions (relationship termination costs,
relationship benefits, shared values, communication, and opportunistic behaviour) act
upon commitment and trust, which in turn mediate five conseque nt outcomes:
acquiescence, propensity to leave the relationship, cooperation, functional conflict, and
decision-making uncertainty. The theoretical contribution of the KMV model was to
demonstrate, using data from automobile tyre retailers and their suppli ers, that trust and
commitment, rather than satisfaction or dependence alone, are the pivotal psychological
states that determine whether an exchange relationship endures. Firms that are trusted and
to whom their partners are committed experience greater a cquiescence to reasonable
requests, lower propensity for partners to exit the relationship, higher levels of cooperation,
more constructive conflict resolution, and reduced uncertainty in decision -making. These
outcomes map closely onto the efficiency, tra nsparency, confidence, and stability
dimensions this study investigates.
Since its original formulation, CTT has been extensively validated and extended within
business-to-business (B2B) and supply chain contexts specifically. Crotts et al. (2001)

applied a trust-commitment model directly to buyer–supplier relationships, showing that a
cooperative, trust -based orientation between wholesale suppliers and their buyers
displaced the older adversarial transaction model. Wu et al. (2012) tested the fit of
commitment-trust theory in high -technology supply chain partnerships in Taiwan,
confirming that higher levels of trust facilitate better interaction and reduce uncertainty
between exchange partners, while higher commitment increases value benefits and
cooperation efficiency. Nyaga, Whipple, and Lynch (2010) extended the model further by
demonstrating, across independent buyer and supplier samples, that collaborative
activities, including information sharing, joint relationship effort, and dedicated
investments, a re themselves antecedents that build the trust and commitment which
subsequently drive satisfaction and performance, with the specific pathways differing
modestly between buyer and supplier perspectives. More recently, Duan et al. (2024)
revisited trust -commitment theory in the context of blockchain -enabled sustainable
sourcing, finding that transparent, verifiable digital governance mechanisms, conceptually
analogous to the AI risk -management proxy in this study, significantly strengthen buyer
trust even as they interact with commitment in complex ways. This confirms that digitally
mediated transparency is now a recognised antecedent within the CTT nomological
network.
The theory has also attracted refinement rather than wholesale replacement. Brown et al.
(2019) re-examined the presumed one -directional causal path from trust to commitment
across three datasets, including Morgan and Hunt's original data, and found that while trust
does enhance commitment, commitment can, under certain conditions, also erode trust.
This is a reciprocal dynamic that this study's cross-sectional design cannot itself disentangle
but which is acknowledged as a boundary condition in the limitations discussed in Section
5. This body of refinement work does not undermine CTT's core p roposition; rather, it
strengthens its applicability by clarifying that trust and commitment operate as an
interdependent relational core rather than a simple linear chain.
Applied to the present study, the Commitment -Trust Theory provides the following
theoretical mapping. Predictive Analytics, Automation of Processes, and AI Risk
Management are conceptualised as organisational -level antecedent conditions,
functionally analogous to Morgan and Hunt's communication and shared -values
antecedents, through which TECNO Mobile Nigeria signal’s reliability, reduces information
asymmetry, and demonstrates fair and accountable conduct toward its suppliers. Predictive
Analytics, by enabling proactive rather than reactive supplier management, reduces the
uncertainty that CTT identifies as a key relational cost, thereby building trust. Automation
of Processes, by standardising and de -personalising routine transactions, reduces the
perceived opportunism and inconsistency that erode trust, while simultaneously
generating th e transactional efficiency and transparency that CTT associates with
cooperative, low -conflict exchange. AI Risk Management, by embedding governance,
explainability, and accountability into AI -driven decisions, most directly addresses the

Empirical Review
Predictive Analytics and Buyer–Supplier Trust
A growing body of empirical work links predictive analytics to improved supplier
relationship outcomes. Nzeako et al. (2024) reviewed the role of AI -driven predictive
analytics in optimising IT -industry supply chains, reporting that predictive tools
meaningfully enhance dem and forecasting, supplier selection, and risk management,
though they note persistent challenges around data quality and skilled -personnel
availability that can moderate these benefits. Similarly, Nweje et al. (2025) found that AI -
driven predictive tools f or demand forecasting and inventory optimisation produced
substantial reductions in holding costs and improved lead times across the case studies
reviewed, which supports the proactive-management mechanism through which predictive
analytics is theorised to build supplier trust. Within the broader African context, Daraojimba
et al. (2024) compared AI -driven supply chain optimisation trends between the United
States and African firms. They found that while African adoption of predictive analytics
remains more gradual due to infrastructural constraints, the direction of effect, improved
visibility and reduced waste, mirrors that observed in more digitally mature markets, which
supports the relevance of predictive analytics research to the Nigerian context examined in
this study.

Automation of Processes and Relational Efficiency
Empirical evidence on process automation similarly points toward improved buyer–supplier
coordination. Zdravković et al. (2021) reviewed AI-enabled enterprise information systems
in manufacturing and identified automation -driven decision -making and logic -based
systems as central to improved supply chain and customer relationship management
functions. Adenekan et al. (2024) similarly reviewed AI -driven supply chain optimisation
integrated with ERP systems, concluding that automation of demand forecasting,
inventory management, and cross -functional data exchange improves forecasting
accuracy, reduces lead times, and enables the real-time visibility that underlies coordinated
buyer–supplier interaction. Consistent with this pattern, survey evidence from large
Nigerian manufacturing firms similarly links automation adoption to measurable gains in
operational efficiency and competitiveness, while identifying implementation cost and
workforce readiness as the principal constraints on wider adoption.

AI Risk Management and Relational Stability
Governance and risk-management dimensions of AI adoption have also received growing
empirical attention. Ahangar et al. (2025) surveyed AI trustwo rthiness in manufacturing,
identifying transparency, fairness, robustness, and accountability as the core principles that
toolkits and governance frameworks must operationalise to build stakeholder trust in AI -
driven decisions. These principles are directl y analogous to the AI Risk Management
construct measured in this study. Vann Yaroson et al. (2025) examined human -AI
collaboration in supply chain outcomes and found that responsible AI practices mediate the
relationship between collaborative AI use and su pply chain wellbeing, confirming that
governance quality, rather than AI use in isolation, is what translates into positive relational
and performance outcomes. In the sustainable -sourcing domain, Duan et al. (2024)
demonstrated that blockchain -based gover nance mechanisms, a close technological
cousin of AI risk -management systems, significantly enhance buyer trust in supplier
relationships. This lends further empirical support to the theoretical link this study draws
between AI governance and relational stability.

AI, Trust, and Buyer–Supplier Relationships in African and Nigerian Manufacturing
Within the African manufacturing literature more broadly, Al -Shboul (2024) found that AI
system adoption significantly and directly improves production managem ent and supply
chain performance among pharmaceutical manufacturing firms across the MENA region,
with strategic supplier reliability emerging as a key mediating pathway. This finding is
structurally consistent with this study's proposition that AI proxies  operate through
relational, trust -based mechanisms rather than through technology alone. Manu et al.
(2024) examined the impact of AI on resource efficiency in West African manufacturing and
found that AI -driven technologies significantly improve resource  efficiency, moderated
positively by regulatory environment and employee training and adaptation. This suggests
that the institutional and governance context, echoing the AI Risk Management construct,
shapes how effectively AI capabilities translate into o perational and relational benefits in
African settings. Most recently, a systematic review of AI's role in supplier relationship
management decision-making synthesised evidence across manufacturing, construction,
banking, and enterprise procurement context s, reporting substantial improvements in
supply chain performance, resilience, and risk -detection accuracy attributable to AI, while
also identifying legacy-system incompatibility, data-quality deficits, and workforce digital-
literacy gaps as the principal  barriers suppressing fuller adoption of AI's highest -impact
supplier relationship management roles (Osewe et al., 2026). These barriers are highly
consistent with the infrastructural constraints (Ifere et al., 2022) that frame this study's
Nigerian mobile manufacturing context.

Summary of Literature Review and Gap in Knowledge
The reviewed literature confirms that AI applications, spanning predictive analytics, process
automation, and risk governance, are consistently associated with improved supply chain
performance, efficiency, and, increasingly, relational trust across both advanced and
emerging economies. However, three gaps remain. First, the large majority of empirical
studies either examine AI as a single undifferentiated construct or fo cus on isolated
dimensions (typically predictive analytics or automation alone), rather than integrating
multiple AI proxies within a single relational model. Second, studies that do adopt a
relational or theory -driven lens have rarely applied the Commitme nt-Trust Theory
specifically to AI -enabled buyer –supplier relationships, more commonly relying on
adoption-oriented frameworks such as TOE or the resource -based view, which explain
adoption decisions but not relational consequences. Third, empirical eviden ce from
Nigeria's mobile manufacturing sector, a fast -growing but infrastructurally constrained
segment of Africa's largest economy, remains especially sparse, with most African AI -
supply chain studies concentrated in food and beverage, pharmaceutical, or general
manufacturing contexts. This study addresses all three gaps by empirically testing, through
the Commitment -Trust Theory, the perceived influence of three distinct AI proxies
(Predictive Analytics, Automation of Processes, and AI Risk Management) on  buyer–
supplier relationship quality at TECNO Mobile Nigeria

Methodology
Research Design and Setting
A quantitative survey research design was adopted. The study was conducted at TECNO
Mobile Nigeria, a leading mobile handset manufacturer operating within Nigeria's growing
consumer electronics supply chain. The study population comprised all employees and
supply chain partners directly involved in procurement, logistics, production planning,
supplier management, and IT systems integration at TECNO M obile Nigeria, totalling 320
individuals (TECNO Mobile HR Records, August 2024).

Sampling
Sample size was determined using the Yamane (1967) formula at an 8%5% margin of error,
yielding a minimum required sample of n = 178. Purposive sampling was employed to select
participants with direct knowledge of and experience in AI-enabled supply chain processes,
ensuring that responses reflected informed, practitioner -level perceptions. A total of 250
valid, fully completed responses were obtained and used for analysis, exceeding the
minimum threshold. Ethical clearance for the study was obtained and Participation was
voluntary, and informed consent was obtained from all respondents prior to questionnaire
administration, with assurances of anonymity and confidentiality.

Instrument
Data were collected using a structured questionnaire comprising two sections. Section A
captured five demographic variables (age group, gender, position, years of experience,
educational qualification). Sections B–D measured respondents' agreement with 15 Likert-
scale statements (five per construct) covering Predictive Analytics (PA), Automation of
Processes (AP), and AI Risk Management (AIR). All items were anchored on a five -point
scale: Strongly Agree (5) to Strongly Disagree (1). Item wording was adapted from validated
instruments in the AI and SCM literature (Wamba, Queiroz, & Trinchera, 2023; Schoenherr
& Speier-Pero, 2022; Baryannis, Validi, Dani, & Antoniou, 2023).

Analysis
Descriptive statistics (frequencies, per centages, means, standard deviations) were
computed for all items and aggregate construct scores. A mean score of ≥ 3.50 was
interpreted as 'Agreed' on the five -point scale, consistent with the Likert -score
interpretation convention. Each research hypothes is was evaluated using a one -sample t-
test, comparing the construct's aggregate mean against the scale's neutral midpoint (μ₀ =
3.00), at a 5% level of significance. This approach is appropriate for perception -based
instruments in which item wording embeds  the hypothesised relationship, as the
instrument in this study measures agreement with statements such as 'Predictive analytics
improves trust and reliability between TECNO Mobile and its suppliers,' so that rejecting μ
= 3.00 in favour of μ > 3.00 directly tests the hypothesis that respondents perceive the stated
effect to be present. Although each hypothesis is framed directionally (predicting a positive
relational effect), a two -tailed test was retained as the more conservative option: it does
not presu ppose the direction of the effect and therefore applies a stricter significance
threshold than a one -tailed alternative would, which strengthens confidence in the
substantive interpretation of the results reported in Section 4. All analyses were conducted
using Python (pandas, scipy.stats).

Discussion
The findings supp ort the proposition that AI applications, operationalised through
predictive analytics, process automation, and risk management, positively and significantly
shape buyer–supplier relationship quality in Nigeria's mobile manufacturing sector. Read
through the Commitment-Trust Theory (Morgan & Hunt, 1994), these results are consistent
with, and extend, prior work in both global and African supply chain contexts.
The significant positive perception of Predictive Analytics (Mean = 3.88) is consistent with
the b roader empirical literature on AI -driven forecasting and supplier trust, including
Nzeako et al.'s (2024) finding that predictive analytics meaningfully improves demand
forecasting and supplier selection, and Nweje et al.'s (2025) evidence that predictive tools
reduce holding costs and improve lead times. Within the Commitment -Trust framework,
predictive analytics enhances the quality of the exchange by reducing the decision-making
uncertainty that Morgan and Hunt (1994) identify as a direct consequence of low trust.
Buyers who can anticipate supplier difficulties signal reliability and commitment, which CTT
predicts should trigger reciprocal cooperative engagement from suppliers.
The significant finding for Automation of Processes (Mean = 3.91) supports the  wider
evidence base linking AI -driven automation to operational consistency and supplier
interaction quality (Zdravković et al., 2021; Adenekan et al., 2024). Process automation
reduces the friction and variability inherent in manual procurement interactions. Within the
Commitment-Trust framework, this standardisation lowers the perceived opportunism
that erodes trust, while the resulting transparency and consistency in transaction handling
are precisely the conditions under which CTT predicts functional, rather than destructive,
conflict resolution and greater cooperation between exchange partners.
The highest aggregate mean, recorded for AI Risk Management (Mean = 3.92), indicates
that respondents perceive governance, transparency, and ethical AI deployme nt as the
most valued dimension of AI for sustaining buyer –supplier relationships. This finding
resonates with Ahangar et al.'s (2025) emphasis on transparency, fairness, and
accountability as prerequisites for AI trustworthiness in manufacturing, and with  Duan et
al.'s (2024) finding that verifiable digital governance mechanisms significantly strengthen
buyer trust in sustainable sourcing relationships. Within CTT, AI Risk Management maps
most directly onto the antecedent conditions Morgan and Hunt (1994) identified as central
to commitment, namely the sense that a partner is trustworthy enough to warrant
continued relational investment, which is consistent with this construct recording the
highest mean of the three. This matters for an emerging -economy con text where
regulatory and governance frameworks are still maturing: the responsible governance of AI
systems is not peripheral to supply chain performance but central to it.
The closeness of the three-aggregate means (3.88 to 3.92) also deserves attention.  It
suggests that at TECNO Mobile Nigeria, the three AI dimensions are perceived as
comparably important, with no single mechanism overshadowing the others. This is
consistent with the Commitment-Trust Theory's emphasis that sustained relational quality

The study's findings also carry implications for the broader AI -SCM literature in emerging
markets. Most prior work has examined individual AI dimensions in isolation or in
technologically advanced economies (Al -Shboul, 2024; Osewe et al., 2026). This study
shows that even under Ni geria's challenging supply chain conditions, including currency
instability, infrastructural constraints, and limited AI expertise among local suppliers (Ifere
et al., 2022), employees and supply chain partners at TECNO Mobile perceive AI as
meaningfully improving buyer–supplier relationship outcomes across all three dimensions
examined. This runs against a pessimistic assumption in some of the development literature
that AI's relational benefits depend on advanced digital infrastructure. It also echoes Manu
et al.'s (2024) finding that AI -driven technologies can significantly improve resource
efficiency in West African manufacturing even amid infrastructural constraints, provided
governance and workforce-adaptation conditions are met.

Limitations of the Study
Several limitations should be noted. First, the study employed a cross -sectional design,
which precludes causal inference. Longitudinal designs would be needed to track how AI
adoption shifts BSR quality over time, and would also allow the reciprocal trust -
commitment dynamic identified by Brown et al. (2019) to be properly tested rather than
assumed. Second, the research instrument measured perceptions of AI's effect rather than
objectively verified AI adoption levels or BSR performance metr ics. This is appropriate for
exploratory empirical work in a nascent research context but limits generalisability to
objective outcomes. Third, the study is limited to a single firm, TECNO Mobile Nigeria,
which constrains external validity; multi -firm or s ector-wide replication is warranted.
Finally, the item -level reliability of the instrument, as measured by internal consistency,
could not be confirmed through Cronbach's alpha due to the cross-sectional nature of inter-
item correlations in this dataset, a n issue that future instrument development should
address.Finally, although the 15-item scale followed established measurement conventions
from the AI -supply chain literature and produced internally consistent response patterns
across items (Tables 2-4), the raw item-level response matrix was not retained for post hoc
computation of Cronbach's alpha, so reliability coefficients are not reported for the three
constructs; this limits the extent to which internal consistency can be formally verified, and
future studies using this instrument should compute and report construct -level alpha
coefficients from the outset.

This study set out to investigate the influence of AI applications, namely Predictive
Analytics, Automa tion of Processes, and AI Risk Management, on buyer –supplier
relationship quality at TECNO Mobile Nigeria, using the Commitment -Trust Theory of

Relationship Marketing as its theoretical lens. Following a review of the conceptual,
theoretical, and empirical literature that identified a gap in theory -driven, Nigeria-specific
evidence on this question, a quantitative survey of 250 employees and supply chain partners
was conducted and analysed using descriptive statistics and one-sample t-tests. The results
showed that all three AI proxies recorded aggregate mean scores well above the neutral
midpoint, ranging from 3.88 to 3.92 on a five-point scale, and all three null hypotheses were
rejected at the 0.1% significance level, with t -values exceeding 40 against a critical
threshold of 1.96.
This study provides empirical evidence that AI applications, specifically Predictive
Analytics, Automation of Processes, and AI Risk Management, are perceived as significantly
and positively influencing buyer –supplier relationsh ip quality at TECNO Mobile Nigeria.
Grounded in the Commitment-Trust Theory, the findings indicate that these AI capabilities
function as relational antecedents that build the trust and commitment on which durable,
cooperative buyer–supplier exchanges depend, rather than operating merely as efficiency-
enhancing technologies in isolation. The study extends the Commitment-Trust Theory into
an AI -enabled, emerging -economy manufacturing context and contributes empirical
evidence from Nigeria's mobile manufactur ing industry, a sector that remains under -
represented in the AI-supply chain literature.
Based on these findings, a holistic AI adoption strategy is recommended for practitioners
and managers at TECNO Mobile Nigeria and comparable mobile manufactu ring firms, in
which predictive tools, process automation, and responsible AI governance are pursued in
parallel rather than sequentially, given the comparably high value respondents placed on
all three dimensions. For Nigerian policymakers, particularly NITDA, the results support the
continued prioritisation of the National AI Strategy (NITDA, 2023) and the development of
sector-specific AI governance guidelines that enable manufacturing firms to build
transparent, accountable AI systems that suppliers can trust. For supply chain practitioners,
deliberate investment in supplier -facing communication around AI -driven decisions,
explaining how predictive, automated, and risk -governance systems work, is
recommended, since the Commitment-Trust Theory suggests that perceived fairness and
transparency, not merely technical capability, are what convert AI adoption into relational
trust. For academics, the study establishes a replicable empirical framework, linking the
Commitment-Trust Theory to three specific AI pro xies and BSR outcomes, that can be
extended to other sectors, firms, and African country contexts. Future research should
employ longitudinal designs, objective BSR performance indicators, and multi -firm
comparative samples to address the limitations ident ified above, and should explore the
boundary conditions under which each AI dimension exerts its greatest relational influence,
including the reciprocal trust-commitment dynamics identified by Brown et al. (2019).
