Introduction: Although artificial intelligence (AI)-powered chatbots are increas-
ingly used in digital marketing, their adoption remains challenging because
of limited understanding of the factors that shape consumer acceptance and
behavioral intention. This study examines the roles of trust, chatbot interactivity,
and anthropomorphic design in influencing consumers’ behavioral intention to
use AI chatbot services, with a particular focus on consumers in Tbilisi, Georgia.
Methods: A quantitative research design was employed using a purposive sam-
pling approach to select consumers with previous experience using AI chatbot ser-
vices. Data were collected through an online questionnaire from consumers who
had previously used AI chatbot services provided by major organizations in Tbilisi,
Georgia, between August and September 2025. A total of 275 valid responses were
retained for analysis. The collected data were analyzed using Partial Least Squares
Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM) to assess the measurement model and test
the hypothesized relationships among the study variables.
Results: The findings show that chatbot interactivity and anthropomorphic design
have significant positive effects on consumers’ behavioral intention. Trust signifi-
cantly strengthens both relationships, indicating that higher levels of trust enhance
the effects of interactivity and anthropomorphic design on behavioral intention.
Discussion: The findings highlight the importance of interactive and human-like
chatbot features and the moderating role of trust in encouraging consumers’
behavioral intention to use chatbot services in digital marketing.

1 Introduction
In our modern world, the importance of technological development has been clearly
proved and marketing methodology has undergone serious streamlining. The discipline has
broken its accustomed boundaries and is now communicating with digitally mediated plat-
forms that enable direct interaction with discrete consumer cohorts. The emergence of the
digital world has radically transformed communicative channels that businesses use to access
their consumers and, as a result, made the digital marketing constructs a necessity in the pres-
ent corporate environment. A digital marketing framework contains a range of strategies, such
as social media marketing, email marketing, influencer marketing, content marketing, and
search engine optimisation, whose interdependence and theoretical basis are thoroughly

studied in recent academic literature ( Akhlaq et al., 2025 ). Digital

Owing to the accelerated development of the digital platforms, the
development of the aspects of technology and its ability to shape and
influence the consumer’s behaviour, companies went over to the digi-
tal in the marketing aspects, and various digital channels serve to
address the consumers. Digital marketing is a major driver of global

The earlier works studied the effect of the Artificial Intelligence (AI)
chatbots on consumer behaviour above all in the digital trade. Sheen
(2020) opined that chatbots designed to mimic human conversation sup-
port customer service and personalise digital marketing experiences.
Araujo (2024) stated that interactive systems strengthen trust and per-
ceived usefulness, pushing the user to accept the AI technologies. In the
same way, Nuss and Moon concluded that consumer trust in AI is pro-
moted when chatbots demonstrate humanness through natural language
understanding, empathy and contextual adaptation. Artasanchez and
Joshi (2025) emphasised that an effective chatbot must be flexible, per-
sonal, available, and relatable to appropriately respond to the user and
handle unforeseen interaction in a polite manner. Further, the literature
also looked at the increasing role of conversational chatbots in the devel-
opment of customer experiences and service delivery. Pillai and
Sivathanu (2021) conceptualise chatbots as applications that simulate
human conversation through the application of natural language pro-
cessing, whereas Sheehan et al. (2020) view them as automated counsel-
lors in decision-making. According to Melian-Gonzalez et al. (2021) and
Mogaji et al. (2021), the use of chatbots in customer service will never be
less than human interaction in the near future. Whereas Jang et al. (2021)
and Agag (2025) draw focus to chatbots as self-service technologies that
are facilitated by intelligent systems to increase efficiency in the sales,
marketing, and support processes. Of more recent opinion, Agag et al.
(2025) and other scholars thought that AI-driven chatbots are now vital
in the tourism and hospitality sector, enhancing interaction with custom-
ers, saving money, and providing around-the-clock services.
However, there are still several lacunae in the field of consumer
behaviour with respect to the impact of trust in AI robots on influenc-
ing consumers’ intention to use them. Most of the review focused on
the aspect of the influence of anthropomorphic features on consumer
behaviour and brand perception across various products. While the
benefits of anthropomorphism are evident, further research is needed
to understand how anthropomorphic design of AI chatbots influences
consumers’ intentions and the joint impact of trust, interactivity, and
anthropomorphic design on consumers’ behavioural intention to use
AI chatbots in digital marketing. Recognising these gaps, there is a
need for a more extensive study that incorporates these variables into
a model that gives a better understanding of how the consumer devel-
ops trust and makes a decision to adopt the AI chatbot in the context
of online shopping (Akhlaq and Ahmed, 2024).
In today’s digital world, AI chatbot technology has taken over and
has made marketing easy. AI chatbots have become essential tools for
assuring customers’ convenience and activities. However, customers
face some difficulty in acceptance due to certain factors such as trust,
interactivity and design perception. The degree to which they are satis-
fied with the acceptance of recent technology based on its implemented
levels requires great attention. Although chatbots are invented to
increase marketing efficiency, more than 70 % of the customers do not
feel fully assured of their reliability and privacy, which makes their
acceptance by the customers easy. Also, it should be further established
how far the interactivity and human-like properties bestowed on AI
chatbots are considerably affecting consumers’ attitudes towards them,
but how far their effect on behavioural intention remains under study.
2 Theoretical background
2.1 Background literature
Digital marketing profoundly impacts consumer behaviour, which
is influenced primarily by past experience with brands. The introduc-
tion of AI technology allows brands to interact with consumers in a
different manner by means of data, automation and personalisation.
One such feature of this technology is chatbots, which help engage in
conversations between consumers and companies in a natural lan-
guage. Research such as that of Gentsch (2023) concludes that firms
invest in chat technology for enhancing conversations and also to be
able to immediately provide required information. The findings by Xu
et al. (2022) have illustrated that trust is essential for both low- and
high-involvement purchasers interacting with AI-generated chatbots.
The consumer perception of the structure of the communication, that
chatbots are reliable or transparent, enables communication (Fox et al.,
2020). Jain et al. (2023) further illustrate that when trust exists between
the consumer and brand chatbots, there is a positive interaction which
results in loyalty intentions and future interaction with the brand. For
successful relationships with consumers, it is very important that firms
identify which factors predispose consumers to trust firm AI chatbots.
2.1.1 Trust in AI chatbot
Digital marketing has a significant impact on consumer behav-
iour, as consumers’ previous experiences with the brand are very
influential. Machine learning technologies are extremely important
as they bring data, automation, and customisation together to capture
consumer attention. The use of chatbots is an extremely important
aspect of technologies that enable brand-consumer communication
in a more effective way, since they use natural language. In a study
conducted by Gentsch (2023), it is shown that many brands make use
of the AI chatbots in order to better the conversation rates and grant
greater access to instant information. Xu et al. (2022) demonstrate
that prominent characteristics influence the degree of trust the con-
sumers have in connection with the dispensing of products and prod-
uct information by means of AI chatbots. Ashfaq et al. (2020) also
found that the use of the AI is greatly governed by opinions held by
the consumers in connection with the chatbot characteristics, such
as trustworthiness, sympathy and frankness, that are so dear to the
consumer involved with communication with AI chatbots. Jain et al.
(2023) showed that when consumers show trust in the chatbot of a
brand, they have a satisfactory experience, resulting in loyalty to that
particular service and a desire to communicate in the future with that
chatbot. Thus, it is necessary for companies to recognise elements
that have an important role in the building of consumer trust in AI
chatbots in order to upgrade that area in their strategies of marketing

2.1.2 Anthropomorphic design
Artificial intelligence features that simulate human qualities are
regarded as anthropomorphic design. This promotes good user expe-
rience in the case of artificial intelligence robots. This design helps
foster warmer, more empathetic relationships between users and
brands (Go and Sundar, 2019). The use of anthropomorphic robots is
helpful in digital marketing to improve user experience and trust. The
robots increase comfort and productivity by allowing interaction to
be more in the form of human-style conversation with the use of social
cues, such as humor and friendliness, which cause them to be per-
ceived as more efficient and trustworthy (Ciechanowski et al., 2020).
However, the other aspect of “The Uncanny Valley” (Luger and Sellen,
2016), indicates that if an engaging and conversational robot is too
human-like, there would be discomfort experienced by the user or
feelings of mistrust. The successful use of anthropomorphic robots
would allow the brands to interact with the consumers through gen-
erating messages that are personalized and attach to the emotions of
the consumer. This would result in higher levels of engagement and
improved conversion rates (Zarouali et al., 2022). With the develop-
ment of fast-paced digital landscape, the understanding of use and
responses to communications with AI is vital. To this end, it is impor-
tant to consider how consumers interpret, trust, and respond to AI-led
communication in the rapidly changing digital market through
anthropomorphic design. Table 2 presents a summary of previous

2.1.4 Chatbot interactivity
Interactive chatbots refer to the continuous, back-and-forth flow
of interaction of data between technology and human beings through
the simulation of human interaction. Thus, it has the potential to create
an atmosphere in which the consumer is more involved in the interface
experience and is more satisfied because of contextualised communica-
tion, emotional support (Kvale et al., 2022) and/or feedback due to
immediate responses to consumer input within the contextual bound-
aries of the interaction. The greater the human personality character-
istics and responses to the consumer shown by the chatbot, the greater
the perceptions of utility, credibility, and enjoyment of the consumer
(Sheehan et al., 2020). The consumer can interact with interactive chat-
bots in order to receive personalised information from the brand and
develop brand loyalty and brand engagement (Mariani and Borghi,
2022). Furthermore, interactive chatbots will result in a psychological
sense of “social presence” by closing the gap created by human-to-
human communication versus human-to-machine communication
(Gnewuch and Reinkemeier, 2026), which results in a greater accep-
tance and increased use of these types of systems. Thus, the nature of
the interactivity of chatbots should be investigated as revealing insight
into consumer satisfaction and trust generation and consumer behav-
ioural intentions in AI-mediated environments. Table 4 presents a
summary of previous studies.
2.2 Hypotheses development
2.2.1 Anthropomorphic design and the intention
to utilize AI
A design that is anthropomorphic is about how closely an artificial
intelligence interface appears to be like a human; e.g., chatbots, or virtual
assistants. A user will perceive an AI system that accomplishes tasks simi-
larly to humans as being more relatable, trustworthy, and social than one
which does not; this is based on Kim and Song (2023). The user’s percep-
tion of an AI system that performs tasks similarly to a human lead to
users forming a greater emotional attachment to the AI; creates a smaller
psychological distance between users and the AI; and fosters an intention
by users to continue using the AI technology ( Epley et al., 2007 ).
Anthropomorphic Cues used in Digital Marketing also create positive
User Attitudes; e.g., Customized Salutations; Empathy Expressed
Through Language; Expressive Avatars (Moriuchi, 2021), etc., are all cues
that create a sense of human warmth and trustworthiness in users.
Additionally, when users interact with chatbots that have human-like
social presence they experience higher levels of user satisfaction and
adoption which makes users feel comfortable and confident in AI (Adam
et al., 2021). Thus, Anthropomorphic Design has many implications
regarding the Behavioral Intention to Continue Using AI-Based Systems.
This supports the hypothesis: H1 research indicates that Anthropomorphic
Design is a favorable factor for Intention to Use AI.

2.2.3 Trust in AI chatbot moderates a relationship
between anthropomorphic design
The users’ perceptions of AI systems are directly linked to the
extent of the trust the users hold in these systems in producing the
desired result. But the users’ perceptions of the ability of AI systems to
produce the desired results is very much determined by the trust that
users place in the design elements of the systems (e.g., empathy,
friendliness, human-type interaction) of the decisions to be made
about making use of these systems. Thus, a recent study showed that
users that had a high degree of trust in the pertinence of the anthro-
pomorphic design of the systems to their acceptability of the systems
would consider that digital interactions were less likely to be risky and
hence less of a uncertainty than those who did not have a degree of
trust in this field (Hajlici, 2022). Though a user lacks trust in the extent
of how there is a use of anthropomorphic cues in AI this deficiency
may lead to the users having the belief that these cues are manipulative
or dishonest, they will tend to depart from using them as a result of
deterrence in their intentions to behave there in response to the use of
the technology. Similarly, AI systems would be able to possibly be
fashioned to produce a positive emotional and cognitive response in
the users marketed, tending to increase the users’ interest in expecta-
tion of the technology (Gefen et al., 2003). This supports the hypoth-
esis: The relationship between Anthropomorphic Design and the
intention to use AI is moderated by Trust in AI Chatbot.
2.2.4 Trust in AI Chatbot moderates a relationship
between anthropomorphic design and intention
to use AI
Trust is important in modulating chatbot interactivity and user
intent regarding AI. On one hand, interactivity has benefits in guiding
users fully into engagement through communication in real-time and
responsive manners but relies on the trust which users place in it
regarding reliability and accuracy of information (Xu et al., 2023).
Trust affects the enhanced interactivity in a positive direction, as it
means that engagement from users is furthered through acceptance
of suggestions by automated means and the ability to continue the
closeness of relational interactions with the AI in question (Mariani
and Borghi, 2022). However, the reverse can be said for low levels of
trust, which deter the perceived advantages of interactivity. The final
factor inherited in this is that the chatbot will have responsive aspects,
but the issues of reliability must be taken into account. In other words,
the relationship between interactivity and trust affects the overall
behavioural intent to continue to interact with AI chat services. This
supports the hypothesis: Trust in AI Chatbot moderates the relationship
between chatbot interactivity and intent-to-use using AI. Based on the

3.1 Research design
The study is founded on the descriptive survey design. The right
conception of the study is explained by the attention to the analysis of
the influence of AI-supplied chatbots, in particular digital marketing
strategies, on the current consumer practices in Tbilisi, Georgia. The
study will utilise a survey study as its research method to gather pri-
mary data of organisational chatbots with consumers who use them
in different sectors, which include banking, retail, e-commerce, tele-
communications and electronics. Through such a design, it is possible
to have quantitative data on user experiences, satisfaction levels, and
behavioural directives of AI-managed digital marketing campaigns.
3.2 Data collection procedure
The collection of data was carried out through a well-structured
online questionnaire administered by Google Forms, while maintain-
ing efficiency, accessibility, and affordability. Two parts made up the
instrument: Section A (Demographic Information): Gathers respon-
dents’ backstory, such as their age, gender and educational attainment
or occupation [Letter]. In Section B (Study Variables), the consumer
is evaluated on their perception of AI-powered chatbots, including
four dimensions: trust, interactivity, anthropomorphic design, and

3.4.1 Trust in AI Chatbots
This construction was measured according to the work of Glikson
and Woolley (2020). It measures users’ knowledge, trustworthiness
and confidence in the integrity and dependability of AI chatbot sys-
tems. This measures the degree to which chatbots are deemed reliable,

security when engaging with AI systems were assessed using six items
to measure their trust in AI chatbots. In fact, as an example, the argu-

at the scholarly level.
3.4.2 Chatbot interactivity
This construct was measured using modified items in Gomes et al.
(2025). The chatbots’ interactivity summarizes their ability to interact
with users by responding and communicating with them in a dynamic
and personalized manner. This feature demonstrates the attentional
acuity of the chatbot and its ability to provide relevant feedback and
personalized experiences during service provision, simulating human-
customer interactions. Chatbot interactivity was measured using three
dimensions, namely responsiveness and user engagement. The findings
were later put up. One of the chatbot service agent attributes allows the
customers to be attended to on a one-on-one basis.
3.4.3 Anthropomorphic design
The construct was operationalized and measured according to the
methodology that was outlined by Polyportis and Pahos (2024). It mea-
sures how human-like chatbots are—in terms of natural language style,
situational awareness, expressiveness, and visual/verbal/conversational
finesse. The anthropomorphic design suggests that the users can connect
with the chatbot as understanding, friendly, and human in communica-
tive style. Five items were used to assess the ability of the chatbot to imi-
tate human behaviour in the course of interaction. One such product
outcome is that the chatbot looks more humane than human.
3.4.4 Intention to use AI
The evaluation of this construct was done through the data pre-
sented by Chin et al. (2022). The research questions potential user
behaviour and their intentions to use AI chatbot services in different
situations. Results outline the satisfaction, perceived usefulness, and
intention to experiment with AI technologies levels. The tendency to
utilise AI was tested with three levels of continuum and with a special
focus on the probability of future use, as well as long-term involve-
ment. As an example, the constructs were measured using a 5-point
Likert scale that was created to measure the intentions of using AI
chatbot services in the future. To confirm the validity and reliability
of the survey instrument, a strict psychometric analysis, including the
measures of internal consistency (Cronbach’s alpha >0.70), composite
reliability (CR > 0.70), and an average variance extracted greater than
0.50, will be conducted to prove the convergent validity. Discriminant
validity will be evaluated with the use of Fornell-Larcker criteria.

Inferential statistics, while statistical analysis using Statistical Package

4.2 Discussion of findings
The empirical evidence shows that the trust in AI chatbots has a
considerable positive impact on the behavioural intentions of consumers
to embrace AI chatbot services in digital marketing. Such considerations
imply that AI chatbots can be made more credible and reliable when
appropriately designed to advance the best interests of users, which will
make them more acceptable. These findings can be related to similar
studies carried out in the past that have highlighted the critical role of
trust as a fundamental factor that dictates the section of consumers to
adopt technology. The original contribution of Gefen et al., 2003 and
recent articles (2021) complain that trust reduces the perceived risk and
uncertainty in the online interaction and makes wider adoption possible.
Similarly, McKnight and associates also proved that trust is a predictor
of user participation in online platforms, which supports the idea that,
in digital marketing, trust is still a core part of consumer-AI relation-
ships. The interactivity of chatbots comes out as a prominent element of
influencing the behavioural choices that consumers make in the use of
AI chatbot services, as shown by hypothesis H2. Quick-replied chatbots
with two-way communications and resembling the natural human dia-
logue are hypothesised to increase user engagement and adoption inten-
tions. In the way modern chatbot prototypes are designed, this premise
is expressed. The results are consistent with the literature of Sundar,
Bellur, Oh, Jia, and Kim, as they hypothesised that attributes of the inter-
active system increase the level of involvement and control among users,
which contributes to positive behavioural results. Also, Ashfaq et al.
(2020) discovered that the more interactive the chatbot is, the more satis-
fied the user will be and will be willing to use AI-enhanced services
again. In general, the current research supports the idea that interactivity
is a vital factor in determining the effect of AI chatbots on consumer
behaviour within the context of digital marketing. In addition, these
results demonstrate that an anthropomorphic approach to designing AI
chatbots has a very strong, statistically significant positive impact upon
consumer behavioral intention to use AI chatbot services. As such, it can
be inferred that chatbots that are designed with human-like characteris-
tics (e.g., conversational tone, emotion, name or avatar) will be more
readily accepted than those without. Thus, this finding supports the
CASA (Computers Are Social Actors) paradigm proposed by Reeves and
Nass (2006) that individuals treat technological entities exhibiting
human-like attributes as they would other humans. In similar fashion,
Epley et al. and Kim and Sundar (2012) demonstrated through their
respective studies that the use of anthropomorphism can enhance the
perceived social presence, warmth and engagement users experience
from utilizing a service. The strong effect observed in this study high-
lights the importance of human-like design elements in shaping positive
consumer responses to AI chatbots. As the findings indicate from

research that found that trust increases users’ cognitive and affective
evaluation of technology (Gefen et al., 2003).
5 Theoretical implications
The research contributes to developing the theory regarding the
AI adoption process, digital marketing behaviours, and the behaviour
in interactions with both humans and machines. The study builds
upon the TAM model by determining that trust and anthropomorphic
design are important external variables which can positively affect
behavioral intentions. Current literature has expanded upon the origi-
nal TAM construct of perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use
to be applicable to AI-based systems; therefore, this study is also plac-
ing a high level of emphasis on trust as an underlying psychological
factor to drive acceptance.

6 Managerial and practical implications
The research presents critical practical insight into business, digi-
tal marketing and artificial intelligence (AI) development. First,
because trust in chatbots is a significant determinant of intended use
of chatbots; therefore, organisations need to focus on developing
transparent, secure and reliable AI systems by making clear commu-
nication of their privacy policy, improving the accuracy of the system,
limiting the number of errors made by the system, and making sure
the chatbot acts ethically and in a consistent manner.
Even the empirical evidence shows that the sheer existence of
chatbot interactivity does not have a statistically significant effect on
the behavioural intentions of users. Therefore, managers ought not to
waste resources on the extravagant interactive processes that do not
directly provide a benefit to the consumer experience. Rather, they
should focus on key features that maintain business effectiveness in
terms of clarity and reliability. A designed chatbot with an easy, quick,
and accurate format is likely to be more beneficial to the organisation
than the one that involves a lot of interaction.
The research under consideration suggests that anthropomorphic
design has a significant influence on intention, which means that the
acceptance of users can be increased as a result of a wise consideration
of the properties of a human. To produce chatbots that appeal to the
user, companies should incorporate functionalities like natural lan-
guage processing tools, friendliness, emotional intelligence, and cus-
tomised messaging. The inclusion of these aspects does not only help
in alleviating the fears of artificial intelligence but also makes online
communication more active.

7 Limitations and further research
directions
Although the study has some merits, it is not without severe limita-
tions. Primarily, the lack of statistically significant results with regard
to chatbot interactivity may be attributed to methodological limitations
such as the inadequacy of survey items or the incomplete understand-
ing of the construct of interactivity by respondents. For that reason,
more studies are needed to refine and increase the number of these
measurement instruments to improve their reliability.
Future studies should combine behavioural measures based on
actual chatbot interaction to produce more objective measures.
Furthermore, the sample of the study was limited to a certain group of
people, which may hinder the generalisation of the study results. The
replication of the investigation in diverse geographical locations, indus-
tries, and demographic strata would improve its generalisability.
Furthermore, the use of a cross-sectional questionnaire limits the
capacity to track changes in the beliefs or behaviours of respondents
over time; a longitudinal research design would produce a more accu-
rate picture of the evolution of attitudes towards AI chatbots over time.
The use of self-reported information can create problems of potential
bias, such as from social desirability and subjective interpretation. In
addition, prospective research should investigate possible moderating
factors—digital literacy, privacy fears, culture, personality factors, or
prior exposure to artificial intelligence. The constructs like trust, anthro-
pomorphism, and interactivity are expected to impact the intentions of
the users to use chatbots. Qualitative inquiry will enable the researcher
to discover even more psychological factors that drive the uptake of AI
and, hence, provide a more beautiful picture of consumer behaviour.
8 Conclusion
The current research questioned whether trust, interactivity, and
human-like design in AI chatbot services affect how likely consumers
are to use digital marketing programs. Results show that trust and
human-like design are key factors in influencing behaviour, while
interactivity does Results indicate that trust and anthropomorphic
design are important predictors of behavioural intention, and interac-
tivity does not make any statistical differences in terms of utilisation.
The joint effect analysis also underlines the importance of incorporat-
ing several attributes in order to maximise the effectiveness of chat-
bots. The investigation sheds light on the theoretical underpinning of
the adoption of AI through the synthesis of the insights of the
technology acceptance, human-computer interaction, and digital mar-
keting literature. The empirical knowledge as such provides practical
advice to those practitioners who may want to build chatbots that can
enhance trust, user experience, and expand acceptance.
Though with a few methodological weaknesses, the study serves
as a good guiding factor for future research and can help organisations
to leverage AI chatbots in digital marketing endeavours. Using AI sys-
tems that are reliable and easy for machines to understand would help
companies enhance the online experience for users by implementing
trustworthy, human-like, and well-designed systems.
