Abstract Digital marketing has evolved into a significant tool for competitiveness
and growth in the tourism industry, enabling engagement among consumers, desti-
nations, and travel brands. The study presents a narrative review of research span -
ning 10 years from 2014 to 2024, examining digital marketing tools and platforms,
strategies, and consumer behaviors in the tourism sector. Specifically, the review
examines how the integration of Artificial Intelligence (AI)-based user personaliza-
tion tools, social media marketing, influences marketing strategies, web analytics,
mobile marketing, location optimization services, targeted content curation, and the
adoption of digital tools by tourists in the funnel. A narrative review methodology
is employed to examine these areas comprehensively. The searched keywords
included smart tourism, digital marketing tools, personalized travel, responsive
design, mobile marketing, etc. The findings benefit tourism marketers and stake -
holders, demonstrating that social media platforms are crucial marketing channels
for optimizing campaigns, facilitating electronic word-of-mouth, providing person-
alized solutions using AI, responding to real-time marketing demands, enabling
instant bookings via mobile devices, and promoting destination reviews.
1  Introduction and Background of Research
Tourism businesses have endorsed marketing tools, including social media and
mobile apps, as the primary channels of communication for their target customers
[1, 2]. Social media and mobile platforms appear to have influenced every stage of
travel planning and the travel experience [2]. Travelers are shifting their interest to
online information to organize their future travel plans, prompting marketers to
invest in digital media and devices to influence consumer decision-making.
Academic research from 2014 to 2024 examined the emerging digital marketing

2  Narrative Review Methodology
This narrative literature review synthesizes insights from scientific publications
between 2014 and 2024 to provide an overview of how tourism marketing is evolv-
ing in the digital era, while identifying critical findings and literature gaps [ 9, 10].
The thematic sections include social media, artificial intelligence-based personal-
ization, influencer marketing, mobile marketing, content marketing, and online
engagement practices. Search terms such as “digital marketing,” “tourism market -
ing,” “content marketing,” “personalization,” “artificial intelligence,” “social media
marketing,” and “consumer behavior in tourism” were retrieved from journal arti -
cles, conference proceedings, books, and book chapters from Scopus and Web of
Science that offer insights into the areas of interest, as well as highly recognizable
tech blogs and magazines. The findings below synthesize the authors’ literature
searches, views, and expertise, facilitating the integration of conceptual and empiri-
cal research and highlighting the evolving nature of digital marketing tools [8, 10].
3  Social Media Marketing in Tourism
Social media has had a leading role in tourism marketing over the past decade,
reshaping how destinations and travel businesses interact with travelers. Social
media platforms are leveraged to promote destinations, communicate with consum-
ers, and facilitate electronic word-of-mouth, which affects tourists’ decision- making
[2, 5, 8]. Travelers get information from social media and content-sharing websites
to discover travel ideas, recommendations, and validation [2, 11]. Tourism market-
ers consider social media a mandatory strategy for engaging with their global audi-
ence [9, 12].

3.1  Social Media in Travelers’ Opinion Shaping
Tourism businesses utilize social media to interact with tourists in real-time, enhanc-
ing their public relations, effectively communicating their packages and offers, and
co-creating value with consumers [6, 12]. Destination Marketing Organizations
(DMOs) and tourism companies foster customer loyalty and generate interest in
their offerings by sharing engaging, user-targeted content [ 3, 12]. Social media
enables a cost-free global reach, where a single organic post or review can impact
thousands of potential customers [ 2, 4]. Social media marketing in tourism affects
destination visibility, changes a travel brand’s reputation, and drives bookings [12].
Electronic word-of-mouth (eWOM) refers to the influence of tourists who typically
share their opinions, reviews, photos, and videos on social media, thereby becoming
content creators for destinations. User-generated content (UGC) has a significant
impact on travelers, who tend to trust peer reviews and recommendations more than
official tourism campaigns, websites, or promotions [ 11]. Consumers trust the
reviews on platforms more than on destination websites. EWOM on social media
can significantly empower a destination’s reputation and attractiveness [12].
3.2  Influencer Marketing
Recent studies have shown that influencers’ positive comments can positively
impact the increase in tourist interest or intention to visit a place. Recent research
on Instagram travel influencers has revealed that they have a significant impact on
users’ destination choices, enabling destinations to shape perceptions and spark
curiosity for lesser-known locations [13]. Because influencers engage with follow-
ers and gain their trust, their recommendations are perceived as authentic and cred-
ible. In tourism, trust is crucial, and the ability to earn tourists’ trust impacts tourists’
decision-making [14].
3.3  Social Media Best Practices
High-quality photos, infographics, and videos are crucial in capturing users’ interest
in social media feeds [ 4, 12]. Storytelling techniques are also employed in social
media content to emotionally engage social media users. It is also notable that dif -
ferent platforms are used for various marketing purposes: Facebook is used by tour-
ism businesses for broad promotion, customer service, and information sharing.
Instagram and Pinterest are used to capture visitors’ interest through visual content.
YouTube is used for long-form video storytelling, while Twitter is used for real-time
updates [12]. Facebook is one of the most popular social media platforms for

4  User Personalization Using AI in Tourism Marketing
AI-based personalization utilizes intelligent algorithms, such as machine learning
models, data mining, and predictive analytics, to deliver real-time, personalized
marketing content, suggestions, recommendations, and services to customers. This
can empower the implementation of decision support systems for products, pricing,
promotional content based on user behavior, user demographics, virtual assistants’
data, and personalized advertisements or content for specific user clusters [13, 14].
4.1  User Personalization
AI enables travel brands to segment users and cater to their preferences, needs, and
expectations [15]. Marketing functions are pulled towards data-driven techniques
where “segmentation will be numerically based on personalization,” with AI using
big data to provide solutions to targeted audiences. AI systems can incorporate vari-
ous and diverse data, such as past purchases, location, and social media, to compre-
hend users’ needs in real-time, supporting tourism marketers in co-creating
personalized user experiences with travelers [ 15, 16]. This way, travelers receive
customized solutions and experiences, as well as suggestions, activity alerts, and
personalized customer services [8, 15].
4.2  AI-Based Chatbots
AI-based chatbots on travel websites, social media business pages, or messaging
applications can gravitate users towards natural language. By automatically gener -
ating answers to their questions, offering suggestions, or even making the actual
bookings, chatbots efficiently operate as 24/7 travel agents for routine demands.
Studies indicate that AI-based chatbots can enhance the customer experience by
offering interactive, personalized suggestions tailored to users’ requests and profiles
[15]. Chatbots can manage a high volume of customers’ requests, give customized
solutions for hotel services or nearby points of interest, and increase customer sat -
isfaction [5, 16].

4.3  Predictive Analytics
AI is also used for predictive analytics insights into tourism marketing. The concept
of “predict tourist behavior” has come up, where machine learning, deep learning,
and data mining are used to proactively predict traveler needs and behavior to
respond with personalized services [16, 17].
4.4  AI-Generated Content
AI-based generated content dynamically offers personalized website or app content
to each user. A 2024 tourism industry report indicates that marketers are operating
and designing beyond generic campaigns, providing customized marketing strate -
gies that aim to deliver user-oriented marketing content tailored to audience
needs [18].
4.5  Smart Tourism
The smart tourism paradigm demonstrates how tourism destinations can integrate
AI technology systems, such as sensors, IoT, and open data platforms, to deliver
targeted and personalized services to tourists. Smart tourism is transforming vast
volumes of data into added value for tourism stakeholders, mentioning that techno-
logical and business innovations are required [19]. While AI allows extreme person-
alization, integrating AI capabilities into destination management systems and
marketing best practices remains a demanding project [18, 19].
4.6  AI in Eco-Friendly Travelling
Ferhataj (2024) examined the adoption of AI-driven suggestions for eco-friendly
traveling, allowing further examination of features that support travelers in accept-
ing AI-personalized solutions. Travelers recognize and appreciate personalized con-
tent and services that align with their values, promoting sustainable solutions.
Tourists with strong environmental concerns were far more likely to support AI
suggestion systems that aid them in traveling sustainably and naturally. Investing in
consumer trust in AI-based marketing strategy and services is crucial. Hence, the
transparency and reliability of AI systems are integral to the reputation of tourism
brands [20, 21].

4.7  Pay-Per-Click (PPC)
Among other research areas, machine learning has been widely used in academic

through rate and conversion rate. It examines criteria that allow us to assess the
significance of varied factors on the probabilities of clicks and conversions. Machine

5  Mobile Marketing Practices in Tourism
Mobile marketing in tourism is currently designed to engage travelers before, dur -
ing, and after their travels, book flights and rooms, have an overview of the entire
trip, have access to instant user-generated content, read reviews and recommenda -
tions, use maps, navigate through the best routes etc.
5.1  Mobile Apps
Online users have been downloading massive volumes of data and travel apps annu-
ally (116 billion mobile apps in 2019 alone), and it is predicted that mobile use will
continue to grow. Most of the apps refer to travel planning, navigation, booking, and
review. As such, surveys have shown that many travelers feel comfortable search -
ing, reviewing, and booking their travel arrangements using their smartphones
exclusively. These trends highlight why tourism businesses have prioritized mobile
channels in their marketing strategies to meet their customers’ needs [21, 22].

5.2  User-Friendly Responsive Design
User friendliness plays an essential role when designing a website or mobile app.
Referring to an optimized marketing strategy, all digital content, including websites,
booking engines, emails, and newsletters, must meet specific technical require-
ments such as mobile-friendly design, responsive design, small page response time,
and easy navigation on small screens. Marketers have learned that poor user experi-
ence is essential to keep users engaged. From web design and development to web
applications, web services, mobile-friendly websites, and data representation, AI
optimizes web development procedures. Visiting time is valuable; content creation
is targeted and optimized for specific user profiles and experiences, utilizing memo-
rable logos, concise text, engaging visuals, and clear calls-to-action buttons [21, 23].
5.3  User-Friendly App Design
Mobile applications enhance user engagement and loyalty by serving as informa -
tion hubs, online booking platforms, user-generated social content sharing tools,
navigation maps, tour guides, and event booking services. According to the
Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), the eWOM can affect travelers’ intention to
use a tourism app [21]. Consumers can gather information about travel offers and
prices, book digital tickets, access location-based suggestions, and make instant
bookings or find restaurants, accommodations, or points of interest [ 24, 25]. From
the brand’s side, managers can disseminate their products and services through
mobile platforms. A mobile app or responsive website can show users high-quality
images, videos, and interactive media. It also offers in-app or push notifications to
send promotions or alert notifications when the user is close to a destination [21, 25].
5.4  Location-Based Marketing
Mobile apps offer location-based marketing through the GPS service. Tourism
brands target users by sending messages or advertisements based on their location.
Mobile tourism apps offer travelers location services, online transaction capabili -
ties, and social sharing features to enhance their travel experience. To increase
engagement rates, content personalization offers personalized marketing promo-
tions [25].

5.5  Online Transactions
Another important aspect of mobile marketing strategy is completing online trans -
actions. Travelers are expected to book and pay for travel services online using their
mobile devices. Many airlines and tour operators offer mobile boarding passes, QR
code tickets, and cashless payment options, which enhance the overall travel experi-
ence and user satisfaction, ultimately increasing conversion rates [8, 21].
6  Discussion and Conclusion
The integration of digital tools into a digital ecosystem offers travelers empower -
ment in their journey. Social media posts, reviews and recommendations, user-
personalized offers, mobile applications for scheduling, responsive design, and
storytelling enable mediums and destinations to interconnect. An omnichannel
strategy is crucial for creating “smart” destinations. As digital transactions influence
consumers’ behavior, marketers are required to use genuine information on social
media, transparent AI recommendations, and adhere to ethical regulatory frame -
works. Some of this narrative review’s major points include that social media has
reshaped the tourism marketing landscape by shifting influence toward consumers,
who can impact destination reputations through content sharing and reviews. This
user-centric landscape presents marketers with opportunities for increased user
engagement, demanding authenticity and responsiveness [3, 12]. AI and predictive
analytics can offer personalized travel experiences, providing customized solutions
and services that enhance experience and satisfaction [ 15, 20]. Meanwhile, mobile
devices play a crucial role in travel journeys, upgrading mobile marketing to a
necessity for consumer engagement [20, 22].
