Abstract
This study examines smart teaching technologies and skill deficiencies among marketing
educators, with emphasis on their implications for curriculum implementation in higher
institutions in Nigeria. The rapid evolution of digital technologies has transformed global
education, making smart teaching tools such as learning management systems, artificial
intelligence, virtual reality, and learning analytics essential for effective instructional delivery.
Despite these advancements, many marketing educators in Nigeria continue to experience
significant skill gaps in utilizing smart technologies in teaching . These deficiencies limit their
ability to effectively integrate smart technologies into teaching and reduce the relevance of
curriculum implementation in contemporary higher education. This study highlights the concept
of smart technologies, concept of skill deficiency, types of smart teaching technologies, role of
smart technologies in enhancing marketing education, skill deficiencies of marketing educators
in Nigeria, implication for curriculum implementation in higher institutions in Nigeria and
upskilling professional development and capacity building. It concludes that improving the
capacity of marketing educators through targeted professional development and effective
integration of smart teaching technologies will significantly enhance curriculum implementation,
teaching quality, and graduate employability in Nigerian higher institutions it was recommended
that The study recommends continuous ICT training for educators, investment in smart teaching
technologies, strong industry collaboration, supportive institutional policies, peer mentoring,
regular curriculum review, and improved infrastructure to enhance effective curriculum
implementation in Nigerian higher institutions.

largely driven by technological advancements that continue to redefine pedagogical practices
across disciplines. Within this evolving context, smart teaching technologies have emerged as
critical tools for enhancing instructional delivery, engagement, and learning outcomes in higher
education. Bond et al. (2020) describe smart teaching technologies as digitally enhanced systems
that integrate artificial intelligence, learning analytics, and interactive platforms to facilitate
adaptive and personalised learning experiences.  Holmes et al. (2022) identify these technologies

Skills deficiency, in this context, refers to the gap between the required competencies for

effectively and the actual abilities possessed by individuals. It is often observed when
professionals lack the necessary technical, cognitive, or practical skills needed to meet job
expectations, particularly in dynamic and technology -driven environments (Redecker, 2020).
Skill deficiency can also be seen as a condition in which individuals are unable to demonstrate
adequate proficiency in relevant knowledge areas due to insufficient training, exposure, or
continuous professional development. This deficiency limits their capacity to adapt to emerging
innovations and evolving workplace demands (OECD, 2021). When educators lack the necessary
competencies to effectively integrate advanced teaching technologies into their instructional
practices, it limits their impact on learning outcomes. Skills deficiency is often exacerbated by
insufficient professional development opportunities and institutional support, particularly in
developing educational systems. These deficiencies are particularly critical in specialised fields
such as marketing education, where dynamic and technology -driven teaching approaches are
essential, thus drawing attention to the role of marketing educators within this evolving
framework.
Marketing educators play a pivotal role in equipping students with the knowledge and
competencies required to navigate an increasingly competitive and technology -driven business
environment. As facilitators of learning, they are expected to integrate contemporary teaching
tools and methodologies that reflect current industry practices. As explained by Buil et al.
(2020), marketing educators are responsible for bridging the gap between theoretical knowledge
and practical application by incorporating innovative teaching strategies that enhance student
engagement and skill acquisition. Schlee and Karns (2021) assert that effective marketing
educators must possess not only subject matter expertise but also the pedagogical and
technological competencies necessary to deliver relevant and impactful instruction. The
effectiveness of marketing education is largely dependent on educators’ ability to adopt and
utilise emerging technologies that align with industry trends in higher education institutions.
Higher institutions are increasingly confronted with the need to adopt innovative teaching
practices that align with global educational standards. However, the integration of smart teaching
technologies in these institutions is often constrained by infrastructural limitations, inadequate
training, and varying levels of technological proficiency among educators. According to Eze et
al. (2021), institutions in developing regions face significant challenges in adopting advanced
educational technologies due to resource constraints and limited capacity development
initiatives. Okoye et al. (2022) observe that the effectiveness of technology integration in
Nigerian higher institutions is largely dependent on the availability of skilled educators who can
effectively utilise these tools. Disparities in access to technology and professional development
opportunities continue to impact the quality of teaching and learning in higher education.  The
challenge is further compounded by limited access to advanced technological infrastructure,
inadequate institutional support, and insufficient training opportunities for educators. These

Concept of Smart Teaching Technologies
Smart teaching technologies represent a deliberate and systematic integration of advanced digital
tools and intelligent systems into the educational environment to enhance the delivery and
acquisition of knowledge. It involves the strategic planning, deployment, and utilization of
resources ranging from Artificial Intelligence (AI) to Learning Management Systems (LMS) in
ways that transform the traditional classroom into an interactive, data -driven ecosystem
(Adedoyin and Soykan, 2023). This modern practice is the strategic integration of cloud
computing, high -speed internet, and adaptive software to ensure that educators deliver
personalized learning experiences that cater to the unique needs of diverse students. It
encompasses activities such as the use of interactive whiteboards, virtual reality simulations, and
real-time feedback mechanisms aimed at generating high engagement levels and superior
learning outcomes (Eze and Nwankwo, 2021).
Smart teaching constitutes a core pillar of academic modernization, as it directly influences
student retention, instructional efficiency, and global competitiveness. Smart teaching
technologies are a pedagogical -enhancing phenomenon that aligns sophisticated software with
the evolving demands of the digital economy, ensuring that education remains both relevant and
accessible. The UNESCO Institute for Information Technologies in Education (2024) considers
smart teaching technologies as hardware and software frameworks that leverage data to foster
collaborative, flexible, and student -centered learning environments. OECD (2021) further notes
that the conceptualization of smart teaching has shifted from simple multimedia presentations to
a highly sophisticated digital infrastructure, providing educators with opportunities to utilize big
data and learning analytics to track student progress in real-time.

Meaning of Skill Deficiency
Skill deficiency is the measurable gap between the existing competencies of an individual and
the evolving requirements of a specific professional role or industry. Maclean et al., (2020)
explain that it is the systemic inadequacy in the acquisition, application, and mastery of technical
or soft skills ranging from digital literacy to analytical thinking in ways that impede the effective
execution of job -related tasks. This professional limitation is the strategic misalignment of
outdated educational curricula, lack of continuous training, and rapid technological
advancement, ensuring that practitioners remain incapable of meeting modern workplace
demands. It encompasses phenomena such as skill obsolescence, where once -relevant abilities
become redundant, and competency gaps, where new technological tools remain underutilized
due to a lack of practitioner proficiency (World Economic Forum, 2023).
Skill deficiency is a core threat to institutional productivity, as it directly influences operational
inefficiency, low employee morale, and a decline in organizational innovation. Skill deficiency is
a performance -inhibiting phenomenon that aligns stagnant pedagogical methods with the
accelerating pace of the global digital economy, ensuring that the workforce remains ill-equipped
for complex problem -solving. The International Labour Organization (2024) considers skill
deficiency as a structural mismatch in the labour market that siphons potential from the economy

Types of Smart Teaching Technologies
Smart teaching technologies are digital tools, such as learning management systems, artificial
intelligence, virtual reality, interactive whiteboards, video conferencing platforms, mobile
learning applications, and learning analytics systems that provide mechanisms for effective
content delivery, learner engagement, assessment, and personalized instruction.

Learning management systems:  Learning management systems are web -based educational
platforms that enable the administration, documentation, tracking, and delivery of t eaching and
learning activities. Garrison & Vaughan, (2020) describes it as centralized digital environments
where instructors upload course materials, assignments, quizzes, and feedback while students
access learning resources anytime and anywhere. LMS platforms such as Moodle, Google
Classroom, and Canvas improve instructional organization, learner participation, and academic
monitoring, thereby strengthening teaching effectiveness and academic continuity.

Artificial intelligence (AI):  Artificial Intelligence refers to computer systems designed to
simulate human intelligence in performing tasks such as decision -making, problem-solving, and
personalized recommendations. Luckin & Holmes, (2021) regard them  as an intelligent teaching
support system that enhances learning through automated grading, chatbots, adaptive learning
pathways, and predictive analytics. AI supports individualized instruction, student performance
tracking, and data -driven teaching decisions, helping educators improve classroom efficiency
and learning outcomes.

Virtual r eality: Virtual Reality is an immersive digital technology that creates simulated
environments where users can interact with three -dimensional learning experiences using
specialized devices (Merchant & Goetz, 2020). It is also characterized as an experiential learning
platform that allows students to engage in practical simulations, virtual laboratories, and real -life
scenarios without physical limitations. VR enhances experiential learning by providing realistic
simulations that improve understanding, problem -solving, and practical application of
knowledge.

Interactive whiteboards: Interactive Whiteboards are digital display systems that combine
projector technology, touch -sensitive screens, and internet connectivity to support classroom
instruction They are also described as smart classroom tools that enable instructors to present
multimedia content, annotate lessons in real time, and encourage collaborative participation
among students  (Thomas & Schmid, 2019) . Interactive whiteboards improve visual learning,
classroom engagement, and the delivery of complex concepts through dynamic presentation
methods.

systems that enable live virtual interaction between educators and learners across different
locations (Anderson & Walvoord, 2020). These platforms, such as Zoom, Microsoft Teams, and

Role of Smart Technologies in Enhancing Marketing Education
Smart technologies play a significant role in improving the quality, effectiveness, and relevance
of marketing education by integrating digital tools and innovative instructional systems into the
teaching and learning process. One major role of smart technologies in marketing education is
the improvement of instructional delivery. Digital teaching platforms enable educators to present
course content using multimedia resources such as videos, simulations, presentations, and
interactive discussions that simplify complex marketing concepts and improve student
comprehension (Eze & Chinedu, 2020). Smart boards, online presentation tools, and virtual
classrooms make lessons more engaging and flexible, allowing students to participate actively
regardless of physical location.
Smart technologies also promote experiential and practical learning, which is essential in
marketing education. Virtual reality, simulation software, and case -based digital platforms
provide students with opportunities to engage in real -life marketing scenarios such as consumer
behavior analysis, brand management, digital advertising campaigns, and market research
exercises (Okafor & Bello, 2022). These practical experiences help bridge the gap between
classroom theory and industry practice, preparing students for the demands of the modern
business environment.  Ibrahim & Yusuf, (2021) states that smart technologies  enhance
communication and collaboration. Video conferencing platforms such as Zoom and Microsoft
Teams support live interaction between educators, students, and industry professionals, creating
opportunities for guest lectures, seminars, and collaborative projects across institutio ns and
geographical boundaries. Social media platforms and discussion forums also encourage peer
learning, teamwork, and professional networking among students.
Smart technologies further support personalized learning and academic performance monitoring.
Artificial Intelligence and learning analytics systems help educators track student progress,
identify learning difficulties, and provide individualized support through adaptive learning

Skill Deficiencies of Marketing Educators in Nigeria
Skill deficiencies of marketing educators in Nigeria are the gaps in professional, technological,
pedagogical, and industry -related competencies that hinder effective teaching and learning in
higher institutions. These deficiencies have become more pronounced due to rapid changes in the
marketing environment, particularly the shift toward digital marketing, data -driven decision -
making, and technology-enhanced instruction. As a result, many marketing educators struggle to
keep pace with evolving global standards, thereby affecting the quality of graduates produced by
Nigerian universities and other tertiary institutions.
One major area of concern is digital literacy deficiency, which reflects the limited ability of some
educators to effectively use digital tools for teaching and learning. Many marketing lecturers still
find it difficult to integrate Learning Management Systems, online teaching platforms, and
digital collaboration tools into their instructional practices. This limitation reduces the
effectiveness of smart teaching technologies in classrooms and restricts students’ exposure to
modern, technology -driven learning experiences that are essential in contemporary marketing
education (Adebayo & Yusuf, 2021). Closely related is ICT competence deficiency, which
involves inadequate skills in the use of information and communication technologies for
academic purposes. Some marketing educators lack proficiency in using presentation software,
data processing tools, online research platforms, and educational applications that enhance
teaching delivery. This deficiency weakens the quality of instruction and limits the ability of
educators to present marketing concepts in more engaging, interactive, and practical formats that
align with current industry practices.
Okeke & Ibrahim, (2020) acknowledge the deficiency in digital marketing knowledge among
educators. Many still rely heavily on traditional marketing theories and fail to adequately
incorporate contemporary practices such as social media marketing, search engine optimization,
e-commerce strategies, and digital branding into their teaching. This creates a gap between what
is taught in classrooms and what is required in today’s digital business environment, thereby
reducing the relevance of marketing education. Data analysis and research skills deficiency also
poses a serious challenge. Effective marketing education requires the ability to analyze consumer
behavior, interpret market trends, and use statistical tools for decision -making. However, some
educators lack competence in modern data analysis software and research methodologies that
support evidence -based teaching. This limits their ability to guide students in developing
analytical and problem-solving skills necessary for the marketing profession.
There is a lso a notable deficiency in online teaching and assessment skills. With the increasing
adoption of virtual learning environments, educators are expected to design and deliver courses
effectively through digital platforms. However, many struggle with creating engaging online
content, conducting virtual assessments, and managing student participation in online classrooms

Implication for Curriculum Implementation in Higher Institutions in Nigeria
The implications of smart teaching technologies deficiency among marketing educators for
curriculum implementation in higher institutions in Nigeria are profound, as they directly
influence how effectively educational objectives are translated into classroom practice.
Curriculum implementation refers to the actual delivery of planned academic content, methods,
and assessment strategies in line with institutional and national educational goals ((Echegu &
Kiu, 2024)). When educators lack adequate digital, pedagogical, and industry -relevant skills, the
curriculum is often implemented in a way that deviates from its intended outcomes, thereby
reducing its effectiveness and relevance to contemporary societal and labour market needs.
One major implication is the reduced effectiveness of curriculum delivery. When educators lack
adequate digital, pedagogical, and technological competencies, curriculum content is often
implemented in a simplified or incomplete form. This leads to a situation where intended
learning outcomes are not fully achieved, thereby weakening the overall quality of teaching and
learning processes in higher institutions (Eze & Chinedu -Eze, 2020). Another implication is the
persistent mismatch between curriculum objectives and classroom practice. Although many
curricula in Nigerian higher institutions are designed to be competency -based and technology -
driven, inadequate educator skills result in the continued use of outdated instructional methods.
This creates a gap between what is prescribed in curriculum documents and what is actually
delivered in classrooms, thereby reducing the relevance of academic programmes (Olawale &
Garuba, 2021).
A further implication is the poor integration of smart teaching technologies into curriculum
implementation. Even where institutions provide digital tools such as Learning Management
Systems and virtual learning platforms, limited educator competence restricts their effective use.
This results in partial adoption of educational technologies, thereby undermining efforts toward
digital transformation in teaching and learning (Awofala & Fatade, 2022). Skill deficiencies also
imply reduced student engagement during curriculum delivery. When educators are unable to
effectively apply interactive and technology -enhanced teaching methods, learning becomes
predominantly teacher-centered. This reduces students’ active participation, critical thinking, and
collaborative learning experiences, all of which are essential for effective curriculum
implementation in modern education systems (Okoye & Nwafor, 2020).
There is also the implication of weak alignment between curriculum implementation and labour
market demands. Inadequate educator exposure to contemporary industry practices and digital
marketing trends leads to the delivery of outdated content. This affects the ability of institutions

Upskilling Professional Development and Capacity Building
Upskilling are the deliberate and structured approaches designed to enhance the existing
competencies of marketing educators to meet the demands of smart teaching technologies and
contemporary curriculum implementation. Upskilling focus on equipping educators with updated
digital, pedagogical, and industry -relevant skills that improve their effectiveness in classroom
instruction and academic delivery. In higher institutions in Nigeria, upskilling is essential for
bridging the gap between traditional teaching methods and the requirements of technology -
driven education systems (Ezeani & Okwu, 2021).
One key upskilling strategy is continuous ICT and digital literacy training. This involves regular
training sessions aimed at improving educators’ ability to use computers, internet -based tools,
and educational software effectively. Such training covers the use of Learning Management
Systems, virtual classrooms, presentation tools, and online collaboration platforms. By
strengthening digital literacy, educators become more capable of integrating smart technologies
into teaching and improving student engagement (Aina & Olaniyi, 2020).
Another important strategy is hands -on training in educational technologies. This approach
emphasizes practical, experiential learning where educators actively engage with smart teaching
tools rather than only receiving theoretical instruction. Workshops and simulations are used to
demonstrate how to create digital content, conduct online assessments, and manage virtual
classrooms (Babalola & Adeyemi, 2022). This practical exposure helps educators build
confidence and competence in using modern instructional technologies
Industry-based upskilling is also a critical strategy, as it exposes educators to current marketing
practices and digital business environments. Through internships, industrial attachments, and
collaborations with marketing firms, educators gain firsthand experience in areas such as digital
marketing, consumer analytics, and e-commerce. This exposure helps them update their teaching
content and align classroom instruction with real -world industry trends (Ogunleye & Ajayi,
2021).
Peer-to-peer learning and mentorship programs also serve as effective upskilling strategies. In
this approach, more technologically proficient educators’ mentor their colleagues who have
limited digital skills. Collaborative learning communities within institutions encourage
knowledge sharing, joint problem -solving, and continuous skill improvement. This informal
learning structure enhances teamwork and accelerates the adoption of smart teaching practices
across departments.

Conclusion
The issues surrounding smart teaching technologies and skill deficiencies among marketing
educators have significant implications for the effectiveness of curriculum implementation in
higher institutions in Nigeria. It shows that when educators lack adequate digital, pedagogical,
and industry-related competencies, curriculum delivery becomes less effective, less practical, and
less aligned with contemporary marketing realities. This situation ultimately affects student
engagement, learning outcomes, and graduate employability, thereby weakening the overall
quality of higher education.
However, the integration of smart teaching technologies offers a transformative opportunity to
improve teaching and learning processes. When effectively utilized, these technologies enhance
instructional delivery, promote interactive learning, and support data -driven academic decision -
making. Despite this potential, their impact remains limited due to persistent skill gaps among
educators, inadequate training opportunities, and weak institutional support systems. Addressing
these challenges requires deliberate and sustained upskilling strategies, including continuous ICT
training, hands -on technological practice, industry exposure, and structured professional
development programmes. These interventions are essential for equipping marketing educators
with the competencies required to effectively implement modern curricula and respond to
evolving educational demands. Such efforts will not only improve teaching effectiveness but also
ensure that graduates are better prepared to meet the demands of a dynamic and technology -
driven global marketing environment.

Recommendations
The ensuing recommendations are proposed:
1. Higher institutions should organize regular training, workshops, and seminars to improve
educators’ digital literacy, pedagogical skills, and competence in smart teaching technologies.
2. Institutions should provide and maintain tools such as Learning Management Systems, smart
boards, virtual classrooms, simulation software, and other digital learning platforms to support
effective teaching and learning.
3. Marketing educators should be encouraged to engage with industry through internships,
consultancy services, and professional partnerships to align teaching with current market
practices.
4. Government and institutional authorities should allocate dedicated funding for staff training
and integrate digital competence requirements into promotion and appraisal systems.
5. Institutions should establish mentorship systems where technologically skilled educators
support colleagues with limited digital competencies.
