Abstract
Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) is transforming entrepreneurship by helping entrepreneurs
generate ideas, analyze information, create content, develop business models, support marketing, and make
decisions. This review examines the growing role of GenAI in entrepreneurial activities, with a focus on
opportunity recognition, creativity, innovation, SMEs, emerging economies, and responsible AI use. GenAI
can reduce resource constraints and provide affordable support to small businesses, but challen ges such as
inaccurate information, overdependence, privacy concerns, and unequal digital skills remain. The article
highlights the importance of human –AI collaboration and introduces the concept of AI entrepreneurial
capability, which includes AI literacy , effective prompting, evaluation of AI outputs, decision -making, and
responsible use of AI. The review concludes that future entrepreneurship should focus not only on using AI to
generate more opportunities but also on improving the quality, sustainability, and responsible development of
those opportunities.
Keywords: Generative Artificial Intelligence, Entrepreneurship, Entrepreneurial Capability, SMEs, Emerging
Economies

1. Introduction
Entrepreneurship is fundamentally concerned with
recognizing possibilities, creating value under
uncertainty and mobilizing limited resources to
transform ideas into viable economic or social
activity. Although entrepreneurial processes have
always been in fluenced by technological change,
digital technologies have increasingly altered how
entrepreneurs search for information, interact with
customers, develop products, organize resources
and communicate value propositions. The
emergence of Generative Artific ial Intelligence
represents a further development because it does
not merely provide access to information or
automate predetermined processes; it can
participate interactively in the production of ideas,
text, images, code, analyses and alternative
solutions. This characteristic makes GenAI
particularly relevant to entrepreneurship. Recent
empirical research, for example, indicates that
ChatGPT adoption can influence opportunity
recognition, digital entrepreneurial knowledge and
digital entrepreneurial self -efficacy, thereby
affecting entrepreneurial intentions and behaviour
(Duong & Nguyen, 2024).  The significance of
GenAI extends beyond individual entrepreneurs.
Small and medium enterprises (SMEs), which
often operate with limited financial and human
resources, may be able to access capabilities that
were previously available primarily to larger
organizations. Rajaram and Tinguely (2024) argue
that GenAI can democratize scalability and
creativity for SMEs, while simultaneously
emphasizing the importance of employee
competence, leadership, organizational culture,
collaboration and relationships with external
stakeholders.  Recent research also suggests that
the relationship between AI and entrepreneurial
outcomes should not be understood as a simple
technology-adoption relationship. Zhang et al.
(2025), using data from 1,021 Chinese university
students, found that AI knowled ge was positively
associated with entrepreneurial ability and that
different modes of GenAI use helped explain this
relationship. Similarly, research on entrepreneurial

4. Applications of Generative AI in
Entrepreneurship
4.1.Opportunity Recognition and Idea
Generation
Opportunity recognition is one of the most
significant areas in which GenAI can influence
entrepreneurship. Recent conceptual work
explicitly proposes that GenAI chatbots may
function as external [3] enablers of entrepreneurial
opportunity recognition, with entrepreneurial
alertness acting as a potential mechanism and
digital literacy and domain experience influencing
the relationship (Hossain Chowdhury et al., 2026).

4.2.Entrepreneurial Creativity
Creativity is another central application.
Entrepreneurs can ask GenAI systems to generate
alternative product concepts, identify unusual
combinations, rewrite value propositions from
different customer perspectives or develop multiple
communication approaches. Mostafiz et al. (2026)
provide an especially [4] important ethical
qualification. Their study, drawing on evidence
from India and Bangladesh, reports that GenAI use
was positively associated with unethical creativity
under certain entrepreneurial personality
conditions, while its direct influence on cre ativity
itself was statistically insignificant.
4.3.Business-Model Development
Business-model development requires
entrepreneurs to integrate customers, value
propositions, resources, channels, partnerships and
revenue mechanisms. GenAI can assist by
producing alternative business -model
configurations and challenging entrepreneurs to
consider different assumptions. For example, an
entrepreneur can request several revenue
mechanisms for a digital service, alternative
customer segments for a product or different
distribution arrangements for a local enterprise.
4.4.Entrepreneurship Education
GenAI is also changing entrepreneurship
education. Students can use AI to develop ideas,
simulate decisions, obtain feedback and experiment
with business models. Zhang et al. (2025) found
that AI knowledge and different patterns of GenAI
use were associated with entrepreneurial ability
among Chinese university students, with creativity
influencing some relationships. The educational
objective should therefore not be simply to teach
students how to generate content with AI [5].
5. Opportunities Created by Generative AI
5.1.Democratization of Entrepreneurial
Resources
One of the most important potential contributions
of GenAI is the democratization of access to
knowledge-intensive capabilities. A small business
with limited financial resources can potentially
access assistance in marketing, communication,
coding, customer interaction and business analysis.
Rajaram and Tinguely (2024) specifically highlight
this potential for SMEs, arguing that GenAI can
democratize scalability and creativity even where
firms have limited technical expertise and financial
resources [6].
5.2.Faster Experimentation and
Productivity
GenAI can reduce the time required to generate and
revise alternatives, allowing entrepreneurs to move
through early-stage experimentation more rapidly.

5.3.New Venture Creation
Emerging evidence suggests that AI capabilities
may influence the entrepreneurial ecosystem itself.
UN Trade and Development (UNCTAD, 2025)
notes that AI is increasingly being used by start-ups
and MSMEs in areas such as marketing, customer
service, logistics, finance and product design, while
emphasizing that skills, finance, data, infrastructure
and enabling ecosystems remain critical to
successful adoption [8].

7. Generative AI in Emerging Economies
The implications of GenAI are particularly
significant in emerging economies because
technology can simultaneously reduce capability
constraints and expose existing infrastructural
inequalities. Shore et al. (2024) found that GenAI
and entrepreneurial orie ntation contributed to
entrepreneurial resilience among SMEs, although
market turbulence altered the relationships under
examination. This produces a paradox: “GenAI
may democratize entrepreneurial capability at the
level of the individual while simultaneo usly
widening capability differences between
individuals and regions.”
8. Challenges of Generative AI in
Entrepreneurship
8.1.Hallucination and Reliability
AI systems may generate confident but inaccurate
information. This creates significant risks when
entrepreneurs use GenAI for market research,
financial analysis, legal interpretation, regulatory
advice or competitor assessment [10 – 11].
8.2.Algorithmic Bias
AI systems can reproduce biases embedded in
training data or system design. Bias can influence
customer segmentation, hiring, market
recommendations and other entrepreneurial
decisions. This is particularly important when AI -
generated recommendations affec t individuals or
groups.
8.3.Privacy and Data Security
Entrepreneurs may unintentionally provide
confidential information to AI systems. Such
information can include customer records, financial
data, product designs, supplier information and
strategic plans. AI governance should consequently
be incorporated in to routine entrepreneurial
management.
8.4.Cognitive Dependence
Perhaps the most subtle risk is cognitive
dependence. If entrepreneurs continuously
outsource ideation, communication and analysis to
AI, they may become less inclined to independently
question assumptions or develop alternatives. This
produces a central r esearch question: “How can
entrepreneurs use AI extensively without becoming

11. Emerging Research Directions
Future research should move beyond measuring AI
adoption toward examining strategic human –AI
collaboration and its impact on entrepreneurial
capabilities. Greater attention is needed to
opportunity quality, including novelty, feasibility,
customer value, s calability, and relevance.
Researchers should develop validated measures of
AI entrepreneurial capability, covering AI literacy,
prompting, verification, orchestration, and
responsible AI use. Micro and small enterprises
also require greater attention beca use they may
gain significantly from accessible AI while facing
limited governance, training, and data resources.

contexts shaped by differences in infrastructure,

Conclusion
Generative AI represents an important
development in the evolution of entrepreneurship
because it expands the entrepreneur's capacity to
generate ideas, synthesize information, experiment
with alternatives and perform knowledge-intensive
activities. Its po tential applications extend from
opportunity recognition and creativity to
marketing, business -model development, product
innovation, decision support and entrepreneurship
education. The emerging literature suggests that the
greatest entrepreneurial value of GenAI may not lie
in replacing human capabilities but in augmenting
them. AI can increase the speed and breadth of
entrepreneurial experimentation, while humans
contribute contextual knowledge, judgment, ethical
reasoning, accountability and implementat ion
capability. This distinction is particularly
significant for SMEs and entrepreneurs in
emerging economies. GenAI can potentially
democratize access to capabilities that were
previously expensive or difficult to obtain, but
unequal infrastructure, skills, finance and
institutional support may also reproduce existing
inequalities. Digital literacy, institutional
conditions and structural inequalities will influence
outcomes. The article therefore proposes AI
entrepreneurial capability as a useful conceptual
construct for future research. The capability
involves AI literacy, effective interaction, critical
evaluation, human –AI orchestration,
entrepreneurial integration and resp onsible
governance. Ultimately, the future of
entrepreneurship is unlikely to be either purely
human or purely artificial. A more plausible
trajectory is human -led, AI -augmented
entrepreneurship, in which entrepreneurs use
machine-generated capabilities wh ile retaining
responsibility for defining opportunities,
evaluating alternatives, making ethical judgments
and creating real-world value.
