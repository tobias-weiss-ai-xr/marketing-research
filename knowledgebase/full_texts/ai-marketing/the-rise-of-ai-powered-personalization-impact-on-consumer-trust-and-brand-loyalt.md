Introduction
AI has brought about a major revolution in digital marketing because of the ways in which it gives an
opportunity for organizations to create customer experiences that are very technologically advanced, to
the extent that technologies such as machine learning, predictive analytics, recommendations, chatbots,
and dynamic content delivery can create personalization. As opposed to conventional mass marketing
techniques where the message is the same for everyone regardless of the differences between customers,
personalized marketing through AI uses data collected about browsing behaviors, purchase histories,
demographics, and interactions of consumers to provide tailored solutions and services to them. Therefore,
AI has become a strategic tool for organizations in achieving greater customer engagement.

Abstract: Artificial intelligence (AI) has changed digital marketing through
personalized experiences of brands through recommendation system, predictive
analysis, chatbot, and dynamic content. Although AI-based personalization is
considered to positively contribute towards customer engagement and satisfaction,
the effects of such personalization on building consumer trust and brand loyalty
have not been well understood. This explains why this study is undertaken in order
to understand the effect of AI-based personalization on consumer trust and brand
loyalty in digital marketing and to examine the mediating effect of consumer trust.
Based on the Commitment-Trust theory, this study hypothesizes that consumer
trust builds up through AI-based personalized interactions and that such trust
enhances the brand loyalty of consumers. In this study, a quantitative research
design was employed where the data was collected from 70 consumers within the
age group of 18-35 years through an online structured questionnaire. The SPSS
software was used for analyzing the data through reliability, correlation, regression
and mediation analyses. Moreover, the study revealed that consumer trust
positively affected brand loyalty and acted as a partial mediator between the
personalization strategy through AI and brand loyalty. The findings expand on the
Commitment-Trust Theory in the realm of AI-driven digital marketing by showing
that trust is an important psychological factor that connects personalization
techniques to the outcome of brand loyalty. From a practical point of view, the study
indicates that firms need to couple AI-based personalization with clear data policy,
ethical use of customer information, and privacy protection to create trust and
promote brand loyalty.

The Rise of AI-Powered Personalization: Impact on Consumer Trust and Brand Loyalty in Digital Marketing
Personalization enabled through artificial intelligence has changed the customer experience in a great
way compared to conventional marketing. Based on the literature, new studies demonstrate how
personalization through the use of AI positively affects the consumer experience, consumer engagement
and even brand loyalty, as the experiences perceived as personalized are viewed by consumers as more
relevant, convenient and beneficial (Abdullah, 2025; Sipos, 2025). If consumers are convinced that
organizations pay attention to the needs and desires of customers, they will build positive attitudes
towards the brand and maintain the customer-brand relationship.
Nevertheless, there are numerous concerns regarding the privacy of consumers, transparency issues,
algorithmic bias and ethical usage of data. As for personalized recommendations, it requires collecting,
analyzing and processing large amounts of personal data. Even though personalized recommendations and
services are welcomed by the customers, in case of lack of transparency, consumers may perceive the usage
of personal data by companies as an invasion into their privacy (Cicek et al., 2025). It is considered a
paradox called the personalization–privacy paradox, as consumers wish to receive personalized services
but, on the other hand, they are concerned about their privacy and the safety of their data (Putri & Milad,
2025).
Under this condition, consumer trust becomes one of the major psychological elements required for
effective AI-based personalization. Consumer trust here means the consumer’s belief in the proper use of
AI technology by companies to ensure protection of their privacy while providing useful and accurate
personalized experiences to them. Based on previous literature, consumers will tend to trust more those
companies that are transparent, fair, and ethical with the use of AI -based marketing (Sipos, 2025).
However, excessive transparency, bias, and intrusion may lead to distrust of AI technology, resulting in
poor customer satisfaction and relationship building in the future (Cicek et al., 2025).
The Commitment-Trust Theory (Morgan & Hunt, 1994) provides a good explanation of the importance
of trust in developing sustainable customer relationships. Trust and commitment are suggested as basic
precondition variables for successful relationship marketing, since they help customers have ongoing
relationships with the organisation. In an AI-driven digital marketing era, personalisation is not just a
technological possibility but a relational approach that showcases expertise, trustworthiness, and
customer-centricity. However, AI-driven personalisation, when done right and responsibly, can make
consumers feel more trusted by the organisations, leading to increased commitment and long-term brand
loyalty (Morgan & Hunt, 1994).
While numerous studies have found positive associations between AI personalization and desirable
customer results, such as customer satisfaction, customer engagement, purchase intent and brand loyalty,
there are key areas that have not yet been explored. The existing literature largely focuses on the direct
effect personalisation has on consumer behavior, with little attention paid to the psychological processes
and pathways behind the impact of personalisation on a consumer–brand relationship over time (Turki,
2025; Ahmed et al., 2025). Specifically, there is limited empirical work that has explored consumer trust
as a mediator in the link between AI-enabled personalisation and brand loyalty. Moreover, much of the
existing research has centered on its short-term behavioural consequences (purchase intention and
satisfaction) while ignoring the study of long-term consequences of customer relationships, which are at
the heart of the relationship marketing concepts (Delgado-Ballester & Munuera-Alemán, 2001).
A lack of use of accepted relationship marketing concepts in the context of AI personalization is also a
gap in the literature. Although the technological dimension of AI personalization has garnered significant
research, a lack of studies have combined theoretical frameworks like Commitment-Trust Theory with AI
personalization to explain how AI personalization fosters long-term consumer relations (Ahmad et al.,
2025). There is a need for additional empirical evidence, therefore, to analyze whether AI -driven
personalization has a positive influence on brand loyalty mainly due to its ability to build consumer trust,
as well as to direct technological impacts.
This study aims to examine the connection between AI-driven personalization, consumer trust, and
brand loyalty in digital marketing, filling the identified research gaps. In particular, this study focuses on
the relationships between AI-powered personalization and consumer trust, AI-powered personalization

Literature Review and Hypotheses Development
AI-Powered Personalization and Consumer Trust
The introduction of Artificial Intelligence (AI) technology has brought about a revolution in digital
marketing, where personalization is the strongest innovation through which organizations can personalize
their products, services and messages to customers based on their behavior and preferences. The difference
between AI technology and conventional segmentation techniques is the fact that it uses machine learning
and predictive analytics along with behavioral data to offer a personalized customer experience, as
highlighted by Ziakis & Vlachopoulou (2023). Through the application of AI, personalization is among the
top priorities for organizations wishing to build stronger customer relationships and drive growth.
There has been enough evidence found in the literature to prove that the effect of personalization
through AI on consumers' perception of service quality and convenience is always positive. It may reduce
the time spent searching for desired goods while red ucing the information overload of consumers,
resulting in more effective shopping experiences. In a similar fashion, Gomes et al. (2025) stated that AI
may help organizations predict customers' needs and give them relevant recommendations, which will
result in higher levels of customer satisfaction. This evidence shows that personalization is not just about
making transactions easier but about making consumers feel special.
Although there are many benefits of personalized transactions provided by AI, their effectiveness
greatly depends on consumers' readiness to trust organizations collecting their private information. AI
personalization is also grounded in data, rather than traditional marketing methods, and it involves large
amounts of consumer data, such as purchase behaviour, browsing history, demographic information and
online activities. This has brought to the forefront important topics concerning data privacy, transparency,
algorithmic fairness, and the ethics of data governance, which shape consumer responses to AI
technologies (Turki, 2025). The perceived value of these personalised experiences is intrusive and not
helpful if a consumer feels that an organisation is collecting too much information and the process is not
transparent, or where they have little control over the information being collected.
Empirical research in the last few years shows that trust is increased substantially when
personalisation is used in an ethical way, based on AI. Hassan et al. (2025) discovered that customers would
have greater confidence in the organisations if there were AI suggestions and those suggestions are deemed
to be accurate, transparent, and helpful. In the same vein, Ribeiro et al. (2025) found that transparency,
fairness, user control, and responsible use of data are important elements in fostering consumer trust in
AI marketing scenarios. These findings suggest that technological competence alone does not suffice to
foster trust if ethical business is not being conducted.
Cross-cultural findings also show that consumer trust is dependent on privacy expectations and
cultural attitudes towards the disclosure of data. There is a positive correlation between control perceived
over the use of personal data and acceptance of AI personalization as well as trust in the organizations
(Teepapal, 2025). Perceptions of unfairness or bias of the algorithm, however, reduce trust even if the

It is crucial to realize there exists a strong connection between AI -powered personalisation and

unscrupulous handling of data even when the potential for personalisation is strong.

consumer trust, there are relatively limited empirical studies that are able to assess this relationship in a
comprehensive theoretical structure where trust is connected to subsequent loyalty outcomes. Although
the effectiveness of personalisation technologies has been studied, few studies have examined the
psychological processes that make personalisation more successful in terms of the consumer–brand
relationship. Building sustainable brand loyalty in the digital space is impossible without building
consumer trust, and it's crucial to close this divide. Accordingly, the following hypothesis is proposed:
H1: AI-powered personalization has a positive and significant effect on consumer trust.

AI-Powered Personalization and Brand Loyalty
One of the most positive results of successful marketing is brand loyalty, which means that repeat
purchases of a brand, recommendations of the brand and long-term relationships with the brand are more
likely to happen in the case of loyal customers. With so many digital markets out there, it's tough to find
a way to build brand loyalty, especially when consumers can easily change brands at will. As a result,
companies are turning toward AI-driven personalisation to provide unique and meaningful experiences
for customers, fostering their ongoing engagement.
Personalization using AI enables companies to provide personalized suggestions, customized
marketing messages, dynamic websites, and customized interactions with consumers depending on the
customer's preferences and behaviors. This kind of personalization enhances perceived relevance and
convenience since what is being offered to the customers becomes relevant to them. Previous studies have
shown that personalized recommendations enhance customer satisfaction and emotional connection more
than non-personalized marketing messages. Thus, Personalization using AI is an important technique for
enhancing consumer-brand relationships.
As per the literature, there are multiple complementary factors that personalisation plays in brand
loyalty. Personalised experiences are a key factor in increasing perceived value by minimising consumers'
search time and giving them the information they need at the right time. When consumers feel like their
personalized interactions provide them with more value, they are more inclined to stick with the same
brand rather than exploring other options (Ahmed et al., 2025). Second, repeated and customized
communications result in emotional brand loyalty to the consumer. Consumers feel appreciated and
understood, which helps them to remain loyal beyond just the act of re-purchasing (Teepapal, 2025).
AI-driven personalisation also serves to strengthen the consumer's appreciation of the organisation's
efforts to satisfy the individual’s needs. Relationship marketing theory suggests that investing in
relationships induces consumers to freely remain in the relationship, as it will be costly to transfer or
strongly worth sacrificing the favours and experiences that have been customised (Kang et al., 2016). The
continuous learning process from consumers' interactions will help improve personalization, which leads
to better consumer experience and increased consumer loyalty.
The relationship has received a lot of support from the empirical studies conducted recently. As
mentioned by Ahmed et al. (2025), the application of artificial intelligence for personalization in marketing

This relationship can be explained by the Commitment-Trust Theory (Morgan & Hunt, 1994). This

important factors that help an organisation to build long-term customer loyalty. One of those investments
is AI-powered personalization, as it is an indication that organisations recognise the needs of individual
consumers or customers and are prepared to offer better customer experiences. These tailored interactions
help to build trust and loyalty in the relationship, thereby encouraging consumers to stick with the brand
in the future.
While some studies have demonstrated the positive relationship between AI-powered personalization
and brand loyalty, many of those have only analyzed direct effects and paid insufficient attention to the
psychological mechanisms that underlie this relationship. Moreover, most literature has focused on
customer satisfaction and buying intentions, but not necessarily on customer loyalty as an outcome of the
strategic relationship. More empirical results are needed to establish if AI-driven personalization can boost
brand loyalty in today's digital marketing landscape. Accordingly, the following hypothesis is proposed:
H2: AI-powered personalization has a positive and significant effect on brand loyalty.

Consumer Trust and Brand Loyalty
Consumer trust has been identified as one of the key factors of relationship marketing and one of the best
consumer-brand loyalty indicators. Trust plays an important role in a digital marketing environment,
where it is more challenging for consumers to personally evaluate products and meet with the service
provider, leading to higher uncertainty and perceived risks in an online transaction (Gefen et al., 2003). In
the end, it's up to the organisations to create safe transactions, protect individual data and deliver the
products/services they were promised and to do so in an ethical way throughout the customer lifecycle. As
a result, organizations that create a high level of consumer trust in them are more likely to create lasting
customer relationships and gain a competitive advantage that they can sustain.
Trust helps to decrease consumers' perception of financial, performance and privacy risks when they
engage in online purchases. If a consumer thinks that the organization is honest, competent and reliable,
then they tend to make repeat purchases, recommend the brand to their friends and family and have long-
term relationships with the organization even if better options are available in the market (Ranaweera &
Prabhu, 2003; McKnight et al., 2002). In the age of digital commerce, it has become an essential business
asset that cannot be sacrificed when it comes to retaining customers in continually expanding markets.
There is a lot of empirical evidence which shows a positive relation between consumer trust and brand
loyalty. The consumers with digital brand trust had higher attitudes towards brand loyalty, had a greater
tendency to repurchase and exhibited more positive word-of-mouth activities. Likewise, Mofokeng (2023)
indicated that trust had a significant effect in decreasing perceived risk, thus positively influencing the
purchase intention of consumers to buy from the same brand. These findings suggest that trust is not
merely a psychological measure but also an important behavioral measure of buying with the view of
developing a long-term relationship.

A field study on the incipient digital markets has found similar results. In fast-growing e-commerce
platforms, where consumers are presented with numerous choices and the service is relatively easy to
switch to, the significant impact of consumer trust on customer loyalty was found by Kurniadi and Rana
(2023). In the same way, Hermawan (2019) said that as digital markets get more competitive, trust
becomes more important in that market as consumers have to rely on brands that they can trust to take
away the uncertainty when they shop online.
A very important factor towards gaining consumer trust and brand loyalty is also the reliability of a
website and the quality of service and its online reliability. Secure payment systems, a user-friendly design
of the website, transparent communication, quality customer service, and product delivery have been
found to continuously enhance consumer trust towards online retailers (Gefen et al., 2003; Cyr, 2008). If
they are consistently served and have a reliable digital experience, customers will be more likely to trust
organisations, and thus continue to purchase the same brand.
Recent research has also revealed that trust has become even more crucial as the use of AI-driven
marketing tools is becoming increasingly widespread. Although AI systems provide highly personalised
surface interactions with customers, the use of confidential information, transparency of algorithms, and
ethical applications of private data are significant concerns for consumers. Ribeiro et al. (2025) argued that
to ensure AI-driven personalization is successful for long-term customer loyalty, it is crucial to develop
consumer confidence through clear communication, ethical data handling practices, and responsible AI
application. Likewise, Verma et al. (2023) concluded that organizations which used an advanced level of
digital technology along with trustworthy business practices had better customer retention and loyalty as
compared to organizations that relied on technological innovation alone.
The relationship between consumer trust and brand loyalty is very well supported by Commitment-
Trust Theory (Morgan & Hunt, 1994). It is proposed that the main ingredient of organisations' ability to
create long-term relationships with consumers is trust. Trust decreases uncertainty, builds relationship
commitment and enhances consumers' relationship continuation intentions when attractive competitive
relationships are present. Digital marketing contexts are ones where consumers cannot see how one
organization acts; therefore, trust plays an even more important role than in other contexts, as it serves
as a safeguard in this situation.
There's no shortage of evidence that customer trust is a basis for consumer loyalty, but the fast-paced
nature of AI-powered marketing presents some new issues to customer trust. In order to maintain the
trust of consumers, organizations will need to const antly demonstrate transparency, fairness,
accountability and responsible management of information. Without tackling these, it could create
mistrust and result in suboptimal personalisation efforts driven by AI. Consumer trust plays an important
role in understanding how organisations can develop sustainable customer loyalty in digitally-inclined
marketing environments. Based on the above theory and evidence, the following hypothesis is proposed:
H3: Consumer trust has a positive and significant effect on brand loyalty.

Mediating Role of Consumer Trust
Although there are some evidences in literature showing how personalisation can positively affect
consumer trust and brand loyalty, there has been a gradual shift towards studying the psychological route
between personalisation and consumer loyalty over time. The importance of AI in personalisation extends
beyond the technology, influencing consumers' perception of the organisations across dimensions like
trust, transparency, and customer service. For this reason, the importance of the mediating role of
consumer trust has been gradually recognized as a key factor that fosters the personalization of brand
loyalty with AI (Markou et al., 2025).
By using AI to make personalized suggestions, send relevant messages, and provide tailored services,
the customer experience is improved. The intelligent interactions reflect an awareness of the worth of
customers and a desire to make an effort to build real customer relationships. However, consumers need
to have a favorable perception of AI's use by the organization and the protection of their personal data.

Therefore, it is not sufficient to only personalize to create sustainable customer loyalty but is essential to

More recently, there is a vast amount of empirical evidence to suggest this mediating relationship.
Hassan et al., (2025) found that the personalization using AI tools can boost customer satisfaction and
loyalty, as consumers tend to trust the effects of such systems. The results of their study revealed that on
an individual level, the personal recommendations positively influence customers' perception of reliability
and trust, which also results in customers' loyalty. Similarly, Ahmed et al. ( 2025) reported that AI
personalization positively affected the goals of boosting customer confidence and value perception of an
entity to achieve long-term commitment and loyalty.
Other studies conducted in broader digital marketing contexts have yielded similar findings. The
findings on the development of website quality, online service quality, and digital customer experiences
indicate that trust is an important psychological mechanism in linking positive experiences online with
customer loyalty. When consumers feel that they are dealing with an organization online that is reliable,
transparent and beneficial, they increase their confidence and are more inclined to keep buying from the
same brand and to recommend it to others. A conclusion of these facts is that technology improvements
alone do not result in loyalty except for an initial customer's confidence in the company.
Additionally, studies of younger digital consumers argue this point. Based on Hidayah (2025), trust
was one of the most significant factors affecting e-loyalty among consumers of Generation Z, which was
quite close to positive digital experiences and customer satisfaction. Similarly, Ashiq and Hussain (2024)
suggested that psychological mechanism of trust is the link through which the digital innovations
influence the ultimate behavioural intentions of consumers. With the continued adoption of AI in digital
marketing, trust is likely to become more crucial than ever in the future to ensure that personalization
efforts yield sustainable customer relationships.
The Commitment-Trust Theory (Morgan & Hunt, 1994) is a solid foundation to base the mediation of
consumer trust on. It is the notion that only by developing trust can true long-term commitment and
loyalty be achieved. First and foremost, personalization is an investment in the relationship in the world
of AI-driven digital marketing that demonstrates an organization's expertise,  responsiveness, and
customer-centricity. As long as they are aware that their cash is being invested in a beneficial and ethical
way, consumer trust would provide a sense of safety and openness to them, reducing uncertainty and
encouraging them to build a longer-term relationship with that brand. Thus, AI-driven personalization
can be anticipated to have a direct impact on brand loyalty, and an indirect effect in terms of building
consumer trust.
Although previous studies have demonstrated the importance of trust in the context of digital
marketing, research examining the mediating effect of trust between AI-driven personalization and brand
loyalty in the same theoretical context is not abundant. The literature examined on these constructs tends
to either focus on them separately or in some cases the studies are limited to just direct relationships;
scarce literature is found that explains the psychological means through which these constructs are related.
The efforts to reduce this gap helps to improve the knowledge of relationship marketing in AI-powered
settings and gives theoretical foundations for the Commitment-Trust Theory in today's digital marketing.
As mentioned above, based on the theories and findings in this paper, consumer trust is hypothesized
to mediate between using AI-generated personalization and brand loyalty. So the following hypothesis is
formulated:
H4: Consumer trust mediates the relationship between AI-powered personalization and brand loyalty.

Methodology
Research Design
The study used quantitative research methodology to explore the associations between artificial
intelligence-based personalization, consumer trust and brand loyalty in the context of digital marketing.
The use of quantitative methodology seemed to be appropriate for the study since its aim was to test the
hypothesized relationships between measurable constructs by applying statistical methods (Creswell &

Creswell, 2018). The research utilized a deductive methodology, where hypotheses were formulated on the
basis of the Commitment-Trust Theory and previous literature and then were empirically tested.
In order to collect data, cross-sectional survey methodology was used. It involves data collection from
participants at one point in time. Such methodology is widely applied in consumer behavior and digital
marketing research due to the ability to investigate variable associations objectively and effectively.
Sample and Data Collection
The sample consisted of consumers ranging from 18 to 35 years old who have experience in using various
digital platforms with AI-powered personalization services such as e-commerce sites, social media sites,
and e-commerce apps. The selection of such an age category of consumers is justified by the fact that
young consumers are considered one of the largest groups of AI-based digital services users.
Primary data were gathered with the help of an online structured survey conducted on various social
media platforms. A total of 70 responses were received and analyzed. All participants in the study
voluntarily participated in it after being informed about the goals of the research. No personally identifying
information was collected during the survey.

Measurement of Variables
There were four sections in the questionnaire. The first one captured the demographic data, such as gender,
age, education qualification, and income levels. The other three sections were used for capturing the
measures of constructs used in the study.
AI-driven personalization was measured through the four-factor scale developed by An et al. (2025).
Consumer trust was captured through the five-item scale developed by Delgado-Ballester (2004). This
scale has been extensively used in the research on relationship marketing. Lastly, brand loyalty was
measured through the four-item scale developed by Chaudhuri and Holbrook (2001).
All the questions were measured using the five-point Likert scale, ranging from 1=Strongly Disagree
to 5= Strongly Agree. Utilizing the pre-tested measurement scales helps in improving the reliability and
validity of the instrument.

Data Analysis
SPSS software version 26 was used in the process of analyzing data. Prior to conducting hypothesis tests,
there was a data examination for missing data, coding issues, and other problems. Descriptive statistics
were calculated to obtain information about demographic characteristics of respondents.
Cronbach’s alpha coefficients were used to test the reliability of the instruments, where a result above
0.70 was taken to indicate that an instrument is reliable (Hair et al., 2019). Then, Pearson correlation
analysis was done to determine relationships between all three research variables – AI-powered
personalization, consumer trust, and brand loyalty. Multiple regression analysis was done in order to test
hypotheses stated in H1, H2, and H3. Moreover, a mediation effect of consumer trust on the relationship
between AI-powered personalization and brand loyalty (H4) was tested.

Ethical Considerations
Ethics have been maintained at each step of the study. Participation was totally voluntary, and the
respondents gave their informed consent before filling in the questionnaire. The respondents were
guaranteed anonymity and confidentiality of their answers and that the data would be used only for
scholarly purposes. Personal data were not collected; the data were aggregated before being analyzed.

Discussion and Conclusion
Discussion
The study explored the connection between AI-driven personalization, consumer trust and brand loyalty
in digital marketing. The study was based on the Commitment-Trust Theory, which suggests that the use

of AI for personalization can foster consumer trust, leading to increased brand loyalty. The results of the
empirical section indicated that all four hypotheses were accepted, confirming that there is a direct link
between AI personalized experiences and consumer trust and consumer loyalty towards a brand, and
mediated link between AI personalized experience and consumer trust consumer trust on one side and
consumer loyalty on the other side.
The initial hypothesis was that personalization with AI has a positive effect on consumer trust. This
proved that there is indeed a correlation since the consumers are loyal to organizations offering them
personalized digital experience. These findings correlate with previous findings about how personalization
through the use of AI increases consumers' feeling of relevance, convenience and organizational
performance hence increasing the level of trust (Sipos, 2025; Hassan et al., 2025). The findings are also
similar to findings of Ribeiro et al. (2025) who argued that AI technologies can increase the trust level of
the patients in the healthcare system only when the AI tools are utilized in a transparent and ethical way
with good data governance practices. Personalization alone is not enough; the companies have to take
responsibility for managing the consumers' personal information.
The second hypothesis sought to find out the correlation between the use of AI and personalization in
brand loyalty. Individualized recommendations, customized marketing communications, and tailored
online experiences seem to increase consumers' emotional engagement and repeat purchase action. This
aligns with Ahmed et al. ( 2025), who found that personalization fosters customer engagement,
satisfaction, and retention, and Beyari (2025), who noted that AI-driven personalization helps service
providers enhance customer engagement and satisfaction. With the growing competition in digital
channels, personalized customer experiences have emerged as one of the bigger advantages for a brand
and fostering customer relationships.
Consumer trust was tested for its impact on brand loyalty within the third hypothesis. It was observed
that consumer trust clearly provides a boost to brand loyalty as per the relationship marketing theory. In
the minds of consumers, trustworthy organizations are more likely to keep their business, to buy the
company's products or services again and to refer the brand. The results corroborate other studies that
show that trust decreases the uncertainty and perceived risk while increasing commitment and retention
of customers (Gefen et al., 2003; Mofokeng, 2023). The findings also validate the fact that trust is one of
the most important psychological factors that influences the sustainable consumer–brand relationships
in the digital marketing contexts.
A final hypothesis was also tested to understand the role of consumer trust as a mediator in the
relationship between AI-based personalization and brand loyalty. The results of the mediation analysis
revealed that consumer trust partially mediated this rel ationship, meaning that AI -powered
personalization can directly and indirectly boost brand loyalty with the addition of consumer trust. The
implications of this finding expand on the work of others, which demonstrated that personalization
impacts long-term consumer behaviour but had not yet investigated the psychological mechanism behind
this. AI-powered personalization does not just build loyalty through technology alone, it first fosters
consumers' trust in the organization, and then increases their loyalty. Hassan et al. (2025), Ahmed et al.
(2025), and Markou et al. (2025) found similar results, as trust was highlighted as a key mediator between
AI-driven marketing tactics and positive consumer outcomes.
Overall, the results are strong evidence of Commitment-Trust Theory (Morgan & Hunt, 1994). The
theory suggests that a company's ability to establish a sustainable relationship with customers is
facilitated by establishing trust first and then long-lasting commitment and loyalty. As the present study
shows, AI-driven personalization is not only a technological advancement but also an investment in
relationships. Personalised experiences that are done transparently, ethically and responsibly result in a
higher level of trust in organisations and greater brand loyalty from consumers as a result of the perceived
competence and integrity of the organisation.

Theoretical Implications
There are a number of theoretical contributions made by the current research in terms of literature on AI-
assisted digital marketing and relationship marketing. Firstly, the paper expands the application of the

Commitment-Trust Theory to AI-assisted digital marketing context showing that consumer trust is an
essential psychological tool for achieving consumer brand loyalty from AI-driven personalization. While
previous research mainly concentrated on direct relat ions between personalization and consumer
outcomes, the current research explains how personalization helps achieve loyalty through consumer
trust.
Secondly, the research contributes to the emerging body of literature on AI-assisted personalization
by combining a technological and psychological perspective in a unified framework. The results show that
AI-assisted personalization can be successfully applied when apart from technical aspects of the process
there are also considerations of consumers' perceptions of fairness and transparency.

Managerial Implications
Implications of the results for companies utilizing AI-driven personalization methods in practice may be
identified. First of all, the company must understand that AI-driven personalization alone is not enough
to build consumer loyalty in the long run. The use of AI should be supported by transparency, responsible
data management and ethics, which will help to raise consumer trust.
The second implication is related to managers' actions, which should prioritize transparency at every
stage of data collection, processing and use. Transparency in the policy on data privacy can help to raise
consumer trust.
Thirdly, it is necessary to review regularly the performance of algorithms and evaluate its efficiency.
Not only does the ethical approach solve the issue of privacy but also builds more trust towards
organizations among consumers and thereby contributes to creating greater consumer loyalty.
Finally, organizations need to view AI-based personalization as an instrument of relationship building
with customers rather than as merely innovative development for themselves. The combination of
personalization and customer-focused marketing strategies can be beneficial for organizations.

Limitations and Future Research
Despite all the above-mentioned merits of the research, there are many weaknesses related to this
particular study. First, this study is based on a relatively small sample of 70 respondents, which may
influence the generalizability of the results. Future studies in this field may take advantage of large and
varied samples from various countries.
Second, the study employs a cross-sectional method of data gathering and analysis. Longitudinal
methodology will be more beneficial for learning about changes in consumer trust and brand loyalty as a
result of constant use of personalized AI technologies by consumers.
In addition to that, this study focused on the influence of consumer trust as the mediating variable.
Further research may focus on other mediating variables including customer satisfaction, perceived value,
privacy risk perceptions, and consumer engagement.
Lastly, further research can be conducted to examine the moderating variables such as privacy
concerns, digital literacy, culture, and algorithmic transparency, amongst others.

Conclusion
AI-based personalized services have become an integral part of today’s marketing. According to the
findings of this research, personalized services contribute positively to consumer trust and brand loyalty.
Moreover, consumer trust acts as a partial mediator of the relationship between personalized services and
brand loyalty. Hence, it can be concluded that a high level of technological advancement is insufficient for
building consumer-brand relationships. In order to utilize AI technologies effectively, companies need to
be responsible and ethical in implementing them.
