Abstract

Objective: To identify the calculation and valuation mechanisms employed by
consumers and sellers in the vinyl record market, exploring how these mechanisms
influence the monetary value of records.
Method: A qualitative and descriptive study with an interpretivist perspective, using
interviews with vinyl record consumers and sellers, as well as data from online sales
platforms. Five categories were identified through thematic content analysis.
Results: The valuation of vinyl records is influenced both by the physical
characteristics of the records and by perceptions of intangible elements related to the
experience of listening to music on vinyl. The analysis identified five categories that
explain price formation in the vinyl record market, with pricing being grounded in
and explained by the other four: price perception, valued elements of the records,
media condition grading, and the influence of buying and selling websites,
highlighting the complexity of the value attribution process in this market.
Theoretical/Methodological Contributions: The study shows that value
attribution is not limited to the individual capabilities of agents, as it is also shaped
by collective aspects. It also emphasizes the importance of subjectivity in value
definition and how calculation influences the market.
Originality/Relevance: The study addresses valuation dynamics in the vinyl record
market, a topic that has been little explored in the literature. It examines calculative
agency and how consumers and sellers interact and evaluate products in a market
that has experienced a resurgence after nearly becoming extinct.
Managerial Contributions: Managerial implications include insights that help to
understand different pricing strategies and the importance of analyzing the dynamics
of value perceptions of products.

responsible consumption and production

2 Constructivist Market Studies

Constructivist Market Studies (CMS) constitute an interdisciplinary field of study aimed
at understanding market dynamics. In contrast to the view of markets as static and predefined
entities, this approach focuses on analyzing the practices that constitute them (Kjellberg & Murto,
2021; Harrison & Kjellberg, 2016). This interdisciplinary perspective examines how these
interactions, mediated by tools, technologies, and material resources, influence market structures.
Thus, CMS examines how specific markets are shaped by a variety of actors, such as consumers,
firms, governments, and other institutions, who interact with one another, employing various
practices and devices to give shape to the market (Geiger et al., 2012).
CMS operates with a more performative view of the market (Andersson, Aspenberg &
Kjellberg, 2008). This approach proposes that market participants themselves, through their

interaction of multiple entities, each with its own distinct qualities.
CMS is strongly influenced by the works of sociologist Michel Callon, who shaped studies
in the area by considering markets as dynamic and constantly evolving social constructions,
influenced by interactions among diverse elements, which include both human  and non-human
agents, such as objects and technologies (Nøjgaard & Bajde, 2021).
CMS is dedicated to analyzing the processes of economization, investigating how social
scientists and market actors collect and qualify actions, devices, and analytical or practical
descriptions as economic elements (Leme & Rezende, 2018). According to Cal lon (1998),
economics does not limit itself to observing reality, but also actively constructs and shapes it.
Economic theories and models prescribe behaviors and legitimize forms of social organization,
exerting a direct influence on how the economic system is configured (Callon, 1998).
Based on Callon’s notes on the importance of economic theories to the market, CMS
studies began to investigate how theories from the field of marketing influence the construction
and dynamics of markets (Araujo, 2007; Kjellberg & Helgesson, 2006; Mason, Kj ellberg &
Hagberg, 2015; Nenonen et al., 2024).
In this regard, Nøjgaard and Bajde (2021) point out that following the adoption of Callon's
ideas by marketing scholars, CMS researchers began dedicating themselves to understanding how
markets are constantly created and shaped by a heterogeneous set of market actors equipped with
material resources and by the performances of these actors as described in marketing theories
(Nøjgaard & Bajde, 2021).
CMS focuses on practices as a way to realize relationships between different entities, with
these practices being essential to establishing the medium through which elements such as
products, consumers, stores, advertising, and money interact and structure markets (Nøjgaard &
Bajde, 2021). Market activities go beyond simply operating within an already established system,
encompassing both the creation and the influencing of this system. This influence occurs through
planned actions, such as marketing, advertising, and sales strategies, as well as through interactions
- whether conscious or not - among the different market actors (Araújo, Kjellberg & Spencer,
2008).

Kjellberg and Helgesson (2006) argue that entities participating in the market do not
possess an inherent capacity for action, but rather that this capacity develops from the social
interactions and practices that occur among them. They suggest three inter connected types of
practices that help shape markets: exchange practices, normative practices, and representational
practices (Kjellberg & Helgesson, 2006, 2007). They also argue that these practices are linked
through sequences of translations that lead to the constitution of the market.
A key concept in market studies is that of market agencements, which are associated with
the creation of structures and the integration of capacities for action within these structures,
involving individuals, objects, or devices. This suggests that the organization of markets goes
beyond creating static and predicta ble configurations, being able to generate active and adaptive
market agencements capable of acting and adjusting to diverse situations (Çalişkan & Callon,
2010).
Another important concept is that of market devices. Muniesa , Millo, and Callon (2007)
define market devices as assemblages of material and discursive elements that shape actions and
interactions in markets. These devices can articulate actions, operating autonomously or
influencing the actions of other agents. Mar ket devices play a crucial role by demonstrating the
influence and structuring action of technical and material elements in market activities and the
economic process (Callon, 2008).

3 Calculative agency in market studies

CMS views agency as collective, attributing the capacity to act to both humans and
technical objects. Thus, CMS holds a sociotechnical view of agency (Andersson et al., 2008;
Çalişkan & Callon, 2010; Cochoy, 2008; Kjellberg & Helgesson, 2006). Market agents can be both
human and non -human, combining to enable action. Human elements, such as skills and
knowledge, complement non -human ones, such as technology, norms, and institutions. The
interaction among these elements is crucial for the functioning of market agents. The importance
of market objects and devices in shaping these agents is highlighted, as they facilitate transactions,
provide resources, influence practices, and connect agents to one another (Geiger et al., 2012).
In this sense, a type of sociotechnical agency linked to CMS is the calculative agency
(Araújo, 2007; Çalişkan & Callon, 2010). The market is configured as a dynamic and interactive
space where diverse calculative agents, guided by their particular interests, negotiate and confront

contract or price (Callon, 1998).
This constant dynamic, marked by tensions and consensus, shapes transaction outcomes
and contributes to the formation of prices and values. Calculation represents a delicate yet
fundamental process within the context shared and collectively constructed by consumers and
other agents (Cochoy, 2008).
For these negotiations to occur, the market agents involved in the process need the capacity
to make evaluations and establish hierarchies regarding what is being assessed. In other words,
they need to know how to calculate (Callon, 1998). People, for inst ance, possess cognitive and
practical skills that enable them to qualify and attribute value to things (Çalişkan & Callon, 2009).
By doing so, agents are able to envision potential courses of action and their respective effects
(Callon, 1998).
Thus, calculation functions as a crucial tool for market participants, enabling them to
compare and rank exchange objects rationally, and allowing agents to maximize their benefits
when making decisions about participating in market exchanges (Nøjgaard & Bajde, 2021).
The determination of value in a market is configured as an intricate process of evaluation
that transcends the individual skills of agents, involving a multiplicity of collective dimensions.
This complex web of factors goes beyond human capabilities and ex isting structures, whether
social or institutional (Çalişkan & Callon, 2010; Callon, 1998).
Operating in the market requires agencies to participate in evaluation processes,
establishing the relative value and prices of their goods. This activity is fundamental to the
exchange of goods, since markets depend on this interaction to function. The ma rket, as an
environment of competition and confrontation, demands that the agencies involved be able to
perform valuation calculations effectively (Çalişkan & Callon, 2010).
During the qualification procedure, there is a collaborative creation in which both objects
and human identities are characterized simultaneously in the creation of value (Çalişkan & Callon,
2009). Establishing the value of an economic entity requires an appropriate definition of that value.
To achieve this, it is crucial to qualify products and identify the diverse ways in which value can
be associated with them (Cochoy & Dubuisson-Quellier, 2013).
Therefore, the capacity to calculate is configured as an important theme in CMS studies,
which recognize it as an essential tool for measuring and comparing values in market exchanges.

The calculation process plays a crucial role in ensuring proper financial organization during

characteristics of a good, compare them with one another, and attribute a value to them. It is worth
noting that this process considers diverse criteria, which may conflict with one another

4 Methodology

A descriptive qualitative study was conducted from an interpretivist perspective. Since
calculative agency depends on the - often subjective and multi -factored - evaluation made by
individuals, and on their computational capacity to determine the relative value and prices of goods
(Çalişkan & Callon, 2010), a qualitative approach was chosen due to its ability to unravel the
meaning of a phenomenon for those involved (Merriam & Tisdell, 2015).

The research subjects were Brazilian vinyl record consumers and sellers. They were
classified as consumers; consumers and sellers (with a primary focus on consumption); and
consumers and sellers (with a primary focus on sales), given that all sellers also considered
themselves consumers, differing only in the position that selling occupied in this relationship. To
select the consumers, individuals who maintained a deeper connection with vinyl records were
chosen - this connection was verified by their declaration that this medium is one of their primary
ways of listening to music, and by the fact that they are colle ctors who frequently purchase vinyl
records. For the sellers, the requirement was to own a record store, whether virtual or physical.
Participants were selected using the snowball sampling technique, characterized by the
progressive recruitment of new respondents based on referrals from individuals previously
included in the study (Tripathy et al., 2026). Under the assumption that the pa rticipants' contact
networks enable access to subjects representative of the investigated population, the initial
identification of the first participants occurred through searches in the Instagram followers network
of one of the authors, as well as through prior mapping of stores known to the researchers.
The first contact was made by sending a direct message with a brief introduction to the
study and an invitation to the interview. The first participants referred other individuals whom they
believed fit the research profile, and these individuals were cont acted via Instagram and
WhatsApp. Data were  collected through audio -recorded semi -structured interviews structured
around an initial guide exploring the valued characteristics of records and methods of qualifying
goods. The interviews lasted 45 minutes on average. Participants signed an Informed Co nsent
Form to participate in the study. To ensure anonymity, participants chose pseudonyms based on
musical artists.
In total, 14 interviews were conducted with consumers and sellers (Table 1) - seven of
whom acted in the market only as consumers and seven as both consumers and sellers. The
criterion for determining the sample size was saturation of data (Hackney, 2020).  In the eleventh
interview, redundancy in responses was identified among both consumers and consumer/sellers,
which led to ending the data collection after conducting three additional interviews that had already
been scheduled, in accordance with the data saturation criterion.

5 Results

The analysis of the interviews and collected data allowed for the identification of five main

The results described above presented elements related to calculative agency in the vinyl
record market. It is worth highlighting here that these categories are interconnected and influence
each other in various ways, as illustrated in Figure 1.

interdependent process in which multiple objective and subjective factors articulate to construct
both the economic value and the symbolic value attributed to the product.

Our findings underscore the relevance of calculative agency capacity in defining vinyl
record prices. Market participants act as calculative agents, conducting analyses and evaluations
to establish record values and prices. This calculation process is not restricted solely to the
individual skills of the agents but incorporates a variety of collective aspects. Market participants
engage in evaluations that transcend their personal capacities, being shaped by and shaping social
practices and interactions (Çalişkan & Callon, 2010; Callon, 1998).
This approach is in line with the perspective of CMS, which recognizes the ability of agents
to conduct calculations and evaluations that impact market decisions, demonstrating the
importance of active engagement in their formation and organization (Callon, 1998).
Evidence is also presented regarding how pricing and exchange actions carried out by those
involved in the market affect vinyl record prices. Using diverse criteria and strategies, buyers and
sellers determine record values, resulting in a diversity of pri ces observed in the market. By
assigning values and negotiating records, agents actively influence the constitution and evolution
of the vinyl record market, demonstrating market performativity and the influence of social
practices in determining economic values (Andersson et al., 2008; Harrison & Kjellberg, 2016).

The results of the study demonstrate how sellers and consumers in the vinyl record market
use calculative devices to make strategic decisions and negotiate prices based on the records' value.
For instance, consumers who mention the importance of digging in  different locations to find
affordable records and statements highlighting the valuation of vinyl due to the scarcity of pressing
plants illustrate how market actors consider various factors - such as supply and demand, record
condition, and historical value - when making buying and selling decisions.
These decisions directly influence market dynamics, affecting the prices, availability, and
diversity of records available to consumers. These findings contribute to the ideas of Çalişkan and
Callon (2010), who view the capacity to qualify goods and perfor m calculations as a form of
market participation. According to the authors, this interaction surrounding the practice of
qualifying and calculating is essential for the existence of markets.
The results show that the attribution of value is not limited to the individual capacities of
the agents, as it is also shaped by collective aspects. The importance of subjectivity in defining
value is also emphasized, along with how calculation influences  the market, even allowing it to
experience a rebirth after a period when it was nearly extinct. Among the contributions to theories
of calculative agency (Cochoy , 2008), the consolidation of theoretical categories stands out,
demonstrating the evolution of calculative mechanisms within the process of market evolution,
bringing a new theoretical gaze to rare products and/or those experiencing a market revival.
The research findings also highlight the presence of confrontations and clashes of interest
in the vinyl record market. For example, mentions regarding the high prices charged by sellers of
rare records and the willingness of consumers to pay these amounts highlight how certain groups,
such as sellers and collectors, influence the prices and availability of specific records. Furthermore,
the mention of inflated records and the lack of transparent criteria to determine values highlights
how a lack of clarity can allow for market manipulation by certain actors.
This element is rarely discussed in the literature on value calculation and constitutes a
contribution to studies in the area. Although Çalişkan and Callon (2010) emphasize that markets
delineate and construct a space for confrontation and power struggles, they do not systematize how
these disputes occur. Multiple definitions and contradictory valuations of goods, as well as agents,
oppose one another in markets until the terms of the transaction are peacefully determined by
pricing mechanisms (Çalişkan & Callon, 2010), and the present work helps systematize knowledge
regarding the mechanisms involved in this dispute.

by intrinsic characteristics of the products, but also by social and cultural processes. For instance,

Conclusion

The present study aimed to identify the calculation and valuation mechanisms employed
by consumers and sellers in the vinyl record market, exploring how these mechanisms influence
the monetary value of records. The results of this study help to understand market dynamics from
the perspective of CMS, demonstrating how the actions and interactions of participants configure
calculative agencies composed of five elements that affect price perception and the establishment
of economic values. The analyses highlight the role of calculative agency, as well as valuation and
negotiation practices, in shaping markets, revealing the complexity and dynamics of the vinyl
record market, which is configured as a socially constructed market.
It is important to emphasize that the results cannot be generalized to other contexts without
caution, given that this is a localized qualitative study with a limited sample size. Additionally, a
limitation to be noted is the use of snowball sampling. Alth ough useful for accessing the specific
audience of this market, this technique introduces potential methodological biases, as participants
tend to refer individuals within their own social networks, which may restrict the diversity of the
sample.
Furthermore, it was difficult to distinguish the practices and perceptions of consumers and
sellers due to the tight entanglement between these two groups when defining the value and price
of records. Future research could benefit from a more distinct boun dary between them, or even a
sole focus on one of these groups.
