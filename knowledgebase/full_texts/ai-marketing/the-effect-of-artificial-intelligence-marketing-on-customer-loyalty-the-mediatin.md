Abstract: The rise of artificial intelligence (AI) -driven marketing
offers small and medium enterprises (SMEs) an opportunity to
manage customer relationships more precisely, yet evidence on
how AI marketing shapes customer loyalty in Indonesian food
SMEs remains limited and inconsistent. This study examines the
effect of AI marketing on customer loyalty, both directly and
through the mediation of brand awareness and brand image,
using Kalfis Mackerel Fish -Bone Sticks as the case product. An
explanatory quantitative design was applied to 200 purposively
selected customers who had been exposed to the brand's
Instagram promotion. Data were collected through a five -point
Likert questionnaire and analyzed using Partial Least Squares
Structural Equ ation Modeling (PLS -SEM). AI marketing
positively and significantly affected brand awareness (β = 0.577),
brand image (β = 0.440), and customer loyalty (β = 0.409). Brand
awareness (β = 0.186) and brand image (β = 0.323) also
strengthened loyalty and partially mediated the effect of AI
marketing (indirect β = 0.107 and 0.142, respectively). The model
explained 54.3% of the variance in customer loyalty (Q² = 0.362).
These findings position AI marketing as an antecedent of
customer-based brand equity in small businesses and suggest
that intelligent marketing investment is more productive when
directed at building brand assets rather than pursuing short-term
transactions.
Keywords: Artificial Intelligence marketing; brand awareness;
brand image; customer loyalty; PLS-SEM; SMEs.

Introduction
Artificial intelligence has shifted from a technological discourse to an operational
instrument in contemporary marketing practice. The ability of Artificial Intelligence (AI) -based
systems to process large volumes of consumer behavior data, recognize preference patterns, and
automatically generate communication responses has made AI a tool that changes how firms
design offerings and maintain customer relationships (Davenport et al., 2020; Huang & Rust,
2021). This shift is not merely an efficiency gain but  a redefinition of marketing touchpoints:
personalization that was once manual and limited can now be delivered continuously at the
individual level (Chatterjee et al., 2023).
Indonesia occupies a strategic position in this transformation. A Southeast Asian digital
economy report recorded that the value of Indonesia's digital economy exceeded USD 82 billion
in 2023, with growth projected to strengthen further, largely driven by e-commerce and data -
based marketing (Google, Temasek, & Bain, 2023). At the same time, data from the Indonesian
Ministry of Cooperatives and SMEs (Kementerian Koperasi dan UKM, 2023) indicate that
approximately 26 million of the 64 million micro, small, an d medium enterprises (SMEs) are
connected to the digital ecosystem. This large gap shows that most SMEs have yet to make
adequate use of customer analytics capabilities, so opportunities to strengthen competitiveness
through intelligent marketing technology remain wide open.
The fundamental problem faced by SMEs is not merely limited promotional budgets but
the difficulty of converting transactional interactions into long -term relationships. The OECD
(2021) identifies low digital literacy and weak customer data processing capa city as the main
obstacles to the digital transformation of small businesses. Consequently, marketing activities
tend to be oriented toward acquiring new customers, while retention is neglected even though
retention is economically more efficient. Dwivedi et al. (2021) emphasize that AI technology has
the potential to close this gap because it enables a deeper understanding of consumer behavior
without requiring a large organizational structure.
This phenomenon finds its concrete context at PT Kalfis Chips Indonesia, the producer of
Kalfis Mackerel Fish -Bone Sticks, a functional snack made from fish bones that is entering the
market in a relatively new product category. A preliminary survey of 30 customers indicated a
still-shallow relationship: 53% of respondents had made only one purchase, 27% had made two,
and only 20% had purchased more than twice. Meanwhile, only 40% of respondents were willing
to recommend the product to others. This pattern indicates that most consumers remain at the
trial-purchase stage and that brand advocacy has not yet formed. Initial observations on e -

Diagnosis of this condition points to two customer-based brand equity constructs, namely
brand awareness and brand image. A product that is not widely recognized struggles to enter
consumers' consideration sets, whereas a product without positive associations struggles to earn
trust (Keller & Swaminathan, 2019). Both serve as cognitive and affective bridges linking
marketing activities to customers' behavioral commitment. Because AI marketing works by
increasing the relevance and frequency of message exposu re, it is reasonable to presume that its
influence on loyalty does not operate entirely directly but is channeled through the strengthening
of these two brand constructs.
The available empirical evidence has not yielded uniform conclusions. Haque et al. (2018)
found a significant effect of brand awareness on customer loyalty, whereas Tandarto and
Dharmayanti (2017) and Mathew et al. (2014) reported the opposite. Marakanon a nd
Panjakajornsak (2017) even recommended further testing of the awareness construct because its
position in the loyalty model remains unsettled. Similar inconsistencies appear in the AI
marketing literature: some studies focus on short-term outcomes such as purchase intention and
purchase decisions (Agustin et al., 2025; Badri & Huda, 2024; Louis et al., 2024), while studies that
position loyalty as the dependent variable with a dual -mediation mechanism remain rare,
particularly in the context of Indonesian food SMEs.
This gap defines the space for the contribution of this study. First, this study integrates AI
marketing into the customer-based brand equity framework by positioning it as an antecedent of
brand awareness and brand image rather than as a sole predictor of  loyalty. Second, it tests two
mediation paths simultaneously, making it possible to determine which mechanism is more
dominant in transmitting the influence of marketing technology to loyalty. Third, the chosen
context is food SMEs with a new product category and limited resources, which differ in character
from the large corporations that dominate the AI marketing literature.
Based on the foregoing, this study aims to analyze the effect of AI marketing on brand
awareness, brand image, and customer loyalty; to test the effect of brand awareness and brand
image on customer loyalty; and to examine the mediating role of these two b rand constructs in
the relationship between AI marketing and customer loyalty for Kalfis Mackerel Fish-Bone Sticks.

Literature Review
Artificial Intelligence Marketing
Artificial Intelligence marketing refers to the use of intelligent systems to collect, process,
and act on customer data so that marketing decisions can be executed adaptively and
automatically. Davenport et al. (2020) map the role of AI in marketing acros s three capability
dimensions: task automation, predictive analytics, and context -based interaction. Huang and
Rust (2021) complement this mapping with a mechanical, thinking, and feeling framework, which
positions AI not only as an efficiency tool but also as an intermediary of customer experience. In
practice, this technology materializes in personalized promotional content, product
recommendation systems, automated message responses, and real-time ad placement adjustment
(Paschen et al., 2020).
Its effectiveness is not automatic. Jöhnk et al. (2021) show that organizational readiness,
encompassing strategic alignment, data governance, and personnel competence, determines the
success of AI adoption. Chintalapati and Pandey (2022) add that limited digital competence often
constitutes a major barrier to optimizing the benefits of AI. From the consumer perspective,
Kietzmann et al. (2018) caution that the acceptance of personalization depends on perceived data
security, so that non-transparent privacy management can reduce the effectiveness of a strategy.
Empirical findings in the Indonesian context relatively support the potential of this technology:
Belinda and Nofitasari (2025) report a substantial increase in conversion rates and a decrease in
customer acquisition costs, while Pangkey et al. (2022) and Syaharuddin et al. (2023) found a
significant contribution of AI -based analytics to digital marketing performance. Theoretically,
user acceptance of this technology can be explained through the Techno logy Acceptance Model,
which positions perceived usefulness and ease of use as determinants of willingness to adopt new
technology (Davis, 1989; Verhoef et al., 2021).
Brand Awareness and Brand Image as Dimensions of Brand Equity
Brand awareness describes the strength of a brand's trace in consumers' memory,
manifested in the ability to recognize and recall the brand when the product category is cued
(Aaker, 1991; Keller, 2013). Its levels are hierarchical, ranging from unawareness, recognition, and
recall to top-of-mind position in consumers' minds. For a new brand, awareness is a prerequisite
for entering the consideration set; without it, product quality has no opportunity to be evaluated.
Bilgin (2018) shows that social media ma rketing activities increase brand visibility and thereby
strengthen awareness.
Brand image is a set of associations stored in consumers' memory about a brand,
encompassing perceived quality, uniqueness, reputation, and trust (Keller & Swaminathan, 2019;

affective. Hsieh and Li (2018) and Wu and Wang (2014) affirm that a strong brand image enhances

making.
Customer Loyalty
Customer loyalty is understood as a commitment to maintain a relationship with a brand,
manifested in repeat purchases and resistance to competitors' offers (Oliver, 1999). The concept
is multidimensional, encompassing a behavioral dimension in the form of  repeat purchases and
an attitudinal dimension in the form of preference and willingness to recommend (Griffin, 2015).
Lemon and Verhoef (2016) position loyalty as the accumulation of customer experiences
throughout the customer's journey with the brand, s o that the quality of every touchpoint,
including digital ones, contributes to its formation. Maulidiyah and Handoko (2024) add that, in
the digital era, loyalty cannot be measured by purchase frequency alone but also by customers'
willingness to become active brand advocates.
Hypothesis Development
AI marketing works by improving targeting accuracy and message relevance. When
promotional content is tailored to individual preferences, the intensity of information processing
increases and the brand's memory trace becomes stronger. This mechanism direct ly affects the
formation of brand awareness. Ramadhana and Hussein (2022) and Syavira and Sutrisna (2025)
show that well -targeted digital marketing strategies improve brand recognition among
consumers. The first hypothesis is therefore formulated.
H1: Artificial Intelligence marketing has a positive and significant effect on brand
awareness.
In addition to strengthening recognition, AI-based personalization also shapes consumers'
qualitative assessment of a brand. Communication that is relevant, responsive, and consistent is
perceived as a sign of professionalism and of the company's attention to customer needs, so that
positive brand associations are strengthened (Ameen et al., 2021; Ramadhana & Hussein, 2022).
Amanta and Ahmadi (2024) also found that the effectiveness of marketing communication is a
primary shaper of brand image strength.
H2: Artificial Intelligence marketing has a positive and significant effect on brand
image.

AI marketing is also expected to have a direct effect on loyalty. Systems capable of
anticipating customer needs and responding quickly create satisfying interaction experiences,
and repeated satisfaction is the foundation of relational commitment (Gao et al., 2026; Haleem et
al., 2022). In the SME context, the ability to maintain communication continuity without adding
to the human resource burden makes this technology relevant to retention efforts.
H3: Artificial Intelligence marketing has a positive and significant effect on customer
loyalty.
Consumers who recognize a brand well require less cognitive effort when making
decisions, and this familiarity serves as a credibility cue that lowers perceived risk. Haque et al.
(2018) and Alvisina et al. (2025) demonstrated the contribution of brand awa reness to loyalty,
although Tandarto and Dharmayanti (2017) reported a nonsignificant result. This inconsistency
makes retesting scientifically relevant.
H4: Brand awareness has a positive and significant effect on customer loyalty.
A positive brand image strengthens consumers' confidence in product quality while
providing symbolic value from owning and using the product. Ernawati and Prihandono (2017)
and Amanta and Ahmadi (2024) found a significant effect of brand image on loyalty, especially
through the path of consumer trust.
H5: Brand image has a positive and significant effect on customer loyalty.
The theoretical arguments above imply that the effect of AI marketing on loyalty is not
entirely direct. Marketing technology generates exposure and relevance, both of which build
brand assets, and it is these brand assets that sustain long -term commitment. Brand awareness
and brand image are therefore positioned as intervening variables that transmit the effect of AI
marketing on customer loyalty.
H6: Brand awareness mediates the effect of Artificial Intelligence marketing on
customer loyalty.
H7: Brand image mediates the effect of Artificial Intelligence marketing on customer
loyalty.

Research Methods
Research Design and Sample
This study employed a quantitative approach with an explanatory research design, that is,
a design directed at explaining causal relationships among variables through hypothesis testing
on numerical data (Sugiyono, 2017). The object of the study is Kalfis Mackerel Fish-Bone Sticks
produced by PT Kalfis Chips Indonesia. The population comprises customers who have
purchased the product and have also been exposed to the brand's digital promotion through the
Kalfis Chips Instagram account. Because the number of  population members changes
dynamically and cannot be identified precisely, the population was treated as infinite.
Respondents were selected using purposive sampling, that is, selection based on the fit
between respondents' characteristics and the research objectives (Sekaran & Bougie, 2016). Three
criteria were set: having purchased Kalfis Mackerel Fish -Bone Sticks, having seen the product's
promotion on the Kalfis Chips Instagram account, and being at least 17 years old. The sample size
was set at 200 respondents following the rule of ten times the number of indicators in the model,
with 20 indicators used across the four research constructs (Memon et al., 2020). This size satisfies
the data adequacy requirement for variance-based structural model estimation.
Variable Measurement
All constructs were measured reflectively using a five -point Likert scale ranging from 1
(strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). AI marketing was operationalized through five
indicators reflecting content personalization, recommendation relevance, system responsiveness,
ease of obtaining information, and quality of the digital marketing experience, developed from

Data Analysis Technique
Data were analyzed using Partial Least Squares-based Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-
SEM) with SmartPLS software. This method was chosen because it can test a model with several
structural relationships simultaneously, including indirect effects, without  requiring strict
normality assumptions (Hair et al., 2022). The analysis was conducted in two stages. The

discriminant validity through cross -loadings, and construct reliability through composite
reliability with a threshold of 0.70. The structural model evaluation assessed the coefficient of

Prior to the main analysis, the instrument was tested using Pearson product -moment

Discussion
The Effect of AI Marketing on Brand Awareness
The path coefficient of AI marketing on brand awareness is the largest in the model (0.577),
indicating that strengthening brand recognition is the most responsive outcome of applying
artificial intelligence -based marketing. This finding is theoretically l ogical because
personalization and targeting increase both the frequency and the quality of message exposure,
and repeated exposure to relevant messages is a classic mechanism for forming brand memory
traces (Aaker, 1991; Keller, 2013). For a relatively ne w brand such as Kalfis, this condition is
strategically meaningful because the main problem of a new brand is not consumer rejection but
unfamiliarity. The result supports the findings of Ramadhana and Hussein (2022) and Syavira
and Sutrisna (2025) on the contribution of well -targeted digital marketing to increasing brand
awareness, and it reinforces Bilgin's (2018) argument regarding the role of digital visibility in
building brand equity.
The Effect of AI Marketing on Brand Image
The effect of AI marketing on brand image is also significant, although of a smaller
magnitude (0.440) and with more limited explanatory power in the model. This difference in
magnitude is theoretically informative. Brand awareness can be built primarily t hrough
exposure, whereas brand image is an evaluative construct formed from the accumulation of
product experience, taste quality, packaging, price, and reputation, which lie beyond the control
of communication channels. Marketing technology can thus impro ve how a brand is presented
but cannot replace product substance. This interpretation is in line with Amanta and Ahmadi
(2024), who position communication effectiveness as one, but not the only, shaper of brand image,

and with the warning of Kietzmann et al. (2018) that personalization that is not managed ethically
may erode consumers' positive perceptions.
The Direct Effect of AI Marketing on Customer Loyalty
AI marketing was found to have a direct effect on customer loyalty with a coefficient of
0.409, which is the largest direct effect on the dependent variable. The mechanism explaining this
finding is the improved quality of the interaction experience, namel y fast responses, easily
obtained information, and interest-matched recommendations, so that customers feel understood
and relational attachment grows (Ameen et al., 2021; Huang & Rust, 2021). This result is
consistent with Gao et al. (2026), who found that AI-based services increase customer satisfaction
and loyalty in digital service contexts. For SMEs, the implication is clear: communication
automation is not merely a labor-saving measure but an instrument of customer retention.
The Effect of Brand Awareness and Brand Image on Customer Loyalty
Brand awareness has a positive and significant effect on customer loyalty, so this finding
corroborates Haque et al. (2018) and Alvisina et al. (2025) while not supporting Tandarto and
Dharmayanti (2017) or Mathew et al. (2014). The divergence can be expla ined by the product
category context. In established, brand-saturated categories, awareness is generic, and therefore
its discriminating power with respect to loyalty weakens. By contrast, in a category that is still
new, such as fish-bone-based functional snacks, brand recognition serves as a credibility cue that
directly reduces consumer doubt. Accordingly, the theoretical contribution of this study is to
show that the relevance of brand awareness to loyalty is contingent on the maturity of the product
category.
Comparing the coefficients of the two mediators yields a more substantive finding. Brand
image, with a coefficient of 0.323, affects loyalty almost twice as strongly as brand awareness,
with a coefficient of 0.186. This confirms that being known is not eno ugh to retain customers.
Recognition merely brings a brand into the consideration set, whereas the decision to stay is
determined by the trust and positive associations embedded in brand image (Ernawati &
Prihandono, 2017; Hsieh & Li, 2018). The managerial  consequence is that a marketing budget
spent on expanding reach without managing quality and reputation will yield suboptimal
returns.

The Mediating Role of Brand Awareness and Brand Image
Both mediation paths proved significant, with the indirect effect through brand image
(0.142) larger than that through brand awareness (0.107). The total effect of AI marketing on
customer loyalty therefore reaches 0.658, of which 62.2% is the direct effec t and 37.8% is
channeled through the strengthening of brand assets. Because the direct effect remains
significant, the pattern formed is complementary partial mediation rather than full mediation.
This finding has two theoretical implications. First, AI marketing deserves to be positioned
as an antecedent of customer-based brand equity rather than merely as a trigger of transactional
responses, as is widely assumed in the literature focusing on purc hase intention and purchase
decisions (Agustin et al., 2025; Badri & Huda, 2024). Second, partial mediation shows that
intelligent marketing technology works through two complementary paths, namely the direct
experience path and the brand asset-building path, so that a model testing only direct effects risks
underestimating the total contribution of the technology.
Methodological Note on the Discrepancy Between the Preliminary Survey and the Main Sample
It should be noted that there is a discrepancy between the initial indication of low loyalty
in the preliminary survey of 30 customers and the very high loyalty score of 4.392 in the main
sample. This discrepancy can be explained by differences in the sampling frame. The preliminary
survey did not restrict respondents to customers who had been exposed to the brand's digital
promotion, whereas the main sample required such exposure. In other words, the main sample
systematically consisted of customers who had interacted with the brand's digital channels, and
it is this group that showed higher loyalty. This interpretation is consistent with the direction of
the model's findings but also carries a methodological consequence in the form of potential
selection bias, which limits the generalizability of the results and should be taken into account
when interpreting the findings of this study.
Research Implications
Theoretically, this study extends the customer -based brand equity framework by
positioning marketing technology capability as an antecedent, and it provides evidence from the
Indonesian food SME context, which has been underrepresented in the AI marketing literature.
The study also offers a contextual explanation for the inconsistent findings on the relationship
between brand awareness and loyalty through the argument of product category maturity.
Practically, the results suggest three priorities for PT Kalfis Chips Indonesia and similar
SMEs. First, because brand image makes the strongest contribution to loyalty, AI -based
marketing efforts should be directed at strengthening the narrative of produc t quality,
uniqueness, and credibility rather than merely expanding reach. Second, because the model's

Conclusion
This study concludes that Artificial Intelligence marketing has a positive and significant
effect on brand awareness, brand image, and customer loyalty for Kalfis Mackerel Fish -Bone
Sticks. Brand awareness and brand image also have positive and significant  effects on customer
loyalty and were shown to partially mediate the relationship between AI marketing and customer
loyalty. Accordingly, all seven research hypotheses are supported by the data. The research
model explains 54.3% of the variance in customer loyalty with strong predictive relevance.
The main contribution of this study lies in positioning AI marketing as an antecedent of
customer-based brand equity at the small-business scale, accompanied by evidence that the brand
image path is more dominant than the brand awareness path in transmitti ng the influence of
marketing technology to loyalty. The implication is that the use of artificial intelligence in
marketing will be more productive when oriented toward building long-term brand assets rather
than pursuing short-term transactions.
Limitations
This study has several limitations. The cross -sectional design limits the ability to draw
definitive causal conclusions and does not capture changes in loyalty over time. The use of
purposive sampling that restricted respondents to active Instagram custome rs potentially
introduces selection bias, so the findings do not yet represent customers on other channels such
as e-commerce and conventional outlets. In addition, the measurement of AI marketing relied
entirely on respondents' perceptions without objecti ve data on the level of technology
implementation, so respondents' assessments may reflect the quality of promotional content in
general.
