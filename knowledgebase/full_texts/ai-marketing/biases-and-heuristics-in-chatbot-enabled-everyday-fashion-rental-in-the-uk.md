Title: Biases and Heuristics in Chatbot Enabled Everyday Fashion Rental in the UK
Abstract
Purpose: This research presents a novel theoretical framework by examining chatbots in an
under-explored context of everyday clothing rental. We apply an interdisciplinary lens,
behavioural economics theory, nudge theory and choice architecture, to a marketing
exploration, to simplify consumers’ decision-making.
Design/Methodology/Approach: Three focus groups and 28 interviews were conducted with
UK consumers across two qualitative phases.
Findings: As shown in Figure 2, identifying rental barriers and drivers led to the development
of four digital, four green nudges, boosts, and sludges. This choice architecture is specifically
designed to steer consumer behaviour toward everyday renting.
Practical implications: This study provides managerial implications for circular and non-profit
companies by leveraging green and digital nudges to enhance choice architecture and consumer
footfall. Policy implications suggest reforms for UK consumer laws, specifically proposing a
'consumer warrant' to enforce sustainability transparency.
Originality/Value: This paper offers novel insights into using nudge theory to shape consumer
heuristics and decisions regarding everyday clothing rentals via chatbots. It introduces specific
digital and green nudges designed to transform the consumer choice environment.
Keywords: Renting, Nudge Theory, Choice Architecture, Chatbot, Heuristics

1. Introduction
Despite growing social awareness of fast-fashion’s environmental and ethical tolls, consumers
still favour it due to the high cost and limited style of sustainable alternatives (Rich et al.,
2025). Regardless of numerous green alternatives (Theotokis and Manganari, 2015), consumer
choices are frequently dictated by marketing tactics and habits (Ritch and Siddiqui, 2023).
Choice is underestimated by consumers during their everyday actions; it wields much influence
on their decision-making (Falcone and Fiorentino, 2025). Artificial intelligence (AI)
innovations present marketers with the possibility to aid consumers’ choices (Mele et al., 2021;
Reeck et al., 2023). Chatbots hold significance in promoting positive changes in behaviour,
such as finding sustainable clothing options (Manzo et al., 2025). Studies discuss how chatbots
positively influence pro-environmental behaviour (Majid et al., 2024), primarily focussing
upon practical and technical solutions (Peniche-Avilés et al., 2016). To advance research on
chatbots' influence on sustainable fashion, this paper explores how chatbots nudge consumers
toward everyday clothing renting.
Studies lack a strong theoretical basis to replicate conclusions on chatbots into other
sustainability contexts (Majid et al., 2024), most exploration is within tourism (Majid et al.,
2024), and sustainable logistics (Gruchmann et al., 2025). The business and marketing
discipline requires exploration of how chatbots influence consumers' sustainable behaviour
(Theotokis and Manganari, 2025; Mele et al., 2021). Plus, understanding fashion consumption
via behavioural economics, which is neglected within literature (Ritch et al., 2025). Our study
examines how using chatbots and broader paradigms of behavioural science (nudging, sludges,
boosts, and heuristics) simplify UK consumers’ decision-making towards everyday rental.
The theoretical framing entails nudge theory (NT) and choice architecture (CA); nudging
comprehends how consumers think and make decisions, resulting in supporting consumer

2.1 Heuristics
Hedonistic consumption is implicated in fashion (Miller, 2013). Technology and digital
marketing is linked to hedonistic design, and the exploitation of behavioural biases, and
utilising system 1 and 2 thinking (Kool and Agrawal, 2016). System 1 (fast) and 2 (slow)
thinking, divides decision-making into two pathways. Fast thinking is intuitive and automatic,
and is driven by emotion, heuristics and cognitive biases. Slow thinking is deliberate, relying
on conscious reasoning (Kahneman, 2011). Studies show nudging and behavioural science
effectively align fashion choices with personal beliefs through better communication (Lee et
al., 2020). Moreover,  how visual or verbal prompts influence choice (Roozen et al., 2021),
these can trigger Systems 1 and 2 differently (Prashar and Kaushal, 2025). Conversely,
hedonistic framing drives slow fashion consumption (Aprianingsih, 2023).
While behavioural economics is expansive, this exploratory paper focuses only on heuristics
and nudges to provide a foundation for rental CA . Unlike ‘fast and frugal’ or ‘simple rule’
approaches that prioritise cognitive shortcuts (Gigerenzer and Gaissmaier, 2011; Marewski,

further away, but clarity is determined by visibility, so clarity is not always consistent with
distance (Tversky and Kahneman, 1974).
Cognitive bias: Relates to where judgments are made intuitively and is guided by perception.
People can have limited willpower; are tempted and can be short-sighted (Jolls, Sunstein and
Thaler, 1998).

Nudge theory (NT) analyses consumer decision-making to support  choices and minimise
negative influences (Mele et al., 2021). It comprises human traits that affect individual
decisions and market outcomes (Mele et al., 2021), bounded rationality outcomes (1957), and
a lack of self-control and social preferences (Mele et al., 2021). Majid et al. (2024) demonstrate
the applicability of using NT as a theoretical grounding to explore chatbot based nudging. NT
is an effective approach towards fostering sustainable consumption behaviours (Wensing et al.,
2020). However, research is limited when examining NT in a sustainable context (Falcone and
Fiorentino, 2025).
Green nudges (GN) are non-financial environmental changes that influence consumer
behaviour without altering economic incentives (Thaler and Sunstein, 2008). Emerging
business and marketing studies have applied NT to a fashion context to understand consumer
behaviour (Roozen et al., 2021; Falcone and Fiorentino, 2025). Nudging is a valuable tool to
incentivise customers’ environmentally friendly behaviour (Gruchmann et al., 2025).
Sustainable contexts to date refer to tourism (Majid et al., 2024), investment (Pilaj, 2017), and
logistics (Gruchmann et al., 2025). Sustainable fashion consumption remains unexplored.
 Digital nudges (DN) are used due to exploring chatbots. DN positively influences online
consumer choice, defined as “the use of user interface design elements to guide people’s
behaviour in the digital environment” (Weinmann et al., 2016: 433). DN consumers' adoption
of climate-friendly (Bartmann, 2022) and eco-friendly choices (Fechner and Herder, 2021).

of the user (Nallur et al., 2024). While these technologies enable enhanced personalisation and

2.3 Context – Driving Everyday Fashion Rental as a Sustainable Behaviour via Chatbots
A chatbot originating from the terms ‘conversation’ and ‘robot’ (Rese et al., 2020) is an AI
powered entity designed to simulate the conversation between humans. It uses natural language
processing and natural language understanding techniques to generate human-like
conversations (Ciechanowski et al., 2019). Chatbots provide automated, human-like
information retrieval and problem-solving (Rese et al., 2020).
Chatbots enable real-time technological application (Akram et al., 2022), impacting visual-
heavy fields like fashion and user experience (Qi et al., 2025). Text-based queries enable more
comprehensive, personalised searches for rental clothing (Mishra et al., 2025). Combining text
queries with image processing creates an engaging, visually-driven shopping experience (Qi et
al., 2025).
Chatbots streamline clothes rentals to increase efficiency and reduce costs. Utility can be
enhanced in four ways, understanding consumers’ needs and requirements, an improved
inventory search based on visual and text descriptions, personalised recommendations based
on previous interaction, and current market trends, and real-time visualisation, allowing a
consumer to virtually try on a garment. Speech-to-text and text-to-speech features facilitate
human-like dialogue and minimise user cognitive load (Inupakutika et al., 2021; Ciechanowski
et al., 2019).
Rental is gaining in UK popularity with expectations for growth (Noe and Hyan, 2024).
Fashion rental involves a temporary exchange of goods for a fee without transferring ownership
(Lang and Armstrong, 2018; Lang et al., 2019; Mukendi and Henninger, 2020; Jain et al., 2023;

2.4 Conceptual model
This exploratory study contributes to the growing body of everyday rental fashion literature
(Pantano and Stylos, 2020; Coscieme et al., 2022; Song and Wu, 2024) and limited research
examining the circular economy and responsible consumption (Kutaula et al., 2022; Talukder
et al., 2024). As a circular business model, rental reduces waste by repurposing products to
prolong their lifespan (Talukder et al., 2024). We consider how chatbots, via nudges, heuristics,
boosts and sludges, and CA can influence consumers’ rental choices. This provides insight into
how chatbots influence sustainable consumption, moving beyond typical research on technical
and practical solutions (Peniche-Avilés et al., 2016).
We apply behavioural science and marketing lenses to explore rental behaviors and CA
examining GN, DN, sludges, and boosts. We contribute to limited understanding of nudge
theory (NT) in a sustainable fashion context (Falcone and Fiorentino, 2025). Applying NT and
GN to chatbot framing for sustainable fashion addresses a significant gap in marketing
literature. We respond to Berger et al.’s (2022) call for further studies to examine DN and
environmental social behaviour, a term interchangeably used with sustainable behaviour
(Paswan et al., 2017). Plus, addressing the need to replicate conclusions on chatbots into other
sustainability contexts (Majid et al., 2024; Gruchmann et al., 2025).

4.1.1 Chatbot encouraging decision-making to rent
The desire for additional support from the chatbot was expressed to save the consumer time,
as shared by Interviewee-15:
[If] you're going on holiday, [AI suggests] linen shorts, but it is difficult to choose the
specific shorts that could make a good combination for you. But this additional chatbot
can, be a lot of help, [and] save a lot of time (Interviewee-15)
Interviewee-15 discusses prompting questions for an occasion which enhances online shopping
experience. Decision-making takes less effort, as the availability heuristic presents choices
based on the preferable criteria applied within the search (Tversky and Kahneman, 1973).
Participant-11 similarly shares chatbots can simplify shopping and this algorithmic inclusion
was viewed positively, as making renting an enjoyable experience:
My recent experiences started through a chatbot, to the stage where I'm ready to check
out the basket before I have to even think about it. It's basically a tick box nearly the
whole way along: knowing the most recent size you bought from us; they have the
analytics. Advanced technology make[s] the process much more enjoyable.
(Participant-11)
Participant-11 demonstrates system-1 thinking, where the shopping experience is simplified
through AI, enabling more space for hedonistic emotions that enabled them to ‘enjoy’ the
experience (Mukendi and Henninger, 2020; Ritch and Siddiqui, 2023; Ritch et al., 2025). The
algorithmic capturing of the size acts as a prompt (Roozen et al., 2021), and frames CA to
simplify decision-making. By streamlining searches based on style, material, and size, this tool
makes sustainable fashion more accessible and enjoyable through easier experimentation

5.2 Managerial implications
Firstly, providing rental fashion companies with a chatbot with DN and GN (which includes
boosts and sludges, see Figure 2) to enhance their sustainable business models to achieve
greater circularity (Fiorentino et al., 2020). AI-integrated chatbots should use conversational
prompts to overcome trust barriers by adapting to user biases and heuristics (Nallur et al.,
2024). GN, including sludges and boosts, can be integrated into a chatbot’s DNs to drive rental
conversions and overcome sales barriers (Purnomo, 2023). Sample DNs and GNs by chatbots
are: "Buying this outfit costs £180 for a single wear, while renting is just £22 and includes
cleaning and returns. Compare two similar rental options?", “Try bold looks without
commitment. Renting offers style experimentation with a personal stylist feel.", “Save 2,700
liters of water and 3 kg of CO₂ by renting instead of buying. Figures verified by WRAP UK.”,
and “Eco-friendly, hospital-grade cleaning and expert inspection on every item. See our
certification? ”.
By leveraging availability heuristics, these chatbot nudges share knowledge proactively instead
of relying on consumer information-seeking (Tversky and Kahneman, 1973). Brands must
mitigate chatbot ethical risks and environmental costs through transparency and 'human-in-the-
loop' oversight (Mills et al., 2023a). Such nudges leverage CA to promote everyday clothing
rental behaviour (Hertwig and Grüne-Yanoff, 2017).

5.3 Policy implications
We provide chatbot AI-based solutions to tackle sustainability issues (Majid et al., 2024), such
as encouraging responsible consumption via rental (Kutaula et al., 2022; Talukder et al., 2024).
WRAP could adopt these nudges as CA tools (Mele et al., 2021; Reeck et al., 2023), using
chatbots to promote sustainable fashion among stakeholders.
Rapidly evolving AI carries significant implications for the Digital Markets, Competition and
Consumer Act (GOV UK, 2025). The Act prohibits unfair practices, including misleading or
deceptive digital content. The Act should regulate AI in tandem with the Green Claims Code
to ensure traders do not provide misleading environmental messages (Green Claims GOV UK,
2025). Rental chatbots must ensure that green nudges (boosts and sludges) provide transparent
environmental messaging without misleading consumers.
5.4 Future research recommendations
Research should quantify the effectiveness of GN and DN in UK clothing rental chatbots .
Future studies may find new nudges that use heuristics to guide consumer decisions. The
sustainable fashion context aligns with current management research priorities. The sustainable
fashion context addresses current management priorities (Falcone & Fiorentino, 2025) and the
essential connection between circularity and responsible consumption (Kutaula et al., 2022;
Talukder et al., 2024).
Rental fashion chatbots help consumers make choices aligned with their sustainability
preferences. Low digital literacy and lack of human connection can diminish personalisation
for some consumers, therefore, AI chatbots require 'human-in-the-loop' oversight (Mills, Costa,
and Sunstein, 2023b). The shifting AI landscape warrants further research into consumer
decision-making.
