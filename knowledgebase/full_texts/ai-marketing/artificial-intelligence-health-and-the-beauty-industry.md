Abstract
This systematic review explores the potential impacts of
artificial intelligence on the beauty industry, examining a
wide range of academic literature, industry reports, and
theses to provide a comprehensive understanding of the
subject. The review delves into key areas where AI has
shown transformative potential, including personalized
skincare and beauty product recommendations, virtual try -
on technologies, predictive analytics in beauty trends, and
AI-driven innovations in product formulation and marketing
strategies. The study synthesizes findings from peer -
reviewed research and professional analyses, highlighting
how AI technologies are reshaping consumer experiences,
optimizing supply chains, and enabling brands to engage
with customers more effectively. Additionally, it identifies
challenges such as data privacy concerns, ethical
implications, and the digital divide that may influence the
adoption and efficacy of AI in this sector. The review
concludes by discussing the implications of these
developments for industry stakeholders, public health, and
researchers, emphasizing the need for further
interdisciplinary studies to address existing gaps and
maximize the benefits of AI in enhancing both the consumer
and industrial aspects of the beauty industry.
Keywords: Artificial Intelligence, Beauty Industry, Personalized Skincare, Virtual Try-on, Ethical Implications
Introduction
Artificial intelligence has redefined traditional practices and created new paradigms of operation. Within the beauty industry,
the integration of AI has ushered in innovations that extend from product development and marketing to consumer experience
and ethical considerations. As a sector heavily influenced by evolving trends and consumer preferences, the beauty industry
finds itself at the forefront of technological adoption. AI technologies, such as machine learning algorithms, virtual and
augmented reality tools, and big data analytics, are increasingly shaping how products are created, marketed, and consumed.
These advancements have redefined customer interactions, enabling unprecedented levels of personalization and efficiency.
The relationship between AI and the beauty industry is defined by several factors that contribute to its transformative poten tial.
AI-powered tools allow for enhanced product customization, helping brands cater to diverse consumer needs. Virtual try -on
technologies, for instance, enable customers to experiment with products in a digital environment, thus bridging the gap
between physical and online retail. Moreover, predictive analytics harnessed through AI facilitates the identification of
emerging trends and the optimization of supply chains, ensuring that businesses remain competitive in a rapidly evolving
market. However, the integration of AI also raises significant concerns, such as data privacy, algorithmic bias, and ethical
dilemmas, particularly regarding the reinforcement of unrealistic beauty standards. The inconsistent implementation of ethica l
guidelines across AI systems creates uncertainty that undermines user trust, reflecting broader institutional challenges wher e
the absence of systematic enforcement mechanisms can compromise the effectiveness of regulatory frameworks (Moein et al.,
2023) [23].
As AI technologies become increasingly pervasive in industries like beauty, researchers like Arsalani et al . (2022)  [2] have
emphasized the critical importance of developing comprehensive digital literacy programs, underscoring the collaborative role
of educators, parents, and policymakers in creating adaptive strategies. These strategies aim to equip users with critical
thinking skills to navigate complex digital environments responsibly, ensuring that consumers can engage with AI -driven tools

Existing research highlights the multifaceted applications of
AI in the beauty industry, including personalized skincare,
virtual makeup trials, and automated customer service
solutions. Studies have shown that these technologies not
only enhance user experience but also drive consumer
engagement and loyalty. User engagement has become
increasingly sophisticated with advanced digital design
techniques. Bagheri et al. (2022)  [3] offer empirical insights
into gamification mechanics, demonstrating how carefully
designed interactive elements can significantly modulate
user participation in digital beauty experiences.
Furthermore, AI has proven instrumental in addressing
global challenges, such as climate change and sustainability,
by enabling the development of eco -friendly products and
packaging. Despite these advancements, gaps remain in
understanding how AI can be harnessed to its full potential
while mitigating associated risks. The integration of AI in

efficiency and innovation, it simultaneously introduces
multifaceted risks that require comprehensive risk
assessment frameworks. For instance, biases inherent in AI
algorithms and data collection processes can perpetuate
exclusionary practices, undermining the progress made
toward inclusivity and representation. Technological
accessibility remains a fundamental challenge in AI -driven
industries. Sakhaei et al . (2024)  [29] offer compelling
research on bridging digital divides, revealing how targeted
educational interventions can enhance technological

underscoring the importance of creating inclusive

This systematic review aims to provide a comprehensive
examination of the impact of AI on the beauty industry,
focusing on its applications, benefits, and challenges. By
synthesizing findings from peer -reviewed articles, industry
reports, and academic theses, this study seeks to address the
following questions: How has AI transformed the
operational and consumer -facing aspects of the beauty
industry? What ethical considerations and societal
implications accompany the integration of AI technologies
in this sector? And what strategies can be employed to
optimize the benefits of AI while addressing its limitations?

Methodology
This study employed a systematic review methodology to
comprehensively examine the impact of AI on the beauty
industry. A systematic review is a rigorous and structured
approach to synthesizing research evidence on a specific
topic, aimed at providing a comprehensive and unbiased
summary of existing knowledge (Aromataris & Pearson,
2014) [1]. Unlike traditional narrative reviews, systematic
reviews follow a predefined methodology that ensures
transparency, reproducibility, and minimization of bias. This
process typically involves clearly defining a research
question, formulating inclusion and exclusion criteria, and
systematically searching multiple sources, such as academic
databases and gray literature, for relevant studies. The
retrieved studies are then screened, assessed for quality, and
analyzed using a standardized framework to extract and
synthesize data (Shahghasemi, Heisey & Mirani, 2011)  [31].
Systematic reviews are particularly valuable in areas where
evidence is fragmented or diverse, as they allow for the
identification of trends, gaps, and consensus in the literature.
They often employ tools such as the PRISMA (Preferred
Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta -
Analyses) guidelines to ensure methodological rigor
(Wright, Brand, Dunn & Spindler, 2007)  [40]. Moreover,
systematic reviews can incorporate qualitative, quantitative,
or mixed -method studies, depending on the research
objectives, and may include meta -analyses to statistically
aggregate findings when appropriate. By presenting a
holistic and critically appraised overview, systematic
reviews serve as essential resources for informing decision -
making, policy development, and future research directions
across various fields, including medicine, social sciences,
and emerging technologies like artificial intelligence.
The review aimed to synthesize findings from diverse
sources, including peer -reviewed journal articles, industry
reports, academic theses, and other scholarly materials, to
identify key trends, applications, benefits, and challenges
associated with AI integration in the sector.

Literature Search Strategy
A thorough search of academic databases, including
PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, and Google Scholar, was
conducted to identify relevant literature. The search also
extended to industry -specific databases, such as
ResearchGate, to capture gray literature and reports by
leading beauty companies and consultancy firms. Keywords

Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria
Studies were selected based on predefined inclusion and
exclusion criteria to ensure relevance and quality. Inclusion
criteria encompassed:
▪ Peer-reviewed articles, industry reports, and academic
theses that explicitly addressed AI applications in the
beauty industry.
▪ Studies focusing on consumer behavior, marketing
strategies, operational efficiency, or ethical
considerations linked to AI.
▪ Research conducted between 2015 and 2024 to capture
contemporary developments.

Exclusion criteria included:
▪ Articles not directly related to AI or its applications in
the beauty industry.
▪ Publications in languages other than English.
▪ Studies lacking empirical data or a clear methodological
framework.

Data Extraction and Synthesis
A standardized data extraction template was developed to
capture key details from each selected study. Information
was extracted on the following variables:
▪ Study authors, publication year, and source.
▪ Research objectives, methodologies, and study design.
▪ AI applications and technologies discussed.
▪ Key findings and conclusions, including benefits,
challenges, and ethical implications.

The extracted data were organized thematically to identify
patterns and recurring themes. A narrative synthesis
approach was employed to interpret the findings, enabling a
holistic understanding of how AI is transforming the beauty
industry. The synthesis also highlighted knowledge gaps and
areas requiring further exploration.

Quality Assessment
The quality of included studies was assessed using
appropriate tools based on their design. For quantitative
studies, criteria such as sample size, statistical methods, and
validity of results were evaluated. Qualitative studies were
appraised for methodological rigor, clarity in reporting, and
depth of analysis. Industry reports and gray literature were
evaluated for credibility, relevance, and alignment with
peer-reviewed findings.

Findings
Ricardo Felicio Souza, Peter Wanke, and Henrique Correa
(2020) [35] analyzed the effectiveness of fuzzy inference
system-based forecasting tools in the beauty industry,
specifically using demand data for 27 nail polish products
from a multinational company. The study compared the
performance of these tools with the company's previous
informal, intuition -based qualitative forecasting methods,
evaluating accuracy using the mean absolute percentage
error (MAPE). Their findings indicated that fuzzy inference
systems outperformed qualitative methods in scenarios with
certain demand time -series characteristics, such as low
kurtosis, negatively skewed distributions, and longer
forecasting horizons. These characteristics are typical of
products with ramping sales in early life -cycle stages. Fuzzy
inference models proved particularly effective in handling
time-series with no clear central tendency or highly
dispersed distributions, where traditional methods might
introduce bias or larger errors. The study highlighted the
value of fuzzy inference systems in reducing forecast errors,
especially for longer -term predictions. It also suggested that
future research should focus on hybrid approaches,
combining fuzzy inference systems with qualitative insights
to improve short -term forecasting accuracy. This is
especially relevant for less dispersed, slower -moving
products where purely qualitative methods tend to perform
well.
Olga Vrublevskaia (2021)  [38] explored the effectiveness and
universality of AI in modern marketing, with a focus on the
media and cosmetics industries. The research aimed to
address skepticism about AI’s role in marketing and to
challenge consumer stereotypes about AI's effectiveness.
The theoretical section provided a framework for
understanding AI types —reactive machines, limited
memory, theory of mind, and self -aware AI —and specific
AI technologies. It also examined the intersection of AI and
marketing using the marketing mix (4 Ps: product, price,
place, promotion) and customer -focused 4 As (acceptability,
affordability, accessibility, awareness), illustrating AI’s
influence on both frameworks. The empirical section
included qualitative analysis based on two surveys and case
studies of Netflix and Sephora, representing the
entertainment and cosmetics industries, respectively. The
study examined how these companies leveraged AI in their
marketing strategies and analyzed its impact on both
company performance and customer perspectives regarding
the marketing mix. The findings demonstrated that AI
implementation in marketing enhanced efficiency, improved
customer engagement, and proved effective across
industries.
The study concluded that AI technologies significantly
benefit marketing efforts, demonstrating universality across
different sectors. However, Vrublevskaia suggested that
further research is needed to explore AI’s impact using
expanded marketing frameworks, such as 7 Ps or 9 Ps, and
to study AI applications in additional industries as AI
technologies continue to evolve.

Martua Parningotan Sihaloho, Kurniawati Kurniawati, and
Yolanda Masnita (2023)  [32] investigated the antecedents and
consequences of using AI Color Cosmetics in the consumer
goods industry, particularly in the cosmetics segment. The
study aimed to address challenges posed by declining sales
growth due to the COVID -19 pandemic by exploring how
advanced AI technologies can drive consumer engagement
and purchasing behavior.
Using an online survey of 180 respondents, the researchers
conducted Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA) and
Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) to analyze the data.
Key findings indicated that price sensitivity, social media
addiction, and perceived usefulness were critical factors
influencing the utilization of AI Color Cosmetics
applications. These elements encouraged users to engage
with the technology, which subsequently increased the
likelihood of actual purchases. The study provided
actionable insights for the cosmetics industry's marketing
and business development divisions. Managers were advised
to prioritize strategies that enhance the perceived usefulness
of AI Color Cosmetics while addressing price sensitivity to
better align with consumer expectations.
Yixuan Ding (2023)  [5] critically examined the influence of
AI on beauty standards and the biases embedded  within AI-
generated visualizations, with a focus on body image and its
historical evolution in the fashion industry. The thesis
explored how societal norms, reflected in fashion media
such as  Vogue magazine covers, have shaped ideals of
beauty over time, often promoting unrealistic standards that
impact self -esteem and societal perceptions. The study
employed generative AI tools like DALL -E2 and
Midjourney to investigate the biases inherent in their
outputs. Using an iterative experimental approach, Ding
visualized and analyzed patterns of bias in these tools,
illustrating their reinforcement of narrow beauty ideals. A
posthumanist design framework was proposed to foster
inclusivity in fashion visuals, emphasizing the need to
reflect diverse body types and aesthetic preferences.
Additionally, the research traced historical beauty standards,
from the Venus of Willendorf’s representation of fertility in
prehistoric art to the symmetrical and mathematical ideals of
ancient Greek and Renaissance artworks. The findings
highlighted the ongoing cultural and technological
influences on body image aesthetics, advocating for
conscious design practices to counteract biases in AI
systems. The thesis concluded by discussing strategies to
mitigate AI -driven biases in the fashion industry, aligning
AI outputs with more inclusive and diverse representations
of beauty. However, these efforts unfold within a broader
technological landscape shaped by complex power
dynamics. As Sarfi et al. (2021) [30] argue, corporate entities
strategically influence knowledge production, subtly
shaping intellectual narratives in emerging fields like AI -
driven beauty technologies. This influence underscores the
need for critical scrutiny of how corporate agendas may
steer research priorities and embed biases into AI systems,
calling for greater transparency and diverse perspectives to
ensure ethical and equitable technological development.
Julie Pognonec and Tiphaine Bornard (2023)  [27] examined
the influence of AI and product recommendations on
consumer purchase intentions in the cosmetics industry. The
study focused on AI -powered digital services for product
recommendations, analyzing two types of skin analysis
methods (selfie-based or questionnaire-based) and two types
of recommended products (pre -existing or personalized).
Using four scenarios, the researchers evaluated 11
constructs, including Rapidity, Enjoyment, Trust,
Innovativeness, Perceived Ease of Use, Perceived
Usefulness, Behavioral Intention, and Attitude Toward the
Brand, among others. Their findings revealed that Rapidity
and Enjoyment positively impacted Perceived Ease of Use;
however, Perceived Ease of Use did not significantly
influence Behavioral Intention or Purchase Intention. In
contrast, Innovativeness and Trust were shown to positively
affect Perceived Usefulness, which in turn positively
influenced both Behavioral Intention and Purchase
Intention. Behavioral Intention itself had a direct positive
effect on Purchase Intention. Interestingly, Technology
Adoption Propensity and Involvement in Product Category
did not significantly impact Behavioral Intention or
Purchase Intention, respectively, though Attitude Toward
the Brand was found to positively influence Purchase
Intention.

Anastasia Georgievskaya and colleagues (2023)  [7] explored
how AI systems in the cosmetic skincare industry can
perpetuate human biases, particularly in applications of
beauty technology. The study emphasized the growing
integration of AI and machine learning across various stages
of the value chain in the skincare market, such as optimizing
supply chains, enhancing product development, and
personalizing customer experiences. While these
technologies offer significant advancements, the researchers
underscored critical challenges related to fairness,
accountability, and data governance. Using published data,
the study systematically identified and classified potential
sources of AI bias throughout the AI lifecycle. These biases
emerged at multiple stages: Target setting, data acquisition
and annotation, modeling, validation and evaluation, as well

limited inclusivity in algorithmic design. These algorithmic

technological systems can inadvertently perpetuate
discriminatory patterns, requiring comprehensive preventive

AI applications in the beauty industry. These concerns
underscore the urgent need for comprehensive AI literacy

Ilya Jackson and Dmitry Ivanov (2023)  [11] examined the
impact of COVID-19-induced disruptions on the accuracy of
AI-driven demand forecasting systems within the beauty
care industry. The study employed causal mediation analysis
to explore the relationship between pandemic shocks and
forecasting errors, using extensive order data from a
prominent beauty care manufacturer and distributor. Data
spanned pre -pandemic, pandemic, and post -pandemic
periods, enabling a comprehensive analysis of the effects of
demand fluctuations. The research revealed that increased
demand volatility, rather than heightened sales volumes, was
the primary driver of forecasting inaccuracies during the
pandemic. This finding underscores the vulnerability of AI -
based forecasting systems to macroeconomic shocks and
shifts in consumer behavior. The study also identified
critical weaknesses in existing forecasting models,
emphasizing the need for more adaptive and resilient
systems capable of managing unprecedented disruptions.
Jinkyung Lee and Ki Han Kwon (2024)  [17] reviewed the
potential of AI -powered customized cosmetic devices as a
response to skin health challenges posed by global warming.
This study aimed to explore how AI can enable cosmetics to
adapt to weather changes and address consumer demands
linked to climate change. The review also considered the
broader implications for human skin care and preventive
strategies, offering key data for future exploration of this
intersection. Using a systematic approach guided by
PRISMA flowchart guidelines, the authors examined 1,308
sources across databases including PubMed, Medline,
Scopus, ResearchGate, and Google Scholar, ultimately
selecting 65 studies for analysis. The findings emphasized
the need for sustainable and safe AI -driven customized
cosmetics that mitigate skin damage caused by ultraviolet
(UV) radiation and climate change -induced stress. The
review identified a gap in research on heat -resistant skin
care materials and "inner beauty" solutions that enhance
resilience against environmental factors.

Vadim Meyl (2024)  [22] explored the philosophical
implications of AI as an emergent object of beauty,
examining its aesthetic valuation through historical and
contemporary lenses. The analysis traced the evolution of
beauty from Platonic ideals to postmodern interpretations,
positioning AI as a unique exemplar of beauty in the modern
era. Meyl argued that AI, despite its lack of consciousness
or emotional depth, represents a new aesthetic form through
its intricate algorithmic designs and problem -solving
capabilities, which are inherently tied to human intellectual
achievements. The study compared the harmony and
elegance of AI algorithms to classical elements of beauty,
suggesting that technological advancements challenge and
expand traditional aesthetic norms. Meyl also investigated
the rise of digital and artificial beauty, drawing parallels
between divine and artificial intelligence. The possibility of
authentic emotional responses, such as awe and love, being
directed toward AI was scrutinized, contributing to a
broader discussion about the interplay of human emotion
and non-sentient entities. The article concluded by reflecting
on AI's transformative role in redefining beauty, prompting
readers to consider the cultural and philosophical
implications of technological progress in the aesthetic
domain.
Woo-Been Kim, Jung -Eun Park, and Eun -Jun Park (2024)
[15] investigated the impact of voice AI beauty experience
services on hairdressing professionals' acceptance
expectations and intentions to use voice AI services. The
study targeted professionals in their 20s and 30s, collecting
data on demographic characteristics and preferences.
Statistical analyses, including frequency, factor, reliability,
and regression analyses, were conducted using SPSS 22.0.
Their findings highlighted key factors influencing the
adoption of voice AI beauty services. Participants identified
interest, usefulness, reliability, and ease of use as primary
drivers of engagement with voice AI beauty experience
services. Additionally, performance and usage expectations
emerged as critical components shaping acceptance
expectations, while usage intention was strongly linked to
overall adoption. Scalp management was the most preferred
consultation topic, reflecting practical applications of voice
AI technology. The study demonstrated that acceptance
expectations and intentions to use voice AI services had a
significant positive effect on participants’ perceptions of
voice AI beauty experience services. These findings

Conclusion
The integration of AI into the beauty industry has
fundamentally reshaped how products are developed,
marketed, and consumed, offering transformative potential
across multiple dimensions. This systematic review has
provided a comprehensive analysis of the key applications,
benefits, and challenges associated with AI in the beauty
sector, underscoring its role in driving innovation and
addressing complex consumer demands. AI -powered tools,
such as virtual try -on technologies, predictive analytics, and
personalized skincare solutions, have revolutionized
consumer experiences, enabling unprecedented levels of
customization and efficiency. Additionally, these
technologies have enhanced operational processes,
optimizing supply chains and facilitating data -driven
decision-making for businesses. The findings also highlight
the contributions of AI to sustainability efforts, such as the
development of eco -friendly products and packaging,
reflecting its capacity to align with global environmental
goals.
Despite its numerous advantages, the deployment of AI in
the beauty industry raises important ethical and societal
considerations. Concerns about data privacy, algorithmic
bias, and the reinforcement of unrealistic beauty standards
remain significant challenges that demand attention from
industry stakeholders. Addressing these issues will be
critical to ensuring that their benefits are equitably
distributed and aligned with ethical practices. Furthermore,
the review emphasizes the need for inclusive design and
well-prepared governance frameworks to mitigate biases
and enhance the fairness and accountability of AI systems.
By doing so, the industry can foster greater inclusivity and
representation, advancing societal perceptions of beauty and
health.
This study also identifies several gaps in existing research,
highlighting opportunities for further exploration. Future
studies should focus on interdisciplinary approaches to
better understand the complex interplay between AI
technologies, consumer behavior, and cultural norms.
Additionally, investigating the long -term impacts of AI on
the beauty industry, particularly in relation to sustainability
and ethical practices, will be essential to guiding its
continued integration. Collaborative efforts between
researchers, policymakers, and industry leaders will be
necessary to develop innovative solutions that address these
challenges while maximizing the potential of AI to enhance
both consumer and industrial aspects of the beauty sector.
