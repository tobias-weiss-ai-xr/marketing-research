Abstract: This study examines the strategic role of lounge services on customer satisfaction and brand
preferences in the airline industry using a hybrid multi -criteria decision-making (MCDM) approach. Lounge
service criteria determined by Skytrax were weighted using the Entro py method based on 1,868 consumer
reviews, and the world's leading airline brands were ranked using the Weighted Aggregated Sum Product
Assessment (WASPAS) method. The findings indicate that catering and staff services play a discriminative role
in differentiating between airlines. However, Wi -Fi service evaluations demonstrate low variability, indicating
a relatively standardized service structure across airlines. The high rankings of brands in both this study and
Skytrax rankings confirm the critical impa ct of lounge service quality on brand reputation. By integrating big
data and MCDM methods, the study offers the industry an objective and dynamic evaluation framework.
Keywords: Airline, Consumer Behavior, Lounge, Service Quality, Hybrid MCDM.

1. Introduction
Achieving competitive advantage in the airline market, development of strategies that create comfortable
environment and atmosphere aligned with consumer expectations has become central for companies. In
this regard, airline lounge services hold a special place for passengers (Kim et al., 2020). Lounges shape the
passenger experience in air transportation, and for airlines competing in the premium segment. A lounge
is not only a comfortable and privileged waiting area but also a strategic service element that strengt hens
customer loyalty. Moreover, passengers perceive lounges as a convenient work or rest place and a practical
service area supported by internet access and basic amenities (Han et al., 2012).
The expectations of passengers from different travel classes and cultural backgrounds regarding lounge
services offer managers important insights into customer segmentation and personalized service design
(Chatterjee et al., 2023). A positive lounge experience can directly influence airline selection by increasing
passenger satisfaction and reuse intention (Curkan and Özkan, 2021; Kim et al., 2016). While various service
options are prominent in passengers' lounge experiences (Han et al., 2012), understanding which service
types are most important for improving loung e service is considered a decisive factor in both customer
loyalty and airline selection (Curkan and Özkan, 2023).
In the airline industry, service quality stands out as a fundamental factor directly impacting passenger
experience and airline preferences. The inherent intangibility of services makes measuring service quality
challenging (Yarimoglu, 2014). Therefore, airline service quality consists not only of tangible elements but
also of intangible dimensions such as safety, comfort, employee attitudes, and the extent to which
passengers' expectations are met (Chou vd., 2011).
Determining consumer attitudes toward airline services is a critical process, and the service quality scale
developed by Parasuraman et al. (1985) has been successfully adapted to airline brands (Tahanisaz, 2020).
This allows for the assessment of service quality by comparing passengers' expectations and perceptions.
However, due to the subjective and variable nature of passenger service perceptions, airline service quality
dimensions and measurement methods can differ (Chou et al., 2011). Lounge services are also an integral
part of this framework; how passengers evaluate their waiting times, the refreshments they receive, ease
of access, atmosphere, and technological facilities reinforce the overall perception of airline lounge service
quality.
Many studies on service quality in air transportation treat lounges as a separate service dimension (Eboli
et al., 2022; Kim and Park, 2017; Zhang et al., 2023). However, the number of studi es examining consumer
attitudes on dimensions specific to lounge services is quite limited (e.g., Curkan and Özkan, 2021; Han et
al., 2012; Kim et al., 2016). These studies have used elements such as accessibility, atmosphere, food and
beverage, amenities (Han et al., 2012), ambient aesthetics, comfort (Chua et al., 2017), and staff behavior
(Cholkongka, 2019) to define service dimensions for lounges.
While there is no consensus on the factors and evaluation criteria used across studies, Skytrax, an air
transportation organization, has recently begun measuring lounge service quality across various
dimensions using passenger reviews. 2
Another critical threshold to be overcome regarding lounge service quality is the evaluation method used
in the study. While surveys are frequently preferred in this regard (e.g., Han et al., 2012; Chua et al., 2017;
Curkan and Özkan, 2021), text mining techniques that consider big data on online platforms (Kim et al.,
2020) are also used. Data from travel sites such as TripAdv isor (Brochado et al., 2019) and Skytrax, the
airline rating organization, can also be used to assess service quality factors and evaluation criteria in air
transportation (Bulatovic et al., 2023). In these studies, factor analysis, regression -based models, semantic
approaches and MCDM techniques are used to determine the dimensions of service quality and the effects
of these factors on consumer attitudes and perceptions (Bakır et al., 2025). Such studies have demonstrated
that airline service quality can b e evaluated and weighted using big data from consumer reviews and
MCDM techniques. However, to the best knowledge of author, examin ation of  Skytrax airline lounge

Service-Dominant Logic, offering an alternative perspective to traditional product -focused approaches to
how value is created in marketing, considers th e concept of value not as a one -sided output offered to the
consumer, but within interaction processes where different actors bring together their knowledge, skills,
and resources. In this context, service transforms into a structure jointly created by brands and consumers.
In this approach, as presented by Vargo and Lusch (2004), customers are positioned not as passive
recipients of value, but as active stakeholders in the value creation process. In this way, value produced in
coordination with consumers e merges as a reflection of knowledge and skills, creating a competitive
advantage (Vargo and Lusch, 2017; Vargo et al., 2020; Alptekin and Doğan, 2022). Especially considering
its role in the global economy, customer expectations in airline services, as in all other service industries,
are driven not only by tangible benefits but also by relationship -based elements such as interaction and
control. This transformation shows that the marketing focus has shifted from solely the exchange between
producer and consumer to the interactions experienced in this exchange process (Gümüş and Aksoy, 2022).
Accordingly, airline lounge services can be considered critical touchpoints where direct interaction
between customers and the business intensifies and value is created  collaboratively.
This study weights airline lounge service dimensions using the Entropy method and ranking top 20 airlines
based on Skytrax 2025 using the hybrid Entropy-WASPAS model. However, to ensure the reliability of the
analysis, it excludes compan ies with insufficient observation numbers and conducts the final evaluation
using a narrower and more representative sample. The study highlights elements such as staff behavior
and refreshments offered in airline lounge services, as rated by consumers onl ine. It also draws attention
to the most intense touchpoints, re -evaluating airline services within a service -dominant framework.
Furthermore, the study's findings point to the "high -tech/high-touch" approach (Naisbitt, 1984; Lee et al.,
2023), which argue s that human -centered service elements retain their importance even in rapidly
digitizing service environments. In this context, services based on human interaction in the airline industry
can play a decisive role in differentiating between brands.

2. Methodology
The current study adopted a hybrid MCDM approach to assess the importance of service quality elements
on passenger experience and airline preferences in the airline industry. The research sample consisted of
the world's top 20 airlines. Service quality elements for these companies were obt ained from evaluation
data on the Skytrax platform, which reflects passenger experiences and satisfaction levels. However, to
obtain reliable and representative results in MCDM analyses, airlines with insufficient observation
numbers (N < 50) were excluded  from the analysis. Accordingly, the final analysis was conducted based
on 12 airlines and 7 lounge service criteria.
The study methodology consisted of two stages. In the first stage, the Entropy method was used to
objectively determine the relative impo rtance of service quality criteria. In the second stage, the WASPAS
method was applied to evaluate and rank the holistic performance of the companies, considering the
obtained weights. This approach not only determined the objective weights of the criteria but also revealed
the comparative performance of the companies according to different service quality dimensions.
2.1. Entropy
The entropy method automatically reveals the difference in objective information between criteria in
quantitative criteria matrices derived from user evaluations on online platforms (Zhu et al., 2020); therefore,
it ensures that the criteria weights are free from subjective assignments and can produce a solid basis for
the subsequent ranking WASPAS (Bakir et al., 2019).

2.2. WASPAS
Weighted Aggregated Sum Product Assessment (WASPAS) is a MCDM model that combines additive and
multiplicative methods. This method, proposed by Chakraborty and Zavadskas (2014), has been applied
to various manufacturing samples and the effect of the λ parameter on ranking performance has been
investigated. In subsequent years, it has also been successfully applied to marketing research in the airline
industry (Bakir et al., 2019).
The key advantage of WASPAS is integrat ing the Weighted Sum (WSM) and Weighted Product Model
(WPM) approaches into a single composite performance score with a parameter (λ) as the inflection point.
This allows ranking of alternatives by considering both additive contribution and multiplicative impact.

3. Results
The current study weights airline lounge services based on online c onsumer reviews and ranks brands
accordingly. Therefore, for this section, we first compiled consumer ratings from Skytrax, then weighted
lounge service dimensions using the Entropy technique, and finally, using these weights, ranked airlines
using the WASPAS method.
3.1. Data
The dataset of the research consists of the world's top 20 airline brands according to Skytrax 2025 consumer
satisfaction surveys 3 and consumer evaluations of these airlines' lounge services on the
www.airlinequality.com official website. 4 Skytrax, which has been evaluating airlines and airports based
on their services through an international voting method since 1999, ranks airlines and airports from 1 to 5
stars based on professional audits and provides objective quality indicators encompassing hundreds of
criteria, including cabin service, catering, cleanliness, and lounge services. 5 As a research unit of Skytrax,
Airline Quality analyzes these ratings using a methodological framework and provides data support,
providing consumer evaluations of companies' aviation services, thus providing subjective customer
experiences.6 On the Airli ne Quality website, consumers rate airline lounge services under the following
headings: lounge comfort (COM), cleanliness (CLN), bar & bar services (BAB), catering (CAT),
washrooms/showers (WAS), internet/Wi -Fi (WIF), and staff/service (STS). The top 20 a irline brands and
lounge services evaluated for the study, along with consumer ratings for each airline, were prepared using
a 5-point Likert scale. Data compiled using the Python BeautifulSoup library on October 27, 2025, was
analyzed using the hybrid Entropy-WASPAS method in Microsoft Excel.
Before implementing MCDM, incomplete lounge service evaluations were removed from the study.
Furthermore, considering the MCDM literature with big data, airlines with fewer than 50 evaluations and
STARLUX Airlines, which had not previously conducted consumer evaluations of its lounge services, were

4. Conclusion
For airlines, lounge services are not only a convenience feature; they are also a strategic competitive tool
that directly impacts customer satisfaction, loyalty, and air line preferences. Accurately analyzing the
lounge service expectations of passengers from different travel classes and cultural backgrounds provides
airline managers with a crucial roadmap for customer segmentation and personalized service design
(Chatterjee et al., 2023). A quality lounge experience strengthens passengers' repeat purchases, fosters
brand loyalty, and plays a decisive role in airline selection (Curkan and Özkan, 2021; Kim et al., 2016). In
this context, evaluating lounge services based on various criteria (e.g., comfort, food and beverage quality,
staff etc.) and ranking airlines based on these criteria using MCDM methods allows for objective and
systematic measurement of competitiveness in the industry. Thus, identifying which service elements most

impact passenger satisfaction can contribute to both the improvement of lounge services and the
optimization of the airline's overall service strategy (Han et al., 2012; Curkan and Özkan, 2023). For this
purpose, the current study weighted the lou nge service criteria determined by Skytrax using the Entropy
method based on consumer comments and ranked the world's top 20 airline brands with these weights.
The first important contribution of this study is to prioritize airline lounge service criteria  using consumer
reviews. Past studies generally used the average ratings of related criteria provided by Skytrax (e.g., Duran
and Macit, 2023; İnan, 2025) or TripAdvisor (e.g., Roszowska  et al. , 2024; Sak , 2024) for evaluating the
service quality in air transportation. However, these data do not accurately represent the criteria weights;
rather, they round these values. This approach can limit the sensitivity of the results by ignoring the
distribution and  variation in user evaluations. In this study, all individual user reviews from different
airlines were directly collected from the web, and the average of the actual scores for each criterion was
calculated and incorporated into the MCDM analysis. Additio nally, to increase reliability and
representativeness, airlines with fewer than 50 user reviews were excluded from the evaluation. Moreover,
the results were evaluated specifically for the 12 airlines included in the analysis. Thus, lounge service
quality data was evaluated in a more detailed, realistic, and representative manner, significantly increasing
the reliability and decision-support value of the results.
Second, this study adopted a hybrid Entropy -WASPAS MCDM methodology to rank criteria and
alternatives respectively. The findings indicate that catering and staff services play a primary role in
differentiating airline lounge services among brands. However, this finding should not be interpreted as
meaning that these criteria are directly the “most important” criteria for consumers. The entropy method
reflects the level of discrimination between criteria among alternatives (Chodha et al., 2022). Therefore, the
weights obtained represent the relative information content and differentiating capacity of the criteria. This
finding suggests that airlines' overall lounge quality is primarily determined by differences in human -
centered service. This distinctive result reveals the critical role of the human touch in creating a competitive
advantage by meeting  consumer expectations, even in today's digitally advanced service environment.
This management finding, pointing to a high-touch approach (Naisbitt, 1984; Lee et al., 2023), indicates that
airline operations should place greater emphasis on service proces ses that involve direct interaction with
the customer, in addition to focusing on technological investment.
 These kinds of services were also found to be significant dimensions that impact the overall consumer
satisfaction (Han et al., 2012) and airline selection (Curkan and Özkan, 2021) and that significantly correlate
with consumer sentiments (Lacic et al., 2016) in the literature. Thus, these findings are in line with these
studies. Another critical criterion like these services is washroom amenities. Washrooms, a hardware-based
service, may be particularly important for frequent travelers. Past studies revealed that the cleanliness of
washrooms may have the top priority for air passengers (Isa et al., 2020). Therefore, this result of the current
study also backed up these studies. Moreover, WIFI services is found to be the least important criteria for
passengers. This result is likely due to whether the service is provided directly by the airline. Therefore,
Wi-Fi is purchased from an external supplier by all airlines, making it a standard service and perceived
similarly by consumers. However, past work pointed out that internet is among the most important
services stimulating consumers’ practical purposes (e.g., Han et al., 2012; Kim et al., 2016; Kim et al., 2020).
Conversely, because catering and staff services, which are differentiated from other services, are provided
directly by the airline, it is difficult to standardize service performance as with Wi-Fi. Therefore, consumer
attitudes towards these services have naturally diversified according to the results.
Third, this study attempts to rank top 20 airline brands according to Skytrax. 12 airlines with sufficient and
reliable number of reviews (N≥50) were ranked by applying the WASPAS method with the Entropy-based
weights derived from lounge service criteria. When comparing rankings, Qatar and Cathay were found to
show similar positions in in both the research and Skytrax rankings, whereas discrepancies were observed
for other airlines. One possibl e explanation for this situation is likely due to the variation between the
criteria considered in the Skytrax rankings and the lounge service criteria used in this study.

Furthermore, the current study makes some practical contributions to the industry.  First, the findings
highlight the strategic role of lounge services and brand competitiveness for airlines. Rankings obtained
using the MCDM method objectively show airlines which lounge service criteria (e.g., catering) contribute
most to passenger satis faction and preferences. This allows airline managers to direct their limited
resources to service areas with the highest potential impact. Moreover, the use of data derived from user
reviews provides airlines with a current and dynamic measure of customer  perception beyond traditional
surveys, making decision -making more realistic, particularly for marketers. Third, the rankings provide
airlines with the opportunity to directly compare their performance with their competitors and enable data-
based competitive analysis within the industry. The similarity of top -ranked airlines, such as Qatar and
Cathay, to Skytrax results demonstrates that lounge service quality is a determining factor in brand
reputation and airline preference. This finding also provides a strategic reference point for other airlines to
improve their lounge services to enhance customer loyalty and brand equity.
Although the current study contributes to the existing literature on airline lounge service quality through
the use of big data by prioritizing service dimensions and airline brands using hybrid MCDM
methodology, future research directions addressing certain limitations should be suggested. First, only
Skytrax consumer reviews about airline lounge services was used as a data source fo r the analysis in this
study. However, various data sources such as social media (Koçak et al., 2024), Tripadvisor (Bakır et al.,
2019), and Google (Koçak, 2020) were used in past work. Thus, analyzing these data with comprehensive
techniques like machine/ deep learning approaches that may allow the sentiment scores of each criterion
for airline lounge services would expand the literature. Second, Entropy -WASPAS hybrid methodology
was applied to prioritize criteria and to rank alternatives in this study. Looking at previous studies, various
hybrid MCDM approaches like fuzzy models (Bakır and Atalık, 2021). Therefore, future work could focus
subjective weighting methods or hybrid approaches to measure consumer perceived importance levels and
using such models in examining airline lounge services. Finally, the research design of the current study
focused exclusively on airlines. However, in previous studies, lounge services were discussed from the
airport perspective or as a service dimension of the airline (e.g ., Chua et al., 2017; Chatterjee et al., 2023;
Zhang et al., 2023). Thus, future studies could benefit from considering lounge services within the
framework of airport-airline collaboration.
