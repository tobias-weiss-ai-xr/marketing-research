Editorial on the Research Topic
Exploring the multidisciplinary roles of sports events
The increasing complexity and diversity of sport events require corresponding advances
in the ways they are observed and analysed within the scientific literature. A key
challenge is the transition from predominantly economic evaluation approaches toward
multidimensional frameworks that integrate economic, social, tourism, environmental,
and territorial dimensions. Such frameworks are essential for understanding and
assessing the role of the multiple stakeholders involved across different spatial scales
(from local to international) and temporal horizons (from short- to long-term). For
example, the systematic review of the literature by Rossini et al. (1) highlights the
variability of the impacts of events of different dimensions on participants, host
communities and destinations, a reality that can only be understood through
observation/evaluation models that integrate economic, social and tourism indicators.
Similarly, Bourbillères and Djaballah (2) reinforce the convergence towards a holistic
approach, where understanding the impacts and legacy of events requires an articulated
analysis of the economic, social, environmental and territorial dimensions. This
integrated and multidisciplinary perspective provides the foundation for the Research
Topic Exploring the Multidisciplinary Roles of Sports Events. This edition seeks to
advance robust and interdisciplinary knowledge that can support evidence-based
strategic decision-making in the planning, management, evaluation, and long-term
legacy of sport events.
An introductory dimension of this research topic is volunteering as an engine of social
legacy, with primary quantitative research on the Special Olympics World Games 2023
(SOWG 23) demonstrating that participation is driven primarily by intrinsic
motivations, such as expressing personal values and seeking interpersonal contact.
A parental history of volunteering is identified as a crucial predictor of long-term
engagement, suggesting that values instilled during childhood shape adult commitment
and requiring organisations to adopt tailored digital communication strategies to foster
lasting engagement across diverse demographic groups. The subjectivity of quality
perception further illustrates the multidisciplinary nature of events, where Service
Quality and Role theories explain how different stakeholders evaluate “success”.

Analysis reveals an “expectation–performance gap": active
participants often perceive lower service quality than organisers
or group leaders due to greater performance pressure and more
stringent logistical requirements. Consequently, sports
management must move toward a tailored experience design
that addresses the specific needs of athletes, families, and
spectators separately to promote long-term loyalty. Similarly,
sporting events serve as a cultural battlefield for national
identity, where qualitative content analysis of media narratives
on the naturalisation of football players reveals a tension
between pragmatic nationalism and cultural essentialism. By
distinguishing between legal “thin citizenship” and cultural
“thick citizenship,” the research highlights how the media act as
mediators of public perception, defining the symbolic boundaries
of an “imagined community” in an era of global talent migration.
From a marketing lens, sponsorship effectiveness is determined by
the “fit” or congruity between the brand and the event. A meta-
analytic review of 31 studies confirms that high levels of fit generate
positive cognitive and emotional responses, though these are
moderated by cultural contexts; Eastern cultures prioritise
emotional harmony and collectivism, while Western contexts see a
stronger impact on behavioural intentions. Finally, the impact and
legacy on Quality of Life (QOL) is examined through a scoping
review that identifies a significant shift from evaluating purely
economic outcomes toward measuring subjective well-being. The
findings suggest that while one-off mega-events offer temporary
“feel-good” effects, recurring participatory events foster more
sustainable community cohesion. To advance this field, researchers
advocate integrating objective physiological measures, such as
Heart Rate Variability (HRV), to complement subjective surveys.
Ultimately, the integration of these diverse methodologies proves
that sports event success is a multidimensional construct requiring
a roadmap that balances marketing strategies, human capital
management, and social wellbeing.
In conclusion, the articles in this Research Topic offer a profound
and critical perspective on the role of sport in contemporary society.
Collectively, they provide academics, event managers, and
policymakers with practical insights and actionable tools to
maximise the social legacies of sports events by understanding the
intergenerational dynamics of volunteer motivation, improve
service delivery through greater recognition of the role-specific
expectations of diverse event participants, navigate the complex
identity politics surrounding sports journalism and national team
recruitment, optimise sponsorship strategies by acknowledging
cultural differences in consumer behaviour, and promote long-
term societal wellbeing by moving beyond short-term economic
metrics towards more holistic quality-of-life indicators. Taken
together, the findings presented in this volume constitute a
significant contribution to the ongoing dialogue on how sports
events can be strategically leveraged to foster more inclusive,
prosperous, and happier societies.
