Hotel competition is no longer determined solely by
price and amenities, but rather by the brand's ability to
build digital expectations that are consistent with
guests' sensory experiences at service sites. This article
aims to formulate a digital -sensory congruence model
in the formation of hotel brand experiences using the
context of three hotel brands in Malang City that have
different positions: Grand Mercure Malang Mirama,
The Alana Malang, and Bobobox Malang. The article
was compiled as a conceptual study based on
exploratory multi-case design through the synthesis of
digital branding literature, online brand experience,
sensory marketing, servicescape, and hospitality
experience. The analysis yielded three conceptual
findings. First, digital branding  functions as a gateway
to experience through websites, social media, online
travel agents, online reviews, photos, videos, and
applications. Second, sensory marketing is proof of the
brand's promise through visual, auditory, olfactory,
tactile, and gustat ory stimuli that affect the perception
of quality, comfort, trust, and memory of the experience.
Third, congruence between digital appointments and
actual sensory experiences is an important mechanism
that bridges pre -stay expectations with post -stay
satisfaction and advocacy. The contribution of this
article is to offer a conceptual model, research
proposition, as well as an empirical validation agenda
for the development of a more measurable, ethical, and
experience-oriented hotel branding strategy.

INTRODUCTION
The development of digital marketing has changed the way hotels build
and maintain brands. In the traditional branding paradigm, a brand is often
understood as a name, symbol, design, or distinguishing feature of products and
services. In the digital context, a brand is no longer just a visual identity, but an
accumulation of consumer experience at various touchpoints, such as websites,
social media, apps, online travel agents, Google Business Profiles, online reviews,
instant messaging services, and conver sations between consumers. As a result,
the hotel's image is shaped not only by the company's official message, but also
by the narrative of guests that circulate openly in the digital space.
The hospitality industry is becoming a relevant context because guest
decisions generally start from digital stimuli, while actual satisfaction is
determined by the physical service experience. Before booking a room, potential
guests can view the photos, v ideos, prices, ratings, reviews, and facility
information. Upon arrival, guests assess whether these expectations are in
accordance with the atmosphere of the lobby, the smell of the room, the quality
of the staff's greeting, the cleanliness of the room, t he texture of the linen, the
comfort of the bed, the quality of the food, the noise level, and the ease of the
check-in process. Thus, hotel branding cannot be separated from sensory
marketing.
Sensory marketing is a marketing approach that designs sensory stimuli
to create brand perception, emotion, and memory. In hotels, these stimuli emerge
through visual design, music, aroma, temperature, texture, taste of food, and
communication style of sta ff. However, in the digital age, most sensory stimuli
are first constructed through the imagination of potential guests. Warm room
photos, amenity videos, culturally nuanced captions, or reviews of scents and
breakfast can shape mental imagery before guests experience service in person.
The main gap to note is the relationship between digital promise and
sensory evidence. Many hotels have engaging digital content, but the actual
experience doesn't always live up to guests' expectations. Conversely, there are
hotels that have a good servic e experience, but fail to translate it into a strong
digital narrative. This mismatch can lower trust and trigger negative reviews.
Therefore, the concept of digital-sensory congruence is important to explain how
the hotel brand experience is shaped as a whole.
Malang City is an attractive location because it has the character of an
educational, business, family, culinary, and tourism destination. Within the
landscape, hotels compete through different positions. Grand Mercure Malang
Mirama represents a premium ho tel with MICE facilities and full -service
services. The Alana Malang represents a four -star hotel that elevates cultural
nuances, traditional-modern design and hospitality of service. Bobobox Malang
represents capsule accommodation based on technology, efficiency, privacy, and
digital-first experiences.
This article is geared towards answering three conceptual questions. First,
how do digital branding and sensory marketing play a role in shaping hotel
brand experiences? Second, how does the digital -sensory integration pattern
differ according to hotel pos itioning? Third, how can conceptual models and

LITERATURE REVIEW
Digital Branding and Online Brand Experience
Digital branding is the process of forming brand identity, differentiation,
and meaning through digital touchpoints. In hotel marketing, digital branding
not only serves to introduce the name and facilities, but also to build experience
expectations. Offic ial websites, social media, apps, OTAs, user -generated
content, and responses to reviews are all part of an ecosystem of brands that
influence each other.
Online brand experience explains that consumers form relationships with
brands through cognitive, affective, sensory, and behavioral experiences in the
digital environment. For hotels, the experience can emerge when potential guests
browse room photos, rea d reviews, watch aperture videos, compare prices, or
interact with social media admins. The more consistent the visual, information,
and tone of communication between channels, the stronger the perception of
professionalism and brand credibility.
In practice, hotel digital branding works through three main functions.
First, the identification function, which is to make the hotel easily recognizable
through the name, logo, color, photo, facilities, location, and booking channel.
Second, the differentiation function, which emphasizes the reason for choosing a
hotel over competitors, such as premium feels, local culture, MICE facilities, pod
technology, or ease of application. Third, the personification function, which is
to make the brand feel relevan t through content that suits the guest segment,
personal responses, and experience narratives that are close to consumer needs.
Sensory Marketing, Servicescape, and Hospitality Experience
Sensory marketing is a marketing strategy that involves the five senses to
form an emotional and symbolic experience with a brand. In the service industry,
especially hotels, sensory marketing is closely related to the servicescape, which
is the physical e nvironment in which services are produced and consumed.
Lobbys, rooms, restaurants, corridors, meeting areas, and digital areas become
the stage for experiences that affect guest perception.
Visual stimuli include color, lighting, interior design, signage, staff
uniforms, photography, and spatial planning. Auditory stimuli include lobby
music, greeting style, sound quality, noise level, and digital notifications.
Olfactory stimuli include the scent of the lobby, the room, the restaurant, and the
effect of cleanliness. Tactile stimuli include the texture of linens, mattresses,
towels, room temperature, furniture materials, and ease of interaction with
devices. The gustatori stimulus comes through breakfast, welcome drinks, room
service, signature dishes, and local culinary experiences.

In hotels, sensory marketing does not stand as an additional decoration. It
is a testament to the quality of service. Beautiful room photos should be
evidenced by clean and comfortable room conditions. Cultural narratives should
be visible in design, music , hospitality, and menus. The promise of technology
should be felt in ease of check -in, room access, facility control, and quick
response. If sensory evidence doesn't match the digital promise, the brand
experience becomes fragile.
Digital-Sensory Confluence
Digital-sensory confluence refers to the fit between brand representation in
the digital space and the actual multisensory experience at the service site. This
concept is important because hotel customers experience brands in two phases:
the digital -dominant pre -consumption phase and the sensory -dominant
consumption phase. The two should not be treated as separate management
areas.
Confluence can arise when photos and videos match real conditions, the
description of the facility is accurate, positive reviews are supported by the actual
experience, the tone of communication of the staff is in harmony with the brand
image, and the physical atmosphere of the hotel reinforces the digital narrative.
Conversely, incongruence arises when photos are too ideal, facility information
is not up-to-date, smells are uncomfortable, rooms are noisy, service is slow, or
the application is not as easy as promised.
In the context of research, digital-sensory congruence can be understood as
a mechanism that strengthens trust, quality perception, memorable experience,
satisfaction, and word -of-mouth. Digital excellence is not enough if it is not
proven sensory. On the other hand, a superior sensory experience is not optimal
if it is not communicated effectively on digital channels.

METHOD
This article uses a conceptual approach with an exploratory multi -case
study design. The conceptual approach was chosen because the main objective
of the article is to formulate models, propositions, and empirical validation
agendas regarding the integration of digital branding and sensory marketing in
hotels. The multi-case design is used to show that digital -sensory integration is
not uniform, but rather influenced by the positioning, market segments, service
character, and operational complexity of hotels.
The three cases used are Grand Mercure Malang Mirama, The Alana Malang,
and Bobobox Malang. The selection of the three cases is theoretical, not statistical,
as each represents a different brand configuration. Grand Mercure represents a
full-service premiu m hotel with a business, MICE, family, and leisure
orientation. The Alana represents a four -star hotel with cultural differentiation
and traditional -modern design. Bobobox represents capsule accommodations
that highlight technology, privacy, efficiency, and digital-first experiences.
Conceptual analysis was carried out through literature synthesis and digital-
sensory dimension mapping. The digital dimension includes digital identity,
visual content, interactivity, online reviews, applications, and information
consistency. The sensory d imensions include visual, auditory, olfactory, tactile,

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Digital Branding as a Gateway to the Hotel Experience
Digital branding is the gateway to experience because most potential guests
start to get to know the hotel from digital channels. At this stage, guests have not
experienced the service directly, but have already formed a perception of quality,
comfort, saf ety, and price suitability. Room photos, website design, clarity of
facility information, guest reviews, ratings, social media content, and admin
responses are all the initial signs used to rate a hotel.
At Grand Mercure Malang Mirama, digital branding needs to affirm
premium consistency and large -scale service capacity. Digital content should
ideally feature not only rooms, but also ballrooms, meeting rooms, restaurants,
sky lounges, leisure facilities, a nd proof of MICE experience. At The Alana
Malang, digital branding needs to connect the hotel's visuals with cultural
narratives, warmth of service, and modern comfort. At Bobobox Malang, digital
branding needs to highlight the ease of application, access security, pod privacy,
cleanliness, and an efficient and unique stay experience.
These differences in positioning show that digital branding strategies cannot
be made generic. Premium hotels need content that fosters trust in the quality of
service. Culturally charged hotels need authentic storytelling. Digital capsule
accommodation requires experiential education, as some potential guests may
not be familiar with the concept of pods. Thus, digital branding must function as
an expectation management tool, not just a promotional tool.
Sensory Marketing as a Proof of Brand Promise
If digital branding shapes imagination, sensory marketing proves that
imagination through bodily experience. Guests not only judge hotels based on
information, but also on what they see, hear, smell, touch, and feel. When sensory
stimuli match the digital promise, guests are more likely to trust the brand. When
it is not suitable, disappointment can arise even if the hotel's objective facilities
are actually adequate.
In a full -service hotel like Grand Mercure, sensory marketing needs to be
managed consistently on a large scale. The lobby should give a premium
impression from the start, the ballroom and meeting rooms should support a
professional image, the room should be comfortable, and the F&B should
reinforce the perception of quality. At The Alana, sensory experiences ideally
present a balance between cultural warmth and modernity. At Bobobox, sensory
design must be minimalist and functional: pod lighting, sleep qua lity, sound
control, cleanliness, privacy, and ease of use of technology are the most decisive
elements.
This analysis shows that sensory marketing is not just an aesthetic affair, but
part of a brand proof system. Too strong scents, inappropriate music, poor
lighting, uncomfortable linen textures, disappointing food, or confusing digital
processes can ruin the brand experience. Therefore, hotels need to make sensory
marketing an operational standard of experience, not sporadic decorative
activities.

Digital-Sensory Confluence as a Trust Mechanism
Digital-sensory confluence is at the core of the model offered in this article.
Confluence occurs when a hotel's digital appointment matches the actual
experience of guests. For example, spacious room photos match the actual size
and condition, state -of-the-art facility information, cultural content reflected in
the service atmosphere, claims of ease of application are truly felt at check-in, and
staff responses are aligned with the brand's digitally built tone.
Confluence produces two important effects. First, the expectation
confirmation effect, which is that guests feel that the booking decision is right
because the actual experience corresponds to the initial shadow. Second, the trust
effect, which is that gue sts rate the hotel as an honest, professional, and reliable
brand. Both of these effects have the potential to increase satisfaction, positive
reviews, recommendations, and intention to stay again.
Conversely, incongruence can result in experiential dissonance. Guests feel
deceived when photos are too ideal, amenities aren't available, rooms aren't up
to expectations, sounds are annoying, smells are uncomfortable, or apps don't
run smoothly. In the d igital ecosystem, this dissonance can easily turn into
negative reviews. Therefore, congruence management must be a strategic
priority in hospitality branding.
Conceptual Models and Research Propositions
The proposed conceptual model places digital branding as the formation of
initial expectations, sensory marketing as evidence of actual experience, and
digital-sensory congruence as mechanisms that influence beliefs and behavioral
outcomes. This model can be explained as follows: digital touchpoints form
mental imagery and expectations; sensory touchpoints confirm or disprove
expectations; The level of congruence determines whether guests experience
trust, satisfaction, memorability, and advocacy intention.

Empirical Validation Agenda
To make this model more robust for follow -up publication, empirical
research can be conducted with a multi-case qualitative design. Key data sources
include digital observations, servicescape observations, management interviews,
guest interviews, promotion al documentation, and online review analysis.
Digital observation is directed at websites, OTAs, social media, Google reviews,
photos, videos, captions, admin responses, and information consistency. The
observation of the servicescape is directed at the lo bby, rooms, restaurant,
meeting area, check -in process, sound, smell, temperature, texture, and flow of
services.
Recommended informants include marketing managers, revenue
managers, front office managers, F&B managers, housekeeping, digital
operations staff, and hotel guests. To strengthen the credibility of the data,
researchers can use source triangulation, member checking, trail audits, and
cross-case comparisons. Data analysis can be done through thematic coding with
the initial categories of digital identity, interactivity, online review, visual cue,
auditory cue, olfactory cue, tactile cue, taste cue, congruence , trust, satisfaction,
memorability, and advocacy.
In the context of the Sinta 4 journal article, empirical validation will increase
the chances of publication because the manuscript not only offers conceptual
ideas, but also shows clear research procedures. If field data collection is not yet
possible, the article can be explicitly positioned as a conceptual paper with the
main contribution in the form of models and propositions. However, if the author
can add interview data and brief observations, the manuscript can be
strengthened into an empirical case study article.
Managerial Implications
First, hotel management needs to manage digital branding as an experience
system, not just a promotional channel. Websites, OTAs, social media, Google
Business Profiles, apps, and reviews should convey a narrative that is consistent
with actual services. Content that is overly ideal but inaccurate can magnify the
risk of guest disappointment.
Second, hotels need to make sensory marketing an experience SOP. The
smell of the lobby, music, lighting, temperature, texture of linen, breakfast
quality, greeting style, and room cleanliness need to be designed, measured, and
evaluated on a regular basis. Consistent sensory management will help the hotel
prove the brand's promise in real terms.
Third, digital -sensory strategies must be adjusted to positioning. Grand
Mercure needs to emphasize premium consistency and MICE capabilities. The
Alana needs to reinforce the cultural authenticity and warmth of the service.
Bobobox needs to ensure that technology, privacy, security, and pod comfort run
smoothly.
Fourth, hotels can leverage online review analysis to read the sensory
patterns that guests appreciate or complain about. Keywords such as

CONCLUSION
Digital branding and sensory marketing are two complementary sides in
the formation of a hotel brand experience. Digital branding builds expectations
through online identity, content, interaction, and reputation. Sensory marketing
proves these expectations  through visual, auditory, olfactory, tactile, and
gustatory experiences at the service site.
This article offers the concept of digital-sensory congruence to explain that
guest trust is formed when digital appointments match the actual experience. At
Grand Mercure Malang Mirama, congruence is mainly related to premium
consistency and large-scale service. At The Alana Malang, congruence is related
to the suitability of cultural narrative, design, and warmth of service. At Bobobox
Malang, congruence is related to the promise of technology, privacy, efficiency,
and pod comfort.
The contribution of this article lies in the formulation of conceptual models,
research propositions, and empirical validation agendas that can be used to
strengthen hospitality branding research. The limitation of the article is the lack
of complete inter view and field observation data. Therefore, the next study is
recommended to test this model through qualitative case studies, quantitative
surveys, or mixed methods so that the relationship between digital branding,
sensory marketing, congruence, trust, s atisfaction, and advocacy can be
empirically proven.
