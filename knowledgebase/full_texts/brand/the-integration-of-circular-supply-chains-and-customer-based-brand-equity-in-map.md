ABSTRACT
The objective of this study is to conduct a systematic literature
review (SLR) by mapping brand strength in the era of
sustainability, integrating the Circular Supply Chain (CSC) and
Customer-Based Brand Equity (CBBE). Through an analysis of
43 sele cted articles from the Scopus and Emerald databases,
this study summarizes how circular operations such as reverse
logistics and resource efficiency can be transformed into
valuable assets in the eyes of consumers. Findings indicate that
supply chain transparency supported by Industry 4.0
technologies serves as a signal that mitigates the risk of
greenwashing while strengthening brand awareness and
loyalty. When combined with a strong brand position, this
integration not only improves environmental performa nce but
also generates long -term competitive advantage. This study
provides a framework for aligning circular operational
strategies with brand communication to optimize brand equity.

INTRODUCTION
The current business environment has been drastically altered by the world wide paradigm
change from a linear "take -make-dispose" economy to a circular economy (CE) model. The
circular supply chain (CSC) approach combines the concepts of sustainable product life cycles
with green supply chain management (Liu et al., 2018) . CSC focuses not only on operational
efficiency but also on creating long -term value through resource recovery and waste reduction.
The urgency of implementing CSC is driven by increasing regulatory pressure and global
environmental awareness. Practical implementations such as reverse logistics and circular
network design have proven capable of maximizing profitability and environmental impact
(Gharibi and Abdollahzadeh, 2025) . However, significant challenges arise in specific sectors such
as apparel retail, where institutional barriers and organizational costs hinder the adoption of
circularity (Zaidi and Chandra, 2025) . On the other hand, these operational barriers for SMEs

Although the operational aspects of CSC have been widely discussed, its relationship with
Customer-Based Brand Equity (CBBE) warrants further exploration. Sustainable marketing,
supported by corporate social responsibility, has been shown to foster responsible consumption
behavior (Jia et al., 2023) . In addition to practical quality, modern customers assess items based
on the "signals" of sustainability that businesses send through their supply chains. The use of
eco-friendly raw materials can now build a positive image in customers’ mind s (Kim and Oh,
2020). This integration is  accelerated by digital transformation via Industry 4.0 technologies like
Big Data Analytics and the Internet of Things (IoT). Digital circular economy innovations not only
enhance efficiency but also foster corporate competitiveness and social well -being (Al-Omoush,
2025). The data transparency generated by these technologies helps validate sustainability
claims, thereby strengthening consumer trust and brand loyalty (Farrukh Shahzad, Liu and Zahid,
2025).
However, the current literature indicates a research gap regarding how circular
operational mechanisms specifically translate into brand strength across various industrial
sectors, ranging from manufacturing to the hospitality industry. Thus, the purpose of this work is
to map the integration between CSC and CBBE by conducting a systematic review of 43
important studies. Throug h this analysis, it is hoped that a framework can be formulated to
explain how supply chain circularity strengthens brand equity and mitigates consumer
skepticism in the digital age. To address this gap, this study examines the following research
questions:
1. RQ1: What are the trends in the literature regarding the integration of CSC  and CBBE over
the past decade?
2. RQ2: What are the key operational practices in CSC that have the  strongest impact on the
equity dimension?
3. RQ3: What are the mechanisms underlying the relationship between
circular operations and the dimensions of CBBE, including the roles of  mediating and
moderating variables?

LITERATURE REVIEW
Circular Supply Chain
The circular supply chain (CSC), which emphasizes the product life cycle, combines the
concepts of the circular economy and green supply chain management (Liu et al. , 2018) . In
practice, integrating green factors into reverse logistics network design has proven capable of
maximizing both profitability and environmental efficiency (Gharibi and Abdollahzadeh, 2025). In
addition, the Green Customer Value has emerged as a key determinant of positive attitudes
toward eco -friendly brands (Amanda and Zai, 2025) . The use of frameworks such as the
“Greening Factor” is crucial for identifying an organization’s transition points toward full circularity
(El-Tahan et al., 2026).

Customer-Based Brand Equity
Customer-based brand equity (CBBE) is driven by consumers’ psychological responses  to a
company’s sustainability commitments. Sustainable marketing supported by CSR activities has
been shown to significantly enhance brand image and drive responsible consumption behavior
(Jia et al.,2023). The biggest factor in creating a green brand identity within the CBBE dimension is
associations with the usage of environmentally friendly raw materials (Kim and Oh, 2020) .
Furthermore, the credibility of information sources regarding circular practices strongly
determines how consumers form their perceptions of the brand image (Wu, Lim and Lim, 2026) .

METHODS
This study followed the SLR protocol in accordance with the PRISMA guideli nes. A search
was conducted in Scopus and Emerald for journal articles published between 2016 and 2026 in
journals ranked in quartiles Q1 –Q4. The keywords used included combinations of “Circular
Supply Chain,” “Brand Equity,” “Sustainability,” and “Environmental Marketing.” The selection
process consisted of four stages: Identification, Screening, Eligibilityand Selection. The final result
was 43 high -quality articles that formed the basis for data synthesis. Based on the established

RESULTS
Bibliometric Analysis and Literature Trends (RQ1).

Figure 2 Literature Trends

Figure 2, which illustrates trends in publications on circular operations and brand strength,
shows exponential growth over the past five years. The peak in publications occurred during the
2024–2026 period, reflecting the global urgency for sustainable business models (Labaran and

emphasis on operational efficiency in green supply chain management (GSCM) at the beginning
of the decade has clearly shifted to a more comprehensive and customer -focused integration of
the circular economy  (Liu et al., 2018 ;Roberto, Lerman and Benitez, 2026).

As shown in Figure 3, the research is dominated by studies in emerging markets,
particularly in Asia. Significant contributions come from China and Pakistan (Jia et al., 2023), India

2025) and Indonesia (Ardini and Fahlevi, 2024) . This indicates that circularity has become a
crucial competitive strategy for industries in regions undergoing environmental re gulatory
transformation.

Eco-Design and Resource Recovery
The second group consists of 11 articles analyzing material and product d esign
innovations. The literature in this category argues that circular attributes inherent in a product
serve as a credible “signal” of quality (Signaling Theory). The utilization of recycled materials and
perceived quality were shown to be significantly positively correlated in nine out of the eleven
articles in this group. (Kim and Oh, 2020; Plotkina, Rabeson and Bambauer -Sachse, 2025) .
Customers tend to place greater value on products that integrate a sustainability narrative from
the design stage, which strengthens the brand’s position through green product differentiation
(Bag et al., 2022;Taherdangkoo, Mona and Ghasemi, 2019).
Digitalization and Traceability
The latest category is supported by 7 key articles published between 2024 and 2026. The
main emphasis is on using Industry 4.0 technologies, such blockchain and the Internet of Things
(IoT), to guarantee complete supply chain transparency (Farrukh Shahzad, Liu and Zahid,
2025;Labaran and Masood, 2025 ;Qian, Gao and Chen, 2023) . Analysis shows that digital
transparency significantly increases Brand Awareness and reduces consumer skepticism toward
companies’ greenwashing claims (Al-Omoush, 2025 ;Tetteh, Obiri -Yeboah and Owusu Kwateng,
2026). This group represents a future trend in which circular data transforms into an intangible
brand asset that strengthens customers’ digital trust (Zhou et al., 2023).

Green Marketing and Corporate Social Responsibility
A total of 10 articles explore how strategic communication regarding circular operations
influences brand associations. This literature finds that ethically communicated circular practices
help brands establish an identity as responsible market leaders (Jia et al. , 2023) . Sustainable
marketing activities aligned with circularity values have been shown to enhance brand appeal
and strengthen purchase intent through green  value co -creation (Santos, Coelho and Neves,
2026;Martínez-Falcó et al. , 2024; Kaur, Gangwar and Dash, 2022) .Such commitments not only
improve brand image but also ensure that brand equity remains competitive (Vehbi, Farmanesh
and Solati Dehkordi, 2025;Yu et al., 2024).
Relationship, Mediation, and Moderation Mechanisms (RQ3)
 The mechanism linking circular operations to the CBBE dimension involves complex
intermediate variables. Technology Mediator through Digitalization through Industry 4.0 (AI, IoT,
Blockchain) acts as a mediator that ensures data transparency, thereby strengthening trust and
brand awareness (Farrukh Shahzad, Liu and Zahid, 2025 ;Labaran and Masood,
2025).Psychological mediators such as green brand image and the credibility of information
sources mediate the influence of operational practices on long -term customer loyalty (Wu, Lim
and Lim, 2026; Jia et al., 2023) .The process of green value co -creation also plays a key role in
mitigating consumer skepticism (Santos, Coelho and Neves, 2026) . Moderating variables such as
organizational marketing capabilities and the level of environmental turbulence serve as
moderators that determine the effectiveness of circular strategies in creating competitive
advantage (Yu, Ramanathan and Nath, 2017; Vehbi, Farmanesh and Solati Dehkord i, 2025) . A
commitment to sustainable marketing as a whole strengthens brand appeal in the market (Yu et
al., 2024).

DISCUSSION
This study successfully mapped the integration between the Circular Supply Chain (CSC)
and Customer-Based Brand Equity (CBBE) through a systematic review of 43 key articles. Broadly
speaking, literature trends indicate a rapid increa se in publications since 2022, with a
predominance of research in emerging markets such as Asia that employs the PLS -SEM

quantitative methodology (RQ1). These findings confirm that circularity is no longer merely a
technical operational issue but a crucial strategic marketing strategy.
The classification results indicate that reverse logistics practices, eco -friendly product
design, digitalization, and green marketing have significant yet distinct impacts on the brand
equity dimension (RQ2). Reverse logisti cs and waste management have been shown to be key
drivers of brand loyalty through the building of consumer trust (Gharibi & Abdollahzadeh, 2024).
Meanwhile, digital transparency through Industry 4.0 technologies such as blockchain and IoT
has emerged as a new asset capable of mitigating consumer skepticism and strengthening brand
awareness in the digital age (Shahzad et al., 2024; Al -Omoush, 2025). This relationship
mechanism is reinforced by the mediating role of psychological variables such as green bran d
image and source credibility, as well as moderating variables such as marketing capabilities and
environmental turbulence (RQ3). In theory, this work broadens the use of the Resource -Based
View and Signaling Theory in the context of the circular economy. From a managerial
perspective, the synergy between circular operational efficiency and transparent brand
communication is key for companies to transform environmental responsibility into a brand
equity asset with global competitiveness

CONCLUSION
Despite making a substantial theoretical contribution, this study contains a number of
shortcomings that present possibilities for further investigation. First, the conclusions cannot be
applied to the service sector and the SME segment, which have different resource structures,
due to the predominance of the research on the large -scale manufacturing sector. Future
research is expected to explore how the service sector adopts circularity to build brand equity.
Second, the variation in regulations across countries from an institutional theory
perspective, as well as differing levels of consumer skepticism across cultural backgrounds, has
not been thoroughly explored as moderator variables. Third, in terms of methodology, the
predominance of cross -sectional studies emphasizes the necessity of further study employing
experimental or longitudinal techniques to confirm the circular brand equity's long -term
durability. Lastly, a critical new field of study for comprehending its influence on brand
awareness in the digital era is the integration of cutting-edge technology like artificial intelligence
(AI) for automating transparency in circular supply chainsl
