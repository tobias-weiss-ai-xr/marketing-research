ABSTRACT
Human Resource Management has transformed from a purely administrative role into one that
is strategic and can influence the sustainability of an organisation. The increasing awareness
of climate change, resource scarcity, carbon emissions, and environmental degradation has
forced organisations to incorporate sustainability into all aspects of their business activities
in recent years. HR is one of these features that has become a key driver of environmental
stewardship by integrating sustainable practices into workforce planning, talent acquisition,
employee engagement, training, performance management, and culture. Green Human
Resource Management offers a framework for organisations to minimise environmental
impacts while maximising operational efficiency, employee engagement, and organisational
long-term sustainability. HR policies that incorporate environmental goals into their employee
management result in an active workforce that is in sync with the company’s sustainability
efforts. HR functions can significantly minimise environmental impact by embracing
digital transformation, paperless processes, sustainable recruitment, green offices, eco-
friendly events, and employee-led sustainability programs. The discussion includes recent
advancements like Artificial Intelligence (AI) powered HR analytics, Cloud-based Human
Resource Management Systems (HRMS), Blockchain-based documentation, Hybrid work
arrangements, Internet of Things (IoT) powered smarter workplaces, and Environment, Social
and Governance (ESG) guided workforce strategies. The examples given by companies such
as Microsoft, Google, IBM, Siemens, Unilever, Adobe, Infosys and Deloitte showcase how
HR-driven sustainability efforts are helping to cut carbon emissions, energy usage, waste and
resource use, and improve employer branding. The International Labour Organisation (ILO,
2025) states that green jobs will be one of the fastest-growing job categories in the world,
highlighting the need for HR to build capabilities to address sustainability issues.

INTRODUCTION
Sustainability in the environment has become one of the strategic priorities of companies
around the globe. Sustainability is no longer a choice, but rather a necessity for businesses
today, as climate-related crises intensify, natural resources shrink, and regulations grow.
Increasingly, governments, investors, customers, employees and regulatory agencies demand
organisations demonstrate measurable initiatives relating to their environmental impact. As
a result, companies are incorporating sustainability principles into their business strategies,
operations and governance to generate long-term economic, environmental and social value.
In this metamorphosis, Human Resource Management (HRM) has played a strategic role in
influencing organisational conduct and in making sustainability a part of everyday working
practices.
Back in the day, HR was mainly about recruitment, payroll management, employee relations,
training, and performance assessment. But the transformation of Green Human Resource
Management has broadened the strategic roles of HR professionals a lot. Green HRM is defined

Green HRM is the integration of HR policies with environmental management systems with

states that HR professionals can act as catalysts for organisational sustainability through
employee attitudes, organisational culture, and strategic decision-making.
The significance of sustainable HR practices has gained tremendous momentum since the
United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) were put in place, with Goal 8 (Decent
Work and Economic Growth), Goal 12 (Responsible Consumption and Production), and Goal
13 (Climate Action) standing out as particularly relevant. These declarations have inspired
organisations to reconceptualise how they design the structure of their workforce to minimise
carbon emissions, natural resources use, and employee environmental responsibility. The
International Energy Agency (IEA, 2025) reported that commercial buildings use almost one-
third of the world’s electricity, so efforts to make the workplace more sustainable are vital for
lowering greenhouse gas emissions. Many of these activities are directly impacted by HR
policies, digital transformation efforts, flexible working options, and employee engagement
initiatives.
Technological advances have helped to pave the way to sustainable HR practices in recent
times. Cloud computing, AI, machine learning, RPA, blockchain technology, and digital
collaboration tools have revolutionised the HR landscape. Recruitment processes that are still
paper-based, employee manuals that are printed, manual performance appraisal systems,
and manual payroll administration are being replaced by more cloud HRMS platforms like SAP
SuccessFactors, Oracle HCM, Workday, BambooHR, and Darwinbox. According to Bondarouk
and Brewster (2016), digital HR transformation is not just about improving efficiency; it’s also
about significantly cutting down on paper usage, storage space, energy consumption, and
administrative costs.
The COVID-19 pandemic also pushed digital transformation to the next level by providing
proof of how remote or hybrid working is possible to keep pace with productivity and at the
same time help to minimise the environmental impact of commuting to work, travelling for
business, and the use of the office. Research from the Global Workplace Analytics Report
(2025) suggests that companies implementing hybrid-work models have managed to decrease
commuting-related carbon emissions by around 25-35% and have also managed to cut down
on office operating costs. HR professionals have played a pivotal role in creating flexible
workplace policies, managing virtual teams, and conducting digital onboarding, and have also
been essential in maintaining the engagement of employees in remote environments.
Sustainable HR practices have a major impact on organisational competitiveness and employer
branding as well as operational efficiency. In today’s world, workers are looking for companies
that are genuinely dedicated to sustainability. According to Deloitte (2025), over two-thirds of
Generation Z and Millennial professionals take into account the environmental performance of
an organisation when making employment decisions. Similarly, environmentally responsible
organisations have higher employee engagement, organisational commitment, and employee

THE IMPACT OF ENVIRONMENTAL ISSUES ON HUMAN RESOURCES
The strategic importance of Human Resource Management (HRM) is one of the most
greatly impacted by external factors today: environmental challenges. The impact of climate
change, biodiversity loss, resource depletion, rising carbon emissions, water scarcity, and
more stringent environmental laws is no longer just an environmental factor and is now a
major challenge in organisational and workforce management. HR is on the frontlines of
companies making the switch to a sustainable business model and must be able to incorporate
sustainability goals into workforce planning, organisational culture, employee engagement,
and talent management. As such, HR is now more than ever a strategic partner in the process
of embedding sustainability throughout the employee lifecycle.
The rising significance of sustainability can be seen in its growing global acceptance of
frameworks such as Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG). The International Labour
Organisation (ILO) reports that ESG has emerged as a key part of the competitiveness of
organisations, and that employer organisations in over 65 countries have made considerable
efforts to broaden the scope of their workforce services and training in the field of sustainability.
Around 80% of employer organisations said that businesses were looking for support with
sustainability, rather than compliance, with ESG becoming one of the top strategic workforce
priorities.

Environmental issues are one of the most easily seen effects on HR when it comes to

compensation. Companies with a robust environmental reputation have better employer

practices. It’s a change in thinking from hiring just for technical skills to hiring people who can

The workplace has also changed, with workplace policies and practices being reshaped
by environmental issues. The paper-heavy, energy-consuming, business-tourism and
inefficient office environments are slowly being replaced by environmentally friendly offices.
HR departments are now promoting a paperless approach to documentation, digital
communication systems, cloud-based HR systems, virtual meetings, flexible working policies,
and thoughtfully purchasing products. Singh (2025) states that digital HR practices have a

the need for paper, printing costs, storage space, and transportation-related emissions.

in environmental sustainability has been further solidified. Flexible work arrangements reduce
commuting-related carbon emissions, decrease office energy consumption, and improve

productivity of their employees. HR teams are thus crucial in crafting remote work strategies,
keeping employees engaged, managing virtual teamwork, and providing equal access to
digital work tools.

awareness campaigns, environmental awareness workshops, green competitions, volunteer
programmes, recycling programmes, celebration of Earth Day, carbon reduction campaigns
and employee-led environmental committees. Guerci et al.  (2016) suggest that employee

UNDERSTANDING HR’S ENVIRONMENTAL FOOTPRINTS
Human Resource Management is often viewed as a relatively low-impact activity within the
organisation compared to manufacturing or logistics, but the cumulative environmental impact
of the HR activity is significant. Traditional HR methods can use a lot of paper, electricity,
office space, business travel, and other resources. These functions are routine aspects of
administration and together add up to the greenhouse gas emissions, waste production,
and overuse of resources that are caused by the use of increasingly large workforces in
increasingly global organisations. As a result, HR professionals need to be aware of the
environmental impact of their roles and the need to implement measures to mitigate it.
Therefore, organisations must be aware of the environmental impacts of their HR operations
and take steps to reduce them.
The continued use of paper records in recruitment, onboarding, and employee administration
is one of the biggest factors impacting HR’s environmental footprint. In the past, companies

stages of the employee life cycle. In addition to raising the costs of operations, paper-based

There is more to waste than just paper. While producing paper, it consumes a large amount

Digital transformation is the most successful way to solve this problem. The use of Applicant
Tracking Systems (ATS), electronic signatures, cloud-based document management systems,
digital employee records, and Learning Management Systems (LMS) greatly decreases
the amount of paper used and enhances administrative efficiency. Platforms like SAP
SuccessFactors, Workday, Oracle HCM, Darwinbox, or BambooHR are growing in popularity
among organisations to securely store employee data across the employment lifecycle.
According to Bondarouk and Brewster (2016), digital HR transformation can considerably
enhance the effectiveness of operational processes, data security, accessibility for employees,
and environmental sustainability.
Another significant source of HR impact on the environment is business travel and
physical events. Sponsorships also often involve a significant amount of travel on a
domestic and international scale, such as recruitment fairs, campus hiring programmes,
leadership conferences, employee training, annual gatherings, performance reviews and
employee engagement events. These are the various areas where air transportation, hotel
accommodation, conference venues, catering, printed material and promotional material will
drive up the carbon emissions of an organisation.
Organisations have recently been able to use new technologies to substitute many of the
physical interactions with virtual ones. Interviews, onboarding, training, workshops and
performance discussions now take place without travelling with video conferencing platforms
like Microsoft Teams, Zoom, Google Meet, Cisco Webex and AI-powered collaboration
platforms. Chia et al.  (2020) found that transportation-related emissions are significantly
reduced as a result of virtual recruitment, which also enhances recruitment efficiency and
candidate accessibility. These digital replacements also cut down on the energy use of
conference space and printed conference supplies.
Another major environmental problem in HR offices is their energy usage. Computers, printers,
servers, lighting systems, air conditioning and heating, and communication technologies are
all used extensively in HR departments throughout normal business operations. Older office
buildings that have inefficient HVAC systems, traditional lighting, and always-on electronic

organisation’s environmental footprint. Thus, sustainable HR practices are not just about
administrative efficiency but about building sustainable organisational cultures and habits
through leadership, communication, education, incentives and employee engagement.
DIGITAL TRANSFORMATION AND PAPERLESS HR OPERATIONS
In today’s digital age, HRM has undergone a complete transformation, empowering
organisations to minimise their environmental impact while optimising their processes,
improving work satisfaction, and making strategic decisions. Historically, HR has maintained
most of the work on paper, with manual documentation, in-person hiring, classroom training,
and physical communication, using a lot of paper, energy, office space, and transportation.
The world is rapidly changing with the evolution of digital technologies, and HR departments
are moving towards cloud-based, data-driven, technology-enabled systems that will greatly
cut down on the consumption of resources and enable organisational sustainability.
Digital HR transformation involves the adoption of various technologies across all HR
processes, including Human Resource Information Systems (HRIS), Cloud Computing, Artificial
Intelligence (AI), Machine Learning, Blockchain, Robotic Process Automation (RPA), and
Predictive Analytics. These technologies enable paperless recruitment, electronic employee
records, electronic payroll, virtual onboarding, online performance management, and AI-
based learning, and minimise reliance on physical facilities for the organisation. Jackson and
Seo (2010) say that digital HR practices help support sustainable workforce management in
several ways: it reduces paper use, enhance data access, decrease administrative mistakes,
and save on organisational operating costs.

The rise of cloud-based HR platforms like SAP SuccessFactors, Oracle HCM, Workday,
Darwinbox, BambooHR, and UKG is one of the major trends. These systems provide a single
location for employee data, payroll, attendance, hiring, learning, and performance information,

One of the most prominent and effective aspects of Green HRM has become Paperless HRM.
Previously, HR departments had to fill out a lot of paperwork during the employee’s lifecycle,
from posting job ads and filling out application forms to drafting employment contracts, payroll
records, leave applications, performance appraisal reports, training manuals, attendance
registers and policy documents, and finally, personnel files. As businesses started to grow
internationally, HR operations started to become inefficient, costly, and not eco-friendly due
to the use of paper.
The paper- based HR operations have a serious impact on the environment. The production
of paper consumes substantial amounts of wood, water, chemicals, and energy and produces
substantial amounts of greenhouse gases in the manufacturing of the paper, transporting,
printing, storing, and disposing of paper. Di Sacco et al.  (2021) highlighted that such a
reduction in paper consumption in organisations directly helps to support forest conservation,
biodiversity protection, lower carbon emissions, and responsible use of resources. Likewise,
Peng et al. (2018) stated that paperless human resource management practices are among
the easiest but most beneficial ways to minimise the ecological footprint of an organisation.
Paper dependence has significantly decreased in the talent acquisition process with the
adoption of digital recruitment. Now the applicants are required to present their electronic
résumés, upload their digital certificates, complete online assessments, and take part in
virtual interviews without any physical documentation. Applicant Tracking Systems automate
the application process, evaluate candidate skills and abilities, and streamline communication
while recruiting. Holm (2020) showed that implementing a digital recruitment system has
benefits in terms of increased recruitment efficiency and decreased paper usage as well as
administrative costs.
These environmental benefits continue with electronic onboarding. New staff are now issued
with employment contracts, employee policies and procedures, compliance documentation,
induction and training resources via secure online portals. Documentation has largely been
replaced by electronic signatures, streamlining onboarding and minimising administration.
Self-service HR portals allow employees to view HR records anytime, without the need for
repetitive printing and documentation.

gone paperless, with electronic salary slips, tax statements, attendance logs, leave records,

SUSTAINABLE TALENT ACQUISITION AND GREEN OFFICE PRACTICES
As the world faces increasing environmental threats and the significance of corporate
sustainability, Talent acquisition has experienced a dramatic shift. Until recently, recruiting
focused on paper applications, involved a lot of travel to interview, physical assessment
centres, and printed onboarding documents, resulting in a considerable amount of resources
used and carbon emissions. In addition, the nature of recruiting has shifted from just finding
technically competent workers to attracting people who are environmentally conscious,
responsible, and committed to sustainable development, reflecting the growing prominence
of the Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) principles among organisations.
Sustainable talent acquisition is the process of making environmental sustainability a part of
the entire talent acquisition and selection lifecycle. Green recruitment is a practice of designing
a process of recruitment that reduces environmental impacts as well as supports the selection
of people whose values are aligned with the organisation’s long-term sustainability goals
(Renwick et al., 2013). They seem to be more and more looking to different online job boards
like LinkedIn, Indeed, Naukri.com, Glassdoor, Workday Recruiting, SAP SuccessFactors
recruitment, and Oracle Recruiting Cloud to post job openings, accept applications, review
applicants, and engage with candidates.
Sustainable recruitment has also been revolutionised by AI. AI-driven recruitment software
automates the screening of resumes, assesses candidate qualifications, can forecast job
suitability, schedules interviews, and offers recruiters data-driven insights for decision-
making. These technologies significantly reduce the amount of repetitive administrative work

Another practical and visible way in which HRM can help in the organisation’s sustainability
is by using green office practices. Office activities might seem less resource-intensive than
manufacturing and logistics, but all of them use a lot of electricity, paper, water, office supplies,
electronic equipment, and transportation resources. HR departments can affect many of these
activities in the workplace by setting policies, influencing the way employees act when buying
goods and services, promoting flexible working, and shaping the culture of the organisation.
Green office practices are the adoption of environmentally responsible policies at work that
lead to the reduction of resource use, generation of less waste, better energy use, and the
adoption of sustainable behaviour by the employees. Schaltegger and Burritt (2018) argue
that to manage workplaces sustainably, environmental issues should be incorporated into
everyday organisational processes and activities and not be viewed as a static project or one-
time investment.

buildings use lots of electricity for lighting, heating, ventilation, air conditioning (HVAC),
computers, printers and communication equipment. HR departments are becoming more
involved with facilities management to promote responsible consumption of energy by raising
awareness, implementing energy-saving policies, and implementing behavioural interventions.
Purchasing and using energy-efficient equipment and supplies, and encouraging employees to
turn off computers at the end of the day, limit unnecessary printing, use natural light wherever
possible, and minimise energy waste during the day.

One of the biggest obstacles to change is the organisational culture. There may be resistance
to switching to digital HR systems among employees/mangers, who are used to paper-
based systems. Resistance can only be overcome through a whole-of-organisation change
management approach, consistent communication, engagement of employees in decision-
making, and clear leadership support for sustainability goals.
Another major obstacle is the need for technological investments. The cost of adopting
cloud HR platforms, AI-powered recruitment tools, blockchain-based documentation, and
IoT-enabled smart workplaces is high. This is especially challenging for Small and Medium
Enterprises (SMEs) to afford and keep viable. For organisations to then adopt phased
implementation strategies, they need to prioritise investments for environmental impact and
return on investment and consider using scalable technology solutions that are suitable for
their organisation’s size and industry environment.
It is difficult to measure environmental outcomes. While financial indicators can be easily
measured, environmental indicators are not so readily measurable. The challenge faced
by organisations is to establish baseline measurements, to identify the specific relationship
between HRM practices and environmental improvement, and to identify the causal relationship
between Green HRM practices and sustainability outcomes. These challenges can be
addressed through the development of robust measurement frameworks, incorporation of
sustainability indicators into balanced scorecards, and through the use of standardised ESG
reporting frameworks.
In the future, the trends that are emerging will impact the development of Green HRM. In
conclusion, the role of Artificial Intelligence in HRM sustainable practices will keep evolving
and reshaping HR strategies with predictive analytics, intelligent workforce planning, and
automated sustainability reporting. With AI-powered sustainability dashboards, organisations
can now keep track of paper usage, business travel emissions, office energy usage,
employee engagement in sustainability programs, and carbon reduction efforts in real time.
These technologies will allow for higher accuracy measurement and optimisation of HR’s
environmental footprint.
Another key future development is carbon-neutral recruitment. More and more companies
strive to become a net-zero organisation in their activities, including in their processes for
recruiting and hiring talent. This includes the calculation and offsetting of carbon emissions
of recruitment processes, moving to 100% virtual assessment centres, using sustainable
travel methods where physical presence is essential, and choosing sustainable recruitment
partners.

on their employees and take action to create improvements beforehand. AI will review patterns
in employee behaviour, resource use, and operational effectiveness to provide personalised
