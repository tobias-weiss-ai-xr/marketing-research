Abstract
The demands of competitive advantage are a growing force in the enterprises, especially in emerging markets
where the expectations of industry, resources and regulations are rapidly changing. From the context of green
procurement, green logistics and recyclability, Green Su pply Chain Management (GSCM) practices can provide
companies with opportunities to optimize operational performances, boost their brand value and realize
long-term benefits. Even though there is an increasing number of people interested in adopting GSCM practices,
the empirical evidence remains fragmented and underd eveloped confirming the ef fectiveness of GSCM in
emerging market contexts. This study reviewed 50+ pe er-reviewed articles that were published from 2018 to
2026 systematically, to discuss the relationship between GSCM practices and firm competitiveness. Articles
were first selected through a structured literature revi ew methodology and following that, filtered to be of
interest and methodological quality and to target articles on emerging-market firms. Overall, the results show
that green procurement and eco-logistics are a profitabl e practice that always improves competitive advantage,
both through the reduction of costs, enhancement of  supply chain efficiency and the indication to the
stakeholders that the company is responsible. Opera tional effectiveness and compliance are also in part
addressed by waste reduction efforts, but limited by financial, technical and/or organizational barriers. Cost
reduction and brand reputation were selected as key mediat ors that are reinforced by the impact of the practices
of GSCM to increased competitiveness. This synthesis gives useful guidance to managers needing to
strategically manage sustainable supply chains and identifies opportunities to develop further research, such as
cross-country comparisons, service-sector applications, and longitudinal analyses. The study adds value to both
theory and practice because of its ability to synthesize the existing know ledge on GSCM in emerging markets
and providing evidence-based guidance for harnessing sustainability as a competitive advantage.
Keywords: green supply chain management, competitive advantage, emerging markets, sustainability practices,
eco-logistics, waste reduction
1. Introduction
1.1 Background of the Study
The key drivers for the use of Green Supply Chain Management (GSCM) in modern business environment are
the pressure of stakeholders to take responsibility for the environment, growi ng consumer awareness of
sustainability, and high expectations from regulation. Customers, investors and regulatory bodies have an impact
on companies to minimize environmental effects and de monstrate their commitment to sustainable practices,
leading to environmental management becoming a motivating factor in supply chain decision making (Ahi &
Searcy, 2013). GSCM can be defined as the inclusion of  environmental considerations within the activities of
supply chain processes, such as procurement, production, transportation and waste management, to achieve a
better ecological impact while preservi ng operational efficiency (Srivastava,  2007). They are much regarded as
strategic practices that facilitate the reduction of costs,  the minimization of environmental risks and improve the
brands' reputation, all of which are important issues from long-term competitiveness perspective.
The challenges to implementing GSCM vary among enterprises in emerging markets. A number of infrastructure
challenges may affect the implementation of green prac tices effectively, including weak sustainable logistic
systems and underdeveloped supply chain infrastructure. (Govindan et al., 2014) Lack of financial resources,
such as limited access to financing sources and fundings for green technologies, is the other barrier to investment
in sustainability initiatives (Dubey et al.,2019). Furthermore, the policy challenge in some emerging economies,

1.2 Research Problem
Even though the strategic value of Green Supply Chain Management (GSCM) is known, the extent of
implementing this approach is not uniform and is not fu lly elaborated in emerging markets. Evidence on the
effects of implementing GSCM practices on competitiv e advantage with regard to green procurement,
eco-friendy logistics and waste reduction has been limited in emerging markets compared to developed markets,
as evidenced by studies (Golicic & Smith , 2019; Testa et al., 2011). Some of the structural issues for firms from
emerging markets include lack of capacity in their logistics infrastructure, scattered supply chains, and poor
access to funding for sustainable investme nts (Govindan et al., 2014). Beside s, the policies in many emerging
markets are weak and not always enforced, which leads to uncertainty regarding the potential of investing in
green projects (Seuring & Müller, 2008).
Those challenges may delay some firms from adopting GSCM, but empirical evidence reveals that those firms
which may have successfully adopted GSCM can enjoy e nvironmental and competitive benefits (Ahi & Searcy,
2013; Srivastava, 2007). Still, the causal link betwee n specific practices of GSCM and improved firm
performance (e.g., cost reduction and enhanced bran d reputation building) lacks in-depth research in
emerging-market environments (Dubey et al., 2019; Zhu et al., 2007). Managers need to feel directed as to which
practices to focus on, and how to succes sfully implement sustainability into their supply chain strategy, without
having a unified opinion of these relationships.
Therefore, it is essential to systemat ically review and evaluate the seconda ry research to get clarity about the
impact of GSCM practices on the competitive advantage in the emerging markets, identifying the factors which
act as a medium of transmission, and identifying contextual  barriers. Such a gap can be filled to help put theory
into practice and give managers and policy makers the advice and guidance they need to manage businesses
sustainably and competitively in low resource environments.
1.3 Aim of the Study
The primary objective of this study is to review for co mprehensiveness the literature regarding the practices of
Green Supply Chain Management (GSCM) that is applied in  companies that operate in  emerging markets, after
which one has to analyse the impact that the GSCM practices have on the competitive advantage. The study
particularly focuses on the three principles of GSCM, namely: green procurement, eco-friendly logistics and
waste minimisation. The Study aims to explain how GSCM  practices affect firm's performance and how that
effect is mediated by cost reduction and brand reputa tion by connecting findings from peer-reviewed secondary
sources.
1.4 Research Objectives
The study is guided by the following research questions:

3. RQ3: How do cost reduction and brand reputation mediate the relationship between GSCM practices

2. Literature Review
2.1 Green Supply Chain Management Practices
Green Supply Chain Management (GSCM) aims to explicitly consider environmental aspects in the scope of
activities of the supply chain activities: procurement, production, logistics and waste management. Research
shows that green procurement positively affects the quality of the supplies and the risks to an operation (Ahi &
Searcy 2013). It is also believed that procurement practices oriented towards sustainability goals may help to cut
costs in the long-term by avoiding material waste (Sri vastava, 2007). Zhu et al. (2007) argue that supplier
involvement along with environmental monitoring to m easure performance improves the efficiency of a green
procurement programme especially in limited resource environments.
An implementation of eco-friendly strategies for logistics, like optimized routes and energy-efficient transport, is
been related with quantifiable aspects of cost saving and carbon emission reduction (Dubey et al., 2019). In order
for green logistics to be adopted, firms must possess th e necessary capabilities and infrastructure, which may be
highly different between emerging markets and developed countries, as Golicic and Smith (2019) state.
Govindan et al. (2014) also find that logistics performance is enhanced when a strategy for sustainability is
integrated into the overall operational planning of businesses instead of being considered as a standalone effort.
Waste reduction principles, like lean manufacturing and reverse logistics, help reach environmental compliance
and also enhance the operational efficiency (Testa et al., 2011). Retrieval strategies are important for the
recovery of value from end-of-life products because not only do they cut material costs but can also improve the
customer's view of environmental responsibility, as pointed out by Seuring and Müller (2008). Other hurdles for
emerging market companies include government regulations and inconsistent infrastructure regulations that
create a lack of access to green technologi es, creating lower potential benefits from such efforts (Dhillon et al.,
2022). Sharabati et al. (2021) conclude that to put waste reduction into practice and manifest it in performance

2.2 Competitive Advantage
Competitive advantage refers to a firm’s ability to achi eve stronger profitability, market share, and strategic
positioning than its competitors. The competitive advantag e it creates when sustainable supply chain practices
are coordinated with firm capabilities and stakeholder demands in the context of GSCM. It has been shown that
green supply chain activities, such as reducing waste a nd optimizing logistics, can make the processes more
efficient and coherent and provide customers and institu tions with a clear indication of environmental care and
responsibility (Kumar et al., 2011). Placing sustainability at the heart of supply chain management helps
companies gain a competitive edge, especially in markets where consumers recognize companies for their
dedication to sustainability (Melnyk et al., 2010).
According to the analytical studies, GSCM practices don't just give competitive advantage, it is when that
practices get involved in the core organizational processes and practices. Companies that apply eco-friendly
concepts in producing goods, such as during the purchasing process, to production planning, and to logistics
management, can obtain benefits both in terms of production costs and also in the name of brand reputation
(Mukhsin & Suryanto, 2022). Furthermore, the capability to dynamically tune and reorganize the processes of
the supply chain when changes in the environment occur will prove more advantageous to sustain their
performance advantages (Hosseini et al., 2019).
Despite the scarcity of empirical findings in emerging ma rkets, evidence suggests th at the practices of GSCM
help to support long-term strategic gains through lower operational costs, greater supply chain flexibility and
stakeholder trust. The benefits of these, however, depend on the context - on the degree to which regulation is
enforced, on the quality of infrastructure, and on managers' attitudes, commitment and involvement in
sustainability programs (Zhu et al., 2007). In this sense it implies that competitive advantage is not guaranteed,
even if it is possible, it depends on the capabilities of the firm, the alignment of the environment and the factors
that hinder or enable the implementation of sustainable practices.
2.3 Mediating Mechanisms: Cost Reduction and Brand Reputation
Studies indicate that the relationship between practices of Green Supply Chain Management (GSCM) and
competitive advantage is not direct, but rather indirect, as going through cost reduction and brand reputation as
some processes can have a mediating effect. It is seen in the reduction of costs due to firms that carry out green
procurement, lean manufacturing and ec o-efficient logistics that maximise the use of resources and reduce the
waste. In fact, some researchers such as Dubey et al . (2019) noted that the companies, which integrated
sustainability with the operational processes could obtain some tangible reduction in the Energy- and
Material-Cost, which shows that GSCM can support not only ecological purpose but also the economic one too.
Likewise, Bhattacharya et al. (2019)  point to a decline in operating costs that firms implementing cleaner
production practices associate with a decrease in waste generation and efficiency in the processes. Overall, these
studies showed that cost savings associated with these gr een initiatives will only occur if they are part of proper
integration, as some of the individual green initiatives have not seen substantial savings.
Brand reputation as a second mediator  is noticed because it also has an influence on the perceptions of the
stakeholders as also on the market positioning. Environmentally friendly companies can stand out, win the
loyalty of eco-sensitive customers and gain the trust of  investors (Dangelico & V ocalelli, 2017). Comparations
reveal that businesses which are able to communicate their sustainability efforts well, have seen improvements in
its customer base, while firms in which there is not transparent communication about green practice have only
achieved small positive gains to this reputation (Green et al., 2012). Further, the reputation as a mediator would
be more effective in certain sectors of industry or ma rkets than others; for instance, in consumer industries,
where businesses are more visible, reputation is more likely to provide favours with respect to B2B business
relationships (Melnyk et al., 2010).
When combined, these results suggest that cut price and reputation of brand are contingent mechanism. The size
of their mediating impact relies on firm capacities, the context, and believability of sustainability implementation.
Multiple studies have shown that companies which simply practice GSCM do not gain competiveness advantage,
but they must rather make environmental activities strategic in the normal functioning of the firm to achieve both
economic and reputation benefits.

2.4 Barriers and Contextual Challenges
A number of barriers have consistently been mentioned in research on the adoption and implementation of Green
Supply Chain Management (GSCM) in emerging market (EM) environments where structural and institutional
issues play a prominent role in hindering and obstructing the adoption of GSCM in supply chain. Much of the
time, financial issues are identified as the main constrai nt - businesses can be hit with high initial investment to
support sustainable technologies, su stainable energy efficient equipmen t and training programmes (Hebaz &
Oulfarsi, 2021). The financial pressu res are exacerbated by economic instab ility in numerous emerging markets
that may result in a more conservative approach to investing in sustainability than in short-term operations due to
lack of capital access. Moreover, th e lack or non-coherenc e of regulation and gove rnment encouragement
aggravates the implementation difficulty of GSCM projects, because companies are not given policy
encouragement, or "regulatory sup port" for supply chain environmental improvement in the absence of it
(Dhillon et al., 2022).
Another important hurdle is the factors having to do with organisations and people. Research indicates that if top
management is not committed to sustainability or lacks sustainability skills in a firm, internal resistance may be
evident against practicing sustainability, where managers may focus on short-term performance measures instead
(Kaur & Awasthi, 2018). This internal resistance is refl ected in the related external  resistance of suppliers:
suppliers are not able or willing to adhere to environm ental standards, which jeopardizes collaboration to green
the supply chain. Also identified by research are know ledge and skill deficits among workers that hinder the
design of the implementation of the GSCM initiative as well as monitoring it.
Contextual challenges also reveal themselves in rega rd to market and customer awareness. Environmental
performance as a differentiator may not be valued by consumers and down stream clients in many developing
regions and this may limit the perceived benefit of implementing GSCM, which may also slow diffusion
(Rahman et al., 2020). Intertwined with these barriers, th ere can be found financial, institutional, organizational
and market-facing barriers, all with multiple dimens ions and which need multiple-targeted policies and
management interventions. The body of literature indicates  that addressing all these challenges requires support
from external environments inclusive of regulatory cl arity, financial incentives, and educating stakeholders,
along with overcoming challenges at the firm level.
2.5 Synthesis of Consensus and Divergence in the Literature
The reviewed literature demonstrates broad consensus th at Green Supply Chain Management (GSCM) practices
positively influence organizational performance and competitive advantage across emerging markets. The
majority of the research accept that the mentioned practices, including green  procurement, eco-logistics, waste
management, and sustainable collaboration with suppliers offer advantages to organisations in the form of
increased operational efficiency, decreased costs, a nd improved environmental performance of firms, thus
improving their competitive edge (Green et al., 2012; Govindan et al., 2014; Mukhsin and Suryanto, 2022).
Equally, there is a general consensus that two most significant mechanisms by which GSCM can generate value
include cost alleviation and brand reputation, and that the Resource-Based View (RBV) and Stakeholder Theory
are the predominant theoretical viewpoints to characterize such relations.
Despite this general consensus, important differences em erge across the reviewed studies. As it was noted in
numerous research papers, cost reduction is the ma in channel, through which GSCM boosts competitive
advantage, but some researchers have also clarified that intangible benefits like corporate perception, the trust of
stakeholders, innovation ability, and organizational agility are some of the most important determinants of the
long-term performance (Dubey et al., 2019; von Berlepsch et  al., 2022). As well, the literature varies on the
aspect of the importance of contextual factors. Regulatory pressure is rega rded in some studies to be the key
driver of GSCM implementation, and managerial commitment, supplier partnership, or market need of the
products based on green practices are also noted to be more important determinants (Rahman et al., 2020; Hebaz
and Oulfarsi, 2021). Such differences imply that even though GSCM usually leads to competitive advantage, it is
only effective when there are a firm-capability and ins titutional-environment interaction especially among the
emerging-market contexts.
3. Theoretical Framework
3.1 Resource-Based View (RBV)
The Resource-Based View (RBV) provides one of the most widely applied theoretical perspectives for
explaining how firms achieve and sustain competitive advantage through the development and effective
utilization of valuable organizational resources and capa bilities. Barney (1991) states that when the resources

that firms have are valuable, rare, inimitable, and organize d in ways that can be used to create value, the firms
get better performance. The practices of green procurement, eco-logistics and waste reduction can be interpreted
as strategic abilities that help the firms to increase the efficiency of the operations at the same time aiming to
meet the environmental sustainability goals in terms of Green Supply Chain Management (GSCM). Instead of
being merely isolated environmental programs, such prac tices are integrated into organizational operations and
form efficiencies that are harder to mimic among competitors (Dubey et al., 2019).
From an RBV perspective, competitive advantage is realized when firms integrate GSCM into their core
operations to generate long-term value through lower op erational costs, improved resource utilization, enhanced
innovation, and greater adaptability to changing environmental requirements. Such capabilities do not only
enhance internal performance but also align the firms to react positively to changes in the market and regulation.
But although RBV is a useful theory in understanding how internal capabilities can lead to higher performance,
it offers very little information about the external forces that either drive firms to achieve sustainable practices or
the circumstances that encourage them to  be effective. As a result, there is  a need to have counter theoretical
frameworks that can be used to understand the role of the stakeholder expectations in determining the strategic
implementation and performance of GSCM, especially in emerging-market contexts.
3.2 Stakeholder Theory
Stakeholder Theory complements the Resource-Based View by emphasizing that organizational success depends
not only on internal capabilities but also on the firm's ability to meet the expectations of stakeholders who
influence or are affected by its operations. These stakeholders are customers, suppliers, governments, investors,
employees and local communities all of which are demanding more and more businesses prove to be
environmentally responsible (Freeman, 1984). In Green Supply Chain Management (GSCM) framework,
environmental initiatives go beyond operational gains to act as a publicity tool on how a firm is determined to be
involved in sustainable business practices. Green procurement and envi ronmentally conscious logistics and
waste minimization practices help firms to build greater trust with stakeholders, enhance corporate legitimacy,
and boost their reputation in the market (Dangelico and V ocalelli, 2017).
Stakeholder Theory suggests that organizations that proactively address environmental concerns are more likely
to build long-term relationships with key stakeholder groups, resulting in greater customer loyalty, stronger
supplier collaboration, improved investor confidence, a nd enhanced brand reputation. These intangibles play a
role in competitive advantage as they distinguish the firms in the ever more sustainability-conscious markets
(von Berlepsch et al., 2022). Neverthele ss, the expectations of the stakeholde rs and the impact that they have on
making decisions in organizations differ significantly in developing markets since not all markets are regulated
in the same way, the market is not so mature and th e society is not as aware of environmental pollution.
Therefore, it is fundamental to learn how the relationship between stakeholder pressures and internal
organizational capabilities works to explain how GSCM can promote competitive advantage in a variety of
institutional settings.
3.3 The Resource-Based View in the Stakeholder Theory in the Emerging Markets
Although the Resource-Based View (RBV) and Stakeholder Theory offer distinct explanations of organizational
performance, integrating the two perspectives provides a more comprehensive understanding of how Green
Supply Chain Management (GSCM) contributes to competitive advantage in emerging markets. The RBV
defines the idea of competitive advantage in an inte rnal context where it focuses on strategic value of
organizational capabilities whilst the Stakeholder Theory focuses on the need to respond to the expectations of
external stakeholders to gain legitimacy, trust, and lo ng-lasting business relationships. Combined, the theories
posit that sustainable competitive advantage is not merely  gained through the creation of green capabilities, but
with the ability to match these capabilities with the social and environmental requirements of important
stakeholders.
The effectiveness of this relationship, however, is in fluenced by the institutional characteristics of emerging
markets. The most common differences between the developed and emerging markets include uneven regulatory
enforcement, weak technological platforms, and limited financial facilities, as well as dissimilar consumer and
corporate environmental awareness. These contextual factors influence the actual practice of GSCM not only in
terms of the gains that firms are able to make out of pursuing green initiatives in order to create tangible
competitive advantages but also the way that such gains will be produced. As a result, even strong internal
capabilities do not ensure that firms can fulfill competitive advantages when the institutional support and
stakeholder engagement are low.
Based on these two complimentary ideas, this review fr ames GSCM as a strategic organizational competence

3.4 Conceptual Framework
The conceptual framework developed for this review  integrates the Resource -Based View (RBV) and
Stakeholder Theory to explain how Green Supply Chain Management (GSCM) practices contribute to
competitive advantage in emerging markets. The framework hypothesizes that, the green procurement,
eco-logistics and waste reduction all are strategic or ganizational capabilities that help firms to promote
operational efficiency, environmental impact reductions, an d add values to firms. In line with the RBV , these
capabilities lead to competitive advantage when effectively incorporated into organizational processes and they
are hard to replicate by the competitor.
The framework further posits that the relationship between GSCM practices and competitive advantage is
reinforced through two key mechanisms: cost reduction and brand reputation. The reduction of costs comes
through the better use of resources, minimization of waste, and, finally, better working efficiency, whereas better
brand reputation will be achieved through evidence of environmental responsibility and meeting the expectations
of stakeholders. The results enhance the strength of fi rms in the market as they  enhance customer loyalty,
attraction of environmentally sensitive investors, and long-term organizational performance.
Recognizing the unique characteristics of emerging mark ets, the framework also acknowledges that institutional
conditions influence the effectiveness of GSCM implemen tation. Quality of regulations, capability of suppliers,
infrastructure, access to financial resources are some of th e factors that define the capability of firms to develop
and utilize green capabilities. Similarly, the customer expectations, government-related policies, supplier-pass on,
as well as the investor expectations that affect the ability of environmental initiatives to translate into
competitive advantages. The integrated framework ther efore offers a theoretical framework on which the
literature reviewed can be synthesized and which points  to the differences in the relationship between GSCM
and competitive advantage in the emerging-market settings.

4. Method
4.1 Research Design
This study adopted a Systematic Literature Review (S LR) approach to synthesize empirical evidence on the
relationship between Green Supply Chain Management (GSCM) practices, mediating mechanisms, and
competitive advantage in emerging ma rkets. This type of approach wa s chosen because the SLR methodology
offers a systematic, transparent, and reproducible way of identifying, assessing, and synthesizing pertinent
research, thus reducing the risk of selection bias and maximizing the credibility of the findings in the reviews
(Tranfield et al., 2003). Systematic reviews use establishe d methods of literature identification, screening, and
analysis, unlike traditional narrative reviews, which allow applying more rigorous and comprehensive synthesis
to current evidence. The method helps to determine common patterns, areas of agreement and disagreement, and
gaps in the research throughout the literature. Therefore, the SLR approach is adequately appropriate in order to

4.2 Literature Search Strategy
A comprehensive literature search was conducted to identify peer-reviewed studies examining the relationship
between Green Supply Chain Management (GSCM) practi ces and competitive advantage in emerging markets.
Four prominent scholarly databases, Scopus, Web of Science, ScienceDirect and Emerald Insight, were chosen
due to the broadness of their coverage of quality resear ch in business, management, sustainability and supply
chain fields. Although the systematic search focused on empirical studies published between 2011 and 2025,
seminal theoretical and methodological publications publis hed before this period were retained where necessary
to establish the conceptual and methodological foundations of the study.
The search strategy was formulated based on the concep tual framework of the study and used a combination of
the keywords and Boolean operators in order to overcome the maximization of getting the relevant studies. The
principal search terms included "green  supply chain management," "sustainab le supply chain practices," "green
procurement," "eco-logistics," "waste reduction," "c ompetitive advantage," "firm performance," "emerging
markets," "lean-green integration," and "supply chain performance." These terms were combined using Boolean
operators such as AND and OR, together with truncation techniques (e.g., "green supply chain" AND competiti*
advantage*) to broaden or refine the search where appropriate. In order to enhance the thoroughness of the
review process, the referring lists of  the significant articles have additionally been reviewed manually to help
determine other useful studies that had not been found initially in the search of the database. As inclusion criteria,
only peer-reviewed journal articles in English that coul d be accessed through university library databases or a
good open-access source were included.
4.3 Study Selection and Inclusion/Exclusion Criteria
The study selection process followed a structured screen ing procedure based on article titles, abstracts, and
full-text reviews to ensure that only studies directly re levant to the research objectiv es were included. After the

relevance based on their titles and abstracts. Muscle on muscle reviews which passed the initial criterion of the

4.4 Limitations
While this is a systematic review, it remains limited by the quality and availability of  secondary studies. There
were differences in the study design, scales, and indust ry setting which limit generalizability. Furthermore, since
this article is a review article, bias  exists in the literature through published material that may have more
significant positive results. Overall, the method outlined represents a strong research conceptual framework to
explore trends, mechanisms and barriers to GSCM implem entation from an emerging market perspective, while
facing those challenges.
5. Results
5.1 GSCM Practices
GSCM practices offer mechanisms which can help companies gain environmental benefits as well as advance
operational performance. Eco-friendly procurement measures such as supplier auditing and the procurement of
eco-certified materials have contributed to the improvement of process efficiency and reduced risk in the supply
chain (Ahi & Searcy, 2013; Zhu et al., 2007). Analytical studies highlight that these practices can be considered
as a strategic capability that can help improve the competitiveness if it is supported by the routine of the firm and
is constantly monitored (Dubey et al., 2019). Research reveals that companies that integrate sustainability into
their procurement processes are able to  cut the costs of their operations and build their levels of trust and
compliance with environmental regulations among their st akeholders (Bhattacharya et al., 2019). However, in
emerging markets, factors like the shortage of infrastructure and suppliers' capability can affect the effectiveness
of green procurement, which shows the need for organizational dedication. There are comparative studies that
show that companies that have good environmental manage ment system are able to obtain higher efficiency in
their processes and supplier adherence, thus green procurement is considered a source of advantage for their
operations and image.

5.2 Eco-Friendly Logistics
Sustainable logistics solutions including route optimization, optimized group transport, and optimized fuel usage
during transportation lowers fleet costs and improve en vironmental impact. Analyti cal evidence has confirmed
that the inclusion of logistics sustainability in the overall supply chain management will result in tangible
reductions in fuel consumption and emission reductions , overall supply chain management and more reliable
service and quality (Gunasekaran, Subramanian, & Rahman, 2017; Kumar et al., 2011). Research has shown that
adopting e-cologistics practices affects infrastructure an d technological readiness; in developing countries,
companies may be constrained when f acing difficult conditions, such as limited access to green transportation
technologies, or a broken transportation system (Sánchez-Flores et al., 2020). Incorporation of logistics
sustainability concepts into both procurement and waste prevention efforts improves the competitive advantage
benefits and highlights the need for coordination between functions to achieve operational and environmental
efficiencies (Bhattacharya et al., 20 19). Research analytical comparison s show that those companies which
integrate 'eco-logistics' with monitoring and perfor mance evaluation have the gr eatest chance of not only
reducing costs but also positively influencing the perception of their stakeholders towards their company; thus
reinforcing the strategic role played by eco-conscious logistics in supply chain management.
5.3 Waste Reduction
Lean manufacturing, process optimization, and reverse logi stics are practices for waste reduction that help to
improve efficiency and meet regulatory requirements. Reverse logistics will help businesses get value from
end-of-life products, lower disposal expenses, and provide chances for resource reuse, thereby boosting
operational efficiency (Sánchez-Flores et al., 2020). Lean-green integration enhances material and energy waste
reduction, thus in turn increases productivity, which help organizations to achieve its competitive advantage (Zhu
et al., 2007; Bhattacharya et al., 20 19). The analytical results indicate that the effectiveness of the waste
reduction measures is dependent on the knowledge level of the employees, the commitment of management and
cross-functional coordination. Incorporating waste management within larger operational frameworks has
benefits both financially and environmentally, while addressing waste reduction in isolation may yield less
benefits as shown by Dubey and colleagues (2019). Other important barriers, however, are cited in emerging
market studies, including the ability of suppliers and scarci ty of resources, that affect the impact on competitive
advantage of waste reduction.
5.4 Mediating Mechanisms: Cost Reduction and Brand Reputation
Reduction in costs and the brand reputation act as intervening variables between GSCM practices and
competitive advantage. Cost reduction occurs as a result of sustainable procurement, eco-logistics, and waste
minimisation strategies which brings efficiency in the operation and enhances the potential achievement of cost
reduction by reduction in material and energy consumption (Bhattacharya et al., 2019; Dubey et al., 2019).
Analytical studies suggest the cost benefits depend not so much on individual initiatives, but on embedding
GSCM practices into the organizational process. The GSCM-competitive advantage relationship is realized
through the mediation of brand reputation, which affects stakeholder perceptions. Corporations with reliable
environmental practices are more likely to generate a loyal customer base, market distinction and investor trust
(von Berlepsch et al., 2022; Dangelico & V ocalelli, 2017). It has been found that the reputational benefits are
higher compared to consumer industries and industries that communicate their sustainability performance openly
and transparently. However, both mediators are dependent on the context: its effectiveness will be affected by the
characteristics of the industry, the knowledge of stakeholders and the ability of the organizations to introduce and
maintain environmental measures.
5.5 Contextual challenges in Emerging Markets
The structural, financial and institutional constraints within emerging market corporations have an impact on the
implementation and effectiveness of GSCM. Most of the constraints reported in terms of R&D are financial
(Hebaz & Oulfarsi, 2021), which include the high initial investment costs needed to embrace green technologies
and infrastructure. Regulatory enfor cement varies, and policies are not alwa ys around environmental measures
making it uncertain to managers what their most important investment should be (Dhillon et al., 2022). There are
organizational constraints such as weak leadership co mmitment and availability of less knowledgeable workers,
which is not conducive to internal coordination and diminishes the effect of sustainability investments (Kaur &
Awasthi, 2018). However, green procurement and wast e minimisation are still hampered by supplier issues,
including limited awareness or sustainability knowledge and expertise. Based on analytical comparisons,
agencies that manage to overcome these barriers and turn GS CM practices into benefits - such as in the field of
operation and reputation - are the ones that manage to connect internal capability with external conditions, but

also those that can involve their stakeholders effectively and integrate sustainability into their strategic principles.
From a standpoint of analytical comparison, firms that align their internal capabilities in line with their external
conditions and conditions, successfully involve their stakeholders and include sustainability in their principles of
strategy are the ones that have more chances of overco ming these barriers to turn investments in GSCM into
benefits, such as in the field of operation and reputa tion (Dangelico & V ocalelli, 2017; von Berlepsch et al.,

6. Discussion
The results obtained from this systematic review indicate that Green Supply Chain Management (GSCM)
becomes one of the important factors that contributes to the competitive advantage of emerging market
companies. Based on the analytical synthesis, author s conclude that the success of implementing GSCM will
have several limitations such as the strategic integration of GSCM, the organizational ability, and the context.
This concept of green procurement, green logistics, and elimination of waste, can help achieve sustainable
competitive advantage, as they are strategic resources that  are valuable, rare, and impossible to imitate, if they
are grounded in firm routines (Bar ney, 1991). While environmental programs are inherently project-based,
integrated GSCM practices enable companies, in addition to their other benefits, to tap into one or more of these
unique characteristics of GSCM to cut costs, manage risks be tter, and differentiate their products or services in a
competitive market. (Dubey et al., 2019; Bhattacharya, Nand, & Castka, 2019)
Green procurement is a good example that makes GSCM an effective strategic capability.For example, in regards
to green procurement, it is actually an illustration of how GSCM can be made a strategic capability.
Organizations that consider environmental factors and do so when choosing and managing their suppliers also
gain a better supply chain reliability and quality (Ahi & Searcy, 2013). According to analytical studies,
competitive benefits can be realised through connecti ng procurement with the organisation's processes and
utilising internal resources, including trained workforce and effective monitoring arrangements (Dubey et al.,
2019). For companies in emerging markets, supplier capacity and infrastructure constraints are found to inhibit
the effectiveness of green purchasing; companies need to gauge the ability to create sustainable supply chain
within their operational context (Zhu et al., 2007).
The same applies to eco-friendly logistics, which also connects environmental, operational and reputational
impacts. A key component of service reliability is servi ce level stability, which transl ates into expected cost
benefits and lower carbon emissions through route optimization and using energy-efficient transport modes, thus
further feeding the conviction of RBV that sustainability can be a competitive asset for firms (Gunasekaran,
Subramanian, & Rahman, 2017). But in developing co untries, access to green technology is limited, and
infrastructure restrictions limit possible advantages, re minding one that competitive advantage is situational.
Bhattacharya et al. (2019) and Sá nchez-Flores et al. (2 020) through analytical synthesis recommend using
eco-logistics in combination with green procurement and waste reduction in order to show that there is a
synergistic effect of integrated GSCM strategies.
Lean manufacturing and reverse logistics fall under waste reduction practices, which help in both cost reduction
and compliance to regulations. Other literature reveals that strategic integration benefits firms by not only
reducing waste, but also adding value to waste products as they enter the end-of-life stage, thus providing both
economic and environmental benefits (Sánchez-Flores et al ., 2020; Bhattacharya et al ., 2019). Importantly, the
analytical evidence shows that practi ces need to be aligned, trained into the employees and managed by
management to get measurable competitive advantage; ot herwise, these practices ha ve only a minimal impact
(Dubey et al., 2019).
Another aspect highlighted by the review is the interm ediary mechanisms, namely the cost reduction and the
reputation of the brand, as drivers of the transformation of GSCM pr actices into competitive advantage.
Reduction in cost is achieved through operational efficiency, energy savings, and minimization of wastage;
allowing firms to invest in strategic initiatives to boost financial performance (Bhattacharya et al., 2019; Dubey
et al., 2019). For brand-based advantages, brand reputation boosts stakeholder trust and market differentiation,
especially in consumer related industries where envi ronmental performance matters in the decision-making
process (von Berlepsch et al., 2022; Dangelico & V ocalelli,  2017). Analytical synthesis uncovers that these are
dependent on the context; their volume depends on the industry, the firm size, the knowledge among
stakeholders, and regulation enforcement. For example, in cases where there is greater visibility and resources of
a large firm, they can better apply the benefits of reputa tion, while smaller firms are more likely to see tangible
benefits in lower costs.
Emerging market situation provides a set of particular issues which affect the adoption and performance of

GSCM. Regulatory uncertainty, financial constraints and limited infrastructure were consistently reported as
constraints faced when evaluating the full competitive advantage gained (Hebaz & Oulfarsi, 2021; Dhillon et al.,
2022). Also playing a significant role is organizationa l aspects, where leadership commitment, employee skills,
and knowledge of sustainability practices are important drivers of outcomes (Kaur & Awasthi, 2018). Across
studies, attention is drawn to the fact that the best outcomes stem from firms that are able to integrate internal
abilities with external environmental factors, including regulations and expectations of stakeholders. This
indicates that competitive advantage is dependent on the match between firm strategy, resources and
environmental context, and depends on competitive advantage being achieved through GSCM.
Managers will find that strategic integration and capability development are critical from the findings. In order to
maximize the benefits of GSCM practices, investments should be made in supplier development, in the redesign
of internal processes, and in training. Communicating sustainability plans and measuring and monitoring cost
efficiencies can magnify good reputation and improve operational efficiencies. Furthermore, the managers need
to be aware of and ready to adapt to the constraints applicable to the emerging market, including a phased
implementation or the willingness to invest in the green dimensions in a limited and targeted manner in line with
capacity or infrastructure in the firm (Bhattacharya et al., 2019; Gunasekaran et al., 2017).
Lastly, the paper identifies gaps that can arise in future  research, such as longitudinal research to evaluate
long-term impacts of performance, inter-industry comparison and understanding the potential of digital
technologies to increase efficiency and scalability of GSCM practices. New empirical evidence of the effects of
the contextual conditions, mediators, and integration strategies on the outcomes of the firm is needed to better
understand how these elements intersect to improve the firm and competitive outcomes (von Berlepsch et al.,
2022).
Although the reviewed studies collectively support a positive relationship between Green Supply Chain
Management (GSCM) and competitive advantage, the evid ence indicates that this relationship varies across
different types of emerging markets. Companies in relatively industrialized economies like China tend to have
better regulatory enforcement, established supplier base  and are more technologically prepared and can have
more comprehensive implementation of GSCM practices. Conversely, in most African and South Asian
economies, organizations are often cash strapped, have substandard infrastructure, cannot enforce environmental
regulations, and have weak supplier base, which could hamper implementation and performance of green
projects. In a similar vein, the levels of institutional support and market demand of environmentally sustainable
products in the Latin American as well as the Middle East settings differ and hence cause discrepancies in
priorities of implementation and performance results. Th ese regional differences suggest that the competitive
benefits of GSCM are highly context dependent and reinfo rce the need to consider institutional conditions when
interpreting empirical findings and developing strategies for sustainable supply chain management in emerging
markets.
Effective implementation of GSCM also requires st rong commitment from top management to integrate
sustainability objectives into corporate strategy and organizational culture. Companies ought to re-examine and
re-design internal operations to make sure that enviro nmental targets get integrated into the procurement,
production, logistical, and performance measurement systems. Building supplier capability through training,
information sharing, and collaborative partnerships can further strengthen the adoption of sustainable supply
chain practices, particularly where supplier expertise is limited. Collectively, these activities allow organizations
to go beyond individual environmental efforts to strategically aligned GSCM practices resulting in a long-lasting
competitive advantage.
7. Conclusion
This systematic review shows that the implementation of Green Supply Chain Management (GSCM) in order to
pursue a competitive advantage is vital for companies from emerging markets (EM) and involves three main
aspects of GSCM: green procurement,  green logistics and waste minimization. The above-mentioned practices
are shown to be valuable, rare and hard to imitate by the competitors and help in operational efficiency, risk
mitigation and differentiation via Analytical Synthesis (A S), which correlates the pr actices to the Resource
Based View of Barney (1991) and Bhattacharya et al. (2019). The paper also shows that integration of GSCM
practices in firm routine, appropriateness of orga nizational capabilities and top management commitment are
most effective in the success of GSCM practices. In ma ny cases, other stand-alone efforts do not deliver any
competitive advantage, stressing the need for strategic embedding in the value chain.
Wealth of literature has focused on discussing the processes involved in achieving a competitive advantage from
GSCM, including cost reduction and brand reputation. Mediating mechanisms like cost reduction and  brand

reputation are contigen mechanisms of achieving competitive advantage from GSCM. Cost reduction is achieved
by energy-saving production, by optimal use of material, by reduction of material waste, which strengthens
financial results and also helps to meet environmental requirements (Dubey et al., 2019; Kumar et al., 2011). The
involvement of brand reputation increases the impact on competitive advantage resulting from perceived brand,
customer loyalty loyalty and market differentiation, espe cially in companies where sustainability is one of the
factors that affect customers or investors decisions to buy or invest (von Berlepsch et al., 2022).
Financial constraints, regulatory uncertainty, limited infrastructure, and broken supplier relationships are some
contextual challenges that hinder th e success of emerging market firms (D hillon et al., 2022; Hebaz & Oulfarsi,
2021). There are analytical reasons wh y firms that match their internal re sources to the market conditions and
create ‘added value' for the actors are more likely to b ecome more profitable through the application of GSCM
and also to have a better image. This review reinforces the notion that GSCM is more of a strategic tool than a
compliance obligation and depends on the ownership of firms, commitment of stakeholders and the environment.
Lastly, the study pinpoints future research opportunities in the field of longitudinal assessment, cross-industry
comparisons and developing the field of digital technology integration that will deepen the sustainability
understanding of how GSCM practices lead to sustainable competitive advantage in emerging markets.
Future research should also examine the role of emerging digital technologies in strengthening Green Supply
Chain Management (GSCM) implementation in emerging markets. Artificial intelligence and blockchain,
Internet of Things (IoT), big data analysis, and digital supply chains can be used to increase visibility of the
supply chain, enhance environmental monitoring, enable  real-time decision-making,  and increase supply chain
partners cooperation. Although they are increasingly gaining importance, there is very limited empirical evidence
of the interactions between these technologies and GSCM practices in resource-constrained environments within
emerging markets. Future research should thus examine the aspect of how digital transformation can support the
GSCM initiatives and contribute to sustainable competitive advantage in various institutional settings.
