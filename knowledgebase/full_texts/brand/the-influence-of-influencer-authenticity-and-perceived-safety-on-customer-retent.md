 Abstract:  The growth of the beauty industry i n Indonesia
and the high tendency of brand switching demonstrate the
importance of understanding factors that can retain
consumers. This study aims to analyze the influence of
influencer authenticity and perceived safet y on customer
retention through emoti onal engagement and brand
attachment among Emina users in Sidoarjo Regency. The
study used an associative quantitative approach with a
five-point Likert scale questionnaire. The study population
was Emina users in Sukod ono and Taman Districts, with
129 res pondents selected through purposive sampling.
Data were analyzed using descriptive analysis and Partial
Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM)
with SmartPLS. The results showed that influencer
authenticity significantly influenced emotional
engagement, brand attachment, and customer retention.
Perceived safety significantly influenced emotional
engagement and brand attachment, but not directly on
customer retention. Brand attachment mediated both
relationships, while emotional engagement did n ot. In
conclusion, brand attachment is a stronger retention
mechanism than emotional engagement.

Introduction
Industrial development in the era of globalization is increasingly influenced by technology,
innovation, and digitalization. The industrial sector remains a crucial sector in the global economy,
contributing approximately 21.3% to globa l GDP by 2023 (Organization, 2024). Digitalization also
encourages companies to adapt to changing markets and consumer behavior (Forum, 2024).
In Indonesia, the beauty industry is growing rapidly in line with increasing purchasing power,
changing lifestyles, and the use of digital technology. The beauty industry has a market value of US$9.17

A problem that arises is the high tendency for brand switching, especially among teenagers and
Generation Z, who actively explore various products. The desire to seek new variations, or variety
seeking, drives consumers to try other brands that are deemed more suitable (Sari et al., 2024).
Furthermore, e-WOM in the form of online reviews, testimonials, and recommendations also influences
consumer perceptions and decisions (Musnaini & Wijoyo, 2021).
This situation also occurs at Emina, a local skincare brand targeting young consumers. Emina
consumers con sider not only product quality but are also influenced by digital information, online
reviews, and brand communications. This makes consumer loyalty dynami c and potentially shifting
when competitors emerge with more attractive marketing strategies (Hutagaol et al., 2022).
Another issue relates to influencer authenticity and perceived safety in building consumer
relationships with brands. Influencers perceived as authentic can increase consumer engagement with a
brand (Hasan et al., 2024), while perceived sa fety is an important consideration in choosing skincare
products because consumers are concerned about product safety and potential risks (Haykal et al., 2025;
Nattassha et al., 2024). However, research combining these two factors with emotional engagement and
brand attachment as mechanisms for customer retention is still limited, particularly among young
consumers and local skincare brands in non-metropolitan areas such as Sidoarjo Regency.
This study aims to analyze the influence of influencer authenticit y and perceived safety on
customer retention through emotional engagement and brand attachment on Emina products (a case
study of Emina product users in Si doarjo). This research is important to understand the factors that
support consumer retention amidst the beauty industry competition and brand switching. Its novelty lies
in the integrated testing of influencer authenticity and perceived safety on customer  retention through
emotional engagement and brand attachment on Emina users in Sidoarjo.

Research methods
The research data were obtained through a questionnaire using a Likert scale of 1 to 5. The
instrument measures five variables: Influencer Authentici ty, Perceived Safety, Emotional
Engagement, Brand Attachment, and Customer Retention. Influencer Authentici ty is measured
through honesty, personal experience, connectedness, and natural communication (Audrezet et al.,
2020), while Perceived Safety is meas ured through compliance with regulations and certifications,
information transparency, credibility of infor mation sources, brand reputation and image, and
product usage experience (Gamage & Mahagamage, 2024). Emotional Engagement uses indicators
of positiv e feelings, emotional attachment, and enthusiasm for the brand (Ghaffari et al., 2024),
Brand Attachment refers to consumers’ emotional attachment to a brand, which is formed through
positive experiences, satisfaction, and trust in the quality and benefits of the brand, thereby increasing
consumer commitment and the tendency to repurchase (Chen, 2020) , while Customer Retention is
measured through loyalty, repeat purchases, and prioritizing the product (Ricadonna et al., 2021).
The data analysis technique used descriptive analysis and path analysis with the Partial Least
Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM) approach through SmartPLS software. Path
analysis was used to test the direct and indirect effects and the role of Emotional Engagement and
Brand Attachment as mediating variables. PLS-SEM was used to test the relationships between latent
variables, construct validity and reliability, and models involving mediating variables (Sugiyono,
2019; Hair Jr et al., 2021).
The study population was Emina p roduct users in Sidoarjo Regency, including teenagers,
students, and young women who used Emina products an d had been exposed to influencer
promotions on social media. The sample focused on Emina users in Sukodono and Taman Districts.
The sample size was determined using statistical power analysis with the help of G*Power, with an
effect size of 0.15, a signifi cance level of 0.05, a power of 0.95, and four predictors, resulting in a

Sampling was conducted using a non -probability sampling technique through a purposive
sampling approach (Bagus Sumargo et al., 2024). Respondent criteria included Emina users who had
used the product at least twice in the past six months, had seen Emina promotional content through
influencers on Instagram or TikTok, were domiciled in Sidoarjo Regency, specifically Sukodono and
Taman Districts, and were aged 15 to 25 years.
The research was conducted from April to July 2026 in Sukodono and Taman Districts,
Sidoarjo Regency. The research process bega n with determining the research object and variables,
establishing criteria and sample size, and then devel oping an instrument based on the indicators of
each variable. Next, a questionnaire was distributed to eligible respondents via Google Forms.
Respondents provided answers based on their perceptions and experiences with Emina products. The
collected data was then selected, processed, and statistically analyzed to test the research hypotheses.

Discussion
1. The Influence of Influencer Authenticity on Emotional Engagement
Influencer Authenticity has a positive and significant effect on Emotional Engagement (β =
0.211; T = 2.510; P = 0.012). The more authentic the influencer, the higher the consumer's emotional
engagement with Emina. This finding supports Source Credibility Theory and Stimulus-Organism-
Response Theory and is in line with Deng et al. (2025).
2. The Influence of Perceived Safety on Emotional Engagement
Perceived Safetyhas a positive and significant effect on Emotional Engagement (β = 0.261; T =
3.118; P = 0.002). Higher perceived security increases consumers' sense of comfort and emotional
involvement with Emina. This finding supports Stimulus -Organism-Response Theory and
Customer Relationship Management Theory and is in line with Widianto & Ginting (2025).
3. The Influence of Influencer Authenticity on Brand Attachment
Influencer Authenticityhas a positive and significant effect on Brand Attachment (β = 0.394; T
= 5.297; P < 0.001) . Influencer authenticit y increases consumer trust and attachment to Emina.
These results support Source Credibility Theory and are in line with Abdul Aziz et al. (2023).
4. The Influence of Perceived Safety on Brand Attachment
Perceived Safetyhas a positive and significant effect on Brand Attachment (β = 0.249; T = 3.013;
P = 0.003). Perceived security creates a sense of trust and positive experiences that strengthen
consumer attachment to Emina. These results are in line with Verma (2024) and Fitri & Kumenaung
(2025).
5. The Influence of Emotional Engagement on Customer Retention
Emotional Engagementhas a positive and significant effect on customer retention (β = 0.218; T
= 2.088; P = 0.037). The stronger the consumer's emotional involvement, the greater the ten dency
to maintain Emina usage. These results support Customer Engagement Theory and Customer
Relationship Management Theory and are in line with Wilujeng (2024).

Conclusion
This study found that influencer authenticity has a positive and significant relationsh ip with
emotional engagement, brand attachment, and customer retention among Emina users. Perceived safety
is positively and significantly associated with emotional engagement and brand attachment, but its direct
relationship with customer retention is not  statistically significant. Emotional engagement and brand
attachment are both positively and significantly associated with customer retention. Regarding indirect
relationships, brand at tachment significantly mediates the relationships of influencer authen ticity and
perceived safety with customer retention, whereas emotional engagement does not significantly mediate
either relationship. These findings highlight the relevance of both emoti onal engagement and brand
attachment in understanding customer retenti on, although the results do not establish that one
mechanism is stronger than the other. Practically, Emina should maintain authentic and transparent
influencer communication, provide cl ear and reliable information about product safety, and create
positive brand experiences to foster consumer engagement and attachment.
This study has limitations concerning its geographical scope and sample characteristics. The
respondents were limited to 129 Emina users aged 15–25 years from Sukodono and Taman Districts in
Sidoarjo Regency. Therefore, the findings may not be generalizable to all Emina users or consumers in
other regions. In addition, the model explains 44.2% of the variance in customer ret ention, indicating
that other factors may also contribute to retention . Future research should involve larger and more
geographically diverse samples and consider additional variables, such as brand trust, perceived quality,
customer satisfaction, brand experience, and electronic word of mouth. Comparative studies involving
Emina and other local skincare brands may also provide broader insights into the factors associated with
customer retention.
