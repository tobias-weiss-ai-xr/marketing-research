ABSTRACT
This research aims to investigate the influence of key brand elements, including brand identity, brand storytelling,
product aesthetics, brand symbolism, and brand name and reputation, on the purchase behaviour of young
Chinese consumers, with a focus on Forbidden City cultural creative products. It also examines the mediating
effect of emotional attachment to provide a more integrated explanation of consumer brand relations in cultural
branding. Data were obtained from 354 respondents in Beijing using a standardized survey adapted from
established studies. Descriptive statistics were generated in SPSS, while the hypothesized relationships were tested
using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modelling in SmartPLS. The measurement model was assessed
through indicator loadings, internal consistency, convergent validity, and discriminant validity, and the structural
model was evaluated through bootstrapped path estimates for direct and indirect effects. The findings show
that all five brand elements exert significant positive effects on purchase behaviour. Emotional attachment also
mediates each of these relationships, showing that affective responses are an important mechanism through which
culturally embedded brand cues shape consumer action. The results support the Stimulus Organism Response
model by demonstrating that brand stimuli activate emotional attachment, which in turn influences purchase
behaviour. This paper contributes to the literature by advancing a cultural branding explanation of Chinese youth
consumption behaviour and by offering managerial implications for museums and cultural organizations seeking
to design emotionally resonant heritage-based products.

1. INTRODUCTION
Over the past few years, China's cultural and creative
industries have witnessed a massive boom, particularly
as consumer demand increases for products embodying
national heritage and artistic expression. This is fuelled
by enhancing cultural confidence among the younger
generations and the government's strategic focus on
advocating traditional Chinese culture as a tool of soft
power (Arantes, 2024). One of the most successful cases
is the Forbidden City, or the Palace Museum, which
has turned its cultural symbols of the past into saleable
creative merchandise appealing to a contemporary
audience (Fan & Kanai, 2023). From stationery and
accessories to domestic goods and cosmetics, these
products have become extremely popular among
young Chinese people because they creatively
blend historical components with modern design
(Barker et al., 2024). The emergence of such cultural
commodities is a shift from traditional tourism souvenirs
toward lifestyle branding based on national identity.
According to scholars, the cultural branding of China
has taken the shape of a hybrid model that bridges
consumerism with cultural renaissance, emotionalism,
and aesthetics (Griffiths, 2024). With the increasing
popularity of cultural creative product consumption, it is
essential to explore the psychological and behavioural
aspects that influence young consumers' preferences,
particularly how specific brand attributes impact
purchase behaviours in rich cultural settings, such as the
Forbidden City brand (Diaz Ruiz & Cruz, 2023).
There is a large amount of empirical work that has
examined the impact of brand strategies on customer
behaviour, most notably how various components
of brands result in customers' perceptions, emotional
responses, and purchasing intentions (Ahmadi & Ataei,

is commonly seen to act as a mediating variable
that encapsulates brand perception and consumer
reaction, affirming its core status in consumer-brand
relationships.
Although a rich literature involving the study of
branding and consumer behaviour exists, specific
research gaps continue to persist, especially in China's
cultural creative industries. First, most current studies
tend to analyse brand components in a separable
fashion, with few providing a comprehensive view that
considers multiple dimensions, such as brand identity,
storytelling, aesthetics, symbolism, and reputation,
simultaneously (Hong et al., 2023; Nguyen-Viet, 2023;
Tsou & Putra, 2023). Therefore, there is little knowledge
on how these components of the brand influence
each other and their combined impact on consumer
behaviour in culture-embedded markets (Ihemereze
et al., 2023). Furthermore, while emotional attachment
has been identified as a major mediator in marketing
psychology, few research studies have explored
its mediating role in how specific brand attributes
influence purchasing behaviour in cultural product
consumption (Mohamed & Adiloglu, 2023; Jahan et
al., 2024; Gautam, 2025). Current research also relies
excessively on Western branding paradigms without
adequately situating consumer reactions within Chinese
sociocultural contexts shaped by distinctive historical
values, Confucian moral principles, and a strong sense
of cultural heritage. Therefore, there is a lack of theory-
based but culturally tailored explanations for how
perceptions about brands influence consumer choices
within China's expanding market for cultural creative
products.
In addition, though the Forbidden City brand has
emerged as a symbol of China's success at cultural
branding, scholarly research into its impact on consumer
behaviour, particularly among younger generations of
Chinese consumers, is still relatively unexamined (Hao,
2024). Much existing research has emphasized the
brand's artistic or commercial achievement instead of
analysing the psychological processes behind youth
participation and consumption (Ng & Li, 2023). There is
a discernible scarcity of empirical models charting the
connection between brand elements and consumer
purchasing behaviour based on emotional affiliation.
Furthermore, although the younger generation is
regarded as the key consumer market for such products,
there is a lack of empirical evidence that records how
Chinese youth translate and emotionally relate to
culturally based brand elements (Zhu et al., 2025). This
is important as young consumers in China are facing
a confluence of cultural pride and consumerism,
frequently wanting products that embody both heritage
and individuality (Zhang et al., 2023). Bridging these
gaps will provide not only scholarly impact but also
real-world guidance to cultural institutions and creative
businesses seeking to create emotionally engaging and
culturally appropriate branding approaches.
Despite growing work on brand elements and consumer
behaviour, the literature still lacks a theory-driven
explanation of how culturally loaded brand cues are
converted into purchase behaviour within heritage-
based consumption. Existing branding studies usually
examine identity, symbolism, design, or narrative
separately and are largely developed in generic
commercial settings, which makes it difficult to explain
how these cues work together when a brand derives
meaning from history, collective memory, and national
culture (Fournier & Alvarez, 2019; Li et al., 2021; Huang et
al., 2023). Emotional attachment is frequently treated as
a positive brand outcome, yet it is less clearly specified
as the internal process through which cultural meaning
becomes behavioural action. This gap is important
because heritage products are evaluated not only as
market offerings but also as carriers of symbolic and
identity-related value, especially for younger consumers
who use domestic cultural brands to express both taste
and cultural belonging (Jin et al., 2018; Qin et al., 2019).
Accordingly, the unresolved issue addressed by this
study is whether multiple brand elements can influence
purchase behaviour through a common affective
pathway when the brand is culturally and historically
embedded. The present study contributes in three
ways. First, it moves beyond a single cue approach
by modelling brand identity, storytelling, product
aesthetics, symbolism, and brand name and reputation
within one framework. Second, it specifies emotional
attachment not as a peripheral outcome but as the
organism-level process that translates cultural brand
stimuli into consumer response. Third, by focusing on

scholarly literature and pragmatic branding approaches
in China's fast-changing cultural and creative industries.

how brand factors combine to drive consumer

results will provide valuable insights for brand managers,

the outcomes of this research could have implications
for strategies for promoting the attractiveness and

aims of cultural heritage conservation, national identity
creation, and sustainable development of creative
industries in China.
2. LITERATURE REVIEW
Research on cultural branding suggests that brands
create value not only through functional performance
but also through their ability to condense cultural
meanings into recognizable forms that consumers
can incorporate into everyday life (Fournier & Alvarez,
2019). In the case of Chinese youth consumption, this
issue is especially salient because domestic brands
and culturally grounded products are often evaluated
through symbolic value, pride, and self-expression
rather than utility alone (Jin et al., 2018; Qin et al.,
2019). However, prior studies remain fragmented. Some
emphasize product design and cultural properties,
others focus on perceived value or cultural features,
and others stress symbolic meaning or narrative
interpretation. As a result, the literature has not yet shown
clearly how the main brand elements work together
in heritage branding, nor how they are translated into
behavioural outcomes through emotional attachment
(Li et al., 2021; Huang et al., 2023; Liu & Zhao, 2024).
This study addresses that gap by integrating the major
brand elements into one model and treating emotional
attachment as the key internal process linking cultural
branding to purchase behaviour.
2.1 Cultural Branding and Youth Consumption
Brand identity has a significant influence on consumer
buying behaviour since it is the face and character
of a brand, providing hints at its values, heritage, and
uniqueness. In cultural creative products, for instance,
products from the Forbidden City, brand identity
will include visual symbols, conventional motifs, and
historical myths that all symbolize the cultural authenticity
of the product (Yu et al., 2024). A robust brand identity
promotes trust and recognition, which are critical in
attracting and retaining consumers, especially among
young people seeking meaningful relationships with the
products they buy (Nguyen-Viet, 2023). Existing research
has established that brand identity impacts consumer
preferences in significant ways through the boost of
perceived credibility and emotional connection (Diaz
Ruiz & Cruz, 2023). If young consumers view a brand as
rich in culture and authentic, they are likely to identify
with it and have higher purchase intentions (Hong et
al., 2023). Further, cultural branding that resonates with
national identity and pride reinforces the effect of brand
identity on behavioural outcomes more powerfully,
and thus brand identity becomes an effective driver of
consumption in Chinese markets where historical pride
and cultural continuity are highlighted (Liu et al., 2023).
H1: Brand identity has a significant impact on purchase
behaviour.
2.2 Brand Elements and Consumer Behaviour
Brand storytelling is the second imperative element
affecting purchase behaviour by establishing narratives
that customers can connect with emotionally. Great
storytelling not only shares the brand's history, purpose,
and values but also enables customers to imprint their
own experiences on the brand's narrative (Kemp et al.,

2023). For Forbidden City cultural products, narratives
that incorporate stories of emperors, life at court, ancient
relics, or traditional celebrations may appeal profoundly
to young Chinese people in search of authentic and
culturally grounded consumption experiences (Kaur
et al., 2023). Empirical findings indicate that engaging
brand narratives promote emotional connection,
improve memory, and result in a more intimate
brand-consumer relationship (Ihemereze et al., 2023).
Storytelling is also used to influence consumer attitudes
towards product value and authenticity, particularly
where it is rooted in historical accuracy and cultural
depth (Chopra et al., 2024). The narrative style turns
the product into something symbolic and imbued with
cultural meaning, thus encouraging consumers to buy
it (Mohamed & Adiloglu, 2023). Consequently, brand
storytelling not only improves brand image but has
a direct, measurable impact on consumer purchase
behaviour.
H2: Brand storytelling has a significant impact on
purchase behaviour.
The aesthetic appeal of products has consistently
been found to affect consumer purchase behaviour,
especially in markets where design, style, and sensory
experience hold premium value. In Chinese youth, who
are also attracted to visually appealing and expressive
products, the aesthetic presentation of cultural creative
products is a key driver of their purchase intentions
(Arantes, 2024). Features such as elaborate packaging,
refined calligraphy, balanced colour schemes, and
symbolic images are frequently applied in Forbidden
City products to articulate traditional beauty and
elegance (Zhang et al., 2023). These design attributes
do more than merely draw attention; they convey
cultural values, express craftsmanship, and increase
the perceived quality of the product (Zhu et al., 2025).
Consumers are more inclined to buy products that
they feel are beautiful, particularly when that beauty
is consistent with their self-concept and lifestyle choices
(Griffiths, 2024). To younger generations, the beauty of
cultural products is a means of demonstrating individual
identity and cultural pride (Kemp et al., 2023). Therefore,
the aesthetics of products are a strategic means of
evoking emotional feelings and influencing consumer
choice in the market of cultural products.
H3: Product aesthetics has a significant impact on
purchase behaviour.
Brand symbolism is the application of symbols or
imagery imbued with cultural or emotional value that
signifies meanings transcending physical attributes of
a product and functioning as an effective mechanism
of shaping consumer behaviour (Bas, 2024). In cultural
creative industries, especially those featuring heritage-
inspired products such as the Forbidden City products,
symbolic motifs like imperial dragons, phoenixes,
palace layout, and ancient relics transmit profound
historical connotations and shared memory (Chen et
al., 2024). These symbols strike a chord among young
Chinese consumers who aim to convey cultural pride
and identity through what they buy (Júnior et al., 2023).
As Yu et al. (2023) explain, symbolic brand assets build
brand equity by creating positive, strong, and distinct
associations within the mind of the consumer. If this
symbolism is perceived to be a sign of social status or
cultural continuity, it provokes an emotional bond that
may create brand attachment and loyalty (Habib et al.,
2024). The semiotic depth of Forbidden City products
enables them to be not only consumer items but also
carriers of tradition and cultural narratives. This attitude
profoundly influences how young consumers assess and
select products, especially in cultures where heritage
and symbolism are of great social importance (Laradi
et al., 2024).
H4: Brand symbolism has a significant impact on
purchase behaviour.
Brand name and reputation are important intangible
assets that play a crucial role in influencing consumer
decision-making processes, especially in competitive
industries with a greater interest in authenticity and trust
(Liu et al., 2023). A recognized brand name associated
with previous credibility, cultural standing, and
consistent quality acts as a heuristic cue to consumer
judgment (Meng et al., 2024). Regarding Forbidden
City cultural products, the identification of the brand
with one of China's most well-known heritage sites
enhances perceived legitimacy and aesthetic value,
which results in higher consumer trust and purchasing
willingness (Suttikun & Mahasuweerachai, 2023).
Empirical evidence indicates that consumers are likely to
employ brand reputation to reduce the risk of purchase
and develop an opinion about product quality and
authenticity (Kemp et al., 2023). For young Chinese, who
are simultaneously influenced by trendy consumption
habits and nationalist feelings, a popular brand with a
firm name rooted in history can satisfy both affective and
cognitive needs (Fan & Kanai, 2023). A good image of
the brand is responsible for greater perceived value and
social recognition, leading to higher purchase intentions
and word-of-mouth communication.
H5: Brand name and reputation have a significant
impact on purchase behaviour.
2.3 Emotional Attachment in Marketing
Emotional attachment has been characterized as an
essential concept for understanding how and why
consumers form enduring relationships with brands.
It refers to the affective bond that develops when
consumers perceive a brand as personally relevant
and emotionally resonant (Zhang, 2023). In the case of

cultural creative goods such as those originating from
the Forbidden City, emotional attachment becomes
particularly pertinent because of the combination of
cultural pride, aesthetic taste, symbolic meaning, and
brand status (Gautam, 2025). Research has established
that brand identity is critical to emotional attachment
by developing a familiar and culturally rooted image
with which consumers can identify (Na et al., 2024). As
consumers perceive that a brand’s identity is closely
linked with their own or their nation’s values, they
are more likely to form emotional attachment, which
in turn strengthens purchase behaviour (Suttikun &
Mahasuweerachai, 2023). In addition, storytelling
has emerged as a powerful tool for branding, not just
enhancing the emotional tone of the brand but also
affecting the consumer's identification with the world
of the brand story (Wang et al., 2025). Specifically,
storytelling grounded in historical stories, imperial
beauty, and Chinese heritage creates a shared cultural
experience, which in turn evokes a sense of closeness
and strengthens purchase intentions (Zhang, 2023).
Aesthetics are also a major driver of emotional
connection, particularly among young consumers who
appreciate design, beauty, and sensory experience
in their consumption behaviours. Aesthetics research
shows that aesthetic experiences bring about feelings
such as joy, admiration, and inspiration, which can
create consumers' emotional connections to a brand
(Zhang & Rau, 2023). In Forbidden City products, visually
appealing attributes such as intricate calligraphy,
traditional motifs, and harmonious colour combinations
not only catch the eye but also generate emotional
responses that bridge the gap between visual appeal
and purchase decision (Yan et al., 2024). In addition,
brand symbolism serves as a mediating variable
in creating affective connections since consumers
decode brand symbols to represent social or cultural
identity (Pang & Zhang, 2024). Symbolic communications
of brand strategy, such as heritage themes or historical
symbols, serve as emotion anchors linking the product
with personal or collective memory (Mamun et al., 2023).
These links are likely to enhance affective relationships,
which in turn create stronger behavioural outcomes like
loyalty and increased purchase intentions. Symbolic
value, therefore, contributes to the emotional meaning
of a product, making it more than a commodity but a
vessel for cultural identity (Yan et al., 2024).
Brand name and reputation also function as trust
signals, influencing emotional responses through
repetition of what consumers believe about brand
reliability, heritage, and worthiness of customers (Yan
et al., 2024). An effective brand name, particularly
one with national heritage or institutionally supported
authority, provides trust and pride, which forms a more
powerful consumer-brand relationship (Yan et al., 2024).
For Forbidden City products, the brand name itself
embodies historical grandeur and authenticity, thereby
enhancing emotional trust and mitigating the impact
of reputation on purchasing behaviours (Jahan et al.,
2024). Also, evidence suggests that consumers are more
emotionally connected to brands that they perceive
as prestigious and trustworthy, and the connection
has a high effect on purchasing intentions (Liu et al.,
2023). Consequently, emotional attachment is more
than a byproduct of brand exposure but an essential
psychological bond whereby various aspects of a brand
identity, storytelling, beauty, symbolism, and reputation
are translated into consumer purchasing behaviour.
Taken together, prior research supports the importance
of brand identity, narrative, design, and symbolism, but
it also reveals an unresolved tension. Heritage products
are simultaneously consumed as aesthetically pleasing
objects, culturally meaningful symbols, and identity-
expressive goods. This means that the direct influence
of brand elements cannot be fully understood unless
the emotional process linking cultural meaning to
behavioural intention is specified. The present study
resolves this tension by jointly modelling the main brand
elements and testing emotional attachment as the
mechanism through which cultural branding becomes
purchasing action.

2.4 Theoretical Framework Supporting the Research
The Stimulus Organism Response model is particularly
appropriate for heritage branding because the stimuli
in this context are not limited to functional product
cues. Heritage brands present historically layered signs,
such as narratives, symbols, institutional reputation, and
aesthetic forms, that invite emotional interpretation
before behavioural judgment. The original model
proposes that environmental stimuli shape internal
states, which then guide approach-related responses
(Mehrabian & Russell, 1974). In branding research,
emotional attachment provides a conceptually precise
organism-level state because it captures the strength
of the bond between a consumer and a brand rather
than a momentary evaluation alone (Thomson et al.,

2005). In heritage settings, cultural symbolism is likely to
intensify this process because symbols carry collective
meanings that heighten self-relevance and identity
congruence. This is especially salient for identity-
based brands, where attachment becomes stronger
when consumers perceive alignment between brand
meaning and the self (Malär et al., 2011). For Chinese
cultural creative products, traditional symbols and
culturally embedded design have been shown to
activate cultural perceptions, emotional value, and
purchase intention, suggesting that heritage cues
operate as stronger affective stimuli than ordinary
commercial signals (Qin et al., 2019; Liu & Zhao, 2024).
Derived from environmental psychology (Mehrabian &
Russell, 1974), the S-O-R model posits that an organism's
internal affective and cognitive state (O) is influenced
by external stimuli (S), such as brand elements, that in
turn influence behavioural responses (R). In the context
of this research, brand identity, brand storytelling,
product aesthetics, brand symbolism, and brand
name and reputation function as stimuli that shape
consumers’ emotional attachment to the brand, which
in turn influences purchase behaviour. Past studies
have established the relevance of the S-O-R model
in marketing and brand contexts, where emotional
processes act as the mediating process between
brand cues and consumer actions (Mosteller et al.,
2014; Kim & Lee, 2023; Zhang et al., 2024). For instance,
brand identity as a stimulus provides a known image
that taps into consumer values, arousing emotional
affection, which consequently drives purchase
behaviour (Pang & Zhang, 2024). Similarly, narrative
branding via storytelling is a dense stimulus involving
feelings, imagination, and identification, lengthening
emotional ties that translate into more potent purchase
intentions (Ahmadi & Ataei, 2024). Product aesthetics
and symbolic meanings inherent in brand design also
serve as sensory and semiotic stimuli that provoke
emotional delight and cultural pride, extending brand
attachment (Bas, 2024). Also, a brand name and
reputation play as credible cues signalling quality,
tradition, and reliability, which decrease uncertainty
and increase emotional confidence in the brand (Erdem
& Swait, 2004). Emotional attachment is therefore the
core "organism" component mediating the influence
of all five brand stimuli on the consumer's behavioural
"response," i.e., purchase behaviour. The suggested
model not only follows but also expands the S-O-R
framework by including culturally based brand qualities
and emotion-based processes in the fast-evolving field
of cultural creative industries. Thus, the conceptual
framework ( Figure 1 ) combines these theoretical
relationships, emphasizing these streams from brand
factors (stimuli) through emotional bonding (organism)
to consumer purchasing behaviour (response), thus
providing a strong framework to investigate the affective
dynamics of consumer behaviour among Chinese
young people in the context of Forbidden City cultural
creative products (Iqbal et al., 2022).

3. METHODOLOGY
3.1 Research Design
This research employed a quantitative, cross-sectional
approach to investigate the influence of brand identity,
brand storytelling, product aesthetics, brand symbolism,
and brand name and reputation on purchase
behaviour, as well as the mediating effect of emotional
attachment. A standardized survey tool was utilized to
collect numerical information from the respondents
at one point in time. This was chosen because it works
best in the measurement of attitudes, perceptions, and
behavioural dispositions among a broader population
and the determination of relationships among
several variables. The design is most appropriate in
hypothesis testing as well as in mediation relationship
investigations that support the deductive methodology
guiding this study. In addition, using a quantitative
research approach provides statistical rigor, objectivity,
and replicability necessary for testing relationships
formulated in the conceptual framework by applying
the S-O-R theory (Jan et al., 2022).
3.2 Population
The population under study was young consumers in
Beijing, China, who understand and have experience
using Forbidden City cultural creative products. Beijing,
as a host for the Forbidden City and a cultural hub
of China, provides a rich environment for examining
consumer-brand interaction in a heritage and creative
industries backdrop. The research targeted individuals
between 18 and 35 years old, as this group constitutes
the core group of consumers for cultural creative
products in China. Prior market studies have shown that
young Chinese consumers are susceptible to brands
that balance cultural authenticity with contemporary
design aesthetics and hence are the best respondents
for measuring emotional and behavioural reactions to
culturally branded products (Enyejo et al., 2024).

Beijing was selected not only for accessibility but also

setting for examining how culturally embedded brand
cues are interpreted under conditions of high symbolic
salience. At the same time, the findings should be
interpreted with caution because Beijing consumers
may display stronger familiarity with the brand and
stronger heritage engagement than youth in other
Chinese cities or regions. The study, therefore, prioritizes
contextual relevance over national representativeness.
3.3 Sample Size and Sampling Technique
Three hundred fifty-four valid responses were retained
for analysis. The sample size was adequate for Partial
Least Squares Structural Equation Modelling (PLS-
SEM) because it exceeded the minimum requirement
based on the maximum number of structural paths
directed at an endogenous construct in the model.
In the present framework, emotional attachment and
purchase behaviour receive multiple predictor paths,
and the final sample therefore provides sufficient cases
for stable parameter estimation and bootstrapping.
A non-probability purposive sampling method was
employed, whereby respondents were selected based
on their familiarity with Forbidden City products and
their presence in relevant cultural consumption settings,
such as museums, souvenir stores, bookstores, and online
fan communities in Beijing. This approach ensured that
responses were obtained from participants with relevant
product knowledge and consumption experience.
3.4 Data Collection
Data were collected using a standardized questionnaire
created to capture all the constructs of concern. The
questionnaire contained several sections reflecting
the study variables: brand identity, brand storytelling,
product aesthetics, brand symbolism, brand name
and reputation, emotional attachment, and purchase
behaviour. Every section contained items that were
scaled on a five-point Likert scale from 1 (strongly
disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). All the items of
measurement were taken from previously validated
scales in the literature, and content validity and
measurement reliability were ensured. The questionnaire
was administered both face-to-face at Beijing cultural
retail sites and online via targeted social media sites. A
pilot test of 30 respondents was conducted before the
full implementation to verify the instrument's clarity and
reliability (Sim et al., 2022).
Several procedural remedies were used to reduce
the risk of common method bias. The questionnaire
employed previously validated items, the wording
was kept clear and construct-specific, respondents
were informed that participation was voluntary and
anonymous, and the instrument was pilot tested to
reduce ambiguity and evaluation apprehension. These
steps are consistent with recommended procedural
remedies for self-reported survey research (Podsakoff
et al., 2003). Nevertheless, because all focal variables
were collected from the same respondents at one
point in time, some residual method bias cannot be
completely ruled out.
3.5 Data Analysis
Data analysis was divided into two phases. To begin
with, descriptive statistics, normality test, and internal
consistency test with Cronbach’s alpha were generated
by using SPSS version 26. Second, PLS-SEM in SmartPLS
was used to estimate the hypothesized model. The
measurement model was evaluated based on the
indicator loading, composite reliability, average
variance extracted, and discriminant validity measured
with Fornell and Larcker’s criterion. Once the satisfactory
measurement properties were determined, the
structural model was tested by estimating both the
direct paths between the five elements of the brand and
purchase behaviour and the indirect paths between
the brand elements and emotional attachment. The
importance of the path coefficients and indirect
effects was evaluated using bootstrapping with 5,000
resamples and 95 percent bias-adjusted confidence
intervals. Indirect effect was also considered significant
when the confidence interval was not equal to zero,
and the mediation pattern was understood as partial
or complete, with the help of which the corresponding
direct effect is significant after the introduction of the
emotional attachment.

5. DISCUSSION
In recent years, the intersection of cultural branding and
consumer psychology has become a rapidly growing
field of study, particularly in culturally diverse societies
like China. With the changing consumer markets,
brands rooted in national and historical identity, like the
Forbidden City cultural creative products, have become
practical tools for emotionally and behaviourally
reaching out to younger consumers. This research aimed
to investigate how brand identity, brand storytelling,
product aesthetics, brand symbolism, brand name, and
reputation influence the purchase behaviour of Chinese
youth, together with the mediating role of emotional
attachment. Based on the S-O-R model, the results
provide new knowledge about how stimulus-based
cultural conformity generates emotional processing
resulting in active consumer action. Through the linking
of theoretical frameworks with empirical data, this study
advances insight into how heritage-based branding
strategies are capable of effectively resonating within a
modern marketplace defined by younger, experience-
hungry consumers.

The results of this research indicated that brand identity
played an essential role in driving purchase behaviours
among Chinese youth who participate in Forbidden City
cultural creative product consumption. This corresponds
with the theoretical foundation in the S-O-R model that
establishes brand identity as a stimulus for triggering inner
psychological responses and ultimately a behavioural
outcome (Kim & Lee, 2023). The strong and distinctive
Forbidden City brand identity, grounded in Chinese
cultural heritage, symbols, and historical narratives,
is attractive to young consumers seeking meaning
and cultural relevance in consumption. These results
are consistent with past studies that have shown that
an openly declared and richly cultural brand identity
enhances trust, loyalty, and consumer behaviour (Zhang
et al., 2023; Gao & Kuipers, 2024; Hao, 2024; Yan et al.,
2024). In the Chinese market, where cultural pride is
combined with modern consumerism, brand identity
is not merely a way of product differentiation but also
a connection between tradition and modern lifestyle
values. Hence, the above evidence validates that brand
identity is a strong driver of customer selection, justifying
the requirement of upholding cultural consistency in
branding initiatives.
In the same vein, storytelling is found to influence
purchasing behaviour significantly. Storytelling allows
brands to communicate meaning, engage emotionally,
and forge enduring relationships with consumers by
telling culturally relevant narratives. In the current
research, the engaging histories and myths incorporated
in Forbidden City product designs seem to have
enhanced consumers' affective engagement, thus
impacting their behavioural intentions (Calvo-Porral et
al., 2024). This follows the S-O-R theory by showing how
narratives are cognitive stimuli that elicit emotional
engagement (organism) and prompt consumers to
respond (response). Previous studies also confirm the
potential of storytelling to improve brand memorability
and create stronger psychological attachment,
particularly in cultural product settings where symbolic
value is high (Houghton, 2023; Júnior et al., 2023; Laradi
et al., 2024). Such results confirm the power of storytelling
to enable brands to develop stronger emotional
resonance and salience with younger generations in
China, who are more willing to pay for authenticity and
cultural density in their consumption.
The stronger effect of storytelling suggests that narrative
is the mechanism through which heritage becomes
personally meaningful to young consumers. Visual
design and symbolism may attract attention, but
storytelling connects these cues to court life, artefacts,
and historical memory, allowing consumers to interpret
the product as part of a larger cultural account.
Narrative processing theory suggests that stories help
consumers organize brand information in self-relevant
ways, which strengthens attachment and behavioural
response (Escalas, 2004). In the context of cultural
brands, this mechanism is intensified because the
story not only explains the product but also activates
collective meaning and social identity (Fournier &
Alvarez, 2019). This interpretation is consistent with Palace
Museum research showing that consumers attend not
only to appearance but also to stories, legends, and
emotional resonance when evaluating museum cultural
products (Huang et al., 2023).
The research also validated that the aesthetics of a
product and the symbolism of a brand considerably
influence consumer purchasing behaviour. Consumers
are attracted to the visual and symbolic qualities of
products, particularly when these are consonant with
Chinese traditional aesthetics and cultural symbols.
Product aesthetics, such as colour, form, and intricate
design, evoke admiration and contentment, thereby
inducing buying behaviour (Jahan et al., 2024).
Symbolism of the brand adds to this effect even more by
embedding values, beliefs, and nationhood into visual
representations. The symbolic and visual are employed,
according to the S-O-R model, as sensory and cognitive
stimuli eliciting pleasant emotional states and behaviour.
This finding corroborates previous research showing
that products with intrinsically meaningful symbols
incorporated well into their designs yield improved
consumer recognition and shopping intentions (Yu
et al., 2023). In conclusion, findings highlight that
symbolic meaning as well as taste are paramount in
stimulating youth consumption behaviour in China's
cultural product market. The intersection of aesthetics
and symbolism not only satisfies personal preference
but also reinforces cultural identification, which further
supports the relevance of heritage-based branding in
explaining youth consumer response patterns.
The result indicated that brand name and reputation
have considerable influence on the purchasing
behaviour of young Chinese consumers in the context
of Forbidden City cultural creative products. This result
aligns with the assumption that established brand
names, particularly those rooted in heritage and
national pride, contain symbolic capital that affects
customers' attitudes and behaviours (Júnior et al.,
2023). The Forbidden City, as a historically revered and
culturally iconic institution, has a rich brand reputation
that evokes trust, authenticity, and respect in young
consumers. This is consistent with the S-O-R model, where
brand name and reputation are external stimuli that
stimulate cognitive evaluation (organism), which finally
results in behavioural responses such as purchasing
decisions. These findings are consistent with previous
studies, which argue that successful brands develop a
competitive advantage via trust and emotional security
of consumers (Ihemereze et al., 2023; Kaur et al., 2023;
Ng & Li, 2023; Chopra et al., 2024). In culturally dense
societies such as China, brands with names that evoke

tradition, authority, and artistic heritage, such as the
Forbidden City, therefore have a more profound impact
on youth consumer culture than do less well-known or
less culturally rooted brands.
In addition, mediation analysis validated hypotheses
H6a to H6e, indicating that emotional attachment to
the brand effectively mediates the influence of all five
aspects of a brand's identity, brand storytelling, product
aesthetics, brand symbolism, and brand name and
reputation on purchase behaviour. This validates the
theoretical framework of the S-O-R model, in which
emotional attachment (organism) serves as a vital
link between external stimuli related to the brand and
consumer responses (Jiang et al., 2024). Emotional
attachment indicates the level of psychological
connection and affective value that consumers have
towards a brand, which also heightens their intention to
be supportive through behaviours such as buying and
word-of-mouth recommendation of the brand (Shah
et al., 2023). The cultural meaning and emotionally
appealing storytelling of Forbidden City products would
have probably evoked strong sentimental attachments
among young Chinese consumers, thus making them
more determined to exhibit supportive behaviour.
Earlier research also claims that emotional attachment
is a key mediating factor for transforming brand stimuli
into responsible consumer behaviour, particularly in
culturally identified marketplaces and marketplaces for
aesthetic appreciation (Meng et al., 2024). Therefore,
this study broadens the application of the S-O-R model
by demonstrating how emotional attachment functions
as a psychological process by which brand attributes
have their effect on consumer behaviour, specifically in
the cultural and heritage branding of China.
The findings also illuminate the cultural identity dynamics
of Chinese youth. For this group, heritage-related
products are not consumed only for utility or novelty.
Prior research shows that young Chinese consumers
attach symbolic value, pride, and a sense of home to
domestic brands, while traditional cultural properties
and traditional cultural symbols can enhance product
intention through cultural perceptions and identity-
related meaning (Jin et al., 2018; Qin et al., 2019). More
recent evidence similarly indicates that traditional
cultural symbol design and cultural involvement can
stimulate purchase intention through perceived value
and positive affective responses (Liu & Zhao, 2024;
Zhou et al., 2024). Therefore, the present results suggest
that Forbidden City cultural creative products function
as identity expressive goods through which young
consumers negotiate modern consumption and cultural
belonging at the same time.
Finally, the acceptance of all the tested hypotheses
confirms that brand elements firmly rooted in cultural
identity strongly influence consumer behaviour among
Chinese youth, mainly through the mediating effect of
emotional attachment. This highlights the central role
of emotions in consumer decision-making behaviours,
particularly in the context of cultural creative industries.
The conclusions provide theoretical insights into the
S-O-R framework and managerial advice for marketers
seeking to develop heritage-driven branding strategies
that resonate with young consumers. Finally, the
research identifies the continued relevance of cultural
authenticity, emotional narratives, and symbolic brand
associations in eliciting consumer involvement in an
evolving yet tradition-sensitive market such as China.
5.1 Implications
The practical significance of this study is highly
applicable to brand strategists, cultural product
developers, and marketers seeking to connect with
young Chinese consumers. The research illustrates that
aspects of a brand like identity, storytelling, aesthetic,
symbolism, and brand reputation have a substantive
impact on consumer purchase behaviour, especially
when these aspects are culturally grounded and
emotionally appealing. For institutions and businesses
handling cultural creative goods such as the Forbidden
City, this emphasizes the importance of strategically
crafting branding that is responsive to national heritage
but still visually attractive and emotionally engaging for
the youth market (He et al., 2022). Emotional attachment
was shown to be a potent mediator, implying that
brands must create engaging and meaningful brand
experiences that extend beyond utilitarian value
(Chebab & Boukerch, 2025). Ad campaigns that
involve established narratives, symbolic imagery, and
established reputations have the potential to enhance
emotional loyalty and purchase intention. Additionally,
cultural industry brand managers should ensure a
consistent narrative and congruent brand image across
media platforms to build long-term brand involvement
by consumers who seek both cultural authenticity and
contemporary presentation.
Theoretically, this research contributes to enriching
the use of the S-O-R model in the area of consumer
behaviour and cultural branding. In empirically
confirming that emotional commitment acts as a
mediator between purchase behaviour and brand
attributes, the research reiterates the pivotal role of
affective processes in consumer choice. It further
extends the domain of branding scholarship by situating
it within a cultural context, more specifically, within the
Chinese environment, where symbolism and identity are
essential factors in influencing consumer mindsets (Chiu
et al., 2024). The study contributes to the scholarship
by integrating the ideas of cultural psychology,
branding theory, and behavioural science to develop
a comprehensive model of how brand cues influence
behavioural reactions based on emotional involvement.
Additionally, the research evidence suggests that

branding in heritage industries needs to target not only
product design and marketing communications but also
the psychological and affective aspects of customer-

From a managerial perspective, emotionally resonant
storytelling should be designed as a coordinated

5.2 Limitations and Future Directions
The primary limitation of this research is its geographical
scope, as the data were collected solely from Beijing
respondents and thus might not reflect diverse youth
opinions in other parts of China. Cultural attitudes, brand
recognition, and consumer behaviour can significantly
differ across rural and urban locations, or the north
and south. In addition, the research was based on
self-reported information, which can be influenced by
response biases like social desirability or memory errors.
This may have increased the likelihood of favourable
evaluations and common response tendencies.
Although several procedural remedies were employed
to reduce common method bias, the current archived
materials do not reproduce an item-level post hoc
diagnostic from which the variance explained by the
first common factor can be verified. The findings should
therefore be interpreted with awareness that residual
common method bias cannot be statistically ruled
out from the archived outputs alone. Future research
could address this issue by incorporating behavioural
measures, longitudinal data, or comparison groups with
different levels of brand familiarity. Another limitation is
that the study was cross-sectional; hence, the capacity
to make causal linkages among brand elements,
emotional affinity, and purchasing behaviour is limited.
Furthermore, focusing on one brand ecosystem, the
Forbidden City cultural creative products, narrows
the applicability of the results to other commercial or
cultural brands. Subsequent research needs to take
into account a larger geographic representation of the
sample in terms of representing multiple provinces and
cities across China, strengthening the representativeness
and external validity of the results. Longitudinal work
may also be carried out to examine the development
over time of brand perceptions and emotional
attachment, particularly concerning repeated cultural
branding campaigns. Another weakness is related to
the retrievability of full model summary diagnostics of
the stored material of analytical properties archived.
Despite the reported study estimates of direct and
indirect paths, even with the archived output, the
current statistics of the coefficient of determination of
the endogenous constructs are not reproducible. The
substantive interpretation is consequently founded on
the checked path coefficients and mediation findings,
predominantly. Future research must keep the entire
model summary files to enable explanatory power and
associated structural diagnostics to be reported along
with path significance.
Further research could also investigate other mediating
or moderating factors like brand trust, cultural identity
salience, or online engagement to establish more
subtle mechanisms motivating purchase. Comparative
analyses between various heritage brands or even
across cultures may also provide valuable insights into
the way brand components function in other socio-
cultural contexts, so that one can have a better global
insight into cultural brand influence. A further avenue for
future research is to examine cultural identity strength
as a moderator of the relationships tested in the present
study. Although the current findings indicate that
cultural brand elements and emotional attachment
are important, the magnitude of these effects may
differ according to the extent to which consumers
regard cultural belonging as a salient part of the self.
Future studies may therefore compare groups with
stronger and weaker cultural identification, including
cross-regional samples within China and cross-cultural
samples outside China, to determine whether heritage
branding operates differently across identity conditions.
6. CONCLUSION
This research has offered rich insights into the effects of
brand factors on Chinese young consumers' purchase
behaviour, taking the Forbidden City cultural creative
products as an example context. The results indicated
that brand identity, narrative, visual aesthetics, symbolic
meanings, and reputation all exerted significant
influences on consumer purchasing decisions, with
affective attachment acting as an essential mediating
variable in these effects. Rooted in Stimulus-Organism-
Response theory, the study verifies that embedded
cultural brand attributes are strong stimuli that are
capable of triggering emotional congruence and
ultimately influencing consumer behaviours. Focusing
on a younger audience in China, the research not only

and emotional marketing but also provides applied
implications for cultural heritage institutions and

the continuing salience of tradition in modern-day
consumption. This study, thus, provides the foundation
