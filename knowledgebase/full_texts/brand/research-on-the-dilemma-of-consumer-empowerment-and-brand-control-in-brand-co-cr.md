Abstract. With the development of the digital economy and platform -based business models,
platform enterprises increasingly need consumer participation in co -creating brands to access
innovative resources, enhance user engagement, and increase brand asset value. How ever, this
process presents a tension between "consumer empowerment" and "brand control": on one hand,
granting consumers greater autonomy can stimulate creativity, foster emotional connections, and
strengthen brand loyalty, thereby improving the effectiveness of brand co-creation; on the other hand,
excessive empowerment may lead to loss of brand control, inconsistent content quality,
misalignment with corporate strategic goals, and even erosion of brand equity and reputational risks,
placing companies in a dilemma between "decentralized chaos and centralized stagnation." Drawing
on theories of brand co -creation, consumer empowerment, and organizational control, this paper
analyzes the operational mechanisms and contradictions involved in brand co -creation within
platform enterprises, exploring the root causes of this dilemma —such as the conflict between
platform ecosystem openness and corporate strategic stability, the tension between consumer
agency and brand consistency management, and challenges in gover ning user-generated content
(UGC) under digital technologies. To address these issues, the paper proposes a "dynamic balance"
approach: first, establishing a tiered and differentiated empowerment mechanism that grants varying
levels of authority based on c onsumers' participation depth and expertise; second, implementing
flexible brand management through soft controls such as value -driven guidance, community self -
governance, and algorithm-assisted content moderation; third, leveraging digital tools to build real-
time feedback and collaborative co -creation platforms that strengthen trust and shared benefits
between firms and consumers, thus achieving a dynamic equilibrium between empowerment and
control. The paper argues that platform enterprises should move beyond binary thinking and instead
adopt a synergistic governance model that balances openness with orderliness, effectively
integrating institutional design, technological capabilities, and cultural development to maximize co-
creation efficiency while safe guarding brand assets. This research not only enriches existing
scholarship on brand co -creation and consumer empowerment but also offers practical
recommendations for enterprises seeking to enhance their brand management and competitiveness
in practice.
Keywords: Platform-based enterprise , Co-creation of brand , Consumer empowerment , Brand

1. Introduction
The digital economy is sweeping across the globe, and platform -based enterprises —acting as
bridges connecting producers and consumers —have significantly transformed the business
ecosystem and the way brands are built. According to statistics from China Int ernet Network
Information Center, by June 2023, the number of online shopping users in China reached 884 million,
with an online service platform user penetration rate exceeding 78%. Under these circumstances,
traditional brand-building approaches—where companies unilaterally communicate brand values and
control all aspects of dissemination—are no longer sufficient to meet the demands of an era marked
by heightened consumer self-awareness and growing desire for participation. An increasing number
of platform enterprises are actively inviting consumers to co -create brands, granting them rights in
design, content creation, and even voting, thereby empowering them to take initiative and foster
emotional engagement. Examples such as Xiaomi Community's product cr owdfunding, Starbucks'
"My Starbucks Idea" platform, and Bilibili's UP (content creator) ecosystem collaboration clearly

value. This approach enables companies to seize market opportunities more quickly, reduce
innovation costs, enhance customer loyalty, cultivate a unique br and community culture , and gain

In the wave of brand co-creation, platform companies are facing an increasingly serious challenge:
as consumers gain greater rights to participate and express themselves, enterprises' ability to shape

empowerment leads to inconsistent quality in user -generated content (UGC), fragmenting brand

erosion and reputational damage. The 2022 incident involving a well-known beauty platform, where
uncontrolled user reviews damaged its brand, and the 2021 public trust crisis on a social e-commerce
platform due to inadequate content moderation, serve as critical warnings about the need for balance
between empowerment and control. This dilemma —where "delegating authority leads to loss of
control, while strict control stifles engagement" —reveals the inherent tension between platform
openness and corporate stability, as well as t he conflict between consumer agency and brand
consistency. This article aims to analyze the root causes and manifestations of this paradox from the
perspectives of service logic and service governance, seeking solutions that satisfy consumer needs
without compromising brand control, and offering recommendations for more effective brand
management in platform-based businesses.
2. The Dilemma in Brand Co-creation for Platform Enterprises: Identifying
Contradictions and Defining the Problem
2.1 The Emergence and Current Practice of Brand Co-creation in Platform-based Enterprises
The rise of brand co -creation in platform -based enterprises stems from the combined impact of
digital technology empowerment, shifts in consumer culture, and transformations in business models.
Since the Web 2.0 era, social media, mobile internet, and clou d computing have enabled ordinary
individuals to easily participate in brand building, significantly lowering participation costs and
barriers, thus facilitating large -scale collaborative creation. Meanwhile, younger generations
increasingly value self-expression, community belonging, and alignment with personal values. Rather
than passively accepting brand narratives, they now seek to express their identities and gain
psychological fulfillment through active involvement in co-creation. According to a 2023 survey by
iResearch, over 62% of Gen Z consumers are willing to pay for products that [2] reflect their values,
and user retention rates among those participating in brand co-creation are more than 40% higher than
those of regular users. In practice, platform enterprises employ diverse forms of co-creation, including
product design crowdfunding and content co-creation communities, crowdsourced brand storytelling,
user review systems, virtual brand ambassador programs, and joint construction of metaverse brand
spaces. As co-creation becomes increasingly deep and widespread, it has emerged as one [3] of the
most effective strategies for platform companies to build competitive advantages.
2.2 The Connotation, Manifestations, and Driving Forces of Consumer Empowerment
Consumer empowerment refers to platform -based enterprises transferring decision -making
authority, content creation rights, and communication rights —traditionally held exclusively by
companies—related to their brands to consumers through institutional desig n, technological tools,
and cultural guidance, thereby giving consumers greater voice and influence in the process of brand
value creation. Consumer empowerment manifests in diverse forms: informational empowerment
enables consumers to access relevant product information, production processes, and reasons behind
corporate decisions; participatory empowerment allows consumers to engage in activities such as
product design voting, brand naming contests, and crowdsourced advertising creativity; and
decisional empowerment enables consumers to influence corporate decisions through community

awareness of their  [4] agency; and commercially, empowerment boosts consumer engagement,
reduces innovation costs, and generates network effects through word -of-mouth marketing,
delivering significant economic benefits and social capital to enterprises.

2.3 The Necessity, Scope, and Implementation Challenges of Brand Control
Brand control is a management activity undertaken by enterprises to maintain brand consistency,
stability of brand value, and clarity of brand identity. Its necessity stems from the process of
accumulating brand assets. As one of the most important intangi ble assets for a company, a brand
must rely on long -term symbolic management, storytelling, and experience design to ensure
consistent consumer perception and trust. If a brand becomes fragmented or its values become
confused, it will diminish brand recognition, reduce consumer loyalty, and even trigger a reputational
crisis. Brand control encompasses managing visual identity systems, reviewing brand communication
content, monitoring alignment with core values, and handling crisis public relations. However, in the
context of platform-based co-creation, brand control faces unprecedented challenges: user-generated
content is vast in volume and rapidly evolving, making traditional manual review methods inadequate.
There exists a tension between consumers' desire [5] for personalized expression and companies' need

free expression, leading to resentment, reduced engagement in co -creation, and ultimately causing

2.4 The Core Tension and Contradictory Manifestations of the Empowerment-Control
Dilemma
The dilemma between empowerment and control manifests in several key contradictions during
brand co-creation within platform-based enterprises. First, there is the tension between openness and
order: platforms need to maintain a certain level of openness to attract more users, but this openness
can lead to diverse content and differing value orientations, complicating unified brand management.
Second, there is the conflict between freedom and consistency: consumers desire flexibility in the co-
creation proc ess, while companies must ensure that every brand touchpoint conveys consistent
messaging and values. Third, there is the contradiction between efficiency and quality: large numbers
of users can quickly generate numerous ideas, but this may also result in uneven content quality,
undermining the brand image  [6]. Fourth, there is the struggle between short -term incentives and
long-term investment: empowering users can rapidly boost engagement and create social momentum,
but without proper oversight, it may negatively impact the brand’s long-term development. In practice,
these contradictions often surface as hesitation in establishing co -creation rules, confusion when
dealing with user overstepping, and constant tension between community autonomy and brand
standards. For example, a well -known sportswear brand a ttempted in 2022 to involve customers in
product design voting, but had to halt the project after some proposed designs deviated significantly
from the brand’s positioning—an illustration of this very challenge.
3. Theoretical Perspectives on the Dilemma: A Dual View from Service-
Dominant Logic and Governance Theory
3.1 Consumer Empowerment Logic from the Perspective of Service Dominant Logic
The service-dominant logic theory, proposed by Vargo and Lusch in 2004, posits that value is not
created unilaterally by firms and embedded into products, but rather emerges through consumers'
integration of resources and interactions during use. In this f ramework, consumers are no longer
passive recipients of value; instead, they become active co -creators of value and integrators of
resources. Brand co -creation in platform -based enterprises embodies precisely the principles
advocated by service-dominant logic: a brand is no longer static information unilaterally defined and
disseminated by the firm, but rather a dynamic network [7] of meanings co-evolved through ongoing
dialogue between the company and its consumers. From this perspective, consumer empowerment is
both reasonable and inevitable —only when consumers have a voice, choice, and decision -making
power can their knowledge, skills, and creativity be fully leveraged to maximize brand value creation.
Service-dominant logic also emphasizes relationality and contextuality; value co -creation within
specific socio-cultural contexts requires communication, negotiation, and mutual adaptation to reach
consensus. Therefore, platform enterprises should establish equitable platforms for dialogue, viewing
consumers as co -builders of the brand rather than mere managers, thereby awakening their agency
and fostering emotional connections. T his provides strong theoretical support for consumer
empowerment, yet it overlooks the importance of brand control.
3.2 Brand Control Mechanisms from the Perspective of Governance Theory
Governance theory originates from research in institutional economics and organizational
management, primarily exploring how order can be maintained and goals achieved within complex
systems involving multiple participants through institutional arrangement s, power distribution, and
incentive mechanisms. Applying governance theory to the context of platform -based brand co -
creation effectively explains the essence and implementation pathways  [8] of brand control. From a

reducing communication costs, and safeguarding brand assets within an open ecosystem. A brand
represents an intangible asset formed through long -term corporate investment and possesses strong

single individual can damage it. This necessitates deliberate brand governance, meaning that
companies must establish clear property rights, behavioral norms, and penalty mechanisms to prevent

culture, value orientations, and reputation mechanisms. In practice, brand control can be implemented
through various approaches: hierarchic al governance relies on corporate authority and direct
management; market governance leverages price signals and competitive dynamics to influence user
behavior [9]; and network governance focuses more on building trust, mutual benefit, and community
self-governance. While governance theory provides a theoretical foundation for effective brand
control, it also raises the challenge of striking a balance between control and empowerment.

3.3 Analysis of the Advantages and Limitations of Two Theoretical Perspectives
Service-dominant logic and governance theory offer valuable perspectives for understanding the
challenges of brand co-creation from the angles of value creation and order maintenance, respectively,
yet each has its strengths and limitations. Service-dominant logic emphasizes consumers' agency and
creative capacity, advocating empowerment to motivate user participation and achieve co -created
value—aligning well with evolving consumer culture in the digital era and guiding platform -based
enterprises in developing user-centered branding strategies. However, it places excessive emphasis
on openness and fairness, overlooking issues such as brand asset protection and consistency of brand
values, and fails to provide effective solutions for problems like low -quality user-generated content
or loss of brand image control. Governance theory, by contrast, offers a detailed framework for
maintaining order and achieving objectives through institutional design in multi-stakeholder settings,
providing brands with a toolkit and methodology for control—particularly relevant [10] in addressing
risks of opportunism, coordination costs, and incentive -constraint mechanisms. Nevertheless,
governance theory typically assumes a power imbalance between governors and the governed,
viewing consumers as objects requiring management and constraint rather than equal partners in
collaboration. This managerial mindset may suppress consumer engagement and provoke resistance.
Moreover, the two theories differ significantly in ontological assumptions, value orientations, and
analytical focus: s ervice-dominant logic prioritizes relationality, reciprocity, and symbiosis, while
governance theory emphasizes efficiency, normativity, and controllability. These contrasting
theoretical viewpoints precisely reflect the tension between empowerment and control that platform-
based enterprises face.
3.4 Exploring the Necessity and Feasibility of Theoretical Integration
To truly resolve the tension between empowerment and control in brand co -creation within
platform-based enterprises, we cannot remain confined to a purely service-dominant logic perspective.
Instead, we must seek integration points between these two framew orks. This is because real -world
challenges abound—platforms need to empower consumers to unleash their creativity for value co -
creation, while simultaneously managing brand assets to prevent damage and chaos. Thus, a single
theoretical lens fails to provide comprehensive explanations or guidance. However, service-dominant
logic and governance theory can be effectively integrated. The resource integration and value co -
creation emphasized by service-dominant logic require institutional foundations to function properly,
just as governance theory's focus on maintaining order aims at fostering value creation rather than
mere control  [11]. Therefore, resolving this issue calls for establishing a model of "empowering
governance"—one that respects consumers' agency and capacity for co-creating value, and leverages
flexible institutions, technological tools, and cultural guidance to achie ve a balance between
"bounded empowerment" and "humane control," transforming the relationship from opposition into
collaboration.
4. Resolving the Dilemma: Constructing a Theoretical Framework Based on a
Balancing Mechanism
4.1 Contractual Design Mechanism: Institutional Arrangements for Defining the Boundaries
of Rights and Responsibilities
Contract design is a fundamental institutional arrangement for resolving the tension between
empowerment and control. At its core, it defines in advance —explicitly or implicitly —the rights,
obligations, and benefit-sharing mechanisms of platform companies and consumers during brand co-
creation. This is not a traditional legal contract but rather a comprehensive governance structure
encompassing formal rules, community norms, and technical protocols. In brand co -creation by
platform enterprises, contract des ign must clearly outline the scope of consumer authority in areas
such as content creation, design participation, and promotion, while also establishing baseline

requirements regarding quality standards, ethical guidelines, and intellectual property rights. Take
Xiaohongshu and Bilibili as examples: in recent years, both platforms have developed detailed creator
agreements, community codes, and content review standards, encouraging user -generated content
while reserving the right to remove material that violates brand values. The effectiveness of contract
design closely depends on its transparency and flexibility. Platforms need to openly communicate
rules, provide case studies, and offer user education to reduce information asymmetry, enabling
consumers to understand [12] expectations clearly before participating. Moreover, contracts should
be sufficiently flexible to adapt to shifts in brand strategy, market conditions, and user feedback. Such
institutionalized allocation of rights and responsibilities can effectively  reduce friction costs during
co-creation, and through targeted incentives and constraints, guide consumer behavior in alignment
with corporate objectives—ensuring brand image and value are protected while preserving creative
freedom.
4.2 Boundary Resource Mechanism: Flexible Control Strategies under Platform Architecture
Boundary resources are a concept derived from platform ecology, referring to the use of technical
interfaces, data formats, development tools, and other "boundary resources" to influence the actions
of external participants in a non -coercive, guiding manne r. In the process of brand co -creation,
platforms can offer a suite of modular co-creation toolkits, establish parameterized content generation
frameworks, and implement intelligent quality inspection systems. These mechanisms grant
consumers creative free dom while subtly embedding brand standards into an "invisible track." For
example, Nike's NIKEiD customization platform allows customers to select shoe colors, patterns, and
other features. However, due to technical constraints, users can only choose from predefined options
regarding color, materials, and logo placement. This approach provides room f or personalization
while ensuring the final product aligns with the brand’s aesthetic vision. The advantage here lies in
integrating management logic into technology rather than enforcing rules directly —consumers
naturally follow these guidelines as they use the tools, avoiding resistance [13] that might arise from
overt control. Moreover, boundary resources are scalable and flexible: platforms can assign different
levels of tool access based on users’ skill levels and trustworthiness. Experienced users enjoy greater
creative autonomy, whil e beginners operate under stricter parameters. With advances in digital
technology, this mechanism becomes increasingly effective. Features such as AI -driven content
recommendations, real-time scoring, and automated compliance checks form a dynamic regulat ory
network, enabling platforms to quickly identify and amplify user -generated content that aligns with
brand identity, while promptly suppressing material that could harm the brand’s reputation.
4.3 Dynamic Coordination Mechanism: Contextualized Balance between Empowerment and
Control
The dynamic coordination mechanism breaks away from traditional fixed institutional
arrangements by emphasizing flexible combinations of empowerment and control tailored to the
different stages, contexts, and characteristics of participants involved in co -creation across brands.
Underlying this mechanism are contingency theory and the concept of contextualized governance —
[14] recognizing that there is no one -size-fits-all optimal balance. Companies must be able to
promptly detect market signals, user sentiments, and brand crises, then adjust accordingly based on
these insights. During the idea generation phase of brand co -creation, platforms tend to grant
consumers greater freedom, encouraging them to unleash their imagination and pursue bold
innovations, where tolerance for failure is high and diversity takes precedence over consistency. In
contrast, during the brand promotion phase, control significantly increases, with platforms using
content review, expert evaluation, and user voting to vet final outputs. This phased, adaptive
management approach has been effectively implemented on the LEGO Ideas platform: user -
submitted desi gns are initially fully open, but when commercial production is considered, they
undergo rigorous brand compliance reviews. Moreover, dynamic coordination also involves handling
unexpected situations —for instance, if a user -generated content sparks public controversy or

4.4 Construction and Logical Elucidation of an Integrative Theoretical Framework
Based on contract design, boundary resources, and dynamic coordination, this paper proposes a
comprehensive approach to resolving the dilemma between "consumer empowerment" and "brand
control." The core idea is grounded in service -dominant logic and platfo rm governance theory,
viewing brand co-creation as an ongoing process of communication and collaboration among multiple
stakeholders within a value co -creation network—rather than a one -way relationship where firms
unilaterally provide or consumers passive ly accept. This framework consists of three interrelated
components: institutionally, contract design establishes the foundational rules of engagement,
initially deline [15] ating the balance between empowerment and control; technically, boundary
resources integrate governance principles into the platform architecture, enabling non -intrusive
behavioral guidance; and procedurally, dynamic coordination mechanisms respond to environmental
changes, ensuring flexibility and adaptability in governance. These elements do not operate
independently but mutually reinforce each other: contract design provides legitimacy for setting
parameters of boundary resources, technical feedback from thes e resources supports data -driven
dynamic coordination, and insights gained through coordination inform the refinement and evolution
of contractual arrangements. A key advantage of this approach lies in moving beyond binary
perspectives that emphasize eithe r control or empowerment alone, instead treating them as
complementary tools within a governance toolkit. Enterprises can strategically position themselves
along the "empowerment -control" continuum according to different stages of brand development,
market competition, and consumer maturity. Furthermore, the framework emphasizes pluralistic
governance, advocating not only for platform enterprises but also for self -governing core user
communities and oversight by third -party professional institutions, thereb y distributing governance
pressure across multiple actors. In practice, platform companies should establish a cross -functional
brand co -creation governance committee, bringing together teams from technology, legal affairs,
branding, and user operations to regularly assess governance effectiveness and adjust  [16] strategic
combinations accordingly. This integrated framework not only effectively mitigates the structural
tension between empowerment and control but also enables platform businesses to transition from
short-term crisis management toward long -term ecosystem cultivation, achieving sustainable brand
co-creation through coordinated governance rooted in institutionalization, technological integration,
and procedural adaptability.
5. Conclusions
This paper addresses the dilemma between "consumer empowerment" and "brand control" in brand
co-creation within platform -based enterprises, arguing that this issue is not merely a management
challenge but rather a profound governance problem arising from the transformation of value creation
models in the platform economy era. The root causes of this predicament lie primarily in the tension
between platform ecosystem openness and brand strategy consistency, the conflict between consumer
agency and corporate control, and the increased governance complexity brought about by the surge
of user -generated content enabled by digital technologies. Traditional binary approaches —either
tightening control at the expense of innovation or fully relinquishing control leadi ng to chaos—are
no longer sufficient for the development needs of platform enterprises. Drawing on service logic and
platform governance perspectives, this paper proposes an integrated solution comprising contract

Theoretically, this paper breaks away from previous literature that framed empowerment and
control as opposing forces. Instead, it proposes that through institutional arrangements, technological
applications, and contextual adjustments, empowerment and control can achieve a mutually beneficial
relationship, thereby expanding research [17] on brand co-creation and consumer empowerment. In
practical terms, this perspective offers valuable guidance for platform -based enterprises, enabling
them to move beyond binary choices and instead pursue win-win collaboration by combining multiple
strategies—harnessing user engagement while safeguarding their brand interests. Future work will
involve empirical studies on governance models suitable for different industries and platforms, as
well as examining the impact of emerging technologies such as a rtificial intelligence on the
empowerment-control dynamic, thus continuously advancing both the theory and practice of brand
co-creation in platform enterprises.
