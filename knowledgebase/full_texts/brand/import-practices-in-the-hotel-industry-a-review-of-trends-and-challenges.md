• ABSTRACT :-
The hotel industry relies on a stream of products and services such as food, drinks, furniture, fixtures, equipment,
linens, technology, guest comforts, maintenance supplies and special operating items. Hotels used to buy from local
suppliers.. Because the world is more connected guests expect more international hotel rules are stricter products are
more specialized and big h otel chains grow, the need to source products from abroad and import them has grown.
Importing gives hotels access to quality items, specialized gear, global brands, cheaper prices and uniform products.
Yet importing also brings challenges: customs rules, taxes, changes in currency value, transport delays, paperwork,
quality issues, supply breaks and worries about the environment.
This paper shows the trends and problems that come with importing for the hotel industry. The review pulls together
data from studies on hotel buying and supply chains. Eight other works that talk about taxes, online reviews, economic
downturns, strategy, change in organizations how customers feel, money matters and staff happiness. The paper says
that good hotel importing is no longer just buying. Good hotel importing is now a part of the supply chain that needs
good supplier relationships, online buying tools, risk control, environmental checks, money planning and a flexible
organization. The paper also notes that little is known about hotel importing as a management task, especially in small
Indian cities.
Keywords: hotel industry, import practices, hotel procurement, international sourcing, supply chain management,
supplier management, e-procurement, tariffs, sustainability

1. INTRODUCTION :-
The hotel industry is often called a service industry. The way hotels provide their services depends a lot on getting the
right physical items. Hotels need food ingredients, drinks, sheets, uniforms, furniture, kitchen tools, electrical items,
cleaning supplies, things for guests, computer equipment, parts for repairs, decorations and many other products.
Because of this buying these items is very important for how a hotel runs.
In years buying things for hotels has become more complicated b ecause hotels work with both local and global
suppliers. Big hotel companies might require items, tools or design choices that are not easy to find from local
companies. High-end hotels might also bring in food, furniture, bathroom items, lights, kitchen tools, technology and
things for guests. Getting these items from countries can be key to keeping the same standards and standing out in the
busy hotel business.
Getting things from countries is not just about finding a supplier. A hotel needs to think about the details of the product
how reliable the supplier is, shipping from one country to another dealing with customs paying taxes making sure the
items are safe dealing with money changes how long it takes to get the items checking the quality how to pay and what
to do if the supply gets stopped. These international trade steps can make buying more difficult and more expensive.
The World Trade Organization said in 2017 that making trade easier and better can help reduce delays and costs.
Kothari and others looked at using computers to buy things in hotel supply chains. Said that using technology is very
important for buying. Their study showed that using computers can help with buying and can help hotels in other ways.
This is especially true when hotels are buying from countries because getting items from far away needs to track orders,
suppliers, things in stock, papers and how the items are sent.
The need for steady supply chains became very obvious during the COVID-19 virus. Hotels saw fewer people coming
at the time as problems getting supplies trouble moving things around and money problems. Rini and Fikri said that
hotels need to be stronger more able to change and ready for problems.
With all this in mind this paper looks at what’s happening and what problems exist in getting things from other countries
for hotels. It also connects this topic with research, about buying things managing a business, taxes, how people act,
changing how a company works how well a company does financially and managing workers.

2. OBJECTIVES OF THE REVIEW :-
The major objectives of this review are:
To examine the role of imports and international sourcing in hotel procurement.
To identify emerging trends in hotel import and purchasing practices.
To analyse the major operational, financial, regulatory, and organizational challenges associated with hotel imports.
To integrate the eight specified research papers with the broader hotel procurement literature.
To identify research gaps and suggest directions for future research on hotel import practices.

3. RESEARCH METHODOLOGY :-
Here is a paper that uses a narrative literature review approach. The authors chose literature around five topics: hotel
procurement, supplier management, e‑procurement, sustainable supply chains and supply‑chain resilience. In addition,
to the hospitality and supply‑chain literature the authors added eight specific studies. Those studies look at taxation,
reviews, recession, strategic management organizational change, consumer perception, financial performance and job
satisfaction. The authors do not claim that all of those studies examine hotel imports. Instead the authors use the
findings to build an understanding of the economic strategic financial, consumer and organizational factors that shape
hotel import decisions. The review is qualitative and narrative not a meta‑analysis. The authors combine the findings
by recurring themes and show how they matter for hotel procurement and international sourcing.

4. CONCEPTUAL UNDERSTANDING OF IMPORT PRACTICES IN HOTELS :-
When reviewing hotel import practices it is evident a systematic process. In this process a hotel first identifies a
purchasing requirement. Next the hotel evaluates suppliers. Then the hotel negotiates purchasing conditions. After that
the hotel arranges transportation and customs clearance. The hotel then. Inspects imported products. Finally the hotel
incorporates those imported products into hotel operations.
The basic hotel import process can be represented as follows:
Requirement Identification → Supplier Search → Supplier Evaluation → Negotiation → Purchase Order →
International Transportation → Customs Clearance → Quality Inspection → Receiving and Inventory → Payment →
Supplier Performance Evaluation.
This list shows each step in the order the hotel must follow.
Unlike purchasing international procurement brings extra considerations. These include customs procedures, import
duties, foreign exchange, transportation risks, documentation, country‑of‑or igin requirements and longer delivery
periods.
Salihoğlu and Gezici (2018) studied where suppliers are located in the hospitality sector. They showed how important
supplier location and purchasing relationships are. Their research tells  that hotels should  not automatically think
international suppliers are better, than suppliers. Instead the sourcing decision should rest on product availability,
quality, price, total landed cost, supplier capability and strategic requirements.
Therefore a hotel should compare international suppliers by looking at total landed cost. The hotel must not rely on the
supplier’s quoted price.

5. REVIEW OF LITERATURE :-
5.1 E-Procurement and Digitalization
Kothari and his colleagues looked at e‑procurement as a tool for managing hotel supply chains. They found that
electronic procurement systems can make buying easier and give hotels advantages. This is especially useful for hotel
imports because buying from abroad means working with suppliers many purchase orders, many invoices, many
shipping papers and many inventory records.
Later Kothari and his colleagues studied e‑procurement in a hotel chain. They discovered that standardizing how
purchases are made is an important issue, for the organization. The work shows that technology alone does not change
procurement. The organization must also set rules and help employees learn how to use the system.
For hotel imports digital procurement can help hotels see their suppliers better manage purchase orders, control
inventory keep records and communicate clearly.. The system needs staff to learn managers to support it good data and
rules that everyone follows.

5.2 Supplier Relationships and Strategic Sourcing
Shi and Liao (2013) looked at how hotels, restaurants and suppliers work in the supply chain. Their research showed
that trust, dependence, teamwork and the overall quality of relationships matter a lot in hospitality supply chains. This
is especially true when dealing with sourcing. When suppliers are located abroad lead times tend to  be longer and
coordination becomes more complex. A hotel may not be able to switch to another supplier if a shipment from overseas
gets delayed.
Aksoy and Akın Ateş (2019) studied how hotels in Turkey manage their purchasing strategies. They found that supplier
development and smart purchasing choices play a role in improving performance.. It’s not enough just to have long-
term relationships. The actual value and quality of these relationships must be carefully checked. Hotels need to focus
on what each supplier can offer, not just how long they’ve been working together.
Because of this international hotel suppliers should be judged using measurable standards. These include:
Product quality,Delivery reliability,Price stability,Responsiveness,Certification,Compliance, Sustainablity,Innovation
capability,Stability
These criteria help hotels make better decisions when choosing suppliers abroad. They ensure that partnerships are
strong, dependable and effective, in the run.
5.3 Supplier Geography and Local Versus International Sourcing
Salihoğlu and Gezici (2018) investigated supplier geography in the hospitality sector. Their research highlights the
importance of local supplier relationships and the economic effects created by hotel purchasing.
This raises an important strategic question: When should a hotel import and when should it purchase locally?
Imports may be appropriate when a product is unavailable domestically, when international brand standards require a
specific product, when imported quality is substantially higher, or when specialized international equipment is required.

Domestic sourcing may be preferable when products are highly perishable, when immediate delivery is important,
when customs costs are high, or when domestic suppliers can provide comparable quality.
Thus, hotel managers should make sourcing decisions based on total value rather than simply selecting the supplier
with the lowest quoted price.

5.4 Green Procurement and Sustainability
Al-Aomar and Hussain from the year 2017 looked at practices in hotel supply chains and showed that being
environmentally responsible when buying things is becoming more important.
Others in 2019 studied lean-green practices and how they affect sustainable performance in hotel supply chains. Their
results show that quality management reducing waste taking care of equipment being friendly and other improvements
in operations can help achieve sustainable performance.
Migdadi from 2023 looked at supply -chain practices in the hotel industry and found that green  procurement being
committed to the environment, green quality management and internal environmental processes are key parts of
sustainable hotel supply chains.
Alreahi and others in 2023 did a review and showed that green supply -chain management has become an important
area of research, in hospitality. However putting supply chains into practice is still hard because hotels work with many
different people and companies.
When it comes to products that are brought in from places being sustainable means hotels need to check not just the
environmental features of the product but also how far it traveled how it is packaged how much carbon it produces,
what the supplier does and how it is disposed of.

5.5 Supply-Chain Resilience and Import Risk
It shows how fragile international supply chains can be. The COVID‑19 pandemic showed that. Rini and Fikri (2022)
studied supply chain resilience in the hotel industry and stressed that flexibility, disaster readiness and backup plans
are vital.
Because of this international hotel sourcing should not rely heavily on a single supplier or a single country. Hotels can
cut risk by:
Using suppliers
Choosing alternative suppliers within the country
Keeping safety stock for critical items
Assessing supplier risk
Having emergency procurement procedures

6. MAJOR TRENDS IN HOTEL IMPORT PRACTICES :-
6.1 Movement from Transactional Purchasing to Strategic Sourcing
It shows that Hotel purchasing move away from price-based buying, toward strategic sourcing. The data reveals Hotel
purchasing now looking at quality, supplier reliability, sustainability, innovation, risk and long-term value.

6.2 Growth of Digital Procurement
Electronic procurement systems are becoming increasingly important. Digital systems can improve purchase -order
management, supplier comparison, documentation, inventory visibility, and approval procedures.

6.3 Increased Use of Multiple Suppliers
Recent disruptions have pushed hotels to look beyond a supplier. Relying on suppliers helps hotels stay strong and
avoid risks, in the supply chain. Multiple suppliers give hotels flexibility and make the business more stable.

6.4 Growing Importance of Sustainability
Considerations are becoming more and more part of how suppliers are chosen and how purchases are made. The hotels
paying attention to sustainability to suppliers who act responsibly toward the environment to packaging to waste, to
certifications and, to the impact of transportation.

6.5 Data-Based Supplier Evaluation
Hotels can increasingly use procurement data to evaluate suppliers on delivery time, qualit y, rejection rates, price
changes, customer complaints, responsiveness, and sustainability.

6.6 Increasing Focus on Total Landed Cost
The purchase price does not fully capture the true cost of an imported product. Hotels must take into account factors.
These include freight charges, insurance, duties, taxes, exchange rate fluctuations, customs fees, storage expenses,
financing costs and possible losses due, to delivery delays. All these elements add up. Affect the final cost. That is why
focusing on the purchase price can be misleading. A complete view of landed cost gives a clearer picture of what a
product really costs. This helps hotels make buying decisions.

7. MAJOR CHALLENGES IN HOTEL IMPORT PRACTICES :-
7.1 Tariffs, Duties, and Taxes
Import duties and taxes can significantly change the final price of imported goods. Changes in tax policy can therefore
affect sourcing decisions.

7.2 Customs and Documentation
Importing goods from countries needs the right paperwork and must follow customs rules. Mistakes, in documents can
cause delays, extra fees or fines. It is important to get everything when preparing for international shipments.

7.3 Exchange-Rate Fluctuations
Hotels purchasing in foreign currencies face uncertainty because exchange -rate movements can increase the final
domestic-currency cost of imported goods.

7.4 Long Lead Times
International transportation generally takes longer than domestic procurement. This can create problems when hotels
require urgent supplies or replacement equipment.

7.5 Quality Problems
Imported products may sometimes fail to meet the required specifications. Quality inspection and supplier certification
are therefore important.

7.6 Supplier Dependency
Relying on one international supplier can put hotels at risk. It is important to find suppliers, for essential products. This
helps reduce the chances of disruption. Having backup options makes operations more stable.

7.7 Sustainability Concerns
Long-distance transportation and excessive packaging can increase the environmental impact of imported products.

7.8 Employee Skills
International procurement demands a range of skills including purchasing, finance, customs, logistics, technology and
negotiation. When employees do not have the expertise mistakes become more likely. This can lead to costs and slower
operations. It is important to have staff who understand these areas well. Without training the entire procurement
process can suffer. Companies need to invest in employee development to avoid these problems. Having employees
helps ensure smooth international trade. It also reduces the risk of delays and financial loss.

7.9 Demand Uncertainty
Economic downturns and sudden crises can reduce hotel occupancy and leave hotels with excess inventories. Import
purchasing therefore needs to remain flexible.

9. RESEARCH GAPS :-
The literature shows that there are areas that still need more attention. First research on hotel proc urement is fairly
advanced.. Hotel import practices as a separate field of study have not been explored enough. Second there is a need
for more real-world studies focused specifically on hotels in India. Most existing research has looked at hotel supply
chains in countries like Turkey, the UAE, Indonesia, Zimbabwe and the United States. Fewer studies have looked at
how imports work in hotels. Third it would be valuable to study sized hotels and hotels in smaller cities. The availability
of suppliers, transp ortation systems, employee skills, purchasing power and access to suppliers can vary greatly
between large metropolitan areas and smaller towns. Fourth future research should look into how the level of importing
affects hotel results. This could include st udying import spending, how quickly inventory turns over gross margins,
overall profitability and cash flow. Fifth another area worth exploring is what guests think about imported goods
compared to sourced ones. Research on guest perceptions could help hotels understand if international products really
add value. Finally researchers might develop models to see how things, like tariffs, exchange rates, how concentrated
suppliers are, lead times, sustainability efforts and digital buying methods impact hotel purchasing performance.

11. CONCLUSION :-
Import practices are becoming an essential part of today’s hotel management. By sourcing hotels can buy special items,
well‑known international brands, high‑tech equipment, unique comforts and goods that may not be easy to find at
home.
Yet buying from away brings many worries. These worries include tariffs, strict customs rules, changing exchange
rates longer delivery times, reliance on one supplier, transport dangers, quality issues, money risks and questions about
sustainability.
The studies we looked at show that hotels are moving slowly from buying habits to smart buying plans. Electronic
buying, helping suppliers grow, buying, having several suppliers, managing risk and making the supply chain stronger
are all becoming key parts of modern hotel buying.
Eight more studies that we added show that hotel importing is driven by things that’re not part of normal supply‑chain
rules. Taxes change the price; what customers say and think changes what items are chosen; a recession changes how
much is bought; smart management changes how well the hotel runs; how change is handled affects whether new
buying systems are used; money health changes buying choices; and staff issues affect how well the hotel works.
The main point is that good hotel import management needs a balance among price, quality, how easy it is to get, risk
being green what guests want and how well it fits the hotel’s goals.
Hotels should not bring in goods just because they are from another country look fancy or seem cheap. Instead hotels
should look at the cost what guests need, how the hotel runs, how good the supplier is, how laws are followed, the
effect on the planet and long‑term goals when they decide to import.
In India in smaller towns this topic gives many chances for new research. Comparing buying inside India, with buying
from abroad looking at how tariffs and rates change things how to manage supplier risk how green buying works and
how import habits affect a hotel’s profit could add a lot to hotel‑management studies.
