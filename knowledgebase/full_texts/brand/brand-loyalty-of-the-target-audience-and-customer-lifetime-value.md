Abstract.  Maintaining brand loyalty within the target audience is the main priority of both the business owner and the
team, which is improving the implementation of this business. Modern brand loyalty is at a crossroads, with businesses
refining brand strategies in the context of changing consumer preferences, the AI boom, e-commerce, and dynamic
economic conditions. This study aimed to analyse the relationship between customer lifetime value and brand loyalty,
to elucidate and identify the different factors that influence customer loyalty and lifetime value. The methodological
basis of the research was an integrated approach that combines general scientific and special methods of cognition for
a comprehensive analysis of brand loyalty of the target audience and customer lifetime value. The analysis identified
the key factors influencing this relationship, including customer experience, perceived value, personalisation, customer
segmentation, and optimisation of acquisition and retention channels. The study found that understanding and measuring
customer lifetime value correctly provides an opportunity to help business actors adapt marketing efforts, optimise the
costs of attracting and retaining consumers, identify and develop segments with high customer lifetime value, ensuring
brand loyalty growth and sustainable success in a competitive market, and allows for informed and strategic decisions
about the feasibility and effectiveness of marketing, sales, and support investments. The results revealed that assessing
customer lifetime value requires consideration of differences between customer segments and the risk of overestimating
customer value, which may influence the effectiveness of marketing decisions. The research carried out was of practical
value to business actors in the context of efficient budget allocation of available channels for attracting and retaining
consumers by business model and industry
Keywords:  customer acquisition cost; brand effectiveness; brand loyalty; business strategy; brand strategy

alty of the target audience and CLV . The research structure
was structured in such a way as to ensure the transition
from theoretical understanding of concepts to theoretical
and analytical analysis of customer lifetime value and brand
loyalty of the target audience, with an emphasis on the fea-
sibility of attracting new consumers. Within the frame -

Based on research on ways to optimise and increase
LTV (Joosten,  2024; Corde & Karpinski,  2026; Har -

increase of LTV , and the attraction of new consumers were
summarised and systematised. Given that LTV perfor -
mance and the strategic vision of brand loyalty are linearly
influenced by a score like CAC, the study used analytical
data from an SEO and marketing leadership company First
Page Sage to determine the optimal LTV to CAC ratio for
29 industries and the average ratio for each industry for the
period 2019-2024. Customer acquisition strategy evalua -
tion was based on anonymous analytics of 140+ B2B cam -
paigns for the period 2019-2024 (approximately 65% B2B
and 35% B2C of the US market, including business entities

through organic SEO (79% of campaigns) with paid search
(13%) and the remaining – a combination of paid and or -
ganic social, video marketing, and email in 20 different in-
dustries, as well as data from 103 B2C customers for the
period 2021-2025. CAC (organic CAC and paid CAC) was
assembled primarily from two channels: SEO and Organic
Social. Paid CAC data was collected from PPC/SEM and
Paid Social, excluding email marketing, direct mail, out -
door advertising, or other marketing channels due to the
limitations of B2C dataset. CAC results from organic and
paid channels were separately averaged, segmenting them
by industry.

Results
The study of the relationship between customer lifetime
value and brand loyalty is based on the need to under -
stand the impact of marketing intelligence and organi -
sational-oriented strategic and tactical decision-making
on customer satisfaction and loyalty. In accordance with
S.K.  Vishnoi et al .  (2026), strategic decision-making sig -
nificantly affects customer loyalty, despite the fact that tac-
tical decision-making is negatively related to satisfaction.
The results of measuring the positive and negative impact
of customer experience on brand loyalty and the corre -
lations between aspects of customer experience (product
quality, emotional engagement, staff behaviour, satisfac -
tion, etc.) and factors affecting brand loyalty (customer
satisfaction, perceived value, brand trust, and emotional
connections), along with levels of brand loyalty among
different consumer segments, prove that sustained posi -
tive interactions build long-term brand loyalty. Negative
interactions lead consumers to consider competitors’ of -
fers in both the short and long term (Anastassova, 2026).
The ratio LTV:CAC is the main indicator of brand profita-
bility, growth potential and overall business condition and
“consumer quality” . Given that LTV shows the amount of
income that a business entity is able to receive or receives
throughout the entire path of consumer interaction with
the business, the values of CAC and LTV indicators are
in opposition to each other – the business is interested in
reducing CAC and increasing LTV . By increasing LTV ,
businesses have the opportunity to increase their budget to
attract new consumers, increase competitiveness, and gain
more market share, resulting in longer consumer retention
and an overall increase in LTV (Table 1).

Discussion
In the dynamic landscape of research on the relationship
between CLV , CAC, and brand loyalty, understandingly
formation (customer experience, perceived value, trust,
personalisation, segmentation, etc.), the choice of chan -
nels for attracting and retaining customers, and their use
by business entities in order to increase business efficiency
is gaining importance. Many small and large firms are still
trying to quantify customer value, optimise relationships
with them, facilitate customer experiences, and identify
customer paths alongside the variety of existing innovative
channels and customer lifecycle models, offering ways to
optimise and improve them with carefully selected data
combined with state-of-the-art analytics (Baesens & De
Caigny, 2022). Given that the digital market poses challeng-
es for firms, making customer loyalty an important strate -
gic goal for sustainable success, structural models allow for
identifying mechanisms for building and attracting cus -
tomer loyalty, where perceived value mediates loyalty rela-
tionships by balancing satisfaction, trust, and brand image.
Digital market features pose challenges for firms, mak-
ing customer loyalty an important strategic goal for sus -
tainable success (Sahyaja et al., 2026). Focusing on the lev-
els of categorical variables that are individually associated
with CLV , researchers note a positive association with CLV
through Google campaigns and a negative association with
CLV through Facebook campaigns (Kvíčala  et al .,  2026),
which requires more research when choosing communica-
tion channels with the consumer in order to preserve and
increase loyalty. CLV is a strategic indicator for customer
segmentation and marketing resource allocation (Ali &
Shabn,  2024). According to Y .  Y an & N.  Resnick  (2024),
modern marketing analytics is based on customer lifetime
value modelling, which focuses on three main strategies:
feature development, improvement of the multi-stage CLV
model (customer churn modelling), and the combination
of discriminative and generative models at the individual
level. In customer-centric systems, CLV is essential, espe -
cially within analytical modelling.
In a highly competitive service sector (banking, IT,
hospitality, etc.), as a rule, information isolation creates
gaps in the customer experience, and short-term connec -
tivity is overestimated; since most companies are interest -
ed in instant transformation, the strategic imperative aims
to maximise the customer’s lifetime value. In addition, the
study noted that the intangible nature of the service sector
requires more efficient information circulation to maintain
quality and trust in the service, and the alignment of sales
and marketing functions based on information exchange
plays a crucial role in stimulating CLV improvement, as
emphasised by M.  Dhanalakshmi & R.  Jith  (2026). This
approach provides an opportunity to understand the na -
ture and capabilities of tangible and intangible assets
through the lens of differentiation and (perhaps further)
diversification to increase CLV . In a study by E.  Adiba  et
al.  (2025), it was emphasised that unlike traditional CLV
models that did not pay attention to contracts, the custom-
er lifetime value (CLV) model, adapted to the subscrip -
tion-based streaming industry, includes both contractual
dynamics and user activity. Because a longer subscription
yields a higher CLV with more predictable churn cycles, the
semi-Markov model captures the time users stay in specific
subscription plans and the transitions between those plans,
allowing, when analysing empirical data, the formation of
effective marketing strategies, loyalty, and resource man -
agement to maximise CLV in the streaming sector.
The study O. Dogan et al. (2025) stressed that BA tech-
niques are a key research area that will shape future CLV
modelling, especially in view of the increasing diversity and
speed of obtaining customer data that businesses can use
to optimise segmentation, improve personalised market -
ing strategies and ultimately stimulate customer retention
and profitability; this allows better prediction of custom -
er behaviour, tailoring offers to their needs and allocating
resources more efficiently which leads to improved satis -
faction and loyalty. In doing so, it should be observed that
the predominance of structured data does not take into
account potential information from unstructured data
sources, posing a problem for comprehensive CLV mod -
elling and signalling the need for more advanced meth -
ods of integration and analysis of such data. According
to B.  Londoño-Giraldo et al.  (2026), loyalty is defined as
a stable pattern of use and time billing, explained by five
constructs: perceived value, satisfaction, reputation, trust,
and commitment. Despite the global decline in satisfac -
tion, recent reviews suggest that post-consumption assess-
ment-an emotional judgment that results from comparing
expectations to perceived performance-plays a key role in
influencing buyer behaviour and ensuring customer loyalty
(Ramasamy et al., 2024). Customer experience is a major
driver of brand loyalty, and companies that prioritise un -
derstanding and improving this experience are more likely
to nurture loyal customers who contribute to sustainable
business growth (Rane  et al., 2023). Given that industries
have specific leverage over consumer effects on CLV , re -
searching and leveraging knowledge on the LTV:CAC ratio
and budgeting available consumer attraction and retention
channels according to an appropriate business model can
help companies adapt strategic priorities and gain valuable
information to maximise CLV , optimise customer experi-
ence, and increase loyalty.
Conclusions
The study showed that understanding and correctly meas -
uring the customer’s lifetime value enables business enti -
ties to understand the nature and relationship between
LTV and CAC, which will allow them to ensure the growth
of brand loyalty, optimise the costs of attracting and

When evaluating LTV (CLV), it is desirable for eco -
nomic operators to consider certain risks and considera -
tions, namely: segment variability (not all consumers have
the same LTV (CLV) and the avoidance of overestimating
the value of consumers, which is related to their behaviour
regarding discounts and returns of goods, which may dis -
tort the calculations of LTV (CLV). By understanding the
potential value of different consumer segments, business
entities can allocate marketing budgets more efficiently,
focusing on the priorities of preserving existing and/or
attracting the most valuable consumers. Increasing con -
sumer retention, encouraging referrals, and building brand
trust and reputation can help balance emotional and trans-
actional approaches to building loyalty and create more
compelling experiences tailored to individual consumers’
lifestyles. Understanding the nuances of LTV (CLV) and
its deliberate application in different consumer segments is
therefore crucial to building and enhancing brand loyalty. In
further scientific research, there is a need to focus attention
on the analysis and features of algorithms for the formation
of brand loyalty in accordance with technological changes,
transformation of brand architecture, and diversification.
