Abstract: Business leadership increasingly relies on the ability of organizations to create durable
stakeholder relationships through positive brand experiences, trust -building mechanisms, and
psychologically driven consumer engagement among Generation Z consumers. While brand loyalty
has been widely examined, limited evidence exists regarding the combined operation of experiential,
relational, and psychological mechanisms within leadership -oriented brand management,
particularly in emerging markets. This study inves tigates the direct effect of brand experience on
brand loyalty, the mediating role of brand trust, and the moderating role of psychological ownership.
The analysis is based on survey data collected from 403 female Generation Z consumers in
Bangladesh with at least one year of beauty -product usage experience. Empirical testing was
performed using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modelling (PLS -SEM) in SmartPLS 4.0,
supported by reliability, validity, collinearity, mediation, moderation, and predictive relevance
assessments. The measurement model demonstrated strong statistical quality, with factor loadings
ranging from 0.71 to 0.89, Cronbach’s alpha values betw een 0.84 and 0.95, composite reliability
above 0.88, Average Variance Extracted (AVE) values from 0.63 to 0.78, and Variance Inflation
Factor (VIF) statistics below 2.51. The structural model exhibited substantial explanatory power,
accounting for 37.4% of  the variance in brand trust and 40.2% of the variance in brand loyalty.
Positive Stone –Geisser’s Q -square predictive relevance value (Q² values) of 0.370 and 0.295
confirmed robust predictive relevance. The econometric results revealed a strong positive e ffect of
brand experience on brand loyalty ( β = 0.53, t = 13.79, p < 0.001) and an even stronger effect on
brand trust (β = 0.61, t = 18.73, p < 0.001), identifying experiential value as the principal driver of
relationship development. Brand trust significantly influenced loyalty (β = 0.33, t = 6.90, p < 0.001),
while the indirect effect of brand experience through trust remained highly significant (β = 0.20, t =
6.31, p < 0.001), confirming a stable partial mediation mechanism. Furthermore, psychological
ownership significantly strengthened the trust –loyalty relationship ( β = 0.20, t = 3.46, p < 0.001),
demonstrating that trust generates considerably stronger loyalty outcomes when consumers
perceive the brand as personally meaningful. The findings indicate that effective business leadership
and consumer relationship manageme nt depend on the simultaneous development of experiential
value, trust -based interactions, and psychological ownership. By integrating mediation and
moderation mechanisms within a single explanatory framework, the study contributes robust
quantitative evid ence to the business leadership literature and provides practical guidance for
leadership-oriented brand management, stakeholder engagement, and long -term loyalty-building
strategies among Generation Z consumers.

INTRODUCTION

In contemporary competitive markets, brand
loyalty can be understood not only as a consumer
outcome but also as a strategic concern for business
leaders seeking to build long -term consumer
relationships. Brand  loyalty has long been regarded
as one of the most valuable strategic assets for
contemporary businesses. It represents a consumer’s
long-term willingness to remain with a favored
brand, even when outside circumstances or
competing alternatives encourage s witching (Oliver,
1999). Importantly, loyalty extends beyond simple
repeat purchasing, as it represents a biased
behavioral response driven by underlying
psychological and evaluative decision -making
processes ( Jacoby & Kyner, 1973 ). In competitive
markets, loyal customers contribute to a sustainable
competitive advantage through increased purchase
frequency, recommending the brand to others, and
greater acceptance of higher prices ( Pravyana &
Soepatini, 2023 ; Deng et al., 2025 ; File & Prince,
1992). Therefore, brand loyalty can also be viewed as
a leadership -relevant outcome, as it reflects how
firms manage consumer relationships and shape
long-term brand value. Consequently, identifying the
factors that foster brand loyalty remains a central
concern in marketing research.
This issue is particularly relevant in the beauty
products sector, where consumption is closely linked
to self -presentation, identity expression, and daily
personal care routines ( Dai & Pelton, 2018 ). Beauty
products include items used to cleanse the body,
enhance appearance, maintain skin health, and
improve personal fragrance ( Alam, 2022 ; Parvin &
Chowdhury, 2006 ). Products such as face wash,
shampoo, cosmetics, and perfumes are deeply
integrated into everyday self -care practices,
particularly among younger consumers ( Park &
Chun, 2020). In Bangladesh, the beauty and skin-care
sector is expected to expand from USD 1.23 billion to
USD 2.12 billion by 2027, with an annual growth rate
of 8.1% ( Kale et al., 2020 ). This context provides an
opportunity to examine how consumer –brand
relationships may inform managerial thinking about
brand communication, customer engagement, and
relationship development. This strong growth makes
it important to examine the psychological factors that
shape brand loyalty within this market.
However, cultivating loyalty among Generation Z
consumers presents unique challenges. Generation Z,
generally defined as individuals born between 1997
and 2012 ( Xin & Lim, 2025 ), are digital natives who
have grown up in an environment characterized by
extensive online connectivity ( Theocharis &
Tsekouropoulos, 2025), social media interaction, and
abundant product alternatives ( Bhalla et al., 2021 ).
Compared with previous cohorts, Gen Z consumers
tend to demonstrate stronger expectations regarding
brand authenticity, transparency, and
personalization (Sakdiyakorn et al., 2021), while also
displaying higher brand-switching tendencies due to
constant exposure to competing options ( Thomas &
Setyobudi, 2024).
From a leadership perspective, these
characteristics make Generation Z a particularly
relevant consumer group for investigation. For
business leaders, understanding how younger
consumers develop and maintain relationships with
brands may provide a useful  lens for reflecting on
broader questions of customer engagement,
relationship building, and long -term value creation.
Because Generation Z consumers interact with
brands across multiple digital and physical
touchpoints, business leaders may benefit from a
deeper understanding of the experiences and
perceptions that shape consumer responses. In this
context, leaders are often concerned not only with
attracting consumers but also with understanding
how meaningful and enduring consumer –brand
relationships emerge over time. Examining the roles
of brand experience, trust, and psychological
ownership may therefore offer business leaders a
valuable perspective on how consumers connect with
brands and how such connections may relate to
loyalty development.
As a result, Generation Z represents an important
context through which leaders and business leaders
can explore the dynamics of contemporary
consumer–brand relationships. Although prior
research suggests that brand experience plays an
important role in shaping brand loyalty, much of this
evidence is derived from older consumer segments,
limiting our understanding of how loyalty develops

beauty industry, where product aesthetics, sensory

Positive brand experiences can strengthen emotional

However, the psychological processes through
which brand experience translates into loyalty
remain insufficiently understood. Thus, examining

Two constructs may be particularly relevant for
explaining how brand experience is connected to

consumers’ belief that a brand is dependable,
credible, and consistent in its actions. Prior research
suggests that trust often serves as an important
mechanism through which favorable brand
experiences are converted into loyalty -related
outcomes (Akoglu & Özbek, 2022; Huang, 2017).
As trust in a brand increases, consumers may feel
less uncertainty, which can make a lasting
relationship with the brand more likely ( Marmat,
2023). Psychological ownership, by contrast, reflects
a more personal and identity-based bond. It refers to
a psychological state in which consumers feel that a
brand belongs to them in a symbolic or emotional
sense (Pierce et al., 2001, Pierce et al., 2003).
When this feeling develops, consumers may
become more emotionally attached to the brand and
more personally invested in maintaining the
relationship. Although earlier studies have mainly
treated psychological ownership as a mediating
variable linking factors such as authenticity and value
co-creation with loyalty (Li et al., 2015; Wang & Yang,
2025; Xie & Lou, 2024 ), relatively limited attention
has been given to the possibility that it may also act
as a boundary condition that strengthens the
influence of brand trust on brand loyalty. This
distinction is important because trust and
psychological ownership represent different ways
through which consumers may evaluate, personalize,
and sustain their relationships with brands, offering
business leaders alternative pathways for
strengthening customer relationships.
Overall, this study positions brand loyalty as both
a consumer behavior outcome and a leadership -
relevant concern for contemporary organizations. By
examining brand experience, brand trust,
psychological ownership, and brand loyalty together,
the study provides a framework for business leaders
to understand how consumer –brand relationships
may be formed, strengthened, and sustained among
Generation Z consumers. Such understanding may be
useful for business leaders for reflecting on how firms
design brand ex periences, communicate reliability,
and encourage deeper consumer engagement
without relying only on transactional or promotional
strategies. In the Bangladeshi beauty products sector,
where younger consumers encounter brands across
multiple digital and ph ysical touchpoints, these
insights may support marketing managers and
business leaders in developing customer relationship
strategies that emphasize trust, meaningful
experience, and long-term relationship development.
LITERATURE REVIEW
Brand Experience and Brand Loyalty
Brand experience may be described as the
collection of sensory, emotional, cognitive, and
behavioral responses that arise when consumers
interact with different aspects of a brand, such as its
visual identity, packaging, communication, and
broader environment (Brakus et al., 2009). From this
perspective, brand experience is treated as the
consumer’s direct response to brand -related cues
rather than as a feature that exists independently
within the brand itself. This view also suggests that
experience may be shaped by a wide range of brand-
controlled stimuli, whether tangible elements such as
design and packaging or more intangible elements
such as communication style and environmental

Schmitt et al. (2015)  also argue d that consumers
often engage with brands not only for functional or
price-related reasons but also to satisfy experiential
needs. This highlights the idea that brand experience
carries value beyond rational evaluation and may
help explain why consumers remain attached to a
brand even when alternative options are available.
Consistent with this broader u nderstanding, brand
experience is commonly organized into four
dimensions: sensory, affective, behavioral, and
intellectual ( Van der Westhuizen, 2018 ). Taken
together, these dimensions provide a useful basis for
explaining how brand -related stimuli are converted
into complex consumer responses that, over time,
may contribute to the development of more enduring
consumer–brand relationships such as brand loyalty
Oliver (1997)  conceptualized loyalty as a strong
and enduring commitment to continue choosing a
preferred product or service in the future, even when
external influences or marketing efforts may
encourage switching. This view presents loyalty as a
forward-looking construct that refle cts consistency
of preference over time rather than a temporary,
favorable reaction. In a related sense, Pappu and
Quester (2016) described brand loyalty as a form of
attachment that consumers develop toward a brand,
which encourages repeated and consistent
preference for that brand (Coelho et al., 2018; Li et al.,
2012; Rubio et al., 2017 ). From this perspective,
loyalty is not merely transactional but also relational,
since it reflects a continuing bond between the
consumer and the brand beyond simple satisfaction.
Lien et al. (2015)  further suggested that loyalty may
be understood through consumers’ willingness or
unwillingness to switch to another brand,
particularly when changes occur in price, quality,
quantity, or product features ( Van der Westhuizen,
2018). Viewed this way, loyalty is reflected not only
in repeated purchases but also in the persistence of
brand preference under conditions that would
normally encourage comparison or substitution.
Nguyen et al. (2011)  similarly associate d brand
loyalty with an intention to repurchase in the future,
even when circumstances change. This continuing
preference suggests that loyalty is maintained over
time and is therefore compatible with consumer –
brand outcomes that develop through repeated
interactions and accumulated experiences. In
addition, Grisaffe and Nguyen (2011)  distinguished
between short -term and long -term loyalty. Their
distinction implies that genuine loyalty is better
represented by long -term commitment, where
consumers continue to favor the same brand over
time rather than responding only to temporary or
situational factors. Overall, this perspective supports
the idea that brand loyalty is best understood as a
stable and enduring commitment that develops
gradually through multiple consumer -brand
interactions rather than through isolated moments.
Building on experiential consumption theory
(Holbrook & Hirschman, 1982 ), consumers assess
brands not only in terms of functional characteristics
but also in terms of the sensory, affective, cognitive,
and behavioral experiences elicited by brand-related
stimuli ( Brakus et al., 2009 ). Because these
experiences are created through exposure and
interaction and accumulate, brand experience may be
thought of as an accumulation of the consumer's
responses to a brand that forms the basis for more
stable brand evaluations and preferences over time.
Consistent with this perspective, previous studies
have examined brand experience as a key antecedent
of loyalty -related outcomes a number of times
(Grisaffe & Nguyen, 2011; Iglesias et al., 2011; Ishida
& Taylor, 2012). Conceptually, if loyalty represents a
stability of tendency of continuing with a brand, then
the quality and richness of the experience consumers

Brand Trust as a Mediator Between Brand
Experience and Brand
Within contemporary marketing research, brand
trust may be viewed as an important foundation for
long-term consumer –brand relationships. It is
commonly associated with perceptions of reliability,
benevolence, and reduced risk. Syah et al. (2024)
described it as a cognitive belief rooted in consumers’
willingness to rely on a brand’s ability to perform as
expected, implying that competence and consistency
play an important role in shaping trust.
This understanding can be extended by a
relational perspective, in which trust reflects the
expectation that a brand will continue to act honestly,
responsibly, and with concern for consumers even
under difficult circumstances.
In increasingly  digital purchasing environments,
trust may also help lower perceived uncertainty and
risk by giving consumers confidence that a brand’s
promises regarding quality and authenticity will be
fulfilled (Tedeschi et al., 2017).
Viewed together, these perspectives suggest that
brand trust is not only an outcome of past
performance but also a forward -looking
psychological state that may help sustain loyalty over
time. Recent research has frequently identified trust
as a mediating m echanism through which brand
experience influences downstream outcomes.
Although brand experience may initiate engagement,
trust appears to be one of the main processes through
which that experience is converted into more durable
loyalty-related responses ( Akoglu & Özbek, 2022 ;
Bae & Kim, 2023 ). Supporting this view, Na et al.
(2023) found that brand trust and brand love play
meaningful roles in connecting brand experience
with brand loyalty.
Similarly, Akoglu and Özbek (2022) reported that
brand trust and perceived quality significantly shape
the relationship between brand experience and
brand loyalty, while Shukla et al. (2023) showed that
brand trust mediates the link between brand
experience and customer purchase intention.
Although prior studies support the mediating role
of brand trust, it remains unclear whether this
mechanism operates in the same way among
Generation Z consumers.
For Gen Z, trust may increasingly develop through
digital communication, online reviews, social media
content, influencer interactions, and AI -supported
brand engagement rather than through traditional
brand-building cues alone ( Guerra-Tamez et al.,
2024; Kim et al., 2023).
Compared with earlier generations, these
consumers encounter a greater volume of competing
brand messages and digital evaluations, which may
make their trust perceptions more fluid, selective,
and more difficult to translate into enduring loyalty.
Therefore, it is worth examining whether brand
experience still contributes to brand loyalty through
brand trust within this cohort.
This issue may be especially relevant in the beauty
industry, where brand consumption is strongly
connected to self -presentation, emotional
involvement, and appearance -based judgment.
Building on this reasoning, the present study
investigates whether brand  trust mediates the
relationship between brand experience and brand
loyalty among Gen Z consumers in this setting.
Accordingly, the following hypotheses are proposed:
H2: Brand experience is positively associated with
brand trust.
H3: Brand trust is positively associated with brand
loyalty.
H4: Brand trust mediates the relationship between
brand experience and brand loyalty.

Psychological Ownership as a Moderator
Between Brand Trust and Brand Loyalty
Psychological ownership (PO) is a state in which
individuals feel as though a target, such as a brand, is
“theirs”, even without legal ownership ( Pierce et al.,
2001). According to Pierce et al. (2001)  and
Raimundo and Proença (2023) , psychological
ownership can be understood through three key
features. First, it arises from individuals’ emotional
and symbolic interpretations of possession, reflected
in feelings of “mine” or “ours” , and concerns what
people subjectively perceive as belonging to them.
Second, it represents a psychological bond between a
person and a target, whether physical or abstract,
where the object becomes closely linked to one’s
sense of self. Third, psychologic al ownership is a
complex construct with both cognitive and emotional
aspects, including the awareness of individuals,
beliefs, thinking, and feelings regarding the
ownership target (Liu et al., 2012).
Psychological ownership is further understood as
multidimensional in that it is composed of self -
identity (the brand represents the self), self -efficacy
(believing in one’s influence over the brand),
belongingness (feeling included or associated), and
accountability (feeling responsible for the well-being
of the brand) (Abbas et al., 2022; Avey et al., 2009).
While psychological ownership reflects the
perceived sense of possession and self -connection
that people feel with a brand, in turn, its relevance
goes beyond conventional relational constructs such
as trust in a brand by providing a deeper, identity -
based explanation of how consumers form their
attitudes and behavioral intentions.
Although brand trust has been widely recognized
as a key mechanism that affects consumers’ attitudes
and behavior intention, trust shows primarily
consumers’ cognitive assessments of the brand’s
reliability, credibility, and integrity ( Marmat, 2023).
On the other hand, psychological ownership is
characterized as a subjective state where people
cognitively and emotionally internalize a target as
part of themselves, regardless of whether it is legally
owned by them or not ( Peck & Luangrath, 2023; Shu
& Peck, 2011 ). Brand trust has been widely
recognized as an important antecedent of brand
loyalty (Akoglu & Özbek, 2022; Samarah et al., 2022),
as consumers who trust a brand are generally more
willing to maintain long -term relationships with it.
However, prior research has largely examined this
relationship as a direct and uniform effect, providing
limited insight into the psychological condit ions that
may strengthen or weaken the influence of trust on
loyalty. In reality, trusting  a brand does not always
guarantee strong loyalty, as consumers may trust
several brands simultaneously without developing a
deeper commitment to any particular one. Therefore,
it is important to consider factors that explain when
trust is more likely to t ranslate into stronger loyalty.
Psychological ownership offers a relevant
explanation in this regard. In order to  fill in the gap,
the current study sets psychological ownership as a
moderator, with the expectation that the influence of
brand trust on brand loyalty is conditional on the
degree to which consumers feel the brand is their
own.
Psychological ownership theory ( Pierce et al.,
2001, Pierce et al., 2003) suggests that the strength of
the relationship between brand trust and brand
loyalty may depend on how strongly consumers feel
that a brand belongs to them. From this perspective,
trust may act as an initial condition for developing a
closer psychologica l bond with the brand. When
consumers view a brand as dependable and
trustworthy, they are likely to experience lower
uncertainty and reduced perceived risk, which may
create a supportive psychological setting for feelings
of ownership to emerge (Faria et al., 2022).
Trust is a cognitive belief that, by itself, is not
sufficient to create psychological ownership, as it
does not include the emotional and self -referential
elements that characterize feelings of ownership (Xie
& Lou, 2024 ). While trust allows continued
interaction with the brand, ownership develops only
when individuals begin to experience the brand as
personally relevant and self-defining.
According to psychological ownership theory
(Pierce et al., 2001), when psychological ownership is
high, trust becomes self -relevant, as the brand is
experienced as personally belonging to the
consumer. This integration strengthens brand loyalty
by activating feelings of attachment, responsibility,
and self -protection. Conversely, when psychological

Synthesis of Literature and Study Positioning
Taken together, these observations highlight
several gaps in existing literature. First, research
examining brand experience and loyalty has largely
focused on older consumer segments or developed -
market contexts, providing limited insight into
Generation Z consumers in emerging markets such as
Bangladesh. Second, although brand trust has
frequently been examined as a mediator, less
attention has been given to the conditions under
which trust translates more strongly into loyalty. This
issue is particularly relevant for managers seeking to
build lasting customer relationships, as trust does not
always lead to loyalty in the same way across
consumer groups.
Third, psychological ownership has
predominantly been conceptualized as a mediating
mechanism rather than a moderating factor in
consumer–brand relationships. These gaps are also
relevant because they limit understanding of how
managers can interpret consu mer experience, trust
formation, and ownership -like feelings when
developing long-term relationship strategies.
To address these gaps, this study investigates how
brand experience shapes brand loyalty among
Generation Z consumers in Bangladesh’s beauty
industry. Although previous studies have recognized
the mediating role of brand trust, much of this
evidence has been generated from consumer groups
other than Generation Z. Accordingly, this study
focuses specifically on Gen Z consumers and places
particular emphasis on psychological ownership as a
moderating factor that may strengthen the
relationship between brand t rust and brand loyalty.
In doing so, the study offers a more refined
explanation of how brand -related experiences may
develop into enduring loyalty among younger
consumers. This focus allows the study to connect
consumer-level psychological mechanisms with
practical concerns that business leaders may
encounter in brand comm unication, customer
relationship management, and sustained consumer

more than one -fourth of Bangladesh’s population

Generation Z a relevant group for consumer research.

because young consumers play an increasi ngly
important role in the country’s beauty and personal
care market. In addition, Generation Z is highly
engaged with digital platforms ( Wandhe, 2024 ),
making this cohort particularly suitable for
examining contemporary brand relationships in the
beauty market. The beauty industry was selected

Brakus et al. (2009) and Sahin et al. (2011). A sample
item is, “This brand makes a strong impression on my
visual sense or other senses.” Brand trust was
measured using the three -item scale developed by
Atulkar (2020) , including the item, “Product
satisfaction always generates brand trust in me”. The
brand-trust items were adapted from the validated
scale of Atulkar (2020).
Although some item wording reflects satisfaction-
and loyalty -related perceptions, the scale was
retained in its adapted form to preserve consistency
with the original measurement approach and to
maintain comparability with prior research.
Psychological ownership was assessed using five
items adapted from Kuchmaner et al. (2019) , one of
which is, “I feel that this beauty brand is my brand of
choice.” Brand loyalty was measured using three
items from Atulkar (2020), including the statement,
“I am always loyal toward the brand when I get the
required values.” All scales demonstrated
satisfactory internal consistency in this study.
Data Collection Procedure
Data were collected over a two -month period
from September to October 2025 from respondents
across all eight divisions of Bangladesh. The study
used a structured, self -administered questionnaire
developed from previously validated measurement
scales. The questionnaire was distributed in person
to potential respondents. Participation was
voluntary, and respondents were informed that their
responses would be used only for academic and
research purposes.
The target population of the study consisted of
female Generation Z consumers in Bangladesh who
use beauty or beautification products. To ensure that
respondents matched the study criteria, screening
questions were included at the beginning of the
questionnaire.
Respondents were asked to indicate their gender,
whether they were born between 1997 and 2012, and
whether they used beautification products. Only
respondents who met these screening criteria were
retained for the final analysis.
All main constructs were measured using a 7 -
point Likert scale, ranging from 1 = strongly disagree
to 7 = strongly agree, in order to capture respondents’
perceptions of brand experience, brand trust,
psychological ownership, and brand loyalty.
A total of 882 questionnaires were distributed to
potential participants. Among these, 404
questionnaires were not returned. Of the returned
questionnaires, 47 were excluded because they were
incomplete, and 28 responses were removed because
they did not m eet the screening criteria. After
applying these exclusion criteria, 403 usable
responses were retained for final analysis,
representing a usable response rate of 45.71%.
Given the difference between the number of
distributed and usable questionnaires, a non -
response bias assessment was conducted using an
early–late respondent comparison. The first 25% and
last 25% of usable responses were compared across
the main study con structs using independent -
samples t-tests.
The results showed no statistically significant
differences between early and late respondents, as all
p-values were above 0.05, suggesting that non -
response bias was unlikely to have substantially
influenced the findings. The detailed results are
presented in Appendix B.

latent constructs.

models and for examining complex relationships in

were used to determine the statistical significance of
the hypothesized relationships.

This paper has explored how brand experience
affects brand loyalty among female Generation Z
consumers within the Bangladesh beauty sector as
well as explored the mediating effect of brand trust
and moderating effect of psychological ownership.

The results support the hypothesis and give some

The findings confirm H1, which implies that the
positive influence of brand experience on brand
loyalty is statistically significant. This implies that
more interactive experiences with beauty brands
make consumers more loyal. The observation is
consistent with the previous studies that have
established experiential value as a means of
emotional attachment and as a motivating factor to
patronize once more ( Iglesias et al., 2011 ; Ong et al.,
2018; Zha et al., 2025). However, the present finding
is not fully consistent with all previous evidence. For
example, Chung and Welty Peachey (2022) found that
brand experience did not directly influence brand
loyalty in the golf club industry. This divergence
suggests that the effect of brand experience on loyalty
may depend on the product category, consumption
context, and the symbolic meaning attac hed to the
brand. In the present study, the significant
relationship between brand experience and brand
loyalty may be explained by the nature of beauty
products, where sensory appeal, emotional response,
self-presentation, and identity expression are closely
connected to consumer evaluation.
Thus, while brand experience may not
automatically generate loyalty in all industries, it
appears to play a stronger role in the beauty products
context among female Generation Z consumers. In the
background of the beauty industry, this finding
exemplifies the fact that brand interactions are
critical to the cultivation of loyalty, which is not
directly determined by the practical performance of
the functional product but is heavily based on th e
experience of the interaction. Due to the experience,
it seems that Generation Z consumers base loyalty
more on experience rather than solely on a basis  of
transactional aspects. The results also confirm H2,
showing that brand experience is a significant
contributor to brand trust. This shows that trust is
earned after regular and positive communication
with the brands and not just through promotional
interaction or brand name. In line with the existing
literature, recurrent positive brand experiences
reinforce the consumer trust in the brands
(Darmawan, 2018 ; Huaman-Ramirez & Merunka,
2019). Experiential engagement is significant when it
comes to developing a perception of trust among
Generation Z consumers.
 In line with H3, brand trust was determined to
have a significant effect on brand loyalty. The
observation is consistent with the relationship
marketing theory ( Möller & Halinen, 2000 ) that
asserts that trust is a key variable in the continuation
of the long -term consumer/brand relationships. On
the same note, Chaudhuri and Holbrook (2001)
showed that trust is a direct factor that leads to brand
loyalty as it lowers the uncertainty and builds more
confidence in the reliability of the brand among the
buyers. According to the current results, trust is a
significant predictor of loyalty in the  beauty market,
even in the case of Generation Z consumers, who are
regarded as being more experimental and facing a
wide range of brand offerings (Bhalla et al., 2021).
However, the trust –loyalty relationship should
not be viewed as universally identical across all
consumption contexts. Agustin and Singh (2005)
showed that loyalty determinants, including trust,
satisfaction, and value, may operate through different
and even curvilinear mechanisms in relational
exchanges. This suggests that the strength of trust as
a loyalty driver may depend on the nature of the
product category and the type of consumer –brand
relationship. In beauty -product consumption, trust
may carry stronger relational importance because
consumers apply these products directly to their skin,
body, appearance, and personal identity. Therefore,
the present finding refines rather than merely
repeats previous knowledge by suggesting that the
trust–loyalty link may be especially salient in
consumption contexts where perceived personal
relevance, bodily use, and self-presentation are high.

that the relationship between brand experience and
brand loyalty is partly mediated by brand trust. This
implies that the experiential contacts have a direct
and indirect effect on the loyalty, which is form ed
because of the creation of trust. That is, positive
brand experiences are associated with creating trust
among consumers in a brand, hence making them
more loyal. This finding is in agreement  with past
researchers who determined trust to be one of the
primary mechanisms by which experiential value will

CONCLUSIONS
This study examined how brand experience
contributes to brand loyalty among Generation Z
consumers in Bangladesh’s beauty products sector by
considering the mediating role of brand trust and the
moderating role of psychological ownership. The
findings provide a more integrated understanding of
how loyalty develops through consumer experience,
confidence in the brand, and a personal sense of
connection with the brand. Overall, the results
indicate that brand experience remains an important
foundation for loy alty formation, particularly when
consumers perceive the brand as trustworthy. The
study further shows that trust plays a central role in
translating favorable brand experiences into loyalty -
related outcomes. This suggests that positive
experiences alone may not be sufficient to generate
long-term loyalty unless they also help consumers
develop confidence in the brand’s reliability,
consistency, and value.
The results also highlight the importance of
psychological ownership in consumer –brand
relationships. Psychological ownership strengthens
the relationship between brand trust and brand
loyalty, indicating that trust becomes more influential
when consumers feel personally connected to the
brand. In other words, consumers who perceive a
brand as “their” brand may be more likely to
transform trust into continued loyalty. This finding
adds a useful explanation to the loyalty formation
process by showing that th e trust –loyalty
relationship may depend on the degree to which
consumers develop a personal and identity -based
bond with the brand. For Generation Z consumers,
whose brand choices are often shaped by self -
expression, emotional connection, and interaction
with multiple brand touchpoints, this mechanism is
especially meaningful.
From a business leadership and marketing
management perspective, the findings suggest that
loyalty should not be treated only as a result of
repeated purchasing or promotional exposure.
Instead, loyalty can be viewed as an outcome of how
effectively firms design experiences, build trust, and
create opportunities for consumers to feel personally
connected with a brand.
For managers in the beauty products sector, this
means that brand strategies should go beyond short-
term sales campaigns and focus on long -term
relationship development. Memorable sensory
experiences, consistent product performance,
transparent communication, and consumer-centered
engagement may help strengthen the psychological
foundations of loyalty.
The findings also suggest that marketing leaders
should pay attention to the emotional and symbolic
meanings that consumers attach to beauty brands,
particularly among younger consumers who may use
such brands as part of identity expression and self -
presentation. The study also offers practical insight
for customer relationship management. Since brand

groups, genders, industries, and cultural contexts.
The findings may not fully represent male consumers,
older consumers, consumers in other countries, or
customers in industries where brand experience and
psychological ownership operate differently.
Future research could test the proposed model
among different generational groups, male
consumers, and cross -cultural samples to examine
whether the relationships identified in this study
remain consistent across broader consumer
segments.
Third, the study used a non -probability
convenience sampling approach. Although this
method was suitable for reaching the target
respondents, it limits the ability to generalize the
findings to the wider population. Future studies could
use probability -based sampling methods or more
balanced sampling strategies to improve external
validity. In addition, the cross-sectional design of the
study means that the relationships among brand
experience, brand trust, psychological ownership,
and loyalty were examined  at a single point in time.
As a result, the study cannot fully explain how these
relationships develop or change over time.
Longitudinal studies would be useful for examining
whether positive brand experiences gradually build
trust, whether psychological ownership strengthens
with repeated interaction, and how loyalty evolves as
consumers continue to engage with a brand.
Another limitation is that the study relied on self -
reported survey responses. Although this approach is
common in consumer research, self -reported data
may be influenced by social desirability, individual
interpretation, or common method bias.
Future research could combine survey responses
with behavioral data, such as purchase frequency,
brand engagement, online interaction, or repurchase
history, to provide a richer understanding of loyalty
formation. Researchers may also extend the present
framework by including additional constructs such as
brand love, perceived value, brand authenticity,
consumer engagement, or social media interaction.
These variables may help explain further how
younger consumers form deeper relationships with
brands in digital and experience-driven markets.
This study is also limited by the absence of a
broader demographic profile of the respondents.
Since the survey intentionally collected only
eligibility-related screening variables, namely
gender, birth-year group, and beauty -product use, it
was not possib le to report detailed respondent
characteristics such as education, income,
employment status, region/division, marital status,
or frequency of beauty-product use.
