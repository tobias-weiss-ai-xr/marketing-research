Abstract
In an increasingly competitive labor market, final -year university students are expected not only to
possess academic qualifications but also to effectively communicate their competencies and
professional identities. Personal branding has emerged as a potential factor associated with
employability perceptions. This study aimed to examine the relationship between personal branding
and self-perceived employability among final -year students at Satya Wacana Christian University.  A
quantitative correlational design was employed involving 96 final -year students selected through
purposive sampling. Data were collected using the Personal Branding Scale and the Self -Perceived
Employability Scale administered through an online questionnaire. The data were analyzed using
Pearson product –moment correlation.  The results revealed a positive and significant relationship
between personal branding and self -perceived employability (r = .418, p < .05). Personal branding
contributed 17.47% to the variance in self -perceived employability, indicating that students with
stronger personal branding tend to perceive themselves as having greater employment
opportunities. These findings suggest that personal branding plays an important role in enhancing
students’ confidence regarding their employability and career readiness. Developing personal
branding skills may therefore support students’ transition from higher education to the labor market.

Keywords: personal branding, self -perceived employability, final -year students, career readiness,
employability.

Introduction
The Fourth Industrial Revolution has significantly transformed the labor
market through the rapid advancement of automation, artificial intelligence, and
digital technologies (Nur’aini et al., 2023 ). These developments have intensified
competition in the employment sector and increased the demand for graduates who
possess not only academic qualifications but also relevant competencies that enable
them to remain competitive in a dynamic labor market (Rahma et al., 2023; Wardani
et al., 2019).
Within this context, final-year university students face increasing uncertainty
regarding their transition from higher education to employment. One important
construct associated with this transition is self -perceived employability (SPE),
which refers to individuals’ perceptions of their ability to obtain and maintain
desirable employment based on their skills, qualifications, and personal resources
(Rothwell et al., 2008 ). Self -perceived employability originates from the broader
concept of employability, defined as an individual’s capacity to gain, sustain, and
progress in employment ( Hillage & Pollard, 1999 ). Previous studies have
demonstrated that high levels of self -perceived employability are associated with
greater career confidence, career adaptability, and psychological well -being,
whereas low levels are linked to stress, uncertainty, and burnout ( Berntson, 2008;
Oosthuizen et al., 2021).
Empirical evidence indicates considerable variation in self -perceived
employability among university students. Nasa et al. ( 2022) reported that 67% of
Generation Z individuals in Indonesia demonstrated high levels of self -perceived
employability, while 32% were categorized as moderate. Similarly, Nugroho and
Fajrianthi ( 2021) found that only 16.11% of final -year psychology students at
Airlangga University reported high self -perceived employability, with the majority
falling within the moderate category. Furthermore, Simiyu and Misuko ( 2024)
revealed that students with pessimistic perceptions of the labor market reported
substantially lower levels of employability. These findings suggest that self -
perceived employability is not homogeneous and may be influenced by multiple
psychological and contextual factors.
One factor that has recently gained attention is personal branding. Personal
branding refers to the strategic process through which individuals communicate
their unique values, competencies, and professional identities to others in order to
create a favorable and distinctive image ( Gorbatov et al., 2019 ). In the digital era,
personal branding has become increasingly relevant because individuals can
actively construct and promote their professional identities through social
networking platforms such as LinkedIn, Instagram, and other digital media
(Perdana & Juhana, 2024).
Several studies have reported a positive association between personal
branding and employability outcomes. Tomas and Bajs ( 2023) as well as Gorbatov
et al. ( 2019) found that individuals with stronger personal branding tend to report

Moreover, studies examining the relationship between personal branding

From a theoretical perspective, this study extends the literature on employability by
examining personal branding as a psychological and strategic self -presentation
resource that may enhance individuals’ perceptions of employability. It integrates
the personal branding framework proposed by Gorbatov et al. ( 2019) with the self -

emerging graduates. Based on these considerations, this study aims to examine the
relationship between personal branding and self -perceived employability among
final-year students at Satya Wacana Christian University. It is hypothesized that
personal branding is positively associated with self-perceived employability.

Method
This study employed a quantitative correlational design to examine the
relationship between personal branding and self -perceived employability among
final-year students at Satya Wacana Christian University. Participants were selected
using purposive sampling because the study required respondents with specific
characteristics. The inclusion criteria were: (1) active students of Satya Wacana
Christian University; (2) third - and fourth -year students from the 2021 and 2022
cohorts; and (3) students who actively used social media platforms such as
LinkedIn, Instagram, Facebook, Twitter/X, YouTube, or TikTok.
A total of 96 students participated in the study. The minimum sample size
was determined using the formula proposed by Lemeshow et al. ( 1990), which was
considered adequate for the objectives of the study. Data were collected online
through a Google Forms questionnaire. Participation was voluntary, and informed
consent was obtained prior to data collection. Respondents were assured that their
information would remain confidential and would be used solely for research
purposes.
Two instruments were employed. Personal branding was measured using the
Personal Branding Scale developed by Gorbatov et al. ( 2019), consisting of 18 items
with a Cronbach’s alpha coefficient of .945. Self -perceived employability was
measured using a 12 -item Self -Perceived Employability Scale adapted from
Rothwell et al. ( 2008), which demonstrated a Cronbach’s alpha coefficient of .803.
Content validity was assessed through expert judgment by psychology academics
who evaluated the relevance and appropriateness of each item in relation to the
measured constructs. Reliability analysis indicated satisfactory internal consistency
for both instruments.

Discussion
The findings of this study revealed a positive and significant relationship
between personal branding and self -perceived employability among final -year
students at Satya Wacana Christian University. This result suggests that students
who are better able to communicate their competencies, values, and unique
characteristics tend to perceive themselves as having greater opportunities to
obtain desirable employment after graduation. The findings support the notion that
employability is not solely determined by objective qualifications but is also
influenced by individuals’ perceptions of how effectively they can present
themselves to potential employers.
From a theoretical perspective, these findings are consistent with the
personal branding framework proposed by Montoya and Vandehey ( 2009), which
emphasizes the importance of creating a distinctive and credible professional
identity. Personal branding enables individuals to communicate their strengths,
achievements, and professional values in a manner that differentiates them from
others. In this regard, personal branding serves not only as a self -promotion
strategy but also as a psychological resource that enhances confidence regarding
career prospects. This interpretation aligns with Rothwell et al.’s ( 2008) concept of
self-perceived employability, which highlights the role of internal factors such as
self-confidence, perceived competence, and career readiness in shaping
employability perceptions.
The widespread use of social media platforms among participants,
particularly Instagram and LinkedIn, further supports the relevance of personal
branding in contemporary career development. Digital platforms provide
opportunities for students to showcase their achievements, skills, experiences, and
professional interests to broader audiences. Previous studies have demonstrated
that social media can function as a strategic tool for professional identity
construction and career advancement (Plachynda et al., 2024; Sembada et al., 2025).
Consequently, students who actively engage in personal branding activities through
digital media may develop stronger perceptions of their competitiveness in the
labor market.
The present findings are consistent with previous studies reporting positive
associations between personal branding and employability -related outcomes
(Murire et al., 2023 ; My et al., 2023 ; Tomas & Bajs, 2023 ). Individuals who
consistently communicate a coherent and positive professional image are more
likely to experience higher levels of confidence and perceived career opportunities.
Furthermore, personal branding can facilitate access to professional networks,

However, the findings should be interpreted with caution because previous
studies have reported mixed results. Gorbatov et al. ( 2024) found that the
relationship between personal branding and employability became significant only
through the mediating role of personal brand equity, while Salsaminia and Santosa

perceived employability. These inconsistencies suggest that the relationship
between personal branding and employability may be context -dependent and
influenced by additional factors such as labor market conditions, prior work
experience, career adaptability, networking behaviors, and the quality of personal
branding itself.
An important finding of this study is that personal branding accounted for
17.47% of the variance in self -perceived employability. Although statistically
meaningful, this contribution indicates that a substantial proportion of
employability perceptions is explained by other factors. Previous research suggests
that career self -management, academic achievement, work experience, career
adaptability, labor market awareness, and psychological resources also play
important roles in shaping employability perceptions ( Listyaratri, 2023). Therefore,
personal branding should be viewed as one component within a broader
multidimensional employability framework rather than as a sole determinant of
career readiness.
This study contributes to the employability literature by extending the
application of personal branding theory within the higher education context. While
prior research has predominantly focused on employees, entrepreneurs, or
professionals, the present study demonstrates that personal branding is also
relevant during the university -to-work transition. The findings provide empirical
support for integrating Gorbatov et al.’s ( 2019) personal branding framework with
Rothwell et al.’s ( 2008) self -perceived employability model in understanding
students’ career development processes.
Another theoretical contribution lies in positioning personal branding as a
psychological and strategic self -management resource. In the context of increasing
labor market uncertainty and digitalization, students are r equired not only to
acquire competencies but also to effectively communicate those competencies to
relevant stakeholders. This suggests that personal branding may function as a
mechanism through which individuals transform human capital into perceived labor
market value.
From a practical standpoint, the findings highlight the importance of
incorporating personal branding development into university career preparation
programs. Higher education institutions may provide workshops, mentoring
programs, and digital portfolio development initiatives that help students build
professional identities and strengthen their online presence. Career centers can also
encourage students to strategically utilize professional networking platforms such
as LinkedIn to enhance their visibility and employability prospects.  Furthermore,
the results underscore the growing importance of digital career management skills.
As recruitment processes increasingly involve online screening and professional
networking, students who effectively manage their personal brands may gain
competitive advantages in securing employment opportunities. Consequently,

Conclusion
The present study concludes that personal branding is positively and
significantly associated with self-perceived employability among final-year students
at Satya Wacana Christian University (r = .418, p < .001). Students who demonstrate
stronger personal branding tend to report higher levels of perceived employability.
These findings suggest that personal branding plays a meaningful role in shaping
students’ confidence regarding their employment prospects and readiness to enter
the labor market.
The study contributes to the growing literature on employability by
highlighting personal branding as a relevant psychological and career -related
resource during the transition from higher education to employment. Although
personal branding explains a significant proportion of employability perceptions, it
represents only one component within a broader set of factors influencing career
readiness.

Recommendations
Future studies are encouraged to employ longitudinal, mixed -methods, or
experimental designs to better understand the causal mechanisms underlying the
relationship between personal branding and employability. Expanding the sample
to include students from multiple universities across Indonesia would improve the
generalizability of findings. Additionally, future research may investigate potential
mediating or moderating variables, such as career adaptability, career self -
management, professional networking, self -efficacy, or digital literacy, to provide a
more comprehensive understanding of employability development among
university students.
