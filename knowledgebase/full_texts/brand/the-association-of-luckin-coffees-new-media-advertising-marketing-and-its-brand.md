Abstract. The coffee industry has been commonly acknowledged as emerging. Given the popularity
of Luckin Coffee in the Chinese market, this research aims to investigate the associations between
Luckin Coffee’s new media advertising and its brand equity, considering brand culture identity as a
mediator. Considering brand equity as a multi -dimensional concept which is constructed of brand
awareness, brand associations, and perceived quality, a total of six hypothetical relationships among
the constructs were developed.  This research collected online responses from 429 Chinese
consumers of Luckin Coffee. Structural Equation Modelling (SEM) was employed to examine the
associations. As a result, new media advertising was significantly associated with all brand equity
dimensions, on which, brand cultural identity was addressed as a significant mediator. The results
of this research successfully contributed to the limited understanding of fostering brand equity
through new media advertising in the coffee industry, and reporte d the essentialness of brand
cultural identity in brand management. Practically, this research provided a robust framework for
practitioners to enhance brand equity through effective advertising and consumers’ cultural identity
of the brand.
Keywords: Luckin Coffee, brand cultural identity, new media advertising, brand equity.
1. Introduction
The coffee industry has become a rapidly developing market with people’s demands for quality of
life and the rise of coffee culture [1]. In the global coffee market, driven by increased demand, coffee
prices have also risen constantly during January 2025 [ 2]. This has brought challenges to coffee
retailers and producers in selling products, as well as purchasing pressure on consumers. In China,
while the coffee market penetration rate in third, fourth, and fifth -tier cities is increasing, the
proportion of coffee shops in the first and second -tier cities decreased between 2023 and 2024 [3],
reporting a potential problem in the sustainable development of coffee industry in the Chinese market.
In the domestic coffee market, Luckin Coffee, as a representative brand, is in the leading position at
all city levels [4]. Given the growing maturity and rapid expansion of the landscape of the Chinese
market, there are some challenges for Luckin Coffee including the lack of innovation in marketing
and taste, and the int ensive fierce with other famous coffee brand, such as COTTI COFFEE and
Starbarks [5]. Therefore, more innovation and special marketing strategies are warranted for Luckin
Coffee’s survival and development in the Chinese market.
Behind the success of Luckin Coffee, brand effect plays a crucial role in current marketing
methods [5, 6] to improve the relationships between marketers and customers. For example, Luckin
takes advantage of awareness of two or more brands, such as Kweichow Moutai, Coconut Palm Juice,
and so on, to collaborate with them in social media which not only strengt hens the perception and
sensation between consumers and brands but also improves brand equity [6 , 7]. With the digital era
coming, new media advertisements drive associations between marketers and consumers [8]. For
example, Facebook ads have a positive impact on customers’ attitudes toward brands so online
marketers use them to increase brand awarenes s and perception, further enhancing brand equity [9]
[8].
However, the knowledge of the promoting role of advertising, especially new media advertising,
in brand equity cultivation is still limited in coffee brands [10-13]. With the scope of advertising, new
media advertising plays a significant role in improving purchase intention which contributes to

Therefore, this study aims to explore what factors affect those three dimensions and how to affect
brand equity, especially in the coffee industry because the culinary business, including cafes or

There are many studies on the coffee industry that explore the factors to influence brand equity and
the impact of brand assets on purchase intention in Southeast Asian countries [16 , 17]. However,
there is little study focused on Chinese local coffee brand. Therefore, this research will explore the
relationship and mechanism of impact between new media advertising and brand equity in the
Chinese coffee market, such as Luckin Coffee to fill the limited understanding of cultivating brand
equity through new media advertising in the coffee industry.
With the customers’ fatigue with co-branded products, Luckin Coffee seeks featured co-branding
to realize differentiation and innovation [5], including cooperating with specific cultural IP, such as
Black Myth: Wukong, and adding cultural elements in adver tising such as promoting new products
with the elements of Chinese New Year. Cultural elements play an important role in enhancing
cultural consistency with consumers, aiming at achieving a higher degree of brand recognition and
enhancing consumers’ understanding and loyalty to local brands [18]. Therefore, it is warranted for
coffee brands’ future expansion to explore identified content in new media advertising to empower
consumers’ perception of a brand.
On the relationship between consumers’ perceptions of firms’ marketing activities and their
psychological responses toward the firms’ brand, the cultural identity of focal brands [19] plays a
significant mediating role [20 -22], which has been ignored in coffee industry literature [23].
According to Amaral and Torelli [24], brand culture identity serves as a critical bridge between firms’
marketing activities and consumers’ psychological behavioral responses, facilitating con sumers’
emotional connections with local brands and enhancing brand equity. However, the mediating role of
brand culture identity has not been thoroughly examined in coffee brands. In the coffee industry, there
exists a common problem that homogeneous and standardized products lead to the lack of uniqueness
of a certain coffee brand [25], such as similar prices and similar marketing methods (e.g. celebrity
marketing and low -price marketing), where culture features can solve this problem by brand
storytelling with national culture to empower the value of a coffee brand whose worth transcends the
beverage itself [25]. Therefore, this study aims to fill this gap by investigating the mediating role of
consumers’ cultural identity with Luckin Coffee in the relati onship between Luckin Coffee’s new
media advertising and its brand equity, using Luckin Coffee as a case study. The understanding of
the mediating role of brand culture identity could provide robust guidance for Luckin Coffee’s
advertising strategies, meet ing their warranted needs in practical marketing strategy and brand
management.
With the fierce competition in the coffee market, brand development has become a crucial link to
enhance the interaction with consumers in the long run. Previous studies have paid little attention to
how advertising affects brand equity among Chinese coffe e brands, and lack exploration of the
uniqueness of coffee brands. Taking Luckin Coffee as an example, this study provides an innovative
suggestion for improving brand equity to integrate cultural elements into advertising to build cultural
identity in loc al coffee brands. This study addresses a critical gap in the intersection of marketing,

2. Literature review
2.1 New Media Advertising
Patrick et al. [26] define advertising constitutes a sponsored activity by a number of stakeholders,
with its primary aims including the instigation of awareness, persuasion (the building of brand liking
and the encouragement of brand switching), and the r eminder of target markets of the brand’s
availability. Typically, advertising is funded by a designated sponsor and designed to influence
individuals’ attitudes toward specific entities, including people, organizations, products, services, or
ideas [27]. I n the contemporary business environment characterized by intense competition,
advertising assumes a pivotal role as a medium through which organizations communicate with their
existing and prospective customers. The overarching objective of any advertising  campaign is to
achieve a specific purpose. These objectives may encompass a wide range of aims, including the
creation of awareness for a new product or brand, the dissemination of information regarding the
benefits of the product or brand, the cultivatio n of desire perceptions, the establishment of a
preference for the product or brand, and the persuasion of customers to make purchases [28].
Today, the average time spent by individuals on mobile phone internet is 3 hours and 39 minutes
per day. In comparison, the average time spent viewing television is 3 hours and 24 minutes per day
[29]. The advent of social media advertising as a distinct f orm of Internet advertising has further
facilitated the ability of businesses to reach consumers [30]. In the context of social media, consumers
engage in the creation of content, the dissemination of information, and the exchange of ideas, thereby
facilitating interactions with others. Consequently, highly active users can generate novel advertising
values for businesses and consumers [31]. Keskin and Ba ş̧ (2015) argued that consumer behavior is
influenced by social media tools. In order to fulfill the obj ective of generating sales, companies
allocate a substantial portion of their resources to advertising initiatives [32]. Cobb -Walgren [33]
found that the allocation of advertising expenditures has been shown to exert a favorable influence
on brand equity and its constituent components. Advertising has been demonstrated to facilitate
positive brand evaluations and attitudes [33]. This is crucial to the development of brand equity.
2.2 Concept of Brand Equity
Kevin Keller’s concept of customer -based brand equity concentrates on how customers’
knowledge and perception of a brand influence their responses to the brand's marketing efforts [34 ,
35]. Positive customer -cased brand equity is reflected when customers react more favorably to a
product and its marketing when the brand is mentioned, compared to when it is not [35]. Keller
emphasized the importance of understanding customer perceptions a nd preferences, creating brand
identity and positioning, developing marketing programs and communications that reinforce the
brand, and evaluating the success of these efforts. According to a model introduced by Aaker [36],
brand equity consists of five el ements: brand loyalty, brand awareness, perceived quality, brand
associations, and other proprietary brand assets, where brand loyalty is especially important for
competing with others [36]. As a dimension, other propriety assets do not have a significant
contribution to measure BE, therefore, this dimension has not been applied in various studies ([37];
[38]; [39]. Moreover, Aaker [36] thought that the last four brand equity dimensions can enhance brand
loyalty. Rizwan et al., [14] pointed out that three dimensions of brand equity, i.e. brand awareness,
brand associations, and perceived quality, have a strong positive influence on p urchase intention in
the food and beverage industry. In this study, because brand loyalty is often studied only in the context
of repeat purchases, the brand loyalty dimension is not included. Therefore, this study focuses on
those dimensions that influence the perceived identity of customers, three dimensions of brand equity
are included in this study, notably brand awareness, brand associations, and perceived quality.

Brand awareness can be defined as the extent to which customers are aware of the brand, and the
depth of their knowledge, including the association between the brand name and the product category.
According to Herdana[40], brand awareness is the ability of consumers to remember and recognize a
brand or product in their own minds. Raising awareness of a brand is one of the ways to introduce
and develop a brand to a wider market [41]. Consumers will buy a product or use a service based on
a brand they know and trust. This is important for every company to create brand awareness because
it can allow a brand to be chosen or purchased because it first appears in the minds of consumers [42].
In the coffee field, brand awareness has a positive and significant effec t on consumer’s purchase
decisions [41] and sales. One of the cafes in Bandung, namely De.U Coffee is one of the experienced
examples of an increase in sales through brand awareness [43].
Brand associations are central to brand equity [44]. brand associations are defined as anything
linked in memory to a brand and brand image as a set of associations, usually in some meaningful
way [45], involving attributes, benefits, and attitudes, which can be stored in consumers’ minds after
they are memorized [34]. brand associations or brand image is perhaps the most accepted aspect of
brand equity [39]. In the food and beverage industry, by aligning the brand associations the company
communicates with the brand associations consumers perceive can strengthen the relationship and
communication between brands and consumers, fostering brand equity [46].
Perceived quality is consumers’ perception of a brand’s products or services. Zeithaml [47] defines
perceived quality as a “product’s overall excellence or superiority”. This suggests that the concept of
quality is a customer-centric issue, as customers assess product performance through the lens of their
expectations and market comparisons [47]. Therefore, it is a subjective evaluation from customers or
users. The product brand plays a primary role in the food and beverage industry: its perception has
an impact on consumers’ preferences [46]. In a study about the coffee experience, La Won an d
Nuangjamnong [48] certified that improving content value and experience value that consumers
perceive contributes to consumers’ satisfaction and purchase intention.
2.3 Relationships Between New Media Advertising and Brand Awareness, Brand
Associations, Perceived Quality
A study conducted by Cobb -Walgren [33] indicated that advertising can exert a significant
influence on brand equity through a variety of mechanisms.
Advertising is an effective medium for increasing brand awareness and enhancing the probability
of the brand being selected by consumers [44]. Aaker conceptualized brand awareness must precede
brand associations. It is imperative that consumers develop an awareness of the brand in order to
establish a foundation of associations [49]. The attainment of successful brand awareness is
contingent upon a brand’s pervasive and readily identifiable presence within the market [50].
Implementing effective advertising  strategies is one of several key components of expanding
influence [50]. Advertising is a significant channel to improve brand awareness. Advertising is one
of the effective methods for marketers. Apart from these, [51] focused on the impact of YouTube
advertising perceptions on advertising value and the subsequent influence on brand awareness. It was
found that advertising value had a positive and significant impact on brand awareness. Thus, we could
say that advertisements positively affect brand awarene ss. Accordingly, the following hypothesis is
proposed:
H1a: New media advertising marketing is positively associated with brand awareness.
Brand associations or brand image is perhaps the most accepted aspect of brand equity [39]. The
phenomenon under customers’ consideration has the capacity to contribute to brand associations.
When these associations are stored in accessible memory, they tr anslate into “non -conscious but
reliable behavioral predispositions.” [44]. The process of constructing a robust brand identity is
predicated upon the establishment of a distinct brand identity. Strong, positive, and unique
associations contribute to stren gthening a brand and increasing its equity. Advertisement
informativeness has a significant positive impact on consumers’ perceived depth of brand
associations [52]. According to research from Efendioglu and Durmaz  [10], the perception of an

concerned with the promotion of brand awareness and the enhancement of brand equity [44]. It is
thus proposed that new media advertising positively affects b rand associations, which leads to the

H1b: New media advertising marketing is positively associated with brand associations.
 Advertising has the capacity to influence the perception of brand quality and the usage experience

H1c: New media advertising marketing is positively associated with perceived quality.
2.4 Brand Cultural Identity
Cultural identity is defined as the concept of nationality or regionalism that is inherent within each
ethnic group, subsequently influencing their integration within the social structure. According to
Clark [55], the term refers to the extent to which individuals within a given cultural context recognize
and identify with a set of focal elements that serve to distinguish that culture from other cultural
contexts. Consumers’ identification with a particular cu lture will influence their preference for
products or brands associated with their cultural symbols. A well - developed, strong sense of cultural
identity provides meanings and symbolic values of one’s belongings to people from the same culture.
As a produ ct or brand, the symbolic meaning of one’s cultural element has been demonstrated to
enhance one’s self-identity [56]. As the level of knowledge of consumers increases, so too does their
propensity to purchase intangible cultural heritage products, a prope nsity that is particularly
pronounced in those consumers who possess a strong cultural identity [57]. According to the research
from He and Wuang [58], cultural identity has been demonstrated to be positively associated with a
preference for domestic brands. For example, the French and Japanese cultures are typified by
hedonistic products, such as those in the automobile industry ([59]; [60]. The re lationship between
brand culture and brand equity has been demonstrated to be positive, with a concomitant impact on
brand equity ([58]; [61]. Besides, McCracken  [62] thinks consumer goods are infused with cultural
significance derived from the ‘culturally constituted world’, with the role of advertising and fashion
serving as instruments for this infusion. It can be posited that the effect of advertising on a brand that
is culturally symbolic is positive [63]. According to Hajdas  [64], the “cultural brand narrative”
constructed by advertising, as an independent variable, significantly improves brand equity by
enhancing dimensions such as brand awareness, relevance, preference, and purchase intention. On
these bases, the following hypothesis is proposed:
H2a: Brand cultural identity is a positive mediator in the relationship between new media
advertising and brand awareness.
H2b: Brand cultural identity is a positive mediator in the relationship between new media
advertising and brand associations.
H2c: Brand cultural identity is a positive mediator in the relationship between new media
advertising and perceived quality.

3. Methodology
3.1 Appropriateness of the Method
To test consumers’ perception to Luckin Coffee’s service quality, this research adopts quantitative
methods by questionnaire. Theoretically, the service quality measurement methods can be broadly
classified into two dominant types: incident-based and attribute-based methods [65]. Specifically, an
incident-based method collects and analyses consumers’ perception to a brand and consumers’
experiences in enjoying brand products through qualitative interviews, while an attribute -based
method measures perceived quality on a multidimensional approach through a structured
questionnaire [65]. The incident -based method is more appropriate to measure customers’ quality
perceptions for simple structured services with one or few phases, rather than a more comprehensive
service process with multiple episodes during the service delivery [66]. Thus, the incident -based
method might not be suitable for this study. Furthermore, the service quality in this study is measured
using a hierarchical and multi-dimensional structure. In light of the merits of a quantitative attribute-
based measurement method, this property -oriented approach is deemed suitable for addressing the
research questions posed in this study. It is distinguished by its capacity to evaluate customers'
experiences from multiple perspectives, in contrast to the process -oriented method that focuses on
specific service encounters during interactions [67, 65, 68]. Based on the above reasons, an attribute-
based quantitative method was thus employed.
3.2 Data Collection and Participant
Before the distribution of the questionnaire, the Chinese version was translated into English
version which was distributed together with the Chinese version. This questionnaire has been
supported by ethics approval. Documents including information sheets and consent forms for
participants were sent out. Participants who agreed to participate in this survey before they answer
this questionnaire. A total of 429 valid samples were collected using a convenience sampling
technique: 153 participants from China and 276 participants from overseas countries. Among all valid
responses, the nationalities of respondents were mainly from overseas areas, accounting for 64.33%,
and the rest of respondents were from China (35.67%). A large majority of the respondents were
currently located in China mainland (45.22%). The ages ranged from 21 to 30 years old (51.98%).

3.3 Instrument Development
The questionnaire included three parts. The first part illustrated the research background,
instructions for answering questions and contact details of the researcher. The second part aimed to
understand players’ demographic profiles. The third part includ ed items measuring social media
engagement and cultural identity perception and consumer perception of the brand, including surveys
of brand awareness, brand associations and perceived quality. The five -point Likert scale with 20
items, developed by Ren et al., [15], Yoo and Donthu [69], Low and Lamb [70], Levesque and Pons
[71] and Pan et al., [72]. Three dimensions of brand equity items were adapted from Ren et al., [15],
Yoo and Donthu [69], Low and Lamb [70]. Cultural identity perception items were developed from
Pan et al.,  [72]. A 5 -point Likert scale with values ranging from one (strongly disagree) to seven
(strongly agree) was used. The tools of IBM SPSS Statistics 27, SPSSAU and AMOS version 28
were adopted to analyzed questionnaire and measure SEM.
3.4 Data Analysis
Firstly, a preliminary analysis was performed to provide insights into the data. Secondly, the global
(goodness - of - fit) and internal (reliability and validity) model fit to the overall dataset were assessed.
Furthermore, a path analysis was carried out  to test hypotheses of new media advertising - brand
cultural identity - three dimension of brand equity (brand awareness, brand associations and
perception quality), reporting statistical significance, effect size and directions of parameter estimates.
In addition to the hypothesis testing, the explanatory power of the model (R2) was examined. Lastly,
to test the brand cultural identity’s mediating effect, a multi-group invariance analysis was used. The
overall model fit to each data set was first examined, followed by a chi-square difference test (ΔX2)
between the unconstrained and constrained models. A mediating effect of brand cultural identity can
be confirmed once a significant ΔX2 value at the probability .05 level is found.

4.4 Discussion
Firstly, according to H1a, H1b, and H1c, new media ads have a positive and significant impact on
brand awareness, brand associations, and perceived quality. It can be argued that brands can still
enhance the value of the brand itself and improve brand assets by adapting to the development of the
times and utilizing new technologies for brand communication. Brands will build associations with
consumers or users through various new media advertising. These findings are in alignment with
previous studies, which argued that new media ads play an important role in brand awareness, brand
associations, perceived quality, and brand equity in social apps and auction industries [8 , 10, 44].
Apart from these, Rajavi et al.,  [12] found that ads expenditure has an important role in perceived
quality. Aileen et al.  [13] studied the effectiveness of advertising has a positive effect on brand
awareness.
Secondly, according to H2a, H2b, and H2c brand cultural identity, as a positive mediator, plays a
role in the relationship between new media advertising and brand awareness, brand associations, and
perceived quality. It is because when customers and a brand come from the same cultural group, they
tend to resonate and respond to the brand, which strengthens consumers’ brand perception and
purchase intention [24]. Brands can enhance self - equity by considering cultural elements in new
media ads. These facts are supported by studies conducted by Holt [22], Kobayashi et al.,  [20], and
Grzeskowiak et al., [21] in sports brands, and retail stores. Holt [22] argued that advertising needs to
build brand assets through cultural identity narratives. Kobayashi et al.,  [20] argued that advertising
plays an important mediating role in brand transnational development. Since brand development is
related to brand equity, it can be said that the current research has drawn similar conclusions to the
study. Grzeskowiak et al.,  [21] pointed that cultural identity has a positive mediating role on
consumers’ perceived quality of shops. Because physical stores are a concrete manifestation of a
brand, it can be posited that the present research has yielded conclusions that are analog ous to
previous studies.
5. Conclusion
5.1 Theoretical Implications
This study makes theoretical contributions to the literature on brand equity, advertising
effectiveness, and cultural identity, particularly within the context of the coffee industry and Chinese
local brands such as Luckin Coffee.
Firstly, the study addresses a critical gap in understanding the cross -industry variability of
advertising effectiveness. New media advertising is a catalyst for brand equity in homogenized
markets. While prior researches have highlighted the role of adver tising in building brand equity
across industries (e.g., luxury, automotive), this study emphasizes the unique significance of  new
media advertising in the coffee sector, which is characterized by product homogeneity and intense
competition [11, 12, 25]. Unlike luxury or automotive brands that rely on exclusivity, coffee brands
must leverage dynamic, digitally native strategies—such as co-branding with cultural IP (e.g. Black
Myth: Wukong) and integrating cultural elements (e.g., Chinese traditional festival themes) —to
differentiate themselves [5]. This finding enriches advertising theory by demonstrating how platform-
specific content (e.g. social media campaigns) can enh ance brand awareness,  brand associations,
and perceived quality —key dimensions of brand equity [14] — in an industry where functional

207
differentiation is limited. It also challenges generalized assumptions about advertising elasticity,
showing that in homogenized markets, content innovation and cultural relevance outweigh sheer
advertising expenditure in driving effectiveness [12]. Besides, unlike the luxury or automotive sectors,
where advertising focuses on cultivating long -term brand prestige, the coffee industry prioritizes
immediate purchase intention through tactics such as social media -driven co -branding and time -
limited cultural ca mpaigns [11, 12, 6, 7]. This finding contributes to consumer behavior theory by
demonstrating how short-term, culturally resonant advertising can drive tangible outcomes—such as
sales and repeat purchases—in a fast-moving, experience-based industry.
Secondly, this study illustrates the mediating role of brand cultural identity is significant. A critical
theoretical advancement lies in identifying consumers’ cultural identity with the brand as a pivotal
mediator between new media advertising and brand equity [19]; [22]. Existing studies on advertising
effects often focus on direct impacts on brand awareness or purchase intention (e.g., Budiman, 2021
[11]; Rizwan et al., [14], neglecting the mediating role of cultural identity. This research reveals that
brand culture identity  acts as a critical bridge between new media advertising (e.g., co -branding
campaigns with Moutai) and brand equity dimensions (brand awareness, brand associations, and
perceived quality). For instance, Luckin’s collaboration with Black Myth: Wukong, whose status as
a national cultural symbol, thereby enhances consumers’ perception of Luckin as a brand that
embodies “Chineseness” and modernity. This insight illustrates how brand cultural identity bridges
marketing actions (e.g., co -branding, cultural storyte lling) and consumer psychological responses
(e.g., loyalty, brand recognition), thereby enhancing brand equity. It highlights that in markets with
functionally similar products, cultural identity transcends mere product attributes to become a core
driver of brand differentiation [25].
Thirdly, this study contributes to local brand management and cultural capital enhancement. By
focusing on a Chinese local brand, Luckin Coffee, this study enriches global branding theory by
highlighting the role of national cultural elements in building l ocal brand equity [15 , 16]. Whereas
existing research on Southeast Asian coffee brands often emphasizes functional factors like price and
product quality, this study demonstrates that integrating cultural narratives into advertising can create
symbolic value that resonates with domestic consumers, fostering deeper brand loyalty [18]. This
aligns with cultural identity theory, illustrating how brands can leverage local cultural capital to
differentiate in saturated markets —an especially relevant mechanism for emerging -market brands
seeking to compete with global giants like Starbucks while affirming the cultural identities of local
consumers.
5.2 Practical Implications
This study provides actionable strategies for Luckin Coffee marketers and managers, coffee brand
marketers, industry practitioners, or researchers — particularly those operating in culturally saturated
or homogenized markets like China — to leverage new media advertising and brand culture identity
as tools to enhance brand equity.
For Luckin Coffee, managers and marketers should enhance brand equity through new media
advertising with cultural characteristics. Firstly, Luckin Coffee managers can leverage cultural IP co-
branding to strengthen brand associations. It should continue str ategic co-branding with culturally
resonant partners, particularly local cultural IPs (e.g., traditional festivals, iconic cultural symbols, or
emerging digital IPs like  Black Myth: Wukong). It is a possible marketing solution to collaborate
with Chinese literary or historical IPs (e.g., Journey to the West, Dunhuang culture) to create limited-
edition products or campaigns. For instance, a “Chinese New Year Zodiac Collection” could pair
coffee with symbolic designs or flavors (e.g., osmanthus latte for Mid -Autumn Festival), embedded
with stories of cultural heritage. Such collaborations enhance  brand awareness by tapping into pre -
existing cultural familiarity and fostering brand associations with authenticity and cultural relevance,
as demonstrated by Luckin’s successful collaboration with Kweichow Moutai [5]. Secondly, Luckin
Coffee marketers and managers can consider integrating cultural narratives into new media content
by developing storyt elling-driven campaigns on social media (e.g., Douyin, Xiaohongshu) that

highlight the brand’s alignment with local lifestyles and values. For example, new media marketing
officers of Luckin Coffee create short videos showcasing the journey of coffee beans from Chinese -
origin farms or partnerships with local artisans, emphasizi ng sustainability and community support.
During Chinese New Year, launch a campaign titled “Reunion in a Cup,” linking coffee consumption
to family traditions and modern lifestyles. Cultural narratives enhance  perceived quality by adding
symbolic value, as consumers associate the brand with shared cultural identities [18]. Thirdly, Luckin
Coffee managers and marketers should actively encourage consumers to create and share content that
reflects their personal connection to the br and’s cultural elements. For instance, Luckin Coffee’s
official account hosts a #MyChineseCoffeeStory contest, where users submit photos/videos of how
Luckin fits into their daily routines or cultural practices (e.g., enjoying a coffee during a traditional
tea ceremony), rewarding winners with cultural -themed merchandise or limited -edition products.
UGC amplifies brand engagement and builds brand loyalty by co-creating cultural meaning, aligning
with the theory of social identity and self -congruity [23]. Fourthly, Luckin Coffee marketers should
optimize targeted advertising for cultural identity fit by using data analysis to segment consumers by
cultural identity (e.g., Generation Z’s preference for modern vs. traditional cultural symbols) and
tailor ads accordingly. Luckin can run personalized WeChat ads for urban millennials featuring sleek,
minimalist designs paired with modern cultural references (e.g.,  contemporary art or tech trends),
while targeting older generations with ads emphasizing tradition and family values, which aligns ad
content with consumers’ cultural identities strengthens the mediating role of  brand cultural identity,
driving higher brand satisfaction and repurchase intention [24].
For other coffee brands, they should adopt culturally tailored strategies. First of all, other coffee
brand marketers could localize new media campaigns with regional cultural elements. Coffee chains
entering new markets (e.g., Southeast Asia, Europe) shou ld embed regional cultural symbols into
their advertising. For example, a Turkish coffee brand could use new media to highlight traditional
coffee rituals (e.g., fortune-telling with coffee grounds) alongside modern lifestyles, while a European
brand might emphasize artisanal brewing techniques tied to local heritage. This strategy addresses
the coffee industry’s homogeneity by creating unique cultural differentiation, as seen in Luckin’s co-
branding success with Chinese Valentine’s Day [6]. Secondly, coffee brands should invest in cultural
storytelling beyond product features, shifting focus from functional benefits (e.g., price, quality) to
cultural values and lifestyle alignment. Starbucks in Japan has successfully integrated local aesthetics
(e.g., seaso nal cherry blossom designs, minimalist packaging) into its new media campaigns,
enhancing brand relevance without direct price competition. This cultural storytelling transcends
product homogeneity, creating emotional connections that drive long-term brand equity [22]. Thirdly,
new media marketing officers of coffee brands should  foster online brand communities around
cultural identity (e.g., Discord, brand-specific forums) where consumers can engage with the brand’s
cultural narrative. Aston Martin’s onlin e community co -creation model [19] demonstrates how
stakeholder interactions strengthen brand identity, applicable here to cultural alignment. A Brazilian
coffee brand could create a community platform for coffee lovers to share stories about Brazilian
coffee culture, recipes, and sustainable farming practices, fostering a sense of cultural belonging.
For industries practitioners, they can harness brand cultural identity as a general strategy to
enhance brand value. Firstly, industry researchers can regularly assess consumer cultural values,
symbols, and narratives through surveys, focus groups, or social listening tools. Luckin could analyze
social media conversations to identify trending cultural topics (e.g., China chic, Chinese national
pride) and integrate them into ad content, ensuring alignment with consumer self -identity. Then,
industry researche rs should balance cultural authenticity with modern relevance, avoiding
stereotyping by blending traditional cultural elements with modern lifestyles. For Example, a
campaign celebrating Chinese craftsmanship could feature young artisans using traditional techniques
alongside modern design, appealing to both heritage-conscious and progressive consumers. Besides,
industry practitioners can measure cultural identity’s impact on brand equity by using metrics such
as cultural congruence scores (e.g., how well ads reflect consumers’ cultural self-concept) alongside
traditional brand equity indicators (awareness, association, quality). For instance, scales from self -

congruity theory [74] can be adopted to measure how cultural elements in ads enhance perceived
brand identity alignment.
In conclusion, for who want to seek to differentiate in crowded markets and to build long -term
brand equity through culturally resonant strategies (e.g., Luckin Coffee marketers and managers,
coffee brand marketers and industry practitioners or researchers ), can explore the intersection of
culture, new media, and consumer behavior to enhance brand awareness, positive associations, and
perceived quality; they can deepen consumer connections. By leveraging brand cultural identity as a
mediator, brands can fos ter emotional loyalty and resistance to price competition; they can gain
adaptable strategies such as cultural IP co -branding, localized storytelling, and community co -
creation, offering scalable models for both regional and global coffee brands.
5.3 Limitation and Future Research
It is imperative to acknowledge the limitations inherent in the interpretation of the results of the
present study. Primarily, one of the purposes of this study is to seek differentiation of coffee brands.
This article provides a cultural perspective on di fferentiating coffee brands. Future researches can
further combine marketing psychology with cultural theory to explore innovative methods. Second,
although the mediation effect has been confirmed in this paper, the specific contents conveyed by
new media advertisements that affects consumers’ perceptions still needs to be explored in future
research. Finally, it is important to note that the present survey was conducted exclusively online,
lacking in in - depth interviews and interaction with participants.  By interviewing with participants,
it is able to identify the causal mechanisms of consumers’ attitudes toward products containing
cultural elements and deriving the result on the phenomenon, which contributes to refining the
quantitative design.
