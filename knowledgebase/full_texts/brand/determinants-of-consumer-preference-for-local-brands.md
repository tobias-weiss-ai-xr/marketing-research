ABSTRACT: This article presents a comprehensive review of recent
literature on consumer preferences for local brands, focusing on 34
empirical studies published between 2018 and 2022. By employing a
systematic literature review method, the study identifies key facto rs that
influence consumers’ decisions to favor local brands over global
alternatives. These factors include cultural identity, emotional connection,
and economic considerations, with a particular emphasis on the role of
brand management, social identity t heory, and consumer behavior in
shaping preferences. The review highlights variations in consumer
preferences across different geographic and economic contexts, especially
in emerging markets, and explores the competition between local and global
brands. T he findings offer valuable insights for both researchers and
practitioners, shedding light on the complex drivers behind local brand
preferences and providing guidance for enhancing market competitiveness.
This review also underscores the importance of ethnocentrism, nationalism,
and strategic brand management in fostering local brand loyalty. Future
research directions are suggested to further explore these determinants and
their implications in diverse settings.
Keywords: consumer preference, local brand, decision making, literature
review

INTRODUCTION
In today's globalized world, consumer behavior has evolved significantly, with many young consumers in
emerging economies increasingly gravitating toward global brands. However, this shift poses potential challenges to
local industries, particularly in dev eloping nations where local businesses play a vital role in national economic
development. Global brands often dominate the market, potentially stifling the growth of domestic enterprises and
limiting opportunities for local producers to compete effectively. This trend raises significant concerns about the long-
term impact on local economies and their ability to achieve self-sufficiency.
Governments and local businesses have recognized these challenges and responded by promoting domestic
products to boost economic growth and reduce dependence on foreign goods. National campaigns and policies
encouraging consumers to "buy local" aim to fost er a sense of patriotism and cultural pride, which may counter the
appeal of global brands. In this context, consumer ethnocentrism —a preference for domestically produced goods —
has become an important factor in shaping purchasing decisions. Research by Mbaga et al. (2018)  and Tran (2020)
suggests that in many emerging economies, this sense of national identity can drive consumer loyalty to local brands,
contributing to the broader economic goals of these nations.

While the importance of supporting local businesses has been widely acknowledged, there remains a lack of
comprehensive understanding regarding the specific factors that influence consumers' preferences for local brands,
especially in different geographic and economic contexts. Existing literature often focuses broadly on consumer
behavior but does not always address the unique variables that contribute to local brand preference. This gap is
particularly relevant in emerging markets, where consumer behavior  is often shaped by distinct cultural, social, and
economic factors.
This study addresses this gap by conducting a systematic literature review of 34 empirical studies published
between 2018 and 2022. The research spans diverse countries, including China, India, and Pakistan, providing a
comprehensive analysis of the determ inants of local brand purchase intentions. By focusing on the regional nuances
of consumer behavior, this study offers a deeper understanding of how cultural identity, emotional connection, and
economic considerations shape consumer preferences for local brands across different contexts.
The findings from this review are crucial not only for academic inquiry but also for local businesses seeking to
enhance their competitiveness in an increasingly globalized market. By identifying the key drivers behind consumer
loyalty to local brands, thi s study contributes valuable insights that can inform strategies for strengthening local
industries and supporting national economic development.

METHODS
Study design
The decision to use a systematic literature review (SLR) methodology for this study was based on its capacity
to inform practices and policies. Additionally, SLR serves as a valuable tool for guiding future research endeavors,
addressing questions that con ventional studies may not be able to answer, and providing an opportunity to evaluate
theories regarding the causation of phenomena. In adherence to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews
and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines, SLR was execute d with diligence (Adnyana, 2023; Page et al., 2021).  This
study adhered to the fundamental principles of rigor, transparency, and replicability (Mallett et al., 2012; Pickering &
Byrne, 2014) . PRISMA was developed as a tool to aid in the systematic review process by clearly articulating the
purpose of the review, the methods employed, and the findings obtained.

Search strategy
The search strategy employed in this study was designed to systematically identify relevant research on the
antecedents of consumer preference for local brands. We utilized the Scopus database as the primary source for
retrieving academic papers due to its extensive coverage of peer-reviewed literature. Our search was limited to articles
published between 2018 and 2022 to capture the most recent advancements in this area. Boolean operators were
employed with search terms such as "purchase intention of local brands" OR "willingness to buy local brands." Articles
were filtered by language (English), and only those from peer -reviewed journals ranked in Scopus (Q1 –Q4) were
considered for review.

Eligibility Criteria
To ensure the quality and relevance of the studies included in this review, we established clear eligibility
criteria. Articles were selected based on the following inclusion criteria: (1) published between 2018 and 2022, (2)
focused on the determinants of local brand purchase intention or consumer preference, (3) empirical studies providing
quantitative or qualitative evidence, (4) published in English, and (5) sourced from Scopus-indexed journals. Exclusion
criteria included duplicate records, studies unrelated to consumer behavior or brand preferences, and articles without
sufficient empirical data.

Data Extraction
For each eligible study, data was systematically extracted to ensure consistency and facilitate comprehensive
analysis. The extracted data included the following elements: author(s), publication year, geographical context,
theoretical framework, key variab les studied, and findings related to consumer preference or purchase intention for
local brands. Additionally, citation counts were noted to gauge the impact of the studies in the field. The data extraction
process was conducted manually, ensuring that all  relevant studies were consistently analyzed across multiple
dimensions.

Data Analysis
The extracted data underwent descriptive and thematic analysis to identify key patterns and trends in the
literature. Descriptive analysis involved categorizing studies by publication year, country, and research stream (e.g.,
brand management, social identity, consumer behavior). Thematic analysis was employed to synthesize findings related
to the main determinants of consumer preference for local brands. We also examined the role of theories commonly
used, such as brand management and social identity theor y, and evaluated their impact on local brand purchasing

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
The results of the literature search conducted using the Scopus database, following the PRISMA 2020 guidelines

filters. Additional exclusions w ere made for articles not ranked in the Scopus tiers (Q1 –Q4), and five articles were
excluded due to discontinuation, cancellation, or inaccessibility. Furthermore, 12 articles were removed as they were
not directly relevant to the research focus. Ultimate ly, 34 articles were selected, all providing quantitative empirical
evidence pertinent to the research topic and aligning with the criteria established for this review.

Antecedents of Purchase Intention of Local Brand
The second theme under scrutiny in this research pertains to the determinants of purchase intentions for local
brands. In particular, the examination of both external and internal factors, such as brand perception and brand image,
is of utmost importance for understanding their influence on consumers’ purchase decisions (Bourdin et al., 2021; De
Villiers et al., 2018; Lopez -Lomelí et al., 2019; López -Lomelí et al., 2019; Safeer, Abrar, et al., 2022; Safeer, Zhou, et

al., 2022; G. Singh & Singh, 2022) . Some studies have explored the influence of brand authenticity (Safeer, Zhou, et
al., 2022; Song & Kim, 2022; Srivastava et al., 2021), brand credibility (Chin et al., 2020; Sekhar et al., 2022; Srivastava
et al., 2020), and brand loyalty (Hasan & Sohail, 2021; Slaton et al., 2020).
Previous studies have investigated the mitigating influence of ethnocentrism on the intention to purchase local
brands (Li et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2021; Verma & Naveen, 2021) . Research has focused on other aspects of brand
management theory, including brand expertise, brand familiarity, brand origin, and the utilization of brands as social
signaling instruments (Dagyte-Kavoliune et al., 2021; Safeer, Abrar, et al., 2022; Safeer, Zhou, et al., 2022; Sekhar et
al., 2022; Souiden et al., 2018; Xiao et al., 2021). Regarding the country chosen as the sample setting, 56% of the sample
focuses on developing countries, while 44% of the sample focuses on developed countries. Meanwhile, this study
identified six theories applied to explain the purchase intention of loca l brands: brand management, consumer
behavior, consumer culture positioning theory, country of origin, social identity theory, and theory of reasoned action.
Although the results were mixed, a substantial correlation between these variables and local brand  purchasing
intentions has been discovered (Firdaus & Kusdibyo, 2021; Hasan & Sohail, 2021; Kumar et al., 2009; Tran, 2020; Yu
et al., 2021).

Implications
The antecedent of a local brand's purchase intention refers to a factor or condition that influences a consumer's
intention to purchase a product or service from a local brand. The factors that precede it can vary depending on the
cultural, economic, and s ocial context, resulting in a customer’s decision. In general, the antecedents of local brand
purchase intent are mediated by small things that can have a large impact, including the existence of a cultural identity
that makes consumers more likely to buy local brands if they have a strong cultural or national identity. This sense of
belonging can give rise to a preference for products associated with their culture or country.
Furthermore, the perception of the quality of a local brand or the services offered by a local brand is a significant
factor in increasing the confidence of consumers who buy the brand. The existence of feelings of patriotism, trust and
familiarity, price and value of a product, social and environmental responsibility, e -WOM, cultural and symbolic
associations, perceptions of innovation, economic factors, marketing and promotion, and availability and accessibility
are triggers of local brand purchase intent ions. The better the service provider handles this predisposition, the more
likely the antecedent of local brand purchase intent will increase. This review provides a comprehensive, evidence -
based perspective on the predispositions that influence the purchase intent of local brands.

CONCLUSION
This study presents a systematic review of the literature on the determinants of consumer preferences for local
brands, drawing on 34 empirical articles published between 2018 and 2022. The findings highlight the significance of
various factors influencing  local brand purchasing decisions, including brand management, social identity, cultural
relevance, and consumer behavior. While brand management theory was the most commonly applied  framework,
other theories, such as social identity theory and consumer culture, also played critical roles in understanding local
brand preferences, especially in emerging markets.
The review also revealed that consumer preferences for local brands are shaped by both internal factors, such
as personal identification with a brand, and external factors, like cultural associations and marketing strategies.
Moreover, the importance of et hnocentrism and nationalism emerged as pivotal in driving local brand loyalty,
particularly in developing countries. These insights offer valuable implications for both academics and practitioners
seeking to understand or influence consumer behavior toward local brands.
Despite the comprehensive scope of this review, several limitations must be acknowledged. First, the study
only included articles published in English and indexed in the Scopus database, which may exclude relevant studies
from other databases or in different languages. Additionally, the review was limited to a five-year period (2018–2022),
potentially overlooking earlier foundational research that could provide a more holistic understanding of the topic.
Another limitation lies in the focus on quantitative empirical studies, which may neglect rich qualitative insights that
could offer a deeper exploration of consumer motives and attitudes.
To build upon the findings of this review, future studies should consider expanding the scope beyond Scopus-
indexed and English-language journals to include a more diverse range of research sources. Incorporating qualitative
studies could also enrich the u nderstanding of the emotional and cultural nuances behind local brand preferences.
Additionally, further exploration of how local brands can leverage digital marketing and social media to enhance
consumer engagement, particularly in the context of global c ompetition, would be valuable. Comparative studies
between local and global brands across various cultural and economic settings could also offer deeper insights into the
determinants of consumer brand preferences in different markets.
This review lays the foundation for future research to explore the complex factors driving local brand
preferences and provides practical insights for local brands aiming to enhance their market competitiveness.
