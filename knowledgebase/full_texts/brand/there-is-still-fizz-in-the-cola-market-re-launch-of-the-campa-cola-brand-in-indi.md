# There is Still Fizz in the Cola Market: Re-launch of the Campa Cola Brand in India

Abstract
Cola wars have been witnessed for quite a while across the globe. Recently,
the Indian conglomerate Reliance Industries forayed into the market with
“Campa Cola” , which had a distinct brand identity and nostalgia associated
with it. This case study analyses the Reliance marketing strategy from
different perspectives using the marketing mix framework. The authors
have used secondary data and descriptive analysis for the research. It
also highlights the company’s strategic approach to entering an already
overcrowded marketplace.

INTRODUCTION
The Indian soft drinks market was valued at USD
21.66 billion in 2025 and is anticipated to reach USD
33.54 billion by 2034, exhibiting a compound annual

soft drink sector encompasses a diverse range of both
carbonated and non-carbonated beverages, catering to
a large and primarily youthful consumer base.

REVIEW OF LITERATURE
Indian markets are flooded with brands which are
either newly launched or have been revived from
the past. Past research on the relaunch of brands and
products has focused on strategies which involved
nostalgic marketing or a wider branding strategy
called “Retro Branding” . The term “retro branding”
was formally coined and defined in the early 2000s by
marketing scholars Stephen Brown, Robert V . Kozinets,
and John F . Sherry Jr. They introduced the concept in
their foundational 2003 paper, “Teaching Old Brands
New Tricks: Retro Branding and the Revival of Brand
Meaning” (Brown, Kozinets, and Sherry, 2003). Retro
branding refers to the practice of revitalising or
preserving heritage brands by merging their historical
essence with contemporary elements. This approach
leverages consumer nostalgia. Businesses utilise this
strategy to establish robust identities and appeal to both
older and younger demographics. In India, various
brands have been relaunched using retro Branding.
Hector Beverages Private Limited has launched an
ethnic drink as a strategic approach to engage Indian
consumers. This initiative seeks to appeal to Indian
consumers by delivering not only health advantages
and nutritional value but also a sense of nostalgia and
emotional fulfilment through cherished memories. Paper
Boat, an ethnic and nostalgic beverage brand under
Hector Beverages, has transformed the Indian beverage
sector, compelling market leaders to innovate and offer
more nutritious drink options. While a relatively newer
brand (founded in 2013), Paper Boat specialises in “retro
marketing” by selling traditional Indian beverages like
Aam Panna, Jaljeera, and Kanji. Their brand messaging
is wholly focused on evoking childhood nostalgia.
By utilising imaginative,  sepia-toned narratives and
packaging that draws inspiration from the past, they
engage with historical memories instead of merely
depending on a legacy brand name.
In a notable development within the Indian beverage
sector, Rasna, one of the nation’s most reputable
drink brands, has officially declared the relaunch
of the renowned ‘Jumpin’ brand, which it acquired
from Hershey India in May 2025. This strategic move
marks Rasna’s bold entry into the rapidly expanding
Ready-To-Drink (RTD) market. Jumpin, previously
a household name under Godrej and later Hershey’s,
holds nostalgic significance for Indian consumers,
particularly millennials who were raised in the 1990s.

are excited to reintroduce Jumpin in a refreshed and
enhanced format. We will maintain the original brand

RESEARCH METHODOLOGY AND
DESIGN
This research paper aims to understand the current
market dynamics of the Cola market in India. It is
descriptive in nature, which seeks to explore how
Reliance, through the recently launched FMCG flagship
Reliance Consumer Products (RCP), has been able to
build this brand in the highly competitive Cola market.
This study makes use of a descriptive analysis approach
to analyse the collected data regarding Campa Cola’s
marketing mix (product, price, place, and promotion),
for the marketing strategy of Reliance in launching the
Campa Cola brand in India.
Secondary research was used to develop this
research paper. No primary data has been gathered
by surveys or interviews, as the study focuses on
understanding the market dynamics of the Indian Cola
market. A variety of keywords that were either directly
or indirectly connected to the research subject were
searched using Google and other search engines to
gather pertinent information related to the topic. Only
information that was publicly and freely accessible
was taken into account. The reliability of the data was
confirmed through multiple sources, and only data
that were relevant to the area of interest were included
in this research paper. Quality assessments for the
data included, but were not limited to: Time frame
(only the most recent data was utilised), consistency
across various sources, and preference for data
from governmental entities and prominent public
organisations over private institutions. Nevertheless,
limitations in the data are present, which are typically
encountered during secondary research. The data
utilised in this study was gathered from freely accessible
public sources through secondary research and has
been referenced appropriately.
DISCUSSION AND FINDINGS
Background: Competition in the beverages market is
not new. The rivalry goes back to 1898 when Pepsi was
launched in the market, directly competing with Coca-
Cola. This competition intensified in the late 1970s
and continued into the 1980s, ultimately becoming
recognised as the Cola Wars. The Cola War refers to
the long-time legendary rivalry between soft drink
manufacturers, the Coca-Cola Company and PepsiCo.
These two companies have participated in targeted
marketing campaigns aimed at directly competing
with each other’s product lines, particularly their
flagship colas, Coca-Cola and Pepsi. The competition
goes back to 1886, when John S. Pemberton developed
the original recipe for Coke. In the year 1892, Asa
Candler purchased Coke and incorporated Coca-Cola.
During the year 1904, annual sales of Coke exceeded
one million gallons. Pepsi was launched in the year
1898 by a pharmacist named Caleb Bradham as Pepsi
Cola (Bhasin, 2013). One of the most prominent cola
war campaigns was the “Pepsi Challenge” rolled out by
PepsiCo in 1975 where it asked people to blind taste
both Coke and Pepsi. Blind taste involved participants
being blind folded and offered both brands to taste
and give feedback about the taste and preference.

Key Differentiators in the Cola wars leading
to competitive advantage:
1. Brand Recognition and Loyalty:
• Coca-Cola: The Coca-Cola brand stands as one
of the most recognisable and valuable brands
globally. Its extensive history, iconic branding, and
profound cultural connections provide it with a
significant competitive advantage. The company’s
capacity to utilise its brand heritage, particularly
in international markets, has been instrumental in
sustaining a loyal customer base.
• PepsiCo: PepsiCo has also established a strong
brand presence, notably through its marketing
initiatives and sponsorships. Although it may not
possess the same historical brand equity as Coca-
Cola, PepsiCo has effectively positioned itself as a
vibrant and innovative brand.
2. Distribution Network:
• Coca-Cola: Coca-Cola’s vast and efficient global
distribution network represents a considerable
competitive edge. The company operates a highly
integrated system of bottling partners, distributors,
and retailers, enabling it to deliver products to a
wide array of locations with efficiency.
• PepsiCo: PepsiCo has created a similarly strong
distribution network, particularly through its
acquisitions of major bottling firms. This network
ensures product availability and bolsters its
extensive product portfolio.
3. Product Diversification:
• Coca-Cola: The company has adeptly diversified its
product range beyond traditional CSD. Acquisitions
such as Energy Brands (Vitamin Water) and the
launch of new products like Coca-Cola Zero have
allowed them to respond to evolving consumer
preferences and seize growth opportunities in non-
carbonated beverage categories.
• PepsiCo: PepsiCo has taken a more aggressive
approach in diversifying its product offerings,
encompassing not only beverages but also snacks
and various food items. Brands like Gatorade,
Tropicana, and Quaker, in addition to their beverage
lines, provide a wide and complementary array of
products.
4. Innovation and Marketing
• Coca-Cola: Coca-Cola’s commitment to innovative
marketing techniques, such as the introduction
of Freestyle soda machines and prominent
sponsorships (for instance, the World Cup), plays
a crucial role in sustaining consumer interest and
brand relevance. The company’s focus on promoting
its core products while adapting to local preferences
further bolsters its competitive edge.
• PepsiCo: PepsiCo prioritises innovation through
new product development and rebranding
initiatives, including the overhaul of its logo and
packaging, which enhances its appeal in the market.
The company’s marketing strategies frequently
target specific consumer demographics and
capitalise on cultural trends.
5. Economies of Scale
• Coca-Cola: Being one of the largest beverage
corporations worldwide, Coca-Cola enjoys
economies of scale in its production, distribution,
and procurement processes. This scale enables the
company to secure favourable terms with suppliers,
realise cost efficiencies, and allocate resources
towards marketing and innovation.
• PepsiCo: PepsiCo similarly reaps the benefits of
substantial economies of scale, which facilitate
its competitive pricing strategies and its capacity
to invest in new product development and global
market expansion.
6. Strategic Partnerships and Acquisitions
• Coca-Cola: Coca-Cola has strategically acquired
bottling operations and various beverage brands to
bolster its market presence and enhance operational
efficiency. A significant instance of this strategy is
the acquisition of Coca-Cola Enterprises’ North
American operations, aimed at consolidating
control over its distribution network.
• PepsiCo: PepsiCo’s acquisitions of prominent
bottling firms and its emphasis on entering new
markets and product categories contribute to
strengthening its market position and operational
capabilities. (Dr Buffalo, 2024)

Driving Factors for Soft Drinks in India:
• Rapid Urban Growth and Increasing Consumer
Base: Urbanisation remains one of the most
significant structural forces influencing demand
in the Indian soft drinks market. As an increasing
segment of the population moves to urban areas,
consumption patterns noticeably shift towards
packaged and ready-to-drink beverage options that
cater to faster daily routines and the convenience-
focused retail environment typical of city life.
The growth of organised retail, quick-commerce
services, and local convenience stores has greatly
minimised the barriers between consumer intent
and the point of sale, facilitating higher transaction
rates and more consistent brand interaction across
urban regions. Additionally, digital and social
media platforms have enhanced this trend, allowing
for product discovery and brand engagement
among younger consumers at a pace and scale that
traditional marketing methods could not achieve.
The climatic conditions in India also serve as a
continuous demand driver. Prolonged heat in many
regions of the country for much of the year reinforces
the appeal of soft drinks as an easily accessible and
immediate solution. Together, these elements not
only contribute to gradual market growth but also
interact in ways that amplify the market’s growth
trajectory over both the medium and long term.
• Middle Class Demand Growth and Increasing
Disposable Income: The growth of India’s middle
class and the rise in disposable incomes are among
the most significant structural factors propelling
growth in the soft drinks sector. As household
incomes rise in urban and semi-urban areas,
consumers are increasingly inclined to invest in
branded packaged beverages, premium product
options, and innovative flavours that were once
viewed as luxury items. This income-driven trend
towards premiumisation is fostering two distinct
growth avenues: A mass market focused on
value-priced beverages and a premium segment
aimed at affluent, health-conscious consumers.
India’s Gross Domestic Product (GDP) growth is
anticipated to be 6.5% for FY2024-25, indicating
the favourable macroeconomic conditions that are
bolstering consumer expenditure across FMCG
sectors, including soft drinks. The penetration of
soft drinks into rural and semi-urban regions is
gaining momentum, fueled by the development of
organised retail infrastructure and enhanced cold
chain logistics that ensure product quality and
availability beyond metropolitan areas.
• Health-Conscious Consumerism and product
innovation:  The soft drinks market in India is
currently undergoing a significant transformation
driven by product innovation, as manufacturers
adapt to the changing health consciousness of
consumers and their desire for unique beverage
experiences. Brands are channelling investments
into creating low-calorie, zero-sugar, functional,
and naturally flavoured options that effectively
merge refreshment with wellness. This wave
of innovation is bolstered by the addition of
fortified ingredients, including vitamins, minerals,
botanicals, and plant-based extracts, which address
specific health benefits such as immunity, hydration,
and digestive wellness. Furthermore, companies
are exploring distinctive flavour combinations that
draw inspiration from regional preferences and
global trends, thereby enhancing the appeal of their
products across various consumer demographics.
In addition, improvements in packaging formats,
such as single-serve, portable bottles and the use of
sustainable materials, are enhancing convenience
while addressing environmental concerns.

Now, with the launch of Campa Cola, the latest round
of the Cola war is between three brands: Pepsi, Coke
and Campa Cola. In 2022, Reliance Industries Limited
(RIL) purchased Campa Cola from the Delhi-based
Pure Drinks Group for a mere INR22 crore. Currently,
Coca-Cola dominates 50% of the Indian soft drink
market, whereas Pepsi accounts for 19.6%. Thumbs
Up continues to be the unquestionable leader in the
cola category, reflecting its robust brand identity and
consumer loyalty. The competition between Coca-Cola
and Pepsi persists, with both firms making significant
investments in marketing and innovation to attract the
attention of Indian consumers (Patil, 2025). Campa
Cola’s marketing approach focuses on fostering “Vocal
for Local” pride, evoking nostalgia, and implementing
competitive pricing strategies. Reintroduced by
Reliance Industries, it challenges the market by
offering lower prices than Coca-Cola and Pepsi, while
leveraging Reliance’s extensive offline and online retail
network to achieve unparalleled shelf visibility. Campa
Cola’s promotional strategy emphasises local pride,
nostalgic connections, and aggressive pricing tactics.
With its relaunch by Reliance Industries, it disrupts the
competitive landscape by pricing below Coca-Cola and
Pepsi, utilising Reliance’s vast retail network to ensure
exceptional shelf presence.
The phrase “marketing mix” was introduced by
Neil Borden, a professor at Harvard Business School,
in the late 1940s. He brought it to prominence during
his presidential address to the American Marketing
Association in 1953. Borden’s concept was inspired
by James Culliton’s notion of a marketing manager as
a “mixer of ingredients. ” Although Borden introduced
the general concept, the well-known 4 Ps of Marketing
(Product, Price, Place, Promotion) were subsequently
formalised by market researcher Jerome McCarthy in
his 1960 publication, Basic Marketing: A Managerial
Approach.
Campa Cola’s 4Ps strategy—Product, Price, Place,
and Promotion is centred on leveraging Indian
nostalgia while offering extreme value to disrupt a
market traditionally dominated by global giants.
Product: While retaining the iconic Campa Cola
logo and visual identity, minor updates were made
to packaging and branding to resonate with modern
consumers. What distinguishes Campa Cola from other
soft drinks is its wide array of distinctive and thrilling
flavours. Starting with the core beverage, classic Campa
Cola Original, which boasts an irresistible sweet,
caramel-flavoured carbonated cola that captures the
classic Indian taste of the 1970s and 80s. Along with
the core classic Campa Cola original, Reliance also
went ahead with the variant expansion by offering the
zesty and refreshing Campa Lemon. There is a flavour
to satisfy every taste bud. Additionally, the invigorating
Campa Orange is filled with citrusy brightness for
those sunny days when there is a need for a burst of
sunshine! While the older Campa Cola was offered in
glass bottles, the relaunched Campa Cola was offered
in contemporary Polyethylene Terephthalate (PET)
bottles, classic glass bottles, and stylish cans, offered in
various sizes from economical 200ml choices to 2-litre
bottles suitable for home use.
Price: One of the leading competitive advantages Campa
Cola had was the pricing advantage. Traditionally, over
the years, the Cola segment maintained price parity to
protect margins. Before Campa Cola, the market was
dominated by Coca-Cola with an estimated market
share of approximately 40 to 45 percent followed by
PepsiCo with an approximately 35 to 40 per cent market
share. Regional and local brands collectively had below
20 per cent market share. The competition primarily
centred on brand recognition, endorsements by
celebrities, visibility of refrigeration, and incentives at
retail locations. Pricing variations among competitors
were minimal, thereby maintaining profit margins
throughout the industry. Reliance did not enter the
market merely as another brand competitor but
rather as a challenger to existing systems (Chauhan,
2026). The brand was discreetly reintroduced in 2023,
priced at INR10 per bottle, which is half the cost of
competing brands (Mukherjee & ET Brand Equity,
2026).  A 200ml serving was priced between ₹20 and
₹25 across brands. Campa Cola was priced aggressively
at around ₹10 for a 200 ml serving, at almost a 50 per
cent discount. 500ml serving was priced around 30 to
40 per cent lower than competitors. This pricing move
is similar to the earlier disruption in telecom with
the launch of Jio in the year 2016, with free voice and
cheap data, which reduced competition dramatically.

In the FMCG sector, distribution generally accounts
for 20 to 30 per cent of the overall product cost,

warehouse integration and last-mile delivery. Last

outlets. This had a huge advantage in reducing the largest
barrier in FMCG, which is distribution acquisition

Promotion: Nostalgia marketing refers to the
psychological impact that fosters a sense of self-

thereby simplifying decision-making. This enhances
receptiveness to messages. Retro marketing involves the
innovative application of this concept. Previous logos,
typography, colour palettes, shapes, or slogans are
revived and adapted into a modern context. Nostalgia
serves as the effect, while retro acts as the instrument.
Reliance Consumer Products launched Campa Cola

consumers aged 40 and older, evoking emotional and
cultural connections to the original brand.

HOW COLA COMPETITION FIZZED
IN INDIA
The return of Campa Cola in the Indian markets has
fizzed the competition in the Indian Cola Market.
Coca-Cola and PepsiCo have begun launching new
product variations and reducing prices to maintain
their competitive edge. Reports indicate that Coca-
Cola has cut the price of its flagship cola to ₹15 in
certain areas, which has contributed to a double-digit
volume increase in the January–March quarter of 2024.
Domestic rivals are also recognising the influence
of Reliance. Sunil D’Souza, Managing Director of
Tata Consumer Products, acknowledged in a recent
conference call that the company was unprepared for
Reliance’s bold retail approach and is now implementing
corrective measures (Devi, 2025). Competitors have
also responded by:
• Increasing the discounts regionally
• By expanding into low price Storage Keeping Unit
(SKU)
• Strengthening the retailers through better incentive
programs
• Increasing investments in rural distribution
(Chauhan, 2026).
The intense competition has significantly challenged
India’s Cola king, Ravi Jaipuria, whose Varun Beverages
ranks as PepsiCo’s second-largest bottler outside the
United States. During an earnings conference call in
July, he remarked, “Of course, competition is present;
however, they will secure their share, and we will secure
ours. ” Although nearly 75% of the company’s revenue,
amounting to 126 billion rupees, is generated in India,
where it operates 36 bottling plants, Varun Beverages
is seeking growth opportunities abroad. In 2024, it
finalised agreements to acquire PepsiCo’s operations in
Tanzania and Ghana, thereby expanding its portfolio
to 13 countries. Despite these strategic initiatives, the
stock has declined by 22% compared to a year ago,
leading to a $4 billion decrease in Jaipuria’s net worth,
which now stands at $13.3 billion (Y essar Rosendar,
2025).
