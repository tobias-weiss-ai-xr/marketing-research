ABSTRACT
Background and Purpose: In Malaysia, the process of positioning halal-branded products
internationally has challenges in enhancing the performance and management of halal food
manufacturers. This underscores the necessity of integrating human resources, strategic management,
and governance policies (PTU) to enhance performance while remaining competitive in the sector. This
paper addresses the connection between Strategic Human Resource Management (SHRM) as well as
organization performance based on the governance policy as a moderating variable in enhancing halal
awareness among the food manufacturers

Methodology: The study utilized a quantitative design, as it was based on the Resource-Based View
(RBV) as well as Institutional Theory (IT), which required gathering data from 265 respondents in
Malaysian companies producing halal-certified food via purposive sampling. Statistical analysis was
performed descriptively and inferentially using SPSS and SmartPLS 4 software.

Findings: The evaluation indicates that both SHRM and governance policy exert a substantial influence
on organizational performance (OPERF). However, the policy of governance fails to significantly
influence the connection between SHRM as well as performance. These findings indicate the relevance
of measures to improve governance, development of halal talents, and SHRM practices within the halal
food sector.

Contributions: As educators and policymakers, there is a need to inculcate a combination of halal
governance principles and SHRM frameworks into curriculum and training programs. This provides
food manufacturers with knowledge and expertise to abide by regulatory frameworks and increase
competitiveness in the global arena. The paper contains important insights for the stakeholders as it
highlights the strategic nature of SHRM and PTU in enhancing a sustainable and competitive halal
ecosystem.

Keywords: Governance policies (PTU), strategic human resource management (SHRM),
organizational performance (OPERF), halal food manufacturing, Malaysia.

1.0 INTRODUCTION
The growth of industries centered on halal products and services is not a recent phenomenon;
it has been expanding quickly and demands strong performance in food manufacturing to
satisfy the varied needs of consumers from different racial and religious backgrounds, while
supporting the delivery of halal services to the market (De Boni & Forleo, 2019). Thus, the
halal concept is considered crucial for food businesses to expand across demographic as well
as geographic boundaries. It helps ensure adherence to established standards of safety, health,
as well as hygiene, which are consistently upheld and assured (Mohd Daud et al., 2023). Over
the preceding decade, the halal food industry witnessed significant expansion, reaching an
estimated value of about USD 6.9 trillion. This robust expansion is largely fueled by the rising
global Muslim population, projected to increase from 1.8 billion in 2018 to 2.5 billion by 2050
(EPU, 2023). Malaysia has also been actively working to ensure that its domestically produced
halal brands can successfully enter the international market through strategic planning.
According to the 12th Malaysia Plan, the country’s halal product exports are forecasted to
contribute MYR 56 billion by 2025 (EPU, 2023). This encouraging progress has heightened
awareness among both domestic and international stakeholders of Malaysia’s capability to
deliver high-standard, market-driven, and halal products that are Shariah-compliant, aligning
with the demands of Muslim consumers globally. This demonstrates that the attainment of
significant confidence in Malaysia’s halal sector, as well as backing from international markets,
which in turn promotes foreign investment across multiple industries in the country, including
food, pharmaceuticals, tourism, as well as cosmetics (Halal Development Corporation, 2020).
De Boni and Forleo (2019) even reported that most research involving empirical analysis or
country-specific cases in halal research frequently references Malaysia’s halal ecosystem and
acknowledged Malaysia’s efforts in establishing good governance policy worldwide. For
example, in the Italian context, only two studies by Di Foggia et al. (2011) and De Boni and
Forleo (2019) have focused on halal studies in Italy, a non-Muslim majority country. A
majority of Muslim countries, such as Indonesia, Rahayu et al. (2025) also carried out a study
on purchasing decisions for halal products, which cites other Malaysian studies, for instance,
Hanaysha (2018), Jaiyeoba et al. (2023), and Khan et al. (2022).
Nevertheless, in spite of the expansion related to the halal food industry, a dearth of
research addressing the impact of internal as well as external elements on the performance of
halal food businesses persists, leaving the area largely underexplored. Although De Boni and
Forleo (2019) has referred to the same theories as in our study (ie. RBV and Institutional
Theory (IT)) to determine internal as well as external aspects for their halal research in Italian
context, their study has focused on pasta industry by using market orientation, halal governance
system, global halal market competition and perceived market benefits by consumer as external
factors. In our research, the focus of our variables is more on strategic human resource
management (SHRM) as an internal factor, as well as governance policy as the external factor.
In this paper, the term governance policies are denoted by the abbreviation PTU, derived from
the Malay term Polisi Tadbir Urus, and is used interchangeably with the broader term corporate
governance found in the international literature. Furthermore, there is little research on halal
food performance that incorporates human resource dimensions, and the moderating role of
PTU towards halal food performance has not been deeply investigated by using a quantitative
approach. Earlier works on halal food performance have instead focused on other variables,
such as certification, knowledge, as well as supply chain aspects (Ab Talib et al., 2020; Abdul
Rashid et al., 2020). Studies exploring halal food manufacturing effectiveness within the
context of both internal management, like SHRM, as well as external management, including
PTU, remain scarce (Othman et al., 2019). The present work seeks to assess determinants
affecting the organizational performance (OPERF) within Malaysian halal food manufacturing
companies by employing a quantitative approach and Structural Equation Modelling (SEM)

2.0 LITERATURE REVIEW
2.1 The Relationship Between SHRM and OPERF
Within the research framework, SHRM is proposed to directly and positively impact OPERF.
This assertion is supported by multiple empirical research (Battour et al., 2021; Chineye &
Ogohi, 2020). From a universal standpoint, a direct link is assumed between SHRM as well as
OPERF. This relationship may be understood via the lens of the RBV theory, which argues
that SHRM enhances competitive advantage as well as OPERF by cultivating distinctive, hard-
to-replicate, as well as irreplaceable human capital (Collins, 2021). Thus, based on this theory,
SHRM has a positive impact on OPERF because it fosters a competitive edge in human
resources by encouraging employee behaviors that enhance overall performance (Al-Khaled et
al., 2020). In light of prior research demonstrating a positive association, as discussed above,
this study hypothesizes that:

H1: SHRM is positively associated with OPERF.
2.2 The Relationship Between PTU and OPERF
Every halal food production business contributes to fulfilling consumer demands and ensuring
satisfaction, which includes preserving the halal authenticity of all food and beverages offered.
Consequently, according to Hazban and Nasohah (2023) all food businesses must comply with
regulations as well as legal requirements to guarantee that halal and tayyib standards are
consistently maintained. In addition, PTU impacts OPERF as well as indicates a positive
correlation between strong governance practices as well as both operational and financial
outcomes (Yusoff & Alhaji, 2024). Moreover, the Malaysian Halal Certification Procedure
Manual (MPPHM) 2020 provides guidelines with regard to improving halal application
processes and halal assurance systems. It has also been shown to exert a positive influence on
OPERF as well as strengthen companies’ competitive advantage (Ali et al., 2022; Tan & Lim,
2024). In addition, research by Nguyen and Tran (2021) indicates that PTU can have a
significant influence on organizational outcomes. Based on these prior findings demonstrating
a positive relationship, this study put forward the following:

H2: PTU is positively associated with OPERF.
2.3 PTU Moderate the Relationship Between SHRM and OPERF
Governance policy, and also known as Polisi Tadbir Urus  in Malay terms (PTU) is
hypothesized to play a critical role in shaping the relationship between Strategic Human
Resource Management (SHRM) and organisational performance (OPERF). Prior empirical
literature suggests that while SHRM practices are essential to sustain a competitive advantage
and elevate organisational outcomes (Barney & Hesterly, 2019; Chineye & Ogohi, 2020), this
strategic linkage can be further optimized and reinforced through robust corporate governance
frameworks (Huselid et al., 1997; Martin et al., 2016).
To establish clear theoretical boundaries within this study, a sharp conceptual
distinction is maintained between internal and external mechanisms. Therefore, SHRM refers

strictly to internal human resource practices, such as recruitment, training, performance

It is important to note that although top management commitment operates within the
firm, it remains conceptually distinct from SHRM. The core focus of top management is
centered on strategic governance and oversight, whereas SHRM is dedicated specifically to the
operational design of human resource practices. According to Ramli et al. (2020), top
management dedication serves as an internal driver of halal compliance. In the context of this
framework, such dedication is viewed as the necessary internal organizational response
required to effectively execute external PTU mandates. Therefore, PTU and SHRM represent
separate constructs, operationalized as external compliance enforcement and internal resource
design, respectively. Based on these theoretical and empirical insights, this study proposes the
following hypothesis:

H3: Governance Policy (PTU) moderates the relationship between Strategic Human Resource
Management (SHRM) and organizational performance (OPERF).

3.0 RESEARCH DESIGN
This research utilizes a purposive sampling approach, targeting halal food manufacturing firms
that possess halal certification. The purposive sampling technique is applied since the
population consists of individuals meeting specific eligibility criteria (Ngah et al., 2023;
Sarstedt et al., 2022). Halal food manufacturers constitute the core segment of Malaysia’s halal
food production industry. Participants in this survey held senior management roles, like
operations or quality managers, as well as positions in relation to halal executive functions or
halal committee memberships, such as managing directors of micro, medium, small, along with
large enterprises in Malaysia.
3.1 Study Population
The participants included in this work consist solely of firms listed with the Department of
Islamic Development Malaysia (JAKIM), while any companies whose halal certifications have
lapsed are excluded. This research targets all organizations in Malaysia that implement the
halal food concept. The study’s locations were selected based on guidance from prior studies,
which highlighted the need for broader investigation throughout Malaysia, as earlier studies

to be included as respondents during the data collection period, as they fail to satisfy the criteria
of this research.
3.2 Sampling Techniques
For the present research, the investigator applied purposive sampling, also known as selective
or subjective sampling, in which organizations were identified from the Halal Directory of
JAKIM. The respondents have been chosen selectively to meet the study’s objectives, ensuring
that all participants possessed specific required characteristics (Ngah et al., 2023; Rowley,
2014). Most respondents were registered to attend halal training organized by the Halal
Training Institute (HTI) of HDC or held any positions that were involved in practicing halal
operations at their organizations.
3.3 Sample Size
For this study, the number of halal food manufacturing firms in Malaysia is considerable. As
noted by Hair et al. (2019a), the sample size should be established using statistical power
analysis, which identifies the lowest number of observations needed, given the complexity of
the model. The present study has successfully obtained 305 responses in total, surpassing the
minimal sample size requirement of 85. Moreover, having a larger sample can boost the
generalizability of the outcomes and offer a stronger and more dependable insight into the
studied phenomenon. Finally, we managed to gather 265 usable responses after data screening
from 305, as we must remove 9 incomplete responses and 31 outlier responses for further
analysis.
3.4 Measurements
The measurement scales for this study were adapted from established frameworks and
evaluated using a 7-point Likert scale. Specifically, the Strategic Human Resource
Management (SHRM) construct was modified from Gurbuz and Mert (2011), the governance
policies (PTU) instrument was derived from Latif et al. (2020), and organizational performance
(OPERF) was adapted from Aminah Baharuddin (2020). While Gurbuz and Mert (2011) offers
a robust foundation for evaluating internal human resource practices, the instruments by Latif
et al. (2020) and Amaninah Baharuddin (2020) provide unique empirical strengths tailored to
institutional pressures and the halal sector, respectively. Because both latter scales were
developed and validated within the Malaysian context, synthesizing these instruments ensures
superior contextual relevance and stronger content validity for the target population of
Malaysian halal food manufacturers compared to generic global scales. Consequently, this
integrated measurement framework accurately captures both the internal operational design of

3.5 Data Collection Process and Research Ethical Conduct
The process related to data collection for this investigation was executed with a major focus on
ethical research standards and strict methodological procedures. To guarantee the accuracy and
consistency of the research tool, a preliminary test was carried out before the primary data
collection. The design of the questionnaire was further refined in this pre-test with the aim of
ensuring that the questionnaire was clear in language, minimized mistakes, and estimated the
time spent on the questionnaire (Amaya et al., 2020). The pre-test included seven professionals:
three Senior Lecturers and an Associate Professor in the Faculty of Business, Economics and
Social Development, Faculty of Fisheries and Food Science at UMT, and two senior officers
and one officer of HDC, one officer of HDC academy (HTI), and one halal executive officer
of the Terengganu Entrepreneur Foundation (YPU). The input of these professionals helped to
enhance relevance, ease of administration, and clarity of the instrument. Moreover, an English
language specialist performed a face validity test to assess and improve language fluency,
understanding, and sentence structure according to expert feedback.
The pre-test was subsequently succeeded by a pilot study aimed at evaluating the
reliability and validity of the instrument to conform to the objectives of the study (Creswell &
Creswell, 2018; Sundram & Romli, 2023). The pilot study also assisted in the identification of
uncertainties and developing the questionnaire items. The investigation was executed in the
Kuala Terengganu region of the Terengganu state, given its leading role in halal food
production, where there are more than 100 registered halal companies in the area. Partnership
with YPU has helped the researcher to issue questionnaires to interested companies and
representatives, especially those who participated in halal food seminars that YPU organizes.
Then the primary data collection was performed utilizing the questionnaire of Google
Forms online survey. This survey link has been shared in different online platforms, such as
Facebook, Telegram, and WhatsApp groups, which are run by the Halal Training Institute.
These channels were used to focus on respondents who have a valid halal certification and a
position associated with the production and management of halal food. The group
administrators distributed the questionnaire link and urged their colleagues in the halal industry
to share the questionnaire.
The researchers used the Halal Malaysia Directory offered by JAKIM to reach out to a
selected number of organizations directly to supplement online distribution. Phone calls were
made to verify attendance and prevent cases of duplication, and to prevent directing ineligible
individuals. This process was tedious because some organizations were not willing to take part
in the process of data collection. Nonetheless, teaming up with various agencies like HDC,
HTI, and YPU has successfully increased respondents’ participation among halal talents and
those specific persons or positions who deal with food quality and operation.
Thus, the process of collecting the data commenced during October 2022 through June
2023, with responses closely followed and controlled during the process. The procedures
followed all the principles of ethics, where the respondents were kept confidential and were
free to participate in the entire process. Ethical considerations were taken seriously as
responsible researchers because we pre-tested the survey to determine its reliability and
validity. A formal letter by the faculty was also included to authenticate the process of data
collection. Additionally, all survey questions were carefully designed to avoid sensitivity or
discomfort for respondents, and participation was completely voluntary, with respondents
having the freedom to discontinue at any point without coercion or obligation to complete.
Prior to completing the online survey for this study, written informed consent was gathered
from each participant. Furthermore, we confirmed that our study adheres to the Declaration of
Helsinki dated 13th December 2024, that respect human participation in the survey and does

4.0 ANALYSIS AND DISCUSSION
4.1 Data Screening and Outliers
Data screening represents an essential initial phase in multivariate analysis, as it enables
researchers to detect breaches of multivariate assumptions (Hair et al., 2010; Islam, 2020).
Moreover, conducting preliminary data screening allows researchers to gain a deeper insight
into the gathered data (Pallant, 2020). For the present research, 305 questionnaires underwent
evaluation to guarantee that every item was completed. In this study, there is no missing data
involved, as the survey questions have been configured in Google Forms to require mandatory
responses before proceeding to the next section. The evaluation showed that 9 questionnaires
were omitted as they did not satisfy the researcher’s criteria or were incomplete, resulting in a
total of 296 questionnaires. Following this review, all remaining questionnaires were
systematically coded and input into the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS)
software for preliminary examination prior to analysis. Following this, all submitted data were
collected without errors; however, 31 respondents were removed given the extreme values,
leaving a final sample of 265 participants for this work. Additionally, a normality assessment
was performed by evaluating multivariate skewness as well as kurtosis employing Web Power.
The analysis showed that Mardia’s multivariate skewness (b = 19.75446, p < 0.01) and
multivariate kurtosis (b = 62.16976, p < 0.01) were significant, indicating that the data deviated
from normality. Hence, employing SmartPLS4 software was considered suitable for this
research.

4.5 Structural Model
The PLS-SEM approach refers to the regression analysis and indicates that multicollinearity
issues do not pose a significant problem in the present work, which is essential. The results
reveal that every VIF value is under five, in line with the recommendation proposed by Hair et
al. (2019a), verifying that collinearity does not constitute a major problem. Direct and
moderating effects were estimated using percentile bootstrapping (Chin, 1998) with 5,000
resamples at a 95% confidence interval. Hypotheses are considered supported when the beta
coefficients correspond with the suggested expectations, t-values are ≥ 1.645, and p-values are
≤ 0.05 (Hair et al., 2019a). Table 5 presents the results of all hypothesis tests, indicating that
the link between SHRM→OPERF (β = 0.25, t = 4.243, p ≤ 0.01) supports H1. The link between
PTU and OPERF (β = 0.097; t = 1.711; p = 0.044) also supports H2, confirming that both direct
hypotheses (H1 and H2) are validated, though at the p < 0.05 level rather than p < 0.01. The
bootstrapped t-value is 1.711 (p = 0.044), which is significant at the 0.05 level.
The moderating effect of governance policy (PTU) on the relationship between SHRM
and organisational performance (OPERF) was assessed using the two-step approach
recommended by Becker et al. (2018). The interaction term (PTU × SHRM) was not
statistically significant (β = −0.074, t = 1.127, p = 0.130), indicating that PTU does not
moderate the relationship between SHRM and OPERF. Therefore, H3 is rejected. According
to Hair et al. (2022), a non-significant interaction effect indicates that there is insufficient
empirical evidence to conclude that the moderator changes the strength or direction of the
relationship between the predictor and the outcome variable. Consequently, the relationship
between SHRM and organisational performance remains consistent regardless of the level of
governance policy.

4.7 Discussion and Implications
4.7.1 Direct Effects of SHRM and PTU on Organizational Performance
The primary objective of this study was to examine the direct relationship between Strategic
Human Resource Management (SHRM) and organizational performance (OPERF) ( 𝐻1 :
SHRM → OPERF). The empirical results presented in Table 5 reveal a positive and statistically
significant relationship ( 𝛽 = 0.250 , 𝑡 = 4.243 , 𝑝 < 0.01 ), thereby supporting 𝐻1 . This
finding demonstrates that comprehensive SHRM practices including targeted training, talent
development, and employee professionalization programs significantly augment the
knowledge, skills, values, and social capital of human resources. Consequently, these enhanced
competencies foster higher employee satisfaction and drive overall corporate performance.
From a theoretical perspective, this positive association is strongly substantiated by the
Resource-Based View (RBV), which posits that firm competitive advantage and superior
performance stem from the strategic cultivation of valuable, rare, inimitable, and non-
substitutable internal organizational resources. Empowering and managing human resources
strategically enables firms to build distinct capabilities that drive effective operational
performance. Furthermore, this outcome corroborates prior empirical literature highlighting the
critical contribution of robust SHRM frameworks toward elevating organizational outcomes
(Al-Khaled et al., 2020; Chineye & Ogohi, 2020; Showkat et al., 2019).
Regarding the second research objective, this study evaluated the direct impact of
Governance Policy (PTU) on organizational performance ( 𝐻2 : PTU → OPERF ). The
statistical analysis validates this hypothesis ( 𝛽 = 0.097 , 𝑡 = 1.711 , 𝑝 < 0.05 ), confirming
that PTU exerts a direct, positive influence on performance, albeit exhibiting a weak effect size
(𝑓ଶ = 0.015 ). This finding indicates that structured operational management centered on
rigorous halal standards and total food quality control remains a key determinant of
organizational performance. This directly aligns with earlier empirical research demonstrating
a strong link between corporate governance frameworks and positive financial and operational
outcomes (Fauzi et al., 2020; Yusoff & Alhaji, 2024).
The relevance of PTU is conceptually grounded in Institutional Theory (IT), which
underscores how institutional rules, norms, schemes, and routines shape structural
organizational behavior. In the context of Malaysian halal food manufacturing, complying with
recognized governance frameworks ensures regulatory alignment and maintains product
integrity, which directly translates into sustained marketplace credibility.

4.7.2 The Non-Significant Moderating Role of Governance Policy (PTU)
The third hypothesis asserted that Governance Policy (PTU) moderates the relationship
between SHRM and OPERF ( 𝐻3: PTU × SHRM → OPERF). As reported in Table 5, the
interaction term was not statistically significant (𝛽 = −0.074, 𝑡 = 1.127, 𝑝 = 0.130), leading
to the rejection of 𝐻3. These findings indicate that within the current sample, PTU does not
alter the strength or direction of the relationship between SHRM and organizational
performance. This suggests that the capacity of SHRM to enhance operational performance
remains independent of the degree of governance policy implementation among halal food
manufacturing companies.
This outcome aligns with Garavan et al. (2020), who noted that organizational context
factors do not uniformly function as significant moderators between human resource practices
and firm performance. Consequently, PTU contributes directly to organizational performance
(𝐻2) rather than acting as a catalytic mechanism that modifies the impact of SHRM. This
interpretation is consistent with methodological guidelines by Becker et al. (2018) and Hair et
al. (2022), who emphasized that an insignificant interaction effect underscores the absence of
a moderating relationship.
4.7.3 Practical and Policy Implications
To establish a resilient and highly scalable halal ecosystem, policymakers and industry
stakeholders must champion specialized educational and intensive training programs focusing
on halal awareness, strategic resource management, and governance refinement. Equip
manufacturing professionals with these integrated competencies to help firms adapt to
international regulatory expectations, minimize existing operational performance gaps, and
achieve the broader strategic objectives of the Malaysian halal sector.
Furthermore, these findings emphasize the necessity of embedding halal governance
and strategic HRM frameworks directly into academic curricula and professional training
modules. Sensitizing food manufacturers to both strategic human resource utilization and
compliance management will ensure strong regulatory adherence and industry
competitiveness. Finally, this study serves as a valuable empirical reference for students,
business practitioners, and policymakers, offering practical insights on leveraging independent
strategic internal designs alongside external governance policies to optimize performance
within the global halal industry. The findings provide important practical and policy
implications by demonstrating how strategic human resource management and governance
policy can be leveraged to improve organisational performance within the halal food
manufacturing sector. For practitioners, the findings highlight the importance of strengthening
halal competencies, developing strategic human resource capabilities, and implementing
effective governance practices to enhance operational efficiency and halal compliance. For
policymakers, the findings provide empirical evidence to support the refinement of existing
halal policies, competency development initiatives, and regulatory frameworks through
relevant institutions such as the Halal Development Corporation (HDC) and Halal Training
Institute (HTI). These efforts are aligned with the objectives of the Halal Industry Master Plan
2030 (HIMP) in developing a resilient, competitive, and sustainable halal ecosystem (MITI,
2023).

This study addresses a critical gap in the management literature of the global halal industry by
unlocking the mechanisms through which internal organisational strategies and external
regulatory systems drive organizational performance among Malaysian halal food
manufacturers. The empirical evidence demonstrates that Strategic Human Resource

Halal Industry Master Plan (HIMP 2030).

context, it opens several strategic avenues for future scholarly inquiry across the Nusantara
archipelago. Future studies should investigate whether distinct forms of organisational culture
influence the joint implementation of governance frameworks and human resource strategies.
Furthermore, given the rapid push for technological adaptation, exploring how halal
manufacturing firms can balance strict regulatory compliance with operational flexibility
through digitalization and automated tracking systems remains an urgent agenda. Finally,
applying this framework to other expanding halal sectors like pharmaceuticals, logistics, and
modest fashion in various economies will help confirm if these results are consistent across
different industries. This will deepen our collective knowledge of governance, strategic
management, and sustainable industrial growth.
