This study examines the influence of Green
Marketing Strategy, Green Brand Image, and
Brand Equity on the Sustainable Competitive
Advantage of Somethinc skincare products. Data
were collected from 130 Somethinc consumers
who had purchased products through e -
commerce, selected using purposive sampling
based o n specific criteria. Online questionnaire
data were analyzed using PLS -SEM with
SmartPLS. The results show that Green
Marketing Strategy has a positive but
insignificant effect on Sustainable Competitive
Advantage, while Green Brand Image and Brand
Equity have positive and significant effects.
Brand Equity has the strongest influence,
followed by Green Brand Image. These findings
indicate that strengthening Green Brand Image
and Brand Equity can enhance Sustainable
Competitive Advantage, while Green Marketing
Strategy requires further optimization.

INTRODUCTION
Changes in consumer behavior  in recent years have shown an increasing
attention to environmental and sustainability issues. This condition encourages
companies to not only focus on product quality and sales but also to integrate
environmental aspects into their business strategies. O ne of the emerging
approaches is green marketing, which is a marketing strategy that incorporates
sustainability values through the use of eco -friendly materials, waste reduction,
and communication regarding environmental concerns. Consistent
implementation of green marketing can shape a positive perception of the brand,
increase consumer loyalty, and support the achievement of long -term
competitive advantage (Sheykhan et al., 2024). This phenomenon is becoming
increasingly relevant in the skincare industry  as consumers are more and more
considering the safety of ingredients, production processes, and environmental
impact when choosing products.
In that context, Green Brand Image becomes one of the important factors
because it reflects consumers' perceptions  of the brand's commitment to
environmental sustainability. A positive green image can enhance consumer
trust, preference, and tendency to choose a brand over competitors (Jabeen et al.,
2023). That perception can further strengthen Brand Equity, which is the added
value attached to a brand through trust, quality perception, associations, and
consumer loyalty. Nguyen et al. (2023) show that corporate social responsibility
practices can strengthen the Green Brand Image, which in turn contributes to the
formation of Brand Equity. In the increasingly competitive skincare industry,
Brand Equity becomes a strategic asset because it can strengthen brand
positioning and support the creation of Sustainable Competitive Advantage
(Rattanangam, 2026).
The development o f e -commerce is also changing the patterns of
distribution, promotion, and consumer decision -making. Digital platforms that
provide product information and transparency regarding sustainability aspects
can enhance consumer trust and encourage more responsi ble purchasing
decisions (Vinayavekhin et al., 2024). However, the competition among local
skincare products in Indonesia is becoming increasingly fierce. The Top Brand
Index data shows that Somethinc was ranked sixth in 2022, then dropped to
seventh place  in 2023 and remained in that position until 2024. The situation
indicates a challenge for Somethinc to maintain its competitive position amidst
the increasingly aggressive development of competing brands.

The issue has become increasingly important because the implementation
of Somethinc's green marketing has not yet been fully and consistently integrated
across various communication channels. Information regarding the commitment
to clean beauty, cruelty -free, and sustainability has not always been clearly
communicated through social media or e-commerce. Inconsistent environmental
communication has the potential to create perceptions of greenwashing, reduce
trust, and weaken the Green Brand Image (Tu et al., 2024). However, research
shows that the implementation of green marketing and sustainable branding can
enhance the perception of quality, loyalty, and the competitiveness of companies
in the skincare industry (Putra et al., 2024). The results of a pre -survey of 22
consumers also showed that 81.8% of respondents were aware of Somethinc's
environmentally friendly promotions, 77.3% viewed Somethinc as a brand that
cares about the environment, and 86.4% expressed loyalty to the brand based on
its quality and image.
Although previous research has discussed green marketing, Green Brand
Image, and Brand Equity, studies that integrate these three variables to explain
Sustainable Competitive Advantage in Indonesian local skincare products within
the e -commerce environment are still limited. Therefore, this research is
conducted to analyze the influe nce of Green Marketing Strategy, Green Brand
Image, and Brand Equity on the Sustainable Competitive Advantage of
Somethinc skincare products in the e-commerce ecosystem.

METHODOLOGY
A survey -designed quantitative approach is applied in this research to
analyze the level of influence of Green Marketing Strategy, Green Brand Image,
and Brand Equity on Sustainable Competitive Advantage in the Somethinc
skincare product line. The allocation of the research implementation period spans
from February to July 2026, focusing on the Jabodetabek area. The targeted
population group includes Somethinc consumers who have experience
transacting through e -commerce channels, including Shopee, Tokopedia , and
Sociolla. The sample determination executed purposive sampling techniques to
gather 130 respondents, where the quota was calculated based on 13 variable
indicators that meet the SEM-PLS analysis requirements.
The data collection procedure was driven by the distribution of digital
questionnaire instruments based on Google Forms, which were disseminated
through social media networks and the digital platform ecosystem. The structure
of the instrument includes detailed indicators consisting of four  indicators for
Green Marketing Strategy, three indicators for Green Brand Image, three
indicators for Brand Equity, and three indicators for Sustainable Competitive
Advantage. All measurement items are rated using a five -point Likert scale,
ranging from a weight of 1 (strongly disagree) to a weight of 5 (strongly agree).
To ensure the clarity of sentence structure and test the validity of the instrument,
a limited-scale trial was conducted first before the primary data collection.
The data analysis process  relies on the Partial Least Squares Structural
Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM) technique, processed using SmartPLS 4.0
software. The evaluation phase begins with the presentation of descriptive
statistics to map the respondents' profiles and the tendency of a nswer
distribution for each variable. Next, an outer model examination is conducted,
which includes convergent validity, discriminant validity, Average Variance
Extracted (AVE), Cronbach’s Alpha, and composite reliability. In the next phase,
the inner mode l evaluation is scrutinized through the acquisition of R -square

DISCUSSION
The Influence of Green Marketing Strategy on Sustainable Competitive
Advantage
Referring to the hypothesis calculation, the Green Marketing Strategy
shows a positive relationship direction, although it is not yet statistically
significant toward the Sustainable Competitive Advantage of Somethinc skincare
products, as indicated by a path coefficient of 0.113, a t-statistic of 1.412, and a p-
value of 0.158 (> 0.05). These results indicate that although the correlation
direction is positive, the contribution of the Green Marketing Strategy is not
sufficient to be categorized as significant in forming a competitive advantage.
The findings of this research indicate that the execution of the green
marketing strategy by Somethinc has actually been well-received by the market,
although it has not yet become the main driver of Sustainable Competitive
Advantage. This phenomenon aligns with the descriptive analysis of the Green
Marketing Strategy indicator, where the item "I believe Somethinc's product
distribution considers environmental impact" received the highest average score
of 2.823. This data proves that the public is starting to pay attention to the
company's environmental commitment, particularly in the supply chain aspect,

although this perception is not yet strong enough to directly intervene in
competitive advantage.
This condition aligns with the view of Kotler et al. (2024), who explain that
the integration of sustainability principles into marketing strategies can provide
added value through differentiati on, strengthening market position, and
enhancing public perception. On the other hand, Sheykhan et al. (2024)
emphasize that Green Marketing plays a strategic role as a supporting element
that will create Sustainable Competitive Advantage when synergized with other
factors such as Green Brand Image, Brand Equity, and corporate social
responsibility. Therefore, the Green Marketing Strategy functions more as a
reinforcement of supporting variables rather than the main driver of competitive
advantage.
The insignificant influence of the Green Marketing Strategy is suspected
to be because Somethinc consumers still prioritize their decisions on brand image
and equity over the company's green campaign. This suspicion is reinforced by
the acquisition of an f -square value of 0.015, which falls into the very small
category compared to the contributions of Green Brand Image and Brand Equity.
This means that the foundation of Somethinc's sustainable competitive
advantage is more shaped by consumers' perceptions of its ecological reputation
and brand strength, rather than solely by the green marketing activities it
undertakes.
This result differs from the findings of Solanki and Lakhera (2023), who
stated that Green Marketing Strategy has a positive and significant impact on
Sustainable Competitive Advantage. This disparity indicates that the
effectiveness of green marketing is greatly influenced by consumer
characteristics, market dynamics, and the depth of sustainability implementation
in each entity. In the context of So methinc, green marketing has not yet become
a main pillar, prompting the company to execute this strategy more consistently,
transparently, and integratively by strengthening the Green Brand Image and
Brand Equity to secure sustainable competitive advantage.
The Influence of Green Brand Image on Sustainable Competitive Advantage
Based on hypothesis testing, Green Brand Image has been proven to have
a positive and significant impact on Sustainable Competitive Advantage for
Somethinc skincare products, as evi denced by a path coefficient of 0.324, a t -
statistic of 3.929, and a p -value of 0.000 (< 0.05). These results confirm that the
more positive the eco -friendly image built by Somethinc, the higher the
Sustainable Competitive Advantage achieved; thus, the pro posed hypothesis is
officially accepted.
This research documents that consumers have a positive perception of
Somethinc's sustainability commitment. This is reflected in the descriptive
achievement of the Green Brand Image indicator, where the item "I view
Somethinc as a brand that cares about the environment" recorded the highest
average score at 2.900. This finding confirms that Somethinc's environmental
concern successfully fosters a positive brand image while supporting the
formation of sustainable competitive advantage.

other independent variables, making Brand Equity the most dominant predictor
in influencing Sustainable Competitive Advantage in this research. Therefore,
the hypothesis stating that Brand Equity has a positive effect on Sus tainable
Competitive Advantage is accepted.
The high influence reflects the strong recognition and positive assessment
of the Somethinc brand by consumers. Descriptive analysis shows that the item
"I can easily recognize Somethinc products compared to othe r brands" received
the highest average score of 3.108. This condition proves Somethinc's success in
establishing strong brand equity in the public's mind, capable of triggering trust,
loyalty, and consumer preference, which ultimately strengthens the compa ny's
bargaining position amidst the competition in the skincare industry.
Theoretically, this finding supports Aaker's (1991) view that Brand Equity
is a strategic asset that adds value through brand awareness, perceived quality,
brand associations, and customer loyalty. In line with that, Sheykhan et al. (2024)
explain that strong brand equity makes it easier for companies to nurture loyal
customers, attract new consumers, and create a competitive advantage that is
difficult for competitors to imitate.
This study is in line with Adewole's (2024) research, which found a
positive influence of Brand Equity on Sustainable Competitive Advantage. From
the perspective of the f -square value, Brand Equity recorded a figure of 0.141
(small category), but this value i s the largest compared to Green Brand Image
(0.126) and Green Marketing Strategy (0.015). This confirms that Brand Equity
provides the largest contribution in explaining Sustainable Competitive
Advantage, so the stronger the equity of the Somethinc brand, the more optimal
its ability to maintain a sustainable competitive advantage.

CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS
Referring to the evaluation results regarding the influence of Green
Marketing Strategy, Green Brand Image, and Brand Equity on the Sustainable
Competitive Advantage of Somethinc skincare products, it is concluded that the
Green Marketing Strategy has a po sitive but not significant effect, thus its
execution needs to be improved in a more tangible, transparent, and consistent
manner to optimally contribute to competitive advantage. Meanwhile, the Green
Brand Image has been proven to have a positive and significant effect, indicating
that an environmentally friendly image effectively fosters consumer trust and
strengthens the company's competitiveness. Brand Equity also has a positive and
significant effect and is the most dominant contributor to Sustainable
Competitive Advantage, so efforts to maintain brand awareness, quality
perception, brand association, and customer loyalty need to be continuously
upheld to support Somethinc's competitive advantage.
Future research agendas are expected to expand the popul ation scope by
involving respondents from various regions in Indonesia, a more heterogeneous
age range, and exploring different research objects or comparing several local
skincare brands. Expanding respondent criteria based on profession, income
level, an d product usage frequency will enrich the variation in perceptions
related to Green Marketing Strategy, Green Brand Image, Brand Equity, and
Sustainable Competitive Advantage. In addition, a comparison between local
brands can test the consistency of these  findings on brands with different
characteristics and sustainability strategies, thereby providing a broader
generalization for the development of the skincare industry.
