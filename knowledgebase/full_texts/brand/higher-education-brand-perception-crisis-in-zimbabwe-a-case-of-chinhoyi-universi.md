Abstract
The study contends that a major branding challenge facing higher
education institutions (HEIs) in Zimbabwe is assumed to be linked to
socio-economic and political  factors. This research thus aims to
establish the relationship between these factors and brand perception,
using the Chinhoyi University of Technology (CUT) as a case study. The
study also examines the branding strategies employed by CUT to
enhance its br and image in the higher education sector. As a
predominantly qualitative research method, the study employs
participatory observation to collect data on the physical branding
pattern. Focus group discussions were conducted with selected
members of the Comm unications and Public Relations Directorate,
management, the Registry Department and lecturers. Additionally,
interviews were held with selected students. Findings indicate the
nation's socio-political climate negatively impacts the university's brand
perception. According to the study, the lens through which the
country's economic and political situation is analysed is also used to
view HEIs. The study further discovered that brand image building,
maintenance and enhancement need financial investment from the
government and other corporations that the university was not
adequately receiving. The Chinhoyi University of Technology, in
particular, and other state universities in general, should seek to
improve their infrastructure as the general ambience tend to shape
public perception of the local higher education (HE) brand. The study

INTRODUCTION
At independence in 1980, Zimbabwe had one university (the University
of Zimbabwe), six t eachers' training colleges, two polytechnics and no
vocational training institutions (Garwe and Thondhlana, 2022). In  the
Southern African region, Zimbabwe‘s higher and tertiary education was
considered one of the best (Shizha and Kariwo, 2012). The new
government expanded primary, secondary and HE to improve access
and equity for the marginalised black majority (Manyonganise, 2012).
This deliberate policy shift to address the imbalances in the education
provision coincided with technological transformation s in the sector,
particularly in HE.

It is noted that during the pre -independence era, the country‘s
education system was racially segregated and highly regarded (Zvobgo,
1994). The colonial Rhodesia Front government, led by Ian Douglas
Smith, declared its Unilateral Declaration of Independence in 1965 and
it resulted in economic sanctions. The government adopted extensive
intervention policies. The education system was not affected by this
exclusion because it was only meant for the white minority (Mashungu
et al ., 2025). The education of the indigenous population, by default,
became the mandate of poorly funded mission schools. There was
unfair distribution of resources in favour of white schools, as the
central government had nothing to do with African education. The
rating of education focused on the quality of education accessible to the
minority whites. The government of the day was financed the education
system through its inward -looking and import -substitution policy
(Mutenga, 2021). There were no perceived challenges in education and

Higher education institutions in Zimbabwe, particularly the Chinhoyi

The study intends to:
• Establish the existence of socio -economic and political
environmental effects on the brand perception of CUT.
• Expose how the socio -economic and political fac tors have
affected the CUT brand image.
• Develop strategies that can reshape the CUT brand perception in

The study confines itself to the perception challenges bedevilling the
Chinhoyi University of Technology. This is one challenge singled out
from a myriad of challenges facing HEIs in Zimbabwe. Other challenges
which might have a collaring effect, but not adequately addressed in
the study, include funding constraints, brain drain and limited
resources. The research limits itself to the HE brand p erception crisis. It
also concentrates on socio -economic and political challenges in
Zimbabwe during different historical epochs. In the study, s HEIs refer
to universities only.

LITERATURE REVIEW
A theoretical framework that can be used to understand the  HE brand
perception crisis at the Chinhoyi University of Technology can be
analysed through several models. One of these models is the Brand
Equity Model. This model focuses on brand awareness, perceived
quality, brand associations and loyalty (Awalludin and Heikal, 2024). It
shows how negative perceptions can lead to a decrease in brand value.
Another model that can be used is the Service Quality Model
(SERVQUAL). This model looks at the gaps that exist between what
students expect and the actual service delivered (Gultom et al., 2024).

The study is also grounded in the Branding Theory. It posits that a
brand is more than differentiation, it is a blend of emotional, physical,
rational and aesthetic elements (Swaminathan et al., 2020). Four major
tenets of the Branding Theory are identification, differentiation,
personification and brand as an asset (Swaminathan et al., 2020). The
identification tenet of the branding concept ensures that consumers
identify or recognise the products or services on offer through the
owner. For instance, CUT should have a market identity. The market
should just know that there is a university that  specialises in
technology, innovation and entrepreneurship. However, Krasnikov and
Jayachandran (2022) contend that owner-name trademarks have weak
marketing value. Identification becomes just one of a process in the
chain to enhance brand image.

The dif ferentiation tenet of the Branding Theory shows a need for a
brand to differentiate itself as competition increases in new world
markets. This phase is also synonymous with brand conception as an
extended product (Wang and Liu, 2020) which includes the fun ctional
aspects of the brand and its services. Creating a brand means
developing a message that communicates  with targeted consumers
about goods and services repeatedly. For example, CUT should
differentiate itself from any other HEIs, its organisational c ulture, brand
reputation, physical presence, community impact and its overall brand
image.

The third tenet is the branding as personification theory which posits
that a brand is more than differentiation; it is a blend of emotional,
physical, rational and aesthetic elements ( Swaminathan et al ., 2020).
Consumers develop a set of relations with the brand, unlike what they
can do with a product that is not differentiated. Therefore, branding
becomes an attribution of symbolic and social meaning to the product
(Kotler et al ., 2021). For a successful brand to be developed and
maintained, the needs of the consumers and the markets should be
appreciated. García -Salirrosas and Gordillo (2021)  suggest that a
consistent brand personality requires integrating a company ‘s identity
system, value system, image, relationships and value-added aspects.

Using the personification metaphor , Deng and Messinger (2022)
ascribe to brand dimensions of personality, in the same way
psychologists have identified and ascribed dimensions  of personality to
humans (Swaminathan et al., 2020). Personifying brands in this phase
moves from the brand being an artefact that can be manipulated to
becoming a living organism (Heding et al ., 2020). The  social and
professional character of a universit y's Vice Chancellor influences the
corporate personality of an institution. Consumers‘ loyalty aligns with
the organisation‘s CEO personality ( Nurhilalia and Saleh, 2024). It
would be desirable to align the customers‘ and companies‘ personalities
(Swaminathan et al., 2020).

A university without a distinct brand personality is like a dull person,
hence, it will only be a second choice in the customer‘s mind . Deng and
Messinger (2022) reiterate  that brand personality is embedded in the
brand‘s DNA and summar ise the internal and external benefits of the
brand across all media types to all stakeholders. Consumers‘ brand
memory usually recognises the historical achievements of that brand as
a way of tracing or even predicting the trend and/or trajectories the
brand may take.

The evolution of HE in Africa is deeply intertwined with the continent's
colonial history. Higher education institutions in Africa largely mirror
the university models established by their former colonisers. Education,
as utilised during the colonial period, functioned as a mechanism of
exploitation, primarily designed to serve colonial administrations. This
system sought to produce educated citizens who faci litated economic
production (Sifuna, 2020). Badugela (2024)  analyses colonial
education as a purposeful strategy to displace indigenous technological
knowledge and skills among Africans. Consequently, this educational
framework has been criticised for lack ing ―Ubuntu‖ or ―Africanness‖,

embodying authentically African characteristics. He critically observes
that these universities function as substandard imitations of their

African scholars on the validation of academic standards set by Western
institutions.

RESEARCH DESIGN
The study adopted a case study research design, a predominantly
qualitative methodology, to explore the HE brand perception crisis in
Zimbabwe. Data on the physical branding pattern were collected
through participatory observation and focus group discussio ns were
held with the Communications and Public Relations department
personnel, management, members of the Registry Department and
selected lecturers from the Chinhoyi University of Technology. In-depth
interviews were also conducted with selected students . The study chose
a qualitative research design because brand perception issues cannot be
quantified (Sallis et al ., 2021). Physical branding patterns of a c ase
study are better analysed through observation. To develop effective
branding strategies that re shape the perception of the University, the
study employed focus group discussions with participants whose duties
were directly aligned with the University branding. Students are critical
stakeholders of the University and the study sought to understand,
through in -depth interviews, the extent the socio -economic and
political environment has contributed to a diminished brand
perception.

CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS
The perception of Zimbabwe‘s higher education system has been
significantly shaped by s ocio-economic and political upheavals,
particularly since the early 2000s, which have undermined the stability
and quality of universities. The economic meltdown, driven by both
internal and external factors, has created an unstable environment that
negatively impacts university performance and reputation. Historical
legacies, policy reversals and neoliberal reforms have further deteriorated
the quality of HE, with state universities being perceived through a political
lens due to their close ties with the government. Overall, the intertwined
influence of socio-economic and political factors has deeply affected the
brand perception of Zimbabwe‘s HE, highlighting the systemic
challenges rooted in the country‘s complex historical trajectory. In light
of the above findings, the following recommendations are put forward:
 Ensuring the continuation of free HE in Zimbabwe is crucial for
fostering socio-economic development, as it provides equitable
access to advanced skills and knowledge necessary for national
progress. Abrogating this promise could undermine social equity,
limit opportunities for talented students from marginalised
backgrounds and hinder the country's overall growth by reducing
the pool of skilled professionals. Therefore, the government should
uphold its commitment to free HE to promote inclusive development
and build a more prosperous future for Zimbabwe.
 The Chinhoyi University of Technology should develop innovative
branding strategies that leverage social media platforms such as
Facebook, X, Instagram and LinkedIn to enhance its visibility, engage
with prospective students, showcase academic achievements and
highlight campus life. Implementing targeted digital marketing
campaigns, creating compelling content like student testimonials,
research breakthroughs and virtual campus tours can position the
university as a modern, dynamic institution. Additionally, fostering
an active online community and utilising analytics to tailor
messaging will help the university compete effectively with well -
established regional and global institutions, ultimately attracting
a diverse and talented student body.

 Policy makers and CUT management should prioritise establishing a
