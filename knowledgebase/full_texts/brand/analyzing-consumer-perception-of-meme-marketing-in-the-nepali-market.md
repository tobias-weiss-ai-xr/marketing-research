 ABSTRACT
As traditional advertising faces increasing saturation and consumer fatigue,
meme marketing has emerged as a disruptive alternative that leverages social
relatability and digital humour. In Nepals rapidly evolving digital landscape,
understanding how consumers filter and value these non-traditional
advertisements are critical for brand communication strategies. The primary
objective of this study was to evaluate Nepali consumers perceptions of meme
marketing, focusing on four key dimensions: effectiveness, entertainment value,
brand relatability, and comparative engagement with traditional advertising.
The study employed a quantitative research design, gathering primary data
from a sample of 500 respondents using a 5-point Likert scale survey . Data
analysis was conducted using descriptive statistics (mean, standard deviation)
and a One-Sample t-test to validate the hypothesis regarding consumer
sentiment. The empirical results reveal an overwhelmingly positive perception
of meme marketing. Descriptive analysis showed consistent mean scores
ranging from 3.87 to 3.89, indicating strong consumer agreement.
Approximately 76% of respondents view memes as an effective advertising
tool, while 78.2% find them more engaging than traditional media.

BACKGROUND OF THE STUDY
The traditional methods of buying and selling are being replaced by the modern digital landscape, driven by the
internet, social media, and an expanding array of online tools and platforms. To attract customers in this competitive
environment, businesses are increasingly employing strategies such as memes, blogs, and videos to boost their social
media presence (Carr & Hayes, 2015). Social media platforms have become powerful tools for e-commerce, with
memes playing a key role in attracting potential customers. By leveraging the popularity and relatability of memes,
marketers can create engaging and shareable content that encourages online shopping.
Memes are regarded as an important part of Internet culture and have become a well-established area of research.
They can help build brand awareness, foster customer loyalty, and ultimately increase sales. In the digital age, memes
have gone beyond their origins as simple humorous images or videos shared among friends (Malodia, et al. (2022).
They have evolved into a significant form of communication, reflecting cultural nuances and societal trends. As Shen
et al., (2024) emphasizes, internet memes have emerged as one of the most prominent forms of digital content, serving
as a universal language of digital culture (Teng, 2022; Deus et al., 2022). This transformation has opened the door to
innovative marketing strategies, especially meme marketing, which harnesses the widespread appeal of memes to
engage consumers effectively. Meme marketing is a strategy that incorporates brand elements such as logos, product
images, and brand names into entertaining and relatable content. This approach allows brands to communicate with
their audience in a manner that feels natural and authentic, rather than overtly promotional. The humour and relatability
of memes can foster a sense of community and connection among consumers, making them more actively engaged
across various social media platforms (Lombard, 2014).
Despite the growing popularity of meme marketing globally , its academic exploration in South Asian countries,
particularly Nepal, remains limited. Nepals digital ecosystem is rapidly evolving, with increasing internet penetration
and social media adoption among younger demographics (Khanal & Badea, 2021). This shift has created opportunities
for businesses to experiment with innovative marketing strategies, yet empirical evidence on how memes influence
consumers purchase intentions in Nepal remains scarce. Understanding this dynamic is crucial, as consumer behavior
in emerging markets may dif fer significantly from that in Western contexts due to cultural, social, and economic
factors (Razzaq, 2023).
Furthermore, the importance of meme marketing lies in its ability to connect brands and consumers by encouraging
engagement through humour and relatability. In Nepal, where conventional advertising often struggles to attract the
attention of digitally savvy youth, meme marketing provides a novel approach to fostering brand loyalty and influencing
purchasing decisions. Exploring this phenomenon will not only enhance academic understanding but also offer practical
insights for marketers looking to improve their digital strategies in Nepals competitive market (Rathi & Jain, 2024).
Consequently, this study is both timely and essential to grasp the role of meme marketing in shaping consumer
purchase intentions within the Nepali context.
RESEARCH METHODOLOGY
The study utilizes a quantitative research design with a descriptive and analytical approach to evaluate consumer
perceptions of meme marketing in Nepal. Primary data was gathered from a sample of 500 social media users through
a structured online questionnaire using convenience sampling to target the digitally active demographic. The survey
instrument employed a 5-point Likert scale to measure five key dimensions: effectiveness, entertainment value, brand
relatability, perception improvement, and comparative engagement. Data analysis was performed using SPSS, involving
descriptive statistics; such as mean and standard deviation; to summarize trends, and a One-Sample T-test to statistically
validate the hypothesis against a neutral midpoint. This methodological framework ensured a robust and objective
assessment of whether meme-based strategies significantly resonate with the contemporary Nepali audience.

• H11: Consumers in Nepal have a positive perception towards meme marketing.

ANALYSIS OF PERCEPTION OF CONSUMERS TOWARDS MEMES MARKETING
The analysis of consumer perception serves as a critical lens through which we can understand the psychological
reception of non-traditional advertising in Nepal. Unlike traditional media, which often relies on authority and repetition,
meme marketing operates on the currency of social relatability and humor. This section evaluates how the average
Nepali consumer filters, interprets, and ultimately values the information presented through digital humor. By measuring
perception, the study seeks to determine if memes are viewed as a creative bridge between brands and people, or if
they are still dismissed as noise within the digital ecosystem. To gain a comprehensive view, the analysis focuses on
several key dimensions of perception: Effectiveness, Entertainment Value, and Trustworthiness. The effectiveness
dimension measures whether consumers believe memes actually work as a tool for brand awareness. Entertainment
value assesses the fun factor, which is essential for organic sharing on platforms like Facebook and Instagram. Lastly,
evaluating trust is vital, as it helps identify the fine line between a brand being relatable and being unprofessional.
Understanding these collective viewpoints is the first step in proving whether the meme culture in Nepal has matured
enough to support large-scale commercial interventions.

The descriptive analysis provides a consolidated view of how consumers view meme marketing, with mean scores across
all statements hovering consistently between 3.87 and 3.89. On a 5-point Likert scale, these scores indicate a strong
leaning toward the Agree category, suggesting a generally favorable and stable perception among the Nepali audience.
The highest mean scores (3.89) are observed for the entertainment value of memes and their ability to improve overall
brand perception, reinforcing the idea that humor is a primary driver of positive brand equity in the digital space. The
standard deviation values, ranging from 1.114 to 1.213, suggest that while most responses are clustered around the
positive end of the scale, there is a moderate level of diversity in consumer opinions. The relatively low standard error
(approx. .05) indicates that the sample mean is a reliable estimator of the true population mean. Collectively, these
statistics confirm that meme marketing is perceived as a cohesive and effective strategy in Nepal, consistently outperforming
traditional benchmarks in terms of engagement, relatability, and psychological impact.
Hypothesis Testing
• H01: Consumers in Nepal do not have a positive perception towards meme marketing.
• H11: Consumers in Nepal have a positive perception towards meme marketing.
The objective of this study was to evaluate the general perception of consumers toward meme marketing in Nepal. To
statistically validate this, a One-Sample T-test was conducted. The test compares the observed sample mean against
a neutral test value (typically 3 on a 5-point Likert scale, though here tested against a baseline of 0 to show absolute
significance) to determine if the collective perception is significantly positive.

 The results of the One-Sample T-test reveal a mean score of 3.8792, which is substantially higher than the neutral
midpoint of 3.0. The calculated t-statistic is 86.935 with 499 degrees of freedom, and the associated p-value (Sig. 2-

Since the p-value (p < 0.05) is statistically significant and the mean score lies in the Agree range of the Likert scale,
we reject the Null Hypothesis (H1) and accept the Alternative Hypothesis (H1). Consequently , it is statistically
proven that consumers in Nepal hold a significantly positive perception of meme marketing. This suggests that the
audience finds the medium effective, relatable, and entertaining, validating it as a successful strategy for reaching the
Nepali digital market.
RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
The empirical findings of this study suggest a transformative shift in the Nepali advertising landscape, where digital
humor has superseded traditional promotional tactics in terms of audience reception. Analysis of the primary data

a mean score of 3.89 for entertainment value, indicating that the fun factor is a primary driver of consumer attention.
Unlike traditional ads, which are often perceived as intrusive, meme-based advertisements are viewed by 78% of
participants as a form of leisure. This aligns with the theoretical framework of Voigts (2018), suggesting that when
brand messages are wrapped in cultural nuances and humor , they successfully bypass the psychological barriers
consumers typically raise against conventional commercial breaks.
Beyond mere entertainment, the data underscores the role of memes in humanizing corporate entities within the digital
ecosystem. A significant 75.6% of respondents noted that meme marketing makes brands appear more relatable,
effectively lowering the barrier between a company and its tar get audience. This authenticity bridge is crucial in a
market like Nepal, where younger, digitally-savvy demographics increasingly prioritize social relatability over corporate
authority. The descriptive statistics show a remarkably consistent mean across all measured dimensions; effectiveness,
relatability, and engagement; all hovering near 3.88. This consistency suggests that the Nepali consumer does not
view memes as isolated jokes, but rather as a cohesive and legitimate communication strategy that improves overall
brand perception. The comparative engagement analysis further solidifies the dominance of digital humor, as 78.2%
of respondents rated memes as more engaging than traditional advertising. This finding provides empirical support
for the shift toward interactive and shareable content as highlighted in previous literature (Carr & Hayes, 2015).
Statistically, the One-Sample T-test results (M = 3.8792, t = 86.935, p < .001) provide conclusive evidence to reject
the null hypothesis, confirming that the positive perception is statistically significant and not a result of random
chance. Collectively, these results indicate that the meme culture in Nepal has matured sufficiently to support large-
scale commercial interventions, offering a potent tool for marketers to cut through the clutter of modern social media
feeds and foster genuine community connection.
CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS
The findings of this study lead to the conclusion that meme marketing has transitioned from a niche internet subculture into
a powerhouse of digital communication within the Nepali market. The overwhelming statistical evidence; highlighted by a
high mean perception score of 3.8792; confirms that consumers no longer view memes as mere noise, but as an effective,
entertaining, and humanizing bridge between brands and people. By successfully stripping away the intrusive nature of
traditional advertisements and replacing it with social relatability, meme marketing has achieved a level of engagement
(78.2%) that conventional media struggles to match. The study proves that for the modern Nepali consumer, humor is the
most effective currency for capturing attention and building brand equity in an increasingly cluttered digital ecosystem.
Based on these conclusions, it is recommended that businesses in Nepal move away from rigid, overly corporate
communication styles and integrate cultural listening into their marketing departments. Brands should invest in creative
teams that understand local nuances, slang, and trending formats to ensure their memes feel authentic rather than
forced. Furthermore, while the entertainment value is high, marketers must maintain a delicate balance between
humor and professionalism to preserve long-term brand trust. As the digital-first audience in Nepal continues to grow,
those who master the art of the commercial will likely see higher conversion rates and stronger consumer loyalty than
those clinging to outdated, traditional advertising models.
