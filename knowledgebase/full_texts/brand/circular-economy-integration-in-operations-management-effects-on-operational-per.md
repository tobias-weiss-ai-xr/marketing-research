ABSTRACT
In the context of globalization and escalating resource depletion pressures, the transition
from the traditional linear economy to a circular economy (CE) has emerged as an inevitable
strategic orientation. This study aims to examine the effects of integra ting a triad of core
circular economy practices—namely eco-design, green procurement, and reverse logistics on
firms’ operational performance and brand equity. This study employs a mixed -methods
design, beginning with a qualitative phase involving semi -structured interviews with 10
industry experts to r efine the measurement scales. In the quantitative phase, data were
collected from a survey of 350 manufacturing firms located in industrial zones in Southern
Vietnam, specifically in Ho Chi Minh City, focusing on small and large enterprises in the
electronics and consumer goods sectors. Data were analyzed using Partial Least Squares
Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM). The empirical results confirm that all three circular
practices, spanning input sourcing, product design, and end-of-life recovery, exert positive and
significant impacts on both operational performance and brand equity. Notably, the findings
reveal that operational performance serves as a complementary partial mediator, substantially
amplifying the effect of green initiatives on brand equity. These results provide practical
evidence cautioning firms against the “greenwashing” trap, while reinforcing a critical
strategic message: sustainability commitments can only generate competitive advantage and
foster enduring market trust when they are deeply embedded in an operational system
characterized by excellence, leanness, and resource optimization.
Keywords: Circular economy, Eco-design, Green procurement, Reverse logistics, Operational
performance, Brand equity.
1. INTRODUCTION
In the context of a global economy facing intensifying pressures from resource depletion,
environmental degradation, and increasingly stringent sustainability regulations, recent
empirical trends (2024 -2025) indicate that while many firms adopt green pract ices, the
integration of multiple pillars into a unified operational strategy is still in its infancy in
emerging markets like Vietnam. The traditional linear economic model based on the “take –
make–dispose” principle has revealed inherent systemic limitati ons. In response to these
challenges, the Circular Economy (CE) has emerged as a strategic paradigm aimed at
optimizing resource utilization through practices such as reuse, recycling, and product life
extension. Beyond mitigating ecological externalities, this model offers firms opportunities to
reconfigure their value chains, thereby enhancing operational efficiency and establishing long-
term competitive advantage.

Within the domain of operations management, the integration of circular economy
principles has driven a transition toward sustainable and closed-loop production systems. The
core pillars of this transformation include eco -design, green procurement, and rev erse
logistics. This ecosystem of practices enables firms to exert tighter control over input
standards, optimize product architecture, and recover value from end-of-life materials. Such a
comprehensive intervention not only directly improves operational performance through waste
reduction and cost efficiency, but also signals a strong commitment to Extended Producer
Responsibility (EPR). These substantive commitments play a critical role in strengthening
stakeholder trust and enhancing intangible brand assets in the marketplace. While previous
research has focused on individual practices, there is a significant research gap regarding how
internal operational improvements translate into intangible market assets like brand equity
through the lens of Signaling Theory, particularly in the post-pandemic Vietnamese context
Addressing this research gap, the present study aims to evaluate the impact of a triad of
circular economy practices (eco -design, green procurement, and reverse logistics) on brand
equity, while elucidating the mediating role of operational performance in this relationship.
By employing a quantitative approach based on Partial Least Squares Structural Equation
Modeling (PLS-SEM), this study seeks to provide robust empirical evidence underscoring the
critical importance of aligning sustainability initiatives  with operational excellence in
contemporary operations management.
2. THEORETICAL BACKGROUND
2.1. Circular Economy (CE)
The circular economy is an economic model that seeks to optimize resource utilization
by extending the life cycle of products, materials, and energy through activities such as reuse,
recycling, remanufacturing, and value recovery, thereby replacing the traditional linear model
based on the “take–make–dispose” sequence. According to Stahel, CE focuses on extending
product lifespans through repair, reuse, and remanufacturing, thereby reducing the need for
new resource extraction, waste treatment costs, and en vironmental pollution [1, 2]. Recent
studies by Sharma et al. and Firoozi et al. suggest that circular economy integration is no longer
just a waste management tool but a source of supply chain resilience [3, 4].
2.2. Green Brand Equity (BE)
Green brand equity refers to a set of intangible assets and liabilities associated with a
brand’s environmental commitments [4]. It is constituted by dimensions such as green brand
image, customer satisfaction, trust, and green loyalty [4, 5]. From a signaling theory perspective,
green brand equity is formed when firms credibly communicate their environmental
performance, thereby reducing information asymmetry between firms and stakeholders.
2.3. Eco-Design (ED)
Eco-design represents a proactive operational capability that embeds environmental
criteria into product development processes, thereby influencing cost structures, material
efficiency, and lifecycle impacts at the earliest stages of production. From a process integration
perspective, eco-design reduces downstream operational inefficiencies by minimizing material
waste, simplifying production processes, and enhancing product durability. Under the RBV
framework, eco -design can be viewed as a valuable and har d-to-imitate capability that
improves operational performance through resource optimization and process standardization.
Empirical evidence from Mishra et al.  and Khan et al. indicates that eco-design enhances
operational management efficiency by optimizing resource utilization and reducing waste
[6, 7]. By embedding environmental criteria into product development, eco -design directly

2.4. Green Procurement (GP)
Green procurement represents a strategic supply chain capability through which firms
integrate environmental standards into sourcing decisions, supplier evaluation, and
procurement processes. Rather than being a compliance -driven activity, GP enhances supply
chain transparency, reduces uncertainty, and strengthens coordination across upstream
partners. From an operational standpoint, GP improves input quality consistency, reduces
process variability, and minimizes waste generation, thereby enhancing operati onal
performance. Appolloni et al. suggest that GP has evolved from a compliance-driven activity
into a strategic advantage, while Bag et al. and Sahoo and Vijayvargy demonstrate its role in
reducing inventory levels, defects, and waste management costs [8, 9, 10]. Accordingly:
Hypothesis H3: Green procurement has a positive effect on operational performance.
From a signaling perspective, GP enhances brand credibility by demonstrating verifiable
commitment across the entire supply chain, not just at the product level. This reduces
reputational risk and strengthens stakeholder trust, particularly in contexts whe re consumers
are sensitive to ethical sourcing. Therefore:
Hypothesis H4: Green procurement has a positive effect on brand equity.
2.5. Reverse Logistics (RL)
Reverse logistics is a critical operational capability that enables firms to recover value
from end -of-life products through collection, sorting, remanufacturing, and recycling
processes [7]. Within CE systems, RL functions as a mechanism for closing mater ial loops
and stabilizing input supply, thereby reducing dependence on volatile raw material markets.
The use of recycled materials typically requires less energy and significantly lowers waste
treatment costs, thereby improving profit margins. Firoozi et  al. argue that well -managed
reverse logistics systems optimize internal material flows, minimize supply chain disruptions,
and directly enhance operational efficiency and profitability [3]. Thus:
Hypothesis H5: Reverse logistics has a positive effect on operational performance.
In the context of increasing consumer sensitivity to environmental issues, initiatives such
as product take -back programs, packaging return schemes, and lifetime recycling
commitments serve as credible and powerful signals of Extended Producer Responsibili ty
(EPR). Proactive recovery of electronic waste or packaging demonstrates a firm’s genuine
ethical commitment to society [4]. These signals help reduce consumer skepticism regarding
greenwashing, thereby strengthening trust, enhancing brand image, and sig nificantly
increasing intangible brand equity. Therefore:
Hypothesis H6: Reverse logistics has a positive effect on brand equity.

3. METHODOLOGY
This study adopts a mixed -methods research design, integrating both qualitative and
quantitative approaches. In the qualitative phase, 10 semi -structured interviews were
conducted with operations managers from the HCMC Industrial Park Association to valida te
the suitability of international scales for the Vietnamese context.
In the subsequent quantitative phase, a purposive quota sampling technique was
employed. The sample of 350 firms consists of manufacturing enterprises in Ho Chi Minh City
(65%, including the former Ba Ria –Vung Tau and Binh Duong provinces), and Dong Nai
(35%). The sectors include Food Processing (40%), Electronics (30%), and Textiles (30%)
After data cleaning , the dataset was analyzed using SPSS 27 and SmartPLS 3.2. The
analytical procedure strictly followed the two-step approach of Partial Least Squares Structural
Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM). For the measurement model, reliability was assessed using
Cronbach’s Alpha and Composite Reliability (CR). Convergent validity was evaluated based
on outer loadings and Average Variance Extracted (AVE). Discriminant validity was
established using the Fornell–Larcker criterion and the Heterotrait–Monotrait (HTMT) ratio.
For the structural model, multicollinearity was examined using the Variance Inflation
Factor (VIF). Subsequently, a bootstrapping procedure with 5,000 resamples was conducted to
test the significance and robustness of the proposed hypotheses. The model’s explanatory power
was assessed using the coefficient of determination (R²) and effect size (f²). Finally, indirect
effect analysis was performed to examine the mediating role of operational performance.

5. DISCUSSION
This study provides empirical support for all proposed hypotheses and offers several
important theoretical insights by explaining the underlying mechanisms through which
circular economy practices influence both operational and market -based outcomes. The
findings confirm that eco -design, green procurement, and reverse logistics significantly
enhance operational performance, which can be interpreted through the Resource-Based View
(RBV). Consistent with prior studies, these practices function as capability -building
mechanisms that improve resource efficiency, reduce process variability, and enhance system
integration [6].
From a theoretical standpoint, this suggests that circular practices are not merely
environmental initiatives but constitute strategic operational capabilities that are valuable, rare,
and difficult to imitate.
Beyond their impact on operational performance, eco -design, green procurement, and
reverse logistics also demonstrate direct and positive effects on brand equity. From the
perspective of signaling theory [2], efforts to enhance supply chain transparency an d
implement sustainable design function as credible signals that resonate with modern
consumers, who are increasingly concerned about product origins and ecological footprints.
The study further reveals that operational performance acts as a complementary partial
mediator in the relationships between eco -design, green procurement, reverse logistics, and
brand equity. This finding reinforces and extends prior research by Lin and Z hou, Chen,
while aligning with the arguments of Firoozi et al. regarding the pivotal role of operational
performance. Specifically, the positive effects of circular practices on brand equity are not
only direct but are also transmitted through the optimiza tion of firms’ operational
capabilities [3, 4, 5].
Compared with prior studies that often examine circular economy practices in isolation,
this study provides a more holistic perspective by analyzing the combined effects of eco -
design, green procurement, and reverse logistics within an integrated model. Th is integrated
approach offers a more realistic representation of how circular economy principles are
implemented in practice and how they jointly influence firm performance.

6. CONCLUSION
This study investigates the impact of circular economy (CE) integration on operational
performance and brand equity using empirical data collected from 350 manufacturing firms in
Vietnam. Employing Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM), the
findings provide robust evidence that eco -design, green procurement, and reverse logistics
significantly enhance operational performance and directly contribute to brand equity. More
importantly, operational performance is identified as a complemen tary partial mediator,
indicating that the influence of circular practices on brand equity is not only direct but also
transmitted through improvements in operational capabilities.
From a theoretical perspective, this study makes several important contributions. First, it
advances the circular economy literature by examining the integrated effects of multiple CE
practices within a unified framework, rather than analyzing them in isolation. This provides a
more comprehensive understanding of how circular principles are operationalized in real -
world manufacturing systems. Second, the study extends the Resource-Based View (RBV) by
conceptualizing eco-design, green procurement, and reverse logistics as strategic capabilities
that enhance operational efficiency and create competitive advantage. Third, it contributes to
signaling theory by demonstrating that operational performance serves as a critical mechanism
through which internal sustain ability efforts are translated into externally perceived brand
value. This integration of capability -based and signaling perspectives offers a more nuanced
explanation of how sustainability initiatives generate both internal and market -based
outcomes, particularly in the context of emerging economies.
From a managerial perspective, the findings suggest several strategic implications. First,
firms should reposition green procurement from a compliance -oriented activity to a strategic
supply chain capability by fostering close collaboration with environmen tally responsible
suppliers and establishing lifecycle -based sourcing standards. Second, eco -design should be
institutionalized as a core design philosophy integrated across product development and
production processes, enabling firms to simultaneously achieve resource efficiency and market
differentiation. Third, reverse logistics should be developed not only as a cost -reduction
mechanism but also as a strategic tool for strengthening brand credibility through visible
sustainability initiatives such as pro duct take -back and recycling programs. Finally, firms
should align operational and marketing functions to ensure that sustainability claims are
supported by measurable operational improvements, thereby reducing the risk of
greenwashing and enhancing long-term customer trust.
Despite its contributions, this study has several limitations. The use of purposive quota
sampling may limit the generalizability of the findings beyond the surveyed manufacturing
sectors in Vietnam. Additionally, the model focuses on three core circular practices and does
not incorporate other potentially relevant factors such as digital transformation or circular
business model innovation. Future research is encouraged to expand the model by including
additional constructs and exploring moderating variabl es such as institutional pressures,
technological capabilities, or industry -specific dynamics. Longitudinal studies could also
provide deeper insights into the causal relationships and long -term impacts of circular
economy implementation.
