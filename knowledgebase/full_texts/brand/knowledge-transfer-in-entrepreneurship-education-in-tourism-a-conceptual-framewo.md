# Knowledge Transfer in Entrepreneurship Education in Tourism: A Conceptual Framework for Learning Design and Curriculum Innovation

Abstract: This paper focuses on improving knowledge transfer in entrepreneurship education in tourism. It assumes that the
mere transmission of theoretical knowledge is insufficient and that its practical application is essential.  The paper presents
a proposal for an innovative undergraduate study programme in Tourism and Hospitality Management at a Slovak public
university, introducing two new courses: Venture Ecosystem and Entrepreneurial Marketing and Branding. The approach
emphasises project-based learning, real-world problem solving and the use of digital tools. The paper proposes a knowledge
transfer model based on the integration of knowledge creation, application and feedback within the learning process. The
model combines principles of knowledge management and entrepreneurship education. It is assumed that eff ective
knowledge transfer depends primarily on the design of the learning process, interaction between students and educators,
and the connection with practice. The proposed framework is expected to support the development of entrepreneurial
competencies and improve graduates’ readiness for the labour market.

1. Introduction
The tourism and hospitality sector is undergoing a significant transformation driven by digitalisation, increasing
competition and changing consumer behaviour (Buhalis and Law, 2008; Sigala, 2018). These changes place new
demands on graduates, who are expected to possess not only theoretical knowledge but also the ability to apply
it in a dynamic entrepreneurial environment.
In this context, the issue of effective knowledge transfer in higher education becomes increasingly important.
Traditional teaching models based on one-way knowledge transmission are insufficient for developing practical
entrepreneurial competencies (Neck and Greene, 2011). This creates a gap between acquired knowledge and
its practical application.  From a knowledge management perspective, this gap reflects the challenge of
transforming explicit academic knowledge into tacit entrepreneurial competencies applicable in practice.
The aim of this paper is to analyse how learning design and programme implementation can support knowledge
transfer in entrepreneurship education in tourism. The paper is based on a proposal for an innovative
undergraduate study programme integrating proje ct-based learning, digital tools and collaboration with
practice.
Despite the growing body of research on entrepreneurship education and knowledge transfer, existing studies
focus predominantly on teaching methods and learning outcomes. Less attention has been devoted to how
programme design and implementation frameworks  influence the transformation of knowledge into practical
competencies, particularly in sector-specific contexts such as tourism.
The paper also proposes a knowledge transfer model based on the integration of solution development,
application and iterative feedback within project-based learning environments.
2. Context and Theoretical Background
Knowledge transfer represents a key process in knowledge management, involving the creation, sharing and
application of knowledge in organisational and educational contexts (Nonaka and Takeuchi, 1995; Argote, 2013).
The proposed framework therefore understands entrepreneurship education as a process of knowledge
transformation, where students gradually convert explicit theoretical knowledge into tacit knowledge through
experiential and iterative learning activities. Its effectiveness depends not only on the quality of knowledge itself
but also on the ability of individuals to internalise and apply it (Grant, 1996).
In higher education, knowledge transfer can be understood as the transformation of explicit knowledge into
tacit knowledge applicable in practice. Traditional transmissive teaching approaches are insufficient for

developing complex competencies, particularly in entrepreneurship (Neck and Greene, 2011). Contemporary
approaches therefore emphasise experiential learning, which actively engages students in knowledge creation
(Kolb, 1984; Pittaway and Cope, 2007).  Recent research further highlights the role of digital and AI -supported
learning environments in supporting entrepreneurial learning, interaction and knowledge sharing processes
(Holmes et al., 2022).
Entrepreneurship education increasingly focuses on the ability to identify opportunities, experiment and
iteratively test solutions (Fayolle and Gailly, 2008; Ries, 2011). This reflects broader economic changes
characterised by the growing importance of innovation, knowledge processes and digital technologies.
In tourism, these trends are particularly pronounced, as business activities are strongly influenced by
digitalisation and rapidly changing customer behaviour (Sigala, 2018). Recent studies also emphasise the
growing importance of digital entrepreneurship ecosystems and digitally supported entrepreneurial learning
environments in higher education (Secundo et al., 2021; Nambisan et al., 2022).
Effective knowledge transfer requires a learning environment that supports interaction between knowledge
creation and application. Project-based learning, digital tools and collaboration with practice play a crucial role
in bridging academic knowledge and real-world entrepreneurial situations (Argote, 2013; Chaffey and Ellis -
Chadwick, 2019).
Furthermore, knowledge transfer is influenced by relational and contextual factors (Argote and Ingram, 2000).
The concept of absorptive capacity highlights the ability of individuals to recognise, assimilate and apply new
knowledge (Zahra and George, 2002) . In this context, absorptive capacity is particularly important, as students
must not only acquire new knowledge but also interpret, adapt and apply it in dynamic entrepreneurial
situations. This is increasingly important in digitally intensive educational and entrepreneurial environments
(Cohen and Levinthal, 1990; Secundo et al., 2021).
Despite these developments, many study programmes still lag. Students often acquire knowledge but struggle
to apply it in practice. Therefore, the focus must shift towards how knowledge is transformed into practical skills.
3. Methodology
The paper adopts a conceptual and design- oriented approach grounded in entrepreneurship education and
knowledge transfer literature.
The proposal is based on an analysis of labour market needs and required competencies in tourism, particularly
in entrepreneurship, marketing and digital skills.
The innovation includes the introduction of two courses – Venture Ecosystem and Entrepreneurial Marketing
and Branding -into the study programme.
Teaching methods include project -based learning, case studies, teamwork and experimentation (e.g. MVP).
These approaches are expected to support active learning, solution development and more effective knowledge
transfer (Kolb, 1984; Ries, 2011).
The paper therefore addresses the following research question: How can learning design and implementation
frameworks support effective knowledge transfer in entrepreneurship education in tourism?
4. Knowledge Transfer Mechanisms in Programme Design
The proposed innovation is based on the premise that knowledge must be actively applied in practice.
Knowledge transfer is therefore understood as a process through which theoretical knowledge is transformed
into practical skills (Kolb, 1984; Nonaka and Takeuchi, 1995).  This transformation reflects broader knowledge
management processes related to knowledge conversion, experiential learning and organisational learning
within educational environments.
The Venture Ecosystem course focuses on idea generation, opportunity identification and solution testing.
Learning occurs through experimentation, feedback and iterative refinement.
The Entrepreneurial Marketing and Branding course builds on this by applying ideas in marketing contexts,
including branding, digital tools and customer engagement. Students work with real -world tasks rather than
theoretical cases.

4.1 Conceptual Model of Knowledge Transfer
The proposed model conceptualises knowledge transfer in entrepreneurship education as a three-stage process:
1. Knowledge creation - students generate ideas and solutions through entrepreneurial exploration
(Venture ecosystem course),
2. Knowledge application - students apply these ideas in real or simulated market conditions
(Entrepreneurial marketing and branding course),
3. Feedback and iteration - students refine their solutions through continuous feedback, testing and
adaptation.
These stages are interconnected and supported by active learning methods, including project -based learning,
teamwork and digital tools. The model reflects the transition from explicit knowledge to tacit knowledge through
experiential learning (Kolb, 1984) and knowledge conversion processes (Nonaka and Takeuchi, 1995).

5. Expected Outcomes
The proposed model is expected to improve knowledge transfer in entrepreneurship education. It is assumed
that students will be better able to apply theoretical knowledge and develop practical entrepreneurial
competencies.
From the perspective of the learning process, the following outcomes are expected higher student engagement,
improved understanding of entrepreneurial processes  and development of problem-solving skills in real -world
situations.
From the perspective of learning outcomes, the following are anticipated:  improved use of digital tools, ability
to design and test entrepreneurial solutions and better preparedness for the labour market.
A key implication of the proposed framework is that knowledge transfer depends not only on the content of
teaching, but primarily on the way it is implemented. In particular, the connection with practice, flexibility of
teaching, and the use of digital tools plays a crucial role.
6. Implications
The conceptual analysis suggests the need for systematic innovation of study programmes in order to better
reflect the needs of the entrepreneurial environment.
It is important to  strengthen the connection between education and practice, emphasise the development of
practical and digital skills  and use active and innovative teaching methods. This is particularly relevant in the
context of digital transformation and AI- supported education, which increasingly influence entrepreneurship
education and knowledge transfer processes in higher education (Holmes et al., 2022).
Such an approach is consistent with trends in entrepreneurship and innovation, as well as with the requirements
of the digital economy (European Commission, 2020).

7. Discussion
The proposed framework suggests  that effective knowledge transfer in entrepreneurship education depends
not only on pedagogical methods but also on the overall design and implementation of the study programme.
This supports previous research emphasising the importance of experiential le arning and active engagement
(Kolb, 1984; Pittaway and Cope, 2007), while extending it by highlighting the role of structured programme
design.
Compared to existing literature, the proposed model contributes by integrating knowledge management
principles with entrepreneurship education in a sector-specific context. The framework also reflects the growing
importance of educational knowledge ecosystems, where students, educators and external stakeholders
collaboratively participate in knowledge creation, sharing and application processes (Nambisan et al., 2022).
While prior studies often focus on isolated teaching methods, this paper demonstrates the importance of linking
knowledge creation, application and feedback within a coherent learning system.
The results also highlight the importance of interaction between students, educators and practical
environments, which aligns with knowledge transfer theories emphasising relational and contextual factors
(Argote, 2013; Argote and Ingram, 2000).
At the same time, the proposed model remains conceptual and requires empirical validation. Its applicability
may also vary depending on institutional conditions, available resources and the level of integration with
industry partners.
8. Conclusion
The paper demonstrates that effective entrepreneurship education requires a shift from knowledge
transmission to its active application.
The proposed conceptual framework represents an approach to achieving this shift through project -based
learning, work on real-world tasks and the use of digital tools.
It is expected that the implementation of this model will contribute to the development of students’
entrepreneurial competencies and to a stronger connection between education and practice.
The proposed knowledge transfer model, based on the integration of solution development, application and
feedback, represents a practically applicable approach to innovation in entrepreneurship education.
At the same time, it should be emphasised that this paper presents a conceptual educational framework that
requires future empirical validation. Its practical applicability and impact should be assessed in future empirical
research, which represents a direction for future research. The proposed approach also has practical relevance
for curriculum designers and educators, as it provides a concrete way of linking theory with practice.
The paper has several limitations. First, the proposed model is conceptual and has not yet been empirically
tested. Second, the analysis focuses on a specific sector, which may limit its generalisability. Third, the
effectiveness of the model depends on in stitutional and organisational conditions that may differ across
contexts.
Future research should focus on empirical testing and validation of the proposed framework, including pilot
implementation and evaluation of learning outcomes . Further studies may also compare the effectiveness of
different knowledge transfer approaches across sectors and educational environments .
The paper also contributes to the knowledge management discussion by highlighting how learning environments
can support the transformation, sharing and application of entrepreneurial knowledge.
