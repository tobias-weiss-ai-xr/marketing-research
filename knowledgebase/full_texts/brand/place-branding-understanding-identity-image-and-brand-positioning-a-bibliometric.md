Abstract

Purpose: To conduct a bibliographic mapping of place branding and its respective ramifications (identity,
image, and brand positioning), aiming to define concrete concepts and establish future research agendas for
the advancement of place brand management.
Design/methodology/approach: A systematic and bibliometric analysis of 182 articles retrieved from the
Scopus database was conducted, focusing on business, management, and social sciences. The analysis utilized
Bradford's, Lotka's, and Zipf's bibliometric laws, combined with thematic  analysis and the use of software
such as VOSviewer and Power BI to examine clusters and trends.
Main Findings The results evidence a predominance of studies focused on European and North American
contexts, indicating a geographical gap in the literature. The need to investigate the effectiveness of marketing
strategies, the influence of social media on place identity, co creation with stakeholders, and the measurement
of economic impacts was identified.
Theoretical contribution  The study contributes to theory by clarifying frequently confused definitions
(identity versus image), mitigating theoretical overlaps. Furthermore, it proposes future agendas that
encourage investigation in underrepresented regions and the use of new methodological approaches.
Practical contribution The research assists managers in discerning how the brand should be built (identity)
and perceived (image), guiding the development of more effective and aligned positioning strategies to attract
tourists, investors, and residents.
Relevance/Originality This work stands out by filling the absence of reviews that clearly define each
ramified concept of place branding, empirically validating research opportunities in sustainability,
technology, and emerging contexts.

Keywords: literature review, place branding, brand identity, brand image, positioning.

Introduction

Brand management plays a fundamental role in various contexts, from supermarkets to
retail establishments and restaurants, where brand strategies are applied to stand out in a
competitive market (Gupta et al., 2020; Mohammad Shafiee, 2022). Recently, manag ers of
brands related to places have realized the opportunity to capitalize on the specific resources of
a location to build a unique brand identity. This approach not only provides competitive
advantages to the place but also makes it more attractive for investments and for the people who
frequent it (Barkun et al., 2021; Ocke & Platt, 2022).
Although it is possible to establish comparisons between the management of a product
brand and that of a place, it is crucial to highlight the unique complexities that exist. Companies
enjoy greater autonomy in building their brands, with employees mainly focused on fulfilling
specific duties. On the other hand, places face distinct challenges, since their brands must satisfy
both the expectations of people who are outside that place and those who live its daily reality
(Anholt, 2010; Kavaratzis & Hatch, 2013; Mariutti & Florek, 2022).

the main assets of companies, as it represents their identity in the market (Rua & França, 2018).

As a differentiating resource for the company, the brand is linked to one or more sets of

Therefore, priority is given to the definition of place branding adopted and concept ualized by

the places themselves. Thus, place brand management evaluates a variety of needs and demands
from the stakeholders of a given  locality and strategically seeks to harmonize all interests and
needs present in the place.

concepts that are inserted under the umbrella of place branding (Ocke, 2017; Ocke & Platt,
2022). The “place brand identity” is a concept that encompasses the unique an d distinctive
characteristics that represent the essence of a particular place (Anholt, 2010; El Damanhoury,
2020; Kavaratzis & Hatch, 2013; Nogueira & Carvalho, 2022). These characteristics may
include natural, historical, and cultural elements that disti nguish one place from another. The
management of this identity involves the evaluation of the needs and interests of local
stakeholders, as well as the strategic harmonization of these elements to create a shared and
positive identity.

Methodological Procedure

To carry out a bibliographic mapping about place branding and its respective branches
(place branding identity, place brand image, and place positioning), a systematic and
bibliometric analysis was conducted using four selected databases from Scopus, recog nized as
one of the main sources of scientific information worldwide (Visser et al., 2021). In this context,
literature reviews on the phenomenon of place branding and its interconnections with place
brand identity, place brand image, and place brand posit ioning will be presented, providing a
comprehensive view of the subject addressed in this study.
When it comes to the concept of bibliometrics, it is relevant to emphasize that this
research approach represents a precise tool for the formulation of future research agendas on a
specific topic, enabling several researchers interested in the area to identify gaps in the literature
and design new studies to fill these gaps (Wallin, 2005). Additionally, the systematic literature
analysis, a method that complements the bibliometric approach, provides more detailed insights
into how the literature related to a specific topic is being explored and treated, facilitating a
thorough and accurate assessment of the studies conducted (Jahan et al., 2016). Using this
methodological procedure, it is possible to achieve a more complete and in-depth understanding
of the research topic, which contributes significantly to the progress of scientific and practical
knowledge.

Conclusion
The objective of this article was to carry out a bibliographic mapping about place
branding and its respective branches (place branding identity, place brand image, and place
positioning). Through a systematic and bibliometric review, it was possible to ex tract four
distinct databases from Scopus, defining the following criteria: (i) containing the study’s
keywords (place branding and its branches); (ii) including only articles; and (iii) belonging to
the areas of Business, Social Sciences, or Economics and  Finance. After evaluating the raw
database and performing a cleaning process, 182 articles were carefully analyzed to create
theoretically grounded future research agendas for the development of place brand management.
Regarding the academic implications, the article presents a bibliographic mapping on
place branding and its various implications, including place brand identity, image, and
positioning. The detailed analysis of 182 articles highlights the need for future r esearch
investigating the effectiveness of place marketing strategies, the impact of social networks on
place identity construction, collaboration with local stakeholders in place brand creation, and
the evaluation of the economic and social impact of plac e branding campaigns. Furthermore,
the study points out the predominance of research conducted in European countries and the
United States, indicating the importance of expanding investigations to other geographic
contexts.
