Introduction: Saudi Arabia’s Vision 2030 links sport sponsorship with commercial
branding, sport-sector investment, tourism, public policy, and contested legitimacy
narratives. This systematic scoping review maps direct evidence on sponsorship
outcomes and distinguishes it from broader contextual scholarship.
Methods: Following PRISMA-ScR and JBI guidance, an updated OpenAlex
search was conducted on 22 August 2026 using 11 title-and-abstract query
blocks, supplemented by backward/forward citation checking and targeted
searches of official sources. Of 303 records identified (289 database hits and
14 supplementary records), 89 duplicates/duplicate versions were removed,
leaving 214 records for screening. Thirty-seven full texts were assessed and 27
reports were included: six direct sponsorship studies and 21 contextual studies
or reports. General theory sources were used only for interpretation.
Results: Direct evidence, concentrated in football and dominated by cross-
sectional, purposive, or qualitative designs, links sponsorship awareness and
identification with sponsor image, attitude, purchase intention, and, more
recently, stakeholder resistance to sponsor legitimacy. Contextual evidence
maps modernization, soft-power, sportswashing, tourism, participation, and
governance mechanisms but does not identify sponsorship effects. Study-
level appraisal indicates moderate-to-high concern across most direct studies
because of non-probability sampling, self-report measurement, narrow
settings, or interpretive dependence.
Discussion: The synthesis supports an evidence-calibrated framework in which
audience processing explains near-term brand outcomes, while governance
credibility and stakeholder participation are proposed boundary conditions for
legitimacy and public value. These latter pathways, and the proposed measurement
dashboard, remain author-derived propositions requiring direct empirical validation.

1 Introduction
Sport is one of the most visible components of the transformation
of Saudi Arabia as part of its Vision 2030. Professional leagues, sports
events around the world, club investment, esports, and tourism
programs and community programs offer commercial opportunities
for the sponsors, national-development opportunities, and a
reputation-building opportunity. These activities are interdependent
and not mutually exclusive. A sponsorship study can be conducted
on a specific sponsor–property relationship, exposure, activation, or
response of the audience; while a sport-investment, tourism,
participation, or political-economy study can be based on the
context in which sponsorship is understood.
If those units are conflated an attribution problem arises. Any
positive effects that are claimed for the tourism and participation
or for the league visibility or national reputation must be separated
from the effects of the infrastructure, ownership, hosting of the
event, media coverage and the wider policy reform, without which,
there would be no positive effects. However, if the review were
confined to six studies on direct sponsorship, certain institutional
factors that might explain variations in the outcomes of similar
sponsorship inputs for domestic and international actors would be
omitted. The review therefore follows a very explicit two-tiered
structure: direct evidence offers support for claims regarding the
sponsorship relationships, while contextual evidence offers support
for mechanisms, boundary conditions and research propositions
but not for estimates of the effectiveness of sponsorship.
The review poses: RQ1: What is the amount, range and
methodological profile of direct and contextual evidence relevant
to the Saudi sport sponsorship in the context of the vision
2030? Which sponsorship exposure-audience processing-brand
outcomes connections are directly supported by evidence
(RQ2)? How do sources in the context define legitimacy, soft
power, sportswashing, tourism, participation and governance,
and how do they overlap or diverge? Which of the RQ4
relationships (in an integrated framework of brand equity and
legitimacy and co-creation) are empirically supported and which
are propositions that need to be researched?
The contribution is intentionally capped. Firstly, the review
introduces a unit of analysis (only uses individually identifiable
reports and separates direct and contextual evidence). Secondly, it
evaluates each of the sources it provides with a suitable assessment
tool for the design. Third, it develops a modal model of the
relationship between sponsorship inputs and brand outcomes
through audience processing, and the inputs of credibility of
governance and participation of stakeholders and the outputs of
legitimacy and co-created public value. This integration illuminates
the context in which marketing salience can generate only short-
term support that simply can’t be achieved by the combination of
brand-equity and legitimacy, and service-dominant-logic approaches.
2 Methods
2.1 Design and reporting
The study is a systematic scoping review (1, 2) that was reported
following PRISMA-ScR (3) and informed by JBI guidance for
scoping reviews (4). Pooled effects or certainty estimates are not
made during the review, except in the items numbered (5) for
which PRISMA 2020 items have been used in order to increase
transparency. The narrative synthesis process was systematic,
beginning with the grouping stage, followed by comparison
within the group, convergence and divergence processes and
ending with the interpretation of the strength of the story (6).
2.2 The scope of boundaries and evidence
tiers
A direct sponsorship report needed to be one of the following:
Saudi sport sponsor, sponsored property, sponsor activation,
sponsorship exposure, or a sponsorship-specific audience outcome.
A contextual report was required to explore Saudi sport investment,
governance, tourism, participation, legitimacy, political economy etc,
and to offer an explicit mechanism or boundary condition that
could be relevant to the interpretation of sponsorship. No attempt
was made to infer sponsorship effects from contextual reports.
Interpretation was informed by general sponsorship theory, nation-
brand theory and non-Saudi value-creation reviews were not
accounted for in the PRISMA denominator. The operational
eligibility criteria and evidence-tier rules are summarized in Table 1.
2.3 Protocol
There was no protocol found that could be registered. It
cannot replace registration as an alternative to the past, it is a
methodological limitation. The eligibility boundary, evidence
tiers, charting fields and appraisal rules below should be pre-
registered in case of any changes that may be made in the future.
2.4 A collection of information sources and
search strategy
The search for updates was automatically repeated using the
OpenAlex Works API on 22 August 2026. Only the
title_and_abstract.search field was used for searching and the
search range was 01 Jan 2016 to 22 Aug 2026 (English). The 11
query blocks were for the following issues: Saudi context,
sponsorship, football sponsorship, brand equity, legitimacy,
sportswashing, tourism, investment, participation, esports and
women sport. Using the identifier and comparing by title/DOI,
88 duplicate publications and one duplicate publication version
were removed from the database, leaving 200 publications.
There were 14 further non-duplicate eligible reports that were
identified through backward/forward citation checking of
eligible reports, and/or systematic search of the Saudi Vision
2030, Sport for All Federation and PwC publication sites. The
supplementary PRISMA screening audit is developed and
includes 214 records screened and decisions. The reproducible
search strategy is provided in Appendix A, and the PRISMA-
ScR reporting crosswalk is provided in Appendix B.

Selection was done by deduplication, abstract/titles screening and
full-text evaluation. The screening and extraction at the revision stage

Synthesis of the direct and contextual tiers before integration.
The following were compared: exposure, mechanism, outcome,
design, direction, and magnitude of reported relationships, all of
which were directly involved in the direct reports. All four of the
following items were coded for the contextual reports: legitimacy

horizon and attribution strength. A claim was categorized as
supported if it was measured directly in a study that investigated
the sponsorship relationship. Only claims made only in the
context were labeled as proposed, illustrative, or contested. No
voting and no decision based upon the number of sources.
2.7 Study-level methodological appraisal
All sources included were reviewed at report stage by the same
person who did the extraction. For this study, empirical qualitative,
quantitative and mixed methods reports were evaluated using the
design specific factors of the Mixed Methods Appraisal Tool
(MMAT) 2018 (7). The six domains of SANRA (8) were used for
the evaluation of narrative or critical reviews. The official and
grey literature from the industry was assessed by authority,
accuracy, coverage, objectivity, date and significance (9). Each
judgment was made by a second pass to ensure accuracy with the
reported design and also the full wording of the item. This is not
reproduced independently, and therefore is not claimed as an
adjudicator or agreement statistic, and is part of the limitations of
the review. The judgments were: Yes, No, Partial, and Cannot tell
and a cross design numerical total was not made.
2.8 Ethics and use of the language/AI tools
This was not an ethics approval review because it was a review of
published documents and publicly available documents and there were
no human participants and/or identifiable private documents
involved. Grammarly was utilized to assist with editing language and
formatting the audit files and manuscript during revision. All
decisions are retained in the included record-level audit. The authors
are solely responsible for the accuracy, originality, citations, eligibility
boundary, appraisal judgments, interpretations and conclusions.
3 Results
3.1 Study selection
The 11 OpenAlex query blocks returned 289 hits. Deduplication
removed 88 cross-query duplicates and one duplicate publication
version, leaving 200 database records. Citation checking and
targeted official-site searching added 14 unique supplementary
reports, so 214 records underwent title/abstract screening. Of these,
177 were excluded because they lacked an eligible sponsorship
relationship or a sufficiently specific contextual mechanism. Thirty-
seven full texts evaluated with 10 excluded: four full text reports
that were not published or finalized, three reports that were
outside of the context/sponsorship boundaries with the study, two
reports that lacked methodological evidence specific to Saudi
Arabia or retrieval, and one preprint after the final text was
identified. The final corpus consisted of 27 reports of which six
were direct sponsorship reports and 21 were contextual reports.

3.2 Characteristics of direct sponsorship
evidence
The direct tier contains six reports and seven empirical
components because Alenazi (10) reports a survey and an
experiment. Five studies examine audience or fan responses in Saudi
football; Krieger and Schultz (11) analyze an athlete-led challenge to
FIFA’s Aramco sponsorship, extending the direct evidence from
commercial response to sponsor legitimacy. The quantitative studies
remain dominated by non-probability, convenience, and self-report
designs, while the qualitative sponsorship-conflict study is
interpretive rather than effect-estimating. The design, results, and
limitations of the direct sponsorship studies are summarized in Table 3.
3.3 Direct sponsorship findings
3.3.1 Convergence
Direct studies converge on a limited but coherent near-term
sequence. There is a correlation between team identification and
awareness of the sponsor (15), which in turn is correlated with
the sponsor image, brand attitude, and purchase intention in
various Saudi football samples (12–15). The most transparent
model reports awareness → brand attitude (β = .389, p < .001),
awareness → purchase intention (β = .188, p < .01), and brand
attitude → purchase intention (β = .552, p < .001). The results
are consistent with audience-processing and associative-transfer
mechanisms, but they are mostly associational.

The direct studies do not establish a uniform sponsorship
effect. Both of these studies yielded only slight correlations

3.4 Contextual findings
Audiences and meanings of sport investment in Saudi Arabia are
the main points agreed upon by contextual sources. Domestic
accounts focus on modernization, participation, inclusion, tourism
and opportunity while political-economy and critical accounts
focus on state strategy, soft power, rights discourse and
sportswashing. New evidence introduces four additional
identifiable legitimacy mechanisms: media normalization, nostalgia,
athlete resistance, and esports governance (11, 31, 32, 36). These
are not exclusive effect findings but rather interpretations which
are contingent upon the stakeholder(s) and the arena(s). No sport
washing perceptions, cross-audience legitimacy or governance
credibility are included as moderators in any of the quantitative
direct sponsorship study analyses included in the reference list.
Plausible downstream pathways are suggested by the tourism
reports, participation reports and economic reports, but not
necessarily by sponsorship. The constructs of competitive balance,
disclosure, ownership arrangements and public-value reporting are
thus considered as contextual conditions, which can potentially
help or hinder legitimacy, but are not empirically proven
mediators. This distinction is important because it allows for a
distinction between what should be tested, and what is the effect
of sponsorship. The characteristics, contributions, and attribution
limits of the contextual evidence are summarized in Table 4. The
report-level methodological appraisal of the included evidence is
summarized in Table 5. The calibrated inferences across the
principal claims are summarized in Table 6.

4 Discussion
4.1 What the evidence does support
The defensible conclusion is smaller than a broad assertion
that Vision 2030 sport sponsorship fosters brand equity,
legitimacy and public value. The relationships between these
factors are supported by direct quantitative evidence related to
the Saudi football context, and the results indicate that the type
of sponsors, integration, processing fluency, and the perceived
benefits of the fans are key factors. Direct evidence is another
form of qualitative evidence that shows that when there is an
ethical conflict, the athlete resistance to the legitimization of the
sponsor may be activated. The evidence is overwhelmingly often
reported from non-probability, cross-sectional, self-reported or
single case interpretive designs, and hence, cannot be used to
directly identify causal effects or generalise to other sports,
sponsors, stakeholders or regions.
4.2 A theoretical contribution of
conditional integration is desired, not
additive
The brand-equity theory can explain how the same brand
association can generate associative salience and evaluative
transfer (37–45); the legitimacy theory can explain why
associative salience and evaluative transfer of the same brand
association may be accepted by some, but not others (46–51);
and the value co-creation theory can explain how a value co-
creation can transform a one-way communication investment
into a two-way experience and benefit (52–59). The integrated
model takes the claim of audience processing a step further:
Areas of audience processing yield short-term marketing results;
however, the ability of governance to be credible is a
prerequisite to areas of audience processing yielding long-term
marketing results that are sustained by the audience and are
participative activation, or are created by the audience
themselves. This conditional sequence is more clear than if four
theories were lined up next to each other. The model also allows
for predictions that can be tested. The link between positive
attitude of the sponsors and multi-stakeholder legitimacy should
be strengthened by transparency in governance, and the
sportswashing perceptions should weaken it. Participation in the
activation should be “trans-mediated” with the effect on
perceived fan benefit on co-created value. The propositions are
not reported as findings since there is no direct study included
in the work that tests these propositions.
4.3 Implications for practice and
governance
Sponsors have the power to make a positive impact based on
direct evidence by enhancing recognizability, property fit,
integration and measurable fan value. They should not make
any assumptions about tourism and inclusion effects or
national-reputation effects based on awareness data. Brand and
governance measurements and independent stakeholders’
measurements should be considered together when assessing
sport properties. The rules of attribution logic should be made a

4.4 Limitations of the review
This review was not registered in advance. The reproducible
update was done based on one broad open bibliographic index,
some targeted and official site searches for citations, and may
have overlooked some relevant records that were poorly
indexed. A two-pass repeated single-reviewer audit was used
instead of duplicate review to achieve the screening, extraction
and appraisal. Restriction to English may be a reason to
discontinue Arabic scholarship. Football-dominant and the
direct corpus is small and heterogeneous. One contextual
synthesis was written by the present lead author and went
through the same appraisal rules, and was not used as effect
evidence. Study-level appraisal continues to be design sensitive
and is not designating equivalency for unlike evidence types.
There were no appropriate metanalyses and a causal or certainty
of effect statement is not provided.
4.5 Research agenda
Priority designs include those based on preregistered
longitudinal panels around sponsorship announcements and
activation phases, randomized or quasi-experimental
experimental tests of the type of sponsorship, sponsorship fit,
integration and disclosure, multi-stakeholder comparisons of
domestic and international legitimacy, difference-in-differences
designs, and synthetic-control designs for tourism and public-
value outcomes. Research needs to be expanded to women’s
football, sports for the disabled, community programmes,
regional clubs and esports.
5 Conclusion
Saudi sport sponsorship is an important field of research, yet it
is still in the early stages of development, from a methodical point
of view. There are six direct reports to the near term audience
processing pathway of identification and awareness to the
image/attitude of the sponsor to intention to athlete resistance,
and no general impacts on tourism and public value. Twenty-
one contextual reports are provided to explain why governance,
stakeholder interpretation, participation, political economy and
media framing may be important, but have yet to have been
tested in designs for sponsorships. The review covers the three
types of information (direct evidence, contextual and
background theory) that are then clearly differentiated, and then
unsupported links (propositions) are clearly identified. The next
step is to make more compelling claims, derived from the
measurement of multi-stakeholders, rather than more claims,
derived from widely variable evidence.
