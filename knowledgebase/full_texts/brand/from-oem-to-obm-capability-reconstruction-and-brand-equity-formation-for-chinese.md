Abstract: The transition of Chinese consumer goods manufacturers from OEM to OBM represents
a shift in value creation from order execution to consumer value creation. The key lies in the
systematic reconstruction of existing manufacturing and market capabilities. This article analyzes
the transformation of business logic, core capability reconstruction, brand equity formation, and the
contradictions inherent in this transformation. Results show that quality control, large -scale
production, and supply chain collaboration remain crucial foundations for independent brands, but
they need further integration with product innovation, market awareness, brand positioning, digital
channels, and consumer relationship capabilities. This requires transforming manufacturing
advantages into perceptible product value for consumers through cross -departmental information
feedback. Brand equity is gradually formed through stable product experiences, brand awareness,
consumer interaction, and relationship building. During the transformation process, companies also
need to coordinate the tension between manufacturing efficiency and market exploration, OEM
customer relationships and independent channels, and short-term operating performance and long-
term brand investment. The key for OEM compa nies to achieve OBM upgrades is to establish a
value creation mechanism that continuously connects manufacturing capabilities, market learning,
organizational collaboration, and brand equity.
Keywords: OEM; OBM; Independent brand; Capability reconstruction; Brand equity

1. Introduction
Chinese consumer goods manufacturers have long participated in the global value
chain through the OEM model, undertaking brand orders through process control, large-
scale production and supply chain collaboration, and forming strong quality and delivery
capabilities. However, under this model, product definition, market positioning, brand
communication and channel control are mainly in the hands of the contracted brand, and
the value obtained by the manufacturing enterprise i s more concentrated in the
production process. With the narrowing of cost advantages, the differentiation of
consumer demand and the intensification of market competition, it is difficult to sustain
value enhancement by relying solely on orders and manufac turing profits. Independent
brands have gradually become an important path for manufacturing enterprises to extend
to high value-added links.
OEM, ODM and OBM correspond to different value creation boundaries and
capability combinations. A study by Tsai-Lin et al. on contract manufacturing enterprises
in Greater China shows that when enterprises transform from OEM to ODM and OBM,
they need to reconfigure innovation, organization and marketing practices [1].

2. The Business Logic of OEM to OBM Transformations
2.1. Value Creation Boundaries and Capability Gap
OEMs primarily organize production based on specifications, quantities, and quality
standards provided by brand owners, with competition focusing on cost, quality, delivery,
and capacity stability. ODMs, building upon manufacturing, further assume R&D, design,
and engineering integration functions, beginning to participate in product solution
development. OBMs, on the other hand, require companies to own and operate their own
brands, indepe ndently completing demand identification, product definition, pricing,
channels, communication, and consumer relationship management. As value activities
extend from the production end to the demand end, profit sources gradually expand from
processing revenue to product differentiation revenue and brand premium.
This change creates a significant capability gap. OEMs excel at efficient execution
according to established standards, but OBMs need to continuously identify target
consumers, assess demand changes , and form stable value propositions. Manufacturing
capabilities can guarantee product quality and stable supply, but cannot replace market
perception, brand positioning, and channel operations. Therefore, transformation is not
about abandoning the origina l manufacturing system, but rather supplementing and
recombining market -side capabilities on top of manufacturing capabilities, enabling
companies to independently complete the business process from demand identification
to value realization.
2.2. From Order-Oriented to Consumer-Oriented
In OEM operations, the information flow primarily revolves around the brand client,
with market judgment and end -user feedback typically handled by the client. Upon
transitioning to OBM (Original Brand Manufacturing), consu mer demand becomes the
decision-making input used by R&D, production, marketing, and service. Companies
need to identify changes in demand from user reviews, search data, after -sales data, and
sales data, and adjust products, prices, and channels accordingly. The operational starting
point shifts from order execution to demand identification, meaning the marketing
department needs to be involved in the product development process earlier.
The core of capability restructuring lies in changing the way informa tion and
resources flow. Quality, process, procurement, and large -scale manufacturing are
fundamental capabilities that need to be retained. Engineering development needs to be
integrated with user needs and product definition, and B2B customer management needs
to be expanded to include channels and consumer relationships. Only by establishing a
stable mechanism for market information to enter R&D, production, and resource
allocation can a company's manufacturing advantages be transformed into its own brand
advantages. Table 1 summarizes this capability change and its connection to brand equity.

3. Reconstruction of Core Capabilities for the Leap Forward of Independent Brands
3.1. Product Innovation and Differentiation Capabilities
Independent brands first need to form product value that consumers can recognize.
The OEM stage mainly solves the problem of how to produce according to the established
requirements, while the brand stage needs to determine what to produce, wh o to serve,
and what kind of differentiation to form. Technological and manufacturing advantages
can only become market value when they are transformed into reliability, convenience,
performance, or user experience through product definition and design. Th erefore,
product innovation plays the role of connecting the manufacturing system and brand
positioning.
Market knowledge needs to be continuously incorporated into product decisions.
Mitręga's research shows that the dynamic allocation of market knowledge and marketing
resources is positively correlated with the speed of product innovation and market
success [4]. OEM companies should combine engineering feasibility with market
acceptability, and adjust the product portfolio through user needs, competitive changes,
and after -sales feedback, so that manufacturing capabilities can shift from passive
execution to supporting proactive differentiation.
3.2. Market Perception and Brand Positioning Capabilities
After facing the end consumer market, companies need to form a continuous market
perception mechanism. User interviews, reviews, search trends, competitor changes, and
channel feedback can help companies identify consumer needs and determine target
groups, core benefits, and price ranges accordingly. Market p erception does not increase
marketing information, but rather transforms scattered external signals into a basis for
R&D, pricing, and resource allocation. Brand positioning further transforms market
judgment into a value direction followed by all departme nts. Clear positioning can
constrain product functions, design, price, communication, and channel selection,
reducing brand fragmentation caused by different departments pursuing technology,
sales volume, and market hotspots separately. For OEM companies, this means that

3.3. Channel Operations and Digital Marketing Capabilities
Digital channels can help manufac turing companies reach consumers directly, but
platform use itself does not equate to forming digital marketing capabilities. Homburg
and Wielgos found that digital marketing capabilities can make additional contributions
to corporate performance beyond tr aditional marketing capabilities, and their value
comes from the integration of digital tools, data, and interactive processes [5]. Pascucci et
al. also pointed out that when digital technology truly changes marketing, marketing roles,
organizational processes, and value creation methods need to be adjusted simultaneously
[6]. For OEM companies undergoing transformation, digital channels simultaneously
serve as sales and market learning tools. Search, reviews, after-sales, and sales data can be
used to iden tify demand, discover quality issues, and verify products and prices, which
are then fed back to the R&D and quality systems. Only by forming a stable data feedback
loop can companies possess relatively independent end -market learning capabilities and
reduce their reliance on market information provided by brand customers.
4. Transformation of Core Capabilities into Brand Equity
4.1. Product Value and Brand Awareness
Capability restructuring is a condition on the enterprise side, while brand equity is
manifested in consumers' stable perception and behavioral responses to the brand.
Related studies typically regard brand awareness, brand association, perceived quality,
and brand loyalty as important dimensions of consumer brand equity [7]. Therefore, even
if OEM companies have established product, market, and channel capabilities, they still
need to transform these capabilities into identifiable brand value through consumer
experience.
Products are the primary carrier of this transformation. Stable quality, cl ear
functionality, and consistent design can reduce consumers' uncertainty about new brands
and gradually form an overall judgment. Iglesias et al. found that brand experience can
influence brand equity through satisfaction and emotional commitment [8]. On ly by
consistently delivering on product promises can manufacturing capabilities further build
trust, repeat purchases, and price acceptance; short -term promotion cannot replace this
accumulation process.
4.2. Consumer Interaction and Brand Relationship
A significant change between OBM and OEM is that companies can directly manage
consumer relationships. Post -purchase evaluations, suggestions, recommendations, and
community interactions not only influence other consumers but also provide companies
with new market knowledge. Zollo et al. found that social media marketing further
influences brand loyalty, brand awareness, and perceived quality through the cognitive,
social, and personal benefits consumers gain, as well as brand experience [9]. The value
of digital interaction therefore lies in promoting experience and relationship accumulation,
rather than simply expanding exposure.
Whether consumer relationships can form long-term assets also depends on whether
feedback enters internal decision -making. Interac tion can only be transformed into
organizational learning when after-sales issues, usage scenarios, and user suggestions are
absorbed by the R&D and quality departments and responded to in subsequent products.
Continuous product improvement and corporate r esponse can enhance consumer trust,
enabling relationship assets to be built on the basis of real experiences.
4.3. Brand Meaning and Authenticity
After functional value and consumer relationships have stabilized, brands need to
form a continuous expressio n of meaning. Chinese manufacturers can differentiate
themselves by leveraging local design, product language, and cultural resources, but these
expressions must be consistent with product capabilities and service behavior. If brand

5. Transformation Path and Operational Conflicts
5.1. Gradual and Dual-Track Transformation Paths
Normally OEM companies have a choice to follow a gradual or dual track approach
for brand development. The gradual approach is based on the accumulation of technology,
capital and customer knowledge in the manufacturing business, which is f rom OEM to
ODM and then to the market and brand capacity. Capability accumulation is relatively
continuous, its limitations are easy to be restricted by the existing order logic and
organizational inertia. The two -track strategy, however, creates a somewha t separate
brand group, and still keeps the OEM business going. The manufacturing business leads
the cash flow, production and supply chain, and the self -owned brand leads the product
definition, product channels and consumer part.
However, two tracks are not completely independent during dual track operation.
Various aspects of manufacturing, procurement, and quality can be shared, but aspects of
product planning, market decisions, and performance evaluation must remain somewhat
independent. Otherwise, the  brand business can be easily limited by the short -term
capacity utilization/ order profit. Lee et al. 's research shows that self -owned sales and
brand customers do not necessarily form a complete conflict. Reasonable market
boundaries and channel arrange ments can alleviate the conflict of interests between the
two parties [2]. So, the selection of the route should take into account the concentration of
customers, market overlap, flexibility of capacity, and cash flow situation.
5.2. Manufacturing Efficiency and Long-Term Brand Building
If OEM and OBM will coexist then there will be a tension between manufacturing
efficiencies and market explorations in the long run. OEM is about standardization,
stability and economies of scale, whereas independent brands need new product trials,
speedy iterations and differentiated investments. Wolf's research on the dual activities of
manufacturing enterprises showed that the dual activities of exploration and utilization
can enhance the performance of the enterprise only  when the enterprise is capable of
coordinating the activities of both types [11]; Sahi et al. also pointed out that knowledge
development and resource restructuring can solve the contradiction between exploration
and operational efficiency [12].
This cont radiction is also apparent in performance evaluation and use of digital
channels. As long as the brand team is measured using only short-term sales, capacity and
inventory metrics, it's simple to focus on short -term discounts, live streaming and paid
traffic; if the brand team decides to focus on these factors in the longer -term, users might
also pay more attention to price and platform. For enterprises, they should pay attention
to quality indicators, costs, gross profit, inventory, etc. of the operation, as well as
indicators such as repeat purchases, natural search, user recommendation and price
stability of the brand, and combine short -term efficiency and long -term brand
accumulation into the same resource allocation.
6. Conclusion
In essence, the transi tion from OEM to OBM by Chinese consumer goods
manufacturers would mean that the value creation focus would no longer be on order
fulfillment but on consumer value creation. Independent brands' manufacturing, quality

embedded in product development, production and resource allocation, to make the
production advantage become real market value.
Brand formation is a perspective that says that enterprise capabilities sho uld have
stable experiences with the product, brand awareness, consumer interaction and
relationship accumulation to become brand assets. The transformation process also needs
to be accompanied by a resolution of the contradictory nature of manufacturing efficiency
and market exploration, OEM customer and independent channels, short term operation
performance and long term brand investment. Independent brands can only evolve from
a side line of the manufacturing system to a stable value creation method, whe n
information flows, decision-making authority and performance evaluation are all aligned.
