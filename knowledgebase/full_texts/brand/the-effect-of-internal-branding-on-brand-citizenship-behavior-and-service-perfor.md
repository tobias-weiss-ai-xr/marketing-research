Abstract
Service is a fast-increasing industry in the global economy in general and in Viet-
nam in particular. Therefore, service companies need to recognize the importance
of employees in service and cultivate specialized manpower and systematic internal
brand management. The current study aims to explore and describe the role of inter-
nal customer orientation on service employee’s perceptions and behaviors, tested
a theory-driven framework of the multidimensional relationship between components
in internal brand management and proposed suggestions for Vietnamese service
organizations to enhance their competitive capabilities in the market. Using quan-
titative data analysis methods and SEM structural modeling, the study confirmed
that the internal customer orientation can positively influence the brand identifica-
tion and brand commitment of salespersons, helping them exhibit brand citizenship
behavior towards the organization and enhance service performance. Besides, the cru-
cial role of emotional exhaustion was investigated. In doing so, the research intends
to implicate internal branding as a tool for enhancing the quality of the Vietnamese
service industry.
Keywords: Internal branding, Internal customer orientation, Emotional exhaustion,
Brand citizenship behavior, Service performance
Introduction
The service industry is considered one of the most important sectors, accounting for a
significant proportion of the economic structure in many countries around the world.
Currently, it is a top priority for developing countries in Asia, holding a critical position
in their economies (Noland et al., 2012). The service industry emerged to meet human
needs, which are becoming increasingly diverse and sophisticated, and it offers higher
economic efficiency compared to other sectors.
In fact, the service industry is rapidly expanding in the global economy in general,
and in Vietnam in particular. It is no longer a minor component of the overall economy,
but has become a major sector that contributes significantly to economic value. Cur -
rently, the restructuring scheme for Vietnam’s service industry aims for a growth rate
of approximately 7–8% during the 2021–2030 period. By 2030, the service sector is
expected to account for about 50% of GDP , with projections indicating a further increase
to around 60% during the 2030–2050 period (Luat Viet Nam, 2021). In this context,

mensional relationship between components in internal brand management outcomes;
and (iii) proposed suggestions for Vietnamese service organizations to enhance their
competitive capabilities in the market.
Literature review
Internal branding
One of the key aspects of internal marketing is internal branding, which centers around
conveying the fundamental brand principles of a company to its employees (Biedenbach

& Manzhynski, 2016; Chong, 2007). Agreeing with that opinion, some scholars believe
that internal branding is a sub-component within the larger field of internal marketing
(Du Preez et al., 2017; Iyer et al., 2018). Many researchers believe that these two concepts
are relatively similar so they can be used interchangeably (Appel-Meulenbroek et  al.,
2010; Hofer & Grohs, 2018). The main reason it is aimed at influencing the awareness
and behavior of employees within the company. However, recent researchers believe that
there should be a clear distinction between the two concepts. Because although they are
closely related, the goals of the two strategies are different. The goal of internal market -
ing is to orient the internal market to create a common vision and value for internal cus-
tomers to achieve common marketing goals. Besides, internal branding’s goal focuses on
brand management among employees to bring to external customers.
Internal marketing incorporates internal branding as a crucial element, as it involves
communicating and promoting the company’s brand values, culture, and identity to
employees, and ensuring that they understand and embody these values in their work
(Bodkin et  al., 2016; Garas et  al., 2018). By doing so, companies can create a more
engaged, motivated, and customer-focused workforce that can help to deliver a consist -
ent and compelling brand experience to external customers. In addition, the process of
internal branding has gained significant attentions as it facilitates the synchronization
of employee conduct with brand values (Punjaisri & Wilson, 2017; Taku et al., 2022). It
is the process of communicating the spirit of pro-branding to employees by utilizing the
organization’s existing resources and capabilities. Therefore, employees are expected to
behave in accordance with the brand, changing internal activities to improve the perfor -
mance of the organization.
In a similar vein, internal branding is explained as the process of promoting the pri -
mary corporate brand of a company to its employees in a manner that enables them to
comprehend the interconnection between delivering the brand and fulfilling its prom -
ise (Tosti & Stotz, 2001). Besides, the success of corporate branding is contingent on
the attitudes and behaviors of employees in delivering the brand promise to external
stakeholders (Punjaisri & Wilson, 2017). Employee understanding and commitment to
brand values lead to the fulfillment of customer expectations of the brand. From there,
the internal brand can shape employee behavior, especially in the service industry. For
organizations to provide services in line with their values, therefore, they must acknowl -
edge the significance of these values and facilitate their comprehension by employees.
Combining these views together, internal branding can be said to be a series of activi -
ties that induce customer satisfaction by internalizing the brand and delivering the
company’s brand to external customers. It is a series of activities that the company was
formed to unify the actions of its employees. Companies sincerely offer the company’s
brand to internal customers to elicit external customer satisfaction by building the
feelings and images that the company wants employees to think of their brand. Inter -
nal branding can be called internal brand management, which internalizes the brand to
employees and leads this to customer. Therefore, a company needs to manage its rela -
tionship with its employees and brands so that employees who are internal customers
can internalize a positive brand.
In the service industry, the foundation of internal branding lies in imparting brand-
related information from the organization to the employees at a fundamental level (King

about them, causes employees to behave consistently well in front of customers based on
the standard they have created.

internal branding campaign is, therefore, to align employees with the company’s brand
values and culture, and to create a customer-oriented organizational culture that deliv -
ers a consistent and compelling brand experience to customers.
Internal customer orientation
Internal customer orientation is a concept that has rarely been used in research in ser -
vice marketing. Prior studies have focused mainly on concepts such as internal market -
ing, marketing orientation, customer orientation, and internal marketing orientation
(Anosike & Eid, 2011). Internal customer orientation has not been developed into an
independent concept by applying the concept of customer orientation to internal
employees, but rather by borrowing the concept of customer orientation (Conduit &
Mavondo, 2001; Matanda & Nduisi, 2013).
In other words, internal customer orientation is the application of the concept of cus -
tomer orientation to internal customers from an internal marketing perspective, which
was applied in a large customer relationship for the performance of an enterprise in
external marketing. Internal customer orientation reflects the management philosophy
that internal customer satisfaction is delivered to external customers at the internal mar-
keting level (Grönroos, 1981; Rafiq & Ahmed, 1993).
As all employees of the organization began to be viewed as internal customers (Bowen
& Schneider, 1989; Peters et  al., 2004), all employees were placed at the same time as
suppliers and customers to other employees in the organization (Foreman & Money,
1995; Grönroos, 1981). These internal customers play an important role in delivering
goods and services to end customers, external customers (Anosike & Eid, 2011). As it
can be stated that internal customer orientation is a part of an organizational culture
which provides a unifying focus to support and guide the attitudes and behaviors of
organization members to deliver quality to other employees (Conduit & Mavondo, 2001;
Peters et al., 2004).
Emotional exhaustion
A lack of organization’s interest and communication will arouse negative employee emo-
tions such as resentment and aggravation during customer interactions and it interferes
with the accomplishment of personal goals, thereby leading to emotional exhaustion.
Emotional exhaustion is known as a psychological state that responds to excessive work
demands, causing stress and exhaustion of emotion (Gaines & Jermier, 1983; Martínez-
Iñigo et al., 2007). Besides, frontline employees, such as salespeople, are the ones who

must directly interact with managers and customers. This face-to-face interaction will
cause long-term stress, suffering at work and emotional exhaustion (Howard & Cordes,
2010; Karatepe & Uludag, 2008; Maslach & Jackson, 1981). Emotional exhaustion and its
effects deserve attention, since its importance to organizational performance and opti -
mization (Halbesleben & Bowler, 2007; Seidler et al., 2014). As such, employees not only
feel lack support from colleagues or superiors, but also lose emotional connections with
the organization when they are under emotional exhaustion.
Frontline employees at work must perform a process called emotional labor, that is
expressing their emotions at the request of the organization (Grandey & Gabriel, 2015;
Hochschild, 1983). Therefore, employees must produce emotional expressions as
required by managers or as expected by customers (Bono & Vey, 2005; Grandey, 2000).
Such emotional expressions are contrary to their own emotional experiences, this incon-
sistency will interfere with their communication with the organization, such as forming
attitudes and behaviors that are attached to the organization.
Brand identification and brand commitment
The extant literatures suggest a common definition of brand identification as employees’
sense of belonging to the brand (Iyer et al., 2018; Punjaisri & Wilson, 2011; Saleem &
Iglesias, 2016). In a similar vein, employee brand identification is the extent to which
an employee associates themselves with their organization’s brand (Bagozzi & Dholakia,
2006; Hem et al., 2016). It is a measure of how strongly an employee feels connected to
their organization’s brand and how much they feel that the brand represents their per -
sonal values and beliefs. Although internal branding has many results, brand identifica -
tion is still considered as one of the important outcomes and crystallized results of brand
awareness (Piehler et al., 2016). The concept of brand identification comes from organ -
izational identification, and the profound source is social identity theory (Helm et  al.,
2016). Through brand identification, it is possible to predict the attitudes of employees at
work as well as predict their other behaviors, especially in the service environment (Van
Knippenberg et al., 2007; Vijayakumar & Padma, 2014).
Employee brand commitment, on the other hand, refers to the degree of loyalty and
attachment that an employee has towards their organization’s brand (Fernandez-Lores
et  al., 2016; Gilani & Cunningham, 2017). In a similar concept, brand commitment is
generally defined as the extent of psychological attachment that employees feel toward
the brand (Burmann & Zeplin, 2005). It is a measure of how dedicated an employee
is to represent and promote their organization’s brand in their work and personal life.
Employees who have cultivated a high level of commitment and established robust con -
nections with their brands often perceive a strong link between themselves and the
brand, considering it an essential aspect of their lives (Baumgarth, 2010; King & Grace,
2010). In the service sector, brand commitment is considered a major driver of employee
brand behavior (Afshardoost et al., 2023; Bartikowski & Walsh, 2011).
Overall, commitment can be viewed as the long-term desire to maintain a valuable
relationship. When employees feel that they belong to the organization, they will have
a certain commitment and especially have extra-ordinary behaviors to support the
organization and its associates (Baker et al., 2014; Hurrell & Scholarios, 2014). Through

Brand citizenship behavior (BCB)
Brand citizenship behavior represents the desired outcome of an internal branding strat-
egy aimed at aligning employees with the company’s values and commitments. There -
fore, brand citizenship behavior refers to out-of-role or voluntary acts which bring the
active reinforcement of brand identity and strength (Chiang & Hsieh, 2012). In the same
vein, brand citizenship behavior encompasses employees’certain generic display of spe -
cific behavioral traits that support brand identity beyond their formal roles (Burmann &
Zeplin, 2005; Burmann et al., 2008). These employee actions aimed at fulfilling the brand
promise to customers and aligning their personal values with the organization’s values
(King & Grace, 2010; Morhart et al., 2009). Therefore, brand citizenship behavior entails
employees consciously championing the brand, performing well in their work, and posi -
tively contributing to brand growth to enhance the overall customer brand experience.
In fact, the personnel within the service industry are responsible for embodying the
brand’s values and promises. Through their actions, they significantly shape brand
awareness among customers (De Chernatony & Segal-Horn, 2001). Consequently, when
employees consistently demonstrate behavior that aligns with the brand, the service
brand can become a distinctive or strong (Vallaster & De Chernatony, 2005). Employee
citizenship brand behavior occurs when individuals internalize the brand image and
express it through their conduct towards consumers and other key stakeholders (Miles
& Mangold, 2004). This conduct is indicative of the employees’heightened awareness,
contentment, dedication, and allegiance towards the brand. They are all important fac -
tors in delivering exceptional services that affect service performance.
Service performance
Given the intensifying competitiveness in the business environment, there is a growing
emphasis on understanding the elements that contribute to favorable customer experi -
ences. Service organizations recognize the pivotal role played by frontline service work -
ers, who act as the interface between the company and its customers, in shaping service
interactions (Solomon et al., 1985). Empirical studies have consistently shown that when
customers receive high-quality service, it positively influences their perception of service
quality, leads to higher satisfaction levels, and has the potential to foster customer loy -
alty (Borucki & Burke, 1999; Bowen et al., 1989). Therefore, it is important to understand
and capture employee service performance.
Services have unique characteristics such as intangibility, production, and consump -
tion at the same time, so customer evaluation is very important (Bowen & Schneider,
1989). Because the consumer experience is important equal or more than the value of
the goods. Any customer expects the company to deliver on its brand promise, by speed-
ing it up, using its resources vigorously, honestly, and with enthusiasm. According to
prior research (Castro et al., 2005; Choi & Hwang, 2019), consumers tend to hold service
providers accountable for certain aspects of the service delivery process.
Overall, employee performance is commonly defined as the behaviors that contribute
to organizational goals and are under the influence of individual employees (Campbell

et al., 1990). In the service sector, the importance of customer perceptions in evaluat -
ing employee performance has grown (Bowen & Waldman, 1999). Additionally, the
quality of interactions between employees and customers significantly affects customer
satisfaction.
Hypothesis development
Internal customer orientation keeps influence on employee
The organizational culture of a service company plays a significant role in shaping its
performance and success (Schraeder & Self, 2003; Warrick, 2017). A strong and posi -
tive culture promotes employee satisfaction, loyalty, and commitment, which in turn
enhances the quality-of-service delivery to customers. To this aspect, internal customer
orientation was regarded as a special type of corporate culture (Conduit et  al., 2014).
Newcomers to the organization will learn the consistent standards and objectives. The
degree of internal customer orientation can significantly affect the effectiveness of inter -
nal brand management (Baumgarth & Schmidt, 2010; Papasolomou & Vrontis, 2006).
Furthermore, internal customer orientation serves as a cohesive focus that directs and
encourages the attitudes and behaviors of organizational members towards providing
quality service to fellow employees.
To incentivize and inspire employees to recognize the brand, embody and concretize
in the working process, managers need to take specific actions to put internal customer
orientations into practice (Baumgarth, 2010). Internal customer orientation involves the
management processes through which brand insights results, including the opportunity
for all employees to be involved in the branding process (Ewing et al., 2019; Gounaris,
2008; Lings & Greenley, 2010). The adoption of internal customer orientation prompts
managers to exhibit behaviors that motivate and inspire employees, leading to a trans -
formation of the entire organization into a customer-centric entity. Therefore, it is nec -
essary to apply supportive initiatives aimed at improving and strengthening alignment in
the goals and brand values of employees and the organization, such as internal customer
orientation.
In fact, each organization has a unique style of dealing with problems and these dif -
ferences are shaped by the organizational culture. Internal customer orientation gives
the organization a cultural identity and guides activities that take place within the com -
pany (Gagliardi, 1986; Schein, 2015). It helps employees define their identity and influ -
ences their commitment to the organization (Jandeska & Kraimer, 2005; Malik et  al.,
2010; Singh Sengupta & Sinha, 2005). Although congruence (e.g., values or beliefs),
forms the basis for employees to identify with the organization (Wieseke et  al., 2009)
or brand (Hem et al., 2016; Hurrell & Scholarios, 2014), internal customer orientation
also contributes to increased relevance. From the origin of the definition, identification
is an individual’s perceived oneness with or belongingness to an organization (Demir,
2015; Van Dick et al., 2004), and from an internal perspective, identification occurs when
the employee sees his or her self-image overlapping brand image (Bagozzi & Dholakia,
2006). In a similar vein, the extent to which employees identify with their brand may rely
on how well the brand aligns with their real and ideal selves.
The employees brand identification is influenced by a lot of efforts from managers
and organizations such as treating employees like customers and investing in them. In

Moderating effect of emotional exhaustion

beliefs and perceptions, causing stress and emotional dissonance (Cooper, 2012; Har -
mon-Jones & Mills, 2019). Previous studies have shown that this emotional dissonance
reduces individual identity and even forms strongly opposing false identities if prolonged
(Hinojosa et al., 2017; Metin & Camgoz, 2011). Obviously, these personal identities can -
not match the corporate identity nor the direction of the organization.
Based on the theory of resource conservation, it is also possible to assess the nega -
tive effects of emotional exhaustion (Martínez-Iñigo et  al., 2007; Moon & Hur, 2011).
To manage burn out and avoid further burn out, employees strive to conserve their
remaining resources by carefully choosing how and when to use those resources. Emo -
tionally drained employees operate with suboptimal psychological functioning so they
only keep their relationship with the organization at the level of job maintenance, but
they cannot identify or affective commitment the brand. In such a situation, they are
unable to engage in actions and behaviors that are consistent with the manager’s desired
orientations (Lewin & Sager, 2009). That emotional exhaustion feeling also reduces their
perception of identity with the brand. It can be speculated that psychological status will
act as a negative moderator between the organization and its employees, reducing the
impact of internal branding on cognitive processes. Accordingly, the following hypoth -
esis was derived:
Hypothesis 2
Emotional exhaustion has a negative impact on relationship between the internal cus -
tomer orientation and brand identification.

The relationship between brand commitment, BCB and service performance
Brand commitment is a precursor to brand-aligned employee behavior (Punjaisri
et al., 2009). Employees who are committed to their organization’s brand often view
themselves as brand ambassadors and take pride in representing the brand (Jacobs
& Washington, 2003; Xiong et  al., 2019). Therefore, they are more likely to align
their behavior with the values and mission of the brand, and to actively promote the
brand to others. Studies have shown that employees who are more committed to their
organization’s brand also tend to have higher levels of job satisfaction, organizational
commitment, and job performance (Bataineh et al., 2017; Du Preez et al., 2017; Tsui
et  al., 2013). These positive outcomes can in turn reinforce employees’commitment
to the brand and motivate them to continue engaging in brand citizenship behaviors.
Whether studying employees’basic attitudes, organizational behavior, or internal
brand management, the results show that employees with a cognitive or emotional
attachment to the brand strive to build, fostering brand image through every behavior
in the working process (Afshardoost et al., 2023; Chiang et al., 2012; Saleem & Igle -
sias, 2016). Accordingly, the following hypothesis was derived:
Hypothesis 5
Brand commitment has a significant positive effect on brand citizenship behavior.
The service performance can be attributed to the employees’act of serving and
assisting customers (Liao & Chuang, 2004). Accordingly, the performance of sales -

mance relates to the actions and approaches adopted by salespeople throughout the
sales process, whereas outcome performance represents the measurable outcomes
of their efforts (Fred Miao & Evans, 2007; Sujan et  al., 1994). In addition, empathy
performance is not just an understanding of the attributes that customers look for;
rather, it involves understanding the emotions that customers are experiencing.
If employees have a commitment to the brand, they really represent an uncondi -
tional promise of future customer satisfaction (Afshardoost et al., 2023; Kimpakorn
& Tocquer, 2010). Salespersons’ brand commitment is one of the ways to reduce
risks for businesses in terms of personnel changes as well as service performance.
Employee commitment allows the organization to differentiate its services from those
of competitors. Maintaining this commitment requires efforts from both the enter -
prises and the salesperson. The salesperson’s trust in the brand, commitment to the
organization and belief in the work they are doing will result in an amazing service

Conclusion
Compared to other countries in the region, Vietnam is in a situation where the service
industry is growing together with the rise in income level. In order to seize these oppor -
tunities, service companies are pursuing various branding activities. As such, internal
branding efforts by a company can be considered a highly significant factor in achieving
a sustainable competitive advantage. In addition, interest in how service companies sys -
tematically manage and implement internal branding and the impact of internal brand -
ing on performance is growing.
This study focuses on the internal marketing and internal branding, in which the posi -
tive value of the brand in the interaction between the organization and salesperson is
transmitted to the salesperson’s behavior and service performance. It deals with the pro-
cess of linking internal brand orientation and employee’s cognitive, which are the inter -
nal marketing performance of a company. Some results can be given as follows:
First, the study examined  the effect of the manager’s internal customer orientation
-driven by the internal branding process- on salesperson’s identification with the brand.
The results confirmed that internal customer orientation positively influences  brand
identification. This finding suggests that understanding employees’ needs, maintaining
effective communication, and demonstrating managerial concern—all key aspects of
internal customer orientation—significantly contribute to enhancing employees’ identi -
fication with the brand.
Second, this study aims to know whether the salesperson’s emotional exhaustion could
reduce the effect of manager’s internal customer orientation on brand identification
of salespersons. The findings confirmed that emotional exhaustion will play a role as a
moderator, negatively affecting the process of receiving orientation and affecting brand
identification. In other words, the positive effect of internal customer orientation on the
salesperson’s brand identity can be enhanced by adjusting emotional exhaustion. In addi-
tion to the hypothesis, the study confirmed that the salesperson’s emotional exhaustion
had a negative (−) effect on brand identification. These findings indicate that the ben -
eficial impact of internal customer orientation-  rooted in managerial comprehension,

Page 21 of 34
Tong and Kang  Journal of Innovation and Entrepreneurship           (2025) 14:76

communication and concern- may be diminished when employees experience high lev -
els of burnout or emotional exhaustion.
Third, the effect of salesperson’s brand identification on brand commitment of sales -
person was studied. As a result, the findings  confirmed that the salesperson’s brand
identification had a positive (+) effect on brand commitment. Perceiving yourself as
belonging to the organization, having the same thoughts and goals, leads to a commit -
ment and long-term stay.
Fourth, the study investigated whether the salesperson’s brand identification could be
transferred to be the brand citizenship behavior. As a result, the findings confirmed that
the salesperson’s brand identification had a positive (+) effect on the salesperson’s brand
citizenship behavior.
Fifth, the study examined the effect of brand commitment on brand citizenship behav-
ior. The results confirmed that brand commitment positively influences brand citizen -
ship behavior.
Sixth, this study examined the effect of the salesperson’s brand commitment on their
service performance. The findings confirmed that brand commitment had a positive (+)
impact on the salesperson’s service performance. In particular, to ensure the practical -
ity and convenience of measurement, service performance was assessed through self-
reported data from the salesperson. This self-assessment is considered to closely reflect
the actual service performance within the company context.
Seventh, the  study examined the impact of salesperson’s brand citizenship behavior
on  service performance, emphasizing that such behaviors are voluntary and not part
of mandatory job obligations. The findings  confirmed that brand citizenship behavior
exerted a positive (+) influence on the service performance. This suggests that investing
in internal branding -particularly by focusing on salespeople as internal customers- can
generate competitive advantages  for service businesses.
As all the hypotheses were supported, the findings suggest that from an internal mar -
keting  perspective, internal branding-by internalizing  the brand among salespeople as
internal customers -effectively fosters brand citizenship behavior and enhances service
performance. Most importantly, the model illustrating the outcomes of internal brand -
ing highlights the influence of the manager–employee relationship, wherein employees,
as internal customers, ultimately contribute to the company’s profitability and customer
loyalty (Churchill et al., 1985; Rogers et al., 1994). From the Vietnamese environment,
the study’s contribution hopes to be of reference value when assessing the service envi -
ronment in countries in Southeast Asia or growing service areas around the world.

First, it is meaningful in that it confirmed the effect of internal customer orientation, a
major outcome variable of internal branding, on employee’s brand identification. This
can be said to confirm that internal customer orientation is a factor that precedes inter -
nal branding, and that internal customer orientation is an area of internal market orien -
tation. Through this, the effect of internal customer orientation on brand identification,
the outcome variable of internal brand management, was confirmed. This suggests that

was mainly studied in the field of personnel organization, but in this study, it was con -

First, it can see that the salesperson, who is a labor in the service industry, is the front
line in contact with customers, but is the last point when receiving information or direc-
tion from the organization. They have a greater need to be cared for and understood
than employees who take on other roles in the organization. Specifically, this study sug -
gests that the positive effects of internal customer orientation (understanding, commu -
nicating, and showing concern) can enhance employee’s brand cognitive.
Second, it is confirmed that emotional exhaustion has a negative effect on the rela -
tionship between the manager internal customer orientation and salesperson’s brand
identity. This issue can be minimized by adjusting the store manager’s internal customer
orientation or reducing this contact burnout in the working process in a variety of ways.
Third, it has been confirmed that the salesperson’s brand identification is shifting to
re-service behavior for organization as a result of the company’s internal branding. This
means that in the future the personal interaction between the salesperson and the cus -
tomer at the service point will be improved, the employee’s emotions are transferred
to the customer, affecting the emotional bond between the customer and the products

and brands. Therefore, companies need to keep in mind that managing the relationship
between the salesperson and the brand is the most important task. Therefore, it is nec -
essary to invest in building internal branding with a systematic and roadmap, possibly
starting from internal customer orientation.
Fourth, it is confirmed that the brand commitment and brand citizenship behaviors
of salespeople, increased through internal branding, has a positive impact on service
performance and later company performance. Therefore, companies and managers
should recognize the importance of salespeople orientation at service touchpoints and
make efforts to maintain positive emotions with the brands of salespeople. Thereby,
forming voluntary behavior without prompting to accomplish the overall goals of the
organization.
Limitations and future research
Previous researchers developed a service marketing pyramid model that adds technol -
ogy to the service marketing triangle model developed by Kotler consisting of a three-
way relationship between a company, an employee, and a customer (Kotler, 2007;
Parasuraman & Grewal, 2000). However, the fact that these technical factors were not
considered in this study is a limitation. Therefore, in the future, there should be studies
that place the role of employees in the multidimensional relationship between the com -
pany, employees, and customers.
Besides, it is necessary to consider the intervention of the power of organization
culture according to the SMEs environment of a developing country. The ability of an
individual or group that can be exercised to influence or dominate the behavior of a per-
son or minority group, especially most of the SMEs in Vietnam are family businesses.
Considering the characteristics of this study, which deals with the internal branding
of service organization, it is expected that it will be a better study if the influence of
organizational culture that can affect the behavior of managers and employees is also
considered.
Finally, this study only examines the role of internal branding in some Vietnamese ser-
vice sectors. The main limitation is that the sample of employees participating in this
study was not classified and grouped into different occupations, and the number of ser -
vice occupations was limited in the survey. Therefore, further investigation with people
working in different settings with different job characteristics is needed. Additionally,
future research could extend this study by comparing groups of employees in different
services and service sectors to understand the nature of the specific impact of internal
branding.
