1. INTRODUCTION
The current competitive landscape in the banking industry necessitates providing good service and offering impactful
customer experiences to address customer satisfaction issues. Banking institutions in the Kurdistan Region – Iraq are
rapidly changing as a result of the adoption of new technology, shifting customer demands, and the growing presence of
private financial brokers. There has, therefore, been a shift in the focus of marketing research from the basic transactional
services customers expect from banks to the emotional and experiential aspects of the banking relationship [1].

Digitalization, changing customer demands, and the increased competition have brought significant transformations
to the banking sector, especially in the context of the private banking institutions. In the developing economies like the
Kurdistan Region of Iraq, the role of the private banks in the financial intermediation is very important and they are
competing fiercely to attract and retain their customers. This significance notwithstanding, empirical research on the
drivers of customer satisfaction in private banks is still scarce, particularly in comparison with that of the public banks
and the developed countries [2].
ABSTRACT:
This paper will analyze how service quality and brand experience influence customer satisfaction in the case of the
private banks in the Erbil City, Kurdistan Region-Iraq. This study is based on empirical evidence, unlike the previous
ones, which were mainly based on the research of the public banks or the developed economies, where the competition
is low and the services change rapidly. The quantitative research design was used, and the primary data were gathered
with 388 customers of the private banks through a structured questionnaire based on validated measurement scales.
Service quality (reliability, responsiveness, assurance, empathy, and tangibles) and brand experience (sensory,
affective, intellectual, and behavioral) were considered as independent variables and customer satisfaction was the
dependent variable. The hypothesized relationships were tested with Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) with
AMOS. The findings indicate that service quality and brand experience have a strong and positive impact on customer
satisfaction, with reliability and affective experience having the most impact. The research has theoretical
contributions in that it shows how functional service performance and experiential branding play a complementary
role in the creation of satisfaction in the emerging banking markets. Practical implications on bank managers and
policymakers are discussed.

Past studies have always highlighted that service quality is a major factor that determines customer satisfaction.
Nevertheless, recent marketing literature claims that customers do not judge services by their functional performance
only, but also by holistic brand experiences involving emotional, cognitive, and behavioral reactions. This paper
combines the two schools of thought in order to give a more detailed account of customer satisfaction in private banking
[3].

Service quality has been an important focus of customer satisfaction research in the banking industry. The
SERVQUAL model proposed by Parasuraman et al. identified the key service quality dimensions as reliability,
responsiveness, assurance, empathy, and tangibles [4]. Multiple researches show a positive relationship between service
quality and satisfaction and the customers' behavioral intentions [5], [6]. In developing countries, where financial
products are not very diversified, service quality remains a key competitive advantage.

Interpretations of overall brand experience as a concept are complex as the individual ties and relationships of service
providers function in a multidimensional manner. For instance, Alharthi focuses on the pivotal impact that various forms
of emotional, cognitive, and behavioral experiences with the service brand have on the overall evaluation [7]. Within the
banking sector, these positive brand experiences and relationships are highly packed with trust, loyalty, and satisfaction,
especially for the more intangible service elements with high credence qualities [2].

Due to the centrality of brand experience and service quality, their effect on consumer satisfaction in the context of
private banks in Erbil City is highly important, yet the empirical evidence is weak. To fill this void, the current study
focuses on an explanation of the data gathered from 388 bank customers to identify the interrelationships of the variables
applying a quantitative method along with AMOS-based SEM.

2. LITERATURE REVIEW
2.1 Quality of Services

Service quality is the overall evaluation of the customers about the performance of a service provider in comparison
to their expectations [4]. The SERVQUAL model suggests five dimensions, including reliability, responsiveness,
assurance, empathy, and tangibles, which have been extensively used in the banking sector and other service sectors
because of their high diagnostic qualities. Despite the fact that other frameworks like the RATER model have been
suggested, SERVqual was used in the study due to its multidimensional nature and the wide empirical validation in the
research on financial services [3].

The previous empirical research findings are consistent in their positive correlation between customer satisfaction
and service quality [2]. Nevertheless, most of these studies are based on the single-country setting or they concentrate
more on the public-sector banks hence restricting the generalizability of the results to the private banks in the emerging
markets. In addition, there is a relative lack of research that critically compares the relative significance of the individual
service quality dimensions in the context of the private banking setting, especially in the developing economies.
Reliability, Responsiveness, Assurance, Empathy and Tangibles.

Empirical data indicate that reliability and responsiveness tend to be the best predictors of customer satisfaction in
the banking industry because of the high-risk and time-sensitive nature of financial services [8]. Assurance and empathy
also increase customer trust and relationship, whereas the tangibles affect the first impressions of professionalism and
credibility by customers. However, the comparative influence of these dimensions can differ in cultural and institutional
settings, and it is necessary to conduct local empirical research.

2.1.1 Tangibles
'Tangibles' includes 'buildings, machinery, communication tools, and personnel appearance'. In the banking industry,
modern tools, clean environments, and attractive surroundings can shape consumers' views on professionalism and first
impressions [9]. Past research indicates that 'tangibles' can be especially significant in developing nations, where physical
signs lessen doubts [10].

2.1.2 Reliability
In this research, reliability is defined as the bank's ability to deliver services with precision and consistency. Customers
assume that transactions will be conducted seamlessly and that the bank’s financial data is accurate; therefore, reliability
has been a positive predictor of customer satisfaction [3]. Given that the banking industry is laden with financial risk,
reliability is the most critical dimension of service quality. [11].

2.1.3 Responsiveness
Responsiveness concerns the willingness of the employees to provide help to clients when the need arises. In banking,
this includes the swiftness of the help, the speed of solving the issues, and the available support for clients. Studies show
that there is an increase in satisfaction due to responsiveness and in for-profit competitive industries, that satisfaction is
achieved with the aid of responsiveness and the ability to focus on detail to provide support and help [8].
2.1.4 Assurance
Assurance involves the knowledge and characteristics of employees, and their abilities to inspire confidence. Considering
the inherent risk of financial decisions, assurance becomes important to alleviate such concern. Earlier studies show how
assurance positively affects satisfaction through perceived security and professionalism [6].

2.1.5 Empathy
Empathy manifests in a bank’s ability to provide caring and individually tailored attention to customers. Customers
emotionally bond and develop loyalty to an organisation when they enjoy personalised service and interaction because
of the ability to meet their needs [12]. Empathy is critical in relationship-based banking, as building trust and partnerships
over the long term is essential [13].

2.2 Brand Experience

Brand experience refers to sensory, affective, intellectual, and behavioral reactions of customers to brand stimuli
[14]. In contrast to the conventional brand attitude measures, brand experience describes lived and holistic customer
experiences with service brands. The available literature proves its beneficial impact on customer satisfaction and loyalty
[15]. Nevertheless, the majority of the previous studies have been concentrated on retail and hospitality industries, and
the banking industry and especially the emerging economies private banks have not been well researched.

Akın and Gürbüz argue that a consumer's internal sensory, emotional, cognitive and behavioral reactions shape
brand experience [16]. Unlike previously defined brand frameworks, brand experience recognizes the emotional impact
that interactions elicit from individuals. Such an impact is vital for every industry, and for services that depend on and
foster long-term relationships like banking, the impact is of special significance.

2.2.1 Sensory Experience
The sensory experience includes the various visuals, sounds, and the feel of the brand. The sensory experiences of
‘banking’ include the building designs, logos, website designs, and other items in the environment. Research indicates
that sensory enhancements increase the perceptions of value and satisfaction. Customers are likely to feel that the bank
is more credible and trustworthy when the bank’s visual brand is improved and the visual cohesive branding of the
branches is enhanced.
2.2.2 Affective Experience
Affective experiences analyze how customers emotionally respond to interactions with brands. Banks are emotionally
attached to their customers through positive, peaceful, trusting, and pleasurable emotional responses, leading to increased
satisfaction [16]. The banking sector is rather symbolic, and there is considerable literature indicating that, in the financial
service sector, the affective experience is one of the main predictors of satisfaction [17].

2.2.3 Intellectual Experience
Experiential learning or intellectual experiences is about capturing the attention of consumers and helping them think of
a problem and solve it. In banking, intellectual stimulation can derive from the specialised innovative digital offerings,
self-service advanced technological systems, or the educational financial content provided. Seeking and solving a
challenge fosters brand competence and gratification satisfaction perception [18].
2.2.4 Behavioral Experience
The behavioral experience is the active and lifestyle-related manifestations customers have with the brand. In the
case of banks, this can be the use of convenient mobile apps, seamless ATM transactions, or active participation in
banking-related brand events. Behavioral experiences facilitate a feeling of engagement and interactivity, which
contributes to satisfaction and loyalty [19].

2.3 Customer Satisfaction

Customer satisfaction refers to the general assessment of a customer on whether a service is satisfactory or not [16].
It is well known as one of the major consequences of service encounters and the antecedent of customer loyalty, retention,
and positive word-of-mouth [20]. Previous studies tend to focus on customer satisfaction as a result of either service
quality or brand experience alone, whereas little has been done to determine their interaction in one integrative model.

2.4 Relationship Among Service Quality, Brand Experience, and Customer Satisfaction
Customer satisfaction has often been a prime outcome in services marketing, seen as a driver of customer retention,
loyalty, and positive word-of-mouth [21], [22]. Among the many determinants of satisfaction in the banking industry,
brand experience and service quality stand out. They both shape the customer’s service encounters and, eventually, the
long-term relationship with the bank.

2.4.1 Quality of Services Rendered and Customer Satisfaction
A plethora of research validates how important service quality is in relation to customer satisfaction [3], [6]. Past
research within the finance industry highlights that for this industry, service quality and service responsiveness and trust
as well as service delivery pace are important. Clients and service providers in the banking industry are exposed to
complex and sensitive issues. Thus, there are clients for whom service quality and service responsiveness and service
delivery trust are critical [23]. Research in lower-income countries indicates that service providers’ extra efforts and care,
and personal attention and service provider initiative affect customer satisfaction in a much stronger way than in cases
when customers are only interested in the service provider’s services [12], [13]. Therefore, it can be concluded that in
service quality, customer trust and satisfaction are of primary relevance.

2.4.2 Brand Experience and Customer Satisfaction
Octavia et al state there are four types of sensations and experiences. Out of these four, affective and behavioural, are the
main experiences customers base their evaluations of a brand on. This proves brand experience is very important and
significant to evaluations.[24]. Numerous studies, like those of Khan and Fatma and Kim, show that satisfying brand
experience generates emotional attachment, fosters trust, and increases satisfaction in a plethora of service sectors [25],
[26]. Customer satisfaction in the banking industry is particularly affected by experiential factors like aesthetically
pleasing bank and website design, digitally and emotionally assistive interactions, and the customer's continued use of
the bank's services over extended periods.

2.4.3 Combined Effects on Customer Satisfaction
Building on previous studies, both service quality and brand experience together affect customer satisfaction levels.
This is because both variables target different, yet complementary, factors of customer evaluation. According to a recent
study, customer satisfaction is highly affected by factors of service quality and functional performance attributes, while
brand experience adds emotional and cognitive value to customer interactions [27]. Studies show that customer
satisfaction increases to a higher level when the customer service at banks is of high quality and the customer leaves with
a positive experience [12], [28]. On the other hand, the integrated effect of the abovementioned variables is one of the
most understudied phenomena in the emergent market of the Kurdistan Region – Iraq. This proposed research is intended
to close this void by utilizing brand experience and service quality to estimate customer satisfaction through SEM on a
study of 388 customers of private banks in Erbil City.

2.5 Conceptual Framework and Hypotheses
2.5.1 Conceptual Framework
Following the criteria from the literature, this study will develop a conceptual model that assesses the impact of
service quality and brand experience on customer satisfaction in private banks in Erbil City. Service quality refers to
customer’s perceptions of the service performance of a bank and can be assessed using the five dimensions model:
assurance, empathy, responsiveness, reliability, and tangibles [4]. These dimensions assess to what extent the bank has
satisfied the customer’s service expectations.

As well, brand experience pertains to the total interaction customers have with the banking brand and can be assessed
using the four components: sensory, affective, intellectual, and behavioral [16]. While service quality looks at the
performance level, brand experience looks at the non-performance aspects such as emotions and feelings which affect a
customer’s overall assessment of a bank.

2.5.2 Research Gap and Hypotheses

According to the literature reviewed, the present study will fill three major research gaps: (1) the paucity of empirical
data on the determinants of customer satisfaction in the context of a private bank in the city of Erbil, (2) the scarcity of
integration of the service quality and brand experience dimension into the banking research, and (3) the lack of critical
analysis of the relative strength of the individual service quality and brand experience dimension in the context of the
emerging markets. Based on this, the hypotheses are as follows:

H1: Service quality positively and significantly affects customer satisfaction at private banks in Erbil City.

H2: Brand experience has a positive and significant influence on customer satisfaction in private banks in Erbil
City.

3. METHODOLOGY

The cross-sectional quantitative research design was used to investigate the connections between service quality,
brand experience, and customer satisfaction. The research sample was made up of clients of the private banks in the city
of Erbil. By the time of data collection, there were about 18 licensed private banks in the Erbil City.

The convenience sampling method was used to gather 388 valid responses. The sampling had to be convenient
because of the limitations of accessibility, time, and the lack of publicly available customer lists in the banking industry.
This sampling technique restricts the external validity of the results, but it is common in the banking and service marketing
research and is deemed suitable in the exploratory research based on Structural Equation Modeling [30].

Data were collected using both in-person and online questionnaires. The use of mixed data collection modes helped
to increase response rates; however, it may introduce systematic differences across collection methods. To minimize such
bias, a standardized questionnaire with identical item wording and ordering was used for both modes.

5. DISCUSSION OF FINDINGS
The results confirm that the most significant dimensions of service quality are reliability and responsiveness, which is
consistent with the risk-reduction theory of financial services, where accuracy and timeliness minimizes perceived
uncertainty and trust-related issues [1] [8]. Additionally, affective and behavioral brand experiences had greater influence
than sensory and intellectual dimensions which implies that emotional involvement and active participation are more
important to customer satisfaction than aesthetic cues alone [14] [15]..

This paper builds on the existing theory by showing that the relationship between functional service quality and
experiential branding together influences the customer satisfaction in new private banking markets, thus going beyond
the single-construct explanations that are prevalent in previous banking literature [2].

The current study provided important information regarding the importance of service quality and brand experience
impact on customers’ satisfaction in the context of private banks in Erbil City. From the service quality attributes,
customers satisfaction is mostly impacted by Reliability and Responsiveness. This is consistent in the literature, which
highlights the importance of banking services being accurate, reliable and prompt. Reliability provides customers with
the peace of mind that banking transactions are being done accurately and on a consistent basis, and therefore they
perceive lower risks. Responsiveness reflects that the bank is concerned and works towards customers’ needs on a timely
basis. Also, Tangibles, Assurance, and Empathy have an impact on patrons satisfaction, which highlights the importance
of the functional and relational aspects of the service delivery system. Tangibles, such as the modern and professional
appearance of staff, contribute towards the establishment of a sense of trust and professional credibility. On the contrary,
Assurance and Empathy enhance and strengthen the emotional tie that customers have towards the bank, which is
necessary for the establishment of a long-term relationship. In general terms, the findings indicate that private banks need
to find a balance between demonstrating efficiency and being able to extend personalised service to customers for their
satisfaction.

The dimension of experiences people go through has shown that clients’ satisfaction revolves more around the
behavioural and affective as compared to the cognitive and sensory. This result proves more about the participation and
emotional engagement of customers in the banking system. Customers trust and feel loyalty and therefore are more
satisfied when there are emotionally positive experiences with the service such as having staff that care and environments
that are friendly. Additionally, the ease of participation in the services, such as sponsored activities and ease of use of the
banking digital platforms, also add to the satisfaction derived from the value of the services. Although intellectual and
sensory experiences are also relevant, their impact is lesser. This means that while cognitive engagement and aesthetics
are relevant, they actually take a back seat to emotional and participatory experiences when it comes to the satisfaction
derived.

This research confirms that the customers' satisfaction level is considerably influenced by both the perceived service
quality of the hemodialysis service and the brand experience (banking sector). Evidently, customers' satisfaction and
qualitative levels are higher if the service is both timely and responsive, and if the brand experience is supportive,
emotional and engaging. They are in tune with and confirm the findings of other researchers, and point out that in order
to elevate the customers' satisfaction and the qualitative service for the customers, both aspects of the service, the core
and the supplementary, should be focused on. If banks, in the highly competitive and rapidly evolving regions such as
the Kurdistan Region, Bangladesh, can provide brand experiences where both psychological and emotional welfare are
attained, and if the functional aspects of the service are delivered, they can be certain that they possess a competitive
advantage in the market.

6. CONCLUSION, RECOMMENDATIONS, AND FUTURE RESEARCH
6.1 Conclusion

The research shows that service quality and brand experience are the two factors that contribute to customer satisfaction
in the private banks. These results highlight that satisfaction is the outcome of associative relationships and not the strict
causal inference because of the cross-sectional design. The study analyzed how the quality of service and brand influence
customer satisfaction in the selected Private Bank in the City of Erbil, Kurdistan Region-- Iraq, based on a sample of 388,
using SEM in AMOS. The study conclusively proved its hypotheses conceptualized in the framework of the study. The
analysis showed that service quality is superior, customer satisfaction is the effect of reliable service, and responsiveness
is more predominant. This finding affirms that customer service in banking needs accuracy and dependability and prompt
service, and in-service banking and the other service delivery dimensions that are important are Tangibles, Assurance,
and Empathy. Customer satisfaction is also impacted by brand experience, with similar results. Of the dimensions of
brand experience, the impact was the Affective and behavioral, meaning there are emotional interactions and active
participation in the experience from the customer. The Sensory and Intellectual experiences were impactfully positive
but a little relatively lower. The study also demonstrated that service quality and brand experience impact customer
satisfaction. Customer satisfaction is higher in Banks with solid brand interactions that also Reliable Service Delivery.
This strengthens the idea that service and experience satisfaction are two sides of the same coin that drive customer
loyalty and positive reviews.

Overall, the study demonstrates the customer satisfaction phenomenon with respect to private banking and the many
interrelated issues and emphasizes the importance of both the effective and efficient delivery of services and the branding
of a service for the attainment of a sustainable competitive advantage in the market.

6.2 Recommendations
The results should be understood by the bank managers as relational associations but not causal effects. Customer
satisfaction can be greatly improved by improving reliability, responsiveness, and emotionally engaging brand
interactions.

The subsequent actionable recommendations are drawn from the recommendations provided to the private banks in the
city of Erbil.
1. Enhancing the Quality of Service: To achieve a high level of service reliability, banks ought to focus on the training
of their employees, fine-tuning of their processes, and the adoption of digital solutions in order to achieve service delivery
in a reliable, timely, and accurate manner. Respondents mentioned reliability and responsiveness as the most important
attributes in determining their service satisfaction.
2. Improving the Relationship and the Experience: The banks ought to provide greater care and guarantee and to include
the clients in a more personalized way, as these strategies enhance the emotional and psychological bond between clients
and banks. Satisfaction and loyalty are a result of positive experiences with brands, and so these experiences ought to be
designed to include the emotions and behaviors of the clients.
3. Enhancing the Experience at the Branch and Online: The current and tangible elements ought to be given attention as
well. Customers appreciate professionalism and active engagement when there is a good design in the branches, the
graphics are aesthetically pleasing, and the digital solutions are easy to use.
4. Service Quality and Brand Experience: There is a need for a more unified approach in the banks as they have to offer
their clients a combination of functional and experiential marketing. Integrating marketing and operations will enhance
the activities to be more effective and consistent.
5. Ongoing Improvement: There is a need for more ongoing and continuous service improvement in the banks in the area
of service quality and brand experience. This will provide banks the opportunity to track and adapt to the changes in the
expectations of customers.
The application of these suggestions will allow private banks to bolster customer satisfaction metrics as well as customer
loyalty, engage in client acquisition, and improve their strategic competitive advantage in the expanding banking sector
within the Kurdistan Region.
