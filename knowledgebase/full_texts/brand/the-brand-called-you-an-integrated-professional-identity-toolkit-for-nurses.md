Abstract
:
Introduction
:
The professional identity of nurses is the product of the interaction of personal values, clinical competence,
workplace relationships, reflective learning and organisational context. Professional identity in contemporary nursing schola
rship is
conceptuali
sed through knowledge, values and ethics, leadership, and professional comportment; however, there is limited evidence
evaluating interventions that target professional identity. Workplace development initiatives often address issues of interpe
rsonal
compe
tence, safe practice or personal confidence as separate issues rather than as mutually reinforcing aspects of professional fo
rmation.

Innovation
:
“Brand Called You” is a three
-
module professional development toolkit that was first developed as employee branding material
for the 2022
-
2023 annual operating plan. “Knock It Out” focuses on conflict
-
management styles, communication, listening, collaborat
ion,
reflection, and escalation. “Be Meticulous” emphasises accurate communication and documentation, medication safety, organisat
ion,
avoiding unsafe shortcuts, and reflective self
-
monitoring
-

all with focused attention. "I Am Enough" looks at self
-
esteem, confidence,
boundaries, self
-
reflection, feedback, professional pride, and dealing with challenges at work. Reflective activities are recorded in a My
Career Growth Diary (MCG).
Proposed framework
:

The toolkit suggests that conflict
-
management competence, reliable professional
practice and healthy self
-
esteem may help to develop stronger nursing professional identity but does not prove these claims. The MCG
diary is presented as a transversal reflect
ive
-
learning tool designed to connect experience, self
-
assessment, feedback, action planning and
behavioural change.

Implications for practice
:
The revamped framework replaces unsupported or potentially unsafe statements with
evidence
-
aligned guidance on context
-
sensitive conflict management, communication of medication, documentation, lawful delegation,
escalation, psychological safety and valida
ted measurement. No conclusions on effectiveness can be drawn due to the lack of data on
systematic implementation and outcomes. "Therefore, initial testing should focus on acceptability, feasibility, fidelity, psy
chological safety
and measurement performa
nce before controlled effectiveness evaluation."

Its value lies mainly in its program theory and educational design. Its effec

curiosity and collaboration when interests can be

Theoretical Background

Set boundaries.

•

Self
-
esteem is how a person evaluates their overall worth.

•

Self
-
efficacy is about the confidence to do things and face
challenges.

•

Resilience is adaptive functioning despite challenges.

•

'Well
-
being' is a term used to describe a broader positive
functioning or experienced quality of life.

•

Professional identity consists of patterns of thinking,
doing, relating, and feeling, grounded in the

knowledge,
norms and values of nursing.

These constructs may be related but are not interchangeable.
For example, a study among nursing students found
associations between self
-
efficacy, resilience and professional
identity, but the cross
-
sectional design did not allow causal
conclusions. Thus, the “I Am Enough” module should direct
its attention to healthy self
-
regard and self
-
awareness, and
self
-
efficacy, resilience, and identity should be considered as
separate potential evaluation outcomes. [
7,3]

Proposed Delivery Model

The core intervention should comprise:

•

Facilitated module sessions: evidence
-
based content,
nursing scenarios, structured discussion and skills
practice.

•

Application to practice: behavioural objectives
appropriate to the scope and clinical context, selected
individually.

•

MCG reflective cycle: describe the event; identify
thoughts, emotions, actions, contextual contributors and
professional values; consider alternatives; plan one
testable adjustment.

•

Feedback and coaching: private, developmental
discussions with trained facilitators.

•

Reinforcement: short team huddles or learning
conversations, de
-
identified cases, no blame.

•

Escalation safeguards: clear separation between normal
interpersonal disagreement and safety incidents, unethical
conduct, bullying, harassment, discrimination, violence or
regulatory concerns.

Organisational conditions are not secondary. Psychological
safety may promote candour and
learning [4]
. The
relationship between psychological safety and objective
patient
-
safety outcomes is complex and not always linear.
[4]

More reporting may mean more transparency, or more risk.
Therefore, leaders should be wary of seeing low incident
-
report counts as success with their toolkit.

Expected Practice Significance

“
Brand Called You” is expected to make professional identity
behaviourally visible through the way nurses communicate,
verify, document, delegate, collaborate, reflect and uphold
professional standards. "This interpretation is aligned with the
professional
standards which focus on effective practice, the
maintenance of safety, professionalism and trust.

The brand
should serve as a memorable metaphor that reflects collective
nursing values, rather than focusing on self
-
marketing,
popularity, appearance, or individual reputation. A defensible
academic definition would be:

A nurse’s “brand called you" is
the consistent familiarity of acting with professional
knowledge, ethical values, relational competence, reliable
practice, self
-
awareness, and accountability from situation to
situation.

Constraints

This paper critically improves existing educational material.
It does not report on implementation, participant
characteristics, acceptability, fidelity, behavioural change,
patient outcomes, or organisational effects. The pathways
proposed are plausible i
n principle but have not been tested.
The three modules share commonalities in communications,
accountability and reflection, and isolating their independent
contribution may be challenging. Some source claims were
stronger than the evidence available and
have been omitted or
qualified.
