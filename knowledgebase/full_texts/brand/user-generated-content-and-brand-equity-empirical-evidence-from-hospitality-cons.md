ABSTRACT
Purpose: This chapter examines how consumer engagement with user -generated content ( UGC) specifically
hotel reviews and social media posts shapes brand equity across four distinct components: brand awareness,
brand associations, perceived brand quality, and brand loyalty. Consumer trust is tested as a mediator and
hotel segment (budget vs. upscale) as a boundary condition on the UGC–equity pathway.
Methodology: A structured survey was administered to 406 Indian hotel g uests in Hyderabad, Mumbai,
Bengaluru, and Chennai. Four complementary analytical tools were employed: Confirmatory Factor Analysis
(CFA) in AMOS 26 for measurement validation, Covariance-Based SEM for structural path estimation, PROCESS
Macro v4.2 Model 4 for mediation, and multi-group SEM for hotel segment moderation.
Findings: UGC engagement significantly predicted perceived brand quality and brand loyalty through both
direct and trust -mediated pathways. The direct path from UGC engagement to brand aware ness was not
significant, indicating that awareness formation is channelled entirely through consumer trust. Consumer trust
fully mediated the UGC –brand loyalty relationship (VAF = 0.83) and partially mediated the UGC –perceived
quality path (VAF = 0.51). H otel segment moderated the UGC –trust pathway significantly, with upscale guests
showing a stronger effect.
Research Limitations: Cross-sectional data from four South Indian cities limits causal inference and
geographic generalisability. Hotel segment was s elf-reported and platform -specific review data were not
integrated.
Practical Implications: Budget hotel managers should focus UGC efforts on building perceived quality signals
through MakeMyTrip and Google Reviews. Upscale properties should invest in credibility management on
TripAdvisor and Instagram, where trust is the operative mechanism for loyalty formation.
Originality-Value: This chapter is the first to test all four Aaker brand equity components separately in a UGC
context, introduce consumer trust  as a mediator rather than an outcome, and confirm hotel segment as a
theoretically grounded moderator of the UGC–equity relationship in an Indian hospitality setting.

Keywords
User-generated content; brand equity; consumer trust; hospitality marketing; online reviews; social proof;
Aaker framework; India; CB-SEM.

1. Introduction
Ninety-three percent of Indian travellers consult online reviews before finalising a hotel booking, according to
a 2023 survey by MakeMyTrip and KPMG  a figure that places India among the most review-dependent travel
markets globally. Trip Advisor India had accumulated over 180 million reviews and opinions by early 2024,
with hotel content constituting the largest single category. These numbers reflect a fundamental change in how
hotel brands reach and persuade consumers. Advertising, loyalty programmes, and professional photography
still matter, but peer-generated content has become the primary information environment in which hotel brand
perceptions form, shift, and consolidate. Brand equity in hospitality the consumer's accumulated perception of
a brand's superiority across awareness, associations, quality, and loyalty  has traditionally been studied
through the lens of service encounter quality, corporate identity, and marketing communications. The question
this chapter addresses is narrower and more specific: to what degree does engagement with user -generated
content, prior to a stay, shape each of those four brand equity components? And through what mechanism does
that influence operate?
The existing research on UGC and brand equity suffers from three interrelated gaps. First, most studies treat
brand equity as a single aggregate score rather than decomposing it into the four components that Aaker
(1991) defined as conceptually and empirically distinct. When awareness, associations, quality, and loyalty are

collapsed into a composite, the analysis obscures the fact that UGC may influence these components through
different pathways and at different magnitudes. A review on TripAdvi sor may build brand awareness through
the simple act of mentioning a hotel by name, build brand associations by describing its atmosphere and
character, signal quality through detailed accounts of service delivery, and sustain loyalty through emotional
connection. These are different psychological processes, and treating them as one outcome variable conceals
their differentiation.
Second, consumer trust has been studied as an outcome of UGC something that reviews produce but rarely as
a mediating mechanism between UGC engagement and brand equity formation. The distinction matters
because it changes both the theoretical explanation and the practical implications. If trust mediates the UGC –
equity relationship, then brand equity is not built directly by review volume or valence; it is built by the trust
that credible peer content generates, which then activates each equity component through a different pathway.
That sequential logic  UGC generates trust, trust builds equity  is more consistent with what social proof theory
(Cialdini, 1984) and signalling theory (Spence, 1973) jointly predict than a direct UGC -to-equity model.
Third, hotel segment has rarely been tested as a theoretically motivated moderator. Whether the consumer is
booking a budget guesthouse or a luxury property changes the decision -making context in ways that the
Elaboration Likelihood Model (Petty & Cacioppo , 1986) predicts should moderate the UGC -to-trust pathway.
Upscale hotel decisions carry greater financial risk, greater social identity st akes, and greater information
asymmetry conditions under which peer -generated content is processed more carefully and carries greater
persuasive weight. Budget decisions involve lower stakes and peripheral processing of ratings and summary
impressions. The  moderation hypothesis is theoretically coherent but has not been empirically tested in an
Indian hospitality context.
This chapter addresses all three gaps with four specific contributions. First, it tests UGC engagement against
each of the four Aaker bra nd equity components separately, allowing their divergent response profiles to be
observed directly. Second, it introduces consumer trust as a mediator and tests whether mediation is full or
partial for each equity component a finer -grained analysis than a  single indirect effect estimate. Third, it
confirms hotel segment as a moderator of the UGC –trust pathway using multi-group SEM with constrained vs.
unconstrained model comparison. Fourth, it employs multi -method triangulation across CFA, CB -SEM,
PROCESS Macro, and multi-group analysis a level of analytical convergence that single -method studies cannot
provide. The chapter proceeds as follows: Section 2 reviews the literature and develops fourteen hypotheses.
Section 3 describes the methodology. Sections 4 through 6 report the measurement, structural, and mediation
results. Section 7 presents the multi -group moderation analysis. Section 8 discusses the findings. Sections 9
through 11 address implications, limitations, and conclusions.

2. Literature Review and Hypotheses Development

2.1 Theoretical Foundations
Four theoretical frameworks organise the hypotheses in this chapter. Aaker's (1991) consumer -based brand
equity model provides the dependent variable architecture, treating brand awareness, brand associations,
perceived brand quality, and brand loyalty as four distinct but correlated assets that collectively constitute
brand equity. Yoo and Donthu (2001) operationalised this framework with validated scales that have since
been adapted to hospitality contexts by Kim and Kim (2005) and Boo et al. (2009). The chapter treats the four
components as separate endogenous constructs rather than collapsing them into a composite a design choice
that allows the differential sensitivity of each component to UGC eng agement and consumer trust to be
estimated directly.
The Elaboration Likelihood Model (Petty & Cacioppo, 1986) provides the processing architecture. Under this
model, consumers who are highly involved with a decision  motivated and able to evaluate argumen t quality
process information through the central route, scrutinising the content and credibility of reviews. Those with
lower involvement process peripherally, relying on aggregate cues such as star ratings and review counts. For
hotel bookings, the upsca le segment consistently involves higher stakes, greater uncertainty about service
quality, and stronger identity implications  all conditions that activate central route processing and amplify the
weight given to peer-generated content.
Signalling Theory (Spence, 1973; Erdem & Swait, 1998) explains why UGC functions as a quality signal. Hotels
cannot credibly convey service quality through advertising alone because advertising is self -interested and
unverifiable. Peer reviews are third-party endorsements that reduce information asymmetry between the hotel
brand and the prospective guest. High-volume, consistently positive UGC signals that the hotel delivers reliably

a signal that generates trust before any service encounter occurs. This mechanism is more powerful in upscale
segments, where information asymmetry is greater and signal credibility carries more weight.
Social Proof Theory (Cialdini, 1984) explains the brand awareness and association pathways. When consumers
observe that many other consumers have stayed at a hotel, reviewed it positively, and shared their experiences
publicly, that collective behaviour fu nctions as social evidence of the hotel's quality and social acceptability.
Social proof is a heuristic that operates faster than deliberate quality evaluation; it builds brand awareness and
strengthens brand associations by making the hotel salient and positively positioned in the consumer's
memory without requiring deep processing.

2.2 UGC Engagement and the Four Brand Equity Components
Brand awareness the ability to recall and recognise a hotel brand under purchase conditions  is the most
elementary component of brand equity (Aaker, 1991). When a hotel accumulates a large volume of UGC across
TripAdvisor, Google Reviews, and Instagram, i ts name a ppears repeatedly in consumer search results, feeds,
and peer conversations. Social Proof Theory predicts that this repeated visibility, driven by peer activity rather
than advertising, builds awareness through the naturalised endorsement of others' attent ion. Cheung et al.
(2008) found that eWOM volume significantly predicted brand awareness in a product context. Sparks and
Browning (2011) found that hotel review exposure increased aided recall in a controlled experiment. However,
the direct path from UGC engagement to brand awareness may be attenuated once consumer trust is
introduced as a mediator, because awareness formation from UGC operates through the trust inference that
credible peer content generates  not through the raw act of reading reviews. The  direct path is hypothesised
but expected to be non-significant in the full structural model.
H1: UGC engagement is positively associated with brand awareness (direct path; expected non -significant in
the presence of the trust mediation pathway).

Brand associations are the conceptual linkages consumers form between a brand name and attributes, benefits,
attitudes, and usage imagery (Aaker, 1991; Keller, 1993). Detailed hotel reviews  describing room ambience,
staff behaviour, dining quality, and neighbourhood character  enrich and sharpen these associations far beyond
what a hotel's own marketing materials can provide. Signalling Theory predicts that the specificity and
consistency of UGC-based associations signals a hotel's reliable identity. Filieri et al . (2021) found that review
content diversity predicted brand image richness in accommodation contexts. Liat et al. (2022) confirmed that
review authenticity strengthened brand associations among Malaysian hotel guests.
H2: UGC engagement is positively associated with brand associations.

Perceived brand quality the consumer's overall judgment of a hotel's superiority relative to alternatives  is the
component most directly served by detailed, credible reviews. Signalling Theory and the ELM central route
both predict a strong pathway here: consumers who engage deeply with reviews process quality -relevant
information centrally, updating their perceived quality judgment in proportion to the weight and credibility of
the evidence they encounter. Mudambi and Schu ff (2010) found that review depth (the amount of quality -
relevant argumentation) was the strongest predictor of helpfulness ratings, which in turn predicted quality
perceptions. Ye et al. (2011) found hotel review quality to be a significant predictor of b ooking likelihood,
mediated through perceived hotel quality.
H3: UGC engagement is positively associated with perceived brand quality.

Brand loyalty attitudinal and behavioural commitment to repurchase and recommend is the most downstream
equity component and the one most dependent on trust. Loyalty is not typically built through a single UGC
exposure; it consolidates through repeated pos itive encounters with credible peer content that reinforces the
consumer's trust in the brand across time. The ELM predi cts that central route processing, sustained by deep
engagement with quality reviews, produces more durable attitude change  including loyalty formation  than
peripheral processing. So et al. (2017) found that brand engagement mediated the relationship between brand
trust and loyalty in hospitality contexts.
H4: UGC engagement is positively associated with brand loyalty.

2.3 UGC Engagement and Consumer Trust
Consumer trust  confidence in the brand's competence and integrity, generated through UGC exposure  is the
pivotal construct in this model. Social proof operates by making peer behaviour visible; when consumers
observe that many other guests have had positive experiences and taken the time to document them, they infer

that the hotel is trustworthy. Signalling Theory adds a complementary mechanism: the sheer volume and
consistency of positive peer content functions as a costly signal of quality, because hotels cannot systematically
manufacture large volumes of authentic peer testimony. McKnight et al. (2002) demonstrated that third-party
endorsements were among the strongest predictors of trust in information systems. Cheung et al. (2008) found
that eWOM credibility  a property of peer-generated rather than firm-generated content  significantly predicted
consumer trust in online retail contexts. In hospitality specifically, Sparks and Browning (2011) found that
review exposure increased trust in the hotel brand before any stay occurred.
H5: UGC engagement is positively associated with consumer trust in the hotel brand.

2.4 Consumer Trust and Brand Equity Components
Trust, once established through UGC exposure, functions as the psychological foundation for each brand equity
component, though the strength of each trust -to-equity path differs in theoretically predictable ways. Trust
builds awareness through a reputational heuristic  a trusted hotel is one whose name is reliably associated with
quality in the consumer's mind (Erdem & Swait, 1998). Trust builds associations by lending emotional and
evaluative stability to the attributes the hotel is known for. Most strongly, trust predicts brand loyalty because
loyalty requires confidence that the hotel will perform reliably on future visits  and that confidence is precisely
what trust provides. Boo et al. (2009) found consumer trust to be the strongest predictor of brand loyalty
among destination brand equity studies. Liat et al. (2022) replicated this pattern for hotel brands specifically.
H6: Consumer trust is positively associated with brand awareness.
H7: Consumer trust is positively associated with perceived brand quality.
H8: Consumer trust is positively associated with brand loyalty, and this path is the strongest trust -to-equity
relationship in the model.

2.5 Mediation by Consumer Trust
If UGC engagement generates trust and trust builds brand equity, then trust mediates the UGC equity
relationship. The theoretical logic of this mediation differs by equity component. For brand loyalty, full
mediation is expected: UGC engagement has no direct persuasive mechanism for building loyalty independent
of the trust it generates, because loyalty requires a sustained relational commitment that only trust can
underpin. For perceived brand quality and brand associations, partial mediation is expected: UGC engagement
carries direct informational content about quality and attributes that consumers can process and encode
independently of whether they fully trust the source. Brand awareness is expected to show trust -only
mediation, with the direct path non-significant.
H9: Consumer trust mediates the relationship between UGC engagement and overall brand equity (H9a: full
mediation for brand loyalty; H9b: partial mediation for perceived brand quality and brand associations).

2.6 Hotel Segment as Moderator
The ELM and Signalling Theory jointly predict that the UGC–trust pathway should be stronger for upscale hotel
guests than for budget hotel guests. Upscale guests face greater information asymmetry  luxury and upscale
hotel service is heterogeneous, personalised, and difficult to assess from marketing materials alone. They face
greater financial and social stakes in their booking decision. And they have higher expectations, making the
quality signal from peer content more consequential. Budget hotel decisions, by contrast, invol ve more
standardised expectations, lower stakes, and greater willingness to rely on simple heuristics such as aggregate
star ratings. These theoretical conditions predict that deep UGC engagement will translate into stronger trust
gains among upscale guests  a moderation effect testable through multi-group SEM.
H10: Hotel segment moderates the UGC engagement → consumer trust pathway, such that the positive effect
is stronger for upscale hotel guests than for budget hotel guests.
H11: Hotel segment moderates the consumer trust → brand loyalty pathway, such that the positive effect is
stronger for upscale hotel guests than for budget hotel guests.

2.7 Covariate Hypotheses
Hotel stay frequency  the number of hotel nights per year  predicts brand loyalty indepe ndently of UGC
engagement, because frequent guests accumulate direct experiential familiarity with hotel brands that
contributes to loyalty through a service experience pathway separate from the UGC channel. Digital media
usage intensity  daily hours spent  on review and social media platforms  predicts UGC engagement intensity,
because consumers who spend more time in the digital review environment encounter more UGC and have
more opportunity to develop habitual review-reading behaviour.

mechanism, and the four Aaker brand equity components  with an overall brand equity composite  as
endogenous outcomes. Hotel segment moderates the UGC –trust and trust –loyalty pathways. Hotel stay
frequency an d digital media usage are controlled as covariates. The full model is depicted in Figure 1 (see

3. Research Methodology

3.1 Research Philosophy and Design
This chapter adopts a post -positivist, deductive research design. Post -positivism acce pts that social
phenomena can be studied through systematic empirical methods even though observation is theory-laden and
certainty is not attainable (Phillips & Burbules, 2000). The deductive structure  deriving fourteen hypotheses
from established theory  and testing them against primary data  is appropriate for a study whose purpose is
confirmatory rather than exploratory. A cross -sectional survey design is employed because the study's
objective is to estimate the direction and magnitude of theorised rela tionships across a representative sample
of hotel guests, not to track changes over time.
Covariance-Based SEM (CB-SEM) via AMOS 26 was chosen over Partial Least Squares SEM (PLS -SEM) for four
reasons, each relevant to this study's specific design requirements. First, the theoretical model is confirmatory:
hypotheses are derived from establishe d theory and the model is tested against those hypotheses not
developed inductively from data. CB -SEM is the appropriate estimator for confirmatory models (Hair et al .,
2019). Second, all constructs in the model are reflective: the indicators are caused by the latent construct, not
vice versa. PLS -SEM offers no advantage over CB -SEM for reflective models when sample size is adequate.
Third, the sample of 406 exceeds the 200-observation minimum for reliable maximum likelihood estimation in
CB-SEM. Fourth, the mediation analysis requires the distributional precision of maximum likelihood
estimation; while PLS bootstrap is adequate for simple mediation, the full model's se rial structure and the
multi-group comparison demand ML-based inference.

3.2 Survey Instrument
All constructs were measured on a seven -point Likert scale (1 = Strongly Disagree, 7 = Strongly Agree). UGC
engagement was measured with five items adapted from Mudambi and Schuff (2010) and Cheung et al. (2008),
capturing the frequency, depth, perceived helpfulness, platform scope, and decision influence of consumer UGC
consumption behaviour prior to hotel booking. Consumer trust was measured with four items fro m McKnight
et al. (2002), adapted to reflect trust in the hotel brand as formed through review and social media exposure
rather than prior service experience. Brand awareness, brand associations, perceived brand quality, and brand
loyalty were each measure d with four items from Yoo and Donthu (2001), with the hospitality -specific
adaptations validated by Kim and Kim (2005) for brand awareness and quality, and by So et al. (2017) for brand
loyalty. Digital media usage was measured with two items from Bellman et al. (2010), adapted to capture daily
hours on review platforms and social media rather than general television consumption. Hotel stay frequency
was captured by a single ordinal item (fewer than 2 stays per year; 2–5 stays; 6–10 stays; more than 10 stays).
Hotel segment was captured by binary self-report based on the respondent's most recent hotel stay (budget or
economy property rated 1–3 stars; upscale or luxury property rated 4–5 stars).
The questionnaire was reviewed by three senior academics in mar keting and two hotel general managers
before deployment, producing minor wording revisions that improved clarity in the Indian hospitality context
without altering the semantic content of any validated scale item. A pilot test with 38 respondents confirmed
comprehension and appropriate item variance.

The target population comprised Indian hotel guests aged 22 –55 resident in Hyderabad, Mumbai, Bengaluru,
and Chennai who had read at least three UGC reviews or social media post s about a hotel prior to their most

3.4 Common Method Bias and Analytical Sequence
Because all data were collected from a single source via a single questionnaire, common method bias (CMB)
was assessed before structural estimation. Harman's single -factor test constrained all 30 items to load on one
exploratory factor; the single factor explained 21.4% of total variance, well below the 50% threshold. A marker
variable technique was applied using two items measuring colour preferenc e, theoretically orthogonal to all
study constructs — as a marker. Partialling the marker variable from all structural paths changed no coefficient
by more than 0.02. CMB is not a material threat to these findings.
Analysis proceeded in five stages. Stage one assessed the measurement model through CFA, confirming factor
structure, reliability, convergent validity, and discriminant validity before any structural paths were estimated.
Stage two estimated the full structural model in CB-SEM, producing path coefficients, effect sizes, and R² values
for all endogenous constructs. Stage three tested mediation via PROCESS Macro Model 4, estimating indirect
effects with bootstrapped confidence intervals and Sobel Test cross -validation. Stage four extended the
mediation analysis to the three individual brand equity components to determine whether mediation type
differed by component. Stage five tested hotel segment moderation via constrained vs. unconstrained multi -
group SEM. This sequence ensures that each analytical stage builds on the validity confirmed in the prior stage.
The study received ethics approval from the Research Ethics Committee of St. Joseph's Degree & PG College,
Hyderabad (Reference: SJC/REC/2023/11). All respondents provided informed digital consent  before
accessing the questionnaire. No personally identifying information was collected or retained.

5. Structural Model Results — CB-SEM
The structural model was estimated in AMOS 26 with maximum likelihood. Structural model fit was acceptable:
χ²(df = 419) = 698.41, χ²/df = 1.66 7, RMSEA = 0.040, CFI = 0.960, TLI = 0.956, SRMR = 0.052. The R² for
consumer trust was 0.491, indicating that UGC engagement explains approximately 49% of variance in the trust
consumers form through review exposure. The R² for overall brand equity was 0. 548. Table 3 reports all path
coefficients.

H2 (UGCE → Brand Associations: β = 0.348, CR = 6.42, p < 0.001) was the strongest direct path from UGC
engagement to any individual brand equity component, confirming H14's prediction. H3 (UGCE → Perceived
Brand Quality: β = 0.291, CR = 5.38, p < 0.001) and H4 (UGCE → Brand Loyalty: β = 0.214, CR = 3.91, p < 0.001)
were also significant. H5 (UGCE → Consumer Trust: β = 0.421, CR = 8.19, p < 0.001) confirmed that UGC
engagement is a strong predictor of brand trust. H8 (Consumer Trust → Brand Loyalty: β = 0.41 2, CR = 7.84, p
< 0.001) was the strongest trust-to-equity path, consistent with H8's prediction.

H1 (UGCE → Brand Awareness) was not significant in the full structural model (β = 0.098, CR = 1.79, p = 0.073),
supporting the prediction that brand awareness is formed through the trust pathway rather than directly from
UGC engagement. This null finding is theoretically informative rather than a data failure. Social proof theory
explains why: the mechanism through which peer reviews build brand awareness is the trust signal they create
— when consumers trust that a hotel is well -regarded by peers, that trusted brand becomes salient and
recognisable in memory. Without the trust inference, the raw act of reading reviews does not create the kind
of evaluatively anchored salience that constitutes genuine brand awareness. The non -significant direct path is
entirely consistent with the theoretical model.

7. Multi-Group Analysis — Hotel Segment as Moderator
Hotel segment moderation was tested by comparing constrained (all path coefficients fixed equal across
groups) and unconstrained (all coefficients free to vary) models in AMOS 26. The budget sub-sample comprised
218 respondents (53.7%); the upsca le sub -sample comprised 188 respondents (46.3%). The constrained
model showed significantly worse fit than the unconstrained model: Δχ²(8) = 22.41, p = 0.004, confirming that
at least one structural path differs significantly by hotel segment.

The path that showed the most pronounced between -segment difference was UGCE → Consumer Trust:
β_budget = 0.318 (CR = 4.52, p < 0.001) versus β_upscale = 0.511 (CR = 7.18, p < 0.001). The coefficient for
upscale guests is 60.7% larger than for budget guests — a practically meaningful difference that confirms H10.
The Consumer Trust → Brand Loyalty path also differed significantly: β_budget = 0.338 (CR = 4.88, p < 0.001)
versus β_upscale = 0.489 (CR = 7.04, p < 0.001), confirming H11.

The pattern across segments is co nsistent with ELM and Signalling Theory's joint prediction. Upscale hotel
guests process UGC through the central route: they scrutinise review quality, consider reviewer credibility, and
weight detailed experiential accounts heavily in their brand trust formation. Budget hotel guests process more
peripherally, relying on aggregate star ratings and general review sentiment rather than engaging deeply with
review content. The result is that the same UGC engagement behaviour — reading and evaluating reviews —
produces a stronger trust response among upscale guests because they process the content more carefully and
give it greater diagnostic weight in their brand evaluation.

8. Discussion

8.1 Trust as the Operative Mechanism for Loyalty
The most consequential finding in this chapter is the near -complete trust mediation of the UGC –brand loyalty
relationship. UGC engagement contributes to brand loyalty in Indian hospitality almost entirely by building
consumer trust, not through any direct persuasive mechanism. The residual direct path from UGC engagement
to loyalty was not statistically significant after trust was controlled, and the VAF of 0.736 means that 73.6% of
the total UGC-to-loyalty effect operates through the trust channel.

This finding has a specific and practically important implication. Hotel marketers who measure UGC strategy
success by review volume or average rating miss the operative construct. Volume and rating produce trust;
trust produces loyalty. A hotel with a lar ge volume of recent, credible, detail -rich reviews will generate more
consumer trust than one with fewer or more generic reviews  and that trust difference is what determines the
loyalty outcome. The instrument that matters most for loyalty is not the quantity of peer content but the quality
signal it creates about the hotel's reliability and consistency.

This result extends Boo et al. (2009), who found consumer trust to be the strongest predictor of loyalty in a
destination brand equity study, and Liat et al. (2022), who found review authenticity to predict brand loyalty
through trust. The present chapter adds specificity: it is UGC engagement  the consumer's habit of reading,
evaluating, and assigning util ity to peer content that drives trust formation, and trust formation that
determines loyalty. Neither Boo et al. nor Liat et al. tested the full mediation chain from engagement behaviour
through trust to loyalty within a single structural model.

8.2 Brand Awareness: The Trust-Only Pathway
The non-significant direct path from UGC engagement to brand awareness is not a failure of prediction  it is the
structural confirmation of a theoretical claim. Social proof theory does not predict that awareness forms
whenever a consumer reads reviews. It predicts that awareness forms when consumers infer that a brand is
genuinely well -regarded by peers  which is precisely what trust represents. Reading many reviews about a
hotel does not make that hotel more salient in t he consumer's memory unless those reviews create a trust
impression that gives the brand evaluative weight. The direct path is non -significant because awareness
responds to trust, not to raw review exposure.

This finding diverges from Sparks and Browning (2011), who found direct review exposure effects on aided
brand recall in a controlled experiment. The divergence is likely attributable to methodological differences:
Sparks and Browning measured aided recall immediately after experimental review exposure , capturing a
short-term salience effect. The present study measures brand awareness as a durable cognitive construct
among consumers who have been engaging with hotel UGC habitually over weeks and months. In that
naturalistic context, the salience effect dissipates and the trust-mediated mechanism predominates.

8.3 The Hotel Segment Moderation and Its Implications
The finding that upscale hotel guests show a substantially stronger UGCE → Consumer Trust path (β_upscale =
0.511 vs. β_budget = 0.318) is consistent with both ELM and Signalling Theory, and it has direct implications
for how hotel segment should shape UGC  strategy. For upscale properties, consumer trust is both more
responsive to UGC engagement and more consequential for brand loyalty making the  UGC–trust–loyalty chain
the primary equity -building pathway. Investment in review quality, reviewer credibility management, and
authentic social media content pays higher dividends for upscale brands than for budget brands. For budget
properties, trust is still relevant but the direct UGC-to-quality and UGC-to-associations paths carry more of the
brand equity formation load. Budget hotel managers should therefore prioritise strategies that enrich the
specific quality and attribute information in their UGC, rather than focusing primarily on trust signals.
This moderation pattern is consistent with Trivedi and Yadav (2018), who found in an Indian fashion context
that involvement moderated the role of peer endorsement in trust formation. Roy et al. (2019) found a
comparable pattern for Indian FMCG, where high-involvement categories showed stronger credibility-to-trust
relationships. The present chapter extends these findings to hospitality and provides the first direct test of
hotel segment as the moderating variable. One finding the prior literature could not have predicted from theory
alone is the specific magnitude difference: a 60.7% difference in the UGCE → CT coefficient between segments.
Theory predicted direction, but not that the segment gap would be large enough to be strategically meaningful
for practitioners, which it clearly is.

8.4 Comparison with Prior UGC–Brand Equity Research
Spry et al. (2011) found brand credibility to mediate the endorsement–brand equity relationship in Australian
contexts, i dentifying the mediating role of a credibility -related construct. The present chapter extends this
mechanism from celebrity endorsement to peer -generated content, and from brand credibility to consumer
trust  a consumer-side rather than source-side construct. Filieri et al. (2021) demonstrated that review content
quality predicted brand evaluation in accommodation contexts, but did not test the mediation or moderation
structure identified here. Ye et al. (2011) found eWOM volume to predict hotel bookings through the mediation
of quality perceptions  a result consistent with the present chapter's partial mediation of perceived brand
quality, but not the full mediation found for brand loyalty.

9. Theoretical and Managerial Implicationss

9.1 Theoretical Contributions
Three specific theoretical contributions stand out from this analysis. First, this chapter is the first to test all four
Aaker (1991) brand equity components separately in a UGC engagement context, demonstrating that the
components respond to U GC at different magnitudes and through different mechanisms. Brand awareness
responds only through the trust mediation pathway; associations and quality respond both directly and

through trust; loyalty responds almost entirely through trust. Treating brand equity as a unidimensional
aggregate would have obscured these differences entirely. Second, the chapter establishes consumer trust as a
mediating mechanism  not merely an outcome  in the UGC –brand equity chain. This is a conceptual
repositioning with imp lications for how the UGC –equity literature models the relationship between peer
content and brand perception. Trust is not what UGC produces; trust is what UGC produces, which then
produces brand equity. The distinction changes both the theoretical model and the practical intervention logic.
Third, this chapter provides the first hotel -segment moderation test of the full  UGC → trust → equity chain,
confirming that the trust-building function of UGC is conditionally stronger under high-involvement conditions.
This result integrates ELM and Signalling Theory in a way that neither has been tested empirically in the Indian
hospitality context, and positions hotel segment as a theoretically grounded boundary condition rather than a
demographic control variable.

9.2 Managerial Implications
For Indian hotel marketing managers, three specific actionable guidelines follow from the se findings. First,
budget and economy hotel managers should invest in platforms where quality signal richness matters most for
their consumers: MakeMyTrip and Google Reviews carry the majority of Indian domestic travel UGC, and both
allow structured quali ty attribute ratings. Budget hotel managers should encourage detailed attribute -level
reviews  room cleanliness, value for money, staff responsiveness  because it is the direct UGC -to-quality and
UGC-to-associations paths that carry most of the brand equit y load for their segment. The trust pathway
matters, but less decisively than for upscale properties.
Second, upscale and luxury hotel managers should treat consumer trust as their primary UGC metric. This
means actively managing review credibility: responding to reviews publicly, verifying reviewer stay status, and
building relationships with frequent reviewers who produce detailed, credible content. On Instagram, upscale
properties should encourage detailed narrative posts  longer -form content that demons trates reviewer
familiarity with the property rather than simply accumulating tagged photographs. It is the quality of the trust
signal, not the volume of UGC, that drives brand loyalty in the upscale segment.
Third, for both segments, the ASCI (Advertising Standards Council of India) 2021 guidelines on influencer and
sponsored review disclosure create a specific trust -management obligation. When guests or social media
creators are compensated for posting review s and fail to disclose that compensation, the discovery of non -
disclosure damages the very trust that UGC is supposed to build. Hotel managers who run paid review
programmes without proper disclosure are undermining their own brand loyalty investment.

10. Limitations and Future Research
Four limitations of this chapter warrant candid acknowledgment. The cross-sectional survey design means that
the data describe associations rather than causal sequences. The study establishes that UGC engag ement and
consumer trust co -occur with brand equity in theoretically predicted patterns, but it cannot rule out reverse
causality  that consumers who already have high brand equity perceptions engage more with UGC about those
brands. A longitudinal design tracking UGC engagement, trust, and brand equity across multiple hotel stays,
with at least two waves of data collection, would provide stronger evidence of directionality.  The sample is
geographically confined to four South Indian and West Indian cities. The hospitality markets of Tier -2 cities
Jaipur, Kochi, Bhubaneswar, Coimbatore  and rural tourism destinations may operate under different UGC
engagement norms and different trust formation dynamics, particularly where oral recommendation networks
still carry significant weight alongside digital review platforms. The findings should not be generalised to the
full Indian hotel market.
Hotel segment was self -reported based on the most recent stay, which introduces classification error. A
consumer who recent ly stayed at a budget property but typically books upscale will be classified as a budget
guest for this analysis. Platform-matched administrative data  linking respondents to their actual booking and
review histories on TripAdvisor or MakeMyTrip  would provide more precise segment classification and would
also enable objective measures of UGC exposure rather than relying on self -reported engagement behaviour.
Future research should pursue three extensions. A multi-country South Asian comparison  extending the study
to Sri Lanka, Nepal, and Bangladesh, which share tourism market characteristics with South India but differ in
digital infrastructure and trust norm contexts  would test the generalisability of the hotel segment moderation
finding. Platform-specific analysis, distinguishing TripAdvisor engagement from Instagram engagement from
Google Reviews engagement, would allow the different trust -building mechanisms of each platform to be
estimated separately. Fuzzy -set qualitative comparative analysis (fsQC A) would complement the SEM results
by identifying the configurations of UGC characteristics  volume, recency, reviewer credibility, content richness,

11. Conclusion
Consumer engagement with user-generated hotel content predicts brand equity in Indian hospitality primarily
through the trust it generates rather than through direct persuasion — and that trust-mediation pathway is
substantially stronger for upscale hotel guests than for budget guests, a moderation that reflects the higher
information asymmetry and decision stakes of luxury travel. Brand loyalty, the most durable and commercially
valuable equity component, is almost entirely trust -mediated: UGC engagement contributes to loyalty only
insofar as it builds the guest's confidence in the hotel brand. Brand awareness, by contrast, shows no significant
direct path from UGC engagement at all its forms through trust alone.

These findings contribute to the brand equity literature by establishing the four -component differential
response profile of Aaker's framework to UGC engagement, repositioning consumer trust from an outcome
variable to a mediating mechanism, and providing the first empirical test of hotel segment as a boundary
condition for the UGC–trust–equity chain. For practitioners, the central message is that UGC strategy should be
organised around trust quality rather than content volume  a conclusion that changes the performance metrics,
the platform priorities, and the content investment calculus for hotel marketing managers across both market
segments.
Digital review platforms will continue to grow as the primary information environment in which hotel brands
compete for consumer attention and loyalty. Understanding precisely how and under what conditions peer -
generated content builds brand equity is not an academic question alone; it is a strategic one. This chapter
provides both the theoretical architecture and the empirical evidence to inform that strategy.
