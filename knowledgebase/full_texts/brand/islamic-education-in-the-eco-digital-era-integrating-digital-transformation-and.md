Abstract: This study investigates how Islamic educational institutions institutionalize digital
transformation, green branding, and Islamic environmental ethics to cultivate sustainable learning
ecosystems in the eco-digital era. Employing an intrinsic qualitative case study design, data were collected
through classroom observations, document analysis, and in-depth semi-structured interviews with school
leaders, teachers, environmental program coordinators, and students. Data were analyzed using a hybrid
thematic analysis integrating deductive and inductive coding procedures. The findings reveal three
interrelated institutional mechanisms. First, digital transformation was institutionalized through Learning
Management Systems, digital governance, online communication platforms, and digital da‘wah, fostering
ethical digital literacy, institutional accountability, and responsible learning practices. Second, green
branding was institutionalized through waste banks, plastic -free campaigns, urban farming, climate
literacy, and āyāt kauniyyah-based learning, embedding sustainability as ecological da‘wah within everyday
school culture. Third, the Islamic principles of khalīfah, amānah, and tawāzun functioned as normative
mechanisms linking technological innovation with environmental stewardship across gov ernance,
curriculum, and institutional practices. Collectively, these mechanisms demonstrate that digital
transformation, green branding, and Islamic environmental ethics operate as mutually reinforcing
dimensions of sustainability-oriented educational cultures. Building on these findings, the study advances
the Eco-Digital Islamic Education Model as an empirically grounded conceptual framework explaining how
technological innovation, ecological responsibility, and Islamic values interact to foster sustaina ble
learning ecosystems. The model contributes to the emerging discourse on Islamic Education for
Sustainable Development while providing practical guidance for institutional transformation and
educational policy supporting Quality Education and Climate Action.

INTRODUCTION
Climate change, biodiversity loss, environmental degradation, and widening social
inequalities have intensified global concerns regarding the role of education in advancing
sustainable development  (Abera, 2023; Agbedahin, 2019; Avelar dkk., 2019; Syihabuddin
dkk., 2023) . As environmental challenges become increasingly complex and
interconnected, educational institutions are expected not only to transmit knowledge but
also to cultivate ecological responsibility, ethical awareness, and transformative
competencies that enable learners to engage meaningfully with sustainability issues  (Bria
dkk., 2025; Maretta dkk., 2026; Misiaszek, 2016; Shutaleva, 2023) . This expectation is

reflected in Sustainable Development Goal 4.7, which emphasizes the role of education in
promoting sustainable lifestyles, environmental stewardship, global citizenship, and
responsible participation in society (De Poza-Vilches dkk., 2019; Newman & Tonkens, 2025;
UNESCO, 2023).
Consequently, educational transformation is no longer limited to curriculum reform
but increasingly encompasses institutional culture, organizational identity, and learning
ecosystems capable of fostering sustainability -oriented values and behaviors.  Parallel to
the sustainability agenda, digital transformation has become one of the most influential
forces shaping contemporary education (Ahmad Rizal dkk., 2024; Ogunbukola, 2025) . The
proliferation of Learning Management Systems, artificial intelligence assisted learning
environments, digital communication platforms, and other technology -enhanced learning
tools has fundamentally altered how knowledge is produced, accessed, and dis seminated
(Holmes et al, 2022; OECD, 2023). Existing research consistently demonstrates that digital
technologies can expand educational access, personalize learning experiences, and
facilitate collaborative knowledge construction  (Abusharif, 2023; Ngongpah & Oni, 2025;
Rahman & Hossain, 2025).
However, an important tension remains within the literature. While digital
transformation is frequently celebrated for improving efficiency and accessibility,
sustainability scholars argue that technological adoption alone is insufficient to cultivate
long-term ecological commitment, ethical responsibility, or environmentally responsible
behavior (Enbaia dkk., 2024; ’Ulyan, 2023) . In many contexts, technology functions
primarily as an instrument for information delivery rather than a catalyst for sustainability-
oriented cultural transformation. This tension raises critical questions regarding how digital
innovation can move beyond technical modernization and contribute to the formation of
meaningful sustainability practices within educational institutions.  In response to these
challenges, educational institutions have increasingly embraced green branding as a
strategy for communicating environmental commitments and strengthening institutional
sustainability identities (Leal Filho et al., 2019; Lozano et al., 2015).
Green branding refers to the deliberate construction of an environmentally
responsible institutional image through policies, programs, communication strategies, and
visible sustainability practices (Dangelico, 2016; Firdausiah & Fuady, 2026; Kusbah & Khozin,
2025; Srivastava dkk., 2020). Within educational contexts, green branding extends beyond
marketing and encompasses efforts to embed sustainability values into organizational
culture, stakeholder engagement, and institutional behavior  (Arise & Muzuva, 2024;
Lambrechts dkk., 2013; Muralidhar dkk., 2025) . Through websites, social media platforms,
sustainability campaigns, and public narratives, schools increasingly seek to position
themselves as environmentally responsible organizations. Nevertheless, a second tension
emerges within this body of literature . Green branding strengthens sustainability
awareness and reputation, but unsupported claims may encourage organizational
greenwashing practices (Delmas & Burbano, 2011; Lyon & Montgomery, 2015; Netto et al.,
2020).
This concern is particularly relevant in educational settings, where institutional
credibility depends not only on public representation but also on the consistency between
values, practices, and educational outcomes.  For Islamic educational institutions, the
sustainability agenda is not merely a contemporary policy concern but is deeply rooted in
a long -standing theological and ethical tradition. Islamic environmental ethics are
grounded in the principles of khalīfah ( stewardship), amānah ( trusteeship), and mīzān or

tawāzun ( balance), which collectively position human beings as morally responsible
custodians of creation (Abdullah, 2014; Foltz, 2003; Khalid, 2019). These concepts establish
a comprehensive ethical framework linking environmental responsibility with spirituality,
worship, and accountability before God (DeWitt, 2025). Islamic environmental ethics frame
sustainability as religious responsibility, social obligation, and stewardship beyond secular
compliance.
Through this perspective, Islamic education possesses distinctive potential to
cultivate sustainability -oriented learning environments that integrate ecological
awareness, moral responsibility, and spiritual development. Consequently, Islamic
educational i nstitutions offer a particularly valuable context for examining how
sustainability values can be institutionalized through both technological innovation and
faith-based educational practices.  Recent scholarship has generated important insights
into several relevant domains. Studies on digital transformation have highlighted the
capacity of educational technologies to support learning accessibility, engagement, and
personalization (Abusharif, 2023; Fazey et al., 2020; Holmes & Tuomi, 2022) . Research on
sustainability education and green schools has emphasized the importance of
environmental literacy, institutional culture, and sustainability -oriented practices in
shaping ecological awareness (Aldawsari et al., 2025; Leal Filho et al., 2019; Wals & Benavot,
2017).
Meanwhile, studies on Islamic environmental ethics have demonstrated the role of
religious values in fostering environmental responsibility and sustainable behavior (Choli &
Fikri Rachman, 2023; Resky, 2025; Taufikin, 2025). Despite these advances, these strands of
scholarship have largely evolved in parallel rather than in dialogue with one another. As a
result, limited understanding exists regarding how digital transformation, green branding,
and Islamic environmental ethi cs may interact as mutually reinforcing dimensions of
sustainability-oriented educational ecosystems.  This fragmentation constitutes a
significant theoretical and practical gap. Existing studies tend to investigate digital
innovation, sustainability initiatives, green school programs, or Islamic ecological values as
separate domains of inquiry, leaving unr esolved the question of how these dimensions
converge within the everyday practices of Islamic educational institutions.
More importantly, the literature has yet to provide a coherent conceptual
explanation of how technological innovation, institutional environmental identity, and
Islamic pedagogical values interact in shaping sustainability-oriented educational cultures.
Consequently, current scholarship remains insufficient for explaining how Islamic
educational institutions simultaneously respond to the dual imperatives of digital
transformation and sustainable development.  The urgency of addressing this gap is
particularly evident in Indonesia, where Islamic educational institutions constitute one of
the most influential educational and socio -cultural forces in society. Through extensive
networks of pesantren, madrasah, Islamic schools, and higher education institutions, these
organizations play a significant role in shaping educational practices, community values,
and public responses to contemporary challenges (Anshori & Sahara, 2020; Junaedi, 2020).
As digital transformation and sustainability agendas continue to reshape
educational priorities, Indonesia provides a particularly relevant context for examining
how Islamic educational institutions integrate technological innovation, environmental
responsibility, and faith-based values within sustainable learning ecosystems. At the same
time, Indonesian educational institutions are increasingly encouraged to adopt digital
technologies while supporting national commitments to sustainable development and

environmental education. Many Islamic schools have begun incorporating digital learning
platforms, sustainability programs, environmental campaigns, and green institutional
narratives (Anshori & Sahara, 2020; Pandia et al., 2023; UNESCO, 2023). However, empirical
understanding of how these initiatives are integrated, experienced, and institutionalized
within everyday educational practices remains limited.
Questions persist regarding whether digital transformation genuinely supports
sustainability-oriented learning, how green branding is embedded within institutional
culture, and how Islamic environmental values shape the implementation of these
initiatives. Addressing these questions is increasingly important for both educational policy
and institutional development.  To the best of current scholarly knowledge, no previous
study has systematically examined the intersection of digital transformation, green
branding, and Islamic environmental ethics as an integrated framework for cultivating
sustainable learning ecosystem s within Islamic educational institutions. The principal
novelty of this research therefore lies in the development of an Eco -Digital Islamic
Education Model that explains how these three dimensions interact in shaping
sustainability-oriented educational cultures.
By positioning green branding not merely as an institutional image strategy but also
as a form of ecological da‘wah embedded within digitally mediated educational practices,
this study introduces a new conceptual lens through which sustainability, technology, and
Islamic educational values can be understood as interconnected rather than separate
domains. Accordingly, this study investigates how digital transformation is institutionalized
within the educational practices of Islamic educational institutions, how green branding is
embedded within organizational culture and sustainability initiatives, and how Islamic
environmental ethics shape the interaction between technological innovation and
environmental responsibility. Specifically, this study seeks to answer three questions: how
is digital transformation institutionalized within Islamic educational practices; how is green
branding embedded within the organizational culture and sustainability initiatives of
Islamic educational institutions; and how do digital transformation, green branding, and
Islamic environmental ethics interact in cultivating sustainable learning ecosystems.?
Through an intrinsic qualitative case study approach, this study examines the
interconnected relationships among digital transformation, green institutional culture, and
Islamic environmental ethics. It proposes an Eco -Digital Islamic Education Model that
integrates technological innovation, environmental responsibility, and Islamic values to
support sustainable educational transformation. The model contributes to the growing
discourse on Islamic Education for Sustainable Development by offering a contextua lly
grounded framework that connects digital advancement with ecological stewardship.
Furthermore, it extends contemporary discussions on sustainability and digital
transformation in education by emphasizing ethical and culturally relevant approaches.
The study provides a conceptual foundation for educational policy and institutional
transformation aligned with Quality Education, environmental sustainability, and Climate
Action within Islamic educational institutions (Leal Filho et al., 2019; UNESCO, 2017, 2021).

METHOD
This study employed an intrinsic qualitative case study design  Stake (2010); Yin
(2018), to investigate how Islamic educational institutions institutionalize digital
transformation, green branding, and Islamic environmental ethics in cultivating sustainable
learning ecosystems within the eco -digital era. Guided by an interpretivist paradigm, t he

study sought an in-depth understanding of a bounded educational phenomenon within its
natural institutional context, viewing institutional realities as socially constructed through
participants’ experiences and organizational practices. Consistent with naturalistic inquiry
Lincoln and Guba (1985), the researcher served as the primary research instrument.  The
study was conducted in three public and private Islamic junior and senior secondary
schools in the Special Region of Yogyakarta, Indonesia, with institutional identities
anonymized to ensure confidentiality.
The research sites were purposively selected as information -rich cases  Patton
(2015), because they had consistently implemented digital learning, sustainability
initiatives, and Islamic environmental values within educational practice. Thirty -three
participants were recruited, comprising three school principals, three vice principals for
curriculum affairs, eight teachers, sixteen students, and three environmental program
coordinators, representing complementary perspectives across leadership, curriculum,
pedagogy, student experiences, and environmental management (Creswell & Poth, 2018).
Data were collected over one month through in -depth semi -structured interviews,
classroom observations, and document analysis  (Flick, 2018) . Interviews with all
participants lasted approximately three to four hours and explored experiences of digital
transformation, green branding, Islamic environmental ethics, and sustainability -oriented
educational practices.
Three rounds of classroom observations examined instructional practices, digitally
mediated learning, environmental programs, institutional governance, and stakeholder
interactions. Documentary evidence included institutional policy documents,
environmental program reports, digital learning materials, school websites, social media
content, and interview records. The integration of these data sources enabled
methodological triangulation and strengthened the credibility of the findings.  Data were
analyzed usi ng a hybrid thematic analysis integrating deductive and inductive coding
following Braun & Clarke, (2021), six-phase framework. Deductive coding was guided by
the study’s conceptual domains of digital transformation, green branding, and Islamic
environmental ethics, whereas inductive coding identified patterns emerging from the
empirical data. Through iterative  comparison of interviews, classroom observations, and
documents, categories were refined into three overarching themes: digital transformation
as a value -oriented educational ecosystem, green branding as ecological da‘wah and
institutional identity, and the institutionalization of Islamic environmental ethics.
The reciprocal relationships among these themes were synthesized to develop the
Eco-Digital Islamic Education Model, explaining how technological innovation, ecological
responsibility, and Islamic values interact to cultivate sustainable learning ecosystem s.
Methodological rigor followed Lincoln and Guba’s (1985) trustworthiness framework,
encompassing credibility, transferability, dependability, and confirmability.
Trustworthiness was strengthened through triangulation, member checking, rich
contextual des criptions, an audit trail, reflexive memoing, and peer debriefing. Ethical
integrity was maintained through voluntary informed consent, anonymization of
institutional and participant identities, and exclusive use of research data for academic
purposes. The se procedures ensured that the findings were credible, contextually
relevant, transparent, and ethically grounded, while supporting the model’s conceptual
development and potential applicability within Islamic educational settings.

RESULT AND DISCUSSION
Digital Transformation as a Value-Oriented Educational Practice
The thematic analysis revealed that digital transformation within Islamic educational
institutions extends beyond technological modernization and functions as a value-oriented
educational practice embedded within religious and pedagogical commitments. Across the
institutions examined, digital technologies were systematically integrated into learning,
administration, communication, and institutional management. Rather than being
perceived as merely technical tools, digital technologies were understood as edu cational
resources that support discipline, responsibility, accountability, and ethical learning
practices. The findings indicate that Learning Management Systems, digital assessment
platforms, instructional media, and online communication systems have become integral
components of everyday educational activities. These technologies facilitated more
efficient le arning management, strengthened communication between teachers and
students, and enhanced institutional transparency through digital governance
mechanisms.
Importantly, digital systems were not only viewed as instruments for efficiency but
also as manifestations of Islamic values such as amānah ( trustworthiness), i ḥsān
(professional excellence ), and responsibility in educational practice.  Another significant
finding concerns the emergence of digital ethics as an important dimension of Islamic

Institutionalizing Islamic Environmental Ethics
The findings reveal that Islamic environmental ethics function as the normative
foundation underlying both digital transformation and sustainability initiatives. Across the
institutions examined, the principles of khalīfah (stewardship), amānah (trusteeship), and
tawāzun (balance) were consistently embedded within institutional narratives, educational
policies, and sustainability practices.  The principle of khalīfah was reflected in programs
designed to cultivate environmental stewardship and ecological res ponsibility among
students. Environmental protection was framed as a religious obligation linked to
humanity’s role as custodian of creation. Meanwhile, amānah informed both technological
and organizational practices, emphasizing responsible technology use, institutional
accountability, and ethical governance.
Tawāzun served as a guiding principle for balancing technological advancement
with environmental sustainability, ensuring that educational modernization remained
aligned with ecological responsibility. The integration of Islamic values fostered eco -
spirituality, in which technology, sustainability, and religious commitment mutually
reinforced one another. Islamic environmental ethics therefore functioned not merely as
abstract theological concepts but as practical principles embedded within educational
culture and institutional governance. Through curriculum implementation, environmental
programs, digital learning, and everyday activities, these values encouraged students and
educators to develop responsible relationships with nature. Consequently, Islamic
educational institutions demonstrated how technological innovation and spiritual values
can collectively support sustainable learning environments, strengthen ethical awareness,
and promote long-term ecological responsibility within educational communities.

Emergence of the Eco-Digital Islamic Education Model
The most significant finding of this study is the emergence of an Eco -Digital Islamic
Education Model that explains how digital transformation, green branding, and Islamic
environmental ethics interact to cultivate sustainable learning ecosystems. The them atic
analysis indicates that these three dimensions do not operate independently; rather, they
function as interconnected and mutually reinforcing components of institutional
transformation. Within the Eco -Digital Islamic Education Model, digital transform ation
functions as the institutional mechanism through which learning innovation, digital
literacy, institutional efficiency, and digital da‘wah are advanced. Green branding functions
as a vehicle for ecological identity formation, environmental communication, and
sustainability-oriented institutional culture. Islamic environmental ethics provide the moral
and spiritual foundation that guides both technologica l innovation and environmental
responsibility.
Together, these dimensions create an integrated educational ecosystem combining
technology, sustainability, and spirituality. Their interaction generates outcomes including
ethical digital literacy, ecological awareness, spiritual responsibility, institutio nal
sustainability, and sustainability -oriented educational cultures. Technology becomes not
merely a learning instrument but also a medium for promoting ethical, religious, and
environmental values. Consequently, Islamic educational institutions move bey ond
conventional religious schooling by developing innovative approaches that connect faith,
digital competence, and ecological responsibility. The Eco-Digital Islamic Education Model
therefore positions Islamic education as a transformative system capable of responding to
contemporary challenges of digital transformation and sustainable development while
