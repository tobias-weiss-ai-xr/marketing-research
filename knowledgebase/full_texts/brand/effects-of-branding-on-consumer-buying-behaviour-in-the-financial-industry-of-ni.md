Abstract This study examines the effects of branding on consumer buying behaviour within the Nigerian
financial industry, using Heritage Bank PLC as a case study. The research was motivated by
increased competition in the banking sector and the need for firms to di fferentiate themselves
through effective branding. Primary data were collected from 220 customers of Heritage Bank
PLC using a structured questionnaire. Quantitative methods were applied, and data were analysed
using descriptive statistics and Pearson correlation techniques. Findings revealed that three brand
attributes—functional, symbolic, and experiential —significantly influence consumer buying
behaviour, with functional attributes ranking highest. The study also identified key challenges
facing Heritage Bank PLC in deploying brand attributes effectively: loss of brand identity, lack of
creativity, development of products without a strong brand foundation, and limited commitment to
the branding process. Remedial strategies were proposed, including improved brand commitment,
service innovation, and building self -expressive brands that resonate with consumers. The study
concludes that branding remains a crucial determinant of consumer preference and loyalty in the
financial sector. Strengthening brand attrib utes can greatly enhance customer perception, trust,
and retention. Recommendations were made to assist Heritage Bank PLC in adopting a more
strategic, innovative, and customer centric approach to branding. The findings contribute to
ongoing discourse on b ank marketing strategies in emerging markets and provide practical
insights for policymakers, practitioners, and academics.
Keywords Brand Attributes, Branding, Consumer Behaviour, Financial Industry, Heritage Bank, Nigeria.

Introduction
Branding has become an indispensable
strategic tool for organizational sustainability in
today’s competitive business environment  by
Atilgan et al.  [1]. In the financial services
sector, where product offerings are largely
intangible and easily replicable, strong
branding serves as a critical differentiator that
shapes consumer perceptions, trust, and
ultimately, buying behaviour  according to
Romaniuk et al . [2]. Modern consumers are
more sophisticated and demand value, security,
and reliability from financial institutions.
Kapferer et al. [ 3]. As a result, banks must
strategically build and maintain brand identities
that appeal to both the functional and emotional
expectations of their customers by Alimen et al.
[4].
Globally, branding has evolved beyond logos
and slogans; it encompasses the total
experience that a consumer forms regarding a
product or service  according to Holt et al.  [5].
In highly competitive sectors such as banking,
consumers tend to align themselves with brands
that demonstrate credibility, transparency, and
consistency. Rust et al. [6]. Several studies have
emphasized that strong brand attributes can
influence consumer purchasing decisions,
enhance loyalty, and generate long -term
competitive advantages by Husain et al [7].
In Nigeria, the financial sector has witnessed
rapid transformation driven by technology,
regulation, and shifting consumer expectations
according to Agbonifoh et al. [ 8]. Heritage
Bank PLC, despite its historical roots and
rebranding efforts, continues to struggle with
brand perception challenges. Issues such as
reduced consumer trust, poor customer service,
and the inability to differentiate its services
from competitors have contributed to its
branding difficulties  by Solomon et al. [ 9].
Additionally, prior industry crises and the
scarcity of liquidity have heightened consumer
sensitivity to banks’ brand reputations
according to Bian et al. [10].
Existing literature shows that functional,
symbolic, and experiential brand attributes play
important roles in shaping consumer behaviour.
Heding et al. [11]. Functional attributes relate to
tangible benefits such as service efficiency and
product performance; symbolic attributes
involve emotional and psychological
associations; experiential attributes connect
with personal preferences and self -image by
Pina et al. [ 12] Although many studies have
examined branding in general, limited attention
has been paid to how banks in Nigeria utilize
these brand attributes to influence consumer
buying behaviour.
The problem this study addresses is the
insufficient understanding of how branding
affects consumer decisions in Nigerian banks
and why institutions like Heritage Bank PLC
experience branding challenges despite
rebranding efforts. Existing solutions includ e
advertising campaigns, service upgrades, and
customer relationship strategies, yet these
efforts have not yielded the desired consumer
confidence and loyalty for the bank.
Limitations in previous approaches include
weak brand identity, inadequate innovation, and
the absence of a solid brand foundation  by
Agwaral et al. [13].
The achievement of this study lies in its
comprehensive analysis of brand attributes and
their influence on banking consumers in
Nigeria. A clear schematic relationship between
brand attributes and consumer buying
behaviour is presented through a conceptua l
framework linking symbolic, experiential, and
functional attributes to behavioural outcomes.
The main objectives of the study are to:
1. Identify the brand attributes that influence
consumer buying behaviour at Heritage
Bank PLC.
2. Assess the challenges Heritage Bank PLC
faces in using brand attributes effectively.
3. Recommend remedial measures to enhance
branding effectiveness.

The novelty of this work lies in its contextual

Materials and Methods
This section describes the methodological
procedures adopted to investigate the effects of
branding on consumer buying behaviour in the
Nigerian financial industry, focusing on
Heritage Bank PLC. The methods were
designed to answer the study’s three primar y
research questions regarding brand attributes,
associated challenges, and remedial strategies.
Description of the Study Site
The study was conducted in Lagos, Nigeria,
the country’s largest commercial and financial
hub. Lagos hosts a high concentration of
Heritage Bank PLC branches and represents a
diverse customer base in terms of age, socio -
economic class, and banking experience. Lagos
was selected purposively because it accounts
for a significant proportion of banking
transactions in Nigeria and offers a
representative reflection of consumer
perceptions within the financial sector. Its
strategic relevance makes it a suitable l ocation
to assess how branding influences consumer
buying behaviour among banking customers.
Research Design
A descriptive quantitative research design
was employed to systematically capture the
perceptions of Heritage Bank PLC customers
regarding branding and consumer behaviour.
The descriptive design was selected because it
allows for accurate portrayal of resp ondents’
opinions without manipulating variables. This
design is appropriate for studies aimed at
establishing relationships between constructs
such as brand attributes and consumer
behaviour.
Research Approach
A quantitative approach was adopted to
statistically analyse branding constructs and
their influence on consumer buying behaviour.
This approach supports the use of numerical
data to test hypotheses and identify significant
relationships. It also aligns with the study’s goal
of determining the extent to which symbolic,
experiential, and fu nctional brand attributes
influence consumer decisions.
Population of the Study
The population comprised all customers of
Heritage Bank PLC operating within Lagos
State. The bank serves thousands of customers
across branches located in major commercial
districts, including Victoria Island, Ikeja,
Surulere, and Lekki. These customers f ormed
the target population because they have direct
experience with the bank’s services and
branding initiatives.
Sampling Technique
A convenience sampling technique was
employed to select respondents who were
readily accessible at the banking halls during
data collection. This technique was appropriate
given time constraints and the need to obtain
responses from actual customers of the bank.
Sample Size
A sample size of  220 respondents was used
for the study. This size meets Cochran’s (1963)
recommendation of a minimum of 100 cases for
survey research and ensures adequate
representation for statistical analysis. Of the
220 questionnaires administered, 120
completed questionnaires were deemed valid
and used in the final analysis.
Data Collection Instrument
A structured questionnaire was designed as
the primary instrument for data collection. The
questionnaire comprised four sections:
1. Section A: Demographic characteristics of
respondents (gender, age, education, years
of banking experience).

2. Section B : Brand attributes influencing
consumer behaviour (functional, symbolic,
experiential).
3. Section C: Challenges hindering effective
use of brand attributes.
4. Section D : Remedial measures for
addressing branding challenges.
A five -point Likert scale (1  = Strongly
Disagree to 5  = Strongly Agree) was used for
Sections B, C, and D.
Data Collection Procedure
Data collection was conducted over a
two-week period. Respondents were
approached within Heritage Bank PLC
branches primarily between 2:00 pm and 3:00
pm, identified as peak customer service hours.
Questionnaires were distributed manually, and
respondents completed them voluntarily. Initial
low r esponse rates during morning hours
prompted a shift to afternoon sessions, which
significantly improved participation.
Validity and Reliability
Validity
Content validity was ensured through expert
review by academic supervisors. Unclear or
irrelevant items were revised or removed before
field administration.
Reliability
Internal consistency of the questionnaire was
assessed using Cronbach’s Alpha:
1. Brand attributes scale = 0.811
2. Branding challenges scale = 0.742
3. Remedial measures scale = 0.788
All values exceeded the acceptable threshold
of 0.70, indicating high reliability.
Statistical Methods Used
Data were coded and analysed using  SPSS
version 20. Analysis included:
1. Descriptive statistics (frequencies,
percentages, means, standard deviations).
2. Pearson’s correlation analysis to test
hypotheses.
3. Mean ranking for prioritizing brand
attributes, challenges, and remedial
actions.
The analytical techniques supported
objective interpretation of how branding
influences consumer buying behaviour.

measures proposed by respondents.
All sections are supported by descriptive
statistics and inferential analysis, including
Pearson correlation results.

Discussion
This section interprets the results presented
in the previous chapter and connects them to the
study objectives, existing literature, and
theoretical foundations. The discussion
highlights the relevance of brand attributes in
influencing consumer buying be haviour at
Heritage Bank PLC and examines how the
findings contribute to broader understandings
of branding in the Nigerian financial context.
Relationship Between Results and Study
Objectives
The study’s findings align strongly with the
research objectives. First, the analysis revealed
that functional, symbolic, and experiential

attributes significantly influence consumer
buying behaviour. Second, several branding
challenges were identified, including weakened
brand identity and limited innovation. Third,
respondents proposed concrete remedial
measures such as brand commitment and
service innovation. Each of these outcomes
confirms the importance of branding as a
strategic tool in the financial sector.
Brand Attributes and Consumer Buying
Behaviour
The results confirm that brand attributes play
a vital role in shaping consumer decisions.
Functional attributes ranked highest, indicating
that Nigerian banking customers tend to
prioritize tangible benefits such as service
reliability, efficient processe s, and overall
performance. This aligns with the Reasoned
Action Theory, which suggests that consumers
evaluate behavioural outcomes based on
expected benefits.
The strong influence of functional attributes
resonates with earlier work by Fianto et al. [14],
who argued that consumers gravitate toward
brands that consistently deliver on their
promises. In the context of Heritage Bank PLC,
functional benefits may include ease of
accessing funds, speed of service delivery,
security assurances, and clarity of
communication.
Symbolic attributes ranked second. These
reflect the emotional and psychological
meanings associated with the brand. The
positive correlation identified echoes the
findings of Heath et al. [15], which emphasized
that branding evokes emotional associations
that shape consumer preference and loyalty.
Many Nigerian consumers evaluate banks not
only as financial service providers but also as
symbols of trust, stability, and social status.
Experiential attributes had the lowest mean
but still showed significant influence. This
suggests that customers value how well the
brand aligns with their personal preferences,
tastes, and self -image. While experiential
factors are important, they appear less
influential compared to functional needs,
possibly because banking services in Nigeria
are viewed more as essential utilities than
lifestyle products.
Challenges in Applying Brand Attributes
The second objective explored challenges
Heritage Bank PLC faces in effectively using
brand attributes. Findings revealed four major
challenges:
1. Losing Touch with Brand Identity : This
ranked as the most severe challenge. A
weak or inconsistent brand identity
undermines customer trust and confuses
the public about the bank’s core values. As
Wu et al. [ 16] noted, brands that fail to
sustain a consistent identity lose relevance,
particularly in fast -changing markets.
Heritage Bank’s identity may have suffered
due to its historical transitions —from
Societe Generale Bank Nigeria to Heritage
Banking Company Limited —affecting
public perception and continuity.
2. Creating Products Without a Strong
Brand Foundation : Developing products
without aligning them to a central brand
philosophy dilutes the value of branding.
Without a foundation, products lack
distinctiveness, making them
indistinguishable from competitors.
Ahmad & Thyagaraj  [17] supported this,
asserting that products must always
reinforce brand purpose and positioning.
3. Lack of Creativity: Innovation is essential
in competitive industries. Respondents
indicated that Heritage Bank PLC lacked
creativity in product development,
marketing communication, and customer
experience. Hurley et al. [ 18] associated
low creativity with reduced competitive
advantage, especially in service industries.
4. Limited Commitment to the Branding
Process: Sustaining a strong brand requires
long-term commitment across all levels of
the organization. Zhang et al. [ 19] found

that employees and management must
collaborate to reinforce brand values
consistently. Respondents perceived that
Heritage Bank had not fully committed to
the branding process, leading to gaps in
service delivery and customer experience.
Comparison with Existing Literature
The results strongly support earlier studies
on branding within service sectors:
1. Apoyomare et al.  [20] found that brand
attributes influence purchase decisions in
Nigerian industries.
2. Wong & Merrilees [21] similarly reported
that symbolic and functional attributes are
dominant in shaping customer choices in
service organizations.
3. Dholakia et al. [ 22] showed a significant
relationship between experiential attributes
and consumer behaviour.
The present study therefore reinforces
existing knowledge but adds contextual
importance by focusing explicitly on Nigerian
banking, where customers tend to value
functional attributes more than symbolic or
experiential ones.
Implications for Heritage Bank PLC
The findings have several managerial
implications:
1. Functional Attributes Must Be
Strengthened: Since functional attributes
are the most influential, Heritage Bank
PLC should prioritize operational
excellence, service efficiency,
transparency, digital banking performance,
and customer support.
2. Symbolic Attributes Should Be
Reinforced Through Emotional
Branding: The bank should cultivate trust,
reliability, and social value through
consistent messaging, community
engagement, and brand storytelling.
3. Enhance Experiential Value Through
Customer-Centric Innovation : Investing
in design thinking, user experience
enhancement, and personalized banking
services would improve experiential
attributes.
4. Address Core Branding Challenges :
Heritage Bank PLC must restore a clear,
consistent brand identity, apply creativity
in all branding elements, and
institutionalize branding commitment
across all staff and units.
Need for Further Research
Future studies could expand by:
1. Including more banks to allow comparative
analysis.
2. Using mixed methods to capture qualitative
insights into customer emotions.
3. Exploring the digital branding dimension,
especially with mobile banking growth.
These areas would deepen understanding of
branding dynamics in Nigeria’s financial sector.
Conclusion
This study investigated the effects of
branding on consumer buying behaviour within
the Nigerian financial industry, using Heritage
Bank PLC as a case study. The findings
confirmed that branding remains a critical
determinant of customer decision-making in the
banking sector. Functional, symbolic, and
experiential attributes significantly influence
consumer behaviour, with functional attributes
emerging as the strongest predictor. This
implies that customers in Nigeria prioritize
reliable service delivery, operational efficiency,
and product performance when selecting
financial institutions.
The study also identified key challenges
hindering Heritage Bank PLC’s branding
strategy, including loss of brand identity,
development of products without strong brand
foundations, low creativity, and insufficient
commitment to the branding process. These
challenges compromise the bank’s ability to
differentiate itself and build lasting
relationships with customers.
