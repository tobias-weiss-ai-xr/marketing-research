Abstract
  This study investigates the relationships among overall service quality, customer satis -
faction, customer engagement behaviors, and hotel brand loyalty within the sustainable tourism
context of Western Thailand's accommodation sector. It further explores the roles of online
reviews, hotel management responses, and artificial intelligence technology in shaping customer
perceptions. Data were collected from 459 Thai consumers with prior hotel booking experience
through online platforms using a structured questionnaire. Structural equation modeling (SEM) was
employed to examine sufficiency relationships among the latent constructs, while necessary con-
dition analysis (NCA) was used to identify minimum threshold conditions for achieving high brand
loyalty. The results reveal that the consumer decision-making process and guest online feedback
significantly influence perceived service quality, which in turn drives customer satisfaction and
engagement behaviors, ultimately leading to brand loyalty. NCA results further demonstrate that
service quality and customer satisfaction function as necessary conditions for high loyalty levels,
while artificial intelligence showed no statistically significant effect within this context. These
findings contribute to the hospitality literature by integrating SEM and NCA to distinguish between
sufficiency and necessity conditions, offering actionable insights for improving service quality and
fostering long-term brand loyalty in Thai hotels.
Keywords: 1) service quality 2) customer satisfaction 3) customer engagement behaviors 4) brand
loyalty 5) artificial intelligence

Introduction

Nyagadza, et al., 2022, p. 2). Contemporary

871; Sitaram and Purohit, 2025, p. 188). Thai -

the imperative for enhanced accommodation
service quality.

fundamental to customer satisfaction and
competitive advantage. The Service Quality

User-generated content is perceived as more

playing a crucial role in shaping consumer trust.
However, fraudulent reviews drive increased
consumer distrust and threaten review plat -

 Online feedback dimensions extend
beyond numerical ratings. Analyzing 37,221

Hotel Management Response Strategies
 Hotel management responses (HMR)
to online reviews represent a critical yet un -
derutilized reputation management tool. Li,
et al. (2017, p. 293) indicated that response
frequency, timeliness, and content quality
jointly influence consumers' perceptions of a
hotel's service recovery commitment. Sparks,
So and Bradley (2016, p. 74) demonstrated
that managerial responses mitigate negative
interpretation of critical reviews, with prompt
and substantive replies signaling organizational
responsiveness and customer-centric orienta -
tion.
 Effective managerial responses are
rooted in impression management theory and
service recovery principles. Public responses
transform potentially damaging negative re -
views into opportunities demonstrating care
for both original reviewers and prospective
guests. However, response efficacy varies con-
siderably depending on context and execution;
generic or defensive replies may exacerbate
reputational harm. Despite strategic impor -
tance, managerial responses exhibit weaker
direct effects on booking intentions compared
to review content itself, underscoring that
genuine service quality improvements remain
more influential than reactive communication
strategies.
Consumer Decision-Making Process and
Selection Behavior
 The consumer decision making process
(DMP) in hotel selection has undergone fun -
damental restructuring due to online review
availability. Chan, et al. (2017, p. 54) revealed
that review valence significantly influences
booking intentions, moderated by demograph-
ic similarity between readers and reviewers.
Casalóa, et al. (2015, p. 49) demonstrated that
ratings on reputable platforms such as TripAd-
visor are perceived as more useful and trust -
worthy, engendering higher booking intentions.
Online reviews fulfill distinct functions across
cognitive stages. During information search,

unavailable through official channels (Ukpabi
and Karjaluoto, 2016, p. 618). In alternative

Overall Service Quality and Customer Sat-
isfaction
 Overall service quality (OSQ) remains
the cornerstone of hospitality excellence, with
online reviews functioning as collective qual -
ity assessment mechanisms. High perceived
quality stimulates positive reviews, creating a
reinforcing feedback loop Chan, et al. (2021, p.
871) found that 1% rating increases correlate
with 2.68% sales rises per available room. Con-
sumers rely on star ratings as screening tools,
though subjective assessments of value, sleep
quality, and service often outweigh official rat-
ings in driving satisfaction (Radojevic, Stanisic
and Stanic, 2017, p. 134). Overall customer
satisfaction (OCS) mediates between perceived
quality and behavioral outcomes. Satisfaction
operates dually: from service experiences (mo-
tivating reviews) and from reading reviews (in -
fluencing bookings). Luo and Qu (2016, p. 311)
established that perceived quality significantly
affects loyalty and revisit intentions through
satisfaction mediation. Berezina, et al. (2015,
p. 1) noted that when positive reviews elevate
expectations, service falling short yields dispro-
portionate dissatisfaction. Satisfied customers
influenced by reviews continue depending on
them for decisions and respond favorably to
managerial responses (Li, et al., 2017, p. 5). This
underscores reciprocal relationships between
service delivery, satisfaction, and digital review
ecosystems.
Customer Engagement Behaviors and
Brand Loyalty
 Customer engagement behaviors
(CEB) extend beyond transactions to include
reviews, recommendations, and advocacy.
Service quality predicts behavioral intentions,
with satisfied customers generating positive
reviews and dissatisfied ones switching provid-
ers. Engagement behaviors enhance loyalty
(Rather and Sharma, 2017, p. 1; Rasoolimanesh,
et al., 2019, p. 559), driven by satisfaction or
dissatisfaction. Trust, usefulness, and enjoy -
ment influence repurchase intentions, while
price dissatisfaction prompts switching (Keav -
eney, 1995, p. 71). Hotel brand loyalty linked
to sustainable tourism (HBST) depends on
service experiences and engagement. Li, Teng
and Chen (2020, p. 184) confirmed CEB as a key
loyalty determinant in social media contexts.
Customers pay premiums based on ratings,
with booking intentions tied to satisfaction
(Martin-Fuentes, 2016, p. 126). Electronic ser -
vice quality affects satisfaction, which influenc-
es engagement and loyalty (Vo, et al., 2019, p.
499). This research demonstrates the pathway
from service quality through satisfaction and

Artificial Intelligence in Hospitality
Decision-Making
 The integration of artificial intelligence
(AI) into the hospitality industry marks a piv -
otal advancement that is reshaping customer
interactions and decision-making processes.
AI applications in hotels range from real time
chatbots for customer service to advanced
recommendation systems that analyze user
preferences and review patterns. Although
prior research has primarily emphasized hu -
man generated reviews and human-managed
responses, AI is increasingly acting as an inter -
mediary in these exchanges, potentially affect-
ing perceptions of review authenticity and the
efficacy of managerial responses. The conver -
gence of AI adoption with traditional service
quality dimensions, customer satisfaction,
and engagement behaviors merits systematic
examination to understand how technological
augmentation modifies established relational
pathways in hospitality contexts. In essence,
AI serves as a mediating force in the digital
hospitality ecosystem, bridging conventional
human centric interactions with automated
efficiencies. This intersection not only amplifies
operational capabilities but also introduces
complexities regarding trust and genuineness,
underscoring the need for balanced human-AI
hybrid models to sustain customer-centric out-
comes in an evolving industry landscape. Re -
cent studies have further explored AI applica -
tions in tourism contexts. Tussyadiah (2020,p.5)
demonstrated that AI-powered chatbots signifi-
cantly improve response time and customer
satisfaction in hotel booking processes. Pillai
and Sivathanu (2020, p. 3199) found that per -
ceived usefulness and anthropomorphism of
AI concierges positively influence adoption in-
tentions among hotel guests. Li, et al. (2020, p.
184) examined the impact of AI-based recom-
mendation systems on tourist decision-making,
revealing that algorithmic suggestions enhance
perceived personalization but may reduce
trust when customers perceive lack of human
oversight. These findings collectively suggest
that while AI holds considerable promise for
the hospitality industry, its effectiveness de -
pends heavily on implementation context,
customer familiarity, and the balance between
automation and human interaction.
 The foregoing literature review reveals
several critical gaps warranting further inves -
tigation. First, while extensive research has
examined service quality, customer satisfac -
tion, engagement behaviors, and brand loyalty
independently, few studies have simultane -
ously integrated these constructs with online
review management and AI technology within
a single analytical framework. Second, the
majority of empirical evidence originates from
Western or East Asian hospitality contexts,
leaving a notable contextual absence in the
Southeast Asian market, particularly in Thai -
land's western region, which represents a high-
growth tourism corridor with distinctive service
characteristics. Third, from a methodological
standpoint, prior studies have relied predom -
inantly on regression-based or conventional
SEM approaches that assess sufficiency con -
ditions, without distinguishing whether certain
factors constitute necessary preconditions

Methodology
Research Design and Theoretical
Framework
 This study employs a cross sectional
quantitative research design to investigate
the relationships among guest online feed -
back, service quality, customer satisfaction,
engagement behaviors, and hotel brand loy -
alty within the context of sustainable tourism.
The conceptual framework is grounded in an
interdisciplinary theoretical foundation and in-
corporates a research model comprising eight
latent variables. The independent variables
include Guest Online Feedback (GOF), Hotel
Management Response (HMR), and Consumer
Decision-Making Process (DMP). The ultimate
dependent variable is Hotel Brand Loyalty/
Sustainable Tourism (HBST), while the mediat-
ing variables consist of Overall Service Quality
(OSQ), Overall Customer Satisfaction (OCS),
and Customer Engagement Behaviors (CEB).
Additionally, Artificial Intelligence Usage (AI) is
introduced as an supplementary variable to
evaluate the influence of modern technology
on the online review ecosystem.
Instrument Development and Variable
Operationalization
 This study develops an integrative
conceptual framework drawing from multidis -
ciplinary theories to assess the influence of
online guest feedback and hotel responses
on consumer behavior. The measurement
structure comprises eight constructs derived
from a systematic literature review. Guest
Online Feedback (GOF) measures perceived
credibility and value of online reviews, adapt -
ed from the frameworks of Radojevic, Stanisic
and Stanic (2017, p. 134). Hotel Management
Response (HMR) evaluates the effectiveness
of online complaint management by assessing
response frequency, timeliness, and clarity,
grounded in service recovery theory. Consum-
er Decision-Making Process (DMP) captures
the utilization of positive reviews and their
influence on booking decisions. Overall Service
Quality (OSQ) extends the SERVQUAL model,
evaluating the linkage between service quality
and the generation of positive reviews. Overall
Customer Satisfaction (OCS) examines expec -
tation confirmation through comparisons be -
tween online reviews and actual experiences.
Customer Engagement Behaviors (CEB) assess
extra-role behaviors beyond purchase expec -
tations, encompassing both positive actions
(e.g., review creation and repurchase) and
negative actions (e.g., criticism and service
switching). These behaviors ultimately con -
tribute to Hotel Brand Loyalty/Sustainable
Tourism (HBST), which measures willingness to
pay a premium and the formation of long-term
relationships. Finally, Technology Acceptance
of AI (TAI) evaluates perceived usefulness and
ease of use of AI technologies in automated re-
sponses and sentiment analysis. All constructs
are operationalized using multi-item scales
validated in prior hospitality research, ensuring
reliability and validity suitable for structural
equation modeling.
Questionnaire Design and Measurement
Scales
 The questionnaire was developed in
Thai and structured into three distinct sections.
The first section comprised screening questions
to ensure respondent eligibility, confirming that

participants had at least one experience book-
ing accommodation via online platforms within
the past year. The second section consisted of
29 items measuring the eight latent constructs,
employing a 6 point Likert scale (1 = strongly
disagree to 6 = strongly agree). The selection of
a 6 point scale, as opposed to the more con -
ventional 5- or 7-point formats, followed the
recommendations of Wattanacharoensil, et al.
(2024, p. 7), who demonstrated that this con -
figuration enhances measurement sensitivity
and discriminant validity without compromis -
ing scale reliability. The third section collected
demographic information, including gender,
age, educational attainment, and patterns of
online platform usage. The questionnaire un -
derwent rigorous content validity assessment
by three experts in hospitality management
and digital marketing to verify item wording
appropriateness and theoretical alignment
with construct definitions. A pilot test was
subsequently conducted with 30 respondents
to evaluate comprehension, completion time,
and overall clarity. Feedback from the pilot led
to minor refinements in phrasing for several
items to improve precision and respondent
understanding. Content validity was assessed
using the Item Objective Congruence (IOC)
index evaluated by three subject-matter ex -
perts. All items achieved IOC values exceed -
ing 0.67, surpassing the commonly accepted
threshold of 0.50 (Rovinelli and Hambleton,
1977, p. 49). Reliability was established through
Cronbach's alpha coefficients, which ranged
from 0.78 to 0.91 across all constructs, ex -
ceeding the recommended minimum of 0.70
(Nunnally and Bernstein, 1994). The selection
of a 6-point Likert scale follows Wattanacha -
roensil, et al. (2024, p. 7), who demonstrated
that even-numbered scales eliminate neutral
midpoint responses, thereby reducing central
tendency bias and enhancing measurement
sensitivity.
Sampling and Data Collection Procedures
 The target population comprised Thai
consumers with prior experience booking
hotels through online platforms a large and
geographically dispersed population for which
definitive enumeration was not feasible. Sam -
ple size determination was therefore guided
by the Krejcie and Morgan (1970, p. 607) table
and the guidelines of Hair, et al. (2006, p. 54),
which recommend a minimum of 384 respon-
dents for populations exceeding one million
or of unknown size at a 95% confidence level
and ±5% margin of error. To accommodate po-
tential incomplete responses, data collection
targeted 450 usable questionnaires. Data were
collected from June 1 to July 31, 2025, using
a mixed-mode approach to enhance repre -
sentativeness and mitigate self-selection bias.
A purposive sampling strategy was employed,
targeting Thai consumers aged 18 years and
above who had booked hotel accommodation
through online platforms within the preceding
12 months. The sample size of 459 exceeds
the minimum requirement recommended by
Hair, et al. (2006, p. 54), who suggest a ratio
of at least 10 observations per estimated pa -
rameter for SEM analyses. With 29 observed
variables in the measurement model, the
minimum sample size would be 290, and the
obtained sample of 459 provides a ratio of
approximately 15.8:1, well above this thresh -

online questionnaire hosted on Google Forms
and disseminated via social media platforms
(Facebook, Instagram, LINE), mailing lists, and
travel related online forums. Concurrently,
paper-based questionnaires were administered

transportation hubs, and university campuses.
All participants received a clear explanation of

the total variance, below the 50% threshold

interpreted as associations rather than causal
effects (Spector, 2019, p. 125). To assess the

employed to derive 95% confidence intervals
for all path coefficients. The stability of the
estimates across bootstrap samples provides
evidence of robustness. Additionally, the
HTMT ratio and Fornell-Larcker criterion were

sensitivity checks for the measurement model

Characteristics of the Sample
 The sample (n = 459) was predom -

(34.6%) or preferred not to specify gender

segment with high familiarity with online book-
ing platforms, aligning with the study's focus on
the role of online reviews in hotel selection
behaviour.
Data Analysis

ized Structured Component Analysis with Mea-
surement Error (GSCAM), a structural equation
modelling technique based on component
principles that effectively accommodates
measurement error without requiring the
assumption of multivariate normality. GSCAM
was selected over covariance-based SEM (CB-
SEM) and partial least squares SEM (PLS-SEM)
for several reasons: (1) GSCAM provides con -
sistent estimates comparable to CB-SEM while
maintaining the flexibility of component-based
approaches; (2) it accommodates both reflec -
tive and formative measurement models; and
(3) it does not require the strict distributional
assumptions of maximum likelihood estima -
tion, making it suitable for the present data

Evaluation of the Measurement Model and
Structural Hypothesis Testing
 The measurement model in this
study was evaluated using a composite-based
structural equation modelling (SEM) approach
implemented through the Generalized Struc -
tured Component Analysis with Measurement
Error (GSCAM) software. This technique is
widely recognised for its ability to provide un-
biased estimates in latent factor models, while
also generating composite scores suitable for
subsequent analyses. A key advantage of this
method lies in its avoidance of the inconsisten-
cy issues that can arise with maximum likeli -
hood estimators, particularly under conditions
of insufficient responses, and its capacity to
utilise full information for model estimation.
Assessment of internal consistency re -
vealed that composite reliability coefficients
(ρ) for all latent constructs ranged from 0.757
to 0.878, exceeding the recommended thresh-
old of 0.70 and thereby indicating strong inter-
nal consistency among the respective indicator
sets. Average variance extracted (AVE) values
generally surpassed 0.50, with the exception
of the Guest Online Feedback (GOF) construct
(AVE = 0.45), which fell slightly below the
0.50 threshold. However, GOF was retained
based on several justifications: (1) composite
reliability exceeded 0.70, indicating adequate
internal consistency (Fornell and Larcker,
1981, p.39); (2) all standardized factor loadings
were significant and above 0.60; and (3) GOF

represents a theoretically essential construct.
Following Hair, et al. (2006, p. 54), constructs
with AVE slightly below 0.50 may be retained
when composite reliability is satisfactory. Stan-
dardised factor loadings ranged from 0.63 to
0.95, with 95% confidence intervals excluding
zero, thus confirming reliable convergent va -
lidity. Furthermore, discriminant validity was
established through the Heterotrait-Monotrait
(HTMT) ratio and the Fornell-Larcker criterion,
where inter-construct correlations were be -
low 0.85, ensuring that the constructs were
sufficiently distinct. Overall model fit for the
measurement component was satisfactory, as
evidenced by a Goodness-of-Fit Index (GFI) of
0.965 and a Standardized Root Mean Square

established benchmarks in the literature.
 In the structural model evaluation,

significance across hypothesised relationships.
Guest online feedback (GOF) exerted a mod -

hotel management responses (HMR) showed
no statistically significant influence on OSQ
(β = 0.049; 95% CI: –0.012–0.135), suggesting
that this factor may not serve as a primary
determinant of service quality perceptions.
The decision-making process (DMP) emerged

0.560; 95% CI: 0.418–0.682), underscoring the
substantial impact of information and reviews
from travel platforms on quality perceptions.
Service quality (OSQ) was a strong predictor of
overall customer satisfaction (OCS), exhibiting
a coefficient of 0.764 (95% CI: 0.724–0.837),
and demonstrated a moderate influence on
customer engagement behaviour (CEB) ( β =
0.361; 95% CI: 0.090–0.553). Concurrently, OCS
represented the most potent driver of CEB
(β = 0.706; 95% CI: 0.478–0.867), indicating
that heightened satisfaction effectively stim -
ulates greater customer engagement with the
brand. The application of artificial intelligence
(AI) in review management yielded a small
negative coefficient that was not statistically
distinguishable from zero ( β = –0.222; 95%

role in this context. Customer engagement
behaviour (CEB) exerted a strong and signifi -
cant effect on sustainable hotel/travel brand
loyalty (HBST) (β = 0.776; 95% CI: 0.706–0.826),

ment behaviour as a precursor to loyalty. Coef-

the model accounted for approximately 54.3%

in CEB, and 47.3% in HBST, representing mod-

the integration of online review information,

framework.

Results of Necessary Condition Analysis
(NCA)
 The NCA was conducted in strict ac -
cordance with the procedures outlined by Dul
(2016, p. 10) and Richter, et al. (2020, p. 2243).
Factor scores for each construct, derived from
the GSCAM analysis, were utilised as input for
the NCA package (version 4.0.2) in R Studio. The
initial step involved explicitly defining the con-
dition variables (predictors) and the outcome
variable. In this study, seven quality-related
constructs were examined as potential nec -
essary conditions for achieving high levels of
hotel booking stickiness (HBST): Guest Online
Feedback (GOF), Hotel Management Response
(HMR), Digital Marketing Process (DMP), Overall
Service Quality (OSQ), Overall Customer Satis -
faction (OCS), Customer Engagement Behaviour
(CEB), and Artificial Intelligence Application (AI).
 The NCA results revealed that GOF (d
= 0.210*, p = 0.002) and OCS (d = 0.203*, p =
0.016) exhibited moderate effect sizes, emerg-
ing as the most critical necessary conditions
for attaining high HBST scores. These were
followed by AI (d = 0.147*, p = 0.002), OSQ (d
= 0.125*, p = 0.002), and HMR (d = 0.118*, p
= 0.002), all demonstrating small-to-moderate
effect sizes and qualifying as necessary condi -
tions. In contrast, DMP (d = 0.077*, p = 0.020)
and CEB (d = 0.043*, p = 0.036) displayed ef -
fect sizes below the 0.10 benchmark and were
therefore not classified as necessary conditions
despite statistical significance. Effect sizes (d)
were calculated as the proportion of the area
above the ceiling line relative to the total
scope, whereby higher d values indicate that
low levels of the condition variable preclude
the outcome from reaching high levels.
 Bottleneck analysis further indicated
the minimum levels required for each con -
dition to support HBST at or above the 80th
percentile (0.8 on the normalised scale).
Specifically, GOF and OCS required minimum
levels of at least 40% and 45% of the observed

range (approximately 3.75–4.0 on a 1–6 Likert
scale), respectively. HMR, DMP, OSQ, and AI
necessitated thresholds of approximately 30–
36% (≈3.0–4.25), whereas CEB required only
11% (≈2.8). This suggests that even modestly
elevated customer engagement behaviour can

 These findings lend support to the
research hypotheses, demonstrating that
insufficient levels of these quality factors par -

Discussion
 The structural model confirms that OSQ
strongly predicts OCS (β = 0.764, p < 0.001), con-
sistent with SERVQUAL theory (Parasuraman, et
al., 1988, p. 12; Ali, et al., 2021, p. 14). The NCA
extends this finding: when OCS is specified as
the outcome variable, OSQ emerges as a nec-
essary condition with a bottleneck threshold
at approximately 70% of the observed range
roughly 4.2 on the 6-point scale below which
satisfaction at the 80th percentile becomes
unattainable (Dul, 2016, p. 10). This threshod
behavior suggests that service quality operates
as a hygiene factor below a critical level, no
combination of supplementary factors can
compensate for its absence. The theoretical
implication is that Expectancy-Disconfirmation
processes may not activate meaningfully until
a baseline service standard has been met, ex-
tending current understanding of satisfaction
formation in hospitality contexts.
 Among the antecedents of OSQ, DMP
emerged as the strongest predictor (β = 0.560,
p < 0.01), followed by GOF (β = 0.257, p < 0.01),
while HMR showed no significant effect
(β = 0.049, n.s.). This contrasts with Li, et al.
(2020, p. 184), who found management re -
sponses significantly associated with hotel
performance, and Li, et al. (2017, p. 293), who
demonstrated a signaling effect of managerial
responses on consumer perceptions. The di -
vergence likely reflects methodological and
contextual differences: whereas those studies
measured hotel-level performance outcomes
using large-scale panel data from major
review platforms, the present study measured
individual-level quality perceptions among
Thai consumers for whom pre-consumption
information from online platforms may carry
greater weight than post hoc provider respons-
es. This suggests that in digitally mediated
markets where peer-generated content dom -
inates, proactive service quality improvement
may be more effective than reactive response
management.
 OCS strongly drives CEB ( β = 0.706,
p < 0.001), and CEB is the primary determinant
of HBST ( β = 0.776, p < 0.001), consistent with
Customer Engagement theory (Kumar, et al.,
2019, p.135). Engagement through co-develop-
ing constructive feedback and brand defending
exerted stronger influence than general expe -
rience sharing. This can be explained through
psychological ownership theory, guests who
invest cognitive effort in co-creating service
improvements develop a sense of ownership

over the brand relationship, transforming trans-
actional satisfaction into relational commit -
ment (Morgan and Hunt, 1994, p. 20). However,
the NCA reveals that CEB is sufficient but not
necessary for high HBST (d = 0.043), indicating
that loyalty can emerge without high engage -
ment if foundational factors OSQ, OCS, and
GOF are adequately maintained. Engagement
thus functions as an accelerator rather than a
prerequisite.
 AI technology showed no significant
linear effect ( β = −0.222) yet NCA classified
it as a necessary condition for high HBST
(d = 0.147, p < 0.01). This divergence contrasts
with Prentice, Wang and Loureiro (2019, p.
50), who found AI-driven service attributes
significantly influencing satisfaction and loyalty
in coastal chain hotels. The discrepancy may
be explained by the maturity gap: Prentice,
Wang and Loureiro (2019, p. 50), examined
international chain properties with established
AI infrastructure, whereas the present study
encompasses a broader range of hotel catego-
ries in western Thailand where AI deployment
remains nascent. Consequently, many respon-
dents may have had limited direct experience
with AI-enabled hotel services, reducing their
ability to evaluate AI's contribution to service
quality and loyalty. The AI construct was also
measured through perceptions rather than ac-
tual usage, potentially attenuating the effect.
The NCA result suggests that AI operates as an
infrastructural necessity its absence constrains
loyalty ceilings yet its presence does not
linearly enhance outcomes, consistent with
Huang and Rust’s (2021, p. 30) augmentation
perspective. Future research should employ
behavioral AI usage metrics to disentangle
awareness from interaction effects.
 The joint SEM–NCA classification (Table
3) demonstrates that statistical significance and
practical indispensability are distinct proper -
ties. GOF, OSQ, and OCS are both necessary
and sufficient baseline requirements that also
drive incremental gains. HMR and AI are nec -
essary but not sufficient enabling conditions
whose SEM paths are non-significant yet whose
absence constrains outcomes. CEB is sufficient
but not necessary a powerful amplifier that is
not a prerequisite. DMP operates exclusively
through OSQ and is neither necessary nor suffi-
cient for HBST directly. This fourfold distinction
provides a resource allocation logic unavail -
able from SEM alone: necessary conditions
define minimum investment thresholds, while
sufficient conditions identify where additional
investment yields the greatest marginal re -
turns.

 In practical terms, the research find -
ings indicate that hotels should (1) maintain
service quality at a minimum level of 70%
as a fundamental condition, (2) develop sys -
tematic strategies to promote CEB, especially
co-developing constructive feedback, and (3)
invest in AI technology cautiously, focusing
on augmenting employee capabilities rather
than replacement. Furthermore, linking loy -
alty with sustainable tourism demonstrates
opportunities for differentiation and long-term
competitive advantage through integration
of sustainability practices. Specifically, hotels
adopting environmentally responsible practic-
es can leverage service quality and customer

Conclusion
 This study integrated Structural Equa -
tion Modeling (SEM) and Necessary Condition
Analysis (NCA) to examine how online informa-
tion, service quality, satisfaction, engagement,
and AI adoption jointly shape hotel brand
loyalty and sustainable tourism outcomes in
the western Thailand accommodation sector.
The dual-analytical approach revealed a clear
power shift in quality perception from service
providers to online consumer communities:
customer decision-making processes and guest
online feedback significantly predicted service
quality, whereas hotel management response
did not. Service quality in turn functioned as
a threshold condition for satisfaction, and en -
gagement behaviours particularly co-develop-
ing constructive feedback and brand defending
emerged as the strongest pathway to loyalty,
consistent with Customer Engagement theory
(Rita, Vong and Santos, 2025). The divergence
between AI's non-significant linear effect and
its necessary-condition status reflects the Ser-
vice Automation Paradox, whereby technology
can constrain loyalty ceilings without produc -
ing linear attitudinal gains. Together, these
results establish a fourfold necessity sufficien-
cy classification that differentiates baseline
requirements, enabling conditions, amplifiers,
and indirect drivers of loyalty formation.
 This study contributes to SERVQUAL
theory by demonstrating that in digitally me -
diated hospitality contexts, peer-generated
online content now surpasses direct provider
responses in shaping service quality percep -
tions, suggesting that the traditional provid -
er-to-consumer quality signaling pathway
requires reconceptualization. The findings also
refine Customer Engagement theory: the NCA
result revealing engagement as a sufficient
accelerator rather than a necessary precon -
dition challenges the assumed centrality of
engagement in loyalty formation models. Re -
garding AI adoption, the divergence between a
non-significant SEM path and a necessary NCA
status suggests that technology can operate
as an infrastructural necessity without pro -
ducing linear attitudinal gains, contributing to
the augmentation perspective in Technology
Acceptance literature. Methodologically, the
dual SEM–NCA approach demonstrates how
a fourfold necessity–sufficiency classification
yields resource allocation logic unavailable
from either method alone. Several limitations
should be acknowledged. The cross-sectional
design precludes inference about how these
relationships evolve over time, and the region-
al focus on western Thailand may constrain
generalisability to other markets and hotel
categories. AI was measured through percep -
tions rather than actual interaction behaviour,
potentially attenuating its observed linear ef -
fect. Future research should adopt longitudinal
designs, extend sampling across broader geo -
graphic and segment contexts, and incorporate
behavioural AI usage metrics to disentangle
awareness from interaction effects in shaping
customer loyalty.

Recommendations
Theoretical Recommendations
 This study raises important issues
requiring further investigation. First, Service
Quality theory in the digital era must be revised
to more comprehensively encompass e-WOM
and online information. Traditional SERVQUAL
may be insufficient for explaining consumer
behavior relying on online platforms. A con -
ceptual framework integrating traditional and
digital service quality dimensions should be
developed. Second, findings regarding AI open
opportunities to study Technology Paradox in
Service Encounters. Theory explaining condi -
tions under which technology genuinely adds
value and when it creates anxiety should be
developed by integrating TAM with Psychologi-
cal Ownership Theory and Human-Robot Inter-
action Theory. Third, the use of NCA alongside
SEM should be promoted in business research.
Best practices for integrating both methods
should be developed, including interpretation
of potentially contradictory results.
Research Limitations
 This study has several limitations. First,
the data were collected only from respon -
dents in the Western Thailand tourism context,
which may limit generalizability. Second, the
sample is concentrated in the 16–25 age group
(48.8%), which may not represent the broader
tourist population. Third, self-reported survey
data introduces response bias possibilities.
Fourth, the cross-sectional design limits causal
inference. Fifth, the AI construct was measured
through perceived usefulness rather than actu-
al usage. Future research should employ longi-
tudinal designs, broader geographic sampling,
actual AI usage metrics, and experimental
approaches.
