Abstract: Amid intensifying market competition in China, the automotive manufacturing industry, as a capital-intensive
pillar sector, harbors various financial risks throughout the entire operational process. Inadequate risk control may lead
to capital chain rupture, profitability decline, and even operational crises. Based on the financial data of FAW Hongqi
Automobile Company from 2019 to 2023, this paper employs literature review and financial indicator analysis to identify
Hong qi’s financial risks across three dimensions: financing, investment, and operations. Using quantitative metrics including
current ratio, debt-to-asset ratio, asset turnover efficiency, and profitability indicators, this study identifies four core financial
issues: significant short-term solvency pressure, weak period expense management, low returns on internal investment,
and decelerating capital turnover. Accordingly, four control strategies are proposed: optimizing the structure of long-term
and short-term debt, strictly controlling period expenses, improving investment feasibility assessments, and enhancing the
efficiency of working capital turnover. This study provides a reference for the financial risk management of Hongqi and other
domestic independent car brands.

1. Introduction
With the continuous opening of China’s domestic market economy, local automobile companies not only face competition
from domestic peers but also challenges from international automotive giants. The increasingly complex and ever-changing
business environment continues to amplify various operational and fi  nancial risks. The automobile manufacturing industry is
characterized by capital and asset intensity, manifested in a high proportion of fi  xed assets, signifi  cant investment in R&D,
and a large amount of funds long tied up in inventory and accounts receivable, making the likelihood of financial risks
signifi  cantly higher. If risk management is insuffi   cient, it will severely restrict enterprise development and may even lead to a
rupture of the capital chain, operational crises, or bankruptcy. Therefore, the identifi  cation and prevention of fi  nancial risks in
automobile enterprises is of signifi  cant research importance.

channels, resulting in substantial capital expenditures, while also increasing fi  nancing, investment, and operational pressures.

2. Enterprise Overview and Current Status of Hongqi Automobile
2.1 Enterprise Overview
The Hongqi brand was established in 1958. In November 2016, FAW Hongqi officially began independent operations,
establishing its position as a high-end domestic automobile brand with national heritage. It has long served as the official
vehicle supplier for major national-level events. The company continuously increases investment in technological research
and development; in 2021, FAW Group’s R&D investment reached 21.42 billion yuan, with 4,757 patent applications filed
throughout the year, achieving breakthroughs in 63 core automotive technologies.
2.2 Current Operational Status
Hongqi Automobile has established three sub-brand lineups: “Golden Sunflower,” “New Energy,” and “Energy-Saving
Vehicles,” while simultaneously advancing intelligent and electrification transformation and continuously expanding into the
domestic premium passenger vehicle and overseas markets. However, at the industry level, declining domestic automotive
consumption demand in 2022, rising component procurement costs driven by commodity inflation, and the impact of
price wars among new energy vehicle manufacturers have collectively exerted pressure on Hongqi’s revenue, profits, and
asset turnover. The contradiction between capital investment driven by expansion and declining profitability has gradually
highlighted financial risks.

4. Core Financial Risk Issues of Hongqi Automobile
4.1 Excessive Short-Term Liability Risk and Imbalanced Capital Structure
Hongqi’s current ratio, quick ratio, and cash ratio have remained below safety thresholds for consecutive years. Notes
payable, accounts payable, and short-term borrowings constitute the principal components of current liabilities. With
concentrated maturity of short-term debt, the company’s monetary funds and quickly realizable assets are insufficient to
cover short-term repayment obligations. The proportion of long-term liabilities is relatively low, resulting in an imbalanced
mix of long-term and short-term debt with concentrated repayment schedules, which can easily trigger periodic capital chain
tightness. Although long-term solvency indicators remain stable, short-term solvency pressure constitutes the enterprise’s
primary financial hazard.

4.2 Inadequate Period Expense Control and Sustained Profit Margin Compression
Selling expenses and administrative expenses remain rigid and substantial. In 2022, total costs and expenses even exceeded
operating revenue, and no standardized budget control mechanism has been established for various expense categories

.
During industry downturns, revenue contracts while expenses fail to decline proportionally, directly driving sharp reductions
in net profit margin and operating profit margin. The weakened self-financing capacity further amplifies both financing and
investment risks.
4.3 Blind Expansion of Internal Investment Scale and Insufficient Investment Returns
The enterprise has continuously increased investment in fixed assets and construction in progress to expand production
capacity. However, amid cyclical downturns in market demand, newly added capacity cannot be fully utilized. Inventory
and accounts receivable continuously tie up working capital, while R&D investment is intermittent and insufficient in scale.
Overall, internal investment exhibits an input-output imbalance, with substantial capital precipitation failing to convert into
revenue and profits. The cost of internal investment is excessive while returns remain inadequate.
4.4 Insufficient Working Capital Turnover Efficiency and Slow Capital Recirculation
Accounts receivable turnover, inventory turnover, and current asset turnover all declined sharply in 2022. Inventory buildup
and extended sales collection cycles have resulted in substantial capital being tied up in inventory and receivables, slowing
capital liquidity and recirculation. The shortage of freely disposable working capital constrains daily operations, R&D
funding, and debt servicing arrangements.
5. Financial Risk Control Strategies for Hongqi Automobile
5.1 Optimize Debt Structure and Scientifically Utilize Long-Term Liabilities
Adjust the ratio of long-term and short-term liabilities, moderately increasing the proportion of long-term loans and long-
term bond financing to spread out debt maturities and avoid concentrated short-term repayment pressure. Use the company’s
fixed assets, plants, and equipment as collateral for medium- and long-term financing, instead of relying on tight short-term
accounts payable and short-term loans. Set up a dynamic maturity management monitoring system and plan repayment funds
in advance to prevent the risk of short-term cash flow interruptions.
5.2 Establish a Full-Process Budget System and Strictly Control Period Expenditures
Set up annual and quarterly sales, management, and financial budgets, set estimated spending targets for each department, and
have strict approval processes for any overspending. Optimize sales channels to reduce ineffective advertising and channel
discount expenses, and use digital marketing to cut sales costs. Create an expense adjustment mechanism linked to revenue,
so that during industry downturns, non-essential management expenses can be cut proportionally to maintain stable profit
margins.
5.3 Improve Investment Feasibility Assessment and Control Internal Investment Costs
Before any capacity expansion, equipment purchase, or R&D investment, first assess the market conditions, profitability,
and cash flow, and set a minimum threshold for return on investment. Slow down inefficient construction and fixed asset
expansion, and adjust capacity based on market sales to reduce capital tied up in idle assets. Maintain stable and continuous
R&D investments, and evenly distribute funds between new energy and smart technology development to increase the value
of intangible asset investments. Moderately expand diversified external equity investments to spread the risk of individual
investments.
5.4 Optimize Working Capital Management and Comprehensively Enhance Capital Turnover
Accounts Receivable Management: Set up a customer credit rating system, extend credit terms for high-rated customers as
needed, and require prepayment for low-rated customers. Establish a regular collection routine to shorten the payment cycle
and boost accounts receivable turnover. Inventory Management: Use a make-to-order approach, dynamically adjust raw
material and finished goods stock, set safety stock levels, and cut down on excess inventory through discounts and promotions
to improve turnover. Coordinate the allocation of current assets to reduce idle funds, speed up overall asset turnover, and keep
operating cash flow steady.

Conclusion
Based on Hongqi’s comprehensive financial data from 2019 to 2023, this article takes a quantitative look at financing,
investment, and operations, and identifies four major financial risks: high short-term debt pressure, weak management of
cyclical expenses, insufficient returns on internal investments, and slow working capital turnover. Currently, Hongqi is in a
stage of expanding its electric vehicle line and updating its high-end brand, with capital investment continuing to rise. If these
financial risks aren’t kept in check over the long term, they could limit the brand’s sustainable growth.
Companies should optimize their debt capital structure, cut inefficient spending, standardize investment decision processes,
and make the most of existing working capital to create an organized financial risk monitoring system. As the automotive
industry, policies, and technology markets continue to evolve, new financial risks will keep emerging. Future research should
dynamically update risk identification indicators, improve early warning and response mechanisms, and keep working to
reduce financial losses.
