Abstract. The paper examines the roles of FoodTech innovation and
ESG in transforming contemporary wine tourism systems from traditional
winemaking models to smart, sustainable destinations. Particular attention is
given to the interaction between technological modernisation in viticulture and
winemaking, sustainability-oriented management practices and the creation of
competitive wine tourism experiences. The study examines how precision
viticulture, digital terroir management, traceability platforms, artificial
intelligence, craft winemaking innovations and indigenous grape varieties
contribute to the development of modern wine tourism ecosystems.  The research
substantiates that FoodTech  extends beyond technological improvements in
wine production and functions as an integrated mechanism that connects
vineyard management, wine quality, destination branding, visitor engagement,
and sustainability objectives. Special emphasis is placed on the role of terroir-
based differentiation, alternative vinification methods and the growing
importance of craft wineries in strengthening the authenticity and attractiveness
of wine -producing territories. The study employs a comparative analytical
approach and a KPI framework comprising five dimensions. The comparative
assessment covers France, Italy, Portugal, Spain and Ukraine. The article
proposes an integrated analytical framework for evaluating FoodTech -driven
transformation in wine tourism. It confirms that the long-term competitiveness
of wine-producing territories increasingly depends on the ability to combine
technological innovation, sustainability principles, local identity and visitor -
centred value creation.
Keywords: FoodTech, ESG, wine tourism, craft winemaking,
innovations, digital transformation, sustainability, artificial intelligence,
destination competitiveness.

Analysis of recent research and publications

Recent scholarship demonstrates a profound
transformation in the conceptualization of wine tourism.
Rather than being regarded merely as a niche tourism
product, wine tourism is increasingly understood as a
multidimensional development mechanism capable of
generating economic value, preserving cultural heritage,
supporting environmental sustainability and facilitating
digital transformation. Contemporary academic
discourse therefore positions wine tourism at the
intersection of sustainable destination management,
innovation ecosystems and ESG-oriented governance.
A substantial body of research investigates the
relationship between wine tourism and sustainability -
driven innovation within the wine industry. Studies by
Toribio-Gómez [1], Gazzola [2], Martínez -Falcó [3; 4]
and García -Cortijo et al. [5] demonstrate that
sustainability has become a strategic determinant of
competitiveness and long -term organizational
resilience. These authors argue that the growing demand
for environmentally responsible tourism experiences
stimulates wineries to ad opt green technologies,
improve resource efficiency, implement sustainability
reporting systems and integrate circular economy
principles into business operations. From this
perspective, wine tourism functions not only as a
tourism activity but also as a catalyst for environmental
modernization and responsible business transformation.
Another important research stream focuses on
the territorial and socio -cultural dimensions of wine
tourism development. Jezierska-Thöle [6], Pechko [16],
Nahurna and Kurylenko [17] emphasize the capacity of
wine tourism to preserve cultural landscapes, strengthen
regional identity and diversify local economies. Their
findings suggest that wine routes and wine -related
experiences contribute to heritage conservation while
simultaneously creating new opportunities for
investment attraction, destination branding and
community development. Such studies reinforce the
notion that wine tourism represents an effective
mechanism for balancing economic growth with the
protection of cultural and environmental assets.
Consumer behavior and value creation have also
become central themes within contemporary wine
tourism research. Dias [7] demonstrates that
environmental awareness increasingly influences
tourists’ destination preferences, partic ipation in wine -
related activities and perceptions of winery brands.
Similar conclusions are presented by Liu, Thanh and
Fang [8], who highlight the role of transparency,
authenticity and responsible business practices in
building consumer trust and enhanc ing brand value.
Collectively, these studies indicate that sustainability -
related attributes have evolved from optional marketing
instruments into critical determinants of consumer
decision-making and destination competitiveness.
The rapid digitalization o f tourism and
hospitality systems has generated another influential
research direction. Trigo [9], Festa [10], Castillo et al.
[11], Kim et al. [12] and Kalinichenko, Hrybinyk and
Avriata [18] explore how digital technologies reshape
tourism experiences an d organizational management
processes. Their findings demonstrate that artificial
intelligence, big data analytics, digital platforms,
service automation and advanced customer interfaces
improve operational efficiency, facilitate visitor
management and sup port more personalized tourism
experiences. Digital transformation is therefore
increasingly viewed as an essential prerequisite for the
sustainable development of wine tourism destinations
and hospitality ecosystems.
The convergence of sustainability and digital
transformation is particularly evident within the
emerging literature on ESG implementation. Wang et al.
[13], Kim et al. [12] and Martínez -Falcó [3] argue that
ESG principles generate the greatest value when
embedded within organizational strategy  rather than
implemented as isolated initiatives. These studies reveal
that the integration of environmental responsibility,
social engagement and transparent governance
enhances adaptive capacity, strengthens stakeholder
relationships and contributes to l ong-term
competitiveness. Consequently, ESG orientation has
become an increasingly important analytical framework
for evaluating the future development trajectories of
tourism and hospitality organizations.

The purpose of this study  is to investigate the

The analysis draws upon an extensive review of

Five European countries were selected for

as mature wine tourism destination s with established
innovation ecosystems and Ukraine as an emerging
destination undergoing significant regulatory and
technological transformation. The selection was based
on their representativeness of different development
models within the European context.
To enable structured comparison, a five -
dimensional indicator framework was developed based
on recurrent themes in the literature. The dimensions
include:
1. Smart Terroir Management,
2. Alternative Vinification Technologies,
3. Wine Traceability and Authenticity,
4. ESG Integration in Wineries,
5. Wine Tourism Product Innovation.
Each indicator was evaluated using a
standardised 10-point scale (1 = very low development;
10 = highly advanced/mature implementation). The
scores were assigned through comparative evidence
synthesis rather than statistical measurement. For each
country, the evaluation was based on the documented
level of implementation, consistency of adoption and
institutional maturity identified across multiple
independent sour ces, including peer -reviewed
publications, international reports and official policy
documents. Individual scores reflected the cumulative
weight of available evidence rather than isolated
examples or individual initiatives. The same evaluation
criteria were applied consistently across all countries to
ensure methodological comparability and transparency.
France was chosen as the benchmarking reference due
to its internationally recognized leadership in precision
viticulture, terroir protection, traceabilit y systems and
premium wine tourism.
To ensure transparency and reproducibility, a
structured scoring rubric was applied. Each indicator
was evaluated according to the following descriptors,
with France used as the international benchmark (10
points):
1–3 ( Very low): Negligible or fragmented
implementation with minimal institutional support.
4–5 (Low/Emerging): Initial policy frameworks
and pilot projects exist, but adoption remains limited in
scale and systemic integration (e.g., Ukraine’s
environmental ESG  component scored 4.5, reflecting
recent regulatory alignment and emerging initiatives,
while still facing constraints in resource efficiency and
decarbonisation).
6–7 (Moderate): Systematic adoption in key
regions with moderate integration between production
and tourism.
8–9 (High): Advanced implementation with
strong stakeholder coordination and measurable
outcomes.
10 (Mature/Leading): Comprehensive ecosystem
integration representing global best practice.
Composite scores for overall FoodTech integration
and ESG maturity were calculated as the arithmetic mean
of the respective indicators. This methodological approach
does not claim statistical precision or generalisability but
aims to provide a transparent, replicable visualization of
relative maturity levels and strategic differences across the
selected countries.

Results of the research and their discussion

The modern development of wine tourism is
inseparable from the evolution of modern winemaking.
While wine routes, tasting experiences and destination
branding remain visible components of tourism
products, the primary motivation for visiting wine
regions c ontinues to be the wine itself. The quality,
authenticity and uniqueness of wine determine the
attractiveness of vineyards, wineries and wine
destinations. Consequently, FoodTech innovations
acquire strategic importance because they directly
influence the characteristics of wines that form the
foundation of wine tourism experiences.
From a theoretical perspective, FoodTech
infrastructure can be defined as a multidimensional
technological environment consisting of digital
platforms, intelligent monitoring sy stems, data -driven
management tools and innovation -based service
solutions that facilitate the efficient production,
distribution and consumption of food and beverage
products. Within wine tourism, this infrastructure
extends beyond the physical boundaries of wineries and
encompasses the entire visitor experience, from
vineyard cultivation and wine production to
gastronomic interpretation, destination marketing and
post-visit consumer interaction [3; 4; 11].
The emergence of FoodTech  infrastructure
reflects broader transformations associated with the
Fourth Industrial Revolution and the digitalization of
agri-food systems. Contemporary wine tourism
increasingly operates within the framework of
Agriculture 4.0 and Tourism 4.0, where re al-time data
collection, predictive analytics, artificial intelligence
and interconnected digital platforms support decision -
making processes at multiple organizational levels.
Consequently, wineries are evolving from traditional
agricultural enterprises i nto technologically
sophisticated organizations capable of generating value
through both physical products and digital experiences.

In modern viticulture and enology, technological
innovation is increasingly focused on preserving and
enhancing terroir expr ession rather than replacing
traditional winemaking practices. Terroir remains one
of the most influential concepts in global wine culture,
referring to the interaction of soil composition, climate
conditions, topography, biodiversity and human
expertise t hat collectively shape wine identity.
Contemporary FoodTech solutions allow winemakers to
better understand these interactions and to preserve
regional distinctiveness while improving production
efficiency and environmental sustainability [10; 28]
The rela tionship between terroir and tourism is
particularly evident in Europe's leading wine regions.
Visitors travel to Bordeaux not simply to consume wine
but to experience the specific environmental and
cultural conditions that produce Cabernet Sauvignon
and M erlot-based blends. Similarly, tourists visiting
Tuscany seek wines derived from Sangiovese grapes
because these wines represent centuries of interaction
between local landscapes, agricultural traditions and
regional identity. In Rioja, Tempranillo serves not only
as a grape variety but also as a cultural symbol
embedded in the tourism image of the region.
Comparable relationships can be observed in Portugal's
Douro Valley through Touriga Nacional and in
Ukrainian wine regions through Odesa Black and Telti
Kuruk [20; 21; 41].
The growing emphasis on terroir authenticity has
encouraged wineries to adopt technologies that support
more precise vineyard management and grape quality
assessment. FoodTech systems enable producers to
monitor vineyard conditions thro ughout the growing
season and identify factors influencing grape
development. Such technologies contribute to greater
consistency in fruit quality and allow wineries to
produce wines that more accurately reflect local
environmental conditions. As a result,  wine tourists
encounter products with stronger geographical identity
and more distinctive sensory characteristics.
The preservation and promotion of indigenous
grape varieties represent another important dimension
of FoodTech-driven wine tourism development. Global
wine markets have long been dominated by international
cultivars such as Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot,
Chardonnay and Sauvignon Blanc. However,
contemporary consumers increasingly seek wines
associated with regional uniqueness and cultural
authenticity. This trend has stimulated renewed interest
in local grape varieties that embody specific territorial
identities.
In France, varieties such as Pinot Noir, Cabernet
Franc and Chenin Blanc remain closely linked to the
cultural landscapes of Burgundy an d the Loire Valley.
Italy continues to strengthen its international reputation
through Sangiovese, Nebbiolo and Nero d'Avola. Spain
increasingly promotes Tempranillo, Garnacha and
Albariño as distinctive components of regional wine
tourism experiences. Por tugal has successfully
positioned Touriga Nacional, Tinta Roriz and Alvarinho
within premium wine tourism markets. Ukraine
likewise possesses significant potential through
indigenous and locally adapted varieties including
Odesa Black, Sukholymanskyi White  and Telti Kuruk
[25].
FoodTech innovations facilitate the preservation
of these varieties through advanced genetic analysis,
vineyard monitoring systems and climate adaptation
strategies. This is particularly important under
contemporary climate change co nditions, where many
traditional wine regions face increasing environmental
pressures. Technologies supporting varietal adaptation
contribute not only to agricultural resilience but also to
the preservation of cultural and tourism resources that
differentiate wine destinations from global competitors.
Modern winemaking technologies have also
transformed the fermentation stage, which remains one
of the most critical processes influencing wine quality.
Fermentation management systems enable precise
control of temperature, oxygen exposure and microbial
activity. Such control contributes to greater aromatic
complexity, improved flavour stability and enhanced
consistency across vintages. Rather than standardizing
wine styles, contemporary enological technologies
increasingly support the expression of terroir -specific
characteristics by minimizing production faults and
preserving desirable sensory attributes [19; 22].
Biotechnological innovations have further
expanded opportunities for quality enhancement.
Research on indigenous yeast populations has
demonstrated that microbial communities play a
significant role in shaping regional wine identity. The
concept of microbial terroir has emerged as an important
area of contemporary wine science, suggesting that
naturally occurring microorganisms contribute to the
distinctive sensory profiles associated with specific
wine regions. For wine tourism destinations, this
scientific perspective strengthens authenticity narratives
by providing evidence that local environmental
conditions influence wine characteristics at multiple
biological levels [6; 33]
The growing popularity of organic, biodynamic
and low -intervention wines represents another major

development. Contemporary wine tourists increasingly
associate wine quality with environmental responsibility
and ethical production practices. Consequently, wineries
are adopting innovative technologies that reduce
chemical inputs, optimize resource consumption and
support biodiversity conservation.
Organic and biodynamic wines have become

sustainability forms part of destination branding
strategies. Wine tourists often perceive environmentally
responsible wineries as more aut hentic and culturally

closely linked to the protection of geographical origin

wine tourists, this creates opportunities to experience
wines that embody highly localized environmental

Consequently, FoodTech contributes not only to
production quality but also to the interpretation and

winery visits. The result is a tourism product where
technological transparency reinforces perceptions of
exclusivity and authenticity [29].
