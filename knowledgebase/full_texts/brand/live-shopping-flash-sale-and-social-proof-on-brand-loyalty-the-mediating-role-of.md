Abstract
This study examines how three customer experience stimuli in TikTok Live Shopping, namely Live
Shopping, Flash Sale, and Social Proof, relate to Brand Loyalty for facial wash products, with
Perceived Value and Customer Satisfaction as sequential mediators. Survey data from 321 TikTok users
in Bali, Indonesia, who had bought facial wash through Live Shopping were analysed with partial least
squares structural equation modelling (PLS -SEM), using bias -corrected and accelerated bootstrap
confidence intervals as the decision criterion. Live Shopping (β = 0.470) and Flash Sale (β = 0.284)
were positively associated with Perceived Value, whereas Social Proof was not. Perceived Value was
the strongest predictor of Customer Satisfaction (β = 0.467), and Customer Sati sfaction the strongest
predictor of Brand Loyalty (β = 0.528). None of the three stimuli had a significant direct path to Brand
Loyalty. The associations of Live Shopping and Flash Sale with Brand Loyalty ran through indirect
routes, including the serial P erceived Value–Customer Satisfaction route (β = 0.116 and β = 0.070),
while Social Proof reached Brand Loyalty only through a small path via Customer Satisfaction and its
total indirect effect was not significant. The results held after excluding responden ts who named more
than one brand and after controlling for reference -brand differences. Because the design is cross -
sectional, the findings describe a pattern consistent with staged loyalty formation rather than proof of
causality. In practice, live selling and flash sales appear to support loyalty mainly when they strengthen
customers' value judgements and satisfaction.

1. INTRODUCTION
Live commerce has changed the way Indonesian consumers find and buy everyday products. On
TikTok, the Live Shopping feature lets viewers watch a product being demonstrated in real time, put
questions to the seller, and check out without leaving the broadca st  (Fortuna et al., 2025;
Wongkitrungrueng & Assarut, 2020). Sellers rarely rely on the broadcast alone. Limited-time price cuts
(Flash Sale) and visible signs of what other buyers have chosen, such as purchase counters, ratings, and
reviews (Social Proof), are usually added to the same session. Personal care prod ucts suit this format
because their use can be shown on camera, and facial wash is a useful test case: it is bought repeatedly,
switching costs are low, and a customer who is satisfied today can move to a rival brand with a single
tap. Whether the experience created during a live session turns into lasting loyalty is therefore a practical
question for sellers as much as a theoretical one.
Research on live commerce has concentrated on purchase intention, impulse buying, and trust as
outcomes (Guo et al., 2021; Hossain et al., 2023; Sun et al., 2023). Fewer studies follow the process as
far as loyalty, and most of those test direct effects or a single mediator. Ye et al. (2023)  are an exception:
they show that loyalty in e -commerce livestreaming develops through a chain running from customer
engagement to value co-creation. A chain built on the cognitive-affective sequence in which Perceived
Value informs Customer Satisfaction, which in turn feeds Brand Loyalty, has not been tested for a
single, narrowly defined product category in Indonesian live commerce. It is also unclear whether the
three stimuli that sellers combine in one session work through the same route. Preliminary conversations
with consumers in Bali suggested that their experience of Live Shopping, Flash Sale, and Social Proof
was generally positive; the open question is how, and for which stimuli, such experiences become
loyalty.
This study uses the Stimulus -Organism-Response (S -O-R) framework (Mehrabian & Russell,
1974)  to model Live Shopping, Flash Sale, and Social Proof as stimuli, Perceived Value and Customer
Satisfaction as organism states, and Brand Loyalty as the response. It has two objectives: (1) to examine
the direct relationships of the three stimuli with Perc eived Value, Customer Satisfaction, and Brand
Loyalty; and (2) to test the single and serial mediating roles of Perceived Value and Customer
Satisfaction. The theoretical contribution lies in testing a value -then-satisfaction chain, which differs
from the engagement and co-creation chain examined by Ye et al. (2023), and in comparing how three
concurrent stimuli use that chain. The practical contribution is guidance on which live-commerce tools
deserve priority when the goal is repeat purchase rather than a single sale.
Bali Province was chosen as a bounded regional market in which verified TikTok Live Shopping
buyers of facial wash could be reached, and where the tropical climate makes daily facial cleansing a
routine need. Confining the study to one province keeps regional market conditions constant, although
it also limits how far the results can be generalised, a point taken up in the Conclusion.

2. LITERATURE REVIEW
The S-O-R framework holds that features of an environment (stimuli) alter a person's internal
states (organism), which then shape approach or avoidance behaviour (response) (Mehrabian & Russell,
1974). Live-commerce research has adopted the framework widely because a broadcast packs several
environmental cues into one shopping episode (Aprianingsih et al., 2025; Guo et al., 2021; Hossain et
al., 2023). In the present model the organism is represented by one cognitive state, Perceived Value,
and one evaluative state, Customer Satisfaction.
2.1. Live Shopping, Flash Sale, and Social Proof
Live Shopping refers to selling through a real -time broadcast in which the seller demonstrates
products and responds to viewers while they watch. Its defining features are interactivity and a shared
viewing experience, which build trust in the seller and engagement with the broadcast (Aprianingsih et
al., 2025; Wongkitrungrueng & Assarut, 2020) . Because viewers can see the product in use and ask
about it directly, live streaming also reduces uncertainty and raises the overall value that consumers
attach to the offer (Guo et al., 2021).
Flash Sale is a price promotion bounded by time, quantity, or both. Scarcity messages make an
offer look more desirable, partly because consumers infer that others are competing for the same limited
stock (Aggarwal et al., 2011) . The time pressure that comes with such offers can raise perceived value
and speed up the decision to buy (Sun et al., 2023) , but it can also increase post-purchase regret when
the decision was rushed (Marjerison et al., 2022).
Social Proof describes the tendency to treat what others do as evidence of what is appropriate or
worthwhile. Cialdini (2007) shows that descriptive norms, that is, information about what other people
actually do, steer behaviour more strongly than people usually recognise. In online shops this
information appears as reviews, ratings, and counts of buyers or viewers. Experimenta l evidence
indicates that such cues can nudge purchase, with product reviews outperforming other formats (Park
& McCallister, 2023), and survey evidence from Indonesia links social proof to purchasing decisions
(Sulistiyani, 2025) . The effect is not uniform: in live commerce, the influence of social value on
purchase intention depends partly on streamer popularity (Zhang et al., 2024).
The three stimuli were chosen because a single TikTok live session typically combines all of
them, yet each appeals to a different mechanism: interpersonal interaction, a time -bound economic
incentive, and observational social validation. Testing them together allows their relative contributions
to be compared within one model.
2.2. Perceived Value, Customer Satisfaction, and Brand Loyalty
Perceived Value  is the consumer's overall judgement of a product's utility, based on what is
received relative to what is given up (Zeithaml, 1988) . Customer Satisfaction  is the evaluation of
whether the consumption experience met or exceeded prior expectations, in line with the expectancy -
confirmation view (Oliver, 1980); in live-streaming shopping, confirmed expectations likewise shape
satisfaction and the intention to keep buying (Ko & Ho, 2024) . Brand Loyalty is a commitment to
repurchase and recommend a brand consistently (Ganesh et al., 2000; Oliver, 1999) . Oliver (1999)
argues that loyalty develops in stages, moving from cognitive to affective, conative, and finally action
loyalty, so external cues are expected to affect loyalty mainly through the beliefs and feelings they
produce rather than directly.
Empirical work in digital commerce supports this sequence. In live commerce, economic value
raises satisfaction, and satisfaction and trust in turn predict loyalty (Kim et al., 2023). Perceived value
in live -streaming e -commerce increases trust and online shopping satisfaction, which then carry its
effect to continued purchase intention (Hameed et al., 2025), and a comparable value-trust-repurchase

category.
2.3. Hypotheses Development
Live Shopping gives viewers information that a static product page cannot. The product can be
seen in use, questions are answered on the spot, and claims can be checked against the demonstration.
This richer information improves the consumer's assessment o f what will be received and lowers
perceived uncertainty, which raises perceived value (Guo et al., 2021; Wongkitrungrueng & Assarut,
2020) .
H1: Live Shopping has a positive effect on Perceived Value.
A Flash Sale lowers the price for a limited period, which changes the sacrifice side of the value
judgement directly, while the scarcity framing makes the offer appear more desirable (Aggarwal et al.,
2011). Time pressure has been shown to operate partly through perceived value (Sun et al., 2023).
H2: Flash Sale has a positive effect on Perceived Value.
Social Proof tells consumers that others have bought or approved of a product, and this
information can act as a quality signal that raises perceived value (Cialdini, 2007; Sulistiyani, 2025) .
Because the signal concerns other people's behaviour rather than the product's own attributes or price,
its contribution to a personal value judgement may be weaker than that of the other two stimuli; the
hypothesis is nonetheless stated in the direction implied by theory.
H3: Social Proof has a positive effect on Perceived Value.
Satisfaction reflects the whole shopping experience, not only the value of the product obtained.
Interaction with the host and the shared atmosphere of a live session can make the experience itself
pleasant, and confirmation of what was shown during the broadcast supports satisfaction after purchase
(Aprianingsih et al., 2025; Ko & Ho, 2024).
H4: Live Shopping has a positive effect on Customer Satisfaction.
Economic value obtained through a price promotion contributes to satisfaction in live commerce
(Kim et al., 2023) . Decisions taken under time pressure, however, may be followed by regret
(Marjerison et al., 2022) , so the net relationship is an empirical question; the hypothesis follows the
direction supported by value-based evidence.
H5: Flash Sale has a positive effect on Customer Satisfaction.
Seeing that many other buyers are content with a product can reassure a consumer that the choice
was sound, which supports a favourable post-purchase evaluation (Cialdini, 2007; Park & McCallister,
2023).
H6: Social Proof has a positive effect on Customer Satisfaction.
Under the expectancy -confirmation view, satisfaction arises when what is received matches or
exceeds what was expected (Oliver, 1980). A favourable value judgement is therefore a direct input
into satisfaction, a relationship also observed in live-streaming e-commerce (Hameed et al., 2025; Kim
et al., 2023).
H7: Perceived Value has a positive effect on Customer Satisfaction.

If loyalty forms in stages (Oliver, 1999), the stimuli encountered during a live session should
shape loyalty mainly through the value and satisfaction they generate. Direct paths from the stimuli to
Brand Loyalty are still specified, because live selling can also build a relationship with the se ller or
brand that does not pass through a product evaluation (Hossain et al., 2023; Wongkitrungrueng &
Assarut, 2020). Testing these paths makes it possible to distinguish full from partial mediation.
H8: Live Shopping has a positive effect on Brand Loyalty.
H9: Flash Sale has a positive effect on Brand Loyalty.
H10: Social Proof has a positive effect on Brand Loyalty.
Consumers who believe that a brand offers good value have a reason to return to it. Perceived
value has been linked to repurchase intention in TikTok live streaming (Fortuna et al., 2025)  and to
loyalty on digital platforms (Yum & Kim, 2024).
H11: Perceived Value has a positive effect on Brand Loyalty.
Repeated satisfaction is the main route through which affective loyalty develops (Oliver, 1999),
and satisfaction predicts loyalty both in live commerce (Kim et al., 2023) and in Indonesian e-commerce
(Tanlim & Ruslim, 2024).
H12: Customer Satisfaction has a positive effect on Brand Loyalty.
Taken together, these arguments imply that Perceived Value transmits part of the stimuli's
influence to Customer Satisfaction, that Customer Satisfaction transmits part of their influence to Brand
Loyalty, and that the two do so in sequence. Perceived Valu e is placed before Customer Satisfaction
because a judgement about what was received logically precedes an evaluation of whether expectations
were met (Hameed et al., 2025; Oliver, 1980) . The indirect route from each stimulus through Perceived
Value directly to Brand Loyalty, bypassing satisfaction, is not hypothesised but is reported as a
supplementary result to check this ordering.
H13: Perceived Value mediates the effects of (a) Live Shopping, (b) Flash Sale, and (c) Social Proof on
Customer Satisfaction.
H14: Customer Satisfaction mediates the effects of (a) Live Shopping, (b) Flash Sale, and (c) Social
Proof on Brand Loyalty.
H15: Perceived Value and Customer Satisfaction serially mediate the effects of (a) Live Shopping, (b)
Flash Sale, and (c) Social Proof on Brand Loyalty.
3. RESEARCH METHOD
The study used a quantitative, explanatory survey design. The population comprised TikTok users
living in Bali Province who had bought a facial wash product through TikTok Live Shopping. Purposive
sampling was applied: two screening questions confirmed act ive TikTok use and at least one such
purchase, and only respondents who met both criteria and lived in Bali could continue. The online
questionnaire was open from 8 to 12 May 2026 and produced 321 usable responses. Its opening page
explained the academic purpose, guaranteed confidentiality, and stated that participation was voluntary;
continuing to the questions constituted consent.
Purchases were made from many different sellers, so no single brand could be imposed. Each
respondent named the facial wash brand last bought through Live Shopping and used it as the reference
for the Perceived Value, Customer Satisfaction, and Brand Loyal ty items. This respondent-nominated
approach matches the unit of analysis, which is the consumer's experience of the live-commerce format
within one product category rather than the equity of a particular brand, and it avoids asking respondents
to rate bra nds they have never bought. The resulting brand heterogeneity was examined empirically
(see Hypothesis Testing and Robustness Checks). Thirty -five respondents (10.9%) named more than
one brand; they were kept in the main analysis and excluded in a robustness check.

All items used a five -point Likert scale. Live Shopping (four items) was adapted from
Aprianingsih et al. (2025), Flash Sale (six items) from Aggarwal et al. (2011), and Social Proof (seven
items) from Sulistiyani (2025) . Perceived Value (four items) was developed from Zeithaml (1988)
give-versus-get conception. Customer Satisfaction (three items covering expectation fulfilment,
intention to reuse, and willingness to recommend) followed the expectancy-confirmation view (Oliver,
1980) , and Brand Loyalty (five items) was adapted from Ganesh et al. (2000).
The data were analysed with PLS-SEM in SmartPLS 4, which suits the study's predictive aim and
a model with twelve structural paths and does not require multivariate normality (Hair et al., 2021). The
measurement model was assessed through indicator loadings, Cronbach's alpha, composite reliability
(ρc), average variance extracted (AVE), the Fornell -Larcker criterion, and the heterotrait -monotrait
ratio (HTMT) with bootstrap confidence intervals (Henseler et al., 2015) . The structural model was
assessed through inner VIF, R² and adjusted R², f², and Q²predict from PLSpredict. Significance was
tested by bootstrapping with 5,000 resamples. Because the sampling distribution of indirect effects is
usually skewed, the 95% bias-corrected and accelerated (BCa) confidence interval was adopted as the
decision criterion for every path: an effect is judged significant when its interval excludes zero (Hair et
al., 2021), t-values and p-values are reported alongside for completeness.
Common method bias was addressed procedurally by assuring confidentiality and by presenting
each construct in a separately labelled section. Harman's single-factor test on the 26 retained indicators
showed that the first unrotated factor explained 43.6% of  the variance, below the 50% benchmark.
Because this test is a limited diagnostic (Podsakoff et al., 2003), its result is read only as showing that
no single method factor dominates the data.

4.4. Discussion
The central result is that none of the three stimuli is related to Brand Loyalty directly once
Perceived Value and Customer Satisfaction are in the model, whereas Live Shopping and Flash Sale
are related to it through indirect routes. This pattern matches Oliver (1999) account of loyalty as the
outcome of accumulated evaluations rather than a reaction to a single cue. It also resembles the chain
structure reported by Ye et al. (2023), although the mediators here are value and satisfaction rather than
engagement and co-creation, and it agrees with evidence from live commerce that satisfaction, fed by
economic value, is the step that precedes loyalty (Kim et al., 2023). Because the data were collected at
one point in time, these indirect effects describe associations that are consistent with a staged process;
they do not establish the temporal order of the stages.
Live Shopping is the most consistent stimulus. It is related to both organism states and reaches
Brand Loyalty through all three indirect routes, while its direct path is not significant, which
corresponds to full, indirect -only mediation spread across sev eral mechanisms. The interaction and
demonstration that define a live session appear to improve both the value judgement and the enjoyment
of the experience itself, in line with studies that link live -stream interactivity to trust and perceived
value (Aprianingsih et al., 2025; Guo et al., 2021) . The route through satisfaction alone suggests that
part of Live Shopping's contribution comes from the experience rather than from any change in how
the product is valued.
Flash Sale is related to Perceived Value but not directly to Customer Satisfaction, and its link to
Brand Loyalty passes only through routes that begin with Perceived Value. A price incentive therefore
seems to matter for loyalty only to the extent that it  improves the customer's sense of having obtained
a good deal, which agrees with evidence that time pressure acts through perceived value (Sun et al.,
2023). The missing direct link to satisfaction may reflect the other side of time-limited offers: decisions
made quickly can be followed by regret (Marjerison et al., 2022), which would weaken satisfaction with
the purchase experience even when the price is attractive. Because the direct Flash Sale → Brand
Loyalty path lies close to the significance threshold in every specification, the safer reading is that Flash
Sale's association with loyalty is predominantly, though not necessarily exclusively, indirect.
Social Proof shows the most limited pattern. It is unrelated to Perceived Value, has a modest
direct relationship with Customer Satisfaction, and reaches Brand Loyalty only through that satisfaction
path, while its total indirect association with loyalty i s not significant. This differs from findings in
which social value was a significant driver of purchase intention in live commerce (Zhang et al., 2024).
Three explanations are offered here as interpretations rather than tested findings. First, follower counts,
viewer numbers, and purchase counters appear in every TikTok broadcast, so they may no longer
distinguish one offer from another. Second, consumer s are widely aware that such metrics can be
inflated, which may lead them to discount social signals when judging value. Third, the results of a
facial wash depend on the individual's skin, so the fact that many other people bought a product says

purchase, which would explain why Social Proof is related to satisfaction but not to value.
The supplementary routes help to evaluate the ordering of the two organism states. Perceived
Value is linked to Brand Loyalty both directly and through Customer Satisfaction, and for Live
Shopping and Flash Sale the routes involving satisfaction carry larg er estimates than the route that
bypasses it. Value judgements therefore contribute to loyalty on their own, but a substantial part of their
contribution is channelled through satisfaction. This supports placing Perceived Value before Customer
Satisfaction without implying that satisfaction is the only channel.
4.5. Novelty and Implications
The study adds three points to live-commerce research. First, it identifies a value-then-satisfaction
chain behind loyalty within a single product category, complementing the engagement and co-creation
chain reported by Ye et al. (2023). Second, it shows that three stimuli offered in the same live session
do not share one mechanism: Live Shopping works through several routes, Flash Sale through value,
and Social Proof only weakly through satisfaction. Third, it shows that one mediation label does not fit
every stimulus in the same model, which argues for reporting mediation stimulus by stimulus.
For practitioners, the results suggest that facial wash sellers on TikTok should give priority to the
quality of the live interaction, such as clear demonstration, responsive answers, and honest claims,
because it is the stimulus most consistently associated with value, satisfaction, and, indirectly, loyalty.
Flash sales are more likely to support loyalty when they are accompanied by information that helps
customers see why the product is worth its price, rather than by urgency alone. Social proof metrics are
better used to reassure buyers after purchase than as the main argument for value, and small sellers with
limited budgets gain more from improving the content and conduct of the broadcast than from inflating
visible engagement figures. These recommendations rest on associations observed at one point in time
and should be tested through field experiments before large budget shifts are made.
5. CONCLUSION
Among TikTok Live Shopping buyers of facial wash in Bali, Live Shopping and Flash Sale are
positively associated with Perceived Value, whereas Social Proof is not. Perceived Value is the main
correlate of Customer Satisfaction, and Customer Satisfaction is  the main correlate of Brand Loyalty.
None of the three stimuli is directly associated with Brand Loyalty. Live Shopping is linked to loyalty
through several indirect routes, Flash Sale through routes that begin with Perceived Value, and Social
Proof only through a weak path via Customer Satisfaction. These findings support a staged view of
loyalty in which value judgements and satisfaction connect what happens during a live session with
continued commitment to a brand.
The study has several limitations. Its cross -sectional design cannot establish the temporal or
causal order of the relationships. It covers one platform, one product category, and one province, and
its sample was drawn purposively. Respondents evaluated br ands of their own choosing; the checks
indicated that brand differences did not explain the results, but brand-level factors such as price tier and
prior familiarity were not measured. Customer Satisfaction and Brand Loyalty are empirically close,
partly because two satisfaction items capture behavioural intentions. The direct link between Flash Sale
and Brand Loyalty and several small indirect effects lie near the significance threshold. Future research
should use longitudinal or experimental designs, comp are platforms and product categories, measure
satisfaction with items that exclude intention content, and model brand characteristics or consumer
involvement as moderators.
