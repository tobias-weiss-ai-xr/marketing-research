Abstract

KEYWORDS
regenerative branding; visual communication design; local wisdom; cultural identity

Introduction
Over the past few decades, the growth of the global creative industry has
fundamentally transformed how brands communicate with their audiences. Visual
identity no longer functions solely as a product identifier; it has evolved into a
medium for constructi ng meaning, articulating values, and building narratives.
Through visual communication design, brands convey stories about product origins,
corporate philosophy, and the cultural values they wish to project to the public. This
Visual communication design has become a strategic medium through which
brands construct meaning, identity, and cultural narratives. Amid growing
globalization, local cultural elements are often reduced to decorative symbols
detached from their social and cultural significance. This study examines how
visual communication design represents and communicates local wisdom within
regenerative branding practices of Indonesian brands. While previous studies
have explored regenerative design in architecture and pr oduct design, and
branding research has focused on cultural identity and heritage, limited attention
has been given to visual communication design as a regenerative branding
strategy through local wisdom representation. This study addresses this gap
through a qualitative comparative case study of three Indonesian brands across
different sectors: SukkhaCitta (sustainable fashion), Javara (local food), and
Utama Spice (natural body care). Data were collected through non -participatory
observation of 24 visual communication items from official websites, product
packaging, and social media platforms pu blished between January and March
2026, analyzed using a descriptive -interpretive visual analysis framework. The
findings reveal that each brand represents local wisdom through a distinct visual
approach: SukkhaCitta  emphasizes community -based production through
artisan and textile imagery; Javara recontextualizes local food knowledge
through biodiversity and indigenous ingredient narratives; and Utama Spice
conveys philosophical values through botanical and sensory v isuals rooted in
Balinese cultural principles. These findings demonstrate that visual
communication design functions beyond aesthetic representation, serving as a
medium for regenerating cultural knowledge, practices, and values. Regenerative
branding amon g Indonesian brands operates across three dimensions —
practice-based, system -based, and value -based — each communicated
contextually through visual design, affirming its strategic potential in sustaining
local knowledge systems within the global marketplace.

has been demonstrated across various contexts, including
how visual content on social media can reinforce locally
grounded branding in the tourism sector (Hanindharputri &

merely as an aesthetic element but as a visual
communication system capable of shaping audience
perceptions and experiences of a brand (Prawesti et al.,
2025).
However, amid intensifying globalization, many brand
identities have shifted toward increasing homogeneity.
Uniform visual aesthetics, globally oriented design
conventions, and communication strategies modeled on
international trends have contributed to th e growing
standardization of brand communication. While such
approaches may enhance market recognition, they risk
flattening cultural distinctiveness and marginalizing local
identities within brand communication. Recent studies
emphasize that integrating cultural identity into branding is
increasingly important for maintaining authenticity,
differentiation, and long -term sustainability in globalized
markets (Putra et al., 2025). Likewise, culturally
responsive visual identities have been shown to
strengthen brand relevance by reflecting local cultural
contexts rather than relying solely on universal design
conventions (Mohamed, 2025 b).
This issue is particularly relevant in Indonesia, where
cultural diversity and local knowledge are frequently
embedded in branding practices. Heritage branding
research shows that cultural elements are commonly
used to enhance differentiation and consumer
engagement (Chen & Chou, 2025 ). However, limited
attention has been given to how visual communication
design may contribute not only to representation but
also to the regeneration of cultural knowledge and
practices. As a result, cultural elements are often
reduced to visual decoration  detached from their socio -
cultural contexts.
Local wisdom, as a system of cultural values and
practices, plays an important role in identity formation
and community empowerment (Zulhuda et al., 2025 ).
Yet in branding practice, it is often fragmented from its
original knowledge systems, risking superficial symbolic
use rather than meaningful cultural continuity (Maryam &
Budiwaty, 2025 ). This highlights the need to examine
how visual communication design can support cultural
regeneration beyond symbolic representation.
Regenerative design offers a useful lens for this
discussion. Unlike conventional sustainability
approaches that emphasize maintenance, regenerative
approaches focus on restoring interconnected
ecological, social, and cultural systems (Macesar, n.d.).
Within this perspective, branding can be understood as
a relational system connecting products, communities,
and knowledge systems, where brands increasingly
communicate social and environmental values  (Golob et
al., 2022).
Visual communication design occupies a particularly
important role within this process because it translates
abstract cultural values into forms that can be
recognized, interpreted, and experienced by broader
audiences (Afdhal et al., 2023 ). Visual identities are not
culturally neutral; they are shaped by social and cultural
contexts, and culturally grounded visual communication
can enhance audience engagement, recognition, and
perceived authenticity (Mohamed, 2025 a). From this
perspective, regenerative branding may be understood
as a branding approach that contributes to the renewal
of social, ecological, and cultural relationships through
the creation and communication of meaningful
narratives, values, and practices. Rather than functioning
solely as a marketing instrument, the brand becomes a
medium through which cultural knowledge, community
identities, and local wisdom can be sustained, revitalized,
and transmitted across generatio ns.
This perspective is reflected in several Indonesian
brands that actively position local wisdom as a central
component of their brand identity and communication
strategies. This study focuses on three cases:
SukkhaCitta (fashion), Javara (local food products), and
Utama Spice (wellness products). These brands were
selected through purposive sampling based on three
criteria: (1) the explicit integration of local cultural
knowledge and traditions into their branding narratives;
(2) th e consistent use of visual communication to
represent local values across digital and physical media;
and (3) recognition of cultural sustainability and
community-based practices as core aspects of their
organizational identity. Although operating in diffe rent
sectors, all three brands provide information -rich cases
for examining how visual communication design
represents local wisdom within regenerative branding
practices.
Despite growing interest in sustainability and cultural
branding, academic discussion at the intersection of
regenerative design and branding remains limited,
particularly within the context of visual communication
design. Existing regenerative design stud ies have
predominantly focused on architecture, environmental
design, and product development, while comparatively
little attention has been given to how visual
communication design may function as a regenerative
branding strategy. Furthermore, research ex ploring the
representation of local wisdom within regenerative
branding remains scarce, particularly in the Indonesian
creative industry context. This study addresses this gap
by examining how visual communication design
represents local wisdom and contrib utes to regenerative
branding practices through a comparative case study of
SukkhaCitta, Javara, and Utama Spice.
This study therefore, aims to analyze the role of visual
communication design in regenerative branding
practices. Specifically, this study asks: how do visual
communication design strategies represent local wisdom
as an active cultural instrument in Indone sian
regenerative branding? Using a qualitative case study
approach across three brands (Javara, n.d.; Uta, n.d.) ,
this study finds that visual communication design can
function beyond aesthetic representation, serving as an
active cultural instrument in revitalizing local wisdom
within contemporary branding practices.

Methods
Research Type
This study employs a qualitative approach using a
comparative multiple -case study design to examine how
local wisdom is represented through visual communication
design within regenerative branding practices. Rather than
measuring relationships between variables, the study seeks
to explore the meanings, narratives, and visual
representations constructed within brand communication.
Qualitative approaches are widely used in design and visual
culture research because they enable researchers to
interpret symboli c meanings embedded in visual artifacts
and understand their broader sociocultural contexts
(Sugiyono, 2013).

Population and Sample/Informants
The cases examined in this study consist of three
Indonesian brands selected through purposive sampling:
SukkhaCitta, Javara, and Utama Spice. The selection was
based on three criteria: (1) the explicit incorporation of
local cultural knowledge and traditions within brand
narratives; (2) the consistent use of visual communication
across digital and physical media to commu nicate these
narratives; and (3) publicly documented commitments to
community-based, environmentally responsible, or
culturally grounded practices. These characteristics
indicate that the selected brands provide information -rich
cases for examining how loc al wisdom is represented
through visual communication design within regenerative
branding contexts.
The three brands operate in different sectors, namely
sustainable fashion (SukkhaCitta), food products (Javara),
and natural body care (Utama Spice, n.d.) . This diversity
provides a comparative context for examining variations in
how local wisdom is visually represented and
communicated across different branding practices.

Research Location
This study was conducted within digital research
setting through the analysis of publicly accessible visual
communication materials produced by three Indonesian
brands: SukkhaCitta, Javara, and Utama Spice. Rather
than focusing on a specific geographical l ocation, the
study examines how these brands communicate visual
narratives across digital and physical communication
media. Data were collected between January and March
2026 from the brands’ official communication channels,
including official websites, official Instagram accounts, and
product packaging materials (Suparti et al., 2026 ). These
platforms were selected because they represent the
primary channels through which the brands communicate
their identities, products, and brand narratives to
audiences.
Only visual materials that were publicly accessible and
actively displayed during the observation period were
included in the analysis. Archived, deleted, or inaccessible
content was excluded. The final dataset consisted of 24
visual communication material s, comprising 10 materials
from SukkhaCitta, 7 from Javara, and 7 from Utama Spice.
The selected materials included website imagery,
Instagram posts, product packaging, and promotional visual
content that contained references relevant to branding
communication and local cultural representation.
By focusing on official communication channels, the
study seeks to examine visual representations that are
intentionally produced and disseminated by the brands
themselves. This digital research setting provides access to
contemporary branding practices while enabling cross-case
comparison across different industry sectors.

Instrumentation or Tools
Data were collected through systematic observation of
visual communication materials obtained from the official
communication channels of three Indonesian brands:
SukkhaCitta, Javara, and Utama Spice. The dataset
consisted of 24 visual materials collected between January
and March 2026, comprising 10 materials from SukkhaCitta,
7 from Javara, and 7 from Utama Spice.
The materials were obtained from three primary
sources: official websites, official Instagram accounts, and
product packaging. Website materials included homepage
visuals, product -related imagery, campaign visuals, and
brand storytelling content. Instagram  materials were
selected through purposive sampling based on their explicit
visual representation of local cultural elements, traditional
practices, community activities, indigenous resources, or
local value systems. Only posts published and publicly
accessible during the observation period were included.
Product packaging materials were obtained from product
images displayed on the brands’ official websites and online
promotional materials.
The selection of visual materials followed three criteria:
(1) the presence of visual or textual references to local
wisdom, cultural traditions, indigenous knowledge, or
community practices; (2) relevance to branding and visual
communication activities; a nd (3) accessibility through
official brand communication channels. Materials that
contained only product information without cultural or
branding narratives were excluded from the analysis. The
collected materials served as the primary research
instruments for identifying visual symbols, imagery,
narratives, and communication strategies related to the
representation of local wisdom within regenerative branding
practices.

Data Collection Procedures
Data collection was carried out through non -
participatory observation of each brand’s publicly available
visual communication materials. The observation focused
on identifying recurring visual elements, narrative themes,
and cultural references across offi cial websites, product
packaging, digital promotional content, and social media
platforms. All data sources were accessed openly without
requiring direct interaction with brand representatives or
informants.

Data Analysis
Data were analyzed using a descriptive -interpretive
visual analysis approach. The analysis was conducted in
two stages. First, each visual material was examined
descriptively by identifying its formal visual characteristics,
including imagery, composition, color, typography, symbols,
and visual storytelling elements. Second, the identified
visual elements were interpreted in relation to brand
narratives and their cultural contexts to understand how
meanings associated with local wisdom were constructed
and communicated (Andrianto, 2018). Following Williams,
(2019), visual analysis was used not only to describe visual
forms but also to interpret how design elements function as

Data Description
SukkhaCitta
Visual data analyzed from SukkhaCitta  were obtained
from the brand’s official website and Instagram account.
The observation focused on publicly available content,
including product photography, social media posts, and
digital promotional materials. The visual content on its
digital platforms  does not merely present clothing
products; it also documents the production process,
artisan communities, and the raw materials used in textile-
making. The analysis identified four recurring types of
visual representations across the observed materials.
The first type features female farmers and weavers
within their working environments, such as agricultural
fields, weaving spaces, or natural open-air settings.
The second type presents natural materials used as
fabric dyes, including fruits, sugar, plants, and other plant-
derived sources of natural color. These photographs are
presented in close -up compositions that emphasize the
texture, color, and material character of each ingredients.
The third type positions local weavers as the primary
subjects of the photographs. In several Instagram posts,
artisans are depicted wearing the textiles they produced
themselves, placed as models within the brand’s visual
materials. These visuals are accompanied by captions that
speak to the role of women’s communities in preserving
textile traditions and their relationship with the surrounding
natural environment.
The fourth type consists of products visually presenting
garments dyed with natural colourants, rendered through
clean product photography and minimalist text. These
visuals foreground the natural color characteristics and
fabric textures produced through plant -based dyeing
processes.

Javara
Visual data analyzed from Javara were obtained from
the brand’s official website and Instagram account. The
observation focused on publicly available content, including
product photography, infographics, and social media
content. Across all observed conten t, Javara’s visual
approach is divided into two distinct yet complementary
visual characters. The first consists of product visuals with
a modern and minimalist aesthetic. Packaging is presented
in clean compositions against simple backgrounds,
sometimes accompanied by supporting elements such as
natural ingredients or relevant props. Product information,
including type and characteristics, is communicated directly
through packaging labels and text within the visuals.
Variations in approach also appear across Instagram
content, ranging from product photography of organic rice,
coconut oil, honey, and nuts to the use of models in
everyday contexts, as well as close -up visuals highlighting
the textures of food ingredients. Several social media posts
are accompanied by brief text connecting the products to
health benefits or particular lifestyles, making text an
integrated component of the brand’s visual communication
system.
The second character is informative and structured
visual content, most clearly visible in the infographic titled
“Javara’s Journey in Reviving Ancestral Wisdom and
Cultivating Sustainable Food Systems,” obtained from the
brand’s official website. This vis ual presents data on
international market reach, the number of farmers and
communities involved, raw material sourcing regions, and
contributions to biodiversity conservation. The infographic
combines text, icons, maps, and statistical figures. Unlike
the more aesthetic Instagram content, this infographic
explicitly positions the brand within a broader production
system and social network

Utama Spice
The visual data analyzed from Utama Spice were
obtained from the brand’s official website and Instagram
account. The observation focused on publicly available
content, including product photography, social media
content, and website materials. The most con sistent visual
element is the botanical illustration on product packaging,
as seen in the essential oil product line. Labels are
dominated by plant imagery rendered in soft, natural tones,
paired with minimalist typography free of excessive
decorative elem ents. Product photography supports the
same character through warm lighting, simple backgrounds,
and calm compositions. This overall approach creates a
visual identity that feels organic and uncluttered.
On social media, several posts take the form of videos
depicting everyday activities, human interactions, and
behind-the-scenes processes. Some content explicitly
references the Tri Hita Karana philosophy as the foundation
of how the brand operates, with v isuals of closeness to
nature and reflective moments serving as supporting
elements. The narrative is present both in caption text and
within the visuals themselves.
On the website, the communication approach shifts
toward more direct environmental messaging. One visual
presents a cleaning product accompanied by the message
that everyday activities, such as doing laundry, need not
come at an environmental cost. Green t ones, simple
graphic illustrations, product photography, and a clean
layout composition are the main elements of this content, in
contrast to the more personal and reflective tone of the
Instagram content.

Representation of Local Wisdom in SukkhaCitta’s  Visual
Communication Design
Based on the visual data analyzed, SukkhaCitta
constructs its visual communication not around the product
itself, but around the process. Clothing products are
present, yet they do not occupy the center of the visual
narrative; rather, they are presented as the outcome of a
chain of relationships invo lving land, natural resources,
artisan labor, and inherited cultural practices. This framing
reflects how the brand positions itself in relation to the
communities and environments that underpin its
production system.
The representation of local wisdom is particularly
evident in the recurring visual depiction of local women in
their working environments and the documentation of
natural dye materials. Neither element functions merely as
a background or decorative compone nt; both are
presented as primary subjects within the visual
composition. When artisans wear the textiles  they
themselves have produced and are positioned as the
central figures of the imagery, the visual narrative departs
from conventional fashion advertising and shifts attention
toward the people and cultural practices behind the
product.
This perspective is reinforced through accompanying
captions that describe these women as “The Guardians of
Ibu Pertiwi” and “the heroes of regeneration” (Flesch, n.d.).
Rather than emphasizing product attributes, the narrative
highlights the role of women in preserving traditional
knowledge, cultivating natural resources, and sustaining
community-based production practices. The visual and
textual elements therefore work  together to construct a
regenerative branding narrative in which local wisdom is
represented as a living practice embedded within
everyday social and ecological relationships. In this
context, branding functions not only as a means of product
promotion bu t also as a medium for communicating
cultural continuity, environmental stewardship, and
community resilience. This finding supports previous
research suggesting that culturally grounded visual
narratives can strengthen audience trust and foster deeper
engagement with the values represented by a brand
(Salonen et al., 2025; Siregar et al., 2023).
The representation of local wisdom is further evident in
visuals presenting the natural materials used as fabric
dyes. Photographs featuring plants, roots, and other
natural materials underscore that the dyeing process
draws on locally available natural re sources. Within the
context of visual communication design, the use of
compositions that foreground natural textures and colors
functions to build a visual association between the fashion
product and a nature -based production process. This
approach simultaneously reinforces the brand's image as
a product rooted in environmentally responsible
production practices.
Within the regenerative branding framework, what
SukkhaCitta demonstrates can be understood as an effort
to revive traditional textile knowledge, not by preserving it
as an artifact, but by making it visible, relevant, and
connected to contemporary consumers. Regeneration
here extends beyond the use of symbols or a esthetics
alone; it reaches into more fundamental practices such as
weaving knowledge, natural dyeing, and community -
based production. This resonates with findings in artisan
entrepreneurship literature, where social, environmental,
and organizational fact ors collectively determine the
sustainability of craft -based enterprises, and where the
empowerment of artisan communities is central to
preserving culturally based products and knowledge
systems (Hasanah et al., 2023 ). By presenting the
production process, natural materials, and the role of artisan
communities in its visual materials, the brand does not
merely promote a fashion product; it also deepens
awareness of the cultural and ecological systems
underlying its production practices. A ccordingly, the visual
communication design employed by this brand functions as
a medium that both represents and regenerates local
wisdom practices within the context of the contemporary
fashion industry. This positioning also resonates with
broader shift s within the fashion industry toward more
responsible and reflective practices. As discussed in
sustainable fashion literature, the integration of design
processes, ethical production, and storytelling can generate
deeper emotional value while aligning bra nds with social
and environmental concerns (Moorhouse & Moorhouse,
2018; Yoga, 2018 ). In this sense, SukkhaCitta’s visual
communication extends beyond representation,
repositioning fashion as a practice embedded within cultural
continuity and ecological awareness.

Representation of Local Wisdom in Javara's Visual
Communication Design
Unlike SukkhaCitta , which places human figures and
everyday practices at the center of its narrative, Javara
presents local wisdom primarily through the food
commodities themselves. Forest honey, local rice varieties,
and coconut oil are not merely products; they function a s
tangible representations of traditional knowledge embedded
within Indonesia's food systems. This aligns with findings
from participatory design research in Indonesian rural
communities, where the reintroduction of traditional food
varieties such as local rice cultivars was identified as a key
strategy for reviving indigenous values and strengthening
community resilience  (Utami et al., 2022 ). Local wisdom
here is not a symbolic attribute attached to a product but is
embedded within the ingredients, cultivation methods, and
production processes from which the products originate.
This is evident in how each product is connected to its
geographical origins, production communities, and
ecological context through product naming, packaging
information, and accompanying visual narratives. The
diversity of local food products is therefore presented not
only as a range of consumer choices but also as p art of
Indonesia’s biodiversity heritage (Choi & Ahn, 2023 ). Local
wisdom emerges as knowledge distributed across different
regions and communities and reintroduced through
products accessible to contemporary markets. In terms of
visual communication design, Javara employs a systematic
and informative approach. Th is is reflected in the use of
structured layouts, clear typographic hierarchy, product -
centered photography, and packaging elements that
communicate ingredient types, regional origins, and product
characteristics. Visual materials observed on the brand’s
website also incorporate infographic -based elements,
including maps, statistical information, and visual
summaries describing farmer participation, biodiversity
conservation initiatives, and distribution networks. These
design elements organize complex info rmation into a
coherent visual system that enables audiences to
understand products not merely as consumer goods but as
outcomes of interconnected relationships among farmers,
local ecosystems, and regional food traditions. Rather than
relying primarily on  emotional storytelling, the brand
communicates local wisdom through information-rich visual
structures that foreground traceability, biodiversity, and
knowledge systems. Design therefore functions not only as
a promotional medium but also as a mechanism f or
communicating knowledge about food, place, and cultural
heritage.

strategy can be understood as a form of cultural
recontextualization rather than simple commodification.
Cultural commodification generally refers to the
transformation of cultural resources into marketable
products in ways that may detach them from their original
social and cultural meanings  (Baillie & Taha, 2010 ). By
contrast, Javara consistently maintains references to the
origins of its products by communicating information about
production regions, farming communities, biodiversity
value, and traditional cultivation practices. Through
packaging, website content , and infographic -based
communication, the products are situated within broader
ecological and cultural systems rather than being
presented as isolated commercial objects. This approach
enables local food products to function as carriers of
cultural knowledge that are translated into contemporary
market contexts while retaining connections to the
communities and environments from which they emerge.
Such a strategy reflects a process of recontextualization in
which cultural meanings are adapted for contempor ary
audiences without entirely displacing their original
significance (Maryam & Budiwaty, 2025 ). In this sense,
Javara demonstrates how visual communication design
can support regenerative branding by preserving the
contextual integrity of local knowledge while making it
visible, accessible, and relevant within modern
consumption practices.
Regeneration here occurs not only at the level of the
product, but in the way, consumers perceive local food.
Through a consistent combination of visuals and
information, Javara gradually shifts the perception that
local food is not a secondary alternative , but an essential
component of a sustainable food system. In this way,
visual communication design becomes an instrument that
both represents local wisdom and actively constructs its
relevance for contemporary audiences.

Representation of Local Wisdom in Utama Spice's Visual
Communication Design
Utama Spice presents local wisdom through a more
conceptual dimension. Local values are not surfaced
through explicit cultural symbols, but through living
principles that form the foundation of everyday Balinese
practice. The Tri Hita Karana philosophy is not merely
declared as a tagline; it serves as the foundational
framework that shapes the brand's entire visual
communication system. Rooted in the Balinese values of
Parahyangan, Pawongan, and Palemahan, this
philosophy represents a holistic and ethically grounded
paradigm that extends beyond local practice into broader
discourses on sustainability and community development
(Johnson et al., 2025 ). The values of balance, simplicity,
and connectedness with nature are translated into
observable visual elements, including minimalist
compositions, botanical imagery, earthy color palettes,
and references to natural materials. Rather than relying on
explicit cultural symbols, local wisdom is embedded within
the brand’s visual language and recurring narratives of Tri
Hita Karana across its packaging, website, and digital
content. In terms of visual communication design, Utama
Spice's approach reflects an e ffort to build a deeper
emotional relationship with its audience. Video content,
vlogs, and narratives about values and processes position
visual communication as a medium for conveying a
worldview, rather than simply selling. Design does not
stand alone as an aesthetic element but becomes part of
a broader communication system in which visuals,
narrative, and product work together to form a single,
consistent impression.
Within the regenerative branding framework, Utama
Spice's approach can be understood as an effort to
regenerate values through visual and narrative
representation. Branding today is no longer merely about
aesthetics but has become part of a broader transformation
toward regenerative practices (Das & Bocken, 2024). Based
on the visual materials analyzed, the Tri Hita Karana
philosophy is presented as a central organizing principle
across the brand’s packaging, website, and digital content.
Through this visual communication system, Balinese
cultural values appear to  be reintroduced in forms that
connect traditional philosophical concepts with
contemporary sustainability narratives.

Recommendations for Future Research
Future research may expand the scope of analysis by
examining a broader range of Indonesian brands across
different creative industry sectors to provide richer
comparative insights into regenerative branding practices.
In addition, qualitative approaches s uch as interviews and
ethnographic studies involving local communities, artisans,
and brand representatives could offer a deeper
understanding of how local wisdom is interpreted, practiced,
and communicated beyond publicly available visual
materials. Futur e studies may also investigate audience
reception to explore how local wisdom representations are
perceived by consumers. Combining visual analysis with
quantitative methods could further contribute to a more
comprehensive understanding of regenerative bra nding
across different cultural, organizational, and geographic
contexts.

Conclusion
This study investigated the role of visual
communication design in regenerative branding practices
among Indonesian local brands and examined how local
wisdom is represented through visual and narrative
strategies across three distinct brand contexts. The
findings suggest that regenerative branding can function

was represented through three distinct approaches: as
