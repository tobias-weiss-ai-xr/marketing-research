RESUMO
Há pouca atenção acadêmica à sustentabilidade sobre place branding. O
objetivo deste estudo é avançar sobre a sustentabilidade na gestão da
marca de lugar ao examinar um cluster regional e suas práticas de
sustentabilidade atuais e potenciais. Essa exploração qualitativa postulou
que as inter-relações estratégicas entre a comunicação de place
branding, as táticas de mercado e os modelos de negócios sustentáveis
constituem a base teórica e prática para o novo modelo proposto — o
Sustainable Place Branding (SPB). Seguindo a lógica dos Objetivos de
Desenvolvimento Sustentável (ODS), esta pesquisa transdisciplinar
avança o debate sob as perspectivas de abordagens de sustentabilidade,
estudando um cluster da Região Norte do Brasil – Estado do Pará como
caso ilustrativo. Ao analisar o conjunto de dados documentais e ponderar
a literatura, os resultados mostram que recursos, processos e
capacidades da cadeia de suprimentos podem ser estrategicamente
integrados ao SPB. A estrutura recomendada para o Sustainable Place
Branding compreende as quatro configurações correspondentes de
planos: Estratégias de Paisagem e Orientação para Sustentabilidade;
Projetos de Infraestrutura e Criação de Valor Estendido; Estrutura
Organizacional e Administrativa e Integração de Stakeholders; e
Comportamento do Lugar e Pensamento Sistêmico.
PALAVRAS-CHAVE: Sustainable Place Branding (SPB), Modelo de Negócio
Sustentável (MNS), Marca de lugar, Cluster regional, Marca-região.

ABSTRACT
There is a limited scholarly attention to sustainability within place
branding research. The purpose of this study settles on the advance of
sustainability in place branding by examining a regional cluster and its
current and potential sustainability practices. This qualitative exploration
postulated that the strategic interrelationships among place branding
communication, market tactics, and sustainable business models
constitute the theoretical and practical foundation for the proposed novel
framework — the Sustainable Place Branding (SPB). By following the
rationale of the Sustainable Development Goals (SDG), this cross-
disciplinary research progresses the debate from the perspectives of
sustainability approaches while taking a cluster of the North Region of
Brazil – Pará State as an illustrative case. By analysing the documental
dataset while pondering the literature, findings show that resources,
processes, and capabilities of the supply chain can be strategically
integrated at the SPB. The recommended framework for Sustainable
Place Branding comprehends the following corresponding four plan
schemes — Landscape Strategies & Sustainability Orientation;
Infrastructure Projects & Extended Value Creation; Organizational and
Administrative Structure & Stakeholder Integration; and Place’s
Behaviour & Systematic Thinking.
KEYWORDS: Sustainable Place Branding (SBP), Sustainable Business Model
(SBM), Place brand, Regional cluster, Regional brand.

Introduction

The integration of sustainability principles into place brand management has been
increasingly recognized within the existing literature (Aguilera-Cora et al., 2024; Florek &
Kavaratzis, 2014; Giraldi & Oliveira, 2017; Go & Govers, 2012; Kavaratzis & Florek, 2021;
Maheshwari et al., 2011; Ryan & Mizerski, 2010). Likewise, the premises and strategies for
the creation and implementation of a sustainable development model must be described in
branding places (Ocke, 2017). This growing acknowledgment aligns with the broader
emphasis on sustainable business development, sustainable branding, and ethical
consumption as fundamental components of contemporary brand management (Nascimento
& Loureiro, 2024).

Place branding is a multifaceted and influential domain, encompassing diverse scientific
definitions, interdisciplinary approaches, and practical applications. It is recognized as a
significant field within academia, public policy, and business practice. Fundamentally, place
branding involves the strategic development of a place's image and reputation, aiming to
reinforce its core local identity, including its tangible and intangible attributes, benefits,
resources, and assets, conveyed through various communication channels. To effectively
adapt business models (BM) to evolving value propositions, organizations are required to
conduct a strategic analysis of existing clusters (Ada et al., 2024). This analytical rigor is
crucial for developing sustainable and competitive business models (Loviscek, 2020;
Meirelles, 2019; Sahebalzamani, 2021; Teece, 2010, 2018; Zott & Amit, 2013).

Therefore, the research question seeks to advance the understanding of Sustainable
Place Branding (SPB) by re-structuring the cluster business model with principles of
sustainability: "How to configure a regional cluster strengthened by sustainable place
branding?" Consequently, this study aligns with prior calls for a cross-disciplinary research
agenda in in this challenging scenario of sustainability in business models. For this purpose,
it is guided by the Sustainability Development Goals (SDGs), based on Huatuco and Ball’s
inquiry (2019): "The importance of addressing the SDGs is very high, but how can
academics, practitioners, policymakers, and wider stakeholders help achieve them?" (p.
357). Thus, this study aims to illustrate a Brazilian açaí cluster located in the North Region of
Brazil – in Pará State. Moreover, it employs Kavaratzis’s place brand communication
framework (2004; 2005), which posits that a brand is communicated through distinctive
forms, alongside Sahebalzamani’s (2021) sustainable business model framework to enrich
the foundational elements of this cross-disciplinary investigation.

Açaí, a berry native to Latin America, demonstrates the substantial economic potential of
maintaining standing forests in the Amazon Region (World Resources Institute, 2017).
Recognized as a "super food" (Nature Conservancy, 2025), the fruit has experienced rapid
demand expansion nationally and internationally in the 21st century, leading to its
widespread cultivation. The "purple gold" trade route is extensive, reaching nearly all
continents, with a financial movement reflecting this global dimension, yielding a surplus of
approximately US US$ 45 million, in 2023 (ABRAFRUTA, 2024). Furthermore, the
considerable volume of native açaí groves in the region represents a significant
environmental advantage, providing a competitive edge over productive arrangements in
other regions, as all production originates from native açaí woods in floodplain areas
(SEBRAE, 2015). The açaí supply chain in Pará exemplifies how a local product can
catalyse the emergence and expansion of an industry by scaling up pre-existing productive
activities embedded in local culture, thereby contributing to forest preservation and value
creation within the territory (World Resources Institute, 2023).

Despite the advantageous place-of-origin for açaí agricultural estates, the northern
region of Brazil requires further sustainable incentives and research (EMBRAPA, 2021;

This qualitative study is predicated on the rationale that place branding exerts a dynamic
influence, involves a strategic communication process, and possesses a sustainable value-
creation capability. Thus, this paper contributes to the research on Sustainable Place
Branding (SPB), particularly concerning the place-of-origin, by exploring sustainability
practices and communication strategies in the regional context (i.e., Pará State). While
sustainable development receives increasing attention for cities and countries, there is a
notable gap in research focusing on regional settings. This gap is significant given the
distinct characteristics of regions, which may vary in scale (smaller or larger), possess a
more specific nature, and involve more variables than a city but fewer than a country
(Caldwell & Freire, 2004). Furthermore, regional branding endeavours to enhance local
competitiveness, leverage unique place-of-origin attributes, and strengthen market targets
through cluster association (Johnson & Bruwer, 2007). This manuscript also supports that
extant research collectively highlights several critical dimensions of regional branding
strategies and tactics. For instance, asserting that the configuration of a cluster, when
examining the potential of new products, fosters governance practices among its
participants, which in turn indicate value-creating properties (Tracey et al., 2014). Also, the
existing literature underscores various crucial aspects of regional branding, including the
significant role of a place's origin in shaping specific regional industries (Johnson & Bruwer,
2007), the considerable influence of regional actors in establishing regional brands (Messely
et al., 2015), and the critical role of political considerations in the brand-building process
(Lucarelli & Giovanardi, 2016). Moreover, studies investigate the dynamic process of
renovating a place's brand identity (Cassinger & Eksell, 2017) and how regional
administrations can enhance place branding effectiveness (Makarov & Illarionov, 2019).
These combined insights emphasize the complex and diverse opportunities inherent in
regional branding, accordingly this study contributes for these imperative issues in the
literature.

The Role of Business Models in Place Branding

Originally, Business Model (BM) stands as a construct that must be considered based
on the contextual issues, the environment particularities, and the genuine interrelationships
with the stakeholders involved in the organizational process. A successful business model
generates value in the organizational and market scenario, as the appropriation of
opportunities occurs through several strategic decisions, among them the delineation of
solution for consumers and the design of the business model (Teece, 2018). Another

definition of the business model construct that is based on the tripod (Meirelles, 2019, p.787):
in which “the main proposition is that the business model is an emerging structure, defined
based on an interaction between strategic decisions of discovery and recognition of
opportunities (value creation), implementation (value configuration), and obtaining return
(value appropriation)”. For Zott and Amit (2013), the business model translates as the system

of human, physical, and capital resources, whether from the firm, from customers, from

is a continued trajectory towards sustainable business models in various industry categories,
despite the creation and delivery of sustainable economic value still being unexplored with
the implementation of new research into unexplored sustainable business modelling
strategies (Agwu & Bessant, 2021). This is true not only by the organization, but also to
cities, regions, and nations.

Taking the business model concept into the strategical scope and resourceful
characterization of the management of a regional place brand, either administered by the
researcher, a policy maker, or a specific stakeholder group, practices for sustainability
achievements are applied for the creating and capturing value among a local cluster (set of
traditional business models located in a specific region). Likewise, Florek and Kavaratzis
(2014) recall that a place brand equity may be provided as an indicator of sustainable place
development. Also, place brands are renowned as marketable assets (Hankinson, 2015;
Kavaratzis, 2004) that are established at this cyclical discipline that is still looking for the next
levels of development (Kavaratzis, 2018). Recently, Kavaratzis and Florek’s special call
(2021) named ‘The future of place branding’ approached such need when claiming for
studies based on the relationship between place branding and sustainability, among other
topics.  Yet, the debate has been signposted throughout the years: “The place brand — if
understood not as the simple result of marketing strategies but as the effect of wider policies:
cultural, social, environmental, and so on — might serve as guidance for sustainable place
development” (Florek & Kavaratzis, 2014, p.105). Go and Govers (2012) also highlighted the
application of brand and marketing strategies to the economic, social, political, and cultural
development of cities and regions for competition through sustainability and smart progress.
After all, productive regions coexist in a constantly changing economic, political and
technological macro environment, in addition to operating in an arena of increasing
competition for investments of the most diverse natures, causing localities to be pressured to
improve their resources through long-term planning that prioritizes quality in their programs
and services (Ocke, 2017).Similarly, sustainability in place branding demands are
progressively evolving in Brazilian communities (Mariutti & Florek, 2022) such as traditional
context-based, disregarded places, and rural settings.

As Kavaratzis and Florek (2021, p. 1) assert, "place branding has the potential to
become public value-driven and to bring communities back to their foundations by stimulating
a sense of unity." This underscores its capacity to foster social cohesion and align
development with the collective well-being of a community. Therefore, this integrative sign
highlights the relevance of reconciling such place brand-owners or managers towards
sustainable business models agendas (Huatuco & Ball, 2019; Kurucz et al., 2017; Loviscek,
2020; Sahebalzamani, 2021) integrating community-based participants, under micro-level
sustainability, and communicating values through an open and transparent approach
(Sahebalzamani, 2021). Moreover, according to the extensive bibliometric study of

Towards the Sustainable Place Branding (SPB)

Açaí — this Brazilian fruit — production generates high added value and a modest risk of
deforestation, nevertheless, without investment, producers may not serve global demand.
Indeed, "the productive chain of açaí is complex and raises many issues that hinder
exportation" (Binois, 2012). Additionally, valuing the sustainable açaí production chain for the
development of food inputs (Barbosa & de Carvalho Junior, 2022) is evident as particular
brands are found in açaí food shops and tourist routes (SEBRAE, 2025). According to
ABRAFRUTA (2024), “there is a need for cultural change to internationalize the economy
and to transcend the simple export of the fruit”. Due to the conceivable risks for the
sustainable future of several clusters as the cases reported here, this study reinforces the
need to this type of investigation. Therefore, in this business context, sustainable
development requires to be a socio-economic process in which the use of matter and energy
and environmental impacts are minimized, social well-being is maximized, and the use of
natural resources aims at maximum efficiency according to the capacity reconstruction of the
environment, that is, fleeing waste. This is the dictum of the triple bottom line (Loviscek,
2020).

In this sustainable development direction, Hankinson (2010) alerts for the need of place
branding research into a cross-disciplinary schema. This call also escorts this work by
applying organizational strategy knowledge about business models into place branding
knowledge.

Accordingly, Kavaratzis's framework highlights that place brand management has
multidisciplinary schemes and involves a diverse range of audiences, which is granted by
authentically communicating values of the sustainable business models (Sahebalzamani,
2021). Likewise, complemented by Ocke´s statement (2017, p.150) that place branding “is
highly intangible and complex, deals with multiple identities, and requires long-term
development”. This reinforces the necessity of this study for exploring a regional place brand

The Açaizeiro Cluster in the North Region of Brazil – Pará State

In the North Region of Brazil, the açaí palm, ( Euterpe oleracea), is a species of palm
tree cultivated for its fruit, hearts of palm, leaves, and trunk wood. Indigenous and traditional
populations, who over centuries developed effective techniques for selecting and increasing
its productivity in agroforestry systems (Nature Conservancy, 2025), used Açaí as food. The
fruit, native of South American rainforests, is considered a superfood, and is popularly used
for smoothie bowls” (Statista, 2021). Açaí grows on palm trees – açaizeiro (Euterpe oleracea
Martus) that rise 15 to 30 meters high — found in the estuary floodplains Amazon as
abundant species with more than 100 trees per hectare (World Resources Institute, 2017).
Specifically, a region in the Pará State was chosen for this study because out of fourteen
Brazilian states contributed to açaí production, with Pará standing out as it maintained its
leadership position by achieving a production of 1.7 million tons, representing a significant
90.4% of national production (ABRAFRUTA, 2024; Pará Rede Liberal, 2025). According to
ABRAFRUTA (2024), Pará State exports more than 61 thousand tons of açaí per year.
EMBRAPA (2021) reports that the obtained production, after processed, is directed to the
national market (99%), and traded to other consumer centres in the Brazilian States, mainly
São Paulo, Minas Gerais, Bahia, and Rio de Janeiro. Regarding the exports, United States
consumes the most, 40% of the export, followed by Japan and Australia, then, in Europe
(ABRAFRUTA, 2024).

Paradoxically, despite its global characterization as a "superfood," açaí holds an
additional, contrasting connotation within Brazil as a staple food for the working population
(Neves Fonseca & Lima, 2024). In these instances, consumption is driven not by health
considerations, but by its perceived attributes as a highly sweet, energizing, and refreshing
food that induces satiety. Such preparations frequently incorporate fresh fruit, peanuts, and
granola, yet are also commonly augmented with ultra-processed ingredients such as
condensed milk, dairy flour, and powdered milk. The authors also identified the increasing
financialization of açaí in the Amazon (Neves Fonseca & Lima, 2024).

Research Design and Analytical Framework

This interpretative study is designed as a qualitative case study to expand the SPB
understanding of a robust economical cluster based on the documental data (Eisenhardt,
1989). Moreover, a case study design qualifies essential phenomena of sustainability issues
(Sahebalzamani, 2021). The selected case is a regional cluster offering nature-based
activities for the açaí palm production. Secondary data gathering was undertaken from the
institutes’ websites, media publications, and academic articles. Content analysis was
adopted throughout the analytical protocol (Miles et al., 2018). As mentioned in the
Introduction section of this work, an analytical rationale was developed due to the cross-
disciplinary nature and to the lack of studies in SPB. Considering the requirement of a BM’
re-configuration for emerging structures resulting from an interaction process in direction of
sustainable developments (Ada et al., 2024; Dyck and Silvestre, 2018; Loviscek, 2020;
Meirelles, 2019; Sahebalzamani, 2021; Teece, 2010, 2018; Zott & Amit, 2013). Additionally,
this study’s secondary dataset was compiled from documentary evidence obtained from
various institutes and organizations, namely ABRAFRUTA (2024), EMBRAPA (2021),
Instituto Peabiru (2016, 2024), Nature Conservancy (2025), Portal da Amazônia (2021),
SEBRAE (2015, 2025), Statista (2021), and the World Resources Institute (2017, 2023). The
utilization of these institutional sources were also supported by the relevant scientific
literature regarding such as Barbosa and de Carvalho Junior (2022), Binois (2012),
Nascimento and Loureiro (2024), Souza et al. (2018), and Yokomizo et al. (2013). In this
study, a triangulation approach was employed during the analysis phase to enhance the
validity and reliability of the findings. This involved a rigorous cross-examination of two
distinct data sources: A comprehensive literature review and a secondary dataset — in which
core theoretical principles derived from the seminal research of Kavaratzis (2004, 2005) and
Sahebalzamani’s SBM principles (2021). By systematically comparing and contrasting these
independent sources, the research sought to corroborate the insights and conclusions,
thereby strengthening the overall robustness of the study’s analytical outcomes.

Results and Discussion

For a detailed and in-depth analytical protocol, the documental results are discussed
aligned with the previous works´ evidences, followed by the visual diagram and the
referenced descriptions of the Sustainable Place Branding Framework.

Açaí extractivists have built-in environmental applications in the açaí supply chain in
terms of the resources, processes, and capabilities as recommended by Binois (2012),
Barbosa and de Carvalho (2022) and Souza et al. (2018) and reported by World Resources
Institute (2025). Likewise, the Instituto Peabiru (2016), based in Bélem, Pará, administers the
Project to Strengthen Local Productive Arrangements that focus on the sustainable value
chains and inclusive policies about the local development, check on the basic needs of the
population, and help to overcome the harmful effects of the chain. The açaí production chain
must be analysed in terms of social return and related impacts local development with the
survey of income indicators, public policy effectiveness, social organization, production and
sustainability, capital accumulation, innovations, and goods collective (positive externalities).
Although açaízais (açaí plantations) do not require the application of fertilizers or irrigation,
different coordination of each açaí extractivists may vary in specifications and
operationalization. These strategies are presented by Binois (2012) and Messely et al.
(2015).

Final Remarks

Place branding is widely recognized as a critical strategic discipline for addressing
contemporary sustainability challenges, yet research is still evolving. Having a sustainable
business model plays a pivotal role in cultivating a favourable reputation and maintaining a
place's appeal to key stakeholders, including residents, businesses, investors, and a skilled
workforce. Given that, clusters are essentially predisposed by common economic, social, and
environmental of the core business, this paper contributes to the well-defined processes and
potentials of the knowledge on SPB related to the place ‐of‐origin, by combining the
sustainability principles and the communication possibilities for added value, growth, and
progress.

Turning back to the research question of this study, "How to configure a regional
cluster strengthened by sustainable place branding?" Accordingly, to reply to this study’s
inquiry — firstly, it is required to recall the analytical rationale based on Kavaratzis (2004,
2005) and Sahebalzamani (2021) along with the empirical reference of the açaí cluster in the
North Region of Brazil – Pará State (Figure 1) for supporting the re-configured business
models impacted by sustainability principles and practices. Secondly, it is required to fully
understand this proposed Sustainable Place Branding (Figure 2) reinforces that the
management of resources, processes, and capabilities are crucial in terms of integration of
the key-dimensions of sustainable business models located at a cluster, such as value
proposition, value network, value creation and capturing (Meirelles, 2019; Sahebalzamani,
