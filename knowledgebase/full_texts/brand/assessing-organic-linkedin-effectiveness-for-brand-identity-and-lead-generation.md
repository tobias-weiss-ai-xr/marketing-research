Abstract
This study evaluates whether a structured organic LinkedIn strategy can strengthen brand identity, improve
professional visibility, and generate early lead indicators for Creativeunoia, an independent Indonesian production
house. The research used a qualitative single case design supported by descriptive platform analytics. Primary data
came from a semi structured interview with the founder and a focus group discussion with three other founding
members. Secondary evidence included company records, industry literature, competitor observations, and LinkedIn
analytics. Internal analysis applied the Resource Based View, Marketing Mix 7P, and STP, while external and
integrated analysis used PESTLE, customer analysis, SWOT, and TOWS. The resulting strategy published three
weekly content pillars for six weeks: brand identity, portfolio highlights, and thought leadership, combined with
targeted professional outreach. The inactive page generated 3,651 impressions, 1,646 clicks, 65 reactions, 2 reposts,
and reached 35 organic followers, approximately 62 percent of whom worked in creative and media related
industries. Outreach to 30 professionals produced 18 responses, five high intent discussions, and one scheduled
studio visit. The findings indicate that consistent, process oriented, identity driven content can convert internal
creative capability into credible professional visibility and early business opportunities without paid advertising.

Keywords: B2B communication; brand identity; creative entrepreneurship; lead generation; organic LinkedIn

INTRODUCTION
Indonesia's film and digital creative sectors increasingly combine cultural production with commercial
competition. The screen industry contributed an estimated USD 8.2 billion in total economic output and supported
approximately 387,000 workers in 2022, while digital transformation continued to expand the number of production
houses, agencies, and creative collectives competing for projects and recognition (PwC Indonesia & LPEM FEB
Universitas Indonesia, 2023; Dellyana et al., 2023). This expansion creates opportunity, but it also raises the cost of
remaining difficult to discover. Small creative firms often possess distinctive artistic capabilities while lacking the
financial resources, specialist marketing staff, and established distribution networks that allow larger firms to
communicate those capabilities consistently.
For an independent production house, professional visibility functions as a strategic resource rather than a
supplementary promotional activity. Creative services require potential clients to assess artistic fit, technical
competence, reliability, and collaboration capacity before initiating contact. Organizational buyers therefore evaluate
not only a finished portfolio but also the coherence of the firm's identity and the credi bility of its professional
presentation. Brand identity gives this presentation continuity because it connects values, visual language, tone, and
organizational meaning into a recognizable external proposition (Ianenko et al., 2020). When a company expresses
its internal identity inconsistently, professional audiences may fail to understand why its capabilities differ from
those of competitors.
Digital channels offer resource constrained firms a practical mechanism for reducing this recognition gap.
Digital presence allows a business to communicate its value proposition beyond local networks, while organic
content can build awareness and trust without requiring a recurring advertising budget (Banner, 2018;
Bandyopadhyay, 2016). The effectiveness of a platform, however, depends on the audience and the purchasing
context. Instagram and TikTok can support public awareness and visual discovery, but their dominant consumption
patterns emphasize entertainment and lifestyle engagement. LinkedIn instead organizes par ticipation around
professional identity, expertise, networking, and business decision making, making it more closely aligned with the
B2B requirements of a production house (Belanche et al., 2023; Saeidi & Hollensen, 2024). LinkedIn has particular

Creativeunoia illustrates the strategic problem that emerges when internal capability exceeds external
visibility. The company was founded in Jakarta in 2021 by graduates of the Faculty of Film and Television at the
Jakarta Institute of Arts and formally registered as a limited liability company in 2024. Its portfolio covers short
films, music videos, photography, branded digital video, and graphic design. The founding team defines the
organization through the philosophy of beautiful thinking, emotional storytelling, a rebellious creative identity, and
multidisciplinary collaboration. Nevertheless, before this study the company relied predominantly on referrals and
personal academic or professional networks. Its Instagram activity lacked a stable editorial structure, and its newly
established LinkedIn page had five followers, no post history, and zero impressions at the baseline measurement.
This mismatch produced a business problem with two connected dimensions. First, the market could not easily
discover or assess Creativeunoia's capabilities through a credible professional channel. Second, the company had no
structured mechanism for translating brand communication into measurable indicators of professional interest. The
study therefore asked how organic LinkedIn content could strengthen Creativeunoia's brand identity, how a
systematic content strategy could increase professional visibility, and which content forms could generate early lead
indicators. The research aimed to diagnose the underlying strategic gap, design an identity aligned organic content
intervention, evaluate its six week performance, and derive practical guidance for small creative firms that cannot
depend on paid advertising.
The study contributes in two ways. Conceptually, it connects brand identity, organic B2B content, and early
lead generation within a creative enterprise context where commercial outcomes depend heavily on trust and artistic
fit. Practically, it treats LinkedIn not merely as a broadcasting channel but as a combined inbound and outbound
system. Consistent content provides a professional reference point, while targeted outreach converts that reference
point into relationship building opportunities. This integrated perspective addresses a gap in research that often
examines engagement metrics without linking them to the strategic conditions and commercial constraints of
independent production houses.

LITERATURE REVIEW
Brand Identity and Professional Visibility
Brand identity represents the internally defined meaning that a company seeks to communicate, whereas brand
image reflects how external audiences interpret that meaning. A strong identity therefore requires more than visual
consistency. It requires alignment among organizational values, creative behavior, narrative tone, and repeated
market communication (Ianenko et al., 2020). In a production house, this alignment carries additional importance
because prospective clients use stylistic and relational cues to evaluate whether a creative partner can understand the
intended project. An impressive portfolio may demonstrate execution, but it does not automatically explain the
philosophy, process, and collaboration practices that distinguish one studio from another.
Professional visibility translates identity into discoverable evidence. Banner (2018) argues that digital
presence primarily functions as communication because it allows organizations to deliver specific value propositions
through online channels. Organic visibility develops when relevant content, audience interaction, and perceived
credibility extend reach without paid placement. For small firms, the strategic value of organic visibility lies in
cumulative recognition. Each post adds a digital trace that helps professional audiences find, assess, and remember
the firm, thereby reducing dependence on referrals and existing personal networks.

Organic Content and Early Lead Generation
B2B content marketing creates value when it addresses professional problems, demonstrates expertise, and
supports a buyer's evaluation process. Holliman and Rowley (2014) emphasize that effective B2B content should
remain useful and relevant rather than overtly promotional. Järvinen and Taiminen (2016) similarly show that
educational and explanatory content can attract more qualified interest because it gives potential buyers evidence of
competence before sales contact. Content therefore performs several sequential functions: it captures attention,
sustains interest, strengthens confidence, and encourages an observable action such as a profile visit, connection

Lead generation in an organic setting should not be reduced to immediate sales conversion. B2B relationships
usually develop through progressive signals that include repeat exposure, content engagement, contact exchange,
project discussion, and scheduled meetings. Saeidi and Hollensen (2024) frame profile optimization, useful content,
and direct professional communication as complementary mechanisms for generating B2B leads. This relationship
based interpretation is particularly appropriate for creative services because clients often need several interactions
before they disclose a brief or invite a studio into a procurement process.

Strategic Alignment in a Resource Constrained Creative Firm
A content intervention can succeed only when it reflects the firm's actual resources, market position, and
external environment. The Resource Based View helps identify capabilities that can support differentiation, including
storytelling competence, multidisciplinary knowledge, and distinctive creative routines (Cardeal & António, 2012).
Marketing Mix 7P analysis clarifies whether promotion and distribution adequately communicate the firm's services,
while STP analysis specifies the professional segments, target clients, and desired positioning. These tools prevent
the content strategy from becoming detached from the revenue model.
External analysis adds the conditions that the company cannot directly control. PESTLE analysis identifies
macro environmental opportunities and constraints, customer analysis explains how organizational buyers evaluate
creative vendors, and SWOT integrates internal and external evidence.  The TOWS matrix then converts that
diagnosis into strategic combinations that connect strengths and opportunities while reducing weaknesses and threats
(Weihrich, 1982; Webster & Wind, 1972; Worthington & Britton, 2006). In the present study, these frameworks
served as a decision chain rather than independent checklists. They established whether LinkedIn matched
Creativeunoia's resources, target audience, and commercial needs before the intervention began.
Existing scholarship explains brand communication, B2B content, LinkedIn engagement, and strategic
analysis separately, but it provides limited evidence about how these elements operate together in a small Indonesian
production house. The present study addresses this gap through an implementation based case design. It examines
whether a strategy derived from internal and external diagnosis can produce measurable visibility and relational
outcomes over a short organic campaign, while recognizing that early platform metrics cannot substitute for
confirmed revenue.

METHOD
The research used a qualitative single case study supported by descriptive quantitative data. The case study
design allowed the researcher to examine Creativeunoia's brand identity, communication practices, and market
visibility within the company's actual organizational context. The quantitative component did not test causal
hypotheses. Instead, native LinkedIn analytics provided supporting indicators of reach, engagement, follower
composition, and early lead activity during the intervention.
Primary data came from one semi structured interview with the Founder and Chief Executive Officer and one
focus group discussion with three other founding members who represented production, distribution, and digital
communication functions. Interviews enabled detailed exploration of organizational perceptions, while the group
discussion allowed the team to compare shared experiences and reach agreement about the company's identity and
content direction (Chand, 2025). Secondary evidence included the company's portfolio and social media records, the
Week 0 LinkedIn dashboard, relevant academic and industry literature, platform analytics, and observations of
comparable independent production houses. The study used these sources to triangulate internal claims and reduce
reliance on a single data type (Ajayi, 2023).

The analysis proceeded sequentially. Internal analysis used the Resource Based View to identify strategic
capabilities, Marketing Mix 7P to examine service delivery and communication, and STP to define priority
professional audiences and positioning. External analysis used PESTLE to assess the creative economy and platform
environment and customer analysis to examine organizational buyer behavior. SWOT synthesized the diagnosis, and
the TOWS matrix converted it into strategic options. The analysis consistently identified a visibility and
communication deficit rather than a creative capability deficit.
The resulting intervention ran from 8 December 2025 to 18 January 2026. Creativeunoia published content
three times per week through three fixed pillars. The founding team produced the content and approved the themes
and tone before implementation. The researcher monitored native analytics and conducted personalized outreach to
30 professionals from agencies, production houses, music labels, animation studios, and related creative
organizations. No paid advertising or sponsored content supported the intervention.\

The evaluation used four groups of indicators. Brand awareness used impressions and follower growth.
Audience education used clicks and post level interaction patterns. Lead generation used content initiated interest
and outreach responses, classified as early stage, medium intent, or high intent. Audience quality used follower
industry and job function. Because repeated clicks can exceed unique impressions in LinkedIn analytics, the study
interpreted click ratios as behavioral intensity rather than as unique audience conversion. The six week duration and
single company setting limit generalization, and the study did not claim that early lead indicators represented
completed sales.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Strategic Diagnosis and Content Direction
The interview and focus group produced a consistent diagnosis. Creativeunoia's founders clearly understood
the company through beautiful thinking, emotional storytelling, experimentation, and multidisciplinary
collaboration. The team could execute film, music video, photography, digital content, and design projects through
an adaptive structure that combined permanent founders with project based specialists. This capability supported
differentiated creative output and generated authentic process material suitable for professional content. The problem
appeared after production, when the company needed to explain these strengths to unfamiliar professional audiences.
The Resource Based View confirmed that storytelling capability, cinematic direction, a multidisciplinary team,
and a distinctive artistic philosophy represented meaningful internal assets. These resources were valuable because
clients seek both technical quality and interpretive creativity. They were also difficult to imitate as a combined

Organic Performance and Audience Quality
The Week 0 dashboard recorded five followers, zero posts, and zero impressions. During the six week
intervention, the page generated 3,651 impressions, 1,646 clicks, 65 reactions, and 2 reposts without paid support.
Impressions increased from 410 in Week 1 to a peak of 914 in Week 5, an increase of approximately 123 percent.
Week 3 also generated 893 impressions. Both high reach weeks combined portfolio material with educational or
interpretive content, suggesting that the platform responded more strongly when evidence of capability appeared
alongside useful professional insight.
The weekly pattern also demonstrates that reach and interaction do not move identically. Week 1 produced
high initial curiosity, while Week 5 achieved the largest reach but a lower average engagement rate. This divergence
indicates that the content began circulating beyond the founders' immediate network. Expansion to less familiar
audiences can reduce the proportion of viewers who interact, even while total visibility improves. Holiday timing
also affected the results. Week 4 coincided with the year end period and generated lower professional activity, while
Week 6 recorded 315 impressions during the final mid January period. These fluctuations reinforce the importance
of evaluating cumulative exposure rather than judging the strategy through one post or one week.

Post level evidence clarified which content forms encouraged deeper interaction. Document posts generally
produced stronger click behavior than static images and videos because they invited users to move through several
slides and engage with a structured narrative. The company introduction generated 299 impressions and 241 clicks,
while the Short Film Breakdown post generated the strongest engagement pattern by explaining the preproduction
process of Bagi Tiga. The result supports the argument that professional audiences value content that reveals how
creative decisions emerge, not merely the final visual output (Arindi et al., 2023; Järvinen & Taiminen, 2016).
Video posts often achieved broader exposure but less active interaction. The Vertical Content Study Case
generated the highest single post reach at 386 impressions, yet its interaction intensity remained below that of
document based process content. This difference does not imply that video lacked value. Instead, the formats
performed different functions. Video supported awareness and algorithmic circulation, while document content
supported education and evaluative engagement. A balanced editorial plan should therefore assign each format a
specific purpose rather than applying one performance standard to all posts.
Audience composition strengthened the interpretation that the campaign reached a relevant professional
market. By the end of the intervention, the page had 35 organic followers. Approximately 62 percent came from
creative and media related industries. Movies, Videos, and Sound and Advertising Services each represented 17.1
percent, followed by Media Production at 8.6 percent. Arts and Design formed the largest job function at 28.6 percent,
Media and Communication represented 14.3 percent, and Business Development represented 8.6 percent. The
presence of business development and marketing professionals matters because these roles can influence vendor
selection and partnership decisions. The quality of the audience therefore provides stronger strategic evidence than
the absolute follower count alone.

Outreach and Early Lead Indicators
The intervention combined inbound content with outbound relationship building. Personalized messages
reached 30 professionals whose backgrounds aligned with the target mar ket. Eighteen recipients responded,
producing a 60 percent response rate. Five interactions advanced to high intent activity, including project scope
discussions, company profile exchange, and one scheduled studio visit. Four additional contacts exchanged
information, profiles, or industry insights, while nine acknowledged the introduction without progressing to a
substantive discussion during the observation period.
These outcomes show how content and outreach reinforced each other. Outreach alone can appear unsolicited
when recipients lack evidence about the sender. The structured company page reduced that uncertainty by providing
a visible identity, portfolio context, and recent professional activity. Conversely, content alone could improve reach
without ensuring that relevant decision makers encountered the page. Targeted outreach directed selected
professionals toward the credibility base created by the content. This combined mechanism aligns with research that
treats LinkedIn as both a content marketing environment and a relationship development network (Mora Cortez et
al., 2023; Saeidi & Hollensen, 2024; Spilotro et al., 2023).

Strategic Interpretation
The evidence answers the first research question by showing that repeated identity communication can make
an internally coherent brand externally legible. Posts about beautiful thinking, studio life, creative process, and
portfolio context expressed the same philosophy through different content functions. This repetition created
consistency without reducing the brand to one promotional slogan. The result supports Ianenko et al. (2020), who
position brand identity as a coordinated system of meaning, and extends that argument by showing how a small
production house can operationalize identity through an organic professional platform.
The second research question concerned professional visibility. The progression from an inactive page to 3,651
impressions and a relevant 35 follower audience demonstrates meaningful early growth, particularly because the
campaign used no paid amplification. The data do not establish long term market dominance or causal sales impact,
but they show that a disciplined content system can expand the digital surface through which unfamiliar professional
audiences evaluate the firm. This mechanism is strategically important for Creativeunoia because it weakens the
previous dependence on referrals and makes future outreach more credible.
The third research question concerned content effectiveness for lead indicators. Process oriented document
content produced the deepest interaction, while video supported broader reach. Identity posts helped audiences
understand the firm, portfolio posts provided evidence, and thought leadership demonstrated interpretive
competence. The strongest strategy therefore did not rely on direct promotion. It combined identity, proof, and
insight, then connected those content functions with carefully selected outreach. This finding supports B2B research
that prioritizes useful content and relational development over immediate transactional messaging (Holliman &
Rowley, 2014; Sundström et al., 2020).
The study also reveals a critical limitation in interpreting platform metrics. Clicks, impressions, reactions, and
replies indicate attention and professional interest, but they do not prove revenue conversion. A scheduled visit and
project discussion represent stronger behavioral evidence than a reaction, yet even these remain precommercial
outcomes. Creativeunoia must continue the relationships, document confirmed briefs, and compare future project
revenue with acquisition source before claiming a direct financial return. The six week results should therefore be
understood as validation of strategic direction, not as final evidence of business performance.

CONCLUSION
This study found that Creativeunoia's principal business constraint did not originate from limited creative
capability. It originated from the absence of a structured professional communication system capable of translating
that capability into market recognition. Internal analysis identified distinctive storytelling, multidisciplinary
collaboration, and a coherent artistic philosophy, while external analysis identified favorable conditions for
professional digital discovery. The integrated diagnosis therefore justified an organic LinkedIn strategy centered on
brand identity, portfolio evidence, and thought leadership.
The six week implementation produced measurable early outcomes. The page generated 3,651 impressions,
1,646 clicks, 65 reactions, 2 reposts, and reached 35 organic followers, with approximately 62 percent of followers
working in relevant creative and media sectors. Personalized outreach generated 18 responses from 30 contacts,
including five high intent interactions and one scheduled studio visit. Process oriented document posts generated the
strongest evaluative engagement, while video supported wider exposure. These results indicate that consistent
identity driven content can strengthen professional visibility and create credible early lead signals without paid
advertising. Creativeunoia should maintain a publication cadence of two or three posts per week, expand behind the
scenes and process analysis, promote revenue generating services more deliberately, and follow up systematically
with high intent contacts. The company should also establish a quarterly editorial calendar and record the acquisition
