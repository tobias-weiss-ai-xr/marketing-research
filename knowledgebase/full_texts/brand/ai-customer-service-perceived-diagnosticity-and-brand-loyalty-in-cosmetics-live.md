Abstract. In China’s cosmetics live-streaming commerce, AI customer service is evolving
from a tool for automated replies into a digital service touchpoint that shapes product
assessments, service evaluations, and brand relationships. Because cosmetics are experiential
products that carry pronounced product-fit uncertainty, consumers attend not only to response
speed but also to whether AI customer service helps them assess a product’s fit for their skin
type, functional needs, shade preference, and usage context. Drawing on the stimulus -
organism-response (S-O-R) framework and uncertainty-reduction theory, this study develops
a serial-mediation model linking AI customer service experience, perceived diagnosticity,
trust, satisfaction, and brand loyalty, and tests it with partial least squares structural equation
modelling (PLS -SEM) using survey data from 450 Chinese consumers of cosmetics live -
streaming commerce. The results indicate that AI customer service experience is positively
associated with perceived diagnosticity, which is in turn associated with greater trust in the
brand and its service information. Trust is associate d with brand loyalty both directly and
indirectly through satisfaction, and the bias-corrected bootstrap estimates are consistent with
sequential mediation through perceived diagnosticity, trust, and satisfaction. These findings
suggest that, by supplying tailored decision support that reduces product -fit uncertainty, AI
customer service can help convert brief service interactions into stronger brand preference.
The study positions AI customer service as an integral element of the digital service systems
that underpin live-commerce platforms and offers operational guidance for deploying it as a
decision-support mechanism rather than a mere cost-saving channel.
Keywords: AI customer service; live -streaming commerce; cosmetics; brand loyalty;
perceived diagnosticity; digital service systems

1. Introduction
Live-streaming commerce has become an important setting in which consumers obtain product
information, conduct interactive consultation, and make immediate purchases. This is especially true in
experiential product categories such as apparel and cosmetics,  where consumers often find it
challenging to assess whether a product really fits them via static product pages alone. Live-streaming
rooms can therefore support purchase decisions by reducing uncertainty through demonstration,
explanation and interaction. Previous research has shown that, in apparel and cosmetics live streaming,
the vicarious product trials and real -time interaction of streamers help consumers develop trust and
reduce product uncertainty, thus increasing purchase intention (Lu & Chen, 2021). Research on live
streaming shows that interactivity, social presence, instant feedback and dialogic quality jointly
influence trust, engagement and purchase responses (Jiang et al., 2022; Konya-Baumbach et al., 2023;
Cui et al., 2024). Compared with conventional image -and-text e -commerce, the advantage of live
streaming is not merely that it is more engaging, but that it integrates product information, social
interaction and decision support into a single transactional scene.
In this study, AI customer service denotes the brand-  or store-deployed automated conversational
agents that consumers engage with for pre-sale and in-sale consultation during live-streaming shopping.
These agents include rule -based and generative chatbots , platform and brand- store service bots, and
human–AI hybrid arrangements in which routine queries are answered automatically while complex
cases are escalated to human agents. This conceptualisation deliberately centres on the service function
performed rather than on any single underlying technology, so that the findings speak to AI customer
service as a service mechanism instead of to a specific chatbot implementation.
However, existing live -streaming commerce research has mainly focused on streamer
characteristics, interactive atmosphere, social presence, and impulse buying, while paying insufficient
attention to AI customer service inside live -streaming rooms. Recent s tudies have begun discussing
virtual streamers, AI streamers and AI live- streaming agents, but the dominant questions still tend to
centre on identity, anthropomorphic cues, language naturalness and purchase intention, instead of
treating AI customer service as an independent service touchpoint that can explain long-term relational
outcomes (Gao et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2026). Meanwhile, AI customer service research has
demonstrated that service quality can affect customer loyalty through perceived value, cognitive trust,
affective trust, and satisfaction, but its main empirical settings are still general e -commerce or digital
service environments (Chen et al., 2023; Hsu & Lin, 2023; Huang et al., 2024; Rita et al., 2026). In
other words, although both st reams of research have advanced rapidly, they have not adequately
addressed a question closer to commercial reality: when consumers interact with AI customer service
during cosmetics live -streaming shopping, can such interaction be translated into long- term loyalty
toward the brand?
This question is important because cosmetics consumption is not a one -time price comparison.
Shade, skin type, ingredients, efficacy and usage method are important individual differences. What
consumers need is not just “someone responds”, but whether thos e responses help them judge more
accurately the suitability of a product for themselves. In the social -commerce and live- streaming
literature, information diagnosticity is regarded as an important cognitive mechanism that turns
visibility, interactivity an d product cues into purchase judgment, particularly for products with high
uncertainty (Shin et al., 2013; Lu & Chen, 2021). Therefore, this study contends that the value of AI
customer service in cosmetics live streaming should not be interpreted simply i n terms of response
speed or anthropomorphic quality. Whether it increases consumers’ perceived diagnosticity, that is,
their capacity to judge more clearly how well a product fits their own needs, should provide a further
explanation.
Based on this, the present paper makes three contributions. First, it extends AI customer service
research from general digital service settings to the more contextually demanding setting of cosmetics

live-streaming commerce, which is characterised by high experientiality and high uncertainty. Second,
this study introduces perceived diagnosticity into the relationship between AI customer service
experience and brand loyalty to explain how service experience is accumulated into a brand relationship
through a decision-support mechanism. Third, it explicitly differentiates “continued use of AI customer
service itself” from “loyalty toward the brand”, thus avoiding the confounding of technology-adoption
outcomes with relationship-marketing outcomes.
Framing the study within service science, we treat AI customer service as a component of the digital
service systems that operate live- commerce platforms. Beyond answering isolated questions, brand-
and platform -deployed service agents participate in pre -sale consultation, real -time processing of
customer information, order- and after-sales coordination, and the routing of complex or sensitive cases
to human agents. Locating AI customer service within these service workflows connects the present
analysis to the operational concerns of digital service management and clarifies how a frontline service
technology, rather than merely reducing response costs, can create relational value within the broader
platform service ecosystem.
2. Theoretical Foundations and Research Hypotheses
2.1. AI Customer Service Experience and Brand Loyalty
The service-quality–loyalty chain proposes that customers’ positive evaluation of a service process is
gradually transformed into trust, satisfaction, and repeat choice (Parasuraman et al., 1988; Morgan &
Hunt, 1994; Oliver, 1999). AI customer service is n ot merely an information- retrieval tool, but a
frontline touchpoint at which the brand presents itself to consumers in digital environments. Previous
studies have revealed the role of AI customer service quality in increasing customer loyalty via
perceived value, cognitive trust, affective trust, and satisfaction. Other studies have demonstrated that
the quality of service recovery and the conversational quality of chatbots significantly affect satisfaction
(Chen et al., 2023; Hsu & Lin, 2023). Consistent w ith this, recent service -science evidence indicates
that chatbot responsiveness, ease of use, and personalization jointly enhance the customer experience
in e-commerce (Megdadi et al., 2025; Hoang & Tran, 2026). In more specific service contexts, learning-
based trust, emotional expression, and light human–AI collaboration can also shape the effects of AI
customer service on satisfaction and repatronage outcomes (Huang et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024).
This implies that when AI customer service is clear, ti mely and need -relevant, consumers are more
likely to attribute this high -quality contact to the overall capability of the brand, thus forming more
positive relational judgements.
Accordingly, H1 is proposed: AI customer service experience positively affects brand loyalty.
2.2. AI Customer Service Experience and Perceived Diagnosticity
Perceived diagnosticity emphasises whether information helps consumers make more accurate and
more confident judgements. In cosmetics live streaming, consumers’ most important question is not
whether information exists, but whether the information can help them judge whether a product is
suitable for them. Prior research has identified the effects of product -related risk, information quality
and interaction cues on consumers’ evaluation of information diagnosticity (Shin et al., 2013; Wang &
Chang, 2013; Lu & Chen, 2021). Compared with standardised live explanations, AI customer service
can provide more targeted responses based on a consumer’s skin type, shade preference, budget and
specific questions. It may thus turn general information in a live -streaming room into diagnostic
information that is more useful to the individual consumer.
Theoretically, perceived diagnosticity is positioned here as an organismic (O) state rather than as a
property of the stimulus. Diagnosticity is not an objective attribute of the information delivered by AI
customer service; it is a consumer’s subjective appraisal of the extent to which that information enables
an accurate and confident judgement of product fit. Two consumers who receive the same response may

form different diagnosticity perceptions depending on their skin concerns, prior knowledge, and
decision goals. Because it is the outcome of cognitive processing of the service stimulus, perceived
diagnosticity belongs to the organism component of the S –O–R sequence, mediating between the AI
customer service stimulus and consumers’ relational responses, rather than constituting an information-
quality feature of the stimulus itself.
Accordingly, H2 is proposed: AI customer service experience positively affects perceived
diagnosticity.
2.3. Perceived Diagnosticity, Trust, and Satisfaction
Research on uncertainty reduction in live -streaming commerce finds that trust is an important
psychological bridge in the purchase of experiential products (Lu & Chen, 2021). Broader social -
commerce studies have consistently proposed that interaction, social presence, information quality and
responsiveness increase trust and then influence purchase, continued purchase and word- of-mouth
behaviours (Hajli et al., 2017; Liang et al., 2019; Meilatinova, 2021). Consumers tend to rate a source
as reliable and trustworthy when they perceive that the source helps them understand a product more
accurately. In the field of cosmetics, if AI customer service can effectively respond to the question of
“whether it suits me”, consumers are more likely to trust the specific  advice in the current interaction
and to transfer the perceived capability to their overall trust in the brand, consistent with a trust-transfer
mechanism (Zhao et al., 2019). Satisfaction is also shaped by trust (Eni et al., 2024). When a brand is
perceived to provide reliable information and useful advice in a high- uncertainty environment,
consumers are more likely to believe that the interaction meets their expectations and to develop a
positive evaluation of the overall service experience.
The path from AI customer service experience to satisfaction (H4) can be grounded more explicitly
in service-quality and expectation-confirmation reasoning. Service-quality theory holds that favourable
evaluations of a service encounter, particularly its r esponsiveness, reliability, and assurance, feed
directly into overall satisfaction (Parasuraman et al., 1988; Hsu & Lin, 2023). Expectation-confirmation
theory adds that satisfaction arises when perceived performance meets or exceeds prior expectations
(Oliver, 1999). When AI customer service supplies clear, timely, and need- relevant assistance, it is
likely to confirm or surpass consumers’ expectations of the interaction, so that a positive service
experience is translated into satisfaction in addition to,  and partly independently of, its effect through
trust.
Accordingly, the following hypotheses are proposed:
H3: Perceived diagnosticity positively affects trust.
H4: AI customer service experience positively affects satisfaction.
H5: Trust positively affects satisfaction.
2.4. Trust, Satisfaction, and Brand Loyalty
Long-term relationships are built on trust and satisfaction, as relationship -marketing theory has long
highlighted (Morgan & Hunt, 1994; Oliver, 1999). Both constructs have consistently been found to
drive continued purchasing, repatronage and word- of-mouth communication in social -commerce and
AI-service research (Chen et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2024; Meilatinova, 2021). When consumers feel
that a brand is both able and willing to offer reliable help, they are more likely to buy again, give the
brand priority and recommend it proactively. Satisfaction, as consumers’ overall judgment of prior
experience, also directly influences repurchase and word-of-mouth.
Accordingly, the following hypotheses are proposed:
H6: Trust positively affects brand loyalty.
H7: Satisfaction positively affects brand loyalty.

2.5. Chain Mediation
Following the logic above, AI customer service experience may not only directly influence loyalty but
also sequentially influence loyalty through perceived diagnosticity, trust and satisfaction. In other words,
AI customer service is the first means by whi ch customers understand the fit between a product and
their own needs. This support for judgement then reinforces trust in the brand, generates greater
satisfaction, and is finally converted into stronger brand loyalty.
Thus, the following hypothesis is proposed: H8. Perceived diagnosticity, trust, and satisfaction play
a serial (chain) mediating role in the relationship between AI customer service experience and brand
loyalty.
Integrating the above hypotheses within the stimulus –organism–response (S –O–R) framework
yields the conceptual model shown in Figure 1. AI customer service experience serves as the external
stimulus (S); perceived diagnosticity, trust, and satisfaction constitute the internal organismic states (O)
that process and accumulate this stimulus; and brand loyalty represents the behavioural -relational
response (R). The model specifies one direct path (H1) and a set of mediating paths (H2–H7) that
together form the serial mediation expressed in H8.
We deliberately model the influence of perceived diagnosticity on satisfaction as operating through
trust rather than as an additional direct path. Conceptually, diagnosticity is a cognitive appraisal of
decision support, whereas satisfaction is an affective-evaluative response to the encounter; the S–O–R
and uncertainty-reduction logic implies that the cognitive benefit of diagnosticity is converted into
affect once it has consolidated into trust in the brand and its information. A direct diagnosticity –
satisfaction path was therefore not hypothesised. We none the less acknowledge it as a plausible
alternative specification and return to it when discussing the boundaries of the model.

3.2. Construct Measurement
The questionnaire used a seven-point Likert scale. AI customer service experience was assessed mainly
through five aspects: information clarity, response timeliness, problem-solving capability, need fit, and
overall service quality. Perceived diagnosticity reflected the extent to which consumers perceived that,
after interacting with AI customer service, they could judge product fit, reduce uncertainty, compare
product differences, and make purchase decisions with greater confidence. Trust comprised consumers’
perceptions of the reliability and authenticity of the information provided by the brand and its AI
customer service, their general trust, and their willingness to rely on such information. Satisfaction
consisted of overall satisfaction, expectation co nfirmation, interaction pleasantness, and retrospective
evaluation. Brand loyalty captured consumers’ future preference for the brand, repurchase intention,
willingness to recommend, and continued preference.
All items were adapted from mature constructs drawn from prior studies of AI customer service,
live-streaming commerce, perceived diagnosticity, trust, satisfaction, and brand loyalty. The wording
was adapted to the Chinese domestic cosmetics live -streaming commerce context to ensure that the
measurement accurately reflected consumers’ self -reported perceptions and behavioural tendencies,
recalled from a specific interaction with a brand or store AI customer service during live -streaming
shopping. The const ruct sources are summarised in Table 2, and the full item wording is reported in
Appendix Table A1.
Each construct was modelled reflectively. For AI customer service experience, the five facets
(information clarity, response timeliness, problem -solving capability, need fit, and overall service
quality) are treated as interchangeable manifestations of a single underlying evaluation of the AI service
encounter rather than as distinct formative components: they are expected to covary, to share a common
cause, and to be mutually substitutable without changing the meaning of the construct. Brand loyalty is
likewise conceptualised as a unidimensional, primarily attitudinal loyalty intention (a general
disposition toward continued preference, repurchase, and recommendation) rather than as a formative
or higher -order construct, and its four indicators are treated as reflective manifestations of that
disposition. This reflective specification is consistent with the high internal-consistency reliability and
convergent validity reported in Section 4.3 and with the absence of indicator-level collinearity (all outer
VIF < 2.1).

3.3. Analytical Strategy
The hypotheses were tested with partial least squares structural equation modelling (PLS -SEM) using
SmartPLS 4. PLS-SEM was selected because the model is prediction -oriented, involves a complex
serial-mediation structure, and does not require the multivariate -normality assumption of covariance-
based SEM (Hair et al., 2019; Hair et al., 2022). Following established guidelines, a two-stage procedure
was adopted. First, the measurement model was evaluated through indicator reliability and outer
loadings, internal-consistency reliability (Cronbach’s α, the reliability coefficient ρA, and composite
reliability), conve rgent validity (average variance extracted, AVE), and discriminant validity.
Discriminant validity was assessed using both the Fornell –Larcker criterion and the heterotrait –
monotrait ratio of correlations (HTMT), and the absence of indicator multicollinear ity was confirmed
through the variance inflation factor (VIF) (Henseler, Ringle, & Sarstedt, 2015; Dijkstra & Henseler,
2015). Second, after the measurement model met the recommended thresholds, the structural model
was estimated by examining inner -model c ollinearity, the significance of the path coefficients, the
coefficient of determination (R²), the effect size (f²), and the out -of-sample predictive relevance (Q²).
The significance of the direct and indirect (mediating) effects was assessed using bias -corrected
bootstrapping with 5,000 subsamples (Preacher & Hayes, 2008; Zhao et al., 2010). Because the data
were collected through a single -source self -report questionnaire, both procedural and statistical
remedies were used to minimise common method bias, including anonymous responses, item -order
randomisation, semantic differentiation among measures, Harman’s single -factor test, and a full -
collinearity assessment (Podsakoff et al., 2003; MacKenzie & Podsakoff, 2012; Kock, 2015).

4.2. Common Method Bias
Because all measures were obtained from a single source, common method bias (CMB) was examined
statistically. Harman’s single-factor test showed that the first unrotated factor accounted for 34.27% of
the total variance, which is well below the 50% threshold, indicating that no single factor dominated
the covariance among the items. In addition, a f ull-collinearity assessment was conducted following
Kock (2015): the variance inflation factors obtained by regressing each latent variable on all others
ranged from 1.21 to 2.18, all below the conservative 3.3 criterion. Taken together with the procedural
remedies described in Section 3.3, these results suggest that common method bias is unlikely to be a
serious concern in this study.

5. Discussion
5.1. Main Findings
The study shows that AI customer service experience positively influences consumer brand loyalty. In
the Chinese cosmetics live-streaming commerce context, AI customer service is not only an auxiliary
tool for instant consultation but also an important digital touchpoint that shapes consumers’ brand-
relationship judgements. When consumers find AI customer service to be clear, timely, effective and
relevant to their needs, they are more likely to continue to choose, repurchase from, and recommend
the brand.
The findings also reveal that perceived diagnosticity is a critical cognitive mechanism explaining
the effect of AI customer service experience on brand loyalty. The value of AI customer service lies not
only in the speed and convenience of service for cos metics consumers, but, more importantly, in
whether it helps them judge how well a product suits their skin type, functional needs, shade preference
and usage scenario. In other words, high-quality AI customer service can reduce product-fit uncertainty
and provide more solid reasons for purchase judgement.
The study further shows that trust and satisfaction form a sequential transmission relationship in
the influence of AI customer service experience on brand loyalty. Consumers are more likely to develop
trust in the brand and its service information when they perceive the diagnosticity of information
provided by AI customer service to be high. Such trust further enhances their satisfaction with the
interaction process and shopping experience, which is finally manifested in loyalty outcomes such as
repurchase, recommendation, and continued preference. In sum, the impact of AI customer service on
brand loyalty is not limited to the surface level of service experience but gradually develops into a long-
term brand relationship through diagnostic judgement, trust formation and satisfaction evaluation.
Two qualifications sharpen this interpretation. First, the direct association between AI customer
service experience and brand loyalty is comparatively small (β = 0.131; f² = 0.019); the total effect
operates largely through perceived diagnosticity, trust,  and satisfaction, so that AI customer service
appears to build loyalty mainly by improving decision support and the relational states it fosters rather

than through a direct route. Second, because the data are cross-sectional, these pathways should be read
as associational rather than strictly causal: the analysis describes a theoretically ordered set of
relationships that is consistent with, but cannot by itself establish, the proposed temporal sequence.
5.2. Theoretical Contributions
The first contribution of this study is that it expands AI customer service research from general e -
commerce to the more contextually sensitive area of cosmetics live streaming. Previous research has
demonstrated that live streaming can reduce uncertainty in apparel and cosmetics consumption and that
interaction, social presence and dialogic quality can affect trust and behavioural responses; however,
relatively little research has examined the operation of AI customer service inside live-streaming rooms
(Lu & Chen, 2021; Jiang et al., 2022; Konya -Baumbach et al., 2023). This study treats AI customer
service as an independent digital service touchpoint and complements the streamer-centred perspective
in live-streaming research.
Second, this study incorporates perceived diagnosticity into the pathway through which AI
customer service influences loyalty, filling the gap of insufficient attention to decision- support
mechanisms for experiential products in the traditional “service quality → satisfaction → loyalty”
framework. In the cosmetics context, consumers face not merely an information gap but a product -fit
judgement problem. Perceived diagnosticity therefore explains the effectiveness of AI customer service
better than information richness does.
Third, this study reconfirms the importance of distinguishing between continued use of a technology
and long- term loyalty toward a brand. Whereas some prior studies of AI chatbots have focused on
continued intention to use the chatbot itself, this study ta kes brand loyalty as the dependent variable,
bringing the analysis closer to relationship marketing and long-term firm value.
Read against prior work, these results both converge with and extend earlier findings. Consistent
with research showing that AI service quality raises loyalty through perceived value, trust, and
satisfaction in general e-commerce (Chen et al., 2023; Hsu & Lin, 2023; Huang et al., 2024), we observe
similar relational mechanisms in cosmetics live commerce; however, the comparatively small direct
path and the dominant indirect transmission through perceived diagnosticity indicate that, in this high-
uncertainty setting, decision support, rather than service quality in general, is the key mechanism. This
refines the largely streamer -centred account of live commerce (Lu & Chen, 2021; Gao et al., 2023;
Zhang et al., 2026) by identifying AI customer service as an additional, decision -oriented service
touchpoint within the platform’s service system.
5.3. Managerial Implications
Brands should not treat AI customer service primarily as a cost -cutting tool. Instead, it should be
designed as a decision-support system inside live-streaming rooms. For cosmetics, it is more helpful to
match skin types, recommend shades, and explain ingredients, usage scenarios and combination advice
than to offer generic greetings.
Second, if brands want to improve loyalty through AI customer service, they need to prioritise
information quality, problem-solving capability and personalised advice, rather than focusing narrowly
on making AI “as human- like as possible”. Recent AI custom er service research also suggests that
information quality and problem-solving ability are more reliable drivers of satisfaction than superficial
anthropomorphic cues (Hoang & Tran, 2026). The effect of emotional expression, humour and informal
language in high-emotion and service-recovery contexts may, however, depend on boundary conditions
such as service-failure severity (Rita et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2024; Shams et al., 2024).
Third, AI customer service should be consistent with the promises made by the streamer, the
product detail pages, and after -sales service. When live explanations, customer -service responses and
actual service delivery contradict one another, consumer trust  in the brand can easily fall back to the

starting point or even deteriorate.
More concretely, brands can operationalise AI customer service as a decision-support workflow: a
structured intake of skin type and skin concerns; shade - and product-matching prompts that translate
stated needs into specific recommendations; ingredient and suitability checks that flag potential irritants
or contraindications; and clear escalation to human agents when a query exceeds the system’s
competence or involves dissatisfaction and service recovery. From a service-operations standpoint, the
AI layer s hould be integrated with inventory, promotion, and after -sales systems and governed by
consistency checks that align streamer claims, product -detail pages, and AI responses, because
contradictions across these touchpoints are precisely where trust erodes. This reframes AI customer
service from a cost -reduction channel into a managed component of the platform’s digital service
system.
6. Limitations and Future Research
Despite using real questionnaire data to explore the mechanism through which AI customer service
experience influences consumer brand loyalty in cosmetics live -streaming commerce, this study has
several limitations. First, cross-sectional questionnaire data were used. Such data can reveal correlations
and theoretical paths among variables but provide little insight into the dynamic evolution among AI
customer service experience, trust, satisfaction, and brand loyalty. Repeated live- streaming shopping
experiences, brand -fulfilment performance and after -sales service may all influence consumers’
evaluation of AI customer service. Future research could employ longitudinal tracking designs or
experimental approaches to examine trust building, satisfaction change , and loyalty development at
different time points.
Second, the study mainly focused on pre-sale and in-sale consultation in cosmetics live-streaming
shopping and did not sufficiently incorporate after -sales situations such as complaints, returns and
exchanges, service recovery, and negative -experience management. Previous studies have suggested
that the role of chatbots in service failure, high- emotion and customer -dissatisfaction contexts may
differ from their role in routine consultation. Future research could therefore compare routine
consultation with service-recovery tasks to explore the differentiated effects of AI customer service on
consumer trust, satisfaction, and brand loyalty under different service-pressure conditions.
Third, brand loyalty was used as an outcome variable focusing on consumers’ continued brand
preference, repurchase, and recommendation tendencies. Future research could deconstruct brand
loyalty into multiple dimensions such as repurchase intention, recomm endation intention, price -
premium acceptance, brand tolerance, and cross -channel continued purchasing. Such differentiation
would enable a more accurate identification of the effects of AI customer service experience on different
loyalty outcomes. In cosme tics consumption, consumers may repurchase a particular brand without
accepting a higher price, or may be willing to recommend only a single product without showing overall
brand loyalty. Thus, disaggregating brand loyalty could improve the explanatory pow er of future
research.
Fourth, this study investigated the chain mechanism of AI customer service experience on brand
loyalty through perceived diagnosticity, trust, and satisfaction, but did not examine boundary conditions
such as consumer and product characteristics in depth. Future research could consider moderators such
as perceived product uncertainty, product involvement, prior brand experience, AI technology
acceptance, cosmetics knowledge, or privacy concerns. This would help to clarify which consumer
groups, product-judgement tasks and live-interaction scenarios make AI customer service more likely
to function effectively.
Fifth, several features of the design constrain generalisation and warrant caution. The sample was
obtained through non-probability online recruitment, so it captures, rather than statistically represents,
the relevant consumer segments. The measures are s elf-reports collected after the interaction and are

Gan et al., Journal of Logistics, Informatics and Service Science, Vol. 13 (2026), No 10, pp 173-200
196

therefore subject to recall and common-method biases; although Harman’s single-factor test and a full-
collinearity assessment indicate that these biases are not severe, residual common -method variance
cannot be ruled out entirely. Moreover, the outcomes ar e perceptual and intentional rather than
behavioural, as the study does not observe actual repurchase or recommendation behaviour. Future
work could combine probability or quota sampling, multi-source or temporally separated measurement,
and behavioural or platform-log data to strengthen both causal and external validity.
