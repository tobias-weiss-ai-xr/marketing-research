Abstract
Introduction: Digital transformation has forced small and medium enterprises (SMEs) to rethink their
communication and business models, and in that process, social media has gained relevance as an accessible
channel to strengthen visibility, relationships, and brand positioning. However, its effect on sales does not occur
automatically. Objective: To analyze how the use of social media influences the perceived sales of SMEs and what
factors modulate that relationship. Methodology: A multidisciplinary System atic Literature Review (SLR) was
conducted, guided by the PRISMA 2020 protocol and structured with the PICO framework; the search was carried
out in Scopus, Web of Science (WOS), Dimensions.ai, Scielo, and Latindex 2.0, in Spanish and English, and was
complemented with the snowball technique. Results: The evidence showed a positive relationship between the
use of social media and perceived sales, although with unequal magnitude depending on the strategy, sector,
geographical context, and organizational capacity. The best results were observed when the digital presence was
articulated with relevant content, segmentation, professional management, and brand building. Furthermore,
brand image (branding) and consumer engagement acted as relevant mediators, while training barriers, low
digital sophistication, and cybersecurity risks limited commercial exploitation, especially within Latin American
contexts such as Ecuador.

Keywords
Social media; SMEs; perceived sales; digital marketing; brand image.

Introduction
Digital transformation has reshaped the competitive dynamics of markets in recent decades, forcing companies
of all sizes to rethink their business models and how they communicate. In this study, small and medium -sized
enterprises (SMEs) are central, as th ey represent more than 90% of businesses worldwide and are essential for
job creation, innovation, and economic resilience (Ukshini et al., 2025). However, despite their importance, SMEs
face certain limitations that must be considered, such as difficulty accessing financing, limited technological
infrastructure, and high exposure to market volatility. Global crises (like the COVID -19 pandemic in 202 0) have
had the potential to exacerbate these limitations by increasing the need for SMEs to implement more flexible
and digitized business models. Because of these limitations, social media has become one of the most accessible
and cost-effective channels for SMEs to strengthen customer relationships, brand visibility, and overall market
reach. Social networks like Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, and WhatsApp provide direct communication channels
that foster greater consumer engagement and help establish long -term relationships with the brand's target
audience (Villon et al., 2025). The ability to build relationships at low cost is crucial for SMEs, as traditional
advertising channels remain inaccessible due to budget constraints.

For its part, academic literature has considered the relationships arising from the influence of networks on
business, identifying social network-based business as a separate area with specific boundaries, in which network
interactions and the business processes carried out within them can be used to strengthen customer relationship
management and provide competitive advantages (Hadiyati et al., 2024). Furthermore, in SMEs, strategies and
tactics for network business are geared towards customer orientation and competition.
A key aspect of this work is branding. Studies such as Bruce et al. (2025) mention that social media acts as a
privileged channel for building corporate identity: sustained interaction on these platforms creates and
reinforces consumer loyalty, generates trust, and helps brands differentiate themselves from their competitors.
Having a strong brand image not only attracts customers but also serves as a bridge between social media use
and business performance, acting as a mediator in the relationship between digital presence and business results.
Therefore, SMEs that manage to build a consistent and authentic brand in the digital environment are more likely
to convert their social media activities into increased sales and customer loyalty.
However, the connection between digital marketing, branding, and business performance is neither automatic
nor uniform. Social media marketing directly impacts business performance in its economic, environmental, and
social dimensions, although its effect as a mediator between market orientation and organizational results may
be limited due to certain context and industry -specific factors. Because of this complexity, SMEs should not
assume that a simple social media presence is enough to guarantee results. Such a presence must be
accompanied by solid strategies, clear objectives, and organizational capabilities that allow them to capitalize on
digital opportunities (Munawar et al., 2024).
The work by Ukshini et al. (2025) provides strong evidence of a positive and moderately strong relationship
between digital marketing and SME performance. However, the authors also note significant heterogeneity in
the results depending on the industry sec tor, geographic region, and methodological design of the studies. In
particular, service-oriented SMEs tend to benefit more from digital marketing than manufacturing SMEs, as the
former business model relies more heavily on customer interaction. This nuanc ed evidence encourages an
analysis of the underlying factors behind the perceived effects of social media on sales, rather than assuming
uniform causality.
Similarly, the adoption of digital marketing by SMEs is determined by technological, organizational, and
environmental factors. The TOE (Technology, Organization, and Environment) framework, proposed by Tornatzky
(1992) and applied to Indonesian SMEs by Mu’Min et al. (2025), shows that, among technological, organizational,
and environmental factors, technological capabilities are the most important predictors of e-marketing adoption,
followed by organizational capabilities and competitive environmental pres sures. These findings are particularly
relevant for emerging economies, where digital barriers, limited resources, and institutional constraints create a
different context than in developed economies, affecting both the pace and depth of digital transforma tion.
In Latin America, and specifically in Ecuador, SMEs are beginning to experience the digitization of business
processes in an uneven manner and within a context of structural resistance. In the city of Quito, Baquero et al.
(2025)a research study in the northern part of the city that 63.6% of SMEs lack qualified personnel in digitization,
and that although WhatsApp is widely used, it is primarily used in its personal version rather than its business
version. This exemplifies the barrier between the existence  of digital tools and the lack of capacity to use them
deliberately or knowledgeably, which can result in perceived sales that do not reflect actual activity on social
media. Digitalization goes far beyond using tools like computers; it includes profound cultural and organizational
changes. In this sense, the use of digital marketing based on Artificial Intelligence (AI) is the most viable way to
optimize processes and competit ive advantages for SMEs, although most of them face a lack of technical
knowledge and high initial costs; this obstacle is one of the most significant challenges in today's business
ecosystem (Muñoz, 2024).
Given this situation, the relevance of the concept of perceived sales is clear. Beyond quantitative revenue
indicators, the perception of SME owners and managers regarding the impact of social media on their sales is
critical for measuring the importance o f digital marketing as a business strategy. Analyzing this perception
illuminates the numerous benefits derived from social media, including brand visibility, increased consumer trust,
and improved customer relationships.

METHODOLOGY
For the methodology of this work, the tools and types of studies that have been considered for the construction
of the research are explained, which has been structured as a multidisciplinary Systematic Literature Review
(SLR), including areas such as digi tal marketing, business management and consumer behavior economics.
Similarly, this research aims to identify, compare, and synthesize the evidence presented in studies addressing
the relationship between social media use by SMEs and its impact on sales. Furthermore, due to the type of

analysis, the research focuses on the use of social media for commercial purposes, with a results -oriented
approach that considers not only the functional aspects of digital platforms in social areas but also the
interrelationship between a marketing strat egy and social media, a specific brand perception, consumer
behavior, and the organizational, sectoral, and geographic factors that influence the relationship between SME
digitalization and the economy.
The RSL was structured in the following stages: identification, review, eligibility and synthesis, following a
methodological transparency structure based on the one proposed by Page et al. (2021), which is based on the
PRISMA protocol (Preferred Reporting Elements for Systematic Reviews and Meta-analyses) and with which each
of the inclusion and exclusion criteria is established.  Consequently, one of the adapted methodologies that
served as a guide for the search and aligned the objective, criteria, and synthesis was the PICO framework –
Population, Intervention, Comparison, and Outcome (Richardson et al., 1995). Although this fram ework was
designed for use in clinical settings, its application in the business and digital marketing context is valid, as it
proved ideal for addressing the study's objectives. The population consists of managers or SMEs from diverse
sectors and geographic locations that incorporate social media into their business strategies; the intervention is
the active and strategic incorporation of social media (such as Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, WhatsApp, among
others) as a means of marketing, customer management, brand visibility, and branding. The comparison is made
by comparing companies that, in terms of their business strategies, have a deficient or nonexistent presence on
social networks, as well as the contexts prior to the implementation of digital marketi ng strategies and studies
that compare impacts of different levels or types of use; and as results, sales and/or business performance and/or
brand image, engagement, digital visibility and the impact modulators (sectoral, organizational and contextual)
of activity on networks.
Based on this framework, the central research question guiding this study is:
- How does the use of social media influence perceived sales for SMEs, and what factors
modulate the relationship between digital activity and business results, compared to contexts of low or
no digital presence?
Furthermore, to identify relevant literature, international academic and multidisciplinary databases were used,
selected for their coverage and output on digital marketing, business performance in SMEs, and social media:
Scopus and Dimensions.ai. Recognized indexes such as Latindex 2.0 were also consulted. Scopus was chosen for
its broad coverage of publications in social sciences, management, and marketing, and for the robustness of its
search and filtering tools. Dimensions.ai, on the other hand, was incl uded for its open access, its thematic
breadth, and its ability to identify empirical studies from regions underrepresented in commercial databases,
including Latin America.
In these databases, the search strategy was executed in two languages: Spanish and English; and was
complemented with the snowball technique from highly relevant documents to reduce dependence on keyword
matching and thus be able to capture relevant studies sub-indexed with the exact terms used. Therefore, search
strings were used for the selected databases, which were modified to encompass the intersection of social media
usage analysis, SMEs, and business or sales results, avoiding overly general searches on digital marketing and
without subjecting them to the business performance dimension of the network.

The following inclusion criteria were considered: empirical articles or reviews with explicit methodology –RSL,
integrative reviews with clear criteria, case studies with defined designs, and quantitative, qualitative, or mixed
studies with described contexts–; studies that concentrate on SMEs or micro-enterprises that use social networks
as a marketing or commercial communication strategy, focusing on the organization or company itself, owner or
manager, or on the consumer, given the function of digital in teraction; studies that include the use of social
media platforms as a marketing, branding, or customer communication tool; articles on perceived sales, business
performance, brand image, engagement, or any other business outcome related to the use of soci al networks;
and a preference for works published in Spanish or English.
Conversely, the exclusion criteria were: reflections or essays without a concrete methodology, or descriptions of
digital platforms without an evident relationship to commercial results; studies that focus only on large
companies or multinational corporations whose results are not generalizable to the context of SMEs; studies that

RESULTS
The 32 studies included in the qualitative synthesis encompass diverse geographic and sectoral contexts —
including emerging economies in Latin America, Asia, and the Middle East, as well as more established economies
like China —and employ heterogeneous meth odologies ranging from longitudinal case studies and PLS -SEM
(Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling) to meta -analyses and time series analysis. This
methodological and contextual diversity, far from being a limitation, allowed for the identifi cation of robust
cross-sectional patterns regarding the relationship between social media use in SMEs and its effects on perceived
business performance, while also revealing nuances and conditioning factors that a homogeneous perspective
would fail to capture.
Similarly, the results are organized into five complementary thematic areas. First, the adoption patterns of social
media in SMEs and the factors that influence them are described; second, the impact of this adoption on
perceived sales and business perform ance is analyzed; third, the mediating role of brand image and consumer
engagement in this relationship is examined, connecting to the next point, which identifies the modulating
factors (sectoral, geographic, and organizational) that explain the heterogen eity of the results across contexts.
Finally, the digital barriers or limitations, as well as the structural challenges that affect SMEs' actual capacity to
capitalize on their presence on digital platforms, are addressed.

Use and adoption patterns of social networks in SMEs: platforms, strategies and implementation contexts
The adoption of social media by SMEs has an uneven trajectory, conditioned by the type of platform and the
organizational capabilities of the business, as well as by the socioeconomic context in which they operate (Minh
& Le, 2025). The studies analyzed allow us to identify recurring patterns in this adoption and highlight distinctive
factors in geographical and sectoral contexts, which are crucial for understanding how SMEs integrate into the
digital ecosystem.

From a global perspective, social media platforms have become the dominant digital presence channel for micro,
small, and medium -sized enterprises (MSMEs). As observed in studies by Molina & Avalos (2024)  published
between 2020 and 2024, 78% of the MSMEs studied are social media users who continuously engage in gathering
competitive intelligence and managing business communications. Furthermore, the strategic use of integrated
platforms, such as those that combine e-commerce, social media management, and real -time data analytics, is
associated with an average increase of 45% in online visibility and a 32% increase in sales conversions. These
findings emphasize that simply having a social media presence is insufficient; effectiveness stems from the ability
to integrate multiple digital functions into a single, coherent strategy.

From a more local perspective, in Ecuador, where specific characteristics are observed within the Latin American
context, an empirical barrier can be identified that distinguishes the availability of tools from the intended
capacity to use them. A descript ive-quantitative study conducted with 35 SMEs in northern Quito revealed that
72.7% of respondents use WhatsApp to communicate with their customers. However, most of them use personal
accounts instead of WhatsApp Business, a commercial version of the appli cation that allows users to create a
catalog and set up automated responses, thus significantly limiting their commercial potential. Furthermore, only
4.5% of SME owners mention using websites or online stores as their primary means of communication,
indicating a low level of digital sophistication. Additionally, 63.6% of SMEs do not have staff dedicated to digital
management, suggesting that the adoption of social media is an improvised effort and lacks strategic planning
(Baquero et al., 2025).

Authors such as [author names omitted Pinto & Granja (2022)] describe how the use of social media by SMEs for
commercial purposes has increased in response to consumer behavior in digital environments. In their research,
they state that in purchasing decis ion-making processes, consumers are positively impacted by marketing
strategies implemented through social networks such as Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, and YouTube (even
though the latter is more of a digital platform than a social network per se). Of a ll the companies surveyed in
their study, 90% consider social media essential to their marketing strategy, while 65% of Ecuadorian digital
consumers believe that researching on social media before making a purchase is fundamental.

This data proves that the incorporation of social media into the business strategy of SMEs is a growing trend,
enabling consumers to find and engage with brands. Therefore, companies that fail to adapt to this digital
consumer reality are excluded from the purchasing process, highlighting the urgency with which they must adapt.
On the other hand, the adoption of digital marketing in resource -constrained environments is addressed with a
degree of theoretical sophistication using the Theory of Environment (TOE) framework. This framework examines

how the conditions of different technologies, the capabilities of different organizations, and the varying
competitive landscape either contribute to or constrain technology adoption decisions. Applying this framework,
Mu'Min et al. (2025), in their resear ch focused on SMEs in Mimika, Indonesia, using PLS -SEM, demonstrate that
technological forces are the most relevant variable in the adoption of digital marketing, followed by
organizational forces and competitive pressures. This finding has direct implicat ions for SMEs in emerging
economies, particularly regarding technological readiness —understood as the availability of infrastructure,
platform compatibility, and access to devices —which is the prerequisite for any digital adoption process, even
more so than organizational incentives or market pressures. In connection with this approach, the authors Minh
& Le (2025)applied the same framework, TOE, in the tourism sector of Vietnam, collecting data from 345
managers and owners of SMEs, where they demonstrate that the perceived relative advantage of adopting Social
Media Marketing (SMM) is the most influential technological factor (β = 0.167, p < 0.001), while competitive
pressure is the strongest determinant in the contextual framework (β = 0.498, p < 0.001), r epresenting 68% of
the variation in SMM adoption.

The results indicate that, in markets exhibiting high dynamism and where competition in the sectors is intense,
the pressure from competitors who have already incorporated the use of social networks becomes the main
catalyst for the adoption of social networks, above any technological or organizational considerations. In another
instance, the strategic dimension of digital adoption takes on particular importance in the context of e-commerce
and the digital economy. E-commerce businesses selling goods or services online must move from large, generic
marketing strategies to data-driven, personalized, and customer-centric approaches based on the analysis of vast
amounts of data (Ma & Gu, 2024). This indicates that simply having a social media account is not enough.
Furthermore, a content strategy, audience segmentation, and  intelligent data utilization across social media
platforms must be implemented to ensure that digital adoption produces tangible results, rather than just activity
that has no impact on the company's revenue.

 Impact of social networks on perceived sales and business performance
The impact of social media use on SME sales is positive. This premise is supported by the selected literature,
although not to the same degree. The magnitude of the positive impact is measured by the management
approach, the platforms used, the sector, and the organization's use of digital tools. Of all the available empirical
evidence, the case study research conducted by Villon et al. (2025) provides the most direct evidence. Using
mixed-methods, longitudinal, and correlational design, the authors examined 5,000 social media posts, as well
as recorded historical monthly sales data for the periods before and after the implementation of strategies on a
company's digital platforms. The results showed an average increase in monthly sales of 51.8% after social media
adoption, and there was a strong, po sitive, and statistically significant correlation between digital business
activities and sales (r = 0.83; p < 0.001). In this regard, the authors state that “the strong positive correlation (r =
0.83) between social media activity and monthly sales confir ms that an active and strategic digital presence is
essential for business growth in the current era” (Villon et al., 2025). In Latin America, the context of this finding
is substantial, since, in a field that has long been seen as resistant to digital (th e hardware sector), it is now
possible to demonstrate that social platforms implemented strategically produce beneficial results for the
commercial sector.

From a multi -sectoral perspective, one study, measuring the impact of digital marketing on SME performance
using a random -effects model estimated with the Restricted Maximum Likelihood (REML) method, reports a
statistically significant and positive relatio nship between digital marketing and business performance, with
remarkable consistency across studies (Ukshini et al., 2025). This suggests that the impact of digital marketing on
SME performance is robust and consistent, independent of geographic or methodological context. Furthermore,
the authors' analysis revealed that SMEs in the service sector enjoyed more pronounced performance benefits
than their manufacturing counterparts, highlighting the importance of the nature of digital customer interaction
in each segment.

Regarding the predictive capacity of Customer Engagement (CE) on social media for operating income, the
researcher Matosas (2024)  conducted a study specifically examining this relationship in 100 retail SMEs. These
SMEs were monitored for eight years using time series analysis with ARIMA (Autoregressive Integrated Moving
Average) models. The study found that internally managed social  media CE, especially when handled by non -
specialized personnel, cannot be used as a predictor of operating income. However, when social media
management is outsourced, combined with CE and other digital marketing strategies, operating income can be
predicted in 45 of the 50 cases analyzed. This analysis highlights a key distinction: the relationship between social

When evaluating the relationship between the size of an enterprise social network and business performance,
few tools are effective. However, models such as the two -way fixed effects model are functional, as
demonstrated by Le & Tien (2025)  in their work analyzing this relationship using this model. The results show
several important findings, indicating that a 1% increase in enterprise social networks was associated with a 2.8
percentage point increase in incremental product innovation and a  1.5 percentage point increase in process
innovation. Furthermore, larger social networks were positively associated with higher real revenue and profit
values, as well as with return on assets, demonstrating that enterprise social networks function not on ly as a
sales channel but also as an engine for innovation and improved profitability.

In turn, authors such as Hadiyati et al. (2024) examined the mediating frameworks involved in the relationship
between digital marketing and business performance for SMEs in the creative industry of East Java, Indonesia.
Using PLS-SEM, based on data collected from 172 companies, the authors found that digital marketing does not
have a significant direct effect on business performance (coefficient = 0.166; p = 0.076), but it has a significant
indirect effect through Customer Relationship Management (CRM) wit h a coefficient of 0.126 and a p -value of
0.006, and through competitiveness with a coefficient of 0.260 and a p -value of 0.000.

On the other hand, the factors that affect the use of web-based social networks and brand awareness, as well as
sales growth and customer engagement, are those linked to theories such as UTAUT2 (Unified Technology Use
and Acceptance Theory 2) and Task -Technology Fit; these include performance expectancy, effort expectancy,
and social influence (Novillo, 2025). In the Latin American context, a study with similar findings obtained through
a multivariate analysis of SMEs in non -metropolitan areas of Ecuador es tablishes that web presence and sales
growth are positively correlated, with values of r = 0.62 and a 95% confidence interval; this is especially true
when combined with other elements of the SEO (Search Engine Optimization) strategy (Alkhasoneh et al., 2025).
In addition, the authors, in their comparative analysis, show that the use of social media is associated with a 22%
increase in sales and a 38% increase in organic visibility, demonstrating the complementary role of social media
within a broader digital ecosystem.

The mediating role of brand image and consumer engagement in the social media -sales relationship
The evaluation of the mechanisms through which social media influences perceived sales in SMEs is not limited
to a direct, unidirectional relationship, but rather is two-dimensional. Recent studies show that two psychological
and strategic variables — brand image and consumer engagement —play a significant mediating role in this
relationship (Baquero et al., 2025). Thus, social media does not automatically increase sales; it does so by
changing the public's perception of the brand and increasing the depth of interaction and customer loyalty.

In an empirical study, authors such as Bruce et al. (2025) document the mediating role of branding through the
evaluation of startups and develop a model. Their findings, compiled using PLS -SEM analysis, show that brand
image is positive (β = 0.401; p < 0.001) and has a mediating effect on business performance (β = 0.287; p < 0.001).
The model yielded an R² of 0.689 and a startup performance score of 0.717, indicating that over 68% of the
variance in business outcomes was attributable to the constructs incl uded in the model.

 Factors modulating the impact: economic sector, geographical context and organizational capacity
The impact of social media use on SME sales is not consistent across all contexts. Recent empirical evidence
illustrates that this impact is limited by at least three cross-cutting dimensions: the economic sector in which the
company operates, its geograph ic location, and its organizational capacity to absorb and leverage digital tools.

Considering these dimensions as modulating factors helps explain why two companies with similar digital
strategies can achieve different results.

In the economic sector, a company's type of productive activity substantially determines the results of its digital
marketing efforts. As described by Feng et al. (2024)in their empirical analysis of B2B e -commerce (business-to-
business e -commerce) based o n data from 325 service providers on the Chinese platform Alibaba, they
demonstrated that service, logistics, and production capabilities impact sales performance through the mediator
of customer service. They also found that these capabilities are valued differently depending on the type of
provider. For example, certified OEMs (Original Equipment Manufacturers) must showcase their service and
logistics capabilities more than non -OEM providers to attract customer attention. This example illustrates how
the business model influences the impact of digital presence on business performance, even within the same
industry. Another study, focusing on the food, beverage, and handicrafts sector, observes that these industries
account for the largest volume of digital transactions through social media, at least in Indonesia. Furthermore,
social media positively impacts business performance, and this is particularly relevant in cases where business
owners possess an entrepreneurial orientation, acting as a moderating factor (Fiona et al., 2024).

In contrast, authors such as [author's name] Kerti et al. (2024), in their study involving SMEs in traditional textile
production, found that while digital marketing strategies have a positive and significant impact on sustainable
business performance, digital orientation alone does not impact performance when there is no business strategy
mediating the cultural factor, such as the Tri Hita Karana model, which is based on harmony with God
(Parahyangan), with people (Pawongan), and with the natural environme nt (Palemahan). This finding suggests
that, in sectors with a strong cultural and identity component, non -digital factors condition the extent to which
digital marketing tools translate into business results.

In another instance, the sectoral dimension is also noticeable in contexts where production processes are primary
or of an agro -industrial nature. Looking at poultry SMEs, the digital business of these companies can offer a
glimpse into virtual economic growth and sustainability indicators linked to the SDGs (Sustainable Development
Goals). This confirms that these SMEs, which have achieved the status of a fully integrated digital business —
characterized by social networks, e-commerce sales platforms, and distribution and storage systems—can shape
the local economy around a network with a master company and its counterparts (Chaichana et al., 2024).
However, this phenomenon is limited to very few cases, indicating that, even within the same sector, the
possibilities for digitalization vary considerably.

For narrative continuity, the literature mentions another modulator, which refers to the geographical context,
encompassing the technology, available infrastructure, and institutional, cultural, and macroeconomic conditions
of the context in which the comp any operates. The authors Shams et al. (2025) specifically studied this variable
in the context of Saudi Arabia, where SMEs constitute 99% of the business sector, emphasizing the SEM -PLS
(Partial Least Squares Structural Modeling) methodology. Using this m ethodology, they processed 300 SME
databases and established that environmental factors —defined as competitive pressure and perceived relative
advantage—positively affect companies' dynamic social networking capabilities (β = 0.486; p < 0.001), even more
so than internal technological factors (β = 0.421). Therefore, the external environment —understood as the
competitive level, the digital level of the economy, and the amount of legislative support for the business sector—
is a decisive factor in SMEs' ability to generate wealth by leveraging their social networks.

Therefore, the geographical context also explains the differences between developing countries, with varying
levels of digital maturity. As explained by Gao (2025), in their study, which comparatively analyzes the economies
of China and Kazakhstan, which have distinct trajectories, the researchers found that between 2018 and 2021, in
China, according to the Business Digital Transformation Index, companies increased the digitization of their
businesses from 37 to 54 points; more than 53% of the companies surveyed planned to double their investments.
In Kazakhstan, growth was also evident; however, the institutional foundation was less consolidated. The study
also revealed that organizational capacity for digital transformation was a mediating factor between d igital
marketing initiatives and business performance, and that consumer engagement was the most significant
predictor of this mediation, more so than investment in technology. The comparative analysis of both countries
demonstrated that the same digitizat ion model can be applied to different realities depending on the country's
institutional context.

Digital divides and structural challenges in the digital transformation of SMEs
Digital transformation has become an essential strategic condition for the competitiveness of SMEs. However,
accumulated empirical evidence demonstrates that a barrier persists between the goal of digitizing and the actual
capacity to do so, defined by str uctural factors that go beyond the acquisition of technological tools.
Understanding these obstacles is fundamental to explaining why, despite the availability of digital platforms and
social networks, a large segment of SMEs has been unable to establish a  sustained online presence, with
inadequate internal technical capacity being one of the most documented barriers.

The author Muñoz (2024) He pointed out that, although 60% of SMEs claim to be interested in incorporating AI
tools into their digital marketing strategies, only 30% do so. The author highlights that “a lack of technical
knowledge and high initial costs are the most common barriers,” along with concerns about information security.
This triad of barriers —knowledge, capital, and trust —creates an environment where the digitization process is
uneven, benefiting companies that already have available resources an d specialized talent, and leaving behind
those with a greater need for the efficiency that technology provides.

From the perspective of developing economies, digital marketing capabilities and the technologies themselves
influence the financial and operational performance of e-commerce-based SMEs, as digital marketing campaigns
are optimized and mobile commerce (whi ch represents 71% of online sales) is key. Therefore, SMEs are
compelled to have a strong digital presence (Mittal & Kumar, 2024). However, differences in digital infrastructure
and technological literacy between developed and developing countries create s tructural disparities that
determine the pace and depth of digitalization, especially in developing economies with highly restricted
technology financing. A relevant finding for understanding these barriers comes from the work of [author's name
missing] Anjaningrum et al. (2024), which, from the perspective of PLS -SEM applied to 313 SMEs in the creative
economy sector in East Java, shows that business intelligence does not directly affect business performance.
Therefore, for data to be valuable in terms of competitive advantage, it must be managed through organizational
learning as a mediating variable. This finding challenges the assumption that incorporating technology always
improves results, since SMEs also need to develop the capacity to interpret, lear n, and innovate based on the
digital information produced. This is especially true in developing economies where resources are scarce and the
technological systems gap between large corporations and small businesses is significant.

dimension that increasingly affects the sustainability of SMEs' digital presence: cybersecurity. According to
[author's name] Alarcón et  al. (2025), in their study on the trend of cyberattacks in European industry,
ransomware (malicious software that encrypts and holds a ransom for the victim's data) has maintained steady

DISCUSSION
The findings of this review help determine the relationship between social media use in SMEs and its impact on
perceived sales. The evidence points to a complex phenomenon, conditioned by strategic, organizational,
sectoral, and contextual factors that det ermine the extent to which digital activity translates into concrete
business results.  Simply using a social network does not guarantee increased sales. This fact is necessary to
understand the literature that has yielded slightly different results. In the case of SMEs, those with a social media
presence experience a 45% increase in visibility and a 32% increase in conversion rate only after institutionalizing
a social media strategy. In Ecuadorian literature, the use of personal WhatsApp instead of WhatsApp Business
demonstrates a lack of usage, reflecting a barrier between simply adopting a tool and using it effectively. Matosas
(2024) provides a relevant methodological example where predictive modeling was applied to differentiate
between in-house social media management by non-specialists (which does not forecast operating income) and
outsourced social media management, which does (in 45 out of 50 cases). Therefore, it is suggested that the
connection between digital activity and revenue depends on the quality of management rather than the
frequency of activity, nor does it depend on audience size. This positive effect of  deliberate social media use on
business performance is strong under the right circumstances, with digital strategy implementation resulting in
an average monthly sales increase of 51.8%, as well as a correlation between social media activity and sales
results of r = 0.83. Furthermore, digital marketing and SME performance demonstrate a positive and consistent
relationship, independent of specific geographic or methodological contexts.

Another important point of discussion is brand image or branding as a mediator and consumer engagement. In
the compiled literature, while the use of social media has a direct positive impact on business performance,
branding is the construct with the highe st coefficient in the model, positioning this aspect as the dominant
mechanism through which social media converts visibility into business results. Along these lines, Safeer et al.
(2025) found that user -generated content (specifically, photos shared on s ocial media) has a direct impact on
purchase intention through the informativeness and persuasive power of the content, implying that engagement
is not a vanity metric, but rather a functional mechanism linked to consumer behavior. In the case of influence r
marketing in the local context, this reasoning is supported: as Baquero et al. (2025) found in the city of Quito,
small influencers with niche audiences have demonstrated greater credibility and effectiveness in brand building
than those with mass audiences.

In another instance, the heterogeneity of results across economic sectors and geographic contexts determines
certain things; digital marketing does not have a direct and significant effect on business performance but rather
operates indirectly through customer relationship management and competitiveness as mediating variables. This
finding regarding sectors with a strong cultural and identity burden suggests that digitization models are not
universally transferable: they need to be contextualized to the org anizational and cultural environment of each
company. As the comparative study of China and Kazakhstan illustrates, the same digitization model leads to
different results depending on the institutional maturity of the environment.

Therefore, digital barriers and structural challenges constitute the real limit of digital transformation in SMEs.
The triad of digital barriers described in the results includes the following: lack of technical knowledge, high
investment costs, and distrust in security. These factors mean that only 30% of SMEs interested in AI tools
implement them, while the perception of cybersecurity as a challenge has gained prominence.

CONCLUSIONS
The analysis concluded that social media use positively influences perceived sales for SMEs. However, this effect
should not be interpreted as automatic or uniform across all companies; the RSL (Research on Social  Media)
shows that a digital presence does not always translate into improved sales, and that positive results only appear
in companies that use social media as part of a marketing strategy that includes objectives, audience segments,
appropriate content, a nd, subsequently, effective management that transforms digital activity into tangible
results. In this sense, the research question is answered by verifying that the relationship between social
networks and perceived sales involves multiple mediating factors that explain the difference in results between
companies with a presence on the same social networks. The approach of this multidisciplinary systematic review
allows for a detailed and contextualized analysis of the findings regarding this phenomenon across different
geographic locations, economic regions, and analytical levels.

Therefore, social media doesn't contribute significant value to SMEs simply because it's a channel for frequent
advertising, as impressions aren't the primary goal of social marketing value. For SMEs, social media marketing
and traditional marketing should be integrated. The actions that social media allows within a marketing strategy
add value to SMEs when they foster trust, brand awareness, and a connection between people and customers
with the brand image (branding), ultimately leading to increased sales .

In addition, the literature reviewed suggests that the use of social media, alongside a commercial ecosystem,
generates business synergies that translate into increased visibility, sales conversion rates, revenue generation,
and levels of innovation and pr ofitability. However, the effect depends on the quality of the professionalization
of the system's structure. Furthermore, engagement and branding are identified as key mediators, representing
the social intersection of trust, brand ethos, and customer con nection, as well as reflecting purchase intent.  In
conclusion, the review reveals several factors that limit the impact of digital transformation and cannot be
ignored: the economic sector, geographic context, and level of organization. Service -oriented SMEs tend to
benefit more than manufacturing SMEs,  as is the case in the Andean region, particularly in Ecuador, where the
unprofessional use of tools, a lack of qualified personnel, high investment costs, insufficient technical training,
and increasing cybersecurity concerns create permanent structural c onstraints. Consequently, the digital
transformation of SMEs must be understood as an organizational and strategic process, not merely a
technological one. Therefore, the use of social media can help increase sales, but for this to happen, companies
must develop skills, build a brand proposition, and be able to convert their digital presence into financial results.
