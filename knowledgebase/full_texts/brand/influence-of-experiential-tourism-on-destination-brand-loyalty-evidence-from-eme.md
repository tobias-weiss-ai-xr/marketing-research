Experiential tourism has emerged as a central strategy through which
destinations in emerging markets differentiate themselves and cultivate
long-term visitor commitment. Although destination image, tourist
engagement, and destination trust have each been studied individually as
antecedents of loyalty, comparatively little empirical work has examined
how these mechanisms jointly translate multi -dimensional tourist
experiences into destination brand loyalty within the specific context of
emerging-market destinations. To close this gap, the present study develops
and tests an Experiential Tourism -Brand Loyalty framework that integrates
five experiential dimensions (sense, feel, think, act, relate) with destination
brand image (DBI), tourist engagement (TE) and destination brand trust
(DBT) as mediating mechanisms leading to destination brand loyalty (DBL).
Data is gathered through structured questionnaire administered to tourists
visiting destinations across emerging economies and examined utilizing
Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modelling (PLS-SEM). Reliability of
the measurement approach confirmed by indicator loadings, Cronbach's
alpha, composite reliability, average variance extracted, the Fornell -Larcker
criterion, and heterotrait -monotrait ratio, while the structural model is
assessed under path coefficients, the coefficient of determination (R²),
predictive relevance (Q²), effect size (f²), and importance -performance map
analysis (IPMA). The results show that the experiential tourism exerts a
significant positive influence on DBI, TE and DBT, each of which, in turn,
positively predicts destination brand loyalty; tourist engagement emerges as
the strongest mediating pathway. This work contributes to the body of
experiential marketing literature by expanding it to the emerging markets
tourism scenario and provides practical insight for destination management
organizations on creating distinctive tourist experiences.

1. Introduction
Tourism destinations in emerging markets increasingly compete not merely on the basis of natural
or cultural endowments but on the quality and distinctiveness of the experiences they offer visitors.
As disposable incomes rise and travel becomes more accessible across Asia, Africa, Latin America,
and parts of the Middle East, tourists are progressively seeking immersive, memorable, and
emotionally resonant encounters rather than standardized packaged services. It is in keeping with
the trend that sees a move away from the economy of service towards the economy of experience,

where value is created by engaging one's senses, emotions, cognitive abilities, behavior, and social
nature at the destination. This has made it important for destination management organizations to
know more about the part of experiential tourism on tourist loyalty to the destination brand.
Loyalty towards destination brands that usually characterized by repeated visits and favorable
word-of-mouth recommendation as an essential element of sustainable destination
competitiveness due to the fact that retaining customers is much easier than attracting new ones.
The previous tourism literature has thoroughly analyzed the influences of destination image,
qualityof service and fulfilmentin loyalty. However, in emerging markets, where destinations often
lack the infrastructural sophistication of mature tourism economies, the experiential dimension of a
visit, that is, how a destination makes a tourist feel, think, and act, may play an outsized role in
brand formation. Despite this, few studies have integrated the full range of experiential dimensions
with the psychological mechanisms of brand image, engagement, and trust within a single
structural model calibrated to emerging-market contexts.
This study suggests an integrated model to close that gap, in this experiential tourism,
operationalised through the sense, feel, think, act, and relate dimensions, exerts both direct and
mediated influence on DBL through DBI, TE, and DBT. By simultaneously modelling these pathways,
the study offers a more complete account of how experiential encounters are converted into
durable brand relationships in emerging-market settings.
The important contributions of this study are as follows,
• Develops an integrated Experiential Tourism-Brand Loyalty framework linking five
experiential dimensions to destination brand loyalty through three parallel mediating mechanisms.
• Provides empirical evidence from emerging-market destinations, extending experiential
marketing and destination branding theory beyond the mature-market contexts in which it was
largely developed.
• Identifies tourist engagement as a distinct and managerially actionable mediating pathway
alongside the more commonly studied destination image and trust routes.
Novelty
The novelty of this research lies in jointly modelling three mediating constructs, DBI, TE and DBT, as
parallel pathways linking multi-dimensional experiential tourism to destination brand loyalty within
a single PLS -SEM model calibrated specifically to emerging -market tourists. Rather than treating
experience, image, engagement, and trust as isolated antecedents examined in separate studies,
this research demonstrates how they operate concurrently, thereby offering a complete and
context-sensitive account of brand constancy formation in developing tourism economies.

2.1 Research Gap
Despite the considerable progress reflected in the studies reviewed above, several gaps remain.
First, most prior research has examined experiential marketing, destination image, engagement, or
trust in isolation or in pairs, rather than as parallel, simultaneously operating mediating
mechanisms within a single model. Second, much of the existing evidence is drawn from single -
destination or single-country case studies in either mature tourism economies or narrowly defined
heritage and religious tourism contexts, limiting generalisability to the broader set of emerging -
market destinations that are increasingly competing for experience -seeking tourists. Third, the
specific role of tourist engagement, as distinct from destination image and trust, in mediating the
experiential tourism -brand loyalty relationship remains underexplored. This work address these
gaps by proposing and empirically testing acombined, multi -mediator model calibrated to
emerging-market tourism destinations.
2.2 Major Objective
• To assess the effect of experiential tourism on DBI, TE and DBT.
• To investigate the impacts ofDBI, TE and DBTon DBL.
• To assess the direct effect of experiential tourism on DBL.
• To examine the relationships among experiential tourism, its mediating mechanisms, and
destination brand loyalty in a holistic manner.
• To investigate the mediating effects of DBI, TE and DBT on the relation between experiential
tourism and DBL.
• To build and validate a holistic experiential tourism model for strengthening destination
brand loyalty in emerging markets.

4. Research Methodology
Using the intermediary variables of DBI, TE and DBT, the study used a quantitative research
approach to examine how experiential tourism affects destination brand loyalty. Primary data
collection was conducted through structured survey questionnaire that was distributed to tourists
who were visiting various destinations in emerging economies located in South and Southeast Asia,
East Africa, and Latin America. Purposive sampling technique sample tourists who have travelled to
the destination at least once during the previous twelve months so as to be able to appraise their
experiences. A minimum of 420 participants were required to meet the sample size criteria in PLS -
SEM model.
The survey was made up of two sections. In 1 stsection, demographic and travel -related variables
such as gender, age, nationality, education, occupation, travel frequency, and monthly income were
collected. The 2 ndsection of the questionnaire assessed the mentioned five constructs through
items drawn from existing scales that had been validated in the literatures on experiential
marketing and destination branding, each rated to five -point Likert -type scale (1 disagree to 5
agree). The data were evaluated under PLS -SEM for measurement and structural model testing.
Cronbach's alpha with composite reliability scaledependability; indicator loading and AVEscale
convergent validity; and the Fornell-Larcker criterion and HTMT ratio scale discriminant validity.

4.1 Proposed Statistical Model
The validity of the conceptual structure was confirmed by PLS -SEM via SmartPLS version 4 because
it is ideal for complicated multi -mediators where the samples may be of small to medium size
without multivariate normality.Analysis proceeded in two stages. First, the measurement mode was
measured for the dependabilityof internal consistency (Cronbach's alpha, composite reliability,
above the recommended 0.70 threshold), convergent strength (pointer loadings above 0.70 and
AVE above 0.50), discriminant strength (Fornell -Larcker criterion with HTMT ratios below 0.85).
Secondly, the structural mode estimated under path coefficients, R², Q² obtained via blindfolding,
f², VIF to rule out collinearity concerns. A bootstrapping approach with 5,000 resamples was used to
test hypotheses and define the importance of both direct and indirect (mediated) correlations.
IPMA was additionally led to findout which experiential and mediating constructs offer the greatest
potential leverage for improving destination brand loyalty.

5.2 Discussion
Tourism is an important element that helps in building destination brand loyalty at emerging
destinations. According to the findings, sensory, emotional, cognitive, behavioral, and social
experiences have anaffirmative effect on DBI, TE, and DBT, which help in building destination brand
loyalty. Out of these factors, tourist engagement was identified as the most effective mediator. It
shows that the tourists who actively engage in the destination experiences build loyalty more
effectively than those who do not actively participate in the experiences. The lesser relation of
experience tourism and destination brand loyalty further indicates that the experiences mainly
make the tourists loyal through their positive impressions and confidence about the destination.
These findings clearly show the significance of development of authentic and personalized
experiences at destinations to make them competitive in the market. Moreover, the suggested

6. Conclusion
This study developed and empirically tested an Experiential Tourism -Brand Loyalty framework to
explain how multi -dimensional tourist experiences translate into destination brand loyalty in
emerging-market tourism contexts. In addition to providing a more complete explanation of how
the correlationamong experiential tourism and destination brand loyalty can be conceptualized.
Specifically, it was found that experiential tourism had a significant impact on all three mediating
variables, which, in their turn, influenced destination brand loyalty. Tourist engagement turned out
to be the most important mediator among the three. Even though there was a significant link
between experiential tourism and loyalty, the impact of the latter on the former appeared to be
relatively low. This means that destination image, engagement and trust are the key mediators in
the process of creating a strong relationship amid the destination and its brand due to the
memorable experiences tourists have. This study broadens the understanding of tourism and
destination branding,as it applies experiential marketing theory to the understudied domain of
emerging markets and provides recommendations for destination managers to create experiences
fostering brand loyalty
