# A study on brand image enhancement of Zhou Hei Ya based on online text analysis

EWA	Publishing

Liangmin Li*, Jing Zhong
School of Business Administration, Nanjing University of Finance and Economics, Nanjing, China
18171393945@163.com
Abstract. Competition in the prepared braised food industry has intensified, making brand image a key factor
influencing consumer choice. This study takes Zhou Hei Ya, a Wuhan-based brand, as its research case. A total
of 2,117 valid reviews were collected from Dianping platform, and ROST CM6 was used to conduct semantic
analysis of online texts. The results show that duck necks, duck collarbones, duck wings, and duck tongues
constitute the main product associations, while terms such as "delicious", "spicy", and "well-seasoned" form a
prominent taste-related image. The semantic network centers on "Zhou Hei Ya", "Wuhan", "flavor", "duck
neck", and "delicious". Positive, neutral, and negative reviews account for 70.13%, 13.83%, and 16.04%,
respectively, indicating that overall consumer sentiment toward the brand is relatively positive. Based on these
findings, this study proposes four measures to enhance the brand image: strengthening the recognition of its
distinctive flavors, deepening its association with Wuhan's regional culture, improving consumers' perceived
value for money, and enhancing service experiences and emotional connections with consumers.
Keywords: Zhou Hei Ya, brand image, enhancement, online text analysis
1. Introduction
Since beginning its operations in Wuhan in 1997, Zhou Hei Ya has gradually expanded nationwide on the
strength of its distinctive flavors and products. The company was listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange in
2016. As competition from brands such as Juewei Duck Neck and Huang Shang Huang has intensified,
consumers have begun to evaluate not only product flavor but also price, service, packaging, and purchasing
convenience. User reviews on online platforms document actual consumption experiences and provide
valuable data for examining how consumers perceive and interpret a brand. Using reviews from Dianping
platform as its data source, this study focuses on three questions: Which product attributes of Zhou Hei Ya
attract the most consumer attention? How do these attributes combine semantically to form the structure of
brand perception? What is the overall emotional tendency of consumer reviews? By integrating high-
frequency word analysis, semantic network analysis, and sentiment analysis, this study identifies the existing
strengths of Zhou Hei Ya's brand image and the areas in which it can be improved.

2. Literature review and theoretical foundations
2.1. Brand image
The concept of brand image can be traced back to the 1950s. In their article The Product and the Brand,
Gardner and Levy argued that brand image is a combination of consumers' emotional attitudes toward a brand
and their functional perceptions of it. A brand not only provides practical value but also carries social and
psychological significance. This perspective went beyond the limitations of traditional economics, which
regarded a brand merely as a product identifier, and laid a theoretical foundation for subsequent research.
Since then, scholars have continued to explore the dimensions of brand image [1]. From the consumer's
perspective, Keller proposed the Customer-Based Brand Equity (CBBE) model and defined brand image as
the set of brand associations held in consumers' memories. According to Keller, the formation of brand image
depends on three dimensions of brand associations: strength, favorability, and uniqueness. Together, these
dimensions determine how a brand is positioned in consumers' minds [2]. From the perspective of brand
identity, Aaker regarded brand image as a comprehensive reflection of brand personality, brand associations,
and brand awareness, and further proposed a framework for measuring brand image [3]. Ge et al. investigated
the pathways through which brand image perception influences consumer purchasing behavior, using
consumers of Mengniu milk as survey respondents. Their findings showed that brand image perception
positively influences consumer purchasing behavior and that brand image affects purchasing behavior by
enhancing consumers' perceived value. This study empirically verified the relationships among brand image
perception, perceived value, and purchasing behavior, providing evidence for understanding the mechanisms
through which brand image exerts its influence [4].
2.2. Online text analysis and its applications
The application of online text analysis in Chinese academia began in the early 21st century, initially focusing
primarily on tourism destination image research. Zhang et al. selected the Huashan Scenic Area as their case
study, collecting online travel blogs and user reviews. Using grounded theory, they identified positive and
negative perceptions of Huashan's tourism image and demonstrated the practical feasibility and effectiveness
of online text analysis in destination image research [5]. Wang et al. conducted a focused analysis of
Fenghuang Ancient Town, examining various characteristics of tourists' perceptions of the scenic area's image
[6]. In the field of destination image research, Xu selected Huaguoshan in Lianyungang as a case study and
collected user reviews from the Tongcheng and Ctrip platforms. ROST CM6 software was used to identify
high-frequency words and conduct sentiment analysis [7]. Wang and Ge used Hangzhou Songcheng as a case
study and employed ROST CM6 to analyze online textual data, examining tourists' perceptions of the
destination's image [8].
3. Research case and design
Zhou Hei Ya International Holdings Company Limited is an enterprise specializing in the production,
marketing, and retail of leisure braised food products. Its main products include braised duck and duck by-
products, braised vegetables, and braised poultry and aquatic products. Founded in Wuhan in 1997, the brand
has gradually developed from a local Wuhan business into a nationally recognized brand, supported by its
distinctive spicy flavor and standardized freshness-preserving packaging. In 2016, the company was listed on
the Main Board of the Hong Kong Stock Exchange. In October 2024, Zhou Hei Ya's Braised Delights Gift
Box (LuweiShiguang) won the Gold Award at the China Tourism Commodities Competition.

This study uses consumer reviews from Dianping platform as its data source. The platform's reviews cover
multiple dimensions, including products, regional identity, pricing, service, environment, and consumption
scenarios, making them suitable for a multidimensional analysis of brand image. A total of 2,207 reviews
posted between January 2024 and May 2026 were collected, comprising approximately 58,000 Chinese
characters. After duplicate removal and data cleaning, 2,117 valid reviews remained. ROST CM6 was then
used to perform high-frequency word analysis, semantic network construction, and sentiment analysis in
sequence. These three sets of results complement one another and jointly provide a basis for evaluating the
brand's image.

The high-frequency word analysis shows that the five most frequent terms are "Zhou Hei Ya", "flavor",
"duck neck", "delicious", and "service attitude", in that order. Terms such as "duck neck", "duck tongue",
"duck collarbone", and "duck wings" occur frequently, indicating that braised duck products remain the most
firmly established category associations. "Well-seasoned", ranked 14th, relates to both the braising process and
the resulting flavor, making it a key indicator for assessing perceived product quality. "Satisfying", ranked
17th, reflects consumers' psychological responses during actual consumption, suggesting that taste satisfaction
often evokes positive emotions. "Taste" and "spicy and numbing", ranked 10th and 26th, respectively, reflect
Zhou Hei Ya's distinctive flavor profile, characterized by both numbing and spicy sensations. "Expensive" and
"group buying", ranked ninth and 23rd, respectively, indicate that consumers are sensitive to price, with group-
buying offers serving as a way to obtain more favorable prices. "Service attitude", ranked fifth, suggests that
in-store service is an important component of consumers' overall impressions of the brand. "Watching TV
dramas", "high-speed rail", and "tourism", ranked 20th, 28th, and 30th, respectively, reflect different
consumption scenarios. The first is associated with leisure activities, while the latter two relate to travel. These
findings suggest that Zhou Hei Ya has become a product associated with both everyday leisure consumption
and travel. "Other places" and "authentic", ranked 19th and 24th, respectively, indicate that consumers
evaluate Zhou Hei Ya's product quality in relation to its place of origin. Wuhan Zhou Hei Ya is generally
regarded as the most authentic. Terms such as "environment" and "packaging" further demonstrate that
consumers pay attention to the in-store environment and product packaging.
4.2. Semantic network analysis
Semantic network analysis examines the co-occurrence of high-frequency words. In this study, ROST CM6
was used to process the co-occurrence matrix constructed from high-frequency words, generating a semantic
network graph that illustrates the relationships among words in consumer reviews of Zhou Hei Ya.
As shown in Figure 1, the semantic network exhibits a multi-core, radiating structure. "Zhou Hei Ya",
"Wuhan", "flavor", "duck neck", and "delicious" serve as the main core nodes, extending to product attributes,
regional perceptions, price perceptions, service experiences, and consumption scenarios. In terms of taste
characteristics, "flavor", "duck neck", and "delicious" all occupy important positions in the network and are
connected to duck wings, duck collarbones, duck tongues, and terms such as "spicy and numbing", "well-
seasoned", and "distinctive". This indicates that consumers have developed a chain of brand associations
centered on "duck neck as the signature product, a spicy and numbing flavor with well-absorbed seasoning,
and an enjoyable taste". The brand's strength lies in its solid product foundation. However, "delicious", "spicy
and numbing", and "duck neck" are all common terms in the braised food industry. Without a more consistent
and distinctive flavor identity, the brand risks becoming indistinguishable from its competitors in consumers'
minds.

From the perspective of brand equity, the current semantic network has the advantage of establishing
relatively stable connections among the brand, its regional identity, taste, and specific products. Consumers not
only recognize "Zhou Hei Ya" but also readily associate it with "Wuhan", "duck neck", and "delicious".
However, this structure still relies primarily on functional value, while terms representing brand personality,
emotional value, and lifestyle are relatively scarce. Therefore, brand enhancement should not involve creating
an entirely new concept detached from the existing product foundation. Instead, it should gradually expand
emotional associations by building on terms that already appear in consumer reviews, such as "friends",
"watching TV dramas", and "tourism".
Regarding regional perceptions, "Wuhan" is one of the most prominent nodes apart from the brand name
and product-related evaluations. It co-occurs with terms such as "local specialty", "tourism", "high-speed rail",
"other places", and "friends", indicating that Wuhan represents more than a simple place of origin; it also
carries significance as a source of regional cultural identity and a destination for purchasing local gifts. The
brand can strengthen its association with Wuhan's cultural heritage, but it should avoid restricting its appeal to
consumers visiting Wuhan. A more effective positioning would be "originating in Wuhan, but not limited to
Wuhan".
In terms of price perceptions, terms such as "expensive", "discounts", "group buying", and "good value for
money" indicate that consumers have developed clear judgments about the relationship between price and
value. Rather than relying on repeated, substantial price cuts, the brand would benefit more from lowering
psychological barriers through different product sizes, combination packages, membership programs, and
group-buying offers, while providing clearer explanations of product quality and value. This approach would
better preserve the brand's market positioning.
Regarding service perceptions, "service attitude" is associated with terms such as "enthusiastic", "store
staff", "environment", and "repurchase", indicating that service can influence consumers' overall emotional
evaluations. Although the brand has established a strong advantage in product flavor, service has yet to
become a distinctive competitive strength. The overall consumer experience could therefore be improved
through standardized service procedures, greater purchasing efficiency, better store environments and
packaging, and more effective complaint-handling mechanisms.

4.3. Sentiment analysis
Sentiment analysis is used to identify the positivity and intensity of emotions expressed in consumer reviews,
helping to clarify consumers' overall emotional attitudes toward Zhou Hei Ya. As shown in Table 2, positive
reviews account for 70.13% of the total, while neutral and negative reviews account for 13.83% and 16.04%,
respectively. Among positive reviews, those classified as mildly, moderately, and highly positive account for
28.12%, 19.49%, and 22.52%, respectively. Highly positive reviews make up more than one-fifth of all
reviews, indicating that some consumers express strong approval of the brand. Negative reviews are
concentrated primarily in the mildly negative category, which accounts for 12.08%. Moderately and highly
negative reviews account for only 2.67% and 0.26%, respectively, suggesting that strongly negative emotions
are relatively uncommon.

5. Conclusion and discussion
5.1. Research conclusion
The results of high-frequency word analysis, semantic network analysis, and sentiment analysis indicate that
Zhou Hei Ya has established a relatively distinctive foundation for brand recognition. Consumers demonstrate
a strong awareness of its flavor profile, its identity as a Wuhan local specialty, and its core braised duck
products. Overall consumer sentiment is predominantly positive. Nevertheless, brand perceptions remain
largely concentrated on functional product attributes, leaving room for further improvement in perceived value
for money, service experience, and emotional brand associations.
5.2. Strategies for brand image enhancement
Based on the above findings, this study proposes four strategies to enhance Zhou Hei Ya's brand image.
5.2.1. Strengthen the recognition of distinctive flavors and establish differentiated brand recall
On the one hand, the brand can develop a more systematic brand communication strategy around existing
consumer perceptions of its spicy and numbing flavor and well-seasoned products. This would help establish
consistent, stable, and recognizable flavor associations, which should be reinforced through advertising,
packaging design, and other communication channels. On the other hand, the brand should continuously
strengthen flavor-based brand recall through its core products, positioning highly recognizable products such
as duck necks and duck wings as representative brand symbols. This would help establish stable associations
between specific products and the brand's distinctive flavor profile. Meanwhile, differentiated product lines

could be introduced to meet the needs of different consumer groups without compromising the brand's core
flavor characteristics, thereby further expanding its product appeal.
5.2.2. Deepen the brand's regional cultural identity in Wuhan and enhance its cultural value
The brand could strengthen the integration of its history, Wuhan's urban culture, and its brand story. Its
marketing communications should present more systematically the relationship between Zhou Hei Ya,
Wuhan's local food culture, and the city's lifestyle, elevating Wuhan from a simple place-of-origin label to a
source of brand culture. In addition, scenario-based brand communication could be developed around Wuhan
tourism, urban cultural events, and travel-related activities. For example, Zhou Hei Ya could strengthen its
positioning as a representative local gift at high-speed railway stations, airports, popular tourist destinations,
and urban cultural events. Packaging with distinctive regional features, co-branded designs, and culture-
themed products could also be introduced to increase consumers' willingness to purchase.
5.2.3. Improve perceived value for money and reduce consumer price sensitivity
First, the brand could lower the purchasing threshold by optimizing its product combinations. Options such as
smaller packages, sharing packs for multiple consumers, combination deals, and products at different price
points would allow consumers to select options according to their actual needs. Second, the membership
program, points-based rewards, and group-buying discounts could be further improved. Targeted promotions,
rather than across-the-board price reductions, would help strengthen consumers' perceived benefits. Third,
communication regarding product quality, ingredient standards, production processes, and brand value should
be enhanced so that consumers can better understand the value underlying product prices. In addition,
differentiated pricing and product designs could be developed for different consumption scenarios.
5.2.4. Improve service experiences and strengthen emotional connections with consumers
Zhou Hei Ya should further improve its in-store services and overall consumer experience. On the one hand, it
should strengthen the standardized management of store services and improve employee training, with
particular emphasis on service attitude, response efficiency, product knowledge and presentation, and problem-
solving capabilities. These measures would help reduce negative reviews arising from inconsistent service
quality. On the other hand, the brand should continue to improve store environments, product displays, and
packaging experiences so that consumers receive a consistent, comfortable, and professional brand experience
throughout the purchasing process.
5.3. Research limitations and future directions
This study primarily draws on consumer reviews from Dianping platform. Future research could adopt a
multi-platform comparative approach by analyzing user reviews and related content on platforms such as
Weibo, Xiaohongshu, and Meituan, thereby comparing differences in perceptions of Zhou Hei Ya's brand
image across platforms. In addition, this study focuses mainly on online reviews of Zhou Hei Ya stores in
specific areas of Wuhan. Future research could select stores in different cities and compare their online
reviews to further determine whether consumers in different cities perceive Zhou Hei Ya's brand image
differently.
