Abstract
Ecotourism has emerged as an important strategy for promoting sustainable development by integrating
environmental conservation, economic diversiﬁcation, and community well-being. However, empirical
evidence on the governance and stakeholder dimensions of ecotourism remains limited in emerging
destinations, particularly within the Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) region. This study examines the role
of ecotourism in promoting economic development and environmental conservation in the Dhofar
Governorate of the Sultanate of Oman. Guided by an Integrated Ecotourism Development Framework, the
study adopts an exploratory qualitative approach based on 47 semi-structured interviews with
government oﬃcials, tourism businesses, local communities, and environmental organizations,
complemented by secondary data from national policy documents, tourism statistics, and academic
literature. The ﬁndings reveal that Dhofar possesses considerable ecotourism potential owing to its rich
biodiversity, unique natural landscapes, cultural heritage, and the distinctive Khareef season. However,
institutional fragmentation, inadequate tourism infrastructure, limited stakeholder participation, uneven
distribution of tourism beneﬁts, and weak destination branding continue to constrain sustainable
ecotourism development. The study demonstrates that environmental conservation, stakeholder
participation, destination management, and ecotourism activities operate as interconnected dimensions
that collectively shape sustainable development outcomes. A key contribution of the research is the
development and empirical support of an Integrated Ecotourism Development Framework, which
provides a holistic explanation of how environmental, institutional, economic, and community factors
interact to inﬂuence sustainable tourism development. The ﬁndings offer practical insights for
strengthening ecotourism governance, enhancing stakeholder collaboration, and supporting the
implementation of Oman Vision 2040. More broadly, the study provides a transferable framework that
may inform sustainable ecotourism development in other emerging destinations facing similar
environmental and governance challenges.
1. Introduction
Tourism has become one of the world's most important economic sectors, generating employment,
attracting investment, supporting regional development, and facilitating cultural exchange. Following the
severe disruption caused by the COVID-19 pandemic, the sector has demonstrated remarkable
resilience. According to UN Tourism (2026), international tourist arrivals reached approximately
1.5 billion in 2025, surpassing pre-pandemic levels and reaﬃrming tourism's role as a key driver of global
economic growth. At the same time, the industry faces growing challenges from climate change,
biodiversity loss, increasing visitor pressure on natural destinations, and geopolitical uncertainty. These
challenges have shifted the focus of tourism policy and research from promoting growth alone towards
achieving more sustainable, resilient, and inclusive forms of destination development (OECD, 2026; UN
Tourism, 2026).
Ecotourism has emerged as one of the most promising approaches to achieving this balance. Rather
than focusing solely on attracting visitors, ecotourism seeks to conserve natural environments, support
Page 2/36

local communities, and provide meaningful educational and cultural experiences (The International
Ecotourism Society [TIES], 2015). When effectively managed, ecotourism can generate employment,
diversify rural economies, strengthen community participation, and ﬁnance biodiversity conservation
(Buckley, 2009; Honey, 2008; Scheyvens, 2021). Consequently, ecotourism is increasingly recognized not
merely as a niche tourism product but as a strategic approach to sustainable regional development and
an important mechanism for advancing the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (UNEP & UN
Tourism, 2023; Bui et al., 2024).
The Sultanate of Oman provides a particularly relevant context for examining these issues. As part of
Oman Vision 2040 and the National Tourism Strategy 2040, tourism is one of the country's priority
sectors for economic diversiﬁcation. National tourism policy places increasing emphasis on
sustainability, environmental stewardship, cultural heritage preservation, community participation, and
private-sector investment, recognizing that long-term destination competitiveness depends on
conserving the natural and cultural assets upon which tourism is based (Oman Vision 2040, 2021;
Ministry of Heritage and Tourism, 2021).
In Oman, the Dhofar Governorate is one of the country's most distinctive tourist destinations because of
its exceptional combination of natural and cultural resources. The annual Khareef season transforms the
region into one of the few green landscapes in the Arabian Peninsula, attracting more than one million
visitors each year (National Center for Statistics and Information [NCSI], 2025). Beyond its seasonal
appeal, Dhofar offers mountains, protected areas, coastal ecosystems, caves, wadis, and the UNESCO
World Heritage-listed Land of Frankincense, providing signiﬁcant opportunities for year-round nature-
based and ecotourism development.
Despite these advantages, the full potential of ecotourism in Dhofar has yet to be realized. Tourism
development continues to face challenges including limited ecotourism infrastructure, fragmented
stakeholder collaboration, insuﬃcient community participation, shortages of specialized human
resources, and relatively weak destination branding beyond the Khareef season. Furthermore, increasing
visitor numbers place additional pressure on environmentally sensitive areas, highlighting the need for
more integrated approaches to destination planning, environmental governance, and sustainable visitor
management (OECD, 2026; UNEP & UN Tourism, 2023).
Although tourism research in Oman has expanded considerably during the past decade, much of the
existing literature has focused on destination attractiveness, visitor satisfaction, destination marketing,
and the economic contribution of tourism. Comparatively little attention has been devoted to
understanding how environmental conservation, stakeholder participation, destination governance, and
local economic development interact to inﬂuence ecotourism development. Moreover, relatively few
empirical studies have adopted a multi-stakeholder perspective that integrates the views of government
agencies, tourism businesses, local communities, and environmental organizations. This gap is
particularly important because sustainable ecotourism depends on effective collaboration among these
Page 3/36

stakeholder groups rather than the actions of individual organizations alone (Freeman, 1984; Byrd, 2007;
Scheyvens, 2021).
This study addresses this gap by examining the contribution of ecotourism to economic development
and environmental conservation in the Dhofar Governorate through an exploratory qualitative study
involving government oﬃcials, tourism entrepreneurs, local community members, and environmental
organizations. By integrating multiple stakeholder perspectives, the research provides a comprehensive
understanding of the institutional, environmental, economic, and governance factors shaping
sustainable ecotourism development in the region.
This study makes three contributions to the literature. First, it provides one of the most comprehensive
qualitative assessments of stakeholder perceptions of ecotourism development undertaken in Oman.
Second, it advances the literature by developing and empirically applying an Integrated Ecotourism
Development Framework that explains the relationships among environmental conservation, stakeholder
participation, destination management, ecotourism activities, and sustainable development outcomes.
Third, it offers evidence-based policy recommendations to strengthen ecotourism governance and
destination management in Oman, while providing insights relevant to other emerging destinations
seeking to balance economic development with environmental conservation and community well-being.
The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. The next section reviews the relevant literature and
presents the conceptual framework underpinning the study. This is followed by the research
methodology, the presentation and discussion of the ﬁndings, and ﬁnally the conclusion, which outlines
the study's theoretical contributions, practical implications, limitations, and directions for future
research.

Historically dependent on hydrocarbon revenues, the country has increasingly positioned tourism as a

regional development. Oman's competitive advantage lies in its diverse natural landscapes, rich cultural

infrastructure gaps in remote destinations, and growing competition from neighboring Gulf countries.
2.2 The Dhofar Governorate
Located in southern Oman, the Dhofar Governorate represents one of the country's most distinctive
tourism destinations. Covering approximately 99,300 km², the governorate is characterized by
mountains, coastal ecosystems, wadis, deserts, caves, and protected natural areas. Its ecological
diversity distinguishes Dhofar from most destinations within the Arabian Peninsula and provides
exceptional opportunities for nature-based and ecotourism development.

The region is internationally recognized for the annual Khareef season, during which monsoon rains
transform the landscape into a unique green environment that attracts visitors from across Oman and
neighboring Gulf countries. According to NCSI (2025), visitor numbers during the 2024 Khareef season
exceeded 1 million, underscoring Dhofar's growing importance in Oman's tourism portfolio.
Beyond its natural attractions, Dhofar possesses signiﬁcant cultural and historical assets, including the
UNESCO World Heritage-listed Land of Frankincense, archaeological sites, traditional settlements, and a
long maritime trading history. These resources create opportunities to integrate nature, heritage, and
cultural tourism within a comprehensive sustainable destination management approach.
Recognizing its strategic importance, the Ministry of Heritage and Tourism has identiﬁed Dhofar as one
of the country's priority tourism clusters. The Dhofar Tourism Master Plan promotes environmentally
responsible tourism development through infrastructure investment, destination management,
conservation initiatives, community participation, and sustainable private-sector investment.
2.3 Ecotourism Potential and Development Challenges
Dhofar possesses many of the characteristics associated with successful ecotourism destinations. Its
biodiversity, protected areas, coastal landscapes, mountain ecosystems, and cultural heritage provide an
excellent foundation for developing high-quality nature-based tourism experiences. Growing international
demand for authentic, environmentally responsible tourism further strengthens the region's long-term
market potential.

Several conservation-oriented tourism initiatives have already been implemented, particularly within
Wadi Darbat, Jabal Samhan Nature Reserve, Al Mughsail, and the Land of Frankincense World Heritage
sites. These initiatives demonstrate increasing recognition of the need to balance tourism development
with biodiversity conservation and community well-being.
Despite these favorable conditions, ecotourism development continues to face important challenges.
Stakeholders have identiﬁed limited ecotourism infrastructure, fragmented institutional coordination,

to become a leading ecotourism destination in the Gulf region.
Accordingly, Dhofar provides an appropriate case for examining how environmental conservation,
stakeholder participation, destination management, and tourism development interact within an
emerging ecotourism destination. The governorate therefore offers valuable insights for both tourism
policy and sustainable destination management in Oman and comparable regions.
3. Literature Review
3.1 Ecotourism as a Sustainable Development Strategy
Ecotourism has evolved from a niche form of nature-based tourism into one of the most widely
recognized approaches to sustainable tourism development. Early deﬁnitions emphasized responsible
travel to natural areas and environmental conservation. In contrast, more recent perspectives position
ecotourism as an integrated development strategy that generates economic opportunities, conserves
biodiversity, strengthens community well-being, and enhances destination resilience (Honey, 2008;
Buckley, 2009; UNEP & UN Tourism, 2023).
The International Ecotourism Society (TIES, 2015) deﬁnes ecotourism as responsible travel to natural
areas that conserves the environment, sustains the well-being of local communities, and incorporates
interpretation and education. Similarly, the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN)
emphasizes environmentally responsible travel that supports conservation and beneﬁts local
populations. Contemporary scholarship extends these deﬁnitions by recognizing ecotourism as a
governance and policy instrument that can support multiple Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs),
particularly those related to decent work, climate action, biodiversity conservation, and sustainable
economic growth (OECD, 2026; UN Tourism, 2026).
Despite broad agreement regarding its objectives, scholars continue to debate whether ecotourism
consistently delivers its promised environmental and socio-economic beneﬁts. While many destinations
demonstrate positive conservation and development outcomes, others have experienced environmental
degradation, the commercialization of natural resources, and an unequal distribution of tourism beneﬁts.
Page 6/36

These contrasting ﬁndings suggest that successful ecotourism depends not only on natural assets but
also on effective governance, stakeholder collaboration, and destination management.
3.2 Ecotourism, Economic Development and Environmental
Conservation
The relationship between ecotourism, economic development, and environmental conservation remains
central to the sustainable tourism literature. Numerous studies demonstrate that ecotourism generates
employment, stimulates local entrepreneurship, increases household income, and supports regional
economic diversiﬁcation, particularly in rural and environmentally sensitive areas (Lai & Nepal, 2006;
Buckley, 2009). Community-based ecotourism has also been associated with improved livelihoods and
reduced dependence on environmentally harmful activities (Stronza & Durham, 2008).
Ecotourism contributes to environmental sustainability by generating ﬁnancial resources for protected
area management, biodiversity conservation, ecosystem restoration, environmental education, and
visitor awareness (Kiper et al., 2016). However, these beneﬁts are not automatic. Inadequately planned
tourism can contribute to habitat degradation, pollution, overcrowding, and ecosystem disturbance
(Pigram & Wahab, 2005). Consequently, recent studies increasingly emphasize the management of
carrying capacity, environmental governance, climate adaptation, and destination resilience as essential
conditions for sustainable ecotourism development (UNEP & UN Tourism, 2023; OECD, 2026).
3.3 Stakeholder Participation and Destination Governance
Stakeholder participation is widely recognized as a fundamental determinant of sustainable ecotourism
development. Stakeholder Theory proposes that sustainable outcomes are more likely when
governments, businesses, communities, environmental organizations, and visitors actively participate in
planning and decision-making processes (Freeman, 1984). Tourism scholars argue that collaborative
governance improves destination legitimacy, enhances conservation outcomes, and promotes more
equitable distribution of tourism beneﬁts (Byrd, 2007; Scheyvens, 2021).
Recent research emphasizes that participation should extend beyond consultation to include genuine
partnership, shared ownership, and collaborative governance (Bui et al., 2024). Nevertheless, many
emerging destinations continue to face institutional fragmentation, overlapping responsibilities, limited
community empowerment, and insuﬃcient coordination among tourism stakeholders. These
governance challenges often constrain the long-term sustainability of ecotourism despite favorable
environmental conditions.
3.4 Ecotourism Development in Emerging Destinations
Ecotourism has become increasingly important in emerging economies seeking to diversify their
economies while conserving natural resources. Studies from Asia, Africa, and Latin America show that
ecotourism can contribute signiﬁcantly to biodiversity conservation, rural livelihoods, and regional
Page 7/36

development when supported by effective governance, infrastructure investment, and stakeholder
collaboration (Scheyvens, 2021; Bui et al., 2024).
Within the Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC), tourism has become a strategic component of national
diversiﬁcation policies. Oman has positioned tourism as a priority sector under Oman Vision 2040,
emphasizing sustainability, cultural heritage, environmental conservation, and community participation.
Among Oman's tourism regions, the Dhofar Governorate possesses exceptional ecotourism potential
because of its biodiversity, protected areas, mountain ecosystems, coastal landscapes, and the unique
Khareef season. However, realizing this potential requires stronger institutional coordination, improved
visitor management, and greater community participation.
3.5 Research Gap and Literature Synthesis
The literature consistently demonstrates that ecotourism can contribute to economic diversiﬁcation,
biodiversity conservation, and community development when supported by effective governance and
sustainable management practices (Buckley, 2009; Scheyvens, 2021; Bui et al., 2024). However, previous
research has generally examined these dimensions independently, focusing on economic impacts,
environmental conservation, stakeholder participation, or destination marketing rather than investigating
how these elements interact within an integrated governance framework.
Although tourism research in Oman has expanded over the past decade, most studies have concentrated
on destination attractiveness, visitor satisfaction, tourism marketing, and the economic contribution of
tourism. Comparatively little attention has been given to understanding the relationships among
environmental conservation, stakeholder participation, destination management, and sustainable
regional development within the context of ecotourism. Moreover, empirical evidence from the Dhofar
Governorate remains limited, particularly regarding the perspectives of multiple stakeholder groups
involved in ecotourism planning and management.
Another limitation of the existing literature is its methodological focus. Much of the research relies on
visitor surveys or secondary data. In contrast, relatively few studies have adopted qualitative, multi-
stakeholder approaches that incorporate the views of government agencies, tourism businesses, local
communities, and environmental organizations. Such perspectives are essential because sustainable
ecotourism depends on collaboration among these actors and the effective integration of conservation
and development objectives.
Collectively, these gaps indicate the need for a more comprehensive framework that explains how
environmental conservation, stakeholder participation, destination management, and ecotourism
activities interact to inﬂuence sustainable development outcomes. Addressing this need, the present
study develops and empirically applies an Integrated Ecotourism Development Framework, drawing on
qualitative evidence from multiple stakeholder groups in the Dhofar Governorate. In doing so, the study
contributes to both theory and practice by advancing understanding of ecotourism governance in
Page 8/36

emerging destinations and providing evidence-based recommendations for sustainable tourism
development.
4. Conceptual Framework
4.1 Theoretical Foundations
Understanding ecotourism requires more than a single theoretical perspective, as its success depends
on the interplay of environmental, institutional, economic, and social factors. Accordingly, this study
integrates four complementary perspectives—Stakeholder Theory, Sustainable Tourism Theory,
Destination Management Theory, and Environmental Conservation Theory—to explain how these factors
collectively shape sustainable ecotourism development.
Stakeholder Theory (Freeman, 1984) provides the governance perspective of the framework by
emphasizing that sustainable tourism depends on collaboration among governments, tourism
businesses, local communities, environmental organizations, and visitors. The theory argues that
involving these stakeholders in planning and decision-making improves governance, strengthens
conservation efforts, and promotes a more equitable distribution of tourism beneﬁts.
Sustainable Tourism Theory extends this perspective by recognizing that long-term tourism development
requires balancing economic prosperity, environmental protection, and socio-cultural well-being. Drawing
on the principles of the Triple Bottom Line, the theory suggests that destinations achieve lasting
competitiveness only when economic growth is accompanied by environmental stewardship and
community development (Weaver, 2001; Honey, 2008).
Destination Management Theory focuses on the institutional mechanisms that coordinate tourism
development. It highlights the role of destination management organizations in planning tourism,
coordinating stakeholders, managing visitor ﬂows, promoting destinations, and monitoring sustainability
performance (UN Tourism, 2024; OECD, 2026). Effective destination management is therefore essential
to translate tourism policies into practical, sustainable outcomes.
Environmental Conservation Theory provides the ecological foundation of the framework. Natural
resources, biodiversity, and ecosystem integrity are the primary assets that attract visitors to ecotourism
destinations. Protecting these resources is therefore not only an environmental objective but also a
prerequisite for maintaining destination competitiveness and ensuring the long-term viability of tourism.
While each theory explains a different aspect of ecotourism, none independently captures the complex
relationships among environmental conservation, stakeholder collaboration, destination governance, and
tourism development. By integrating these perspectives, the proposed framework offers a more
comprehensive explanation of how sustainable ecotourism can be achieved in emerging destinations.

Building on the literature reviewed in the previous section, this study proposes an Integrated Ecotourism
Development Framework that explains how environmental conservation, stakeholder participation,
destination management, and ecotourism activities interact to generate sustainable development
outcomes. The framework addresses a key limitation in previous research, where these dimensions have
often been examined independently rather than as interconnected elements of destination governance.

The framework comprises ﬁve interconnected constructs that collectively explain the processes through
which ecotourism contributes to sustainable development.
Environmental conservation forms the ecological foundation of the framework. Biodiversity, protected
areas, natural landscapes, and healthy ecosystems provide the essential resources upon which
ecotourism depends. Conserving these assets ensures the long-term sustainability of tourism while
maintaining the environmental quality that attracts visitors.
Stakeholder participation reﬂects the collaborative involvement of government agencies, tourism
businesses, local communities, environmental organisations, investors, and visitors in tourism planning,
implementation, and governance. Effective collaboration encourages shared decision-making, improves
conservation outcomes, promotes community ownership, and enhances the distribution of tourism
beneﬁts.
Ecotourism activities represent the operational component of the framework. Nature-based experiences,
environmental education, cultural tourism, wildlife observation, and community-based tourism transform
environmental and cultural resources into economic, environmental, and social value while encouraging
responsible visitor behaviour.
Destination management organisations (DMOs) serve as the coordinating mechanism that links
stakeholders, tourism activities, and sustainability objectives. Through strategic planning, destination
marketing, visitor management, sustainability monitoring, and policy implementation, DMOs help
balance tourism growth with environmental protection and community well-being.
The ﬁnal construct, sustainable development outcomes, represents the desired results of ecotourism
development. These outcomes include economic diversiﬁcation, employment generation, community
well-being, cultural preservation, biodiversity conservation, and improved environmental stewardship.
Together, they reﬂect the principles of sustainable tourism and the Triple Bottom Line.
Unlike many existing models that examine these dimensions separately, the proposed framework
conceptualises ecotourism as an integrated governance system in which environmental conservation
and stakeholder participation create the conditions for effective destination management and
successful ecotourism activities. These interactions ultimately contribute to sustainable development

while reinforcing conservation efforts and stakeholder commitment, creating a continuous cycle of
sustainable destination development.

5. Methodology
This study employed an exploratory qualitative research design to investigate the role of ecotourism in
promoting economic development and environmental conservation in the Dhofar Governorate of the
Sultanate of Oman. A qualitative approach was considered appropriate because the study seeks to
understand how different stakeholder groups perceive ecotourism development, destination governance,
environmental conservation, and community participation within a context that remains relatively
underexplored. Guided by an interpretivist perspective, the research focuses on understanding
participants' experiences and the meanings they attach to ecotourism development rather than
measuring predeﬁned variables (Creswell & Poth, 2018).
The study used the Integrated Ecotourism Development Framework presented in the previous section.
The framework guided the development of the interview guide, informed the thematic analysis, and
provided the analytical lens for interpreting the ﬁndings. It focused on ﬁve interconnected dimensions:
environmental conservation, stakeholder participation, destination management, ecotourism activities,
and sustainable development outcomes.
To address the research objectives, the study combined primary and secondary data. Primary data were
collected through semi-structured interviews with key stakeholders, while secondary data were drawn
from government reports, policy documents, tourism statistics, and academic literature to provide
context and support triangulation.
Participants were selected using purposive sampling, a widely accepted strategy in qualitative research
for identifying individuals with relevant knowledge and practical experience (Patton, 2015). Rather than
seeking statistical representation, purposive sampling enabled the inclusion of stakeholders directly
involved in ecotourism planning, management, and development in the Dhofar Governorate. A total of 47
participants took part in the study, representing four principal stakeholder groups involved in ecotourism
development (Table 2).

Government participants represented the Ministry of Heritage and Tourism, the Environment Authority,

tourism and cultural heritage initiatives, while representatives from environmental organizations

continued until data saturation was achieved, whereby additional interviews generated no substantial
new insights and recurring patterns became evident across stakeholder groups.
Semi-structured interviews were conducted between February and April 2025. This format provided
suﬃcient ﬂexibility to explore participants' experiences while ensuring that all interviews addressed the
key issues relevant to the research objectives. The interview guide was developed from the literature
review and the Integrated Ecotourism Development Framework and focused on ﬁve broad themes: the
current state of ecotourism development, environmental conservation, stakeholder participation and
destination governance, economic and community beneﬁts, and future priorities for ecotourism
development. Before ﬁeldwork began, two academics with expertise in tourism and qualitative research
reviewed the interview guide to ensure its clarity, relevance, and content validity.
Interviews were conducted face-to-face or by telephone, in Arabic or English depending on participants'
preferences, and typically lasted 45 to 90 minutes. Informed consent was obtained from all participants
prior to the interviews. With participants' consent, interviews were audio-recorded and subsequently
transcribed verbatim. Interviews conducted in Arabic were translated into English while preserving the
original meaning and contextual nuances of participants' responses.

The interview data were analyzed using the six-phase thematic analysis approach developed by Braun
and Clarke (2006). Analysis began with repeated readings of the interview transcripts to become familiar
with the data, followed by generating initial codes. Related codes were then grouped into broader
categories and progressively reﬁned into higher-order themes through constant comparison across
stakeholder groups. The emerging themes were continually reviewed against the original transcripts to
ensure that they accurately reﬂected participants' perspectives. Throughout the analytical process, the
Integrated Ecotourism Development Framework served as the primary analytical lens, enabling the
empirical ﬁndings to be interpreted in relation to environmental conservation, stakeholder participation,
destination management, ecotourism activities, and sustainable development outcomes.
To enhance the trustworthiness of the study, the quality criteria proposed by Lincoln and Guba (1985)
were applied. Credibility was strengthened through purposive sampling, the inclusion of multiple
stakeholder groups, prolonged engagement with participants, and data triangulation. Transferability was
supported by providing detailed descriptions of the research setting and participant characteristics.
Dependability was enhanced through transparent documentation of the research process, interview
procedures, and analytical decisions, while conﬁrmability was strengthened through systematic coding,
reﬂexive note-taking, and verbatim quotations to support the interpretation of the ﬁndings.
Given the interpretive nature of qualitative inquiry, reﬂexivity was maintained throughout the research
process. The researcher's academic and professional experience in tourism management and
sustainable tourism development in Oman facilitated access to participants and provided an informed
understanding of the local context. At the same time, the researcher made conscious efforts to minimize
potential bias through reﬂexive practice, systematic coding, data triangulation, and continuous
comparison of stakeholder perspectives, ensuring the ﬁndings remained grounded in participants'
experiences rather than the researcher's prior assumptions.
6. Findings
This study examined stakeholders' perceptions of ecotourism development in the Dhofar Governorate to
understand the opportunities, challenges, and conditions necessary for achieving sustainable tourism.
Analysis of the interview data revealed eight interrelated themes that capture stakeholders' perspectives
on the institutional, environmental, economic, and social dimensions of ecotourism development. While
participants consistently recognized Dhofar's exceptional natural and cultural assets, they also identiﬁed
several institutional, infrastructural, and governance challenges that continue to constrain the sector's
long-term development.
The ﬁndings demonstrate that sustainable ecotourism depends on the interaction of institutional
capacity, tourism infrastructure, community participation, environmental conservation, economic
opportunities, stakeholder engagement, and effective destination management. Collectively, these
Page 15/36

themes provide empirical support for the Integrated Ecotourism Development Framework by illustrating
how environmental conservation, stakeholder participation, destination management, and ecotourism
activities inﬂuence sustainable development outcomes in the Dhofar Governorate.
The ﬁndings are presented under eight themes: (1) institutional capacity and governance, (2) tourism
infrastructure and visitor services, (3) community participation and stakeholder collaboration, (4)
environmental conservation and sustainable management, (5) economic contributions of ecotourism, (6)
stakeholder satisfaction, (7) future priorities for ecotourism development, and (8) challenges affecting
sustainable ecotourism development. A SWOT analysis is then presented to synthesize stakeholders'
views of the strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats shaping the future of ecotourism in the
region.
6.1 Institutional Capacity and Governance
Institutional capacity emerged as one of the most inﬂuential factors shaping ecotourism development in
the Dhofar Governorate. Across all stakeholder groups, participants agreed that Dhofar possesses
exceptional natural landscapes, rich biodiversity, favorable climatic conditions, and a distinctive cultural
heritage that provides a strong foundation for ecotourism. However, these competitive advantages have
not yet been fully translated into sustainable tourism development because of weaknesses in
institutional capacity, human resources, and governance.
Participants consistently identiﬁed shortages of specialized expertise, limited technical skills, and
fragmented institutional coordination as major constraints. Government representatives acknowledged
that important progress has been made through tourism planning, infrastructure development,
destination promotion, and protected area management. Nevertheless, they recognized that the sector
continues to face a shortage of professionals with expertise in ecotourism planning and sustainable
destination management. As one government oﬃcial explained: "Dhofar possesses exceptional natural
assets, but we still need more specialized expertise and trained professionals to manage ecotourism
effectively" (GOV3).
Private-sector stakeholders broadly shared this assessment but emphasized the practical challenges
associated with operating ecotourism businesses. Many reported that bureaucratic procedures, lengthy
approval processes, and limited access to ﬁnancial support constrain investment, particularly for small
and medium-sized tourism enterprises. One tourism operator observed: "The challenge is not attracting
visitors; it is having enough qualiﬁed personnel who understand ecotourism management and sustainability
practices" (BUS4).
Similarly, another participant noted: "Support mechanisms exist, but obtaining approvals and funding can
be a lengthy process, particularly for smaller tourism businesses" (BUS7).
Community participants and environmental organizations also highlighted the need to strengthen local
capacity through training programs for eco-guides, conservation specialists, and tourism entrepreneurs.
Page 16/36

They argued that investing in local skills would not only improve service quality but also increase
community participation and strengthen environmental stewardship.
Although stakeholders recognized the government's commitment to tourism development through
infrastructure investment, destination marketing, support for tourism-related SMEs, and conservation
initiatives, many believed that greater emphasis should be placed on ecotourism-speciﬁc policies,
stronger inter-agency coordination, and more effective implementation of existing strategies. As one
government representative observed: "The government has invested signiﬁcantly in tourism infrastructure
and destination promotion, but more targeted support is needed for ecotourism projects" (GOV2).
6.2 Tourism Infrastructure and Visitor Services
Tourism infrastructure and visitor services emerged as among the most signiﬁcant constraints on
ecotourism development in the Dhofar Governorate. Although stakeholders agreed that the region
possesses exceptional natural and cultural attractions, many believed that tourism infrastructure has not
kept pace with visitor demand, limiting both the quality of visitor experiences and the sector's long-term
sustainability.
Across all stakeholder groups, participants identiﬁed shortages of eco-lodges, environmentally sensitive
accommodation, visitor information centers, interpretation facilities, eco-trails, directional signage,
sanitation facilities, and sustainable transport services. These shortcomings were considered
particularly evident outside Salalah, where many environmentally and culturally signiﬁcant sites remain
diﬃcult to access and lack the facilities necessary to support nature-based tourism.
A tourism business operator explained: "Visitors are attracted by Dhofar's natural beauty, but many sites
lack basic services and facilities needed to support a quality ecotourism experience" (BUS8).
Community participants expressed similar concerns, particularly regarding the unequal distribution of
tourism infrastructure across the governorate. As one respondent noted: "Tourists visit our area, but we
have no visitor center, limited facilities, and poor access roads" (COM11).
Stakeholders also highlighted the challenges created by the strong seasonality of tourism demand.
During the Khareef season, the sharp increase in visitor numbers places considerable pressure on
existing infrastructure, leading to congestion at popular attractions and reducing the quality of the visitor
experience. Many participants suggested that expanding sustainable tourism infrastructure beyond the
main tourism centers would help disperse visitor ﬂows, encourage year-round tourism, and create new
economic opportunities for rural communities.

6.4 Environmental Conservation and Sustainable
Management
Environmental conservation was widely recognized as the foundation of ecotourism development in the
Dhofar Governorate. Across all stakeholder groups, participants agreed that the region's biodiversity,

its greatest tourism assets. Consequently, conserving these natural resources was viewed not only as an
environmental responsibility but also as a prerequisite for sustaining tourism and maintaining the
destination's long-term competitiveness.
Government representatives highlighted the role of Environmental Impact Assessments (EIAs),
protected area management, and environmental regulations in safeguarding natural resources. At the
same time, many participants expressed concerns about inconsistent policy implementation, limited
environmental monitoring, and increasing pressure on sensitive ecosystems resulting from growing
visitor numbers. These concerns suggest that existing conservation policies provide an important
regulatory framework but require stronger implementation and more effective management to achieve
their intended outcomes.
As one environmental stakeholder observed: "Environmental protection should be the foundation of
tourism development because natural resources are the main attraction for visitors" (NGO2).

communities would encourage more responsible tourism practices while reducing pressure on fragile
environments.
6.5 Economic Contributions of Ecotourism
Stakeholders consistently viewed ecotourism as an important driver of economic development in the
Dhofar Governorate. Across all participant groups, respondents agreed that tourism has created new
employment opportunities, supported local entrepreneurship, stimulated demand for locally produced
goods and services, and contributed to the diversiﬁcation of the regional economy. Many participants
also highlighted the growing importance of ecotourism in creating income-generating opportunities
linked to cultural heritage, traditional crafts, local food production, and nature-based tourism services.
Community participants emphasized the direct economic beneﬁts that tourism provides to local
households. As one respondent explained: "Tourism provides additional income for many families through
handicrafts, local food production, transportation, and guiding services" (COM15).
Similarly, tourism business operators highlighted the sector's contribution to entrepreneurship and
investment, particularly in rural areas where alternative economic opportunities are relatively limited.
"Ecotourism creates opportunities for entrepreneurs and stimulates investment in rural areas" (BUS10).
Despite these positive contributions, stakeholders acknowledged that the economic beneﬁts of tourism
are not shared equally across the region. Many participants observed that larger businesses and external
investors often capture a disproportionate share of tourism revenues, while smaller enterprises and local
communities receive more limited economic returns. Respondents therefore stressed the importance of
strengthening community-based tourism, supporting small and medium-sized enterprises, and creating
policies that encourage greater local ownership and participation in the tourism economy.

tourism in Dhofar has progressed over the past decade, particularly through increased investment,

Government representatives were generally optimistic about recent developments, highlighting progress

communities, and environmental organizations.
Institutional and governance challenges were the most frequently cited concerns. Participants
highlighted weak coordination among public institutions, lengthy administrative procedures, and limited
integration between tourism planning, environmental management, and local development. These

implementation of ecotourism initiatives. As one government representative explained: "One of the

Environmental sustainability has been identiﬁed as another major concern. Participants warned that

7. Discussion
This study examined the factors shaping ecotourism development in the Dhofar Governorate through the
perspectives of government agencies, tourism businesses, local communities, and environmental
organizations. Drawing on the Integrated Ecotourism Development Framework, the ﬁndings demonstrate
that the interaction of environmental conservation, stakeholder participation, destination management,
and inclusive economic development shapes sustainable ecotourism. Although Dhofar possesses
exceptional natural and cultural resources, the study shows that these assets alone are insuﬃcient to
ensure sustainable tourism. Their long-term value depends on effective governance, coordinated
destination management, active stakeholder participation, and continued investment in institutional
capacity and sustainable infrastructure.
7.1 Environmental Conservation as the Foundation of
Ecotourism
Page 23/36

The ﬁndings reinforce the widely recognized view that environmental conservation forms the foundation
of successful ecotourism development. Across all stakeholder groups, Dhofar's biodiversity, protected
areas, mountain landscapes, coastal ecosystems, and distinctive Khareef environment were consistently
identiﬁed as the destination's principal competitive advantages. This supports previous research that
argues ecological integrity is the primary resource on which nature-based tourism depends (Buckley,
2009; Honey, 2008).
However, the study also demonstrates that conservation cannot be regarded as a static condition.
Stakeholders expressed growing concern about visitor pressure, waste management, habitat
disturbance, and the limited capacity to monitor environmental impacts. These concerns suggest that
increasing tourism demand must be accompanied by stronger visitor management, continuous
environmental monitoring, and adaptive conservation strategies. Similar challenges have been identiﬁed
in emerging ecotourism destinations where tourism growth has outpaced institutional capacity and
environmental management (Gössling et al., 2021; Scheyvens, 2021).
Rather than treating conservation as an outcome of tourism development, the ﬁndings indicate that it
should be viewed as the starting point for sustainable destination planning. Protecting ecological
resources is essential not only for biodiversity conservation but also for maintaining destination quality,
visitor satisfaction, and long-term economic competitiveness.
7.2 Stakeholder Participation and Governance
The study highlights stakeholder participation as a principal determinant of sustainable ecotourism
development. Although consultation mechanisms have been established, many participants perceived
existing governance arrangements as highly centralized, with limited opportunities for meaningful
community involvement in planning and decision-making.
These ﬁndings reinforce Stakeholder Theory (Freeman, 1984), which argues that sustainable outcomes
are more likely when those affected by development actively participate in governance processes.
Previous studies show that collaborative governance improves conservation outcomes, strengthens
local support for tourism, and promotes a more equitable distribution of tourism beneﬁts (Byrd, 2007;
Scheyvens, 2021).
The ﬁndings further suggest that community participation should extend beyond consultation to include
genuine involvement in planning, implementation, and destination management. Empowering local
communities not only strengthens trust among stakeholders but also encourages greater local
ownership of conservation initiatives and tourism enterprises. In emerging destinations such as Dhofar,
participatory governance therefore represents both a social objective and a practical mechanism for
improving destination resilience.
7.3 Ecotourism as a Driver of Regional Development
Page 24/36

The ﬁndings demonstrate that ecotourism has considerable potential to contribute to economic
diversiﬁcation, employment creation, entrepreneurship, and rural development. Participants consistently
recognized tourism as an important source of income, particularly for local businesses, traditional
handicrafts, guiding services, and community-based enterprises. These observations support previous
research highlighting ecotourism's capacity to generate local economic opportunities while encouraging
environmental stewardship (Stronza & Durham, 2008; Scheyvens, 2021).
At the same time, the study reveals that economic beneﬁts are distributed unevenly. Stakeholders
expressed concern that larger businesses and external investors often capture a disproportionate share
of tourism revenues, thereby limiting tourism's contribution to local livelihoods. This ﬁnding reﬂects
broader debates in the ecotourism literature on beneﬁt leakage and the importance of community
ownership for achieving inclusive development.
The ﬁndings therefore suggest that increasing tourism activity alone is insuﬃcient to achieve
sustainable regional development. Policies that strengthen local entrepreneurship, support small and
medium-sized enterprises, and expand community participation are equally important for ensuring that
tourism contributes to long-term economic resilience.
7.4 Destination Management as an Enabling Mechanism
Destination management emerged as a critical factor linking tourism development, environmental
conservation, and stakeholder collaboration. Participants consistently identiﬁed deﬁciencies in tourism
infrastructure, visitor management, interpretation facilities, transport services, and institutional
coordination as constraints affecting both visitor experiences and environmental sustainability.
These ﬁndings support destination management literature, which argues that tourism competitiveness
depends not only on the quality of attractions but also on the effectiveness of planning, governance, and
visitor management systems (UN Tourism, 2024; OECD, 2024). In Dhofar, infrastructure limitations
appear to reﬂect broader governance challenges rather than simply investment shortages. Fragmented
institutional responsibilities and weak coordination reduce the effectiveness of tourism planning and
slow the implementation of sustainable development initiatives.
The ﬁndings therefore suggest that destination management organizations should play a stronger
coordinating role by integrating tourism planning with environmental management, infrastructure
development, community engagement, and sustainability monitoring. Such an approach would improve
both destination competitiveness and long-term environmental resilience.
7.5 An Integrated Approach to Sustainable Ecotourism
Development
A key contribution of this study is the empirical support it provides for the Integrated Ecotourism
Development Framework. Rather than operating independently, environmental conservation, stakeholder
Page 25/36

participation, destination management, and ecotourism activities were found to interact in mutually
reinforcing ways that inﬂuence sustainable development outcomes.
The ﬁndings indicate that weaknesses in one component of the system can undermine progress in
others. For example, inadequate stakeholder participation weakens governance, limited infrastructure
reduces visitor satisfaction, and ineffective environmental management threatens the natural resources
upon which tourism depends. Conversely, improvements in governance, conservation, and community
participation create positive feedback loops that strengthen destination resilience and support more
sustainable tourism development.
This integrated perspective extends existing ecotourism research by demonstrating how environmental,
economic, institutional, and social dimensions interact within the context of an emerging tourism
destination in the Gulf region. The framework therefore provides a practical foundation for future policy
and research while illustrating that sustainable ecotourism should be understood as a coordinated
regional development strategy rather than simply a tourism product.
8. Theoretical Contributions
This study makes three interrelated theoretical contributions to ecotourism and sustainable tourism
literature. First, it extends understanding of stakeholder participation in ecotourism governance by
demonstrating that sustainable tourism development depends not only on the presence of multiple
stakeholders but also on the quality of collaboration among them. Second, Sustainable Tourism Theory
advances by showing how environmental conservation, economic development, and community
participation operate as mutually reinforcing rather than independent dimensions of sustainability. Third,
the study develops and empirically supports an Integrated Ecotourism Development Framework that
explains the relationships among environmental conservation, stakeholder participation, destination
management, ecotourism activities, and sustainable development outcomes within the context of an
emerging tourism destination.
8.1 Contribution to Stakeholder Theory
The ﬁndings reinforce the central premise of Stakeholder Theory (Freeman, 1984) by demonstrating that
sustainable ecotourism development depends on effective collaboration among government agencies,
tourism businesses, local communities, and environmental organizations. However, the study extends
existing theory by showing that stakeholder participation should not be viewed simply as a governance
principle but as a strategic capability that directly inﬂuences destination sustainability.
Participants consistently highlighted that limited community involvement, fragmented institutional
coordination, and weak collaboration reduce the effectiveness of tourism planning and constrain long-
term support for conservation initiatives. These ﬁndings suggest that meaningful stakeholder
participation extends beyond consultation to shared decision-making, collaborative governance, and
joint responsibility for destination management. In doing so, the study contributes empirical evidence
Page 26/36

from the Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) region, where research examining stakeholder relationships in
ecotourism remains relatively limited.
8.2 Contribution to Sustainable Tourism Theory
The ﬁndings also contribute to Sustainable Tourism Theory by demonstrating that environmental
conservation, economic development, and community well-being are closely interconnected rather than
competing objectives. While stakeholders recognized ecotourism as an important source of
employment, entrepreneurship, and regional development, they consistently argued that these beneﬁts
depend on maintaining ecological integrity and ensuring that local communities participate meaningfully
in tourism development.
This ﬁnding reinforces the core principles of sustainable tourism while extending existing knowledge by
illustrating how sustainability is achieved through the interaction of governance, environmental
stewardship, and inclusive economic development. In the context of Dhofar, tourism growth alone was
not considered a sustainability indicator. Instead, stakeholders emphasized that long-term success
depends on balancing economic diversiﬁcation with biodiversity conservation, cultural heritage
preservation, and equitable beneﬁt-sharing. These ﬁndings contribute to the growing literature on
sustainable tourism in environmentally sensitive and emerging destinations.
8.3 Contribution of the Integrated Ecotourism Development
Framework
The principal theoretical contribution of this study is the development and empirical support of the
Integrated Ecotourism Development Framework. Unlike many existing approaches that examine
environmental conservation, stakeholder participation, destination management, or economic
development separately, the proposed framework conceptualizes these dimensions as an
interconnected system that collectively shapes sustainable ecotourism outcomes.
The ﬁndings demonstrate that environmental conservation provides the ecological foundation of
ecotourism, while stakeholder participation and destination management function as enabling
mechanisms that inﬂuence the effectiveness of tourism planning and implementation. Ecotourism
activities, in turn, generate economic, environmental, and socio-cultural outcomes that reinforce
conservation efforts and strengthen stakeholder commitment, creating a continuous cycle of
sustainable destination development.
By integrating these relationships into a single conceptual model, the framework offers a more
comprehensive explanation of how ecotourism contributes to sustainable regional development. It also
provides a foundation for future comparative and quantitative research to test the relationships among
the model's core constructs across different tourism contexts. More broadly, the framework extends
ecotourism theory by demonstrating that sustainable destination development is best understood as a
dynamic governance system in which environmental, institutional, economic, and community
dimensions operate in a mutually reinforcing manner.

9. Practical Implications
The ﬁndings offer several practical implications for policymakers, destination managers, tourism

destination management, community participation, and economic development.
9.1 Strengthening Governance and Strategic Planning
The ﬁndings highlight the need for a more integrated governance approach to ecotourism development.
Although tourism has become an important pillar of Oman Vision 2040 and the National Tourism
Strategy, stakeholders consistently identiﬁed institutional fragmentation and limited coordination among
government agencies as barriers to sustainable development. Strengthening collaboration between the
Ministry of Heritage and Tourism, the Environment Authority, Dhofar Municipality, and other relevant
institutions would improve policy implementation, reduce administrative duplication, and promote more
coherent destination management.
A dedicated ecotourism strategy for Dhofar could provide a common policy framework for coordinating
conservation, tourism investment, infrastructure development, visitor management, and community
participation. Such a strategy should also establish sustainability standards and encourage the adoption
of certiﬁcation schemes that promote environmentally responsible tourism practices.
9.2 Investing in Sustainable Tourism Infrastructure
Improving tourism infrastructure emerged as one of the highest priorities identiﬁed by stakeholders.
Future investment should focus on environmentally sensitive infrastructure that enhances visitor
experiences while protecting natural resources. This includes eco-lodges, visitor information centers,
interpretation facilities, eco-trails, signage systems, public amenities, and sustainable transport services,
particularly in rural and nature-based tourism areas.
Participants also emphasized the importance of integrating carrying-capacity assessments,
environmental monitoring, and visitor management into infrastructure planning to minimize ecological
impacts while maintaining the quality of visitor experiences.
9.3 Empowering Local Communities and Tourism
Enterprises
The ﬁndings demonstrate that local communities should be recognized as active partners in tourism
development rather than passive beneﬁciaries. Expanding community participation in tourism planning,
supporting community-based tourism enterprises, and investing in education and capacity-building
programs would strengthen local ownership and improve the distribution of tourism beneﬁts.
Page 28/36

Similarly, greater support for small and medium-sized tourism enterprises would encourage
entrepreneurship, stimulate innovation, and diversify tourism products beyond the seasonal Khareef
market. Simplifying administrative procedures and improving access to ﬁnancial support would help
create a more enabling environment for sustainable tourism investment.
9.4 Strengthening Environmental Stewardship
The study reinforces the importance of integrating environmental conservation into all stages of tourism
planning and destination management. Participants consistently emphasized that the long-term
competitiveness of Dhofar depends on protecting the natural and cultural resources that underpin its
tourism appeal.
This requires stronger environmental monitoring, regular carrying-capacity assessments, improved
enforcement of environmental regulations, and greater investment in biodiversity conservation.
Environmental education programs targeting both residents and visitors should also be expanded to
encourage responsible tourism behavior and strengthen public support for conservation initiatives.
9.5 Enhancing Destination Positioning and Market
Competitiveness
Stakeholders also highlighted the need to reposition Dhofar as a year-round ecotourism destination
rather than one primarily associated with the Khareef season. Developing a distinctive ecotourism brand
that showcases the governorate's biodiversity, protected areas, coastal landscapes, outdoor recreation
opportunities, and cultural heritage would strengthen its competitiveness in domestic and international
markets.
Greater use of digital marketing, strategic partnerships with international tour operators, and destination
branding centered on sustainability and authentic nature-based experiences would further enhance
Dhofar's market position while supporting tourism diversiﬁcation.
9.6 Towards Integrated Ecotourism Governance
The ﬁndings indicate that sustainable ecotourism development ultimately depends on strengthening
collaboration among government agencies, tourism businesses, environmental organizations, local
communities, and academic institutions. Rather than operating independently, these stakeholders should
work within an integrated governance framework that promotes shared decision-making, coordinated
planning, information exchange, and joint responsibility for sustainable destination management.
Such an approach would improve policy coherence, strengthen environmental stewardship, encourage
community participation, and support more inclusive economic development. More broadly, it would
contribute to the implementation of Oman Vision 2040 by positioning Dhofar as a leading model of
sustainable and resilient ecotourism development within Oman and the wider Gulf region.
10. Conclusion
Page 29/36

This study examined the role of ecotourism in promoting economic development and environmental
conservation in the Dhofar Governorate of the Sultanate of Oman. Drawing on the perspectives of
government agencies, tourism businesses, local communities, and environmental organizations, the
study explored the factors shaping ecotourism development. It assessed its potential to support
sustainable regional development.
The ﬁndings demonstrate that Dhofar possesses exceptional natural and cultural resources that provide
a strong foundation for ecotourism. Its biodiversity, protected areas, coastal landscapes, mountain
ecosystems, cultural heritage, and unique Khareef season position the governorate as one of the most
promising nature-based tourism destinations in the Gulf region. However, the study also shows that
these assets alone are insuﬃcient to achieve sustainable tourism development. Institutional
fragmentation, limited tourism infrastructure, weak stakeholder coordination, uneven community
participation, and limited destination branding continue to constrain the sector's long-term development.
The study further demonstrates that ecotourism can contribute to economic diversiﬁcation, local
entrepreneurship, employment creation, and community well-being while supporting biodiversity
conservation and environmental stewardship. These ﬁndings reinforce the view that ecotourism
represents an integrated development approach capable of generating economic, environmental, and
socio-cultural beneﬁts when supported by effective governance and strategic destination management.
A key contribution of this research is the development and empirical support of the Integrated
Ecotourism Development Framework, which explains how environmental conservation, stakeholder
participation, destination management, and ecotourism activities interact to inﬂuence sustainable
development outcomes. The ﬁndings indicate that these dimensions should not be viewed
independently but as components of an interconnected system in which improvements in one area
reinforce progress in others.
The study suggests that the future of ecotourism in Dhofar will depend on strengthening governance,
investing in sustainable infrastructure, empowering local communities, protecting natural resources, and
improving collaboration among tourism stakeholders. By adopting an integrated, conservation-oriented
approach, Dhofar has the potential to become a leading model for sustainable ecotourism development
in Oman and the wider Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) region.
11. Limitations of the Study
Several limitations should be considered when interpreting the ﬁndings of this study. First, the research
focused exclusively on the Dhofar Governorate; therefore, the ﬁndings provide analytical rather than
statistical generalization to other regions of Oman or different tourism contexts. Second, the study
adopted an exploratory qualitative design based on stakeholder interviews, meaning that the ﬁndings
reﬂect participants' experiences and perceptions rather than quantitatively measured relationships.
Page 30/36

The limited availability of ecotourism-speciﬁc data in Oman also constrained the study. Information on
ecotourism investment, visitor expenditure, environmental performance, employment generation, and
conservation outcomes remain limited, while oﬃcial tourism statistics continue to focus primarily on
visitor arrivals and seasonal tourism activity.
Despite these limitations, the qualitative approach provided rich insights into stakeholder perspectives. It
enabled a comprehensive understanding of the opportunities, challenges, and governance issues
inﬂuencing ecotourism development in the Dhofar Governorate.
12. Future Research Directions
This study provides several avenues for future research. Comparative studies involving other
governorates in Oman and neighboring Gulf countries would improve understanding of how different
governance systems, environmental conditions, and tourism policies inﬂuence ecotourism development.
Such research could contribute to a broader regional framework for sustainable ecotourism.
Future studies should also use quantitative and mixed-methods approaches to examine the economic,
environmental, and socio-cultural impacts of ecotourism and test the relationships proposed in the
Integrated Ecotourism Development Framework. Longitudinal research would further improve
understanding of how tourism policies, conservation initiatives, and destination management practices
inﬂuence sustainability outcomes over time.
Additional research is needed to investigate visitor behavior, environmental carrying capacity, climate
change adaptation, biodiversity conservation, community-based tourism, and the application of digital
technologies and artiﬁcial intelligence in sustainable destination management. Such studies would
deepen understanding of ecotourism development in Oman and provide evidence to support more
effective tourism policy and practice across the GCC region.
