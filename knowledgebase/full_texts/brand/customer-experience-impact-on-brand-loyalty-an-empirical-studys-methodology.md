ABSTRACT

In fiercely competitive markets, providing an outstanding customer experience can serve as a crucial     differentiator.
Brands that emphasise customer experience can distinguish themselves from competitors while attracting and retaining
loyal customers (Havir,2019; Patov,2024). Engaging with clients and soliciting their feedback can further enhance this
experience. Brands that actively consider customer input and implement improvements demonstrate a commitment to
satisfaction that fosters loyalty. In this paper the methodology of research about the influence of customer experience on
brand loyalty is presented. The main research goal is to investigate how various aspects of customer experience
(including product quality, emotional engagement, staff behaviоr, satisfaction etc,) correlate with levels of brand loyalty
among various consumer segments. The study will examine the importance for customers of multiple factors influencing
brand loyalty, such as customer satisfaction, perceived value, trust in the bra nd, and emotional connections. Research
focus in the paper is on the issue of how sustained positive interactions shape long -term loyalties through metrics like
repeat purchases or retention rates over extended periods. Another important research aspect is  to measure negative
customer experiences' impact on brand loyalty, namely, to assess how adverse experiences (such as complaints or service
failures) affect consumer allegiance toward the brands, leading them towards competitor options. The target population
of the study are the current customers of multiple consumer brands. The sampling method is planned to be a convenience
sample with a size of more than 1000 respondents in order to guarantee data representativeness. The paper discusses in
its second part the data collection methods and the statistical methods for data analysis, which will include descriptive
statistics (frequencies, mean, median or mode) and Inferential statistics (t -tests, ANOVA, correlation and regression
analysis) used for identifying relationships among variables.

INTRODUCTION
Customer experience encompasses every interaction a consumer has with a brand, spanning the stages of pre-
purchase, purchase, and post-purchase. This includes elements such as customer service, product quality, user
interface, and overall satisfaction (Becker & Jaakkola, 2020). The customer experience is essential in shaping
brand loyalty. Customer experience serves as a fundamental driver of brand loyalty. Companies prioritising
the understanding and enhancement of this experience are more likely to nurture loyal customers who
contribute to sustainable business growth (Rane & Choudhary, 2023). By focusing on fostering p ositive
interactions, personalising experiences, and ensuring consistency across touchpoints, brands strengthen   their
base of loyal clients while thriving in competitive environments.

Research Goal and Objectives
When defining research objectives regarding the influence of customer experience on brand loyalty, it is crucial
to adhere to specific criteria: specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound (SMART). The main
goal of the current research is to investigate and analyze the relationship between the customer experience and
brand loyalty, depending on the various social-demographic factors. The research objectives are as follows:
-To investigate how various aspects of customer experience (including product quality, emotional
engagement, staff behaviоur, satisfaction etc,) correlate with levels of brand loyalty among different consumer
segments.
-To evaluate customer satisfaction's role in mediating between customer experience and brand loyalty:
explore how customer satisfaction mediates th e relationship between overall experiences with a company’s
offerings and levels of brand loyalty impacting repeat purchasing behavior.
-To compare customer experience across various demographic segments, and namely to examine variations
in perceptions regarding customer experience among different demographic and social groups (age categories,
income and education levels) concerning its effects on brand loyalty for targeted marketing strategies of the
companies.
-To measure negative customer experiences' im pact on brand loyalty, and namely, to assess how adverse
experiences (such as complaints or service failures) affect consumer allegiance toward a brand, leading them
towards competitor options.
-To explore emotional and psychological aspects related to cus tomer experience: to investigate emotional
responses linked with consumer experiences, including factors like trust or attachment, which correspondingly
relate back toward promoting advocacy for a particular brand.
-To examine the importance for customers of various factors influencing brand loyalty, such as customer
satisfaction, perceived value, trust in the brand, and emotional connections.

LITERATURE REVIEW
As well known, customer experience is a key marketing concept, yet the growing number of studies focused
on this topic has led to considerable fragmentation. The purpose of some papers is to review the literature on
customer experience to develop a better understanding of the concept and propose a research agenda (Jain at
al.,2017).
Other authors examine existing definitions and conceptualizations of customer experience as a construct
and provide a historical perspective of the roots of customer experience  within marketing. They attempt to
bring together what is currently known about customer experience, customer journeys, and customer
experience management (Lemon & Verhoef, 2016; Palmer A (2010). Some authors put stress on the impact
and implications of th e new technologies on each broad stage of the shopping journey (pre -transaction,
transaction, and post -transaction) and advance a new conceptualization for managing these new AI
technologies along customer experience dimensions to create experiential value (Hoyer et al., 2022).
Another research topic related to customer experience   is a measurement of the experience. Klaus and
Maklan (2013) introduce a new measure appropriate for the modern conceptualisation of customer experience:
the Customer experience  quality (EXQ) scale. In their article the authors extend that work and compare
(EXQ's) predictive power with that of customer satisfaction. They establish that EXQ better explains and
predicts both, loyalty and recommendations, then customer satisfaction (Klaus and Maklan, 2013).
By integrating contributions from different fields some researchers are conceptually developing Customer
Experience Model (CEM) to represent the different aspects of customer experience in a holistic diagrammatic
representation (Verma et al. 2012). There are authors which purpose of their research is to explore innovations
in customer experience at the intersection of the digital, physical and social realms. It explicitly considers

A favourable customer experience can result in repeat purchases, advocacy for the brand, and enduring
relationships with customers. Positive experiences foster emotional connections between customers and brands;
when customers feel appreciated and understood, their likelihood of developing loyalty increases. Brands that
elicit positive emotions tend to build deeper relationships  with their clientele. High levels of customer
satisfaction are closely linked to brand loyalty; satisfied customers are more inclined to make repeat purchases,
thereby lowering churn rates and enhancing customer lifetime value (CLV). Moreover, those who have positive
experiences are more likely to recommend the brand to others (Mostafa & Kas amani, 2020), facilitating
organic word-of- mouth marketing. Brand advocates can significantly sway potential customers and improve
the brand's reputation. Maintaining consistency across all customer touchpoints—such as online platforms,
physical stores, and customer service—is vital for establishing trust and loyalty. Inconsistencies may lead to
confusion and dissatisfaction, which could harm loyalty efforts.
Personalizing the customer experience based on individual  preferences significantly boosts loyalty;
consumers appreciate brands that acknowledge their unique needs and tailor their offerings accordingly.
Conversely, negative experiences can prompt customers  to switch brands and diminish their loyalty (Yakin,
2023). Brands that neglect complaints or provide subpar service risk damaging their reputation and losing
clientele. In recent years, there has been a growing interest in the role and application of artificial intelligence
and robotisation in many sectors  incl. in tourism and hospitality (Cain et al., 2019; Ivanov & Webster, 2019;
Samala et al., 2020). The implementation of AI can improve the customer experience by analyzing customer
behavior and personalising the offers and services which can lead to higher brand loyalty. Those who invest in
crafting positive experiences cultivate long-term relationships built on trust and satisfaction that ultimately lead
to increased loyalty over time.

RESEARCH METHODOLOGY
The outlined research objectives create an extensive framework for examining how customer experience
impacts brand loyalty, effectively allowing scholars insight into substantiating business strategies, improving
relationships formed between businesses and their clients and consumers.
Research Design and Sampling
As far as the type of research is concerned, it will be a descriptive study and the approach will be mixed -
qualitative and quantitative- based on the research questions. The target population are the current customers
of various consumer brands. The sampling method will be a c onvenience sample with a size of more than
1000 respondents in order to guarantee data representativeness.

Research Hypotheses
When developing hypotheses regarding brand loyalty influenced by age, social status, and income, it is crucial
to evaluate how t hese elements may affect consumer behaviour and brand perceptions. Below are several
hypotheses to direct my research:
Age and Brand Loyalty:
H1: Younger consumers (ages 18-24) tend to demonstrate lower brand loyalty compared to older consumers
(ages 45 and above), as they are more inclined to seek out new brands and products.
H2: Middle-aged consumers (ages 35-44) exhibit higher brand loyalty than both younger (ages 18-24) and
older consumers (ages 55 and above), owing to established preferences and familiarity with brands.
Social Status and Brand Loyalty:
H3: Consumers from higher social status backgrounds show stronger brand loyalty than those from lower
social status backgrounds due to better access to premium brands and a heightened perception of bran d
prestige.
H4: Individuals with lower social status are more prone to switch brands in response to price promotions
and discounts, leading to reduced overall brand loyalty compared with their higher-status cou
Income and Brand Loyalty:
H5: Higher-income consumers display greater brand loyalty because they can afford premium brands that
offer quality perceived as valuable.
H6: Lower-income consumers tend toward decreased brand loyalty as they exhibit greater price sensitivity
and a propensity for switching brands in search of cheaper options.
Combined Effects:
H7: Consumers who are older, belong to higher social strata, and possess higher incomes will likely exhibit
the greatest levels of  brand loyalty. Conversely, younger individuals from  lower social statuses with lower
incomes will likely show the least amount of brand fidelity.

Data Collection Methods
Every research relies on a set of data collection methods, depending on the goals and objectives set. In this
case, the methods will be a combination of descriptive and qualitative techniques: online
surveys/questionnaires aimed at collecting quantitative data on consumer attitudes towa rd brand loyalty and
customer experience assessment. The questions will include Likert scale items, multiple -choice queries, and
open-ended questions. The other method will be Focus groups, which will facilitate group discussions focused
on exploring consumer perceptions about the brand in a collaborative environment. Existing secondary data
sources will be investigated and analyzed as well, such as sales figures, customer feedback reviews, and market
research reports as supplemental information alongside primary data collection efforts. The statistical methods
for data analysis will include standard methods for quantitative analysis like descriptive statistics (frequencies,
mean, median or mode) and Inferential statistics (t -tests, ANOV A, correlation and regression analysis) used
for identifying relationships among variables. A qualitative analysis will be conducted based on content
analysis approaches aimed at highlighting recurring themes relevant to brand loyalty.
The Research Report
The final part of the  research will include a conclusion and provide recommendations for marketers and
branding professionals derived from research conclusions about the customer experience impact on brand
loyalty The research report will state any limitations present in the s tudy, such as sample size constraints or
geographical biases, and will propose potential avenues for future studies that further explore the dynamics
surrounding brand loyalty.

CONCLUSION
This kind of research is important for the company marketers as consistent positive experiences build brand trust, which
is a direct precursor to loyalty. Conversely, bad experiences can drive customers away, with a significant percentage
leaving after jus t one or a few negative interactions. The presented methodology of a future research can be useful for
many marketers as in today's competitive market, a strong customer experience is a powerful differentiator. Research
helps brands understand what custome rs value most, enabling them to prioritize improvements that truly matter for the
customers in various product categories. The research data analysis will support the efforts of the companies producing
and delivering multiple groups of products to improve their experiential marketing which will lead to higher customer
satisfaction and sales.
