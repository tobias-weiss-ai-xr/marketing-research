Abstract. Chinese time-honored brands are a crucial part of Chinese business culture and
history. Many brands have operated for decades or even hundreds of years. Their long
history gives them strong cultural value, customer trust, and brand recognition. However,
many traditional brands now face problems such as an aging customer base, old-fashioned
brand images, limited product innovation, weak customer experiences, and insufficient
digital marketing. This paper examines how Chinese time-honored brands can become more
competitive in the modern market. It uses brand equity theory and experiential marketing
theory as the theoretical foundations. Through literature review and case analysis, the paper
focuses on four strategies: product innovation, IP collaboration, scenario reconstruction, and
digital marketing. Three cases are discussed: Holiland × Chiikawa, Tongrentang Zhima
Health, and Luckin Coffee × Kweichow Moutai. The findings show that product innovation
serves as the foundation of brand revitalization. IP collaboration facilitates the formation of
new brand associations and enable brands to attract younger consumer groups. Enhanced
customer experiences can boost satisfaction and loyalty, while digital marketing elevates
brand awareness and consumer engagement. Overall, Chinese time-honored brands should
protect their traditional culture while changing their products, experiences, and marketing
methods to meet modern consumer needs.
Keywords: Chinese time-honored brands, Product innovation, IP collaboration, Brand
equity, Experiential marketing
1. Introduction
Chinese time-honored brands have a long history and are important symbols of Chinese business
culture. Many of them have operated for several decades or even hundreds of years. Their history,
traditional products, and cultural meaning provide them important advantages in the market. Yet, a
long history does not always guarantee future success. Changes in technology, consumer behavior,
and competition have created new challenges for traditional brands. Recently, the growth of the
"China Chic" trend has created new opportunities for Chinese brands. Younger consumers are
becoming more interested in products that combine Chinese culture with modern design. Research
on Chinese time-honored brands shows that these companies need to balance cultural inheritance

2. Theoretical foundation
2.1. Brand Equity Theory
Brand Equity Theory is useful for understanding how traditional brands can create long-term value.
Aaker defines brand equity as a group of assets connected to a brand name that can add value to
products or services [4]. The assets include brand awareness, perceived quality, brand associations,
and customer loyalty. This theory is particularly applicable to Chinese time-honored brands.
Traditional companies often have strong brand awareness among older consumers because of their
long history. However, younger consumers may have weaker connections with these brands. Their
choices are often influenced by social media, popular culture, design, and online communities.
Keller's customer-based brand equity model also emphasizes the importance of what consumers
know and associate with a brand. Strong brand awareness and positive, unique brand associations
can lead consumers to respond more positively to a brand's marketing activities [5]. This means that
traditional brands should not only protect their existing image. They should also create new and
positive associations for younger consumers.
Product innovation can help build these associations. When an old brand develops products that
fit modern consumer needs, consumers may begin to see the brand as more creative and relevant. At
the same time, the company can continue to use its traditional skills and cultural identity.
IP collaboration can also change brand associations. A traditional food company, for example,
may have been associated mainly with history and traditional products. Working with an animation
character or popular entertainment IP can add new associations like youth, creativity, and fashion.
2.2. Experiential Marketing Theory
Experiential Marketing Theory focuses on what consumers feel and experience when they interact
with a brand. Schmitt argues that consumers do not only buy products because of their functional
benefits. They also care about sensory, emotional, cognitive, behavioral, and social experiences [6].
This theory is important for traditional brands because many traditional stores mainly focus on

3. Current development and main challenges
3.1. Current development
Chinese time-honored brands have new opportunities because of changes in Chinese consumer
culture and the growth of digital markets. The China chic trend has encouraged consumers to pay
more attention to Chinese culture and domestic brands. This gives traditional companies an
opportunity to present their cultural heritage in a modern way [1]. Digital transformation is another
important development. E-commerce, livestreaming, short-video platforms, and social media have
become important channels for communication and sales. Social media can help companies increase
consumer interaction and strengthen brand equity [2]. Cross-industry cooperation has also become
more common. Traditional companies now cooperate with animation characters, fashion brands,
museums, and entertainment companies. These collaborations have the potential to produce limited-
edition goods and garner online attention.
3.2. Main challenges
Despite these opportunities, traditional brands still face several problems.
Product innovation is one of the biggest challenges. Some time-honored brands still depend on
products that have remained almost unchanged for many years. Although these products have
cultural value, they may not meet the needs of younger consumers. Modern consumers often care
about convenience, appearance, health, personalization, and emotional value. If traditional brands do
not update their products, they may lose customers to newer domestic and international competitors.
Traditional companies may also be slow to develop new products because they are afraid that major
changes could damage their historical image. However, being too conservative can make it difficult
to respond to new trends.
While a long-standing history can serve as both an asset and a liability for brands, it fosters trust
yet risks projecting an old‑fashioned image, leading some young consumers to perceive traditional
brands as offerings primarily for older generations. Old logos, store designs, advertising styles, and
communication methods may not connect with younger consumers. Therefore, brand revitalization
requires more than a new logo. Companies need to update their personality, communication, and
customer experience while keeping their cultural identity. Research on retro branding suggests that
old brands can create new meanings by using their past in new ways [9]. This means that history
should not be removed. Instead, it should be presented in a way that fits modern consumer culture.

4. Commercial revitalization strategies
The modernization of Chinese time-honored brands should not depend on a single strategy. This
paper proposes four interrelated strategies: product innovation, IP collaboration, scenario
reconstruction, and digital marketing.
4.1. Product innovation
Product innovation should be the foundation of revitalization. A traditional brand may have a strong
reputation, but consumers still need products that fit their current lifestyles. Innovation can include
new flavors, healthier ingredients, smaller packages, new designs, improved functions, seasonal
products, and limited editions. These changes can make traditional products more attractive without
destroying their original identity. Product innovation can also improve perceived quality. When
consumers see that an old brand continues to develop new products, they may see the brand as both
trustworthy and creative. This can help create stronger brand equity [4].
However, innovation should not completely remove traditional culture. Heritage is part of the
value of a time-honored brand. Research on heritage brands shows that history and authenticity can
remain important parts of brand identity [3]. Therefore, the goal should be to combine tradition with
modern design instead of replacing tradition.
4.2. IP collaboration: Holiland × Chiikawa
IP collaboration is an effective way to connect traditional businesses with younger consumers.
Companies can work with animation characters, movies, games, artists, or fashion brands to create
limited products and marketing campaigns.
The collaboration between Holiland and Chiikawa provides a useful example. Holiland combined
its bakery products with the popular Chiikawa IP. The cooperation included special cakes, desserts,
drinks, gift boxes, and themed packaging. The value of the collaboration was not limited to putting
cartoon characters on products. The brand also used themed decorations and promotional materials
to change the store environment. This gave consumers a reason to visit the stores, take photos, and
share their experiences online. Limited-edition products also created excitement. Consumers had an
additional reason to purchase because the products were connected to a popular IP and were
available for a limited time. Research on brand alliances shows that the image of one brand can
influence consumers' attitudes toward another brand in a partnership [11]. Therefore, choosing the

4.3. Scenario reconstruction: Tongrentang Zhima Health
Scenario reconstruction represents another valuable strategy, through which traditional enterprises
can reshape consumers' product‑related experiences by developing innovative retail spaces.
Tongrentang is a well-known Chinese traditional medicine brand. Its Zhima Health concept
combines traditional health ideas with a modern café-style environment. Instead of only selling
traditional medicine products, the brand provides health drinks, herbal tea, desserts, and other
products in a modern setting. The store environment is designed to be more comfortable and
attractive. Consumers can sit down, order products, and spend time with friends. This makes
traditional health culture easier for young consumers to understand. This strategy changes the
meaning of the product. Traditional Chinese medicine can sometimes be viewed as serious or old-
fashioned, while a modern café environment can make the same cultural ideas feel more accessible.
Experiential marketing research shows that sensory and emotional experiences can influence
customer satisfaction and loyalty [10]. Therefore, creating a better retail environment can help
traditional brands develop stronger relationships with customers. Scenario reconstruction can also
help traditional brands compete with modern lifestyle stores. By combining culture, products,
leisure, and social interaction, a traditional company can establish a distinctive market position.
4.4. Digital marketing: Luckin Coffee × Kweichow Moutai
Digital marketing is now an important part of brand revitalization. A useful example is the
collaboration between Luckin Coffee and Kweichow Moutai. Although Luckin Coffee is not a time-
honored brand, the case shows how a traditional heritage brand can use cross-industry cooperation
and digital marketing to reach younger consumers. The two companies created the "Sauce-flavored
Latte." The product quickly attracted attention online. Mobile ordering, social media content, and
consumer sharing helped spread the campaign.
The case shows that digital marketing is not only about advertisements. Consumers can
participate by purchasing the product, taking photos, posting reviews, and discussing it online.
Consumers therefore become part of the communication process. Research on social media
marketing shows that social media activities can influence brand equity by increasing consumer
interaction and engagement [2]. This suggests that traditional brands should use digital platforms not
only to promote products but also to communicate with consumers.
Digital platforms can also provide information about consumer preferences. Companies can use
this information to understand which products and campaigns perform better and use the results in
future product development.

5. Integrated revitalization framework
The four strategies outlined above should function synergistically. First, product innovation gives
consumers a reason to try the brand. Second, IP collaboration gives traditional products new cultural
meaning and helps them connect with younger consumers. Third, scenario reconstruction makes the
purchasing process more enjoyable. Fourth, digital marketing spreads information and encourages
consumers to share their experiences.
These strategies support each other. For example, a new IP product can attract consumers, a
themed store can create a better experience, and social media can encourage consumers to share
photos of the product and store. This can increase brand awareness and attract more customers.
At the same time, brands must protect their authenticity. Heritage brands should not remove their
history simply to appear young. Authenticity can help consumers understand why a heritage brand is
different from newer competitors [12]. The challenge is therefore to make traditional culture
understandable and attractive to modern consumers.
The basic framework can be summarized as: Traditional Culture → Product Innovation → IP
Collaboration → Better Consumer Experience → Digital Communication → Stronger Brand Equity.
This process can help traditional brands create both cultural and commercial value.
6. Conclusion
This paper uses brand equity theory and experiential marketing theory to discuss the modernization
of Chinese time-honored brands. The analysis shows that product innovation, IP collaboration,
scenario reconstruction, and digital marketing are four important strategies. Overall, successful
revitalization does not mean abandoning tradition. Traditional brands should keep their cultural
identity while changing how their products, stores, and marketing are presented. This balance
between heritage and innovation is important for long-term competitiveness.
However, this study has some limitations. It mainly uses literature review and case analysis rather
than large-scale consumer surveys or interviews. The selected cases are also successful examples
and may not represent every Chinese time-honored brand. Future research could use surveys and
interviews to study consumer attitudes toward different revitalization strategies.
In conclusion, Chinese time-honored brands can achieve sustainable revitalization by combining
cultural heritage with modern business strategies. Product innovation should provide the foundation,
IP collaboration should create new cultural connections, experiential marketing should improve
consumer engagement, and digital marketing should expand communication. When these strategies
are used together, traditional brands can remain connected to their history while becoming more
attractive to new generations.
