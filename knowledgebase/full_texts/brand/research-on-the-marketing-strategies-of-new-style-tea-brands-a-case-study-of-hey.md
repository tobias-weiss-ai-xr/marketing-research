Abstract. With social development and consumption upgrading, the scale of China's new-
type tea drink industry has kept expanding. In 2025, the market scale will occupy a
dominant position in the existing tea market. As the head brand of the industry, Xicha has
achieved stable development with the original product research and development and
diversified marketing strategies, but at the same time, is also confronted with realistic
dilemmas including low-price competition, product homogenization, the squeeze from low-
quality products, and consumer aesthetic exhaustion caused by over-frequency co-branding.
Most of the existing research focuses on communication mode and brand operation, and
pays little attention to traffic marketing and UGC content flooding. This paper takes hi-tea
as the research object, taking the new tea culture marketing strategy as the research
breakthrough, combing the development status of tea industry in regional consumption,
population structure and regional culture, summarizing typical marketing methods such as
consumer psychology, hunger marketing and UGC co-creation, analyzing the advantages of
hi-tea in marketing methods, and finding its difficulties in industry competition, store
management and joint strategy.And puts forward targeted suggestions for marketing strategy
optimization, including improving membership system, carrying out precise co-branding
cooperation and enhancing new media operation capabilities The main purpose of the study
is to enhance the competitiveness of hi-tea brand, realize sustainable development, and pave
the way for the whole new tea industry to explore high-quality development.
Keywords: Heytea, Digital marketing, Marketing optimization strategy, Brand publicity
1. Introduction
With the rapid development and transformation of society, the scale of China's new-style tea
industry has continued to expand. By 2025, it is expected to dominate the ready-to-drink tea market,
achieving a market size of 374.93 billion yuan and a year-on-year growth of 5.7%. In 2024, the
market size was 354.72 billion yuan, accounting for 90% of the entire ready-to-drink tea market and
over 65% of the overall market share, making it the absolute mainstream. The chain rate is as high
as 58%, and it is widely distributed globally, dominating the market. In 2025, the retail sales of new-
style tea are expected to grow by 9.5%, with new-style tea contributing 85% of the increase [1, 2].
As a leading brand in the new-style tea industry, Heytea has focused on original brand building and
redefined the business model for tea brands.

2. Current development status of new-style tea culture
2.1. Regional consumption level disparities
With the development of new-style tea brands, the differences in regional consumption levels
directly determine brand positioning stratification, product pricing standards and consumption
scenario design [1]. In recent years, the regional consumption levels have shown a pattern where the
per capita consumption in the eastern regions have high per capita consumption level, while central
and western regions have higher growth rate. The new-style tea culture precisely targets the
consumption levels between cities, stratifying consumers. High-end products are invested in first-
tier and new first-tier cities, with an average price ranging from 20 to 30 yuan, such as theight-
luxury brands such as Heytea, Chayanyuese and Baowang Chaji . Most of these brands feature low
sugar and limited editions [1, 6]. In second- and third-tier cities, products with a balance of cost-
effectiveness and quality are invested in, such as the mid-range chain brands like Guming and
Chabaodao, mainly catering to light milk tea and fresh fruit tea. In fourth-tier cities and county
areas, the average price of brands like Meixue Bingcheng is around 10 yuan, and consumers in these
areas are highly price-sensitive [2].
2.2. Gender proportions of consumers
According to relevant literature, the core consumer group of the new-style tea culture is young
women in large and medium-sized cities, gender consumption ratio of approximately 4:6.. The
proportion of women purchasing new-style tea is about 63.3%, while that of men is 36.7% [1, 7].
However, in recent years, with the rapid development of the new-style tea culture, the proportion of
male consumers has increased significantly, and the gender gap has gradually narrowed. More
Female consumers tend to buy for social media check-in and social interaction and other purposes,
while male consumers pay more attention to product taste and quality [7]. Additionally, women
dominate the new-style tea consumption market in first-tier cities [1].

2.3. The impact of regional culture on tea drinks
The new-style tea culture itself is a typical manifestation of secondary innovation of traditional tea
culture. The differences in regional cultures have further promoted the diversified development of
new-style tea cultures. Many tea brands are break through the single product category of light milk
tea and fresh fruit teabut innovate and develop based on the similarities and differences of regional
cultures [8]. For instance, Chayanne, a local brand in Changsha, attracting a large number of tourists
to check in specially, and this new-style tea brand has become a new consumption ecosystem.
Another example is the original brand in Xi'an, Chahuanao, which uses Shaanxi Fu tea as its base
and is a unique flavor of Xi'an [8]. Due to the differences in regional cultures, various new-style tea
brands have their own distinctive flavors, which effectively stimulate consumers' purchase intention
and significantly deepen city memory and brand effect, driving the development of the new-style tea
industry [8].
3. Marketing strategies of new tea culture
3.1. Psychological effects of brand influence on consumers
Most consumers have obvious herd mentality in consumption, believing that high sales volume
indicates outstanding quality of a brand. This psychological tendency of avoiding being out of date
has enabled new tea culture brands to identify a breakthrough in brand marketing [7]. Most tea
brands independently develop various types of tea beverages to attract consumers, who follow the
trend and purchase them, significantly increasing sales. The scarcity perception and comparative
psychology of consumers are also factors contributing to the rapid development of new tea industry
brands. Most tea merchants take advantage of this psychological trait by launching city-limited
stores and other related marketing strategies, creating a sense of superiority for consumers and
stimulating their purchasing desire [9].
3.2. The traffic code of hunger marketing
New tea brands use hunger marketing to attract a large number of consumers by offering limited-
time and limited-quantity products [9]. Heytea, for instance, innovatively develops seasonal fruit tea
beverages in strict accordance with the seasonal cycle, and takes the initiative to remove these
products from the shelves after the season ends [10].
3.3. Brand benefits from UGC culture
New tea culture brands leverage UGC culture to make full use of UGC communication mode to
capture public attention. UGC culture refers to consumers' consumers independently create and
share of evaluations about products through social media, rather than official brand promotions [5].
UGC culture is more persuasive and trustworthy to consumers and can enhance brand awareness
more effectively than official brand promotions [5]. Most consumers will consider public opinions
before purchasing related beverages. Heytea utilized UGC culture to launch a DIY cup sticker scene
for new tea beverages by the end of 2025, attracting many consumers. By setting a low threshold
and encouraging independent innovation, it transformed a cup of milk tea into a creative platform,
an emotional outlet, and a social medium for users, turning them from consumers into brand
designers [5]. This has brought a significant improvement to the brand's social influence and cultural
communication effect.

4. The effectiveness and existing problems of Heytea's marketing strategies
4.1. Economic effects of UGC culture
UGC boosts product sales and brand premiums through three core paths: trust construction, social
influence transmission and emotional resonance forming a closed loop of "grass planting" [5]. By
leveraging the equal status of consumers, UGC culture reduces information asymmetry and builds
purchase intention. Through word-of-mouth and the formation of group evaluations, UGC culture
creates a herd effect and promotes repurchase behavior. Due to the presence of UGC on various
social media platforms, it easily triggers emotional resonance among consumers and creates product
dependence. Heytea has utilized this feature to promote DIY cup stickers, stimulating consumers'
desire to purchase beverages. According to data, each collaboration generates an average of 35,000
UGC posts, with a repurchase rate of 68% among UGC creators, which is 32% higher than that of
ordinary users [5]. After the event, UGC creators contribute 15% of the daily traffic within a month,
creating asignificant long-tail communication effect . UGC creators not only drive high sales for the
tea brand and increase the purchase intention of other consumers but also maintain Heytea's long-
term brand reputation. The cup sticker activity introduced by Heytea has also created many job
opportunities, such as online drawing services. Many college students have seized the opportunity,
using the popularity to apply their talents and earn profits, thereby enhancing social welfare and
living standards. This has given rise to a new industrial chain, providing crucial assistance to
economic development [1].
4.2. Establishing differentiation barriers through original products
As a leading brand in the new tea culture, Heytea, as the first brand to introduce cheese milk cap
fresh tea, has breaking the traditional pattern of powder milk tea dominating the market [10].
Initially, Heytea established its market position through its innovative capabilities. By upholding
strict quality control standards, operating strict production lines, and employing efficient
manufacturing processes, Heytea pioneered the combination of cheese tea and fresh fruit tea,
establishing itself as an industry benchmark. Its first hit product, the "Plump Grape", has become a
classic that is widely imitated [10]. Subsequently, many new tea brands have attempted to replicate
Heytea's original products, but they have been unable to match the quality of Heytea's genuine
ingredients. Heytea mainly adopts global direct supply of raw materials, refuses to use artificial
flavors, preservatives, or additives, and uses high-quality original tea from regions such as Yunnan
and Fujian. All stores follow implement standardized on-site production processes of fresh cutting,
mashing, extraction, and blending. Such rigorous quality control has enabled Heytea to maintain its
leading position in the new tea beverage industry. It is precisely because of Heytea's original
products that the brand does not need to engage in low-price competition. Even with a higher-than-
market pricing, a large number of consumers are willing to purchase due to the original design. The
use of authentic ingredients and in-store production has attracted a large number of repeat purchases.
The inherent nature of original products as hot topics in marketing has also reduced Heytea's
marketing and communication costs. Most consumers make purchases driven by consumer
psychology and brand preference.

4.3. The impact of the "bad money drives out good money" phenomenon
The economic law of "bad money drives out good money" has been particularly prominent
throughout Heytea's development in recent years [4]. Low-price competition has directly
compressed Heytea's profit space To maintain basic operations, Heytea has had to reduce production
investment and lower product quality. At the same time, low-price competition has led to a lack of
funds for product innovation [11]. In the short term, low-priced products reduce consumers'
shopping costs, but in the long term, the low-price competition leads to a decline in product quality
and service guarantees. Without cost control, businesses use inferior raw materials, simplify
production processes, and lower product quality. Consumers have to spend a lot of time screening
products, reducing their trust in consumption, resulting in a short-term benefit but long-term decline
[4]. It also leads to a homogenization dilemma. For example, Heytea's original "Cheese Plump
Grape" was quickly imitated by other tea brands. In 2026, Luckin Coffee launched a similar product
at a lower price, making the imitation cost extremely low. In the hot topic of "takeout war" that
emerged in 2025, it was more evident that consumers demanded lower prices from new-style tea
drink culture brands. In the continuous competition on takeout platforms, Heytea was forced to
lower its prices, even to as low as 5-6 yuan, directly changing its brand's high-end positioning to
mid-range positioning one. However, as the production costs did not decrease, the profit margin was
severely squeezed [2]. Additionally, overly dense franchise store placement led to intensifying
internal competition. In some tourist attractions and snack streets, two to three Heytea stores would
appear within a few hundred meters, resulting in internal customer diversion and a decline in daily
store turnover, creating a vicious cycle. At the same time, the rapid expansion of franchised stores
significantly increased management difficulties for Heytea's headquarters and weakened the user
experience, gradually eroding the brand's reputation. In response, Heytea announced in 2025 that it
would temporary suspension of franchised store cooperation , and adjust its management approach.
Whether it was the low-price competition, homogenization, or internal friction, all these factors
forced Heytea to change its business model. Heytea focused on these external factors rather than
concentrating on its brand marketing, leading to a decline in profits and a subsequent reduction in
the cost of producing beverages, which seriously hindered market expansion, product innovation,
and even led to a decline in brand reputation [6, 10].
4.4. Excessive collaborations leading to aesthetic fatigue
Heytea's excessive collaborations would cause a significantly reduce consumers' purchase intention.
Initially, when the first collaboration was launched, most consumers followed the trend to purchase
to enhance their sense of satisfaction. However, in the subsequent second and third months, with
continuous collaborations, consumers' novelty was exhausted, and the expected effect was not
achieved. Eventually, with over a hundred collaborations in a year, it would instead lead to
consumer aesthetic fatigue [12]. At the end of 2024, Heytea vigorously promoted "handwritten
style" and "low-saturation Morandi colors". Although these designs were innovative based on the
original ones, they did not truly attract consumers and even drew dissatisfaction from many
consumers, who believed that Heytea's designs were too monotonous and standardized, losing the
brand's original vitality. In 2025, Heytea adjusted its sales strategy, launching only two collaboration
products in a year, focusing on high quality and high-quality and refined cooperation options. It first
collaborated with Chiikawa, a brand inspired by cute and soothing elements, to create a good
emotional value and precisely target the consumer group, catering to the emotional needs of young
people seeking stress relief, thereby achieving emotional resonance through a cup of drink [10, 12].

5. Optimization of Heytea's new media marketing strategy
5.1. Optimization of the membership system and precise collaboration placement
Heytea can integrate data from Go mini-program, takeout platforms, Xiaohongshu, Douyin to build
a comprehensive 360-degree user labeling system. The tags can mainly be based on age, city, store
preference, and consumption frequency to segment consumers into different groups and form
hierarchical membership benefit system, so as to optimize the membership system [6, 12]. By
relying on membership data to form a complete user profile, high-level members can enjoy benefits
such as priority purchase to pre-sale products. Members can receive corresponding rewards for
sharing collaboration products, secondary creations, and check-ins, creating a dissemination effect.
Heytea can also customized co-branding activities to members from high to low tiers, further
refining product positioning to maximize marketing and sales results [5].
5.2. Leveraging new media for long-term development
Heytea utilizes new media to provide important communication carrier for brand rejuvenation and
experiential scenarios. Relying on the WeChat social platform, short video shooting, micro-film
production and other methods, various co-branded products, cup sticker DIY, LAb exclusive stores
are launched to maintain consumer demand, ensure revenue and promote sustainable development.
It also weaken short-term gimmick marketing and focuses on creating high-quality products and
continuous brand value output. Through interactive communication on new media platforms, Heytea
can better understand consumer demands and market trends, while consumers cantimely understand
product price changes and new product launches. In the middle of 2024, the black and white color
combination launched by Heytea was opposed by most consumers. Heytea learned the fundamental
reason through new media, which was the difference caused by traditional cultural concepts and
provided corresponding solutions.
6. Conclusion
This article takes the development of the new-style tea beverage industry as the background and
focuses on Heytea as the core research object. It systematically reviews the current development
status of the new-style tea beverage industry in terms of regional consumption, consumer
demographics, and the integration of regional cultures. It summarizes the mainstream marketing
strategies such as the application of consumer psychology, hunger marketing, and UGC user co-
creation. It also deeply analyzes the effectiveness and existing problems of Heytea's marketing
strategies and proposes optimization suggestions and solutions. The research shows that the reason
why Heytea can maintain a leading position in the highly competitive new-style tea beverage market
lies in its two core marketing strategies. The first is to establish a differentiation barrier through
original products and avoid price wars by selling its pioneering offerings. The second is to take
