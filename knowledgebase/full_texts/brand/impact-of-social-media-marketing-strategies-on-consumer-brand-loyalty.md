This paper is a systematic literature review (SLR) on the impact of social media marketing
(SMM) strategies on consumer brand loyalty and closely related constructs, such as brand
equity, brand trust and repurchase intention. The review is performed accord ing to the
PRISMA 2020 protocol. A search in Scopus, Web of Science and Google Scholar resulted
in 687 records. The author has no institutional access to Web of Science and Google Scholar
does not have a bulk citation-export function; full title-and-abstract data suitable for record-
level screening were available only for the Scopus export. The 589 records from those two
sources could therefore not be screened and the review is in effect a single -database review
of Scopus-indexed gold open-access literature.
Of 98 records screened (exclusion: off topic or non-qualifying, n=13), 85 reports were to be
retrieved. 2 records had no full text available. 83 reports were assessed for eligibility. At this
stage, another 34 were excluded, mainly because one of the four focal constructs was not the
ultimate tested outcome but merely a mediator toward another dependent variable, because
the predictor was not a social-media-marketing or influencer-marketing construct, or because
the sample was not consumers. This resulted i n 49 included studies published from 2020 to
2026. One reviewer did the screening, eligibility assessment, quality appraisal and data
extraction without independent-rater cross-validation.
The most tested terminal outcome among the 49 included studies is brand loyalty (27 studies)
followed by brand equity and repurchase intention (12 each). Brand trust is a terminal
outcome in only 4 studies but is a mechanism mediating in a further 8 studie s, suggesting
that it is more often a mechanism than an endpoint in this literature. The two main predictor
flows are social media marketing activities (SMMA) - entertainment, interaction, trendiness,
customization and electronic word -of-mouth (eWOM) – and influencer marketing. The
influence of influencer marketing on loyalty and equity is through both direct and indirect
channels. The main mediating factors in the SMM-loyalty and the SMM–equity relationships
are consumer brand engagement and brand trust. T he paper concludes with theoretical and
managerial implications, a synthesized research model and an agenda for future research
emphasizing multi-database, multi-coder and longitudinal designs.

1. INTRODUCTION
Social media platforms have changed the way brands interact with consumers, moving from one -way marketing to interactive,
personalized and community -oriented marketing (Kim & Ko, 2012; Godey et al., 2016). Today, companies across the board - from
banking and insurance to fashion, food and beverage and consumer electronics - are investing a lot of marketing effort into social media
marketing (SMM) activities such as entertainment, interaction, trendiness, customization, and electronic word -of-mouth (eWOM), and
influencer marketing, expecting that these investments will translate into stronger and more loyal customer relationships (Ya ng et al.,
2022; Jung & Kim, 2025).  However, the empirical literature on SMM and consumer brand loyalty is fragmented despite this general
managerial interest. Studies vary according to the SMM dimensions they address (some focus narrowly on influencer credibility , while
others measure the f ive-dimension SMMA scale), the outcome construct they highlight (brand loyalty, brand equity, brand trust, or
repurchase intention), the mediating and moderating mechanisms they explore (brand engagement, brand love, brand trust, gender), and
the industry and cultural context in which they are conducted. This fragmentation makes it difficult for researchers and prac titioners to
draw a consolidated picture of how and under what conditions SMM strategies build brand loyalty.

This study aims to fill this gap by conducting a systematic review of empirical research published between 2020 and 2026 that examines
the relationship between social media or influencer marketing and consumer brand loyalty, brand equity, brand trust, and repurchase
intention. The review follows the PRISMA 2020 protocol (Page et al., 2021) and has five research questions:

RQ1. What social media marketing strategies and dimensions have been empirically examined in relation to consumer brand loyalty
and what are the results?
RQ2: How does social media marketing affect brand equity?
RQ3: What is the impact of social media marketing on brand trust?
RQ4: The impact of social media marketing on repurchase intention?
RQ5: What are the mediating and moderating processes that explain the link between social media marketing and consumer brand
loyalty?

Following the systematic review approach demonstrated in the course -provided exemplar reviews on digital transformation and
electronic service quality this review is organized into five further sections: a literature review defining the core constru cts; a
methodology section describing the search strategy, screening procedure and quality assessment; a results and discussion sect ion
organized around the five research questions and closing with a synthesized research model; a conclusion summarizing theoreti cal and
managerial implications and limitations; and the references and appendices.

2. THEORETICAL FRAMEWORK

This section defines the core constructs examined across the 49 included studies and fits them within the established branding literature
from which they derive.

2.1. Social media marketing and social media marketing activities (SMMA)
Social media marketing (SMM) is the use by firms of social media platforms (e.g., Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, WeChat) to
communicate, engage and influence current and prospective customers (Kim & Ko, 2012). A large number of the studies employ th e
five-dimension social media marketing activities (SMMA) scale developed by Kim and Ko (2012) in a luxury -fashion context and
subsequently adapted across industries as diverse as banking (Hafez, 2022), telecommunications (Hafez, 2021), logistics (Lin et al.,
2024), and green or sustainable products (Hu et al., 2024). Throughout this review we reserve the term SMMA for this multi-dimensional
scale. Studies that measure social media marketing as a single undifferentiated construct are described as using general SMM (Zhou,
2023; Puspaningrum, 2020; Chen et al., 2023).

2.2.  Influencer marketing
A related but conceptually distinct line of research is influencer marketing, in which brands use people with an existing online audience
to endorse products and shape consumer perceptions (Jung & Kim, 2025; Sarkis et al., 2026). Most often is operationali zed within the
included studies through source -credibility attributes such as credibility, intimacy, attractiveness and product match -up (Jung & Kim,
2025; Narongkananukul et al., 2025; Amperawati et al., 2024 ). Its effect on brand outcomes is mediated through significant direct paths
as well as significant indirect paths via consumer engagement, brand trust and brand attachment (Zahran & Aljuhmani, 2025; Li  et al.,
2026; Jung & Kim, 2025).

2.3.  Brand loyalty
Brand loyalty is a deeply held commitment to rebuying a preferred brand consistently in the future, despite situational influ ences and
marketing efforts having the potential to cause switching behavior (Oliver, 1999). It is often conceptualized as a multidimensional construct
with cognitive, affective, conative and action-based loyalty (Bashir et al., 2025) and is the most studied terminal outcome in this review,
appearing in 27 of the 49 included studies.

2.4. Brand equity
Brand equity is the additional value a brand name adds to a product and is typically measured by brand awareness, brand association or
image and perceived quality (Aaker, 1991; Keller, 1993). Consumer-based brand equity is the most widely used conceptualization across
the reviewed studies (Cheung et al., 2020; Dananjoyo & Udin, 2025) and a terminal outcome in 12 of the 49 studies included.

2.5. Brand trust
Brand trust is the consumer’s willingness to rely on a brand’s ability to perform its stated function, given the consumer’s perceptions of
reliability, honesty and benevolence (Chaudhuri & Holbrook, 2001). Its place in this literature is unique: brand trust is the final outcome
in only 4 of the 49 studies included (Haudi et al., 2022; Rico et al., 2024; Sarkis et al., 2026; Bashir et al., 2024) while in another 8 it is
modelled as a mediator transmitting the effect of SMM on brand loyalty (e.g., Puspaningru m, 2020; Althuwaini, 2022; Santoso, 2023;
Jung & Kim, 2025). Therefore, in the literature reviewed, brand trust is largely an explanatory mechanism rather than an end point in its
own right.

2.6. Repurchase intention
Repurchase intention is considered in the services literature as an important behavioral outcome of positive brand evaluations (Zeithaml
et al., 1996). It is defined as the subjective probability that a consumer will purchase again from the same brand. It is the final outcome in
12 of the 49 studies incorporated (e.g., Zhou, 2023; Jalil et al., 2021; Hu et al., 2024), often investigated together with brand awareness or
brand equity as antecedents. Electronic word-of-mouth is one of the SMMA dimensions and is rooted in the consumer-opinion-platform
literature (Hennig-Thurau et al., 2004).

3. METHODOLOGY

This study was conducted in five stages, following the systematic review procedures demonstrated in the course -provided exemplar
reviews (Digital Transformation on Product Innovation; Electronic Service Quality on Electronic Loyalty) and the PRISMA 2020 reporting
guideline (Page et al., 2021): (1) data sources and search strategy, (2) inclusion and exclusion criteria, (3) PRISMA-based screening and
eligibility assessment, (4) quality assessment, and (5) data coding and synthesis. No protocol was published a nd the review was not
preregistered.

3.1. Data sources and search strategy
Records were identified by searching three databases and search engines using combinations of the keywords “social media marketing”,
“SMMA”, “influencer marketing”, “brand loyalty”, “brand equity”, “brand trust”, and “repurchase intention”, restricted by e mpirical-
study terms (“empirical”, “survey”, “correlation”, “SEM”, “regression”). All searches were last run on 13 August 2026. The exact search
strings were:

Scopus: TITLE-ABS-KEY(("social media marketing" OR "SMMA" OR "influencer marketing") AND ("brand loyalty" OR "brand equity"
OR "brand trust" OR "repurchase intention") AND ("empirical" OR "survey" OR "correlation" OR "SEM" OR "regression")), filtered to
2020-2026, Article, English, Final publication stage, Gold open access.

Web of Science: TS=(("social media marketing" OR "SMMA" OR "influencer marketing") AND ("brand loyalty" OR "brand equity" OR
"brand trust" OR "repurchase intention") AND ("empirical" OR "survey" OR "correlation" OR "SEM" OR "regression")), filtered
identically.

Google Scholar: "social media marketing" "brand loyalty" "correlation" OR "regression" OR "survey", limited to articles and reviews from
2025-2026.

3.2. Inclusion and exclusion criteria
The only source for which full title and abstract data for each record was available for individual-record screening was the Scopus export
(n = 98). Two access constraints prevented equivalent screening of the other two sources: the author did not have institutional access to
Web of Science through the university library, and Google Scholar does not have a bulk citation-export function comparable to Scopus or
Web of Science. The same search strings identified 65 Web of Science records and 524 Google Scholar records that were thus excluded
at the identification stage due to issues of database access rather than relevance, duplication or quality. In practical term s, the review is
substantively limited by the use of a single-database, gold open-access corpus, as discussed further in Section 5.

3.5. Data coding and synthesis
Each of the 49 included studies was coded for: (a) country and context, (b) industry, (c) the social media marketing predictor construct(s)
examined, (d) the terminal outcome construct(s), (e) any mediating or moderating variables tested, (f) the empirical method used, and (g)
the sample size. The full coding for all 49 studies is reported in Table 4 (Section 4.7). These codes are the basis for the narrative synthesis
in Section 4. One reviewer performed the coding and synthesis. A second coder was not available, so no statistics for inter-rater reliability
could be calculated.

4.2. RQ1: Social media marketing dimensions and consumer brand loyalty
Of the 49 studies included, 27 examine brand loyalty as a terminal outcome. The five-dimension SMMA scale emerges as the dominant
predictor, with a consistent influence on brand loyalty, albeit with varying pathways. Several report significant direct effects. Abdou (2025)
reports a strong direct path from SMMA to brand loyalty among Gen Z coffee -shop consumers. Al -Hujri et al. (2025) also report a
significant direct effect in Yemen’s telecom sector, along with a satisfaction-mediated path. Tazkia and Ariyanti (2025) report a significant
direct effect for a sportswear brand. Some other models are fully mediated. For example, Malarvizhi et al. (2022) do not specify any direct
path from SMMA to loyalty, routing the effect entirely through brand awareness and brand image.

Literature is generally dominated by indirect ways. Brand love (Linh et al., 2024), brand romance (Bashir et al., 2025), brand attachment
(Laradi et al., 2026), consumer brand preference (Abdou, 2025), and online brand engagement (Nguyen et al., 2024) give rise to loyalty.
Also, general non-decomposed SMM is associated with brand loyalty in insurance, digital banking and retail (Albdour et al., 2024; Sang,
2023; Limphaiboon, 2025). In a Saudi Arabian sample, where SMM is broken down into sub -dimensions, word-of-mouth is found by
Alanazi (2023) to be the highest impact dimension. This is a single-study finding and is not supported elsewhere in the corpus.

Influencer marketing is a smaller, but growing, stream in the loyalty literature. The evidence suggests influencer marketing works through
direct and indirect routes, not just indirect routes. Zahran and Aljuhmani (2025) found that influencer marketing had a significant direct
effect on brand loyalty (β =.351) which was greater than its indirect effect on consumer engagement (β =.215). Jung and Kim (2025) found
the significant direct paths from influencer credibility (β = .301) and intimacy (β = .186) to brand loyalty. Li et al. (2026) found that all six
of their influencers and fan-page content dimensions have significant direct paths to brand loyalty, in addition to indirect effects.

4.3. RQ2: Social media marketing and brand equity
Twelve studies treat brand equity as a terminal outcome, most often operationalized by awareness, association or image, and perceived-
quality subdimensions. The evidence shows two different structures. First, SMMA dimensions enhance brand awareness and brand image,
which further strengthen the overall brand equity (Malarvizhi et al., 2022; Yang et al., 2022). In the second, brand equity i s directly
impacted by SMMA, and brand equity itself mediates toward a downstream behavioral outcome (Lin et al., 2024, w here brand equity
mediates the pathway to reuse intention). A third group directs the effect through consumer brand engagement instead of through awareness
or image (Chen et al., 2023; Ali & Alqudah, 2022; Nguyen et al., 2026; Rossanty et al., 2024). Another tested pathway is brand experience
(Hafez, 2022; Dananjoyo & Udin, 2025). Value co-creation is tested in one study (Hafez, 2021).

Entertainment is the SMMA sub-dimension most frequently reported as non-significant for brand equity (Lin et al., 2024; Hamarat et al.,
2026). Yet, as is discussed in Section 4.6, interaction is reported as non -significant just as often, thus there is no o ne dimension that is
consistently weaker across the corpus. A similar pattern of positive effects on brand equity is observed in influencer market ing.
Narongkananukul et al. (2025) found that source attractiveness, source credibility, product match-up, and meaning transfer are important
drivers of brand equity, whereas Kalaivani et al. (2026) considered influencer credibility as a single predictor that influen ces consumer
engagement, consumer trust, and purchase intention.

4.4. RQ3: Social media marketing and brand trust
Literature has a peculiar position for brand trust. The terminal outcome is only seen in 4 of the 49 studies included (Haudi et al., 2022;
Rico et al., 2024; Sarkis et al., 2026; Bashir et al., 2024) and is present as a mediating mechanism in another 8. Thus, the most substantively
useful finding of this review regarding RQ3 is structural: researchers overwhelmingly treat brand trust as the psychological mechanism
through which social media marketing produces loyalty rather than as an end in itself.

Among the studies that do test trust as an outcome, Bashir et al. (2024) and Haudi et al. (2022) find significant positive effects of SMM
on brand trust using general consumer samples. Within the framework of influencer marketing, Sarkis et al. (2026) find that influencer-
brand fit is the most important driver of brand trust (indirect β =.182) above credibility (β =.109), with the effect fully mediated through
consumer engagement. Jung and Kim (2025) examined credibility, expertise, intimacy and informativeness in their study and found that
only credibility (β = .286) and intimacy (β =.243) significantly predict brand trust whereas expertise and informativeness ar e non -
significant and expertise is negatively signed on brand loyalty.

Rico et al. (2024) report an unusually large coefficient (β =.952) for social media marketing on trust in Colombian retail ba nking. This
estimate should be viewed with skepticism: the study reports t -statistics in excess of 70,000 on a sample of 528, repor ts the identical
coefficient for two different dependent variables, and reports elsewhere in the same paper that no relationship was found between social
media use and customer trust. It is included in the corpus for completeness but is not considered as evidence of effect magnitude in this
synthesis.

4.5. RQ4: Social media marketing and repurchase intention
In twelve studies, repurchase intention is studied as a final outcome. Effects are transmitted through several mechanisms. Brand awareness
is a repeated mediator: Jalil et al. (2021) found that social media marketing significantly predicts repurchase intention for halal cosmetics
both directly and indirectly through brand awareness, whereas eWOM did not significantly predict repurchase intention in that  model.
Repurchase intention is mediated by brand awareness and brand image via SMMA effects (Yang et al., 2022). In the Chinese context, the
mediating effect is through relational bonding, with the mediating mechanism being the guanxi (Zhou, 2023), and for Indonesian MSMEs,
the mediating effect is through brand resonance value (Munir et al., 2025).

Sustainability-related mechanisms appear in eco-conscious product categories. Hu et al. (2024) identify environmental concern and brand
image as mediators of the SMMA -repurchase intention relationship for green products in Taiwan. Yosita et al. (2026) fram e the same
relationship within the theory of planned behaviour, with attitude and subjective norm as mediators and green awareness as a moderator
rather than a mediator. Brand love and brand centrality mediate the relationship in Indonesian fashion (Mayasari et al., 2023), and brand
equity does so for e-commerce repurchase (Maskuroh et al., 2022).

Li et al. (2026) also model consumer engagement and brand loyalty as parallel mediators of influencer and fan-page content on repurchase
intention. The model estimates separate paths from each content dimension to engagement and to loyalty and does not tes t a serial
engagement-to-loyalty chain. Working in a doctor-influencer context, Amperawati et al. (2024) find that expertise, success appeal, and
congruency are significant drivers of brand attitude, while physical attractiveness and familiarity are not, as a result the authors suggest
challenges the traditional emphasis on influencer attractiveness.

4.6. RQ5: Mediating and moderating mechanisms
Consumer brand engagement is the most frequently tested mediator across the corpus, appearing in models linking SMM to brand loyalty,
brand equity, and repurchase intention alike (Chen et al., 2023; Nguyen et al., 2024; Ali & Alqudah, 2022; Sarkis et al., 2026; Zahran &
Aljuhmani, 2025). Brand trust is the second most common mediator, appearing in 8 studies, and as discussed under RQ3 it typic ally

constitutes the final psychological step before loyalty is realized (Puspaningrum, 2020; Althuwaini, 2022; Santoso, 2023; Taz kia &
Ariyanti, 2025). Affective mechanisms form a smaller but theoretically important cluster: brand love (Linh et al., 2024; Mayasari et al.,
2023), brand romance (Bashir et al., 2025), brand attachment (Laradi et al., 2026; Jung & Kim, 2025), and brand affect (Santoso, 2023).
Their presence indicates that emotional bonding, and not only cognitive trust, is a route from social media exposure to loyalty.

The relative strength of individual SMMA dimensions showed that entertainment was non -significant in 3 studies (Lin et al., 2024;
Hamarat et al., 2026; Linh et al., 2024) and interaction in 3 studies (Malarvizhi et al., 2022; Hamarat et al., 2026; Li et al., 2026). Thus,
there is no one dimension that is consistently weaker. Claims that entertainment is uniquely ineffective are not borne out by this corpus.

On the moderation side, gender is tested as a moderator in 2 studies (Amperawati et al., 2024; Li et al., 2026), and brand awareness (Abdou,
2025), product involvement (Cheung et al., 2020), and green awareness (Yosita et al., 2026) are tested in each one study. Some moderation
tests show non-significant or marginal results, for instance nationality in Yuswita and Maulidah (2024). Given the small number of studies
testing any given moderator, the evidence for moderation in this literature is best described as preliminary rather than established.

4.8. A synthesized research model for future testing
Bringing together the mediating and moderating mechanisms revealed in Sections 4.2 to 4.6, this subsection synthesizes the re viewed
evidence into one conceptual model (Figure 5). The model is framed within the stimulus-organism-response (S-O-R) paradigm used by a
number of the included studies (Malarvizhi et al., 2022; Abdou, 2025). Social media marketing activities and influencer marketing are the
stimulus; consumer brand engagement, brand trust and brand love or attachment are the internal organism states through which consumers
process that stimulus; and brand equity, brand loyalty and repurchase intention are the behavioral responses. The model, pres ented as a
synthesized output of this review, is for guiding future primary research and meta-analytic testing; it is not empirically tested here.

Thirteen hypotheses are proposed (Table 5). We hypothesize that social media marketing activities will affect all three organ ism-level

equity and brand loyalty are hypothesized to affect repurchase intention (H10-H11). The influencer marketing pathway is proposed to be
moderated by gender due to its recurrent role in reviewed literature (H12). The loyalty repurchase intention link is proposed  to be

5. Conclusion
This systematic literature review analyzed 49 empirical studies that have been published between 2020 and 2026 to explore the impact of
social media marketing and influencer marketing strategies on consumer brand loyalty, brand equity, brand trust and repurchase intention.
There are three patterns in the evidence. First, the five -dimension SMMA framework remains the dominant framework used to
operationalize social media marketing, but no one dimension is consistently stronger or weaker across the corpus: en tertainment and
interaction were reported as non-significant in three studies. Second, consumer brand engagement and brand trust are the two important
mediating mechanisms through which social media marketing effects are transmitted onto loyalty, equity an d repurchase intention. In
particular, brand trust functions predominantly as a mechanism. It is a terminal outcome in only 4 of the 49 studies but a me diator in a
further 8. Third, influencer marketing has a significant direct and a significant indirect p athway, so treating it as a purely indirect lever
underestimates its effect.

The review theoretically integrates a fragmented literature that has studied the relationship of social media marketing to loyalty, equity,
trust and repurchase intention largely in isolation and clarifies the sequential and parallel roles of engagement, t rust and affective
mechanisms across these four outcomes. From a managerial perspective, the findings imply that marketers should develop content around
informational, interactive and personalized value and authentic influencer partnerships, and should treat engagement and trust metrics as
intermediate performance indicators that lead to loyalty rather than as end goals. The importance of entertainment and intera ction is

comparable, which means that it is not a question of choosing between different types of content in practice, but that the effectiveness of
content seems to be dependent on the context.

There are some limitations to this review. The most consequential is the search coverage: the author had no institutional access to Web of
Science, and Google Scholar does not have a bulk export function, so only Scopus records could be screened at the level of the individual
record. 65 Web of Science and 524 Google Scholar records retrieved by the same strings were not assessed individually and may contain
relevant studies not found in Scopus. It’s an access constraint, not a suggestion that those records were duplicate or irrelevant. It means
that the review is, in practice, a single-database review. The Google Scholar search window (2025-2026) is also different from that used
for the two indexed databases (2020-2026).

Three additional limitations qualify the findings. First, the corpus was limited to English-language, gold open access articles. Gold open
access was an access constraint, not a design choice, and it biases the corpus toward article-processing-charge-funded outlets, which are
unevenly distributed across regions and disciplines. Second, the requirement for methodological keywords ("empirical", "surve y",
"correlation", "SEM", "regression") to appear in the title, abstract or keywords will have excluded many quantitative studies that do not
use those words in indexed fields and is a probable reason the Scopus search returned only 98 records for a large literature.  Third,
screening, assessment of eligibility, quality appraisal and data extraction were all undert aken by a single reviewer, so no inter‐rater
reliability could be calculated, and the review is subject to the interpretive bias of a single coder. This bias is most consequential for the
34 full-text exclusions, which hinged on the judgement of whether a focal construct was a genuine terminal outcome, or an upstream
mediator; the full list of those decisions is reported in Appendix B so that readers may assess them independently. The quality assessment
instrument also showed a marked ceiling effect and should not be used as a detailed ranking of rigor.

Future research should replicate this review with full -text and bulk exports from all three databases, preferably with institutional Web
of Science access and a systematic Google Scholar extraction tool; expand the search window and eliminate the methodological-keyword
constraint; employ at least two independent coders with a reported inter -rater reliability statistic; and progress toward a formal meta -
analysis of the effect sizes reported in the quantitative studies identified here, so that the strength of  the social media marketing-brand
loyalty relationship can be quantified rather than merely described. Section 4.8 discusses a conceptual model that offers a s tarting
structure for such work.
