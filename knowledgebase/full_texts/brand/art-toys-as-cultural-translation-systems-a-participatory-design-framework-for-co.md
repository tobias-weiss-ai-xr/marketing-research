ABSTRACT

           Art toys have become highly influential across Asia, particularly in China,
Japan, South Korea, and Singapore, where culturally inspired character design
has been used to strengthen tourism branding and enhance community product
value, generating economic impact worth billions of baht. In Thailand, however,
the application of art toys for community development and tourism promotion
remains limited and lacks systematic design guidelines. This study aims to
develop design guidelines fo r art toy s based on regional cultural capital in
Thailand to support tourism promotion and enhance community products.
A mixed-methods research design was employed, including field observation,
in-depth interviews, focus groups, expert evaluation, and ques tionnaires. The
study involved 510 participants across three regions of Thailand:  Central,
Northeastern, and Southern. Cultural identity and regional symbolic animals
were used as key inspirations for art toy character development and packaging
prototypes for community products. Qualitative findings reveal that art toy s
derived from local identity effectively communicate cultural narratives and
strengthen place recognition. Quantitative results indicate high l evels of tourist
satisfaction, emotional engagement, and purchase intention. The study
demonstrates that art toys function as contemporary mediators that translate
local identity into marketable forms, thereby enhancing community product
value and supporting the creative economy. The findings contribute to a practical
design framework for art toy development in tourism and community product
contexts in Thailand. The framework emphasizes community participation,
cultural capital integration, and intellectual  property management to ensure
sustainable implementation and scalability across regions.

1. INTRODUCTION

In recent years, art toy s have emerged as influential cultural artifacts within the global creative
economy, particularly across Asia, where character -based design has been integrated into tourism branding,
urban identity, and community product development. Countries such as Chin a, Japan, and South Korea have
successfully utilized character design and collectible toy culture to enhance place identity, stimulate local
economies, and strengthen destination branding (Howkins, 2001; Morgan et al., 2011). These developments
reflect the  growing role of symbolic products and visual storytelling in shaping tourism experiences and
consumer engagement within the experience economy (Pine & Gilmore, 1999).
Art toys function as contemporary cultural mediators that translate local narratives int o tangible and
marketable forms. As collectible objects, they often embody place -based identity, memory, and cultural
symbolism, aligning with theories of cultural capital and the creative economy that emphasize the
transformation of cultural knowledge into economic value (Howkins, 2001; Mankong, 2017; Wongchaum, 2010).
When applied within community contexts, character -based design can strengthen emotional connections
between visitors and destinations, contributing to destination branding and place identity  formation
(Kavaratzis & Hatch, 2013; Pike, 2009). Moreover, souvenirs and character merchandise have long been
recognized as symbolic carriers of cultural meaning that reinforce tourists’ memories and experiences
(Gordon, 1986; Swanson & Timothy, 2012).
Within the field of design, visual identity and product symbolism play critical roles in brand perception
and value creation. Strong brand identities are built through consistent visual narratives and symbolic
representations that communicate meani ng and au thenticity ( Keller, 2013). Packaging and product design
further support this process by translating cultural values into tangible forms that enhance market
competitiveness and consumer recognition (Klimchuk & Krasovec , 2013; Rundh, 2016). In community -based
contexts, such visual systems can help differentiate local products in increasingly competitive tourism markets.
Thailand possesses rich cultural diversity, traditional crafts, and strong community -based production
systems, particularly within the One Ta mbon One Product (OTOP) 1 framework. However, many community
products still face challenges related to branding, storytelling, and visual differentiation. While cultural capital
exists in abundance, the translation of local identity into contemporary visual  language remains limited
(Louhapensang, 2015; Mankong, 2017). As a result, community products often struggle to compete in tourism
and souvenir markets where visual identity and emotional engagement are key factors influencing consumer
behavior (Kotler & Keller, 2016).
Recent research in community development and design innovation highlights the importance of
participatory design and social innovation approaches in strengthening local economies and cultural
sustainability (Manzini, 2015; Sanoff, 2000). Par ticipatory processes enable communities to identify and
express their own identities through collaborative design, ensuring authenticity and long -term adoption. Such
approaches align with national strategies emphasizing creative economy development and cul ture-based
tourism as key drivers of sustainable growth in Thailand  (Office of the Secretary of the National Strategy
Committee, 2018).
Despite these developments, the use of art toys as tools for tourism promotion and community product
enhancement remains  underexplored in Thailand. Existing studies have examined cultural product
development and packaging design from creative economy perspectives (Louhapensang, 2015; Pruettikomon,
2022), yet few have proposed systematic design guidelines for art toy  development grounded in regional
cultural identity. Therefore, research is needed to integrate cultural capital, participatory design, and creative
economy principles into a structured framework for art toy design applicable to community contexts.
This study aims  to develop design guidelines for art toy s that can promote tourism and enhance
community products across different regions of Thailand. By examining case studies from the central,
northeastern, and southern regions, the research explores how local identity, cultural narratives, and symbolic
elements can be translated into character -based design and community product packaging. The study adopts
a mixed -methods approach combining qualitative field research, participatory design workshops, and
quantitative ev aluation to assess the effectiveness of art toy  design in strengthening tourism identity and
product value.
The significance of this research lies in its contribution to both design scholarship and community
development practice. From a theoretical perspec tive, the study expands the role of art toy s beyond
commercial collectibles, positioning them as tools for cultural communication, place branding, and creative

2. PROJECT OBJECTIVES

This study aims to develop design guidelines for art toy s as cultural translation tools that promote
tourism and enhance community products by integrating cultural identity, participatory design, and creative

3. REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE

3.1 Art toys and character-based design
Art toys represent a contemporary form of character -based design that merges artistic expression,
consumer culture, and collectible object design. Unlike traditional toys, art toys function as symbolic artifacts
that communicate identity, narrative, and cultural meaning through stylized visual forms. Their development
is closely linked to the rise of the creative economy, in which ideas, creativity, and symbolic value become key
drivers of economic production (Howkins, 2001). Within this context, character -based design plays a crucial
role in constructing visual identities that resonate emotionally with audiences and consumers.
Character design has long been used in branding and visual communication to create recognizable
identities and emotional connections. Stron g brand symbols and characters contribute to brand equity by
establishing familiarity, trust, and narrative continuity (Keller, 2013). In tourism and place branding contexts,
character-based visual systems can strengthen the memorability of destinations an d products by translating
local identity into accessible and engaging forms (Morgan et al., 2011; Pike, 2009).
Art toys extend these principles by functioning as collectible representations of identity. They operate
at the intersection of design, culture, and consumer behavior, where symbolic meaning and visual storytelling
enhance perceived value and desirability. As souvenirs and collectible objects, they serve as tangible reminders
of experiences and places, reinforcing the emotional relationship between  visitors and destinations (Gordon,
1986; Swanson & Timothy, 2012).
Recent studies have further highlighted the role of art toy s as cultural communication media that
extend beyond entertainment and collectible functions. Tan and Wei (2026) argued that contemporary art toy
intellectual property (IP) increasingly relies on cultural symbolism and narrative design to establish emotional
connections with consumers. Similarly, Zhou (2025), through a case study of Pop Mart, demonstrated how
data-driven design refinement, scarcity strategies, and cross -media storytelling can transform art toy s into
influential cultural symbols and community -building platforms. These findings suggest that art toys possess
significant potential as design tools for tr anslating local cultural identity into meaningful and marketable
products.
3.2 Cultural identity and design translation
The translation of cultural identity into design is a key concern in contemporary design research.
Cultural capital theory suggests that traditions, symbols, and local knowledge can be transformed into creative
products that generate both economic and social value (Mankong, 2017; Wongchaum, 2010). In design practice,
this translation process involves interpreting cultural narratives and vi sual elements into forms that are
understandable within contemporary visual culture.
Place identity and place branding theories highlight the importance of visual representation in
communicating the uniqueness of locations. According to Kavaratzis  and Hatch (2013), place brands emerge
through dynamic interactions between cultural meaning, visual symbols, and stakeholder participation.
Character-based design can act as a mediator within this process, translating local narratives into visual forms
that can be widely recognized and shared.
Recent research has emphasized the importance of cultural storytelling in the interpretation and
communication of place identity. Rahmanto and Fariza (2025)  found that storytelling serves as a mechanism
for transform ing cultural heritage and collective memory into recognizable place identities, thereby
strengthening community engagement and destination branding. In the context of design, storytelling enables
cultural symbols, traditions, and local narratives to be tra nslated into visual forms that are accessible to
contemporary audiences while preserving cultural authenticity.

Packaging and product design further support this translation by embedding cultural meaning into
physical objects. Visual elements such as form,  color, and material can communicate authenticity and local

2016). In community-based contexts, such design translation is essential for enabling local products to compete

3.3 Art toys in tourism and creative economy
The integration of character design and creative products into tourism reflects broader shifts toward
experience-based consumption. The experience economy framework emphasizes the role of symbolic and
emotional engagement in shaping consumer behavior and t ourism experiences (Pine & Gilmore, 1999). Art
toys, as collectible and narrative -driven objects, can enhance tourism experiences by providing tangible
symbols of place identity.
Creative economy theory further explains how design and cultural production can generate economic
value by transforming cultural resources into mar ketable products (Howkins, 2001 ). In this context, art toys
can function as creative economy tools that support local entrepreneurship, product differentiation, and
tourism branding. Whe n grounded in local cultural identity, such products can strengthen community -based
economies and foster cultural sustainability.
Character-based branding has increasingly been recognized as an effective strategy for enhancing
destination visibility and cu ltural communication. Wu and Yang (2025) argued that mascot and character
design function as symbolic ambassadors that communicate local values, strengthen brand recognition, and
foster emotional engagement among audiences. Such findings reinforce the potential of art toys to operate not
only as souvenir products but also as strategic branding tools capable of supporting tourism promotion and
place differentiation.
Participatory cultural design has also emerged as an important approach for developing culturally
meaningful creative products. Gao (2024) emphasized that participatory design processes encourage
collaboration between designers and stakeholders, resulting in products that better reflect local values,
cultural relevance, and user experiences. This perspective supports the integration of community
participation into art toy  development, ensuring that character designs remain culturally grounded and
socially s ustainable.
However, the development of art toys within community contexts requires careful consideration of
authenticity, participation, and intellectual property rights. Participatory design approaches emphasize
collaboration between designers and commun ities to ensure that cultural representations remain
meaningful and locally grounded (Manzini, 2015; Sanoff, 2000). Such approaches are particularly important
when translating local identity into character -based forms that will be used for tourism and comm ercial
purposes.
3.4 Research gap
Although art toys have gained global popularity as cultural and commercial artifacts, research on their
role in community -based tourism and local product development remains limited. Existing studies have
examined cultural  product development, packaging design, and creative economy strategies in community
contexts (Louhapensang, 2015; Pruettikomon, 2022) , yet few have focused specifically on art toys as design
tools for translating local identity into tourism and product branding systems.
Moreover, there is a lack of systematic design guidelines that integrate cultural capital, character
design, and participatory processes for art toy development within regional contexts. This gap highlights the
need for research that establ ishes structured approaches to designing art toy s rooted in local identity and
cultural narratives.
3.5 Conceptual direction of the study
This study builds upon theories of character -based design, cultural capital, and creative economy to
develop design gu idelines for art toy s that promote tourism and enhance community products. By
integrating participatory design processes with regional cultural analysis, the research seeks to establish a
framework for translating local identity into contemporary character  design applicable across different
regions of Thailand .

4. METHODOLOGY

This study employed a mixed -methods research design integrating qualitative cultural analysis,
participatory design processes, and quantitative evaluation. The methodology was structured to develop
design guidelines for art toys that reflect regional cultural identity while supporting community-based tourism
and product development in Thailand.
4.1 Research design
The research adopted a multi-regional case study approach to examine how local cultural identity can
be translated into art toy  design for touris m and community product enhancement. The study combined
qualitative methods —including field observation, in -depth interviews, and participatory workshops —with
quantitative survey methods to evaluate audience perception and design effectiveness.
This approa ch aligns with participatory design frameworks that emphasize collaboration between
designers and communities in translating cultural identity into visual forms (Manzini, 2015; Sanoff, 2000). It
also reflects creative economy perspectives that position des ign as a mediator between cultural capital and
economic value (Howkins, 2001).
4.2 Study areas
The research was conducted across three regions of Thailand to capture diverse cultural contexts:
          Central region: Nakhon Pathom, Ratchaburi, Bangkok
Northeastern region: Surin, Khon Kaen
Southern region: Trang, Narathiwat, and Prachuap Khiri Khan. Ko Sukon in Trang Province represents
a community context characterized by local livelihoo ds and occupational development  (Inyarat & Ponpichai,
2023).
Each region was selected based on the presence of strong cultural identity, distinctive local products,
and tourism potential.

4.3 Participants
The study involved a total of 510 participants, selected through purposive sampling. Participants
included:
● Community artisans and product producers = 15 (2.94%)
● Local entrepreneurs = 15 (2.94%)
● Designers = 15 (2.94%)
● Tourists = 450 (88.24%)
● Cultural experts = 15 (2.94%)
The sample size was determined using Krejcie and Morgan’s (1970) sampling table to ensure adequate
representation.
4.4 Data collection
Data were collected through four main methods:
1. Field observation of community products, cultural symbols, and tourism environments.
2. In-depth interviews with community leaders, designers, and cultural experts .
3. Participatory design workshops for developing art toy prototypes.
4. Questionnaire surveys assessing tourist perception, emotional engagement, and purchase intention.
The participatory workshops enabled communities to identify symbolic elements —such as animals,
materials, and local narratives—that informed character design and packaging concepts.
The research instruments, including the questionnaire and interview guidelines, were developed
based on the research objectives and a review of relevant literature. To ensure content validity, the instruments
were evaluated by three experts using the Index of Item-Objective Congruence (IOC) method. All items met the
acceptable criterion (IOC ≥ 0.50) and were subsequently revised according to the experts’ recom mendations
before data collection, ensuring the clarity, relevance, and appropriateness of the research instruments.
4.5 Design development process
The design process followed a co-design framework consisting of:
● Cultural identity exploration
● Character concept development
● Prototype creation
● Evaluation and refinement
Art toy  characters were developed based on regional symbolic elements, including animals,
agricultural products, and local materials. Prototypes were then applied to community product packagi ng and
souvenir design.
4.6 Data analysis
Qualitative data were analyzed using thematic analysis to identify recurring cultural themes, local
narratives, symbolic elements, and design characteristics that informed the development of art toy characters
and packaging concepts. The analysis involved coding, categorization, and interpretation of qualitative data
obtained from field observations, in-depth interviews, and participatory design workshops.
Quantitative data collected through questionnaire surveys we re analyzed using descriptive statistics,
including frequency, percentage, mean, and standard deviation. These statistical techniques were employed to
examine participants’ perceptions of cultural representation, design quality, emotional engagement,
destination recognition, product value, and purchase intention associated with the developed art toy
characters and packaging designs.
In addition, expert evaluation was conducted to assess the design outcomes in terms of cultural
authenticity, aesthetic qualit y, design appropriateness, communication effectiveness, and market potential.
The evaluation results were used to refine the design concepts and validate the applicability of the proposed
framework for community -based tourism promotion and product branding . The resulting art toy prototypes
and packaging applications were subsequently assessed for their effectiveness in communicating local cultural
identity, enhancing community branding, and supporting creative economy development.

The findings revealed that art toys function effectively as cultural translation tools that transform local
identity into contemporary visual forms. Across the three regions studied, community participants consistently
identified symbolic elements —such as animals, materials, and local narratives —as key sources of identity.

5.2 Impact on tourism perception
Quantitative results indicated that tourists responded positively to the art toys developed from local
cultural identities. Survey results from the 450 tourists showed high levels of satisfaction and recognition,
particularly in terms of emotional appeal and memorability.
Participants reported that character -based design increased their interest in local products and
enhanced their perception of the destination. Art toys were perceived as effective souvenirs that communicated
cultural meaning while providin g aesthetic and emotional value. These findings support the idea that
character-based design can strengthen destination identity and improve visitor engagement.
Furthermore, the presence of art toy  characters within product packaging and tourism contexts
contributed to stronger place recognition. Respondents indicated that such visual systems made community
products more distinctive and easier to remember, reinforcing the role of design in tourism branding.
5.3 Enhancement of community product value
Quantitative findings from the evaluation of the developed cultural mascot and packaging designs
revealed a positive overall perception among participants, with a mean score of 3.78 (SD = 0.64), indicating a
high level of satisfaction. The results suggest that the integration of local cultural identity into character-based
design effectively enhanced participants’ recognition of community products and strengthened destination
identity.
Participants agreed that the developed mascot and packaging designs successfully reflected the
cultural identity of the community (M = 3.82, SD = 0.36) and communicated local stories and distinctive cultural
characteristics (M = 3.62, SD = 0.52). Furthermo re, respondents indicated that the visual identity system
improved product recognition and facilitated communication with consumers (M = 4.23, SD = 0.42). The
designs were also considered well suited to  online marketing and tourism promotion activities (M = 4.21,
SD = 0.32).

5.5 Design guideline development
Based on the findings, the study proposes a set of design guidelines for art toys intended to support
tourism and community product development:
1. Cultural identity should serve as the primary design foundation.
2. Character design should balance authenticity and contemporary aesthetics.
3. Participatory processes should be integrated into design development.
4. Visual systems should extend beyond characters to packaging and branding.
5. Intellectual property considerations should be incorporated early in the design process.
These guidelines provide a structured framework for translating cultural identity into character-based
design applicable across different regions.

6. DISCUSSION

The findings of this study indicate that art toy s can be conceptualized as cultural translation systems
through which local cultural resources are transformed into contemporary visual representations for
community branding and  tourism development. The design process involved the interpretation of cultural
symbols, local narratives, traditional occupations, and regional identities into character-based forms that could
be communicated to broader audiences. This process enabled in tangible cultural resources to be materialized
as tangible design artifacts while maintaining their symbolic associations with place and community identity.
From a semio tic perspective, the developed art toy  characters function as a system of signs through
which cultural meanings are encoded and communicated. The characters incorporate symbolic references to
local resources, agricultural products, community practices, and regional narratives, allowing cultural
meanings to be represented through visual forms . Consequently, the art toy s extend beyond their material
function as products and operate as cultural communication media through which local identity can be
interpreted and recognized by consumers and tourists.
The findings further suggest that character  design performs a mediating role between local
communities and external audiences. Through the transformation of complex cultural meanings into accessible
visual representations, the characters facilitate communication between community knowledge and
contemporary consumer culture. In this regard, character -based design may be understood as a form of
cultural mediation that supports the interpretation, dissemination, and preservation of local identity within
tourism and branding contexts.
These findings are  consistent with creative economy perspectives, which emphasize the capacity of
symbolic products and cultural narratives to generate economic and social value (Howkins, 2001). The positive
evaluations reported by participants indicate that character -based visual identity systems contribute to
product recognition, perceived value, and marketing communication. The integration of cultural storytelling
within character and packaging design therefore provides a mechanism through which local cultural resources
may be transformed into market-oriented creative products.
The participatory design process was a key component  of the research framework. Consistent with
participatory design theory (Manzini, 2015; Sanoff, 2000), community members were involved in the
identification of cultural symbols, the interpretation of local narratives, and the development of design
concepts. Such involvement contributed to the cultural relevance of the resulting designs and strengthened
community engagement throughout the develop ment process. These findings support recent studies that
emphasize the importance of participatory cultural design in facilitating culturally grounded innovation and
community-based creative development (Gao, 2024).
The theoretical contribution of this study lies in the integration of cultural capital, participatory design,
cultural storytelling, and character -based branding within a unified framework for art toy  development.
Previous studies have examined these dimensions independently; however, limited at tention has been given
to the role of art toys as mechanisms for translating cultural identity into branding and tourism applications.
The proposed framework contributes to design research by demonstrating how character -based design can
facilitate the tran sformation of cultural resources into visual identity systems that support community
branding, tourism promotion, and creative economy development.
Nevertheless, several limitations should be acknowledged. The study was conducted within selected
community contexts in Thailand, and the findings may therefore reflect the characteristics of those specific
cultural environments. In addition, issues relating to intellectual property management, ownership, and long-term

7. CONCLUSION

This study examined the development of art toy  design guidelines for tourism promotion and
community product development through the translation of local cultural identity into contemporary visual
forms. Drawing upon participatory design processes and mult i-regional case studies in Thailand, the findings
indicate that art toy s can function as cultural translation systems through which cultural resources, local
narratives, and community identities are transformed into character-based visual representations for branding
and tourism applications.
The findings suggest that art toy s extend beyond their conventional role as collectible or
entertainment-oriented products. Rather, they may be understood as design artifacts through which cultural
meanings are represented, communicated, and interpreted. By incorporating local symbols, cultural narratives,
and visual storytelling strategies, art toy s facilitate the transformation of intangible cultural resources into
tangible forms that can be recognized and engaged wit h by contemporary audiences. In this regard, art toys
operate as mechanisms for translating cultural identity into accessible and market -oriented design outcomes.
From a theoretical perspective, the study contributes to the literature by integrating cultur al capital
theory, participatory design, cultural storytelling, and character -based branding within a unified conceptual
framework. The findings indicate that character design performs a mediating function between local
communities and external audiences b y transforming culturally embedded meanings into visual
representations that support communication, interpretation, and engagement. This perspective extends
existing discussions of character -based design beyond aesthetic and commercial considerations by
emphasizing its role in cultural mediation and identity representation.
A further contribution of the study lies in the development of a Cultural Translation Framework
consisting of five interrelated stages: (1) identification of cultural capital, (2) participatory cultural interpretation,
(3) character-based translation, (4) visual identity and packaging development, and (5) tourism and community
branding application. The framework provides a structured explanation of how cultural resources may be
transformed into branding and tourism assets through design processes while maintaining cultural relevance
and community participation.
The findings also contribute to creative economy scholarship by illustrating how cultural value can be
converted into social and economic value through design. Rather than relying solely on the production of
physical goods, the proposed framework emphasizes the role of sy mbolic meaning, cultural narratives, and
identity-based branding in generating value for community products and tourism destinations. Consequently,
the study provides an applied model through which local cultural resources may be mobilized as creative
economy assets while supporting cultural sustainability and community development.
From a practical perspective, the proposed framework offers guidance for designers, community
organizations, and tourism stakeholders seeking to develop culturally grounded bran ding systems. The
integration of participatory processes throughout the design process further highlights the importance of
community engagement in ensuring cultural authenticity, local ownership, and long -term sustainability.
Nevertheless, several limitat ions should be acknowledged. The study was conducted within selected
community contexts in Thailand and may therefore reflect the characteristics of those particular cultural
environments. In addition, issues relating to intellectual property management, g overnance structures, and
benefit-sharing mechanisms require further investigation. Future research may examine the application of the
proposed framework in different cultural contexts and explore models for the sustainable management of
community-owned cultural assets.
In conclusion, the study demonstrates that art toy s may serve as cultural translation systems that
connect cultural identity, community branding, tourism promotion, and creative economy development. By
integrating participatory design, cultu ral storytelling, and character -based branding within a coherent
framework, the research contributes to a broader understanding of how local cultural resources can be
transformed into sustainable cultural and economic value through contemporary design prac tice.
