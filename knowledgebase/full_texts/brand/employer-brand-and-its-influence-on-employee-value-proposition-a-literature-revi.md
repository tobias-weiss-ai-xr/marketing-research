1. Introduction
In today's highly competitive and dynamic business environment, organizations are increasingly recognizing that
their ability to attract, engage, and retain talented employees is a critical determinant of long-term success. The growing
emphasis on talent management has led organizations to focus not only on building strong corporate brands for customers
but also on developing a compelling employer brand for current and prospective employees. Employer branding has
emerged as a strategic human resource management practice that helps organizations differentiate themselves in the
labor market and position themselves as employers of choice.
The concept of employer branding was first introduced to describe the package of functional, economic, and
psychological benefits associated with employment and identified with the employing organization. It reflects the image
and reputation of an organization as a workplace and communicates what employees can expect in terms of career
growth, organizational culture, work environment, rewards, and overall employee experience. A strong employer brand
creates positive perceptions among potential candidates an d existing employees, thereby enhancing recruitment
effectiveness, employee commitment, and organizational performance.

Closely linked to employer branding is the concept of Employee Value Proposition (EVP), which represents the
unique set of benefits, opportunities, and experiences that an organization offers in exchange for the skills, capabilities,
and contributions of its employees. EVP serves as the foundation of employer branding by articulating the value
employees receive from working with an organization. It encompasses both tangible rewards, such as compensation,
benefits, and career advancement opportunities, and intangible aspects, including organizational culture, leadership
quality, work-life balance, recognition, and purpose-driven work. An effective EVP helps organizations attract high-
quality talent, improve employee engagement, and strengthen retention efforts.
The relationship between employer branding and EVP is highly interdependent. While EVP defines the promises
and benefits offered to employees, employer branding communicates these promises to internal and external
stakeholders. A well-designed EVP provides credibility and substance to the employer brand, while effective employer
branding enhances the visibility and attractiveness of the EVP. Together, they create a coherent and consistent employee
experience that influences employee perceptions, attitudes, and behaviors throughout the employment lifecycle.
The rapid growth of digital communication platforms and social media has further increased the importance of
employer branding and EVP. Organizations now have multiple channels through which they can showcase their
workplace culture, employee achievements, diversity initiatives, and career opportunities. However, the effectiveness of
employer branding depends on the authenticity of the EVP being communicated. Any disconnect between organizational
promises and actual employee experiences can result in dissatisfaction, reduced trust, negative employer reputation, and
increased turnover.
As employee expectations continue to evolve, organizations must move beyond traditional recruitment strategies
and adopt a more holistic approach to talent management. A strong employer brand supported by a compelling EVP can
provide a sustainable competitive advantage by fostering employee engagement, loyalty, and organizational
commitment. Therefore, understanding the influence of employer branding on Employee Value Proposition is essential
for organizations seeking to build a motivated workforce and achieve sustained success in the contemporary business
landscape.
2. Methodology
This research deploys a secondary literature-based research design drawing contents from published academic
sources. It relies on existing published literature rather than primary data collection. To ensure credibility, only scholarly
sources were included, excluding non-academic blogs, opinion pieces, or unpublished manuscripts. Relevant paper and
content were identified from databases such as Google Scholar, HR journals, books, published reports from the Internet,
press reports from various press and newspaper articles etc. Keywords used in the search was Employee Value
Proposition, Employee Brand, Engagement, Retention, Recruitment. The words were used in combination or as one. As
this study relies solely on published literature proper attribution and citation practices were strictly followed to maintain
academic integrity. Some points to note are that there may be some limitations attached like restriction to English -
language publications may exclude relevant studies in other languages and reliance on secondary data means findings
are constrained by the scope and quality of existing studies.
3. Employer Branding
With the increasing intensity of the war for talent, employer branding has become an important consideration for
organizations. The term “employer brand” was first coined by Barrow   and Ambler (1996) and who defined employer
brand as "the package of functional, economic and psychological benefits provided by employment, and identified with
the employing company.”  Interestingly, this was also the first published attempt to test the application of brand
management techniques to human resource management. Human capital increases the value of any firm; hence,
employer branding is important to enhance the performance of the firm through skillful and careful investment in human
capital (Backhaus & Tikoo, 2004).

Internal Branding
Organizations internally brand itself to achieve the employer of choice status. Effective employer branding requires
a clear understanding and articulation of the unique Employee Value Proposition (EVP) of the organization. Walker
(1992) defined EVP as “a set of attributes and qualities – often intangible – that make an organization distinctive, promise
a particular kind of employment experience and appeal to people who will thrive and perform their best in its culture.”
EVP comprises a comprehensive package of tangibles and intangibles that employees receive in return for their
contributions to the organization. Tangibles include salary & benefits, health plans, subsidized meals, recognition
schemes etc. Intangibles include exciting and meaningful work, learning and development, inclusive and value-based
culture, meritocracy, career progression, work-life balance initiatives etc. Nevertheless, employer branding is not just
about projecting a company as an employer of choice but a tool has been adopted as a tool to motivate and engage
employees (Barrow and Mosley, 2005).
Further, Richard Mosley an employer branding guru, emphasizes on ensuring alignment between the  external
employer brand and the actual delivery of the EVP to the employees (Krishnan, 2010). If there is a mismatch between
these two, employees can feel significantly demotivated leading to attrition, which would eventually lead to erosion of
employer brand equity. Thus, once the EVP is articulated, the organization can create a compelling internal and external
communication plan around it to ensure that a strong employer brand can be created.  Further, EVP is strongly regarded
by the employees as it refers to their work and their organization (Patra 2012). Figure 2 illustrates the detailed EVP

4. Employer Branding Outcomes
Every organization strives to develop a brand one for the public, investors, customers, and suppliers, termed as
external branding and the other for its employees, internal branding. Employer branding initiative when planned and
executed with diligence would generate rapport between an employer and its employees, build mutual beneficial working
arrangement and establish commitment mutually (Patra, 2012).  Further, employees who are satisfied in their work
would stay with the organization and develop loyal customers thus work engagement is more importantly related to
organizational commitment (Levinson, 2007). Quantitative research from the Corporate Leadership Council (2006)
shows that organizations which manage their EVP well show higher levels of new hire commitment. This commitment
continues even after a year’s tenure in the organization.
Engagement with employee affects their performance (Kahn, 1990). Lockwood (2007) suggests that “Engaged
employees work harder, are more loyal and are more likely to go the ‘extra mile’ for the corporation.” Similarly,
engagement considered as an elusive force by Macey and Schneider (2008) motivates the employees to achieve increased
performance. Employees who are engaged advocate the cause of the organization, its services and products and brand it
as a place to work (Scottish Executive Social Research, 2007). On the contrary those who disengaged can discourage
other performing employees and those who intend to join the organization (Penna, 2006). Though there is no strong
evidence for the link between organization performance and attitude, a number of studies show that the reverse is true
thus linking the organization performance and attitude (Balian and Sparrow, 2009). Harter, et al. (2002) proposed that
increasing employee engagement and environment to support the cause can result in the success of the business.
Employer branding has other benefits for the organization besides those points discussed above. These benefits are
(Corporate Leadership Council, 2006):
a. Increased organizational ability to attract passive candidate segments, providing a greater choice in
candidate selection to employers. Passive candidates refer to those who are currently employed and not
actively seeking another job.
b. Reduced costs of vacancy due to faster recruiting cycle times
c. Reduced sourcing costs (due to less reliance on recruitment agencies and search firms)

5. EVP Development Stages
Now that a business case has been established for having a clearly articulated and well managed EVP, the different
steps involved in managing and enhancing EVP are analyzed.
The four main stages of EVP management as identified by Davenport (2013) are:
Tactical Stage – The organizations at this stage do not have a formally defined EVP, even though an organizational
culture and a compensation plan are there, since these are essential for any organization to remain in existence. However,
at this stage no attempts have been made to formally understand and articulate the EVP. Employees have to understand
and interpret the EVP for themselves.
Integrated Stage – These organizations have formally articulated EVPs and well-established linkages between their
talent management and rewards strategies. They have clear connections between competencies, selection processes,
performance management, leadership development and career progression.
Communicating & Delivering Stage – After articulating the EVP and establishing strong linkages between
different talent management programs and processes, organizations need to communicate the EVP to employees and
deliver on the promise of the EVP. These organizations usually have multiple and high-quality communication platforms
to communicate their EVP to their employees and the external world.
Segmenting & Differentiating Stage – This is the highest stage in the evolutionary spiral of the EVP. Organizations
that reach this stage are able to clearly differentiate the uniqueness of their EVP from their competitors in the talent
market. They also invest considerable effort in articulating and customizing the EVP for their key talent like High
Potentials and High Performers. These organizations are twice as likely to report a significantly higher financial
performance than their competitors.

7. Implications of EVP Management For HR Practitioners
Employee Value Proposition articulation and management is a nascent and growing discipline in Human Resources
Management. Given its importance in attracting and retaining high quality talent in a fiercely competitive talent market,
it is imperative that HR practitioners make it an integral part of the HR function. A compelling employee value
proposition can drive higher engagement by ensuring employees feel valued and recognized for their contribution. When
employees feel that their needs are being met, the attrition is bound to reduce thus saving an organization of the high
cost of replacing talent. HR practitioners need to prioritize a compelling Employee Value Proposition that goes in line
with organizational goals. This will not only enhance employee experience but also enhance business outcome such as
increased engagement and reduced employee turnover.
