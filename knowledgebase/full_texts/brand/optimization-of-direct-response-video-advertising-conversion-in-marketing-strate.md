Abstract. The relevance of the study is determined by the growing role of
video advertising in the digital environment, the intensification of competition in
performance-oriented marketing, and the increasing requirements for the validity of
managerial decision-making. The increasing complexity of audiovisual content
structure and the dependence of its effectiveness on algorithmic content distribution
mechanisms necessitate the development of scientifically grounded approaches to
optimizing the conversion of direct response video advertising in the marketing
strategies of direct-to-consumer brands. Purpose. The purpose of the study is to
enhance the effectiveness of conversion of direct response video advertising in the
marketing strategies of direct-to-consumer brands through the development and
application of scientifically grounded approaches based on mul timodal content
analysis and data-driven decision-making models. Methods. The study applies
methods of analysis and synthesis to generalize theoretical approaches to conversion
formation and optimization; systematization to identify key parameters of video
content; comparative analysis to assess the influence of various content

Problem statement. The rapid expansion of digital ecosystems and the
intensification of competition in performance marketing have fundamentally
transformed the role of video advertising within direct-to-consumer (DTC) brand
strategies – shifting it from a primarily awareness-oriented tool to a key driver of
measurable conversion outcomes. Under conditions of rising customer acquisition
costs, algorithmic mediation of content distribution, and fragmentation of audience
attention, ensuring stable conversion efficiency of direct response video advertising
becomes a critical scientific and practical challenge. Existing approaches to
campaign optimization remain largely focused on isolated metrics or platform -

interaction between creative structure, multimodal content characteristics, and user
behavioral responses. This creates a methodological gap in understanding how
semantic, visual, and temporal parameters of video content jointly influence
conversion mechanisms in dynamic digital environments.
At the same time, the increasing integration of data analytics, artificial
intelligence, and automated testing systems opens new opportunities for
constructing adaptive optimization models capable of operating in real time – yet
raises unresolved issues related to interpretability, data reliability, and the validity
of evaluation metrics. The lack of unified methodological frameworks that connect

constrains both theoretical generalization and the practical implementation of
effective marketing strategies for DTC brands. Consequently, addressing the
problem of optimizing conversion in direct response video advertising is directly
linked to the development of scientifically grounded approaches to multimodal
content analysis, the formalization of causal relationships between video attributes
and consumer behavior, and the design of robust decision -support systems that
ensure sustainable marketing performance under conditions of continuous digital
transformation.

Identification of unresolved parts of the problem . Despite the active
development of video marketing and analytical tools, the problem of conversion
formation in direct response video advertising remains conceptually and
methodologically insufficiently elaborated. Existing studies are characterized by
fragmentation and focus on separate aspects of content or performance indicators,
which limits a holistic understanding of how content structure interacts with user
behavior and platform distribution logic. At the same time, the integration of
semantic, visual, and temporal parameters into unified analytical models remains

In practical DTC marketing, the effectiveness of these determinants is realized
through their coordinated configuration within specific platform environments. For
instance, in short-form video ecosystems, initial visual framing and semantic clarity
within the first seconds directly affect scroll-stopping behavior, which subsequently
influences algorithmic promotion and cost efficiency of traffic acquisition.
Campaigns promoting consumer products often demonstrate that a clearly
articulated value proposition combined with a visually distinctive opening frame
increases click-through rates, while consistent temporal structuring improves watch
time, which is a key signal for content scaling [12, p. 58].
At the same time, behavioral determinants are operationalized through
embedded interaction triggers such as explicit calls to action or implicit cues
integrated into narrative progression. For example, videos that synchronize the
moment of peak emotional engagement with the presentation of an actionable offer
tend to generate higher conversion rates compared to content where these elements
are temporally disconnected. Contextual adaptation also plays a decisive role, as
identical creative concepts may yield different outcomes depending on platform-
specific ranking models and audience segmentation parameters [10, p. 2820]. In
practice, this is evident when content optimized for one platform underperforms on
another due to differences in preferred duration, visual density, or interaction
formats.

process in which each determinant is continuously refined based on performance

In applied DTC marketing, the analyzed parameters function as controllable
variables that shape the sequence and intensity of user responses within a constrained
attention window. Semantic configuration determines whether the user recognizes
personal relevance at the moment of first exposure. For example, creatives that
explicitly articulate a concrete benefit such as time saving or cost reduction in the
opening seconds tend to reduce hesitation and increase the share of intentional
clicks, particularly in high-frequency advertising environments. This effect becomes
more pronounced when the message aligns with an already formed user need,
thereby shortening the path from perception to action [14, p. 635].
Visual parameters operate at the level of perceptual prioritization and
emotional activation. In practice, campaigns for consumer products show that the
introduction of a dominant visual anchor, such as a clearly identifiable usage
scenario or a high-contrast focal element, stabilizes attention and improves recall of
the offer during subsequent decision-making. At the same time, empirical testing
reveals diminishing returns when visual density exceeds processing capacity,
leading to fragmented perception and reduced clarity of the core message [1]. Thus,
effectiveness depends not on the maximization of visual stimuli but on their
functional alignment with semantic content.
Temporal structuring defines the logic of engagement progression and the
synchronization of informational and motivational stimuli. In short-form formats,
positioning key arguments within the first segment of the video reduces early
audience loss, while a structured escalation of value demonstration followed by a
clearly timed call to action increases the likelihood of completed conversion.
Practical evidence indicates that videos in which the moment of peak emotional
engagement coincides with the presentation of the offer generate more stable
conversion responses compared to content where these elements are temporally
misaligned [13, p. 168].

These approaches operate as an integrated analytical and experimental system
that connects content diagnostics with real-time optimization decisions. Multimodal
feature extraction enables the transformation of video assets into structured data
representations, allowing marketers to systematically compare creative elements
across campaigns. For example, identifying recurring combinations of concise
textual overlays and product-focused visual sequences that consistently correlate
with higher engagement provides a basis for replicable optimization strategies.
Cross-modal correlation analysis further enhances this process by revealing
interdependencies between content dimensions and user behavior. In practical DTC
applications, it is often observed that the effectiveness of visual intensity is
contingent upon semantic clarity; high-impact visuals generate stronger engagement
only when the accompanying message is immediately interpretable  [12, p. 61]. This
allows for the identification of causal configurations rather than isolated
performance effects, supporting more precise targeting of optimization efforts.
Automated experimentation functions as the validation mechanism within this
system. Through continuous A/B and multivariate testing, marketers can evaluate

also contributes to a cumulative understanding of effective content configurations,
thereby supporting sustainable scaling of DTC video advertising strategies.
The identification of key scientific and practical problems of conversion
optimization in direct response video advertising reflects the increasing complexity
of data-driven marketing systems, where analytical sophistication often exceeds the
capacity for reliable interpretation. A fundamental challenge lies in the limited
interpretability of advanced analytical models, particularly machine learning
approaches, which provide high predictive accuracy but insufficient transparency
for establishing causal relationships between content features and conversion
outcomes. This constrains both theoretical explanation and the practical justification
of optimization decisions.

confidence in analytical conclusions.
Methodological weaknesses in experimental design amplify these problems.

short-term performance effects reduce the validity and reproducibility of A/B testing
outcomes [9]. At the same time, continuous changes in platform algorithms
introduce temporal instability, meaning that identical content configurations may
yield divergent results under different distribution conditions.

differences in scale, structure, and timing complicate unified analysis, and from
organizational constraints, including limited analytical capacity and weak
coordination between creative and data functions. Collectively, these issues indicate
that conversion optimization remains methodologically underdeveloped, requiring
more robust frameworks that ensure interpretability, data integrity, and consistency
of evaluation in dynamic digital environments.
