Abstract: Against global carbon neutrality and China’s Dual Carbon targets, the FMCG sector faces plastic
pollution and organic waste crises. Taking Starbucks’ coffee ground cup as a case, this paper builds a closed-
loop system following the 3Rs  of circular economy. It sorts out the full life cycle covering waste collection, low-
carbon intelligent manufacturing, scenario-based green communication and post-consumer remanufacturing,
and analyzes its value in elevating ESG performance, shaping differentiated brand competitiveness and building
low-carbon supply chains. Three bottlenecks are identified: defective coffee composite materials, insufficient
public sustainable aesthetic awareness and high nationwide recycling costs. Target countermeasures on material
upgrading, hierarchical recycling layout and diversified recycled products are proposed. This study extends
circular design research and delivers replicable green transformation references for China’s coffee and new tea
chains, enriching interdisciplinary studies on circular design and brand marketing.
Keywords: Circular Material Innovation; Sustainable Brand Design; Green Product Aesthetics; Brand
Differentiated Competition; Circular Design
1 Research Background and Significance
1.1 Research Background
Decades of extensive industrialization have
precipitated the overexploitation of global
natural resources. The traditional linear “extract-
produce-dispose” model generates massive
solid waste annually. Non-biodegradable single-
use plastics persist in terrestrial and aquatic
ecosystems for centuries. damaging ecological
balance and threatening public health. To
mitigate environmental pressure, governments
worldwide have issued policies on resource
conservation, plastic pollution control and carbon
constraints, integrating circular development into
regional economic planning. Aligned with global
sustainable agendas, China has incorporated the
Dual Carbon goals into its medium-and-long-
term national strategies and released supportive
policies for waste valorization, forcing catering
enterprises to shift from profit-only operation to a
balanced model coordinating commercial returns,
design value and ecological responsibility.
Policy shifts have reshaped Chinese
consumers’ value perceptions. Previously,
purchasing decisions centered on price and
basic functionality; today, consumers prioritize
corporate environmental governance, sustainable
materials and ecological aesthetics. Merchandise
combining practicality, circular material design
and unique sustainable aesthetic symbols gains
stronger market favor. Product texture, material
visual features and brand symbolic connotations
have become core drivers of consumer loyalty.
Brand aesthetic design is no longer superficial
decoration but a core strategic medium for
enterprises to communicate environmental,
social and governance (ESG) concepts and form

Over the past decade, China’s coffee and
new tea chains have expanded rapidly, producing
large quantities of waste coffee grounds
and tea residues alongside heavy disposable
packaging consumption. Conventional landfill
and incineration of coffee grounds cause soil
contamination, groundwater seepage and excess
carbon emissions. Meanwhile, updated plastic
restriction policies impose strict limits on
single-use plastic tableware, making systematic
sustainable design an inevitable strategic choice
for all catering brands.
Most Chinese catering and tea brands
merely replace plastic packaging without
systematic circular design schemes to exploit
commercial and aesthetic value from self-
produced organic byproducts. Homogenized
environmental initiatives fail to build
differentiated brand assets through product
design. Against this backdrop, Starbucks stands
out by developing circular composite materials
and iterating product designs based on stable
waste coffee grounds from its thousands of
offline stores, thereby launching a full line of
reusable daily goods with distinctive material
aesthetics. Organic residues previously disposed
by outsourced contractors are converted into
marketable commodities. Supported by its
mature global supply chain and long-term
sustainable brand strategy, this circular design
project achieves full commercialization. By
contrast, China’s coffee market keeps expanding,
yet supporting circular resource systems remain
underdeveloped, leaving organic waste disposal
a universal industry-wide challenge lacking
design-driven solutions.
1.2 Research Significance
1.2.1 Theoretical Significance
Existing Chinese circular economy research
mainly focuses on macro industrial parks,
agricultural clusters and cross-regional resource
coordination, while micro studies targeting fast-
moving consumer goods (FMCG) chains and
single circular design products remain scarce.
Most literature separates circular economy
theory and brand aesthetic design, with few
interdisciplinary case studies taking recycled
merchandise as carriers to link circular material
innovation and sustainable brand construction,
forming an obvious research gap at the
intersection of design and management. Taking
Starbucks’ coffee ground cup as an empirical
case, this paper sorts out the full-life-cycle design
and operation logic spanning waste collection
to post-consumer remanufacturing. With a
three-tier product aesthetic value framework,
it explains how circular material innovation
endows brands with unique cultural symbolic
assets. This research broadens the application
boundary of circular design theory in consumer
brand construction, establishes theoretical
links between circular material innovation
and differentiated brand design, and provides
innovative analytical frameworks for subsequent
interdisciplinary studies covering sustainable
design, brand marketing and environmental,
social and governance (ESG) management.

competitive paths supported by exclusive
design resources. Restricted by Dual Carbon
and plastic restriction policies, catering chains
bear heavy environmental governance costs
but lack operable design-led green roadmaps to
convert environmental investment into market
competitiveness. This paper dissects the closed-
loop design and operation system of Starbucks’

2 Closed-Loop Circular Operation Design
System of Starbucks’ Coffee Ground Cup
The core difference between circular economy
and the traditional linear model lies in
constructing an uninterrupted closed-loop chain
of “resource utilization – waste recovery –
secondary remanufacturing”. Circular design
thinking provides standardized implementation
procedures for resource circulation, guiding
enterprises to control waste output in early
product design and preset feasible recycling
layouts for end-of-life products
[2]
. Centered on
circular material innovation, the coffee ground
cup project includes four integrated design and
operation modules: standardized collection
system design for waste raw materials, low-
carbon intelligent production technology design,
integrated online-offline scenario green brand
communication design, and full-life-cycle
recycling remanufacturing design for post-
consumer products. The natural tactile aesthetics
of coffee fiber are embedded into raw material
formulation and product appearance design,
breaking empty green marketing discourse
and realizing organic integration of ecological
performance and unique brand humanistic
connotation.
2.1 Upstream Raw Material End:
Standardized Waste Coffee Ground Recycling
and Refined Material Modification Design
Stable large-scale commercialization of the coffee
ground cup closed-loop system fundamentally

coffee residues from domestic retail outlets were
categorized as kitchen waste and handed over

and odor caused by residual moisture. The
brand signed long-term cooperation agreements
with regional professional recycling logistics
enterprises, dividing service zones for regular
door-to-door collection to realize standardized
management of waste sorting, short-distance
transfer and centralized storage.
Collected coffee grounds are transported
to cooperative composite material factories
and undergo refined processing including deep
dehydration, high-temperature sterilization,
impurity screening, ultrafine pulverization,
fiber chemical modification and food-grade
substrate blending. Untreated raw coffee fiber
features loose molecular structure and strong
water absorption, leading to damp deformation
of finished products. Combined physical and
chemical modification significantly improves
fiber compactness and structural stability.
Modified coffee fiber is mixed with eco-plastic
substrates according to scientific formulation
ratios to produce exclusive circular composite
raw materials for cup injection molding. This
circular material design reduces consumption
of virgin petrochemical plastics and alleviates
ecological pressure of fossil resource
exploitation, practicing the 3Rs (Reduce, Reuse,
Recycle) of circular economy. The integrated
recovery and modification system solves the
universal organic waste disposal dilemma of
retail stores, cuts long-term outsourced waste
removal costs and supplies stable low-carbon
raw materials for mass production of circular
merchandise, forming the core foundation for
sustainable operation of the whole closed-loop
brand system.
2.2 Midstream Production End: Low-Carbon
Production Process Design to Eliminate Brand
Greenwashing Risks
Numerous eco-labeled consumer goods suffer
from greenwashing controversies: though
claimed natural and recyclable, their production
processes consume excessive energy and
discharge large volumes of industrial solid
waste, turning ecological value into hollow
marketing slogans failing market inspection.
To avoid similar doubts for coffee ground
cups, Starbucks issued mandatory low-carbon
production standards for all cooperative factories
and completed energy-saving technical upgrades
for exclusive production lines. Special injection
molding lines are equipped with high-efficiency
energy-saving devices and intelligent constant-
temperature control systems that dynamically
adjust heating parameters according to product
specifications. While guaranteeing complete
molding and unified dimensional standards, the
intelligent control system drastically cuts unit
consumption of electricity and thermal energy.
Factory technical teams redesigned processing
workflows to eliminate redundant heating and
cooling waiting links, shortening molding cycles,
lifting daily output and further lowering unit
energy costs.
Meanwhile, factories established an
internal full waste recycling mechanism.

2.3 Downstream Consumption & Circulation
End: Scenario-Based Green Brand
Communication Design to Consolidate Brand

Circular recycled design products are tangible
carriers for sustainable brand value transmission,
disseminating low-carbon lifestyle concepts via
multi-scenario online and offline communication
and guiding consumers to form new sustainable
aesthetic standards and green consumption
habits. Integrating offline store spatial design and
full-platform new media visual communication
design, Starbucks built an immersive two-way
value transmission system interpreting both ESG
connotations and natural material aesthetic value
of circular merchandise. Offline, coffee ground
cups are permanently displayed in merchandise
zones, matched with thematic posters, vertical
explanatory boards and in-store electronic
screens intuitively presenting the full processing
flow of coffee grounds, quantifiable annual waste
and carbon reduction data, and core highlights

in coffee ground cups to deepen consumers’
environmental and aesthetic cognition.
Supporting consumption incentive policies are
formulated: customers carrying self-owned
coffee ground cups for beverage purchases

merchandise and building emotional identity
with low-carbon lifestyles.
Online official platforms including WeChat,
Douyin and Xiaohongshu regularly release

material performance evaluations and annual
ESG reports. Professional circular economy
knowledge is explained in plain language to
build youth-focused environmental topics and
encourage spontaneous user-generated content
(UGC) sharing and secondary communication.
Different from competitors’ one-time discount
promotions and short-term recycling marketing
activities, coffee ground cups realize long-

merchandise instead of fading after campaign

ground cups transcends simple commodity
transactions, enabling consumers to participate
in practical low-carbon behaviors and perceive
natural sustainable material aesthetics shaped
by circular design. Long-term integrated
multi-scenario communication continuously
strengthens public recognition of Starbucks
as a low-carbon brand with unique design
temperament, steadily improving brand
favorability and repurchase willingness

Most eco-friendly products launched by brands
only cover design and sales stages without
dedicated recycling layouts for discarded goods,
breaking the whole resource circulation chain

usage and post-consumer remanufacturing. Green

4 Core Barriers Restricting Large-Scale
Commercialization of Circular Material
Brand Design
4.1 Inherent Material Design Defects
Coffee plant fiber features loose molecular
structure, strong water absorption and weak high-
temperature resistance. Even after multi-stage
physical and chemical modification to optimize
composite substrate performance, circular design
products still have obvious functional gaps in
sealing, heat insulation and surface smoothness
compared with traditional food-grade virgin
plastic products. Current coffee ground cups face
prominent user pain points derived from material
design limitations: poorly sealed lids, easily
scalded outer walls for hot drinks and liquid
leakage risks during long-distance transportation,
which are most prominent in daily commuting
and long-term liquid storage scenarios. Further
breakthroughs in material texture, sealing
structure and heat-resistant formula require
massive R&D investment, directly raising
unit manufacturing costs and terminal retail
prices higher than ordinary reusable water
cups, hindering market penetration via low-
cost popularization strategies. Most consumers
make one-time purchases out of curiosity
for innovative circular material design and
stop repurchasing after unsatisfactory usage
experience, limiting overall market penetration
scale and diffusion speed of coffee ground cup
merchandise.
4.2 Uneven Public Sustainable Aesthetic
Literacy
Although environmental awareness has
improved across China, most consumers
only hold fragmented superficial cognition of
circular economy and industrial waste upcycling
design. While theoretically recognizing low-
carbon concepts, they are unwilling to pay
premiums for circular design merchandise with
insufficient practical functionality. Consumers
still prioritize commodity price, core practical
functions and traditional appearance standards
during purchase decisions; environmental
friendliness and natural sustainable material
aesthetics only act as secondary supplementary
advantages rather than core purchasing drivers.
Most ordinary consumers lack cognition of
ecological pollution hazards caused by random
disposal of waste coffee grounds, fail to perceive
full carbon reduction value embedded in coffee
ground cup circular design and cannot appreciate
unique sustainable aesthetic connotations of
natural coffee fiber texture, evaluating circular
merchandise merely by unified judgment
standards of ordinary plastic water cups.
Brands need long-term heavy investment in
environmental science popularization and
sustainable aesthetic value communication
design, accompanied by prolonged green
consumer cultivation cycles with limited short-
term economic returns, obstructing large-scale
market popularization of circular material brand
design.
4.3 High Operation Costs of Nationwide
Recycling Layout
Starbucks retail stores cover China’s first-tier,
second-tier, third-tier, fourth-tier and county-
level markets, with huge gaps in daily customer
flow and divergent output volume of waste coffee
grounds and discarded coffee ground cups across
regions. Stores concentrated in central business
districts of first-and-second-tier cities generate
large daily waste coffee ground output, forming
scale effects for recycling layouts with low unit
logistics and labor costs. In contrast, county
and township stores operate on small daily
transaction volume with minimal waste output,
leading to inflated unit recycling costs caused by
long-distance round-trip logistics and exclusive
labor allocation. The nationwide recycling
layout maintains persistently high annual full-
chain operation expenditure. Meanwhile,

5.3 Hierarchical Zoned Optimization of
Recycling Network Layout
Aiming at unbalanced national store distribution
and differentiated regional recycling cost levels,
enterprises adopt differentiated tiered zoning
recycling layout and operation strategies. Dense
retail outlets in central business districts of first-

regional unified batch collection schemes are
implemented to eliminate resource waste caused
by fragmented short-distance transit and control
total annual recycling expenditure. Meanwhile,
consumer incentive activities including old cup
exchange for store merchandise and accumulable
public welfare recycling points are launched
to stimulate consumers’ voluntary initiative to
return discarded coffee ground cups, stabilizing
recycled raw material supply for processing
factories, mitigating production scheduling
fluctuations caused by supply volatility and
alleviating long-term operation pressure of the
whole closed-loop recycling design system.

6 Research Conclusions and Industrial
Reference Implications
6.1 Research Conclusions
Through comprehensive dissection and
systematic analysis of Starbucks’ full closed-
loop circular design and operation system, this
paper draws the core conclusion: developing
tangible circular recycled design merchandise
from internal production byproducts is an
effective strategic path for catering, coffee
and new tea FMCG brands to implement
sustainable development strategies and

6.2 Industrial Reference Implications
Domestic chain catering, coffee and new
tea brands can systematically learn from
Starbucks’ circular material design and product
development framework summarized in this
paper. Enterprises should fully exploit self-
generated organic production byproducts
including tea grounds, fruit residues and
waste coffee grounds to develop matching
tangible circular recycled design merchandise,
transforming abstract environmental slogans
into practical daily goods with unique
aesthetic design value accessible to consumers.
Enterprises construct complete closed-loop
design and operation systems covering raw
material recycling layout, refined material
processing design, terminal retail communication
design and post-consumer waste recycling
management, matched with consumer incentive
mechanisms such as beverage discounts and
membership point accumulation to guide public
participation in daily low-carbon consumption
behaviors. During circular recycled merchandise
development, brands avoid overemphasizing
environmental conceptual marketing at the cost
of basic product practical design, balancing
material research and development (R&D) input
costs and consumer usage experience through
continuous technical iteration and scaled mass
production management. Brand sustainable
development planning cannot be limited to
superficial environmental renovation such as
packaging and straw material replacement;
instead, enterprises build complete green
supply chain management systems centered on
circular material design merchandise to actively
respond to tightening plastic restriction and
Dual Carbon regulatory policies and construct
enduring differentiated competitive barriers
in saturated consumer markets. In China’s
current consumption landscape, sustainable
development is no longer merely a corporate
public relations marketing tool, but a core growth
driver directly convertible into product design
innovation capacity, differentiated brand market
competitiveness and long-term industrial layout
potential. FMCG chain brands can develop
distinctive circular design brand assets by
utilizing internal industrial byproducts, driving
comprehensive brand green transformation
through breakthrough single-product material
design innovation and ultimately achieving a
tripartite balance of corporate economic benefits,
ecological governance outcomes and humanistic
social design value.
