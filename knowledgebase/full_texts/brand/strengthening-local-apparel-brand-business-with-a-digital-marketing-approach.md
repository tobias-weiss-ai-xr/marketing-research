This study aims to examine the impact of Electronic Word of Mouth (E -
WOM), Social Media Marketing (SMM), Brand Engagement (BE), and
Brand Image (BI) in forming Purchase Intention (PI), by placing Brand
Equity (BEQ) as a mediator variable in the Generation Z consumer segment
who consume local Indonesian fashion products in the Batam City area. This
study adopted a causal-quantitative design in designing its study framework.
The data collection process involved 246 respondents, selected through
purposive sampling based on specific criteria. The data were then processed
and statistically analyzed using the PLS -SEM (Partial Least Squares
Structural Equation Modeling) method, implemented in SmartPLS. The
results of the analysis revealed that E -WOM, BE, and BI mad e significant
positive contributions to the formation of BEQ, while E -WOM, BE, and
BEQ demonstrated a real capacity to encourage the formation of PI. In
addition, BEQ was confirmed to play a significant role as a mediator in bridging
the relationship betwe en E-WOM, BE, and BI with PI. On the other hand, SMM
did not show a significant influence, either directly on BEQ or PI, or indirectly
through the BEQ mediation channel. These findings indicate that brand equity plays
a crucial role in enhancing the purcha se intentions of Generation Z consumers
through customer engagement, a positive brand image, and digital
recommendations. This study enriches the literature on brand equity and offers
implications for the local fashion industry in navigating the "Local Bra nd Winter"
phenomenon, grounded in the Theory of Planned Behavior.

INTRODUCTION
The evolution of social media has transformed how consumers —particularly
Generation Z seek information and make product choices. In Indonesia, the number of
internet users has surpassed 191.4 million, with a social media penetration rate of 68.9%
of the population (DataReportal, 2024). This high level of social media usage is reflected
in the consumption habits of Generation Z. An INSIG Indonesia survey (2025) reveals
that 68% of Generation Z have purchased products based on online recommendations,
while 76% follow at least one influencer they deem credible. These trends indicate that
Social Media Marketing (SMM), Electronic Word -of-Mouth (E-WOM), and consumer-
brand interactions play pivotal roles in shaping perceptions and purchase intentions. This
is particularly relevant to the local apparel industry, where the local fashion category saw
38% growth between 2024 and 2025, with over 87% of buyers belonging to Generation
Z and the Millennial demographic (Tokopedia Trend Report, 2024–2025). Consequently,
the success of local brands hinges not only on the products themselves but also on the
ability to establish communication and build relationships with consumers via digital
channels.
Despite the growing importance of SMM, empirical findings regarding its impact
on purchase intention remain inconsistent. Aziz (2025) found that SMM has a direct effect
on purchase intention, whereas Fadhlillah Al-Hanaan et al. (2023) demonstrated that this
influence is largely indirect, mediated by brand equity. Similarly, H et al. (2023) found
that SMM influences brand equity, which in turn relates to purchase intention. These
inconsistencies suggest that SMM does not necessarily generate purchase intenti on
directly; rather, it may operate through psychological mechanisms, such as the perception
of brand value. On the other hand, E-WOM and Brand Engagement play distinct roles in
shaping Brand Equity and Purchase Intention, yet research concerning Brand
Engagement and Brand Image among Generation Z within the context of local Indonesian
apparel brands remains limited. The research gap addressed by this study lies in three
aspects. First, prior studies have yielded inconsistent results regarding the impact of SMM
on Purchase Intention, necessitating further examination that considers Brand Equity as

a mediating mechanism. Second, some previous studies have examined E -WOM and
SMM in isolation, failing to integrate Brand Engagement and Brand Image into a unified
model to explain the formation of Brand Equity. Third, research on the relationships
between these variables remains relatively limited regarding Generation Z consumers and
local Indonesian apparel brands, particularly in Batam. Therefore, this study develops a
model to examine the effects of E -WOM, SMM, Brand Engagement, and Brand Image
on Brand Equity and Purchase Intention, while also investigating the role of Brand Equity
as a mediating variable.

LITERATURE REVIEW
Theory of Planned Behavior as a Theoretical Foundation
This research is based on the Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB), formulated by
Icek Ajzen in 1991 as a development of its predecessor, the Theory of Reasoned Action
(TRA), initiated by Martin Fishbein in 1975. TPB was born of the need to explain human
behavior that is not entirely within an individual's control. Ajzen argued that behavioral
intention is the strongest predictor of actual behavior, and this intention is formed by three
main constructs: attitude toward behavior, subjective norm, and perceived b ehavioral
control. In the context of this research, TPB provides a relevant conceptual framework to
explain how Generation Z consumers form purchase intentions (Purchase Intention)
towards local Indonesian apparel brands. Consumer attitudes towards a brand are formed
through the accumulation of information and experiences, which in the digital era come
from Electronic Word of Mouth and Social Media Marketing activities. When consumers
are exposed to positive reviews from fellow users or relevant marketing content on social
media, their attitudes towards the brand tend to shift in a more favorable direction.
Subjective norms are reflected in the influence of digital communities and social
references formed through Brand Engagement, where active interactions between
consumers and brands on digital platforms encourage positive social pressure that
strengthens purchase intentions. Meanwhile, perceived behavioral control in this study is
represented by Brand Image and Brand Equity, namely, the extent to which consumers
feel confident and believe that the chosen brand can meet expectations and reflect their
self-identity. Thus, TPB is not only relevant as a theoretical foundation but also functions
as an integrative framework that connects all variables in this research model. E -WOM,
Social Media Marketing, Brand Engagement, and Brand Image act as antecedents shaping
consumer attitudes, norms, and perceptions, while Brand Equity serves as a mediating
construct that aggregates these influences before ultimately translating them into concrete
Purchase Intention. Although the Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) has been wid ely
applied to various consumer behavior contexts, its application to local apparel businesses
remains limited. Accordingly, this study addresses this gap by examining TPB within the
local apparel business context, which constitutes the novelty of the research.

The Influence of E-WOM on Purchase Intention (H1)
Several studies have found that E -WOM significantly influences Purchase
Intention, especially among Generation Z, who have a strong habit of searching for
information and product references across various digital platforms before deciding to
buy. Within this framework, Electronic Word of Mouth (E -WOM) serves as a source of
information considered trustworthy because it is based on the direct experiences of other
consumers (Maringan et al., 2023). Reviews, recommendations, and testimonials across
digital spaces have been shown to provide significant indications of a product or brand's
quality and help consumers weigh the benefits and potential risks before making a
decision. Furthermore, E -WOM also helps minimize the information uncertainty
commonly encountered in online transactions, given that consumers lack the opportunity
to see or try a product directly before making a purchase. Therefore, the better and more
convincing the information receive d through E -WOM, the greater consumer trust in a
brand will be (Rais et al., 2023) . This, in turn, can strengthen the perception of brand
value in consumers' minds while encouraging growth in purchase intentions, especially
among Generation Z, who are highly dependent on digitally based information at every
stage of their consumption de cision-making process (Verma, 2021). When consumers
find consistent positive reviews or recommendations, they'll be more confident in their
purchasing decisions.
Kintan Oktadinna & Rachmat Fauzan (2024)  emphasized that the credibility of
digital messages from other consumers is a major factor influencing PI. E -WOM helps
consumers assess a product's risk, quality, benefits, and reputation. On e -commerce
platforms like Shopee or TikTok Shop, consumers ofte n pay more attention to reviews
and star ratings than to promotional content provided by the company.
International research by Tafolli et al. (2025) also interpreted that the intensity of
positive E-WOM exposure on social media can increase consumers' positive attitudes
towards brands, thus forming strong purchase intentions. Gen Z consumers, who are
highly active in seeking information online, trust other users' experiences more than
company claims, making E -WOM one of the most important determinants of Purchase
Intention.
The Influence of Social Media Marketing on Purchase Intention (H2)
SMM has been shown to significantly influence purchase intentions, especially
among digital -native consumers such as Generation Z. SMM operates through
mechanisms such as increasing Brand Engagement, creating emotional experiences, and
fostering two-way relationships between brands and users. Ellitan et al. (2022) interpret
that attractive visual content, a communication style that is close to consumers, and high
interactivity contribute to increasing Purchase Intention.
Study Thamrin (2021) also interprets influencer marketing as an element of SMM
that can significantly influence purchasing decisions. Gen Z consumers are more likely

to trust recommendations from influencers perceived as credible and emotionally
connected. When influencers demonstrate product usage in everyday contexts, consumers
perceive the product as more relevant and worth purchasing.
In addition, international research by Sağtaş (2022) interpreted that SMM creates
positive attitudes toward brands through perceptions of enjoyment, informativeness, and
credibility. The higher the content quality, the greater consumers' interest in purchasing
the product. Consumers exposed to creative cont ent such as video tutorials, unboxing
videos, reviews, challenges, or storytelling tend to show higher purchase intention.
However, not all SMMs have a positive impact. A study by Rajalakshmi & Thabassum
(2024) found that overly promotional content, without providing informative value,
actually decreases purchase interest because consumers perceive it as inauthentic. This
suggests that content quality and relevance are key factors in SMM effectiveness.
The Influence of Brand Engagement on Purchase Intention (H3)
Brand Engagement significantly influences Purchase Intention because the
interaction between consumers and brands creates strong emotional, cognitive, and social
connections. Consumers who feel involved with a brand tend to show high interest in the
product, feel a personal closeness, and consider the brand relevant to their identity. A
study by Emini & Zeqiri (2021) found that engagement in the form of likes, comments,
and participation in interactive content, such as challenges or giveaways, had a positive
impact on Gen Z consumers' purchase intentions.
Similar results were obtained by Wijaya & Susilawaty (2023) , who interpret
engagement strategies as strengthening positive attitudes toward a brand because digital
interactions create deeper emotional experiences. Consumers who actively engage in
brand activities are more likely to believe in the product's quality  and reduce their
perceived risk of purchasing.
In the context of digital marketing, brand engagement is also closely related to
hedonic experiences, namely feelings of pleasure, satisfaction, and excitement when
interacting with a brand. Research by Emini & Zeqiri (2021) has shown that the higher a
consumer's psychological and emotional involvement, the greater their likelihood of
making a purchase. Thus, engagement is a crucial variable that facilitates the relationship
between consumers and brands and helps shape purchase intention.
The Influence of Brand Image on Purchase Intention (H4)
Brand Image has been shown to have a substantial influence on Purchase
Intention, as positive consumer perceptions of a brand directly shape beliefs and
attitudinal orientations that support purchasing decisions. A strong brand image can
elevate consumer trust, reduce the perceived risks inherent in the purchasing process, and
solidify the belief that the product is appropriate and offers value commensurate with the

sacrifices made. Syifa and Ahmadi (2024)  find that consumers are more interested in
buying products with a professional, consistent brand image and a positive reputation.
Research by Sari and Wahjoedi (2022)   found that a positive brand image
increases purchase intention because consumers associate the brand with high quality,
strong emotional value, and clear functional benefits. Brand image also helps consumers
differentiate one brand from another in a competitive market environment.
For Gen Z consumers, brand image plays a more dominant role because this
generation pays close attention to lifestyle, visual identity, and symbolic value. Research
by Mubarak et al. (2024) explains that Gen Z tends to purchase products from brands that
are not only high -quality but also reflect their identity, aesthetics, and personal
preferences. Visual branding, color, packaging design, and the quality of storytelling on
social media are all influential factors that significantly influence Gen Z's purchase
intention.
In addition, research by Azzahro (2025)  emphasized that brand image is also
influenced by UGC (user -generated content), such as reviews, unboxing videos, and
consumer experience videos. When this content creates a positive image, consumers are
more likely to make a purchase. Therefore, brand image is a crucial predictor of purchase
intention in the context of modern digital marketing.
The Influence of E-WOM on Brand Equity (H5)
E-WOM not only influences purchase intentions but also plays a crucial role in
shaping brand equity. Positive information shared repeatedly and consistently by
consumers through digital platforms can increase brand awareness, strengthen brand
associations, and build better perceptions of quality. Verma (2021)  explains that
consistent digital reviews create a higher perceived quality because consumers consider
other users' experiences as tangible evidence of a product's or brand's quality and
credibility.
Maringan et al. (2023)  argued that E -WOM can improve perceived quality and
build a strong brand reputation by spreading positive consumer opinions. Conversely,
negative E-WOM can significantly reduce brand equity because negative information
tends to spread faster and have a lo nger-lasting impact than positive information.
Therefore, the quality of consumer interactions and conversations in the digital space is a
crucial factor in building brand value.
These findings are supported by research by Tahir et al. (2024) , which states that
E-WOM is one of the most trusted sources of information for consumers in evaluating a
brand. Information obtained from fellow consumers is considered more objective than
marketing communications originating directly from the company. Wh en consumers
receive positive and credible reviews, they will form a better perception of the brand's
quality and reputation, thereby increasing brand equity.

In the context of Generation Z, strengthening Brand Equity through E-WOM occurs
more quickly than in previous generations because this group is very active in seeking
social validation through comments, ratings, reviews, and testimonials on various digital
platforms. Generation Z tends to rely on other consumers' experiences as a primary
reference when evaluating a brand. They trust brands more when they have many positive
reviews, high levels of engagement, and recommendations from digital communities.
Thus, the more positive and credible E -WOM consumers receive, the higher the Brand
Equity formed in their minds, thus strengthening the brand's position in an increasingly

The Influence of Social Media Marketing on Brand Equity (H6)
SMM significantly increases BE by providing a space for ongoing interaction
between consumers and brands. Consistent SMM content can build brand awareness,
strengthen brand associations, and enhance perceived quality. Rais et al. (2023) explain
that repeated exposure to visual content strengthens brand memory and creates positive
associations in consumers' minds.
Study Koay et al. (2021)  interprets SMM as building BE through interactive
digital experiences. When consumers experience high -quality interactions, such as
responsive comments or relatable content, they develop a better perception of brand
quality. Continuous interaction also creates emotional bonds, thus increasing loyalty.
In the context of Gen Z, SMM strengthens BE because this group assesses brand
reputation based on digital presence, content consistency, and social validation through
likes, comments, and follower counts. Research by Maringan et al. (2023)  finds that
brands that are active and consistent in creating personalized content on social media can
increase the BE dimension, especially perceived quality and associations.
The Influence of Brand Engagement on Brand Equity (H7)
Brand Engagement plays a crucial role in strengthening BE, as high engagement
fosters a deeper understanding of the brand. Actively engaged consumers have higher
levels of brand awareness, demonstrate better perceptions of quality, and have stronger
emotional associations with the brand. He et al. (2022)  interpret that engagement
increases perceived quality because repeated interactions allow consumers to become
more familiar with product characteristics.
Study Tarabieh (2022) interpreted that engagement strengthens brand associations
because consumers gain direct experiences that increase emotional closeness. Consumers
who frequently participate in digital activities such as voting, live streaming, or
commenting also develop brand loyalty because they feel part of the brand's community.
In the context of Gen Z, engagement plays a much more important role because they are
highly responsive to interactive digital experiences. Maringan et al. (2023)  explain that
Gen Z consumers judge a brand's quality and relevance based on its level of engagement

The Influence of Brand Image on Brand Equity (H8)
Brand Image is a fundamental component in shaping brand awareness because
positive brand perceptions increase awareness, perceived quality, and brand associations.
When consumers have a positive image, they tend to rate products as higher quality and
more valuable. This aligns with research. Aquinia et al. (2021) argue that a strong brand
image strengthens perceptions of quality and consumer loyalty.
Study Maringan et al. (2023)  finds that the consistency of digital imagery, such
as visual branding, communication tone, and storytelling, significantly strengthens brand
awareness and associations, two key components in building brand awareness. A strong
brand image helps consumers recognize the brand easily, triggers positive memories, and
creates deep emotional preferences. Furthermore, international research by Tahir et al.
(2024) explains that a strong brand image with high visual appeal and the ability to
represent consumer values and lifestyles play a significant role in establishing a
sustainable relationship between a brand and its customers.
When consumers begin to perceive a brand as an embodiment of their identity,
hopes, or principles, a stronger, deeper emotional bond with the brand naturally forms.
This bond not only increases positive perceptions of the brand but also encourages a more
consistent preference over competing brands. Over time, repeated positive experiences
and the alignment of the brand image with consumer expectations will strengthen
satisfaction and trust, ultimately leading to customer loyalty. Thus, a relevant brand image
that aligns with the target market's characteristics is a strategic factor in fostering
sustainable loyalty, especially among Generation Z, who tend to choose brands that align
with their personal identity and values. Consumers with a positive image perception feel
more connected to the brand, thereby forming a strong BE. Thus, brand image is one of
the most important variables in increasing BE in the digital marketing era.

immediately form purchase intentions based solely on positive reviews. Still, the
perception of brand value formed from that information is the primary mechanism driving
purchase intentions. E -WOM first increases brand awarene ss, perceived quality, and
brand associations before ultimately influencing purchase intentions.
Similar findings were reported by Lin et al. (2023), confirming that brand equity
strengthens consumers' interpretation of digital information, thereby increasing the

psychological role in transforming digital information into purchasing beliefs.
Additionally, Verma (2021)  explained that consistently receiving positive
recommendations and reviews will increase brand credibility and strengthen perceptions
of product quality. When consumers perceive a brand as having superior quality and a
strong reputation, they are more con fident about making a purchase. Maringan et al.
(2023) also found that positive E-WOM can increase consumer trust in a brand, thereby

The Mediating Role of Brand Equity in the Influence of Social Media Marketing

Several studies have interpreted the influence of social media marketing on
purchase intention as often mediated by brand equity. Setiawan et al. (2024) explain that
marketing activities on social media do not always directly drive purchase intention;
rather, they first build positive perceptions of brand value. Social media marketing
increases awareness and associations by distributing engaging content, fostering brand
equity and driving consumers toward purchase intention.
A study by Dwivedi et al. (2021)  also found that brand equity plays a crucial
psychological role, as consumers assess brand quality based on the digital experiences
they gain from social media activities. The stronger the brand equity, the greater the
influence of social media marketing on purchase intention.
Koay et al. (2021) added that consistent interaction on social media can increase
emotional closeness between consumers and brands, thereby strengthening loyalty and
perceived quality. Rais et al. (2023)  found that brand equity is the primary pathway
explaining how digital marketing activities translate into purchase intention. For
Generation Z, who are highly active on social media, informative, authentic, and
interactive content is more effective in bui lding brand equity than simply promoting
products. Therefore, brand equity plays a crucial role in linking social media marketing
activities to the formation of purchase intention.

METHOD
Research is a systematic process used to understand, explain, and predict a
phenomenon through structured scientific steps. (Maringan et al., 2023) In quantitative
research, preparing a research design is an important step to ensure the research is on the
right track, from problem identification and variable selection to analysis methods and
conclusions (Siyoto & Sodik, 2015). The research design provides guidelines regarding
how relationships between variables are tested, how data are collected, and how re sults
are interpreted.
The research design used is ex post facto causal research, meaning data are collected
after the phenomenon, without the researcher's intervention in the variables under study.
This approach is used to systematically analyze causal relationships among varia bles
based on a theoretical framework and relevant empirical evidence (Kartika & Pandjaitan,
2023).
This study was designed to examine the influence of E -WOM, Social Media
Marketing, Brand Engagement, and Brand Image on Purchase Intention, with Brand
Equity as a mediating variable explaining the mechanism underlying the relationship. The
overall relationship between variables is formulated in hypotheses H1 –H13, which are
based on a theoretical framework that includes the Customer-Based Brand Equity Model.
(Verma, 2021), Theory of Planned Behavior (Noviyanti, 2025), as well as the latest
research related to digital consumer behavior.

The use of a causal design in this study is very relevant considering the rapidly
growing digital marketing phenomenon among Generation Z. This group is very active
in using social media, is frequently exposed to digital marketing content, and is highly
influenced by user reviews (E -WOM), influencer credibility, brand visual imagery, and
interactive experiences offered by brands. Therefore, a causal research design allows
researchers to test the extent to which these factors contribute to the formation of Purchase

Visually, the relationship between variables in this study is depicted in a structural
model comprising four independent variables (E -WOM, SMM, Brand Engagement,
Brand Image), one mediating variable (Brand Equity), and one dependent variable
(Purchase Intention). This research model aligns with recent research, including H et al.
(2023), Sari & Wahjoedi (2022), and Kartika & Pandjaitan (2023), which suggests that
digital marketing and consumer psychology variables are mutually influential in shaping
value perceptions and purchasing decisions among young consumers.
Thus, this ex post facto causal research design becomes the basic framework for
evaluating the influence of each variable and testing whether Brand Equity plays a
significant role as a mediator in explaining Purchase Intention.
The development of the local Indonesian fashion industry and the high level of

Discussion
The findings of this study provide an integrated explanation of how digital
consumer interactions and brand -related perceptions contribute to purchase intention
among Generation Z consumers of Indonesian local apparel brands in Batam City. Rather

 321 Fendy Cuandra, Sefryansah, Listia Nurjanah
than interpreting each relationship only from a statistical perspective, the findings can be
understood through the Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB), which explains purchase
intention through attitude, subjective norms, and perceived behavioral control. In  this
study, E-WOM and Social Media Marketing represent digital stimuli that contribute to
consumer attitudes, Brand Engagement reflects the social and relational dimension of
subjective norms, while Brand Image and Brand Equity represent consumers’ perceptions
of the value and capability of a brand. The findings further demonstrate that these
mechanisms do not operate equally. In particular, Brand Engagement and Brand Equity
emerge as important mechanisms through which digital interactions are converted in to
purchase intention.
H1: E-WOM has a positive and significant influence on Purchase Intention
The finding that E -WOM contributes to Purchase Intention indicates that
information generated by other consumers remains an important source of influence for
Generation Z in evaluating local apparel brands. Reviews, recommendations,
testimonials, and consu mer experiences provide information that is perceived as more
experience-based than brand-controlled promotional communication. This is particularly
relevant for Generation Z consumers, who frequently use digital platforms to compare
alternatives and obtai n references before making purchasing decisions. The finding is
consistent with Maringan et al. (2023), who explain that E-WOM can increase consumer
trust and perceived quality, as well as H et al. (2023), who identify E -WOM as an
important mechanism for reducing uncertainty in purchasing decisions.
From the perspective of TPB, this finding supports the role of attitude in the
formation of behavioral intention. Information obtained from other consumers can shape
how consumers evaluate a local apparel brand before they decide whether the brand is
worth considering. Positive consumer experiences therefore function not merely as
promotional messages but as social information that contributes to the formation of
favorable attitudes toward the brand. This interpretation is also consistent with Tafolli et
al. (2025), who found that positive E -WOM in the fashion context can strengthen
consumer attitudes and purchase intentions.
The empirical implication is that E -WOM provides local apparel brands with a
form of consumer -generated credibility that cannot be created solely through brand -
controlled communication. In an environment where consumers are exposed to a large
amount of commercial content, experiences shared by fellow consumers may provide a
more credible basis for evaluating a brand. Thus, the finding extends the application of
TPB by showing how consumer-generated digital information can operate as an important
antecedent of purchase intention in the local apparel context.

H2: Social Media Marketing has a positive and significant influence on Purchase
Intention
In contrast to E -WOM, Social Media Marketing does not directly contribute to
Purchase Intention in this study. This finding is important because social media is widely
used as a marketing channel by local apparel brands, yet the presence of marketing
content itself does not necessarily translate into stronger purchase intention. The finding
is consistent with H et al. (2023), who found that Social Media Marketing does not always
produce a direct effect on purchase intention. Sağtaş (2022) further explains t hat the
effectiveness of social media marketing depends on the informativeness, enjoyment,
relevance, and credibility of the content presented to consumers.
The finding can be interpreted through the attitude component of TPB. Social
Media Marketing represents communication controlled by the brand, meaning that
consumers may encounter the content frequently without necessarily developing a
sufficiently favorab le evaluation of the brand. This is particularly relevant in an
environment characterized by information overload and digital clutter. As discussed in
the introduction, the increasing volume of commercial content on social media makes
consumers more selective
H3: Brand Engagement has a positive and significant influence on Purchase
Intention
Brand Engagement is found to contribute to Purchase Intention, indicating that
active consumer involvement with a brand is more consequential than passive exposure
to brand communication. Engagement includes consumers' willingness to interact,
participate, respond, and develop emotional connections with brands through digital
platforms. Emini and Zeqiri (2021) found that digital participation, including interaction
and participation in brand activities, can strengthen purchase intention among younger
consumers. Wijaya and Susilawaty (2023) similarly explain that engagement creates
deeper emotional experiences that strengthen consumers' attitudes toward brands.
Within TPB, Brand Engagement can be understood primarily through the
subjective norm component. Digital interaction does not occur in isolation; consumers
participate in communities, respond to content, observe the behavior of other users, and
receive social cues from their digital environment. These interactions can make the brand
more socially relevant and strengthen consumers' willingness to consider the brand.
Therefore, engagement represents a relational process through which consumers become
socially and psychologically connected to a brand.
The finding is particularly relevant to local apparel brands because apparel
consumption often involves identity, lifestyle, and community affiliation. Activities such
as user -generated content, interactive campaigns, challenges, and community -based
communication can create opportunities for consumers to participate rather than merely
receive promotional information. The empirical finding therefore suggests that the

strength of a local brand is not determined only by how much content it produces, but
also by how effectively it creates opportunities for consumers to become participants in
the brand experience.
H4: Brand Image has a positive and significant influence on Purchase Intention
Brand Image does not directly contribute to Purchase Intention in this study. This
finding does not mean that Brand Image is unimportant. Rather, it suggests that a
favorable perception of a local apparel brand may need to be translated into broader
perceptions of brand value before it becomes a purchase intention. This interpretation is
consistent with Sunaryanto and Noviyanti (2025), who argue that Brand Image may
influence purchase intention through Brand Equity rather than directly. It also differs
from studies such as Fadya Al Yasha Syifa and Mirzam Arqy Ahmadi (2024) and Sari
and Wahjoedi (2022), which reported direct effects of Brand Image on Purchase
Intention.
The finding can be explained through TPB, particularly the role of perceived
behavioral control. A positive image can make consumers perceive a brand favorably, but
this perception alone does not necessarily provide sufficient confidence to purchase.
Consumers may still evaluate whether the brand offers consistent quality, meaningful
associations, credibility, and sufficient value compared with competing brands. In the
highly competitive local apparel market, visual appeal and brand popularity may therefore
be insufficient to convert perception into intention.
This finding becomes particularly meaningful when viewed together with the
significant relationship between Brand Image and Brand Equity. It indicates that Brand
Image contributes to the formation of a broader perception of brand value, while Brand
Equity provides the psychological basis through which that perception becomes relevant
to purchase intention. Thus, the role of Brand Image in this study is more accurately
understood as a foundation for brand value formation rather than as an immediate trigger
of purchase intention.
H5: E-WOM has a positive and significant influence on Brand Equity
The positive contribution of E -WOM to Brand Equity demonstrates that
consumer-generated communication does not only influence short -term purchase
considerations but also contributes to the accumulation of long-term brand value. Verma
(2021) explains that consistent digital reviews can strengthen perceived quality because
consumers use other consumers' experiences as evidence when evaluating a brand. Tardin
and Pelissari (2021) similarly associate E -WOM with increased brand awareness and
perceived quality, both of which are important components of Brand Equity.
This finding strengthens the TPB framework by showing that information
originating from other consumers can have a broader role than simply shaping immediate
attitudes toward purchase. Repeated positive experiences communicated through digital
platforms can accumulate into perceptions of quality, credibility, reputation, and value.

 324 BALANCE : Economic, Business, Management, and Accounting Journal  Vol. 23 No.2 2026
These perceptions form a stronger psychological foundation for consumers' subsequent
behavioral intentions.
For local apparel brands, this finding highlights the importance of maintaining the
quality of the actual consumer experience. E-WOM cannot be sustainably generated only
through promotional efforts because its credibility depends on consumers' genuine
experiences. Consequently, encouraging satisfied customers to share authentic
experiences can contribute simultaneously to immediate purchase consideration and
longer-term brand equity.
H6: Social Media Marketing has a positive and significant influence on Brand
Equity
The absence of a significant contribution of Social Media Marketing to Brand
Equity provides one of the important findings of this study. This result differs from Koay
et al. (2021) and Verma (2021), who reported that social media marketing can strengthen
brand equity. The difference suggests that the effectiveness of Social Media Marketing is
highly dependent on the context in which the communication occurs and on the quality
and relevance of the content.
One possible explanation is the digital clutter experienced by consumers. The
introduction of the study highlights the rapid increase in commercial content on social
media, which makes it increasingly difficult for consumers to distinguish meaningful
information from promotional messages. In such an environment, frequent exposure to
brand content does not automatically create stronger brand associations or perceived
value. Sari and Wahjoedi (2022) similarly emphasize the importance of content
consistency and relevance in converting social media activity into brand value.
From the TPB perspective, this finding indicates that brand -controlled
communication does not automatically produce the psychological conditions required for
behavioral intention. Social Media Marketing may create opportunities for consumers to
encounter a brand, but communication must subsequently generate favorable attitudes,
social interaction, or stronger perceptions of brand value. The finding therefore
demonstrates that digital presence should not be equated with digital effectiveness.
H7: Brand Engagement has a positive and significant influence on Brand Equity
One of the most important findings of this study is the strong contribution of
Brand Engagement to Brand Equity. Compared with the other antecedent variables,
Brand Engagement demonstrates the most substantial role in the formation of Brand
Equity. This fi nding is consistent with He et al. (2022), who explain that repeated
engagement allows consumers to understand product characteristics more deeply, while
Tarabieh (2022) shows that engagement strengthens brand associations through direct
and emotional experiences. Vo Minh et al. (2022) also identify social brand engagement
as an important factor in building brand equity in the fashion industry.

This finding provides a stronger interpretation of the subjective norm component
of TPB. Subjective norms are not limited to direct social pressure from other people; in a
digital environment, they can emerge through repeated interaction, participation,
community experiences, and social identification with a brand. When consumers actively
engage with a brand, the relationship moves beyond simple exposure to marketing
content. The brand becomes part of consumers' social and experiential environment.
The findingis particularly important for local apparel brands because apparel
consumption can involve self -expression and community identification. Engagement
activities allow consumers to participate in constructing the meaning of the brand rather
than simply receiving that meaning from the company. Consequently, Brand Engagement
becomes an important mechanism through which digital interaction is transformed into
perceived brand quality, stronger associations, and greater loyalty. This explains why
engagement becomes particularly relevant to the formation of Brand Equity among
Generation Z consumers.
H8: Brand Image has a positive and significant influence on Brand Equity
The significant contribution of Brand Image to Brand Equity demonstrates that
consumers' overall perceptions of a local apparel brand are important in constructing
long-term brand value. Purwianti et al. (2024) explain that a strong brand image can
strengthen perceived quality and consumer loyalty, while Verma (2021) emphasizes the
importance of consistency in building brand awareness and associations. Tahir et al.
(2024) further show that brand images aligned with consumers' identities and lifestyles
can strengthen emotional connections.
This finding supports the perceived behavioral control dimension of TPB.
Consumers need to perceive that a brand is credible, relevant, and capable of fulfilling
their expectations before a favorable perception can contribute to stronger behavioral
intention. Brand Image therefore provides an initial perception of what the brand
represents, while Brand Equity reflects the more comprehensive value consumers attach
to that brand.
For Generation Z, this relationship is particularly relevant because brand meaning
can be connected to lifestyle, identity, and self -expression. A local apparel brand that
communicates a consistent and authentic identity can therefore accumulate stronger
associations in consumers' minds. However, as demonstrated by the non-significant direct
relationship between Brand Image and Purchase Intention, the image must be translated
into broader brand value before it can effectively influence purchase intention.

The finding of H9 shows that Brand Equity mediates the relationship between E-

This finding is consistent with Lin et al. (2023), who explain that E -WOM can
strengthen consumer evaluations of a brand through the formation of brand-related value.
Purwianti (2023) also explains that E -WOM can increase consumer awareness and
perceived quality, which are important components in the development of Brand Equity.
Thus, the present finding extends these studies by demonstrating that the influence of E-
WOM on purchase intention among Generation Z consumers of local apparel brands
operates not only as a direct informational influence but also through the development of
Brand Equity.
From the perspective of TPB, this mediation can be related primarily to the
formation of favorable attitudes toward the behavior. E -WOM provides consumers with
information that helps them evaluate whether a brand is credible and worth purchasing.
When repeated consumer experiences strengthen the perceived value of the brand, these
evaluations become more favorable and are subsequently reflected in purchase intention.
Therefore, Brand Equity functions as a psychological mechanism that converts consumer-
generated information into a stronger behavioral intention.
The finding provides an important empirical contribution because it shows that E-
WOM has a dual role in the model. It can influence Purchase Intention directly, while at
the same time strengthening Brand Equity as an intermediate mechanism. This suggests
that the value of E-WOM for local apparel brands is not limited to generating short-term
consumer attention but also extends to the development of long -term consumer-based
brand value.
H10: Brand Equity mediates the influence of Social Media Marketing on Purchase
Intention
The finding of H10 shows that Brand Equity does not mediate the relationship
between Social Media Marketing and Purchase Intention. This finding indicates that the
presence of brand-generated content on social media is not sufficient to develop the level
of Brand Equity required to influence consumers' purchase intention. Although social
media provides local apparel brands with opportunities to communicate with Generation
Z, exposure to promotional content does not automatically result in stronger brand
associations, perceived quality, or loyalty.
This finding differs from Setiawan et al. (2024) and Dwivedi et al. (2021), who
identified Brand Equity as an important mechanism connecting social media marketing
with consumer responses. The difference may be related to the characteristics of
Generation Z consumers, who are exposed to large amounts of commercial content and

may therefore become more selective in evaluating brand -generated information. Social
Media Marketing needs to provide meaningful, relevant, credible, and interactive
experiences before it can contribute to stronger Brand Equity.
The finding can also be interpreted through TPB. Social Media Marketing may
provide information that creates opportunities for consumers to form attitudes toward a
brand, but communication controlled by the brand does not necessarily create sufficient
social or psychological reinforcement to produce behavioral intention. In other words,
social media exposure represents an initial communication process rather than a guarantee
of behavioral response. The absence of mediation therefore suggests that Brand Equi ty
cannot function as a bridge when social media communication has not first generated
sufficiently meaningful consumer perceptions.
This finding represents an important empirical qualification of previous research.
It demonstrates that Brand Equity does not automatically mediate every digital marketing
activity. The effectiveness of the mediation depends on whether the digital activity  is
capable of generating genuine consumer value. For local apparel brands, this suggests
that social media strategies need to move beyond content distribution and focus more
strongly on interaction, relevance, credibility, and consumer participation.
H11: Brand Equity mediates the influence of Brand Engagement on Purchase
Intention
The finding of H11 demonstrates that Brand Equity mediates the relationship
between Brand Engagement and Purchase Intention. This indicates that consumer
engagement with a local apparel brand contributes to purchase intention partly because
repeated intera ction strengthens the value consumers attach to the brand. When
consumers actively participate in brand -related activities, communicate with the brand,
and develop emotional connections, these experiences can strengthen brand associations,
perceived qualit y, and loyalty. The accumulated perceptions then form Brand Equity,
which contributes to purchase intention.
This finding is consistent with Cuandra  et al. (2026) and Febriani and Cuandra
(2023), who explain that Brand Equity can function as a psychological bridge between
consumer engagement and purchase intention. Tarabieh (2022) similarly demonstrates
that digital engagement strengthens brand associ ations and emotional relationships
between consumers and brands. The present study extends these findings to Generation
Z consumers of Indonesian local apparel brands by demonstrating that engagement is not
merely an interaction outcome but an important mechanism in the formation of consumer-
based brand value.
The mediation can be explained through the subjective norm component of TPB.
Brand Engagement involves interaction with other consumers, brand communities, and
the brand itself. Through these interactions, consumers receive social cues and develop a
sense of connection with the brand. Repeated engagement can therefore strengthen the

social relevance of the brand and contribute to stronger brand perceptions. Brand Equity
subsequently translates these relational experiences into a stronger basis for purchase
intention.
This finding represents one of the important contributions of the study because it
demonstrates that the influence of Brand Engagement extends beyond direct behavioral
intention. Engagement becomes more meaningful when it creates accumulated brand
value. T herefore, for local apparel brands, encouraging consumers to participate in
conversations, user -generated content, interactive campaigns, and brand communities
may be more valuable than simply increasing the frequency of promotional posts.
H12: Brand Equity mediates the influence of Brand Image on Purchase Intention
The finding of H12 demonstrates that Brand Equity mediates the relationship
between Brand Image and Purchase Intention. This finding indicates that a favorable
perception of a local apparel brand does not necessarily translate immediately into
purchase intention. Instead, Brand Image first contributes to broader perceptions of brand
value, including perceived quality, brand associations, and loyalty, which subsequently
strengthen consumers' willingness to purchase.
This finding is consistent with Sunaryanto and Noviyanti (2025), who explain that
Brand Image may influence Purchase Intention through Brand Equity rather than directly.
Aquinia et al. (2021) also demonstrate that perceived quality and loyalty are importan t
components in strengthening Brand Equity. The present study therefore reinforces the
argument that Brand Image should not be viewed only as an external perception of a
brand, but as an important foundation for the development of consumer -based brand
value.
The mediation is particularly relevant when interpreted through TPB. Brand
Image can shape consumers' initial evaluation of a brand and influence their perception
of whether the brand is relevant, credible, and capable of fulfilling their expectations.
However, these perceptions need to be consolidated into stronger brand value before they
can contribute to behavioral intention. Brand Equity performs this function by integrating
consumers' perceptions and experiences into a more comprehensive evaluation of the
brand.
The finding also explains why Brand Image does not directly contribute to
Purchase Intention in the present study while its mediated relationship through Brand
Equity is supported. This indicates that Generation Z consumers may not purchase a local
apparel brand merely because they perceive its image positively. Instead, the positive
image needs to be supported by perceived quality, meaningful associations, and loyalty
before it becomes sufficiently strong to influence purchase intention. Thus, the mediation
finding provides a more nuanced explanation of how Brand Image contributes to
consumer behavior.

H13: Brand Equity has a positive and significant influence on Purchase Intention
The results show that Brand Equity has a positive and significant contribution to
Purchase Intention, supporting H13. This finding indicates that the higher the brand value
perceived by Generation Z consumers, the stronger their intention to purchase produ cts
from Indonesian local apparel brands. Brand Equity reflects consumers’ overall
evaluation of a brand, including perceived quality, positive brand associations, brand
strength, preference over competing brands, and willingness to remain loyal. When
consumers perceive that a local apparel brand provides meaningful value and has a strong
position in their minds, they are more likely to consider the brand when making future
purchasing decisions.
This finding is consistent with Verma (2021), who explains that brand equity
dimensions such as perceived quality, brand associations, and loyalty contribute to
consumer purchase-related responses. Similarly, Vo Minh et al. (2022) demonstrate the
importance of Brand Equity in the fashion context, particularly because strong brand
value can strengthen consumers’ perceptions and behavioral responses toward fashion
brands. These findings support the argument that purchase intention is not determined
solely by consumers’ exposure to promotional information, but also by the accumulated
value that consumers associate with a brand.
From the perspective of the Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB), Brand Equity can
strengthen the psychological conditions underlying behavioral intention. Consumers who
perceive a brand as having good quality, strong associations, credibility, and distinctive
value may develop greater confidence in choosing that brand. This perception can
contribute to a more favorable evaluation of the behavioral choice and reduce uncertainty
when consumers compare the brand with alternatives. Therefore, Brand Equity can
function as an important psychological foundation that supports the formation of
Purchase Intention.
The finding is particularly relevant in the context of Generation Z consumers of
Indonesian local apparel brands. Apparel products are not only evaluated based on
functional characteristics but can also represent lifestyle, identity, and self -expression.
Consequently, consumers may be more willing to consider purchasing a local apparel
brand when the brand has developed a strong and meaningful identity in their minds. A
strong Brand Equity can make consumers perceive the brand as more valuable and
differentiated from competing apparel brands, thereby increasing their willingness to
purchase.
Furthermore, the significant relationship between Brand Equity and Purchase
Intention provides an important explanation for the mediation findings in this study.
Brand Equity mediates the relationships between E -WOM, Brand Engagement, and
Brand Image and P urchase Intention. This means that these antecedent variables can
contribute to purchase intention partly because they help build consumers’ perceptions of

brand value. The significant H13 therefore confirms the role of Brand Equity as the
central mechanism connecting consumers’ digital experiences and brand perceptions with
purchase intention.
This result also helps explain why some variables in the model do not have
significant direct effects on Purchase Intention. For example, Brand Image does not
significantly influence Purchase Intention directly, but it significantly contributes to
Brand Equity. Brand Equity subsequently contributes significantly to Purchase Intention.
This pattern suggests that consumers’ favorable perceptions of a local apparel brand may
need to be transformed into broader perceptions of brand value before they become
sufficiently strong to influence purchasing intentions.
Therefore, H13 demonstrates that Brand Equity is an important determinant of
Purchase Intention in the local apparel context. The finding suggests that strengthening
local apparel brands should not focus exclusively on increasing digital exposure or
promotional communication. Instead, digital activities need to generate perceived quality,
positive associations, brand preference, and loyalty that accumulate into stronger Brand
Equity. Once these elements are established, they can provide a stronger basis for
consumers’ intention to purchase local apparel products.

CONCLUSSION
This study investigates the contribution of Electronic Word of Mouth (E -WOM),
Social Media Marketing (SMM), Brand Engagement, and Brand Image to Purchase
Intention, with Brand Equity as a mediating variable, among Generation Z consumers of
Indonesian local apparel in Batam City. The research model is grounded in the Theory of
Planned Behavior (TPB), in which E -WOM and SMM are positioned as antecedents
shaping consumer attitude toward the brand. Brand Engagement reflects the influence of
subjective norms for med through digital community interaction, and Brand Image,
together with Brand Equity, represent s perceived behavioral control, namely the extent
to which consumers believe the brand can fulfill their expectations. PLS -SEM analysis
of data from 246 respondents tested 13 hypotheses, yielding the following findings.
E-WOM has been shown to have a positive and significant influence on Purchase
Intention (H1 is accepted), confirming that recommendations and reviews on digital
platforms are crucial determinants of Gen Z's purchase intention for local apparel
products. This finding is consistent with TPB’s attitude construct, in which exposure to
positive peer -generated information shifts consumer attitude toward the brand in a
favorable direction. In contrast, SMM does not show a significant direct influence on
Purchase Intention (H2 is rejected), suggesting that brand -controlled marketing content
alone is insufficient to shape attitude unless reinforced by other sources, and that its
impact on purchase intention is indirect and dependent on intermediary variables. Brand
Engagement positively and significantly influences Purchase Intention (H3 is accepted),

 331 Fendy Cuandra, Sefryansah, Listia Nurjanah
indicating that consumers' active participation in digital interactions with brands serves
as a subjective norm that strengthens social pressure to purchase. Meanwhile, Brand
Image is not shown to have a direct influence on Purchase Intention (H4 is reject ed),
indicating that perceived behavioral control built through brand image alone is not yet
sufficient to drive intention directly, and that its influence is mediated by brand equity.
E-WOM has a positive and significant influence on Brand Equity (H5 accepted),
strengthening the argument that digital communication among consumers contributes
substantively to the accumulation of brand equity as a form of perceived behavioral
control. SMM does not have a significant influence on Brand Equity (H6 rejected), a
condition suspected to be driven by digital information saturation and a lack of content
consistency among local brands. Brand Engagement recorded the highest path coefficient
in the m odel. It exerted a very significant influence on Brand Equity (H7 accepted),
confirming engagement -driven subjective norms as the most determinant consumer
structure in the formation of brand equity. Brand Image has been shown to have a positive
and significant influence on Brand Equity (H8 accepted), indicating that the perception
of a consistently built brand image contributes to the progressive strengthening of equity
and, in turn, to consumers’ confidence that the brand can meet their expectations.
Mediation testing revealed that Brand Equity partially mediates the influence of E-
WOM on Purchase Intention (H9 accepted) but does not mediate the influence of SMM
(H10 rejected), consistent with the insignificant direct path of SMM on Brand Equity.
Brand Equity mediates the influence of Brand Engagement on Purchase Intention, with
the largest indirect effect in the model (H1 1 accepted), and fully mediates the influence
of Brand Image on Purchase Intention (H12 accepted). Brand Equity itself has a positive
and highly significant influence on Purchase Intention (H13 accepted), confirming its
position as a central predictor of purchase intention in the tested model. Within the TPB
framework, these mediation results confirm that Brand Equity functions as the p oint of
convergence where consumer attitude (E-WOM), subjective norm (Brand Engagement),
and perceived behavioral control (Brand Image) are accumulated before being translated
into actual purchase intention.
Holistically, viewed through the lens of the Theory of Planned Behavior, this study
reveals that in the landscape of the Indonesian local apparel industry, as a novel, with the
Generation Z segment in Batam City, purchase intention is formed through the
accumulation of attitude shaped by E -WOM, subjective norms reflected in Brand
Engagement, and perceived behavioral control represented by Brand Image and Brand
Equity. Br and Engagement and Brand Equity emerge as the main pillars driving this
process, with Br and Equity not merely an output of marketing activities but rather the
psychological mechanism through which these TPB constructs translate into concrete
purchasing decisions. These findings have strategic implications for industry players,
urging a shift from short-term virality to sustainable investment in consumer engagement

 332 BALANCE : Economic, Business, Management, and Accounting Journal  Vol. 23 No.2 2026
and brand equity as an anticipatory response to the threat of the Local Brand Winter
phenomenon.
The sample size, focused on Gen Z consumers in Batam City, limits the
generalizability of the findings to a broader geographic context. The cross -sectional
design employed does not allow for temporal monitoring of changes in consumer
behavior. Furthermore, an R-squared value evaluation is necessary to assess the model's
predictive capacity more comprehensively. Future research is recommended to expand
the regional scope, incorporate moderating variables such as brand loyalty and consumer
involvement, and adopt a longitudinal approach to deepen understanding of the dynamics
of brand equity and consumer purchase intentions.
