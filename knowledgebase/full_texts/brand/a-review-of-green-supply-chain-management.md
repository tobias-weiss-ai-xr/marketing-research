ABSTRACT

Green supply chain management is a comprehensive approach that balances maximum profitability with
minimizing environmental impact. This approach covers the entire supply chain (from raw material
procurement to product recycling) by transforming a traditio nal supply chain into a green supply chain,
enhancing organizational performance and increasing competitiveness. This study is developmental in
terms of its purpose. The method employed for gathering data in this research involved conducting a
systematic r eview. The collected sources included reputable articles related to green supply chain
management keywords. The findings show that initially, 343 articles were identified based on the keywords;
after filtering and closer examination, this number was reduced to ten articles. The results indicate that the
successful implementation of green supply chain management not only reduces pollution and waste but
also offers benefits such as improved brand image, reduced long-term costs, increased resource efficiency,
and achieving a competitive advantage. Finally, strategies such as collaboration among supply chains,
enhancing green supply chain management performance, and similar recommendations are provided. The
main research question is: "What are the main variables in implementing green supply chain management?"
Experts have examined and confirmed that these include process -related factors such as green design,
green purchasing, and competition.

1. INTRODUCTION
The production supply chain is important from the consumer perspective. Today, consumers face
limitations in evaluating and assessing raw material sourcing, its ethical dimensions, and what happens
during the production process of products ( Stipic, 2020). Changing traditional supply chain processes
through green innovation leads to a green supply chain ( Sujatha & Krishnaveni, 2015). The green supply
chain refers to the environmental capabilities and collaborations of organizations within a supply chain.
For the successful implementation of ISO 14000 standards, resource savings, prevention of harmful
substance emissions, and training of stakeholders are among the actions that can be jointly undertaken by
organizations within the supply chain ( Chin et al., 2015 ). In the past, competition existed between
companies but has gradually shifted to competition between supply chains. Therefore, sustainability in
supply chain management has emerged as a new approach to improve performance and enhance
organizational competitiveness (Sangbor et al., 2022 ). While companies and organizations seek flexible
solutions to improve responsiveness and adopt technologies to adjust their operational strategies,
implementing supply chain management has become global even in business contexts and is defined as a
set of interconnected activities, including planning, coordination, and control of materials, components,
and finished goods from source to customer ( Gorelick, 2005 ). Now, customers, shareholders,
governments, and even competitors place great importance on environmental issues. Under global
pressure, organizations seek to produce products and services that are compatible with the environment.
In addition, industrial m anagers follow methods that are not only environmentally compatible but also
improve organizational performance. Previously, supply chain management was used only to implement
supply chain stages in an orderly and goal-oriented manner, aiming to improve performance, productivity,
and profitability. Organizations and managers, without paying attention to environmental issues, mainly
focused on reducing the rapid delivery of goods and services (Hsu & Hu, 2008). Alongside organizations,
businesses have also placed special emphasis on green supply chains owing to increased awareness of the
harmful effects of industrial activities on the environment. Therefore, in addition to improving
sustainability, the green supply chain is recognized as an effective strategic management tool that enhances
organizational environmental performance. However, the green supply chain is not merely a tool for
reducing environmental impacts; it is also a strategy for optimizing economi c resources and increasing
social welfare (Hussain et al., 2018 ). Green supply chain management improves both economic and
environmental conditions by enhancing performance parameters, which leads to the production of goods
using fewer materials and less energy (Mohajeri et al., 2019).
Cooperation among supply chain partners is crucial for enhancing green environmental
performance throughout the product lifecycle (Kang & Hwang, 2017). In addition, achieving green goals
through routine organizational activities alone is not possible and requires communication and
cooperation among all active members of the supply chain. Accordingly, the concept of green supply chain
management emerged in the literature (Zhang et al., 2017). The scope of the green supply chain is broad
and includes numerous elements and categories ( Rajeev et al., 2017 ). However, these elements can
generally be classified into key dimensions of the green supply chain, including green purchasing, green
design, environmental management, and environmental collaboration. In addition, reverse logistics can be
considered an im portant component that encompasses various aspects of supply chain activities within
these dimensions (Chen & Kitsis, 2017). Furthermore, green supply chain management addresses all steps
of the product life cycle, with particular emphasis on green purchasing, green procurement, investment in
recovery and rebuilding processes, and cooperation with customers. Organizations can improve their
overall performance by implementing green supply chain management practices and focusing on
environmental, social, economic, and operational performance (Chen & Kitsis, 2017).
In recent years, increasing environmental concerns, regulatory pressure, and rising public
awareness have encouraged organizations to adopt more sustainable practices. Consequently, green supply
chain management has emerged as an important approach for int egrating environmental considerations
into supply chain activities. Despite the growing body of research on Green Supply Chain Management,
the existing literature remains fragmented and lacks a comprehensive synthesis. To address this gap, this

2. LITERATURE REVIEW
2.1. Supply Chain Management
With changes in the business environment and increasing market competition, organizations
require production systems that can effectively respond to customer needs. Customers also demand
products with greater usability and availability and expect to receive them as quickly as possible. Therefore,
supply chain management has been recognized as a strategic factor for increasing organizational
effectiveness and achieving organizational goals, such as enhancing competitiveness, providing better
customer service, and increasing profitability (Gunasekaran et al., 2001 ). Companies need green supply
chain management because the processes and activities involved in sourcing raw materials, manufacturing
products, and delivering services to customers all depend on effective supply chain management. The
primary objective of s upply chain management is to enhance supply chain performance. Using accurate
supply chain information, producers can manufacture and deliver products that meet market demand,
while the supply chain system helps both producers and sellers reduce unnecessar y activities (Rajabipoor
Meybodi et al., 2021). The use of information technology contributes to development across all areas of
an organization and has a significant impact on supply chain management and organizational performance,
leading to new opportunities (Gunasekaran et al., 2001). Supply chain management can be defined as the
strategic coordination of activities within an organization and among business partners throughout the
supply chain, with the aim of improving performance at both the organizational and green supply chain
levels (Li et al., 2006).
2.2. Supply Chain
Today, dynamic business environments, rapid market changes, increasing competition among
businesses, and sudden shifts in demand have made organizational success more dependent than ever on
supply chain strength and resilience. This highlights the need for  a dynamic assessment of all aspects of
supply chain performance (Khanzadi et al., 2023). Such changes have compelled organizations to compete
through their supply chains, making the supply chain an important tool for gaining competitive advantage
and enhancing global competitiveness ( Mani et al., 2016 ). Moreover, global supply chains are highly
complex and face various uncertainties ( Kshetri, 2018). Similarly, many contemporary supply chains still
rely heavily on centralized structures ( Saberi et al., 2019 ). Furthermore, business functions and
geographical boundaries are often connected through extensive partner transaction networks. This
increases the transfer of vulnerabilities across supply chains and may lead to inefficient structural waste
(Min, 2019).
2.3. Green Supply Chain
The term “green supply chain” was introduced by researchers at the University of Michigan in
1996 to address environmental issues within production processes (Kannan et al., 2013). Since then, many
scholars have examined various aspects of supply chains. Over time, as awareness of environmental
protection increased, government regulations became stricter, and customers became more conscious of
green practices and their economic benefits, green supply chain management gained importance, ultimately

leading to the widespread adoption of green supply chains (Ghorbanpour et al., 2017). The implementation
of green supply chain management and sustainable logistics not only contributes to mitigating
environmental impacts but may also act as an unconscious catalyst for impulsive purchasing behaviors
among consumers. In this context, custom ers' perception of a brand's environmental stewardship can
inadvertently fuel spontaneous, affect-driven buying decisions (Farokhi et al., 2024). A green supply chain
not only contributes to environmental improvement by reducing the use of hazardous chemicals, energy
consumption, production waste, and air pollution, but also enhances economic performance and creates
competitive advantages (Rajabipoor Meybodi et al., 2021). Furthermore, the green supply chain provides
opportunities for stakeholders concerned with sustainable consumption and environmental performance
(Tseng et al., 2019).
The effective use of human resource management practices facilitates the achievement of green
supply chain objectives, whereas inadequate human resource management may hinder the recruitment of
competent employees and create barriers to achieving supply ch ain goals (Jabbour & de Sousa Jabbour,
2016). In addition, green human resource management positively influences both internal and external
green supply chain practices and improves organizational sustainability performance ( Zaid et al., 2018 ).
Among the factors that hinder environmental performance, the lack of a tangible competitive advantage
resulting from the implementation of green supply chain practices has the greatest impact and ranks first
(Amirkhani & Gozmeh, 2021 ). Furthermore, the green supply chain plays a mediating role in the
relationship between strategic orientation and organizational performance and directly influences
organizational performance (Lashgari & Hamidi, 2017).

2.4. Green Supply Chain Management
Owing to the increase in environmental issues and concerns, along with strict regulations and rising
public environmental awareness, manufacturers have begun to design products to improve environmental
performance. Consequently, companies have implemented green supply chain management, which
integrates environmental considerations into supply chain management ( Kim & Hemmert, 2016). Green
supply chain management refers to planning and implementing practices aimed at reducing the
environmental impacts of production processes and environmental management activities, as well as
facilitating the exchange of information to learn about ea ch other's operational plans and set goals for
environmental improvement. Companies directly engage with suppliers and customers, strengthening
cooperation to reduce the environmental impacts associated with material flows in green supply chain
management (Tseng et al., 2019). In addition, some scholars have identified green supply chain activities
as including green purchasing, green design, repair recycling, and remanufacturing. They also argue that
cooperation with customers and green suppliers can help organizations achie ve their green supply chain
objectives ( Agi & Nishant, 2017 ). In essence, green supply chain management provides solutions for
reducing the environmental impact of organizations and promotes synergy and productivity among supply
chain partners. This has led to a reduction in environmental performance issues, waste, and costs, and has
attracted the attention of both researchers and practitioners in operations supply chain management
(Karimi & Iranian, 2016).
A study found that green supply chain management has a positive effect on economic performance,
and the impact of green supply chain management on customer relationship governance was confirmed.
In addition, customer relationship governance positively infl uences economic performance. Therefore,
customer relationship governance plays a moderating role in the relationship between green supply chain
management and economic performance. Furthermore, managers can strengthen relationships with
customers by enhanc ing knowledge capabilities, improving employee skills, and promoting experience
development through training programs and the utilization of experienced personnel (Asgari et al., 2020).
Green supply chain management incorporates environmental requirements throughout all stages of the
supply chain, including product design, raw material selection and procurement, production and
manufacturing, distribution and transportation processes, d elivery to customers, and post -consumption,
recycling, and reuse. This aims to maximize energy and resource efficiency while improving the economic
performance across the entire supply chain (Pakfetrat & Behboodi, 2023).

Another study found that green entrepreneurship (Kannan et al., 2013; Laari et al., 2016; Mirbarg

ventures aimed at generating profits while minimizing negative environmental impacts, and it can
positively influence the green supply chain management of products and servic es. In addition, market
orientation can positively affect green supply chain management by promoting supply chain greening and
incorporating environmental criteria or considerations throughout green supply chain activities (Pakfetrat
& Behboodi, 2023).
Knowledge management can positively influence green supply chain management ( Rajabi Farjad
& Matieian Najjar, 2018). It contributes to green supply chain management through the direct participation
of companies with suppliers and customers in planning to reduce the environmental impacts of production
processes, improve environmental management, and exchange information  to learn about each other’s
operational plans and establish goals for environmental improvement. These activities strengthen
collaboration among supply chain partners and help reduce the environmental impacts associated with
material flow in green supply chain management (Pakfetrat & Behboodi, 2023 ). Human resource
management has a positive effect on green supply chain management, whereas green production
innovation plays a fully mediating role in the relationship between green human resource management and
green supply chain management ( Kara & Edinsel, 2023 ). Human resource management encourages
employees to adopt environmental ideologies and standards, facilitating the implementation of internal
and external green supply chain management practices (Nejati et al., 2017).
Green supply chain management provides organizations with several benefits, including an
enhanced corporate image, improved brand reputation, increased productivity, and greater employee
participation ( Mishra, 2017 ). However, internal green supply chain management practices may not
immediately generate social benefits, such as improved employee morale, loyalty, and customer satisfaction.
This is because the social dimension of sustainable performance is not yet fully recognized by society and
customers. Internal green supply chain management practices may not have an immediate impact on the
organization’s image among employees, suppliers, and customers. Nevertheless, they can positively affect
the economic and environmental dimensions of an organization’s performance. In addition, internal green
supply chain factors may not significantly influence social performance in the short term, but they can
produce positive long-term effects (Ghollami & Nazeri, 2018; Zaid et al., 2018). In addition, green supply
chain management, through the effective use of resources and data, can reduce costs throughout the
product life cycle, promote innovation, and prevent resource waste. It also contributes to improved
product quality and more efficient processes while supporting the development of new products ( Carter
et al., 2000; Yang, 2012; Zhu et al., 2012).
There are several barriers to the implementation of green supply chain management, including a
lack of proper understanding of existing conditions among supply chain participants ( Mathiyazhagan et
al., 2013 ) and insufficient training ( Govindan et al., 2014 ). Another obstacle is the lack of employee
competency in green supply chain practices (Yazdani et al., 2015). However, one of the key success factors
in green supply chain management is the exchange of environmentally friendly knowledge between
producers (Wang et al., 2017 ). Activities related to green supply chain management are influenced by
employee training and the knowledge gained from it; however, this knowledge must be effectively applied
to be useful (Agi & Nishant, 2017). In addition to technology transfer, green innovation can facilitate the
development of green supply chain management (Bag et al., 2022). It has been suggested that green supply
chain management does not directly affect export performance; however, through the mediating role of
environmental performance, it has a positive indirect effect on exports ( Zakipour Bahambari & Oushak
Saraei, 2019 ). Export behavior is essential for companies, and managers must identify the factors
influencing exports, including green supply chain management and environmental performance, and
consider export development as a strategic priority (Kim & Hemmert, 2016).
The results show that technological and operational compatibility has no significant impact on
green supply chain management. However, the network infrastructure and information technology

3. METHODOLOGY
Research methods, regardless of their underlying paradigms and classification into quantitative,
qualitative, or mixed approaches, play a significant role in human life and scientific progress as tools for
conducting applied research and expanding the boun daries of knowledge. Researchers must adopt and
consistently adhere to appropriate research methods in their studies ( Mansourian, 2013 ). Given the
increasing complexity of research, review studies have become essential in this field. A review study
provides new insights and a comprehensive overview of existing research, synthesizing a large body of
literature so that readers are not requi red to examine each individual study on a specific topic separately
(Aveyard, 2010). Furthermore, a review study involves a systematic examination and interpretation of texts
related to specific topics. Therefore, in this type of study, researchers aim to identify relevant literature,
review it critically, and demonstrate their understanding of previous studies and methodologies in the field
of interest. Review studies are generally classified into narrative reviews, systematic reviews, and meta -
analyses (Aveyard, 2010; Petticrew & Roberts, 2006).
A systematic review is a type of review study that aims to comprehensively identify, evaluate, and
synthesize all available studies related to a specific topic. Systematic reviews are often conducted to test a
specific hypothesis or a set of related hypotheses (Aveyard, 2010; Petticrew & Roberts, 2006). Moreover,
unlike meta-analyses, a systematic review is structured, transparent, and reproducible to answer a specific
research question ( Williams, 2019 ). One of the key strengths of this method is that by identifying
relationships among many studies, it can address a research question in a broader and more comprehensive
way than a single empirical study (Baumeister & Leary, 1997). Systematic reviews aim to provide a balanced
synthesis of the evidence within a specific field by going beyond individual studies that are often conclusive
and may contain contradictory findings (Petticrew & Roberts, 2006).
3.1. Research Design
This study employed a systematic literature review (SLR) design to systematically identify, evaluate,
and synthesize findings from relevant previous studies. The review process was conducted in accordance
with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA 2020) guidelines
to ensure transparency, methodological rigor, and reproducibility. The research followed a structured
process involving literature identification, screening, eligibility assessment, quality evaluation, data
extraction, and synthesis of the selected studies.
3.2. Search Strategy
A comprehensive literature search was conducted across the selected academic databases,
including Scientific Information Database (SID), Silivica, Magiran, and Normags to identify relevant
studies published between 2016 and 2026. The search strategy was de veloped using a combination of
keywords related to Supply Chain, Green Supply Chain, Supply Chain Management and Green Supply
Chain Management. Both the full terms and their commonly used abbreviations were considered. Searches
were conducted within the Title, Abstract, and Keywords fields to maximize the identification of relevant
studies. Boolean operators, including “OR” and “AND,” were used to combine and refine the search
terms (Table 1).

3.3. Eligibility Criteria
To ensure the relevance and consistency of the selected studies, predefined inclusion and exclusion
criteria were established before the screening process. Studies were included if they were published
between 2016 and 2026, were directly related to Green Supply Chain Management, were published as peer-
reviewed journal articles, and provided sufficient information to support data extraction and analysis.
Studies were excluded if they were published outside the specified time period, were not directly
relevant to GSCM, were duplicate records, or did not provide sufficient information for the purposes of
the review.
3.4. Study Selection
The study selection process was conducted through a multi-stage screening procedure. The initial
search using the term “Supply Chain” identified 343 sources. During the initial screening, 95 sources were
removed due to duplicate records, irrelevant titles, and studies outside the defined research scope.
Subsequently, searches using the terms “Supply Chain Management” and “Green Supply Chain”
identified 99 and 32 sources, respectively. These records were further screened based on their titles,
abstracts, and keywords. A total of 53 sources were excluded because they were duplicates, did not address
the required concepts, or lacked sufficient information in the title, abstract, or keywords to justify further
assessment. Following this stage, 48 supply chain management related and 30 green supply chain relate d
sources were retained for further evaluation.
In the final screening stage, the remaining 78 sources were examined in greater detail to identify
studies directly addressing Green Supply Chain Management. A total of 68 sources were excluded because
they focused primarily on conventional supply chain ma nagement, addressed isolated green practices
without a clear green supply chain management focus, lacked sufficient methodological or empirical
information, or did not adequately correspond to the objectives of the review. Finally, 10 studies that
satisfied the predefined eligibility criteria and directly addressed GSCM were included in the final synthesis
(Figure 1).

3.5. Quality Assessment
To ensure methodological rigor and trustworthiness of the evidence included in this systematic
review, all ten eligible studies were independently assessed using the Mixed Methods Appraisal Tool
(MMAT). The MMAT was selected because it provides a structure d framework for evaluating the
methodological quality and potential risk of bias across different study designs. The appraisal focused on
the appropriateness of the research design, sampling strategy, data collection procedures, data analysis,
and the coherence between the research objectives and methodological approach.
Each of the ten included studies was systematically assessed against the relevant MMAT criteria
according to its methodological design. The assessment results were recorded using the categories “Yes,”
“No,” and “Can’t tell,” allowing methodological strengt hs and potential limitations to be identified
transparently. The appraisal was conducted prior to evidence synthesis, and the findings were considered
when interpreting and integrating the evidence across the included studies.
Overall, the MMAT assessment indicated that the included studies demonstrated an acceptable
level of methodological rigor and provided sufficient methodological information to support their
inclusion in the evidence synthesis. No study was excluded solely on the basis of methodological quality;
instead, the appraisal results were used to inform the interpretation of findings and to ensure that
conclusions were appropriately grounded in the strength and limitations of the available evidence.

3.6. Date Extraction
A standardized data extraction framework was developed to systematically record and organize the
relevant information from the included studies. The extracted data included the study number, researchers,
publication year, research title, study design, samp le, industry or research context, variables, analytical
methodology, main findings, and quality/risk of bias assessment. This structured approach facilitated
systematic comparison of the selected studies and ensured consistency throughout the data extracti on
process.

3.7.  Thematic Analysis and Categorization
Following the data extraction process, the findings of the included studies were systematically
reviewed and grouped according to similarities in concepts, relationships, mechanisms, and outcomes. An
inductive thematic analysis approach was used to identify recurring patterns across the studies and organize
them into broader themes and related categories.
A total of 37 initial findings were identified from the included studies. Following a process of
comparison, consolidation, and thematic screening, 19 key findings were selected for further synthesis.
These findings were subsequently organized into 14 cate gories, which were further consolidated into 5
overarching themes based on conceptual similarities and relationships among the findings.
This process enabled the integration of findings from different studies while preserving the main
concepts identified in the literature. The final themes, categories, and corresponding findings are presented

3.8.  Data Synthesis
The findings of this review indicate that green supply chain management is not limited to a set of
environmental activities but involves a broad range of practices across different organizational and supply
chain functions. The reviewed studies highlight t he importance of internal environmental management,
resource management, energy consumption and optimization, waste management, training and research,
culture building, green purchasing, green design, and reverse logistics in supporting green performance. In
particular, internal green supply chain management can improve the use of organizational data and
resources while reducing costs and resource waste. The findings also point to the complementary role of
green innovation, as both green innovation and GSCM  have been found to positively affect green
performance. Taken together, these findings suggest that the effectiveness of GSCM depends on the
extent to which green practices are incorporated into different operational and managerial activities rather
than being treated as isolated environmental initiatives.
The first theme, Green Supply Chain Management Practices, was therefore developed to capture
the practices through which GSCM is implemented. The categories of Internal Green Practices, Green
Purchasing and Green Design, and Green Innovation Practices were distinguished because they represent
different but closely connected aspects of green supply chain implementation. Internal practices focus
mainly on resource and environmental management within the organization, while green purchasing,
design, construction, and reverse logistics extend green considerations across supply chain activities. Green
innovation was considered separately because the findings associate innovation with improvements in
green performance and with the effectiveness of GSCM efforts. Th is categorization helps clarify how
different types of green practices contribute to the broader implementation of GSCM.   The findings
further indicate that both internal and external green supply chain management contribute to
environmental, economic, and social performance. However, an important distinction is observed in
relation to social performance: while external green  supply chain management has an effect on social
performance, internal green supply chain management does not show a significant effect. This suggests
that the location of green practices within or outside the organizational boundary may influence the type
of performance outcomes achieved (Figure 2).

3.9. Rigor and Quality Assurance
Several procedures were implemented to enhance the rigor, consistency, and transparency of the
review process.

3.9.1. Transparent Study Selection
The study identification and selection process was systematically documented, including the stages
of screening, eligibility assessment, exclusion, and final inclusion. The selection process was presented
through a study selection flow diagram to improve transparency.
3.9.2. Standardized Data Extraction
A structured data extraction framework was used to ensure that the same types of information
were consistently extracted from all included studies. The framework covered study characteristics,
methodological information, variables, analytical methods, quality assessment, and main findings.
3.9.3. Study-Level Quality Appraisal
The methodological quality of each included study was assessed according to its research design
using the MMAT. This assessment helped evaluate the methodological rigor and potential risk of bias
across the included studies.
3.9.4. Independent Checking and Consensus
The extracted information and methodological assessments were reviewed for consistency, and
discrepancies were resolved through discussion and consensus to minimize potential errors in the review
process.
3.9.5. Transparent Narrative and Thematic Synthesis
The synthesis process was conducted systematically by comparing findings across studies,
identifying recurring patterns, and organizing related findings into categories and broader themes. The
basis for the development of themes and categories was document ed to ensure that the final synthesis
remained traceable to the extracted evidence.

5. DISCUSSION
5.1. Interpretation of the Main Findings
The findings confirm that Green Supply Chain Management (GSCM) is a multidimensional
approach that can enhance organizational performance across economic, environmental, and operational
dimensions, ultimately contributing to sustainable competitive advanta ge. Rather than functioning as a
single operational practice, GSCM appears to involve a combination of internal capabilities, supply -side
practices, organizational orientations, and human factors that collectively support sustainability and
competitiveness.
Among the identified dimensions, green purchasing and green innovation emerge as particularly
important practices, enabling firms to integrate environmental considerations into both upstream supply
activities and internal organizational processes. The find ings further indicate that market orientation and
knowledge management orientation can act as important mechanisms through which green

entrepreneurship is translated into effective GSCM practices. This suggests that the successful
implementation of GSCM depends not only on adopting environmental practices but also on the
organization's ability to acquire, manage, and apply market- and knowledge-related resources.
The findings also reveal that social capital can play a dual role in GSCM implementation, serving
as both a facilitator and a potential constraint depending on the organizational context. In contrast, cultural
compatibility emerges as an important enabling factor for successful implementation. Furthermore, human
resource management and green training are essential for developing employee awareness, competencies,
and engagement while reducing barriers to the adoption of green practices.
Overall, the findings suggest that competitive advantage from GSCM does not result from isolated
green practices alone. Rather, it emerges from the interaction between green supply chain practices,
organizational capabilities, knowledge and market orientat ions, organizational culture, and human
resources. This integrated perspective provides a basis for understanding how GSCM can be transformed
from an environmental initiative into a strategic source of sustainable competitive advantage.
5.2. Comparison with Previous Literature
The findings of this study are largely consistent with previous literature on green supply chain
management (GSCM). The results confirm that GSCM practices, including green purchasing, green
innovation, internal environmental management, waste and energy management, green design, and reverse
logistics, contribute positively to green, environmental, economic, and organizational performance. These
findings support previous studies showing that GSCM can improve efficiency, reduce resource waste and
costs, enhance environmental performance, and contribute to sustainable competitive advantage.
The findings also reinforce the importance of organizational capabilities in implementing GSCM.
Consistent with previous research, green training, knowledge management, human resource management,
innovation, organizational culture, and market orientation were identified as important factors supporting
GSCM implementation. In particular, employee awareness, skills development, knowledge sharing, and
green innovation can strengthen both internal and external green supply chain practices. The findings
further agree with previous studies that cultural compatibility facilitates GSCM, whereas technological and
operational compatibility may not necessarily have a significant effect.
However, the findings also highlight that GSCM implementation is influenced by both internal
and external barriers, including financial constraints, technological limitations, insufficient training, cultural
resistance, and inadequate managerial support. O verall, the results are in line with the existing literature
and further demonstrate that GSCM is not only an environmental initiative but also an important strategic
approach for improving organizational performance, operational effectiveness, and long -term
sustainability.
5.3. Theoretical Implications
The findings contribute to the GSCM literature by highlighting its multidimensional nature and its
relationship with economic, environmental, and operational performance. They also emphasize the
importance of green purchasing, green innovation, knowledge m anagement, market orientation, human
resource management, and organizational culture in explaining effective GSCM implementation.
5.4. Practical and Managerial Implications
The findings suggest that managers should adopt GSCM through integrated green practices rather
than isolated activities. Particular attention should be given to green purchasing, green innovation,
employee training, knowledge management, and organizational culture to improve GSCM implementation
and organizational performance.
6. CONCLUSION
The findings indicate that green purchasing and green design encourage organizations to place
greater emphasis on environmentally compatible raw materials. In particular, the consideration of
recyclable materials and materials that contribute to extending product life can reduce the environmental

more efficient resource utilization.
The results further demonstrate that the effective implementation of green supply chain
management is highly dependent on employees’ environmental awareness and capabilities. Even when
organizations adopt various green practices, their effective implementa tion may be constrained in the
absence of knowledgeable and adequately trained employees. Green training can enhance employees’

appropriate organizational culture is important for embedding environmental cons iderations into
employees’ attitudes and daily practices.
The findings also highlight the role of green entrepreneurship in identifying and exploiting business
opportunities that contribute to environmental protection or address environmental challenges. Moreover,
green innovation can strengthen organizational co mpetitiveness by facilitating the development of
environmentally friendly products and the adoption of more sustainable production processes. The results
suggest that green innovation can contribute to improvements across different stages of the supply chain

Overall, the findings suggest that effective green supply chain management can generate
improvements not only in environmental performance but also in economic and social performance.
Green purchasing and green design encourage more careful selection of ra w materials, while reverse
logistics contributes to waste reduction and more efficient resource utilization. At the same time, green
innovation supports improvements in products and production processes. From a social perspective, these
practices can contr ibute to safer and more favorable working conditions, while green training enhances
employees’ knowledge, capabilities, and environmental competencies. Thus, the simultaneous
implementation of these practices can support a more sustainable supply chain by creating environmental
