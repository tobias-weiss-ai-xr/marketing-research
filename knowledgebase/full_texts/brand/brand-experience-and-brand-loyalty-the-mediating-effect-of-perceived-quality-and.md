ABSTRACT
The fast-fashion industry continues to grow rapidly amid increasing market competition.
Although market expansion continues, companies often face challenges in maintaining long-term
brand loyalty amid the abundance of brand alternatives. This study aims to examine how brand
experience affects brand loyalty, with perceived quality and brand trust as mediating variables. This
quantitative study collected data through surveys of 200 UNIQLO customers in the Special Region
of Yogyakarta and analyzed the data using Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) with AMOS 24.
The research results indicate that brand experience has a significant direct impact on brand loyalty.
This study reinforces the understanding that loyalty is not only shaped directly by physical and
emotional experiences but is also significantly mediated by perceptions of product quality (partial
mediation). However, a unique finding was that brand trust cannot act as a mediator in this
relationship in the Special Region of Yogyakarta market. This finding provides insights for fast-
fashion retailers on designing marketing strategies that focus on enhancing in-store experiences and
product quality consistency as key to creating sustainable brand loyalty amidst intense market
competition.

Keywords: Brand Experience, Brand Loyalty, Brand Trust, Perceived Quality.

INTRODUCTION
In recent years, the global business environment has experienced profound
transformation driven by globalization and rapid digitalization, fundamentally changing
how consumers perceive and evaluate brands (Balchandani et al., 2024). Purchasing
decisions are no longer based solely on functional attributes, price, or product design;
instead, consumers increasingly prioritize the overall experience created through their
interactions with a brand, making brand experience a key determinant of consumer
perceptions, attitudes, and behavioral intentions (Fernandes, 2024). This trend is
especially evident in the fashion industry, where digital technology, social media, and
evolving lifestyles accelerate trend diffusion while encouraging consumers to express their
identities through fashion consumption (Frade, 2025). Consequently, fashion companies
must deliver consistent brand experiences, compelling narratives, and meaningful
emotional connections across multiple touchpoints to sustain competitiveness in an
increasingly dynamic global market.
One global fashion brand that has successfully adapted to these changes is UNIQLO,
a Japanese retailer under Fast Retailing Co., Ltd. Through its LifeWear concept,
UNIQLO emphasizes functional innovation, minimalist design, and efficient supply
chain management to create long-term value for consumers (Mirza et al., 2020).
According to Fast Retailing Co. (2025b), UNIQLO achieved global sales of 3.10 trillion
yen (approximately USD 20.2 billion) in fiscal year 2024, reflecting a growth rate of
12.2%. In Southeast Asia, including Indonesia, UNIQLO continues to expand its

In competitive environments, consumers tend to switch brands when alternative

reinforces the importance of managing brand experience, perceived quality, and brand
trust as key drivers of sustainable competitiveness. Fernandes (2024) further emphasizes
that consumers increasingly prioritize authentic and consistent experiences over short-
term price-based incentives.
Brand experience is shaped by multiple elements, including store atmosphere, service
quality, product presentation, and symbolic brand meaning (Brun et al., 2017). Both
online and offline emotional engagements further strengthen long-term relationships
between consumers and brands (Hoang et al., 2023). Brakus et al. (2009) state that brand
experience includes behavioral, emotional, intellectual, and sensory aspects that affect
consumer reactions. These encounters help build perceived quality and brand trust, which
in turn influence loyalty and repurchase intentions. Thus, brand experience is positioned
by Akoglu and Özbek (2022) as a crucial precondition for long-term client loyalty.
While brand trust demonstrates customers’ faith in a company’s dependability and
consistency, perceived quality reveals consumers’ overall assessment of a brand’s
superiority. Both concepts are frequently seen as essential mechanisms that connect brand
loyalty and brand experience. According to earlier research, favorable brand experiences
boost perceived quality and trust, which in turn reinforce loyalty intentions (Pratiwi et al.,
2021). Empirical results, however, continue to be contradictory. According to some
research, brand experience has a significant direct and indirect impact on brand loyalty
through perceived quality and brand trust (Akoglu & Özbek, 2022). However, depending
on the industry and context, other studies report varying or negligible mediating effects
(Ridhwan et al., 2021; Ogawa & Cuandra, 2022). These discrepancies point to a lack of
knowledge on the precise processes by which brand experience results in consumer
loyalty, especially in fast-fashion markets in developing nations like Indonesia.
Based on this gap, this study aims to examine the influence of brand experience on
brand loyalty with perceived quality and brand trust as mediating variables in the context
of UNIQLO customers in Yogyakarta. Specifically, this research seeks to analyze the
direct and indirect relationships among these variables and to provide empirical evidence
regarding the dominant mechanisms shaping brand loyalty in the fast-fashion industry.

The Effect of Brand Experience
Brand experience refers to consumers’ sensory, emotional, cognitive, and behavioral
responses to brand-related stimuli, including product design, communication, visual
identity, and the brand environment (Brakus et al., 2009). As a source of brand value, it
differentiates brands while shaping perceptions of product and service quality through
direct interactions (Pina & Dias, 2021). Positive and consistent experiences strengthen
quality perceptions, reduce uncertainty when quality cannot be assessed before purchase,
and reinforce favorable evaluations across multiple brand touchpoints (Jeon & Yoo, 2021;
Dewasandra et al., 2025). Empirical evidence supports this relationship, with Bae and

Similarly, brand experience plays a crucial role in developing consumer trust, as trust

Brand experience plays an important role in fostering long-term brand loyalty by

experiences strengthen attachment, enhance satisfaction, reduce perceived risk, and foster
enduring consumer-brand relationships (Bae & Kim, 2023; Afiftama & Nasir, 2024).
Empirical evidence consistently supports this relationship. Mostafa and Kasamani (2021)

Ogawa and Cuandra (2022) reported that consumers with favorable brand experiences
exhibit stronger loyalty. Similar findings were confirmed by Budi et al. (2021) and Sarmad
et al. (2024), demonstrating that positive brand experience is an important antecedent of
long-term brand loyalty.

The Effect of Perceived Quality and Brand Trust on Brand Loyalty
Perceived quality represents consumers’ overall evaluation of a product or brand’s
excellence relative to competing alternatives and is formed through subjective assessments
based on prior experiences, expectations, and available quality cues rather than objective
attributes (Zeithaml, 1988; Kim, 2012). Because consumers often cannot fully evaluate
quality before purchase, they rely on brand-related information and previous experiences
to reduce uncertainty, while product performance, service quality, brand atmosphere, and
design further shape these perceptions (Ribeiro & Prayag, 2018; Solin & Curry, 2023). As
positive quality perceptions accumulate, consumers become more confident in
repurchasing, perceive lower risk, and are less likely to switch to competitors, thereby
strengthening brand loyalty (Robertson et al., 2022; Yuan et al., 2024; Saktiana, 2025).
Empirical studies consistently support this relationship, showing that perceived quality
positively influences brand loyalty by enhancing evaluations of brand superiority and
long-term commitment (Taqi & Muhammad, 2020; Lacap et al., 2021; Akoglu & Özbek,
2022).
Brand trust refers to consumers’ confidence that a brand is reliable, credible, and
capable of consistently fulfilling its promises, which develops through repeated positive
experiences and reinforces expectations of future performance (Chaudhuri & Holbrook,
2001; Delgado-Ballester, 2004). As trust strengthens, consumers become more willing to
rely on the brand, perceive lower purchase risk, and maintain long-term relationships
despite competing alternatives, making brand trust a key driver of customer commitment
and continued patronage (Masud et al., 2024; Pratama et al., 2025). Empirical evidence
consistently supports this relationship. Lacap and Tungcab (2020) found that brand trust
significantly enhances brand loyalty, while Ogawa and Cuandra (2022) reported that
consumers with higher brand trust exhibit stronger loyalty. Similar positive effects have
also been confirmed by Budi et al. (2021), Maduretno and Junaedi (2022), and
Nizharikhutama and Murwanti (2024).

Perceived Quality and Brand Trust as a Mediating Variable
Positive brand experiences enhance consumers’ evaluations of product and service
quality, which subsequently strengthen brand loyalty (Oliver, 1999; Russell-Bennett &
Parkinson, 2014). Perceived quality serves as an important cognitive mechanism that
translates positive brand experiences into loyal consumer behavior. Favorable
interactions with a brand enable consumers to evaluate product performance more
positively, resulting in stronger perceptions of quality (Eslami, 2020). As consumers
become increasingly convinced that a brand consistently delivers superior products and
services, they are more likely to maintain long-term relationships and continue purchasing
from the same brand (Akoglu & Özbek, 2022; Wilcox et al., 2024). This process suggests
that brand experience strengthens loyalty indirectly by first enhancing consumers’ quality
evaluations (Paswan et al., 2003). Empirical evidence supports this mechanism.  The
relationship between brand experience and brand loyalty was mediated by perceptions.
Likewise, Dewasandra et al. (2025) reported that consumers’ quality evaluations
constitute an important pathway through which positive brand experiences encourage
loyal behavior. These findings indicate that favorable brand experiences reinforce
consumers’ perceptions of quality, which subsequently strengthen brand loyalty.
Brand trust also represents an important relational mechanism linking brand
experience to brand loyalty (Delgado-Ballester, 2004; Hegner & Jevons, 2016). Repeated
positive experiences strengthen consumers’ confidence in a brand’s reliability, credibility,
and ability to consistently fulfill its promises (Lacap & Tungcab, 2020).  As trust develops,
consumers perceive lower levels of uncertainty and become more willing to maintain
enduring relationships with the brand (Durmaz, 2022). Consequently, positive brand
experiences may influence loyalty indirectly by fostering stronger brand trust before
encouraging long-term commitment (Tong & Feng, 2023; Griffin et al., 2025). Empirical
studies provide evidence for this mechanism. Pratiwi et al. (2021) found that trust in a
brand mediated the relationship. Similarly, Ridhwan et al. (2021) reported that positive
brand experiences strengthen consumers’ trust, which subsequently promotes higher
levels of brand loyalty.

H7: Brand trust mediates the effect of brand experience on brand loyalty.

RESEARCH METHODS
The research methodology used in this study was quantitative because it is appropriate
for testing the causal relationships among latent variables through statistical modeling.
Customers of UNIQLO in the Special Region of Yogyakarta, Indonesia, served as the
subjects of the study, which concentrated on the UNIQLO brand. The primary data used
in this study came directly from respondents via an online survey. The indicators for the
research variables brand experience, perceived quality, brand trust, and brand loyalty
were used to create the questionnaire. A five-point Likert scale, with 1 representing
“strongly disagree” and 5 representing “strongly agree,” was used to evaluate each
measurement item.
This study examined four variables: brand experience, perceived quality, brand trust,
and brand loyalty. All measurement items were adapted from established scales in
previous studies. Brand experience was measured using 15 items covering the sensory,
affective, behavioral, and intellectual dimensions adopted from Bae and Kim (2023).
Perceived quality was assessed using six items reflecting product reliability, performance,
conformance, durability, fitness for purpose, and brand superiority, adapted from Akoglu
and Özbek (2022). Brand trust was measured with four items representing credibility,
reliability, integrity, and security, also adapted from Akoglu and Özbek (2022). Finally,
brand loyalty was measured using four items capturing brand preference, repu rchase
intention, commitment, and willingness to recommend the brand, following Mostafa and
Kasamani (2021).
Non-probability purposive sampling was the sampling method used in this study,
where participants were chosen according to predetermined standards (Sekaran & Bougie,
2017). Respondents had to meet three requirements: (1) live in Yogyakarta ’s Special
Region; (2) be at least 17 years old; and (3) have at least two UNIQLO purchases.
According to Ghozali (2017), a minimum sample size of 200 respondents is advised for
Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) analysis employing the Maximum Likelihood
Estimation (MLE) approach.
Data were collected using a Google Forms questionnaire distributed through email,
Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp. The analysis began with respondents ’
demographic profiling to describe the sample characteristics. Subsequently, the
measurement model was evaluated through validity and reliability tests, including
standardized factor loadings, Construct Reliability (CR), and Average Variance Extracted
(AVE). The structural model was then assessed using goodness-of-fit indices, including
Chi-square/df, GFI, AGFI, CFI, TLI, RMSEA, and SRMR, to determine the model’s
adequacy. Finally, hypothesis testing was conducted using Structural Equation Modeling
(SEM) with AMOS 24 to examine both direct and indirect relationships among variables,
including the mediating roles of perceived quality and brand trust in the relatio nship
between brand experience and brand loyalty.

DISCUSSION
The results show that brand experience has a positive and significant effect on
perceived quality. Positive consumer experiences with UNIQLO, including store
atmosphere, service quality, product display, and technologies such as Airism and
Heattech, enhance consumers’ perceptions of product quality and functionality. These
findings emphasize the role of sensory and emotional experiences in shaping quality
evaluations. The results are consistent with Ahmadian et al. (2023) and Rahayu and
Ruswanti (2024), as well as Eslami (2020), Jeon and Yoo (2021), Akoglu and Özbek
(2022), and Bae and Jeon (2022), which similarly confirm that brand experience
significantly improves perceived quality across contexts.
The findings show that brand experience has a positive and significant effect on brand
trust. Positive experiences with UNIQLO enhance consumers’ confidence in the brand’s
reliability and consistency. Consistent product quality, service standards, and shopping
experiences across outlets strengthen a trustworthy brand image, while repeated positive
experiences further reinforce consumers’ belief that the brand fulfills its promises and
expectations. These findings are supported by Pratiwi et al. (2021), Na et al. (2023), and
Firdausiah et al. (2024). Similar results are also reported by Mekebbaty et al. (2020),
Lacap and Tungcab (2020), and Durmaz (2022), confirming that brand experience
significantly enhances brand trust.
The results show that brand experience has a positive and significant effect on brand
loyalty. Positive interactions with UNIQLO enhance long-term loyalty, as memorable
shopping experiences, product comfort, and emotional engagement increase repurchase
intention and brand preference. These findings indicate that UNIQLO has created
meaningful experiences that strengthen emotional attachment beyond functional benefits.
These results are consistent with Angeline (2023), Bae and Kim (2023), Widiyantoro and
Kuswati (2024), and Sarmad et al. (2024). Similar findings are also reported by Ogawa
and Cuandra (2022), Eslami (2020), Santos and Schlesinger (2021), and Mostafa and
Kasamani (2021), confirming that brand experience significantly influences brand loyalty.
The findings show that perceived quality has a positive and significant effect on brand
loyalty. Consumers who perceive UNIQLO products as high quality are more likely to
remain loyal, as positive evaluations of durability, material quality, functionality, and
overall performance encourage continued purchase intention. These findings indicate that
UNIQLO’s focus on consistent product quality through its LifeWear concept has
successfully strengthened brand loyalty in the Yogyakarta market. The results are
supported by Eslami (2020) and Ogawa and Cuandra (2022), who identify perceived

The results show that brand trust does not have a significant effect on brand loyalty.
This indicates that although consumers trust UNIQLO’s reliability and credibility, trust

a basic expectation, while consumers face many competing brands with similar reliability.
This finding aligns with Rather et al. (2021), but contradicts Ridhwan et al. (2021),
Maduretno and Junaedi (2022), Ogawa and Cuandra (2022), and Masud et al. (2024),
who found that brand trust positively affects brand loyalty. The results suggest that in fast-
fashion markets, perceived quality and experience play a more dominant role in driving
loyalty than trust.
The results show that perceived quality significantly mediates the relationship between
brand experience and brand loyalty, indicating that positive brand experiences enhance
loyalty both directly and indirectly by improving consumers’ perceptions of product
quality. Positive shopping experiences and product usage lead consumers to perceive
UNIQLO products as higher in quality, which in turn strengthens their loyalty. These
findings are supported by Eslami (2020), Akoglu and Özbek (2022), and Dewasandra et
al. (2025). In contrast, brand trust does not significantly mediate the relationship between
brand experience and brand loyalty, suggesting that although positive experiences may
increase trust, it is not sufficient to strengthen loyalty in this context. This result aligns
with Rather et al. (2021) but contradicts Lacap and Tungcab (2020), Ridhwan et al.
(2021), and Pratiwi et al. (2021). The findings indicate that in the fast-fashion context,
perceived quality plays a more dominant mediating role than brand trust in translating
brand experience into brand loyalty.

CONCLUSION
This study concludes that brand experience is the key determinant of UNIQLO brand
loyalty in Yogyakarta. This relationship is strengthened by perceived quality, which
partially mediates the effect, indicating that positive consumer experiences lead to loyalty
only when they are accompanied by strong perceptions of product quality. In contrast,
brand trust does not mediate the relationship, suggesting that in the fast-fashion industry,
trust is relatively passive and does not directly translate into repeat purchase behavior.
These findings support the Customer-Based Brand Equity framework by emphasizing
that perceived quality plays a more dominant role than brand trust in building loyalty.
Brand loyalty is formed through the interaction of emotional experiences and functional
evaluations, while trust does not always function as an effective pathway in dynamic and
competitive markets. The results suggest that managers should prioritize an Experience-
to-Quality strategy by integrating brand experience with tangible product quality,
particularly through material innovation and product comfort in every consumer
touchpoint. Efforts to build brand trust should also be made more concrete, for example
through loyalty programs that provide direct and measurable benefits to encourage repeat
purchases.
This study has several limitations. The sample is limited to UNIQLO customers in
Yogyakarta, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to other regions or retail
contexts. In addition, the study only focuses on brand experience, perceived quality, and
brand trust, without considering other relevant variables such as brand love or social
influence. The use of a cross-sectional design also limits the ability to capture changes in
consumer behavior over time. For future research, it is recommended to include brand
love as a mediating variable and to examine brand trust as a potential moderator between
brand love and brand loyalty in order to better understand loyalty formation in the fast-
fashion industry.
