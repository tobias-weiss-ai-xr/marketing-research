1. Introduction

Purchase intention is a frequently studied concept as a sign
of whether a customer will make a purchase. It reflects an
individual's behavioral orientation toward purchasing a special
product or service and is shaped by their experiences, attitudes,
and perceptions (Ajzen, 1991). Because the concept represents
a consumer's predisposition to future consumption, a stronger
purchase intention reflects a higher likelihood of purchase
(Schiffman and Kanuk, 2004).
When defining service quality in service sectors, the gap
between purchasers' expectations and their impressions of the
service they receive is investigated. While product quality
often embodies objectivity, service quality, in contrast, is
shaped by the personal experience and evaluation of both
tangible and intangible service aspects (Parasuraman et al.,
1988). In service management, the value of interaction or the
quality of the natural surroundings are considered some of the
most critical elements during evaluation (Oliveras-Villanueva
et al., 2020). Interactions between service providers and
customers play a critical role in shaping overall evaluations of
service quality and are shaped by perceptions of intrinsic and
extrinsic value (Thuy et al., 2019; Walker et al., 2006).
Especially in competitive markets where companies strive to
differentiate themselves, higher perceived service quality can
positively influence consume rs' purchase intentions (Liao et
al., 2022; Puta and Lestari, 2023).
Another important construct in consumer decision-making
processes is trust. Trust is the individual's willingness to be
vulnerable to the actions of another interacting party, with the
expectation that the other party will act meaningfully and
beneficially (Mayer et al., 1995). The concept involves the
expectation that the confidence that the service provider will
act with integrity, predictability, and competence, will not take
advantage of one's weaknesses (Rousseau et al., 1998). Trust
is a critical need for developing long-term relationships in both
social and commercial contexts (Karhapaa et al., 2022). The
concept of trust holds a special place in the business world.
This is because trust lies at the foundation of long -term
relationships and repeated actions. In the service sector,

The study employed survey method  and the sample consisted of 400 airline passengers. The
questionnaires were completed online and were previously tested for reliability and validity.
The Service Quality (SERVQUAL) Scale, the Brand Trust Scale, and the Purchase Intention
Scales were used.
Exploratory factor analysis was practiced to the measurement tools, the factor structures were
checked and validated, and the reliability analyses demonstrated consistency. Correlation and
regression analyses revealed the following findings: Perceived service quality increases both
customer trust and purchase intention. Perception of trust partially mediates the relationship
between service quality and purchase intention. Accordingly, providing high -quality service
and fostering trust are both crucial for influencing airline consumers' purchase intentions.
This article examines service quality, trust, and purchase intention within the framework of
Saudi Arabia's evolving aviation market. The results and recommendations are believed to offer
future implications for airline management, executives, academics, and consumers.

2. Literature Review

This section includes conceptual reviews about the variables
included in the study.

2.1. Purchase intention
Purchase intention is the likelihood that a customer will
decide to buy a product or service depending on  their
perceived value of its features before purchasing (Chen et al.,
2019). The concept reflects a  human’s desire to display a
behavior and links it to a specific action before it is executed
(Ajzen, 1991). This concept is particularly critical for
marketing professionals, as it is believed to help predict future
sales and shape consumer behavior (Morwitz, 2012). Kotler
and Keller (2016) argue that purchase intention is influenced
by personal preferences and external factors and represents a
cognitive state that indicates the consumer's readiness to
purchase. In other words, consumer purchase intention is
shaped by attitudes , social norms, and behavioral beliefs
(Ajzen, 1991).
Why is purchase intention important? Because it is the
most predictive measurement of consumer purchase behavior.
Marketers use the concept to understand the factors
influencing it, meet customer demands, anticipate market
trends, and maintain competitive advantage. They can also
develop specific strategies by setting targets based on their
measurements (Morwitz, 2012). Furthermore, if purchase
intention is considered a valuable metric for market
effectiveness, it allows businesses to evaluate the success o f
their promotional campaigns and refine their approaches
accordingly (Kotler  and Keller, 2016). Furthermore,
consumers' positive perceptions of the quality and value of
their purchases increase long -term brand equity by
encouraging loyalty and repeat purchases (Chen et al., 2019;
Özçiftçi, 2017).
The Theory of Planned Behavior is widely used to
understand purchase intention (Ajzen, 1991). According to
this theory, beliefs or evaluations regarding  a behavior,
perceived social expectations, and sense of ability collectively
influence purchase intention. Attitudes reflect the purchaser's
favorable or unfavorable evaluations of a n outcome.
Subjective norms, also, refer to social pressures from family,
friends, or society. For example, in collectivist cultures like
Saudi Arabia, social influence, which refers to subjective
norms, plays a significant role in purchase decisions
(Hofstede, 2001). Perceived behavioral control, besides, refers
to the customer’s belief that they can influence a purchase
decision by being influenced by factors such as price and
availability (Ajzen, 1991).
The Gulf region is home to major airlines, such as
Emirates, Etihad Airways, and Qatar Airways, that serve as
regional benchmarks  (O'Connell and Bueno, 2018) . These
carriers have helped shape a globally recognized aviation
market spanning the Middle East and the Gulf Cooperation
Council (GCC). These airlines are competitive players on the
international stage and contribute to the region's economic
development (O'Connell and Bueno, 2018). The empirical
focus of this study is limited to the rapidly growing Saudi
Arabian airline sector, encompassing the national flag carrier,
Saudia, as well as prominent low-cost carriers like Flynas and
Flyadeal. Driven by the "Vision 2030" economic
transformation program, Saudi Arabia is strengthening its
position within the aviation industry. In 2023, the Kingdom
served over 112 million domestic and international passengers
(GACA, 2024). In this unique competit ive landscape, it is
pertinent to examine how service quality builds passenger trust
and influences purchase intentions. In the Saudi Arabian
airline sector, online brand experience and brand love
significantly strengthen customer engagement and increase
repurchase intention, while service quality plays a critical
moderating role (Alamoudi and Alharthi, 2021). Customer
purchase intention, trust, and service quality are of vital
importance to Saudi airlines as they aim to maintain their
domestic market share and capitalize on the rising demand for
regional travel.
Understanding customer purchase intentions will help
companies optimize their service offerings, target their
marketing activities, and enhance their brand value. In this
way, companies will be able to align their strategies with
customer expectations and maintain their leadership positions
(Keller, 2013). In the airline industry, however, service
personalization is even more critical for maximizing customer
satisfaction and increasing profitability (Hameed et al., 2024).

2.2. Service quality
Service quality is the extent to which a service exceeds
customer expectations (Cronin and Taylor, 1992). Customer
satisfaction is a critical factor in measuring corporate success
within highly competitive, service -oriented sectors such as
aviation. It is widely accepted that if a company consistently
delivers high-quality services, it can retain customers, thereby
achieve a strong competitive advantage and improving its
financial results (Parasuraman et al., 1988). The most widely
used fundamental dimensions for evaluating service include
reliability, responsiveness, assurance, empathy, and tangible
values.

High service quality offers numerous benefits to
businesses. It increases customer loyalty, encourages repeat
purchases, and lowers the expense of obtaining additional
customers (Zeithaml et al., 1996). It also serves to differentiate
a company's competitive advantage (Gr önroos, 1990). It
fosters high customer loyalty and contributes to business
revenue through word -of-mouth recommendations (Cronin
and Taylor, 1992). Continuous evaluation of service quality
facilitates process improvements and helps reduce errors
(Ladhari, 2009). Organizations with a strong reputation for
service quality can safeguard their brand image and strengthen
their market position.
Measuring service quality in a valid and reliable manner is
important. There are two models frequently used by
companies: SERVQUAL and SERVPERF. Both enable
companies to assess service deficiencies. Firms that evaluate
these results can improve customer satisfaction and long-term
performance. The SERVQUAL model was developed by
Parasuraman et al. (1985). It serves to evaluate service quality
by measuring the gap between customer expectations and
perceptions. The model identifies five factors that are critic al
to customers' perceptions of service quality. Tangibles relate
to physical facilities and appearance. Reliability is the
company's ability to deliver promised services accurately.
Responsiveness refers to the company's willingness and speed
in assisting  consumers. Assurance involves the confidence
instilled in customers through employee knowledge and
courtesy. Empathy entails putting oneself in the customer's
shoes and providing personalized attention and care.
SERVQUAL addresses the gaps between custome r
expectations and experiences. SERVQUAL measurements are
used to assess both expectations and perceptions. With this
measurement tool, service quality is determined by calculating
the difference between perception and expectation.
The SERVPERF model, developed by Cronin and Taylor
(1992), does not include an expectation component; it focuses
solely on performance. The model retains the five fundamental
dimensions and evaluates only the customers' actual service
experiences. SERVPERF is particularly effective in business
environments where expectations are stable and free from
uncertainty or fluctuation.

2.3. Customer trust
The terms "customer trust" and "brand trust" are used
appropriately; both refer to the confidence people place in a
brand and its promises (Chaudhuri and Holbrook, 2001). Trust
often intertwines personal experiences with brand perceptions,
making it difficult to distinguish between the two (Delgado -
Ballester and Munuera-Aleman, 2001). Regardless of whether
it is termed customer trust or brand trust, it is evident that both
play a similar role in fostering loyalty and establishing long -
term relationships with the brand (Morgan and Hunt, 1994).
In dynamic and competitive sectors such as aviation,
gaining, maintaining, and strengthening customer trust over
time is invaluable for companies. Trust is fragile and does not
form overnight; rather, it accumulates over time. While trust
can be easily lost, it is earned in the long run. Companies that
recognize this adopt and implement proactive strategies that
embrace both the functional and emotional dimensions of trust
(Hameed et al., 2024).
Examples of these strategies include engaging in proactive
communication and maintaining transparency  (Gilbertson,
2024), establishing a customer -centric service culture
(Matikiti et al., 2020), ensuring digital trust and data protection
(IATA, 2021) , maintaining consistency in decision -making
reliability (Gilbert & Wong, 2003) , making the creation of
emotional connections with customers a core brand purpose
(Puckett, 2020) , and prioritizing long -term relationships
(Morgan and Hunt, 1994)

2.4. Hypotheses development
The SERVQUAL design allows service quality to be
determined using five factors: tangibility, reliability,
responsiveness, assurance, and empathy (Parasuraman et al.,
1988). These elements significantly influence customer
perceptions and behavioral intentions (Zeithaml et al., 1996).
Cronin and Taylor (1992) further argue through the
SERVPERF model that perceived service performance is more
influential on purchase decisions than expectations. In various
industries, including aviation, consistently high -quality
service has been shown to positively affect customers’
willingness to make a purchase (Dabholkar et al., 2000).
Hypothesis 1 (H1): Perceived service quality has a positive
effect on purchase intention.
Trust is often shaped by a business’s capacity to provide
high-quality service reliably and consistently. The
Commitment-Trust Theory (Morgan and Hunt, 1994) suppose
that trust arises when c onsumers notice a service provider as
competent, fair, and benevolent. In online and high -
involvement service settings, perceived service quality is a
central driver of trust (Gefen et al., 2003). Kim et al. (2009)
also demonstrated that consistent and responsive service
builds trust by reducing perceived uncertainty. Therefore,
businesses that invest in reliable and empathetic service
provision are more likely to foster trust among consumers.
Hypothesis 2 (H2): Service quality has a positive effect on
customer trust.
Trust acts a key role in decreasing perceived risk and
increasing customer commitment. The Theory of Reasoned
Action (Fishbein and Ajzen, 1975) suggests that ideas, for
example trust, influence emotions, which then guide behavior.
When consumers trust a service, they are more ready to believe
the company will meet their expectations, which increases
their intention to buy (Chaudri and Holbrook, 2001). In digital
or high -risk services such as air travel, trust becomes even
more critical (Agag and El-Masry, 2016), as customers must
rely on the provider for both safety and service reliability.
Hypothesis 3 (H3): Customer trust has a positive effect on
purchase intention.
The mediation model proposed by Baron and Kenny
(1986) suggests that a variable can serve as a mediator when it
explains how or why an independent variable affects a
dependent one. In this case, trust may function as the
mechanism through which service quality influences purchase
intention. Morgan and Hunt (1994) argue that trust facilities
relationship continuity and drives customer commitment,
acting as a bridge between service evaluations and behavioral
outcomes. Recent studies support this mediating rol e of trust,
particularly in online and service -based frameworks ( Agag
and El-Masry, 2016; Gefen et al., 2003), suggesting that
service quality indirectly affects purchase intention through
the development of trust.
Hypothesis 4 (H4): Customer trust mediates the
relationship between service quality and purchase intention.
Derived from the theory above, the following section
details the methodology employed to empirically test these
relationships.

3.2. Type of sampling
This study adopts snowball sampling; a non -probability
technique wherein initial participants refers others within their
networks. Suitable samples for this method include
populations lacking central databases and those that are
difficult to access (Goodman, 1961). As in the current study, it
is not possible to instantly access a sample of individuals aged
18 and over who have used ai rline services in Saudi Arabia
within the past year. This would be impossible to reach all of
this population at once. Given the demographic diversity and
geographic distribution of this population, snowball sampling
offers an effective way to identify suitable participants through
peer referrals and directing surveys to acquaintances who have
traveled by air. Snowball technique is widely used in e-surveys
because it allows researchers to easily achieve targeted
individuals through social networks (Noy, 2008).
Considering that it is not practical to create a convenience
or random sample of participants who have traveled by air in
the last year, the snowball sampling method was considered
appropriate for this research context.  Starting with verified
airline users and expanding through referrals ensures that
participants have relevant experience, thereby improving the
study’s validity and aligning with established research
practices in consumer behavior and service quality studies.

3.3. Research universe and sample
The study comprises 400 participants aged 18 and above
who used airline services in Saudi Arabia within the last year.
The targeted population include adults in Saudi Arabia who
recently traveled by air. In 2023, over 112 million domestic
and international passengers were recorded in the country
(GACA, 2024). The research utilized a non -probability
snowball sampling method which enabled access to active
travelers by air in Saudi Arabia. Sample size adequacy was
justified in the light of item-to-respondent ratio guidelines for
regression and factor analysis approaches to avoid reliance on
probability-based assumptions. The methodological literature
indicates that the item -to-respondent ratio must never be less
than 1:10 or 1:15 to keep the parameters stable and limit
estimation errors (Hair et al., 2010; Kline, 2016). The total
number of items in the measure is 33 (22 SERVQUAL items,
8 Customer Trust items, and 3 Purchase Intention items), and
therefore, with our sample of N=400 we maintain a highly
acceptable ratio eq ual to 1:12.1, which is well above the
minimum threshold, and statistics show that the power of the
procedures performed is greater than 0.99 at alpha = 0.05.  A
final sample of 400 was chosen to enhance representativeness
and support model validity in line with Structural Equation
Modelling recommendations (Hair et al., 2010).

3.4. Measures
The measurement instruments are as follows:
Service Quality Scale (SERVQUAL):  SERVQUAL
consists of 22 items, includes some reverse -coded items, and
comprises five sub-dimensions (Parasuraman et al., 1988). In
this study, we treated the scale as a single -factor construct,
addressing overall service quality through a holistic approach.
Although the SERVQUAL model was originally developed
with five dimensions —Tangibles, Reliability,
Responsiveness, Assurance, and Empathy—research supports
the validity and appropriateness of using a composite service
quality index to measure overall asse ssment ( Babakus and
Boller, 1992; Cronin and Taylor, 1992). Meta-analytic studies
on SERVQUAL applications emphasize that these dimensions
can be grouped under a single index. This approach is
particularly useful for structural models focusing on relational
outcomes such as trust and purchase intention (Carrillat et al.,
2007; Ladhari, 2009). Similarly, a unidimensional, composite
approach has already been validated and utilized in the
aviation sector (Gilbert and Wong, 2003). In this research, the
use of a composite SERVQUAL index (Cronbach’s alpha =
0.981) ensures strong internal reliability and consistency with
the adopted framework, while simultaneously preventing
significant multicollinearity issues among the sub-dimensions.
The single -factor SERVQUAL model can be statistically
reliable in certain contexts (Babakus and Boller, 1992).
Carrillat et al. (2007) note that many researchers combine
SERVQUAL dimensions into a single index when examining
relationships with constructs such as trust or purchase
intention. The use of a unidimensional SERVQUAL measure
is common in empirical studies aiming f or simplicity and
clarity (Ladhari, 2009). Therefore, consistent with the existing
literature, a composite SERVQUAL score was calculated in
this study to represent overall perceived service quality.
Ultimately, the SERVQUAL scale has been extensively
validated across various service sector s, including aviation
(Gilbert and Wong, 2003).
Customer Trust Scale : The study employs the
unidimensional Customer Trust Scale to measure overall trust
in a brand. This scale, suggested by Delgado-Ballester et al.
(2003), consists of eight items answered on a 5 -point Likert
scale. It has showed strong consistency, with an alpha of 0.89.
The Purchase Intention Scale : Scale used to evaluate
customers’ purchase intentions and was tested by Saaksjarvi
and Morel (2010) and d isplayed good reliability with a
Cronbach’s alpha of 0 .85. The scale is one -dimensional and
has three items. Items are rated on a 7 -point Likert scale
extending from -3 (Very Negative/Not at All Likely) to +3
(Very Positive/Very Likely).

3.5. Data analysis method
Data were compiled using Google Forms. Survey
collection ended when a total of 400 usable data sets were
reached. Relationships between variables were analyzed using
SPSS Version 29.  Reliability and validity tests, including
Cronbach’s alpha for internal consistency and Exploratory
Factor Analysis (EFA) to confirm construct validity. Kaiser -
Meyer-Olkin (KMO) and Bartlett’s tests were also performed.
Correlation analysis for understanding the relations of the
variables. Regression analysis to examine the direct effects
among variables. Mediation analysis to test if customer trust
mediates the effect of service quality on purchase intention.

3.6. Common Method Bias Check
As data for all independent, mediating, and dependent
variables were collected at a single point in time from self -
reported survey respondents, statistical remedies were used to
test for the presence of Common Method Bias (CMB).
Podsakoff et al. (2003) indicated that Harman’s single -factor
test can be used through entering all 33 items from the scale
into the Principal Component Analysis (PCA) without rotating
the data. After conducting PCA, four distinct factors with
eigenvalues higher than 1.0 were ext racted with the largest
factor accounting for 41.2% of all variance. Since this
percentage is significantly less than 50.0% (Podsakoff et al.,
2003; Fuller et al., 2016), the analysis proves that common
method variance would not be an issue.

To investigate the mediating function of customer trust in
the link between service quality and purchase intention, the
traditional causal steps approach suggested by Baron and
Kenny (1986) was applied.  This procedure requires assessing
the significance of four regression paths using standardized
beta coefficients.
First, the total effect of service quality on purchase
intention (path c) was tested. The results revealed a strong and
statistically significant relationship ( β=0.830, p<0 .001),
indicating that higher perceived service quality is associated
with the greater purchase intention.
Second, service quality significantly predicted customer
trust (path a), with a beta value of β=0.892 (p<0 .001),
suggesting that improvements in service quality led to
increased trust.
Third, when both service quality and customer trust were
included in the regression model predicting purchase intention,
customer trust remained a strong and significant predictor
(path b; β=0.773, p<0.001). In this model, the direct influence
of service quality on purchase intention (path c′) was reduced
but remained significant (β=0.141, p=0.004).
This pattern supports partial mediation, indicating that
service quality influences purchase intention both directly and
indirectly through trust. Specifically, the total effect was
β=0.830, the direct effect (controlling for trust) was β=0.141,
and the indirect effect -calculated as a x b (0.892 x 0.773)- was
β=0.690. The reduction in the direct effect from 0.830 to 0.141
demonstrates that a large portion of service quality’s influence
on purchase intention is transmitted through customer trust.
To further confirm the statistical significance of the
indirect effect beyond the traditional Baron and Kenny (1986)
causal steps, a bias -corrected bootstrapping procedure with
10.000 samples was performed at a 95% confidence level. The
standardized indire ct effect of service quality on purchase
intention through customer trust was β=0.690 (SE=0.075,
Z=14.85, p<0.001). The unstandardized 95% bootstrap
confidence interval ranging from 0.9625 to 1.2554 (and
standardized Confidence Interval ( CI) [0.5997, 0.7808]) did
not contain zero. This resampling result empirically
corroborates the statistical significance of the indirect effect,
confirming the partial mediating role of customer trust.
To visually represent these relationships, a mediation
model is presented in Figure 2, showing paths a, b, and c′ along
with their respective standardized beta values. The total effect
(c=0.830), direct effect (c′=0.141), and indirect effect (a x b=
0.690) are also indicated. Mediation occurs when the effect of
an independent variable (service quality) on a dependent
variable (purchase intention) is transmitted through a mediator
(customer trust). In this study, partial mediation is evident, as
the direct impact of service quality on purchase intention
remains statistically significant even in the presence of the
mediating construct . Therefore, H4 is supported. Full
mediation would be indicated if the direct effect became non -
significant.

5. Discussion and Conclusion

The findings confirm that service quality significantly
influences purchase intention among airline customers :
respondents perceiving higher service quality exhibited
stronger intentions to repurchase and recommend the airline.
This aligns with recent research, for instance, Shen and Yahya
(2021) demonstrated that,  in the low -cost airline segment in
Southeast Asia, superior service quality boosts passenger
satisfaction and, consequently, enhances loyalty (an indirect
proxy for purchase intention).  Similarly, Ravishankar and
Cristopher (2023) identified service quality impacts
repurchase intention in Indian airline markets. Furthermore,
Hussain et al. (2015) found that service quality positively
impacted customer satisfaction and brand loyalty at a UAE -
based airline. This finding is culturally consistent with existing
findings influencing airline choice and repeat purchase
intentions in the Middle East and North Af rica (MENA)
region. On the other hand, although no research has yet been
found on this subject, for airlines, digital interaction and
mobile platform availability can sometimes surpass traditional
service quality in shaping passengers' purchase intention.
These differences can be explained by regional variations in
customer expectations, differences in focus, in -flight service
quality, digital conveniences, and the potential impact of
technological advancements. Our study reinforces the finding
that, despit e global trends, traditional dimensions of service
quality remain critically important in shaping purchasing
decisions within the context of Saudi Arabia, thereby
contributing to ongoing discussions on the subject.
Another key finding was the strong positive relationship
between service quality and customer trust. This is consistent
with the results of Song et al. (2019) who reported that
responsiveness and reliability sub -dimensions of service
quality affect customer trust i n the South Korean airline
industry. Another study conducted in India found that overall
service quality was highly correlated with both passenger
satisfaction and preference, but the relationship between
passenger and preference for a particular airline was less clear
(Sanyal and Hisam, 2016). In contrast, Song et al (2019) found
that in South Korea airline industry, tangibles, empathy and
assurance of service quality are not significantly affect
customer trust. Harris and Goode (2004), in their study of two
different contexts—book buyers and online airline ticket
buyers—positioned trust at the center of service dynamics.
However, they noted that service quality significantly
influenced trust only in the context of book purchases and had
a positive, but not significant, effect on airline ticket
purchases. These contrasting findings suggest that, while
service quality is a necessary condition for building trust, it
may not be sufficient on its own in complex service
environments or those subject to strict regulations. We can
state that the results of our stu dy, centered on Saudi Arabia,
underscore the strong link between service quality and
customer trust and appear culturally consistent. Our findings
support existing theoretical approaches while offering context-
specific insights by situating them within the  cultural and
sectoral framework of Saudi Arabia.
The study also identified a significant relationship between
customer trust and purchase intention, with customers who
trusted the airline demonstrating a higher likelihood of future
purchases. This finding is in line with the study of Kakkar et
al. (2025) on s-commerce websites in India, which showed that
service quality and trust affect perceived value and thus also
affect customer purchase intention . In their studies, with two
different contexts, purchasers of books and online flight
purchasing, Harris and Goode (2004), similarly found that
trust directly affects loyalty in online markets for flight tickets.
However, the universality of this relationship has been
questioned. For example, Kim and Lee (2008) state that factors
such as perceived internet usefulness for searching for apparel
product information, frequency of searching for product
information on the internet, and satisfaction with previous
purchases made on the internet also ha ve a positive effect on
the frequency of product purchases.  From this, we can
understand that the impact of trust alone may not be sufficient
to influence purchase behavior, but the outcome may differ
when considering market structure and customer decision -
making context. Therefore, while trust played a central role in
the current study and its relative importance was found to be
context-specific, it should still be noted that it may differ
across trip types, competitive environments, and customer
expectations.
The main contribution of this study is the finding that
customer trust in the airline industry is a significant mediator
of the relationship between service quality and purchase
intention. This means that service quality has a direct impact
on customer intentions, and a significant portion of this impact
is transmitted indirectly through trust. This finding supports
the model proposed by Qalati et al  (2021), who reported a
similar mediating role of trust in the context of online
shopping. It is also consistent with Koçak-Alan and Tümer -
Kabadayı (2014) , who found that, trust bridges the gap
between service quality perceptions and their behavioral
intentions. In contrast, Moslehpour et al. (2014) found that in
the Vietnamese airline market, satisfaction -rather than trust -
served as the key mediating variable  between c onsumer
expectation and purchase intention . This proposes that in
certain contexts, emotional evaluations of service may either
precede the formation of trust or diminish its relative
importance. Therefore, this study offers important insights by
reaffirming the mediating role of trust within a cultural and
geographic context where both personal and institutional trust
are central to commercial relationships. It underscores that
trust not only complements service quality but may amplify its
effect on purchase behavior -particularly in a post -pandemic
environment where safety, hygiene, and brand transparency
have become critical to consumer decision-making.
From a theoretical perspective, we can state that this study
extends the SERVQUAL and trust -purchase intention
frameworks by applying them in the context of Saudi Arabia—
an area that has not been sufficiently researched. The study
also contributes new insi ghts to current theories regarding
issues such as heightened trust concerns in the post -COVID
era, the role of service quality, and region -specific customer
behavior patterns.
From a practical standpoint, our findings suggest that
airline executives need to go beyond merely providing quality
service. Executives should proactively build customer trust
and strive to safeguard it to ensure sustainability. In this
process, they must  invest in customer service, ensure
operational transparency, maintain consistency in
communication, and effectively manage complaints.
From the perspective of practitioners, we suggest that , in
addition to enhancing service quality, organizations must also
establish customer trust to maximize purchase intentions. By
building trust, they can amplify the impact of service quality
on the consumer decision-making process.

The implications of the results for researchers are that they
reinforce the recognition that trust acts a main role  as a
mediating construct in service marketing businesses and stress
the importance of including both direct and indirect influences
when modeling consumer behavior.
These insights contribute to the development of service
quality improvement strategies and offer valuable context -
specific improvements to theoretical models of purchase
intention.
This study has some constraints. First, the evidence was
gathered merely from airline passengers in Saudi Arabia. This
is a significant limitation, as it borders the generalizability of
the results to other samples and fields. We recommend that
future studies consider testing the model with different airlines
and in different geographical contexts. Second, the survey
used a cross-sectional research form, which restricts the ability
to draw causal inferences. Future analysis could consider long-
term or observational designs instead of cross -sectional ones.
Third, SERVQUAL was considered a single dimension rather
than five dimensions in measuring service quality. Future
researchers could develop a model that analyzes the
differential effects of its five sub -dimensions. Such an
approach could provide clearer insights into improving service
quality strategies. As a final research recommendation, the
composition of additional variables namely customer
satisfaction, perceived value or brand reputation, brand image,
and ease of use of digital applications could give a more
overall understanding of how service quality, trust, and
purchase intention are interrelated.
