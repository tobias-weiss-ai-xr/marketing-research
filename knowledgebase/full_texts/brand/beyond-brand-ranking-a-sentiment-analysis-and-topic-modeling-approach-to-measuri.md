ABSTRACT: The present study aims to evaluate how brand performance is reflected in
customer perceptions by analyzing airline passenger reviews. In order to measure customer
satisfaction and service quality, sentiment analysis and topic modeling techniques were
employed. A comprehensive analysis of online reviews from five major airline companies was
conducted to identify sentiment distributions, with a focus on the most frequently complained
about and praised service aspects. The findings indicate that American Airlines  and United
Airlines received substantial negative feedback, particularly regarding ticketing, reservations
and flight experiences. In contrast, Lufthansa and Emirates demonstrated a more balanced
sentiment distribution, especially in terms of seat and cab in comfort. It is worthy of note that
Delta Airlines received a comparatively higher proportion of positive feedback with regard to
flight experience and service quality. The present study makes a contribution to the
improvement of service quality by means of analyzing customer feedback and thereby supports
strategic decision -making by ensuring that airline services are aligned with passenger
expectations. Moreover, it emphasizes the significance of incorporating natural language
processing and machine lear ning-based analyses into marketing and brand management
processes.
Keywords: Sentiment analysis, Topic modeling, Customer feedback, Airline industry, Service
quality

1. Introduction
A systematic analysis of customer feedback is imperative for firms seeking to
achieve sustainable success in competitive markets, as it enables the enhancement
of products and services. As Gandomi and Haider (2015) demonstrate, social media,
online review platforms and other digital channels serve as valuable data sources
for understanding customer experiences and expectations. However, the effective
processing of textual data from these platforms and its transformation into
meaningful insights requires the application of comprehensive and scientifically
grounded analytical methods ( Pang and Lee, 2009 ; Taboada et al., 2011 ). In the
airline industry, the analysis of customer feedback is of pivotal significance in the
evaluation of service quality, the enhancement of customer satisfaction and the
acquisition of a competitive advantage ( Farzadnia and Vanani, 2022). As Kwon et
al. (2021)  demonstrate, topic modeling and sentiment analysis techniques are
widely utilized to extract meaningful insights from customer reviews. As posited
by Bergamaschi and Po (2015) , the employm ent of topic modeling methods,
including Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA), Latent Semantic Indexing (LSI) and
Non-Negative Matrix Factorization (NMF), facilitates the identification of latent
themes within customer feedback, thereby providing a foundation  for the
development of business strategies. On the other hand, sentiment analysis
approaches, encompassing machine learning, lexicon -based and hybrid
methodologies, are utilized to examine the emotional trends associated with these
themes (Birjali et al., 2021).

In recent years, studies adopting a hybrid approach that integrates topic modeling
and sentiment analysis have enabled a more comprehensive evaluation of customer
feedback (Adeborna and Siau, 2014 ; Hasib et al., 2021 ; Shahid et al., 2024 ; Xin,
2024). These approaches primarily focus on identifying the main themes within
customer reviews and subsequently analyzing the emotional trends associated with
these themes. Furthermore, the integration of topic modeling and sentiment analysis
techniques has been demonstrated to yield more robust and significant results in the
analysis of customer satisfaction (Kwon et al., 2021). In particular, deep learning-
based methods have been found to improve the accuracy of both topic modeling
and sentiment detection processes, thereby allowing for a more thorough
assessment of customer reviews (Farzadnia and Vanani, 2022).
The present study employs machine learning -based sentiment analysis and topic
modeling techniques to analyze customer reviews of Lufthansa, Emirates, Delta
Airlines, American Airlines and United Airlines. The airline industry is a globally
strategic sector ( Nam and Lee, 2019 ) and continuously develops innovative
strategies in response to intense competition and rapidly evolving customer
expectations (Bae and Chi, 2022 ). Customer satisfaction is widely recognized as
one of the key determinants of success in this industry (Kumar and Zymbler, 2019),
while the growing importance of digital customer interac tions has increased the
value of large-scale online review analysis ( Song et al., 2024 ). Although previous
studies have demonstrated the usefulness of sentiment analysis and topic modeling
in evaluating airline service quality, most have focused on either a single analytical
approach, a limited set of airlines, or a specific service dimension. Consequently,
there remains a need for a comprehensive framework that combines multiple
sentiment analysis techniques with topic model ing to provide a broader
understanding of customer experience across leading international airline brands.
Accordingly, this study contributes to the literature in three ways. First, it provides
a comparative evaluation of three different sentiment analysi s paradigms: deep
learning, lexicon -based methods and supervised machine learning, thereby
extending methodological understanding of customer sentiment analysis. Second,
it integrates sentiment analysis with LDA-based topic modeling to identify airline-
specific service strengths and weaknesses from large -scale customer reviews.
Third, it contributes to the marketing and brand management literature by
demonstrating how the integration of sentiment analysis and topic modeling can
generate actionable insights from large -scale customer feedback to support
evidence-based service quality evaluation and customer experience management.
From a managerial perspective, the findings provide airline managers with
actionable insights into the service dimensions generating  positive and negative
customer experiences, thereby supporting data -driven service improvement and
strategic decision-making. In this context, the present study addresses the following
research questions:

analysis in customer feedback?

2. Literature review
2.1. Sentiment analysis
Sentiment analysis is a Natural Language Processing (NLP) task that aims to
identify emotions and opinions expressed in texts ( Chaturvedi et al., 201 8). In
recent years, sentiment analysis has been widely adopted not only in academic
research but also by businesses, governments and various institutions ( Sánchez-
Rada and Iglesias, 2019). The primary objective of sentiment analysis is to ascertain
individuals' attitudes towards a specific subject or product and it is consequently
employed to evaluate positive, negative and neutral sentiments ( Farzadnia and
Vanani, 2022 ). The method is ge nerally conducted at four analytical levels:
document level, sentence level, phrase level and aspect level. Each of these levels
involves different analysis techniques (Wankhade et al., 2022). While the initial two
levels of analysis – document and sentence – are of significance and analytically
demanding, the aspect level is more complex in nature as it necessitates a more
detailed evaluation. In the domain of sentiment analysis research, three
predominant methodological  approaches are generally adopted: The following
methods are employed: (1) machine learning -based methods, (2) lexicon -based
methods and (3) hybrid methods ( Birjali et al., 2021 ). Hybrid methods integrate
machine learning and lexicon-based techniques to construct more robust models.
These approaches combine the use of topic modeling with sentiment analysis,
frequently resulting in enhanced accuracy (Ansari et al., 2019).
The analysis of sentiment holds strategic importance in the fields of marketing and
customer satisfaction (Bernabé-Moreno et al., 2020). It is possible for businesses to
identify trends in consumer behavior and brand perception by analyzing customer
feedback shared on social media platforms, e -commerce sites and online review
platforms ( Birjali et al., 2021 ). As Liu and Zhang (2012)  demonstrate, these
analyses offer critical insights that can be used to improve customer experience,
enhance product and service quality and develop marketing strategies that more
effectively reach target audiences. Especial ly, in the context of customer -centric
industries, such as the airline, hospitality, automotive and retail sectors, sentiment
analysis has been shown to be an effective tool for the identification of customer
complaints and the identification of potential service deficiencies ( Farzadnia and
Vanani, 2022).

There has been a marked increase in both advancements and research activities in
the field of sentiment analysis. Liu and Zhang (2012)  and Piryani et al. (2017)
examined the early stages of sentiment analysis. They developed a framework for
computationally processing unstructured data between 2000 and 2015. The primary
focus of their research was opinion extractio n and the identification of emotional
trends. Yue et al. (2019) conducted a thorough investigation into the reliability of
online reviews, assessing the efficacy of internet-based user comments. Iqbal et al.
(2020) analyzed Twitter data from airline companies and found that the highest
accuracy rates were achieved using artificial neural network -based models. Saad
(2020) reported that the SVM model demonstrated the o ptimal performance for
sentiment analysis of Twitter data. Gouthami and Hegde (2023)  achieved an
accuracy rate of 94.26% in sentiment analysis using the Bi-LSTM model. Badanik
et al. (2023)  conducted an evaluation of airline service quality through sentiment
analysis. This involved the generation of word clouds from customer feedback and
the conducting of analyses across various categories. Raihen and Akter (2024 )
evaluated customer feedback from six major airlines in the United States using a
multi-class sentiment analysis approach and reported a 90.13% accuracy rate with
the Random Forest model. Idris and Mohamad (2023) published a new framework
for evaluating service quality in the airline industry. This innovative approach
utilizes sentiment analysis, a sophisticated method of analyzing text to measure and
predict consumer sentiment. The extant literature demonstrates that sentimen t
analysis plays a critical role in understanding customer experience and serves as a
valuable tool for improving service quality, particularly in the airline sector.
Furthermore, it has been observed that deep learning -based sentiment analysis
models offe r a superior level of accuracy in comparison to traditional machine
learning techniques.
2.2. Topic modeling
Topic modeling is a widely utilized analytical method in the domain of information
retrieval, aimed at identifying latent themes within large-scale document collections
(Abdelrazek et al., 2023 ). This technique automates the classification of large
corpora of text, with the objective of identifying shared topics and meaningful
structures across documents. In the topic modeling process, the main topics within
a document collection are inferred statistically without the use of any supervised
learning methods ( Blei et al., 2003 ). Each resulting topic is expressed as a
semantically interpretable concept, thereby enabling the contents of documents to
be represented in a latent but meaningful manner (Crain et al., 2012).
Topic modeling, a technique founded upon unsupervised learning methodologies,
is widely regarded as a potent instrument for the identification of structural patterns
within textual data and the subsequent decomposition of texts into meaningful
components. A comprehensive review of topic modeling techniques has been
conducted by researchers, who h ave defined four primary categories of topic
models: algebraic, fuzzy, probabilistic and neural models (Abdelrazek et al., 2023).

It is evident that the most prevalent topic modeling algorithms include Latent
Semantic Indexing (LSI), Non -Negative Matrix Factorization (NMF) and Latent
Dirichlet Allocation (LDA). As demonstrated in previous studies (Bergamaschi and
Po, 2015), while LSI has been shown to outperform LDA in terms of identif ying
similar topics across texts, it has been found to offer limited advantages in
commercial applications. Conversely, LDA has been identified as a notable
approach in exploratory research settings, given its capacity to accommodate topic
and word variability within clusters, thereby enhancing interpretability ( Farzadnia
and Vanani, 2022 ). Topic modeling constitutes a pivotal unsupervised learning
method employed in the identification of prevailing themes within customer
feedback data. For instance, Koçak (2018) utilized automated text classification
techniques to generate perception maps for service dimensions by analyzing airline
brand mentions on Twitter. Kwon et al. (2021) conducted a comprehensive analysis
of customer feedback from Asian airlines, identifying three overarching themes that
significantly influence satisfaction levels. These themes encompass seat comfort,
service quality and the availability of meal options. In the study undertaken by Sim
(2023), the author explored customer feedback on SkyTrax and TripAdvisor,
linking perceived service quality to airline performance through the utilization of
topic modeling. In a similar vein, Xin (2024) employed natural language processing
(NLP) and deep learning techniques to analyze customer feedback on Singapore
Airlines, integrating latent Dirichlet allocation (LDA) with word embedding
methods.

3.2. Data preprocessing
In order to ensure a reliable analysis process, it was necessary to cleanse and prepare
customer feedback for analysis. At this stage, data preprocessing is applied to
enhance the performance of machine learning models ( Aljedaani et al., 2022 ).
Initially, the dataset was imported using the  pandas library, after which its overall
structure was examined. Four key variables were identified: airline name (Airline),
customer feedback text (Comment), sentiment classification (Sentiment: positive,
negative, neutral) and assigned topic indices (Top ic, using the LDA method). The
function 'df.isnull().sum()' was utilized to detect missing or invalid entries and
missing data were cleaned using appropriate techniques. Yang and Zhang (2018)
emphasized that probabilistic machine learning models rely on word frequency and
may treat 'good' and 'Good' as different words. Therefore, following the elimination
of numeric values, the text was converted to lowercase in order to eliminate case
sensitivity, a critical step for ensuring consistent analysis. The following additional
preprocessing steps were included: integrity and consistency checks were
performed (i.e. missing, erroneous, or duplicate records were removed to ensure
data integrity), special characters were removed (in order to reduce noise during
analysis) and punctuation marks were deleted (in order to prevent semantic
distortions in text mining). Following the preprocessing stage, records with missing
comment text or sentiment labels were excluded from the dataset, resultin g in a
final dataset comprising 19,050 customer reviews.
3.3. Sentiment analysis
In order to ascertain the sentiment of customer feedback for airline companies, a
comparative analysis was conducted using deep learning -based language models,
lexicon-based metho ds and supervised machine learning models. This approach
addressed question 𝑅𝑄1. The selection of these methods was made on the basis of
their distinct strengths in the fields of text mining and natural language processing.
The utilisation of deep learning models has been demonstrated to be advantageous

in the capture of contextual meaning. In comparison, lexicon -based methods have
been shown to offer analysis that is both fast and transparent. Finally, the
employment of supervised machine learnin g has been evidenced to result in
improved classification accuracy. This multi -method approach was designed to
facilitate a more comprehensive understanding of the multidimensional nature of
customer feedback and to highlight performance differences among the selected
techniques.
In this study, three sentiment analysis approaches were compared for analyzing
customer feedback: a deep learning -based Hugging Face Transformer model,
lexicon-based methods (TextBlob, V ADER, Afinn), and supervised machine
learning algorithms (SVM, LR, RF, NB, GB). To establish a rigorous and equitable
benchmark among these diverse methodologies, all models were evaluated against
an identical 20% holdout test dataset extracted from the preprocessed corpus. Since
the pretrained transformer model was used directly for inference without additional
fine-tuning, its generated annotations served as the reference labels rather than a
human-annotated ground truth for training and testing the supervised algorithms.
The comparative performance  across these approaches was quantified utilizing
uniform multi-class classification metrics, specifically Accuracy, Precision, Recall,
and F1 -Score. The SVM demonstrated strong performance by effectively
replicating the decision boundaries within the high -dimensional textual data. LR
was found to offer a structure that was both fast and interpretable, while Naïve
Bayes' lower performance may be associated with its assumption of word
independence. Meanwhile, the Hugging Face Transformer model demonstrated
efficacy in capturing contextual depth and emotional tone; however, its
performance faced limitations when dealing with long and complex reviews, a
constraint that can be attributed to its training on social media-based datasets.
3.3.1. Sentiment analysis using the hugging face transformer model
Initially, sentiment analysis was conducted using a deep learning -based Hugging
Face Transformer model. The model selected for this task was 'cardiffnlp/twitter -
roberta-base-sentiment', while alternative models were exclud ed due to potential
limitations in capturing contextual depth and expressive diversity ( Birjali et al.,
2021; Sánchez-Rada and Iglesias, 2019; Bhatia et al., 2015). The reviews were then
classified into three sentiment categories: positive, negative and neutral.
The analysis process was comprised of the following steps: (1) Model Loading: The
model was loaded using the Transformers library. (2) Data Proce ssing: Each
customer review was entered into the model to determine the corresponding
sentiment label. (3) Storing Results: The predicted sentiment labels were appended
to the dataset for further analysis (Kim et al., 2023)
In this study, the pretrained Transformer model was applied directly for zero -shot
inference without further fine -tuning on the airline review dataset. While the
model's capacity to capture contextual meaning with greater efficacy is attributable
to its training on large-scale language data, the fact that its training data is primarily

derived from online platforms creates a possibility of experiencing contextual loss
when applied to long and complex customer reviews (Srinivas and Ramachandiran,
2024). Additionally, it is important to und erscore that all passenger reviews

3.3.2. Lexicon-based sentiment analysis
During the sentiment analysis process, le xicon-based methods were employed to
calculate the overall sentiment of customer feedback based on predefined sentiment
scores assigned to individual words. The primary methodologies employed in this
context were as follows:
(1) TextBlob: The method employed in this study involved conducting word -level
analysis and subsequently categorizing reviews into positive, negative, or neutral
based on their content (Loria, 2025).
(2) V ADER was specifically designed for the purpose of evaluating social media
data. V ADER calculates overall sentiment scores by evaluating word tonality and
contextual cues (Hutto and Gilbert, 2014).
(3) Afinn: As stated by Nielsen (2011) , Afinn assign ed numerical values to
individual words based on predefined sentiment scores and subsequently computed
an overall sentiment score for each review.
Lexicon-based methods are advantageous due to their speed and ability to directly
evaluate specific words. Nevertheless, their failure to adequately capture context or
discern irony may result in erroneous classification in specific reviews. The
findings from lexicon-based methods were then juxtaposed with those yielded by
the Hugging Face Transformer model. It w as observed that while the Transformer
model captured contextual meaning more effectively, lexicon -based approaches
offered faster and more interpretable results by relying on individual word
evaluations.

independently detecting actual customer sentiment. Nevertheless, as the primary
objective of this study is to benchmark different methodologies under a uniform
reference framework, these results remain highly meaningful for assessing relative
model performance.

ability to successfully identify de cision boundaries in high -dimensional textual
data. Similarly, Logistic Regression offered an equally robust, fast, and

In conclusion, three different approaches to sentiment  analysis were compared to
evaluate customer feedback for airline companies, thereby addressing the first
research question ( 𝑅𝑄1). The Transformer -based Hugging Face model
demonstrated strong performance by effectively capturing contextual meanin g;
meanwhile, lexicon-based methods offered advantages in interpretability but were
less effective in understanding context -dependent expressions. SML models,
notably the SVM, attained elevated classification accuracies, thereby evidencing
their marked efficacy. In light of the findings, the Hugging Face model was selected
for utilisation in this study, on account of its capacity to capture contextual nuances
and generate robust results. Despite the ease of implementation and rapid
processing speeds afforde d by lexicon -based methods, they were found to be
deficient in their ability to detect contextual elements such as irony and metaphor.
Overall, while the transformer model was found to be effective for the purpose of
contextual analysis, the SVM model was identified as the most efficient method
with regard to accuracy and consistency. The findings underscore the merits of deep
learning in terms of its capacity to facilitate contextual understanding and the
significance of supervised machine learning in ensu ring precision and the
extendibility of its applications. It is important to emphasize that the target
sentiment labels utilized for training and evaluating the conventional machine
learning models were generated via the pre-trained Transformer architecture, rather
than an independent human -annotated standard. Consequently, the reported
performance metrics -including accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 -score-do not
measure the traditional models' absolute correctness in capturing true underlying
human sentim ent. Instead, these metrics specifically quantify the degree of
conformity, alignment, and functional emulation between the classical classifiers
and the Transformer's decision boundaries.

5. Discussion, implications and limitations
5.1. Theoretical implications
This study contributes to the service marketing literature by illustrating how
sentiment analysis and topic modeling can be jointly employed to identify customer
perceptions of different service dimensions and compare customer experiences
across int ernational airline brands (Lemon and Verhoef, 2016). Whilst previous
studies have primarily employed sentiment analysis to examine customer opinions
in the airline industry ( Riaz et al., 2019 ; Kwon et al., 2021 ; Iddrisu et al., 2023 ;
Samir et al., 2023 ), the present study extends this line of research by integrating
multiple sentiment analysis approaches with LDA -based topic modeling, enabling
a more comprehensive evaluation of customer perceptions across different service
dimensions and international airline brands. The employment of machine learning

  International Journal of Economics and Innovation, 12(2), 2026, 713-737  731
techniques, such as the deep learning-based Hugging Face Transformer and Latent
Dirichlet Allocation (LDA), provides a vigorous methodological framework for the
analysis of the relationship between computational text analysis and consumer
behavior.
The findings of this study are consistent with those of the study conducted by
Anitsal et al. (2019) , which analyzed customer reviews for ten major airline
companies. The aforementioned study identified a higher degree of customer
dissatisfaction among North American airlines, a conclusion that is consistent with
the findings of the present study and may help explain the relatively higher
proportion of negative customer feedback observed for American Airlines and
United Airlines. Moreover, the identification of recurrent grievances within the
"Ticketing & Reservation Issues" category aligns with the conclusions of studies
conducted by Badanik et al. (2023)  and Raihen and Akter (2024) . These studies
highlight the deleterious impact of disruptio ns in digitalized ticketing and
reservation processes on customer experience within the airline industry. It has been
determined that inadequate digital infrastructure, system errors in reservation
platforms and customer service deficiencies are key driver s of customer
dissatisfaction, particularly in the case of U.S.-based airlines. Likewise, the results
of the "Seating & Cabin Comfort" category are consistent with the findings of a
study conducted by Kwon et al. (2021), which focused on Asia-based airlines and
identified seat comfort as a critical determinant of customer satisfaction. The
comparatively more positive feedback received by Lufthansa and Emirates in this
area further corroborates the importance of comfort in mee ting customer
expectations.
The findings of this study are also consistent with the service marketing literature,
which emphasizes the importance of systematically analyzing customer feedback
to identify service shortcomings and support continuous service improvement
(Lemon and Verhoef, 2016). Additionally, the amalgamation of sentiment analysis
and topic modeling techniques, as evidenced by Farzadnia and Vanani (2022) ,
substantiates the efficacy of these methodologies in pr oviding a robust and
replicable methodological framework for evaluating service quality in the airline
industry. In conclusion, this study integrates natural language processing (NLP) and
machine learning techniques with marketing, service quality and cons umer
behavior literature, providing valuable theoretical and methodological insights for
future research, particularly in the airline sector.
5.2. Managerial implications
This study provides valuable strategic insights for airline executives aiming to
enhance customer satisfaction and improve service quality. A survey of customer
complaints revealed that the most common issues were related to ticketing and
reservation processes, particularly with regard to American Airlines and United
Airlines. This finding suggests that these companies should make their reservation
systems more user -friendly, reduce processing errors and improve the

Uluslararası Ekonomi ve Yenilik Dergisi, 12(2), 2026, 713-737  732
responsiveness of customer service. The implementation of automated support
systems has the potential to facilitate the streamlining of the ticketing process.
Apart from that, the provision of seating and cabin comfort has been identified as a
pivotal factor in influencing customer satisfaction. While Lufthansa and Emirates
have received comparatively more favorable feedback in this category, the presence
of inconsistencies in passenger experiences highlights the necessity for
standardized enhancements to be made with a view to improving passenger
comfort. It is recommended that investment be made in ergonomic seat designs and
that flexible seating options be offered, tailored to the preferences of passengers.
To add to this, the concept of "Flight Experience & Service Quality" assumes
significant importance in shaping customer perceptions. Although Delta Airlines
received a comparatively more favorable evaluation in this dimension, the findings
suggest that further improvements in in -flight services may represent a potential
area of managerial focus for airlines seeking to enhance customer experience.
Finally, although "Baggage & Airp ort Services" received a comparatively low
number of complaints, it remains a crucial factor in fostering customer loyalty. It is
recommended that airline companies continuously improve their operational
efficiency by employing data -driven customer feedbac k systems and aligning
services with regional customer expectations.
5.3. Limitations and further research
The present study is constrained by its exclusive focus on the analysis of customer
reviews related to specific airline companies, thereby precluding its  generalization
to other sectors or service types. Furthermore, the exclusive reliance on online
customer reviews is limited in terms of the available data sources. A key
methodological constraint stems from training and evaluating classical machine
learning algorithms on Transformer -generated sentiment labels rather than an
independent, human -verified ground truth. The reported performance metrics
quantify the models' alignment with the Transformer baseline rather than absolute
human sentiment accuracy-prompting our adoption of the term “reference labels”
instead of “ground truth ” throughout this paper. It is recommended that future
research endeavors consider the incorporation of supplementary data collection
methodologies, such as surveys and in -depth int erviews, with a view to more
comprehensively evaluating customer satisfaction. In addition to these, conducting
studies that encompass diverse geographical regions would facilitate the
examination of how cultural differences influence customer perceptions. In light of
the progressive advancements witnessed in the domain of machine learning,
particularly with regard to sentiment analysis and topic modeling techniques, there
is an increasing inclination towards the implementation of analyses that are
characterized by enhanced nuance and contextual richness.
