Introduction
In recent years, the rapid expansion of social media platforms has significantly transformed the
landscape of marketing communication and consumer – brand relationships. Social media has
moved beyond being a simple communication channel and has evolved into an interactive
environment where consumers actively participate in brand -related activities. Unlike traditional
marketing approaches that rely on one -way communication, social media enables brands and
consumers to engage in continuous, two -way interactions. This shift has encouraged organizations
to focus on relationship-building strategies rather than solely emphasizing product promotion. As a
result, social brand engagement has emerged as a critical concept in understanding modern
consumer behavior. Social brand engagement refers to the extent to which consumers cognitively,
emotionally, and behaviorally interact with brands through social media platforms. These
interactions may include liking brand posts, commenting on content, sharing experiences,
participating in online discussions, or engaging with brand communities. Such engagement
activities allow consumers to develop a closer connection with brands, which may influence their
perceptions and attitudes over time. Previous research suggests that higher levels of engagement
can enhance consumers ’  familiarity with brands and create favorable impressions, which are
essential for influencing purchase -related decisions.  One of the key mechanisms through which
social brand engagement affects consumer behavior is brand awareness. Brand awareness
represents consumers’  ability to recognize and recall a brand when making purchase decisions. In
highly competitive markets, consumers are often exposed to numerous brands offering similar
products, making brand awarenes s a crucial factor in the decision -making process. Social media
marketing activities, such as interactive campaigns, influencer endorsements, and user -generated
content, significantly enhance brand visibility and exposure. When consumers repeatedly
encounter brand-related content on social media, they are more likely to remember the brand and
consider it during purchase situations. Therefore, brand awareness plays an important mediating
role between social brand engagement and purchase intentions.  Another i mportant mechanism
influencing consumer decision -making is brand association. Brand association refers to the set of
meanings, beliefs, and mental connections that consumers link with a brand. These associations
may relate to product quality, reliability, social status, or emotional value. Strong and positive
brand associations can differentiate a brand from its competitors and increase consumers ’
willingness to purchase. Social media engagement provides consumers with opportunities to co -
create brand meani ngs by sharing opinions, reviews, and experiences. Through continuous
interaction, consumers form stronger associations with brands, which can positively influence their
purchase intentions. Despite its importance, brand association has received comparativ ely less
attention as a mediating variable in the relationship between social brand engagement and purchase
intentions. In addition to mediation effects, contemporary marketing literature emphasizes the role
of contextual factors that may strengthen or wea ken the influence of brand -related perceptions on
consumer behavior. Trust is one such critical factor, particularly in online environments. Trust
reflects consumers’  confidence in a brand’ s honesty, reliability, and ability to fulfill its promises.
In digital contexts, where consumers cannot physically examine products before purchasing, trust
becomes an essential determinant of purchase intentions. Even when consumers hold positive
brand associations, a lack of trust may prevent them from converting these  perceptions into actual
purchase intentions. Therefore, trust is expected to play a moderating role by influencing the
strength of the relationship between brand association and purchase intentions.  Although prior
studies have explored the direct effects of social brand engagement on consumer outcomes, several
gaps remain in the existing literature. First, many studies focus on single mediating mechanisms,
such as brand awareness or brand association, without examining their combined influence within
a unified framework. Second, limited research has integrated moderation effects, particularly the
role of trust, into models examining social media engagement and purchase intentions. As a result,

Literature Review
Theoretical Background
The theoretical foundation of this study is grounded in consumer engagement theory and brand
equity theory, which explain how consumers’  interactions with brands influence their perceptions
and behavioral intentions. Consumer engagement theory suggests tha t active interactions between
consumers and brands create psychological connections that go beyond simple transactional
relationships. In social media environments, consumers are no longer passive recipients of
marketing messages; instead, they actively en gage with brands through content creation, sharing,
and online discussions. These engagement activities are believed to strengthen consumers ’
cognitive and emotional bonds with brands, thereby influencing subsequent decision -making
processes. Brand equity theory further supports the proposed relationships by emphasizing the role
of brand -related assets, such as brand awareness and brand associations, in shaping consumer
behavior. Brand awareness enhances the likelihood that a brand will be recalled and cons idered
during purchase decisions, while brand associations reflect the meanings and beliefs that
consumers attach to a brand. Prior research indicates that higher brand awareness and stronger
brand associations contribute positively to consumers ’  purchase intentions by reducing perceived
risk and increasing confidence in the brand (Keller, 1993; Aaker, 1996). In addition, trust theory
provides an important lens for understanding consumer behavior in online contexts. Trust
represents consumers’  belief in a brand’ s reliability and integrity, which is particularly critical in
digital environments characterized by information asymmetry and uncertainty. When trust is
present, consumers are more likely to act on their positive brand perceptions and translate them
into purchase intentions. Therefore, trust is expected to strengthen the relationship between brand
association and purchase intentions by enhancing consumers ’  confidence in their decision -
making. By integrating these theoretical perspectives, the present study develops a comprehensive
framework that explains how social brand engagement influences purchase intentions through
mediating and moderating mechanisms.

Variables Development
Brand Engagement
Brand engagement refers to the extent to which consumers a ctively interact with a brand on social
media platforms through cognitive, emotional, and behavioral activities. These interactions may
include liking posts, commenting, sharing brand -related content, or participating in online brand
communities. Unlike tr aditional marketing communication, social brand engagement allows
consumers to become co -creators of brand value by expressing opinions and sharing experiences.
Such engagement strengthens consumers ’  connection with the brand and enhances their
involvement in brand-related activities.
Prior research suggests that social brand engagement plays a critical role in shaping consumer
perceptions and attitudes. When consumers frequently engage with brand content, they develop a
sense of familiarity and attachment,  which positively influences their evaluation of the brand.
Engagement also enables brands to communicate their values more effectively, creating
meaningful relationships with consumers. Studies have shown that higher levels of social brand
engagement can lead to stronger brand -related outcomes, such as improved brand awareness,
favorable brand associations, and enhanced purchase intentions (Hollebeek et al., 2014; Dessart et
al., 2015). Therefore, brand engagement is considered a key antecedent in understa nding consumer
behavior in social media contexts.
Brand Awareness
Brand awareness refers to consumers ’  ability to recognize and recall a brand when considering
purchasing decisions. It represents the strength of a brand ’ s presence in consumers ’  minds and
plays a fundamental role in the formation of brand equity. High brand awareness increases the
likelihood that a brand will be included in a consumer ’ s consideration set, particularly in markets
characterized by intense competition and information overload. Social media platforms have
become effective tools for enhancing brand awareness by increasing brand visibility and exposure.
Interactive content, influencer marketing, and user -generated content enable brands to reach a
wider audience and reinforce brand recall. When consumers are repeatedly exposed to brand -
related messages, they become more familiar with the brand, which reduces uncertainty and
increases confidence in decision -making. Previous studies indicate that brand awareness
significantly influence s consumers ’  purchase intentions by making brands more accessible and
memorable (Keller, 1993; Aaker, 1996). As a result, brand awareness is widely recognized as an
important mediating variable between marketing activities and consumer behavioral outcomes.
Brand Association
Brand association refers to the set of meanings, beliefs, and perceptions that consumers connect
with a brand. These associations may be functional, emotional, or symbolic in nature and are
developed through consumers’  experiences, interactions, and exposure to brand communications.
Strong and positive brand associations help differentiate a brand from its competitors and enhance
its overall image in the marketplace.
Social media engagement plays an important role in shaping brand associa tions by enabling
consumers to interact with brands and share experiences with others. Through continuous
engagement, consumers form perceptions related to brand quality, credibility, and social value.
Positive brand associations can increase consumers ’  confidence in the brand and strengthen their
emotional attachment, which positively influences purchase intentions. Prior research has
demonstrated that favorable brand associations significantly affect consumer attitudes and
behavioral intentions, including willingness to purchase and brand loyalty (Keller, 1993; Yoo et al.,

Trust
Trust refers to consumers ’  belief that a brand is reliable, honest, and capable of fulfilling its
promises. In online and social media environments, trust is particularly important because
consumers often face uncertainty and information asymmetry. When consumers trust a brand, they
are more willing to rely on brand -related information and make purchase decisions based on their
perceptions. Trust strengthens the relationship between brand -related perceptions and behavioral
outcomes. Even when consumers hold positive brand associations, a la ck of trust may prevent
them from converting these perceptions into actual purchase intentions. Conversely, high levels of
trust increase consumers ’  confidence and reduce perceived risk, making them more likely to act
on their favorable evaluations. Prior studies suggest that trust plays a moderating role in consumer
behavior by enhancing the impact of brand associations on purchase intentions (Chaudhuri &
Holbrook, 2001; Morgan & Hunt, 1994). Therefore, trust is incorporated in this study as a
moderating variable that influences the strength of the relationship between brand association and
purchase intentions.
Purchase Intentions
Purchase intention refers to consumers ’  willingness and likelihood to buy a particular product or
brand in the future. It represents a psychological state that precedes actual purchasing behavior and
is widely used as a key indicator of consumer decision -making. Purchase intentions are influenced
by consumers’  attitudes, perceptions, and evaluations of a brand. In the context of so cial media
marketing, purchase intentions are shaped by consumers ’  engagement with brand-related content,
brand awareness, and brand associations. When consumers are familiar with a brand and hold
positive perceptions, they are more likely to develop stron g intentions to purchase. Previous
research consistently indicates that purchase intention is a strong predictor of actual buying
behavior and serves as an important outcome variable in marketing studies (Ajzen, 1991; Spears &
Singh, 2004). Therefore, unde rstanding the factors that influence purchase intentions is essential
for explaining consumer responses to social brand engagement.

Methodology
Research Design
This study adopts a quantitative research design to examine the relationships among social brand
engagement, brand awareness, brand association, trust, and purchase intentions. A quantitative
approach is appropriate because it allows the researcher to empirically test the proposed
hypotheses and examine the strength and direction of relationships among the study variables
using statistical techniques. The  study is explanatory in nature, as it seeks to explain how social
brand engagement influences purchase intentions through mediating and moderating mechanisms.
The research follows a cross -sectional design, in which data were collected from respondents at a
single point in time. This design is commonly used in social media and consumer behavior
research due to its efficiency and suitability for examining perceptions, attitudes, and behavioral
intentions. The unit of analysis in this study is the individual consumer who engages with brands
on social media platforms.

Discussion
Introduction
This chapter discusses the findings of the study in relation to the proposed research objectives and
hypotheses. The purpose of this discussion is to interpret the empirical results obtained through
PLS-SEM analysis and to explain their theoretical and practical implications. The fi ndings are
discussed by comparing them with prior studies in the area of social media marketing, brand
engagement, and consumer behavior. Each hypothesis is examined to understand how social brand
engagement influences purchase intentions through brand awareness, brand association, and trust.
Discussion of Direct Relationships
Brand Engagement and Brand Awareness (H1)
The results of the study indicate that social brand engagement has a significant and positive impact
on brand awareness. This finding suggests that consumers who actively engage with brands on
social media platforms are more likely to recognize and recall those brands during purchase
situations. Interactive activities such as liking, commenting, and sharing brand -related content
increase consumers’  exposure to the brand, thereby strengthening brand visibility and recall. This
result is consistent with previous research, which highlights the role of engagement in enhancing
brand familiarity and recognition. Social media provides repeated b rand exposure in an interactive
environment, making the brand more salient in consumers’  minds. Therefore, the findings support
the argument that social brand engagement is an effective strategy for building brand awareness in
competitive digital markets.
Brand Engagement and Brand Association (H2)
The findings also reveal a significant positive relationship between social brand engagement and
brand association. This implies that consumers ’  interactions with brands on social media help
shape the meanings, b eliefs, and perceptions they attach to those brands. Through engagement,
consumers are exposed to brand values, stories, and peer -generated content, which contribute to
the development of stronger and more favorable brand associations. This result aligns with existing
literature suggesting that engagement allows consumers to co -create brand meaning and
strengthens emotional and cognitive connections with brands. When consumers perceive brand -
related interactions as meaningful and authentic, they are more li kely to form positive associations
related to quality, credibility, and social value.

Brand Awareness and Purchase Intentions (H3)
The study finds that brand awareness has a significant positive effect on purchase intentions. This
indicates that consumers are more likely to intend to purchase brands that they can easily recognize
and recall. Familiar brands reduce uncertainty and perceived risk, making consumers more
confident in their purchase decisions. This finding supports prior research suggesting th at brand
awareness plays a crucial role in the early stages of consumer decision -making. In social media
contexts, enhanced brand awareness resulting from engagement activities increases the likelihood
that consumers will consider the brand when making purchase decisions.
Brand Association and Purchase Intentions (H4)
The results further demonstrate that brand association significantly influences purchase intentions.
Positive brand associations, such as perceptions of quality, reliability, and emotional appeal,
enhance consumers ’  evaluations of a brand and increase their willingness to purchase. This
finding is consistent with brand equity theory, which emphasizes the importance of strong brand
associations in driving consumer behavior. Social media engag ement strengthens these
associations by enabling continuous interaction and experience -sharing, ultimately leading to
stronger purchase intentions.
Discussion of Moderation Effect
Moderating Role of Trust (H5)
The moderation analysis reveals that trust sig nificantly strengthens the relationship between brand
association and purchase intentions. This suggests that even when consumers hold positive brand
associations, trust plays a crucial role in determining whether these perceptions translate into
purchase intentions.
When trust levels are high, consumers are more confident in relying on their brand -related
perceptions and are more willing to make purchase decisions. Conversely, low trust weakens the
effect of brand associations on purchase intentions. This finding highlights the importance of trust
in online environments, where uncertainty and perceived risk are higher.
Discussion of Mediation Effects
Mediating Role of Brand Awareness (H6)
The results confirm that brand awareness mediates the relationship be tween social brand
engagement and purchase intentions. This indicates that social brand engagement enhances brand
awareness, which in turn increases consumers ’  purchase intentions. Engagement alone may not
directly lead to purchase intentions unless it fir st improves consumers ’  ability to recognize and
recall the brand. This finding supports the notion that brand awareness serves as an important
mechanism through which engagement activities influence consumer decision-making.
Mediating Role of Brand Association (H7)
Similarly, the findings demonstrate that brand association mediates the relationship between social
brand engagement and purchase intentions. Social brand engagement strengthens brand
associations, which subsequently influence consumers ’  willingn ess to purchase. This result
highlights the importance of emotional and cognitive brand perceptions in explaining how
engagement translates into behavioral intentions. Together, the mediation findings indicate that
social brand engagement affects purchase intentions indirectly through multiple brand -related
mechanisms.

Overall Discussion
Overall, the findings of this study provide strong empirical support for the proposed moderated
mediation model. Social brand engagement influences purchase intentions through the combined
effects of brand awareness and brand association, while trust strengthens the impact of brand
associations on purchase intentions. These results contribute to a deeper understanding of

Conclusion And Recommendations
Conclusion
The primary objective of this study was to examine the role of social brand engagement in shaping
consumers’  purchase intentions in a social media  context. Specifically, the study investigated how
social brand engagement influences purchase intentions through the mediating roles of brand
awareness and brand association, while also examining the moderating role of trust. Using a
quantitative research approach and applying Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling
(PLS-SEM), the study empirically tested the proposed relationships.
The findings of the study indicate that social brand engagement has a significant positive impact
on both brand awareness and brand association. This suggests that consumers who actively interact
with brands on social media platforms are more likely to recognize brands and develop stronger
perceptions and meanings associated with them. Furthermore, the results reveal that brand
awareness and brand association significantly influence purchase intentions, highlighting the
importance of brand-related perceptions in consumer decision-making.
In addition, the mediation analysis confirms that brand awareness and brand associ ation act as
important mechanisms through which social brand engagement translates into purchase intentions.
This implies that engagement alone is not sufficient; rather, engagement must first enhance
consumers’  awareness and associations with the brand to  influence their intention to purchase.
Moreover, the moderation results demonstrate that trust strengthens the relationship between brand
association and purchase intentions, emphasizing the critical role of trust in online environments.
Overall, the find ings provide strong empirical support for the proposed research model and
contribute to a better understanding of consumer behavior in social media marketing contexts.
Theoretical Implications
From a theoretical perspective, this study contributes to the existing literature on social media
marketing and consumer behavior in several ways. First, the study extends consumer engagement
theory by demonstrating that social brand engagement influences purchase intentions indirectly
through brand awareness and brand association. By integrating these mediating mechanisms into a
single framework, the study provides a more comprehensive explanation of how engagement
affects consumer decision-making.
Second, the inclusion of trust as a moderating variable enriches the brand equity literature by
highlighting the conditional role of trust in strengthening the impact of brand associations on
purchase intentions. While prior studies have examined trust as a direct predictor of consumer
behavior, this study demonstrates its importance as a contextual factor that shapes the effectiveness
of brand-related perceptions. As such, the findings offer valuable insights for researchers seeking
to develop more nuanced models of consumer behavior in digital environments.

Practical Implications
The findings of this study offer several practical implications for marketers and brand managers.
First, the results suggest that brands should focus on creating engaging and interactive content on
social media platforms to enhance consumer engage ment. Activities such as responding to
comments, encouraging user -generated content, and collaborating with influencers can help
increase brand awareness and strengthen brand associations. Second, marketers should recognize
the importance of building trust  in online environments. Transparent communication, consistent
messaging, and delivering on brand promises can enhance consumers ’  trust and increase the
likelihood that positive brand perceptions will translate into purchase intentions. By prioritizing
engagement, awareness, association, and trust-building strategies, brands can improve their overall
effectiveness in influencing consumer purchase behavior through social media.
Limitations
Despite its contributions, this study has certain limitations that sh ould be acknowledged. First, the
study employed a cross -sectional research design, which limits the ability to establish causal
relationships among the variables. Future studies may use longitudinal designs to examine changes
in consumer perceptions and behavior over time.
Second, the data were collected from a specific context, which may limit the generalizability of the
findings to other countries or cultural settings. Additionally, the study relied on self -reported data,
which may be subject to response bias. These limitations should be considered when interpreting
the results of the study.
Future Research Directions
Future research may build upon the findings of this study in several ways. Researchers may
explore additional mediating variables, such as b rand loyalty or emotional attachment, to further
explain the relationship between social brand engagement and purchase intentions. Moreover,
future studies may examine the role of other moderating factors, such as perceived risk or cultural
values, to gain  deeper insights into consumer behavior. Additionally, comparative studies across
different industries or countries could enhance the generalizability of the findings. By addressing
these areas, future research can further advance the understanding of soci al brand engagement and
its impact on consumer decision-making.
