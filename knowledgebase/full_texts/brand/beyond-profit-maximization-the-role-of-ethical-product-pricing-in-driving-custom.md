Uganda’s petroleum retail industry operates within a volatile pricing environment characterized by global oil
price fluctuations, taxation dynamics, and intense competition among fuel stations. While price competition
remains central, ethical product pricing practices such as transparency, fairness, consistency, and honesty
in fuel measurement are increasingly influencing long-term customer relationships. This study examined the
influence of ethical product pricing on customer loyalty, focusing on Stabex Uganda Limited in Wakiso District.
Grounded in pricing fairness theory and equity theory advanced by John Stacey Adams, the study adopted a
quantitative correlational cross-sectional design supported by qualitative insights. Data were collected from 80
respondents, including employees, retail customers, and commercial clients. Descriptive statistics, Pearson
correlation, and regression analyses were conducted using SPSS. Results revealed a strong positive and
statistically significant relationship between ethical product pricing and customer loyalty (r = 0.689, p < 0.01).
Ethical pricing practices explained 46.8% of the variance in loyalty (Adjusted R² = 0.468). Constructs such
as price transparency, perceived fairness, consistency, and honesty in measurement significantly influenced
repeat purchase intentions and brand advocacy. The study concludes that ethical pricing is a strategic
determinant of sustainable loyalty in Uganda’s petroleum sector and recommends transparent pricing policies,
customer communication strategies, and strengthened regulatory compliance.

Customer loyalty has become a strategic priority in industries characterized by standardized products and minimal differentiation. In Uganda’s
petroleum retail sector, fuel products are largely homogeneous, and price changes are highly visible to consumers. Consequently, while competitive
pricing remains important, ethical product pricing practices increasingly determine whether customers remain loyal to a particular service station.
Loyalty today extends beyond economic value; it reflects perceptions of fairness, transparency, and integrity in pricing decisions (Kotler & Keller,
2016).
Uganda’s petroleum industry has expanded significantly, with multinational and indigenous firms such as TotalEnergies, Shell, Vivo Energy Uganda,
and Stabex Uganda Limited competing within urban and peri-urban markets. In districts such as Wakiso and Kampala, customers often encounter
multiple fuel stations within short distances. Under such conditions, perceptions of unfair price increases, hidden charges, inaccurate fuel dispensing,
or inconsistent pricing can quickly erode trust and trigger brand switching.
Ethical product pricing refers to the adoption of transparent, consistent, and justifiable pricing strategies that align with legal standards and customer
expectations. Pricing fairness theory posits that customers evaluate prices not only based on monetary cost but also on perceived justice relative to
reference points (Xia, Monroe, & Cox, 2004). Equity theory further suggests that customers assess fairness by comparing their input–output ratios
with those of others (Adams, 1965). When consumers perceive exploitation or opportunistic pricing, negative reactions such as dissatisfaction and
reduced loyalty may result.

Literature review
The concept of ethical product pricing is deeply rooted in several
theoretical traditions within marketing, economics, and social
psychology. Among the most influential frameworks explaining
customer reactions to pricing practices are equity theory, price fairness
theory, distributive and procedural justice theory, stakeholder theory,
and relationship marketing theory. Together, these perspectives provide
a comprehensive explanation of how ethical pricing shapes customer
loyalty in competitive sectors such as Uganda’s petroleum industry.
Equity theory, originally proposed by John Stacey Adams, posits that
individuals evaluate fairness by comparing their input–output ratios with
those of others (Adams, 1965). In a consumption context, inputs may
include money spent, time invested, and effort exerted, while outputs
include product value, service quality, and perceived benefits. When
customers perceive that the price paid for fuel is proportionate to the
value received—considering factors such as accurate measurement,
service quality, and convenience—they experience a sense of equity.
Conversely, perceived overpricing, hidden charges, or fuel adulteration
creates a sense of inequity, which can result in dissatisfaction and
withdrawal behaviors. Equity theory thus explains how perceived
pricing injustice undermines trust and loyalty in petroleum retailing
environments.
Closely related is price fairness theory, which suggests that consumers
evaluate prices not only in absolute monetary terms but also in relation
to internal reference prices, competitor prices, and perceived cost
justifications (Xia, Monroe, & Cox, 2004). Consumers often rely on
reference price frameworks shaped by past experiences and market
information. When price increases are perceived as reasonable and
justified—such as due to global oil price changes—customers are more
likely to accept them. However, when increases appear opportunistic or
inconsistent with broader market conditions, customers may interpret
them as exploitative. In Uganda’s petroleum sector, where price
information is highly visible and frequently discussed in public discourse,
fairness perceptions are particularly salient.
Theoretical Review
In petroleum retailing, ethical pricing involves accurate fuel measurement,
clear display of prices, timely communication of changes, and avoidance
of deceptive practices. Because fuel is an essential commodity affecting
transportation, production, and household expenditure, pricing decisions
are socially sensitive. Customers are highly attentive to even small price
variations. Therefore, trust and ethical conduct may be as critical as
price level itself.
Despite the importance of pricing ethics, most studies in Uganda focus
on taxation, supply chain management, and regulatory compliance, with
limited empirical analysis of how ethical pricing influences customer
loyalty outcomes. This study seeks to address this gap by examining
the influence of ethical product pricing on customer loyalty at Stabex
Uganda Limited.
Specific Objectives
1. To assess the level of perceived ethical product pricing among
customers in petroleum service stations.
2. To determine the level of customer loyalty among customers of
Stabex Uganda Limited.
3. To examine the relationship between ethical product pricing and
customer loyalty in Uganda’s petroleum retail sector.
4. To determine the predictive effect of ethical pricing dimensions
(transparency, fairness, consistency, and honesty in measurement)
on customer loyalty outcomes such as repeat purchase intention
and brand advocacy.
Justice theory further enriches understanding of ethical pricing by
distinguishing between distributive, procedural, and interactional
justice (Tax, Brown, & Chandrashekaran, 1998). Distributive justice
concerns the perceived fairness of outcomes—in this case, whether
the price paid is fair relative to the product received. Procedural justice
relates to the fairness of processes used to determine prices, including
transparency in price setting and communication. Interactional justice
involves respectful and honest communication when explaining price
changes. Research shows that when companies communicate price
adjustments clearly and respectfully, negative customer reactions are
mitigated even if the price itself increases (Bolton, Warlop, & Alba,
2003). Thus, ethical pricing extends beyond numerical price levels to
include fairness in procedures and communication.
Stakeholder theory also provides a relevant theoretical lens. According
to Freeman (1984), organizations have ethical responsibilities not
only to shareholders but also to stakeholders, including customers,
employees, regulators, and communities. Ethical pricing practices reflect
a firm’s commitment to balancing profitability with social responsibility.
In petroleum retailing, fuel pricing directly affects transportation costs,
commodity prices, and household expenditures. Therefore, pricing
decisions carry broader social implications. A stakeholder-oriented firm
is more likely to prioritize long-term trust and societal legitimacy over
short-term profit maximization, thereby fostering sustainable customer
loyalty.Relationship marketing theory further explains how ethical
pricing strengthens long-term relational bonds. Morgan and Hunt’s
(1994) commitment–trust theory argues that trust and commitment are
central mediators of successful relational exchanges. Ethical pricing
enhances trust by signaling honesty and reliability. When customers
believe that a petroleum company consistently practices fair pricing
and accurate measurement, they are more likely to develop affective
commitment. Commitment, in turn, reduces switching behavior even
when competitors offer marginally lower prices. In highly competitive
fuel markets with minimal product differentiation, relational factors such
as trust may outweigh short-term price advantages.
Signaling theory offers additional explanatory power. Spence (1973)
argues that firms communicate quality and integrity through observable
signals. Transparent price displays, standardized pump calibration
certificates, and publicized compliance with regulatory standards
serve as signals of ethical conduct. Such signals reduce information
asymmetry between firms and consumers, thereby increasing
perceived credibility. In contexts where consumers may suspect fuel
adulteration or inaccurate measurement, credible signals of ethical
pricing significantly enhance loyalty.
Behavioral economics also contributes to understanding customer
responses to pricing. Prospect theory, developed by Kahneman
and Tversky (1979), suggests that individuals are more sensitive to
perceived losses than equivalent gains. An unexpected or unexplained
fuel price increase may therefore be perceived as a loss, triggering
stronger negative reactions than the positive response generated by
an equivalent price decrease. Ethical pricing practices that emphasize
transparency and predictability can mitigate such loss perceptions and
preserve loyalty.
Collectively, these theoretical perspectives converge on the proposition
that ethical pricing influences loyalty through fairness perceptions, trust
formation, and relational commitment. In Uganda’s petroleum retail
context, where price fluctuations are frequent and consumer scrutiny
is high, ethical pricing practices serve as a strategic mechanism for
building durable customer relationships.

Empirical research across industries consistently demonstrates that
perceived price fairness significantly influences customer satisfaction,
trust, and loyalty. Bolton et al. (2003) found that customers’ perceptions
of price fairness directly affected repurchase intentions, particularly
when price increases were inadequately explained. Their study revealed
that transparent communication mitigates negative responses to higher
prices. This finding is particularly relevant in petroleum retailing, where
global oil price fluctuations frequently necessitate adjustments.
Chaudhuri and Holbrook (2001) established that brand trust and brand
affect positively influence brand loyalty and market share. Although their
study did not focus exclusively on pricing, trust emerged as a central
determinant of loyalty outcomes. Ethical pricing contributes directly
to brand trust by reinforcing perceptions of honesty and reliability.
In petroleum markets, where suspicions of pump tampering or price
manipulation may arise, trust derived from ethical pricing becomes a
competitive asset. Similarly, Martin, Ponder, and Lueg (2009) found
that perceived price unfairness triggers negative emotional responses
such as anger and betrayal, which reduce purchase intentions and
increase negative word-of-mouth. Their findings emphasize that pricing
decisions have psychological as well as economic consequences. In
fuel retail environments where word-of-mouth spreads rapidly within
communities, perceptions of unethical pricing can significantly damage
brand reputation.
Research by Xia et al. (2004) demonstrated that price fairness
perceptions are influenced by social comparisons and perceived
justifications for price differences. Customers are more accepting of
price variations when they understand cost drivers such as taxation or
exchange rate fluctuations. In Uganda, where fuel price adjustments
are often linked to international crude oil markets and foreign exchange
movements, effective communication about these drivers may enhance
fairness perceptions and loyalty.
Empirical studies in service industries further confirm the relationship
between ethical practices and loyalty. Kumar and Shah (2004)
observed that transparent pricing and loyalty programs contribute to
long-term profitability by increasing customer retention. Their research
suggests that retaining customers through ethical practices is more
cost-effective than acquiring new customers through aggressive price
competition.
In emerging markets, relational and ethical dimensions may exert
even stronger influence. Nwankwo and Gbadamosi (2011) argued that
African consumers place high value on trust and relational integrity.
Ethical misconduct, particularly in essential goods markets, can lead
to rapid erosion of reputation. Conversely, firms perceived as fair and
community-oriented enjoy stronger customer commitment. These
findings align with stakeholder theory, emphasizing the importance of
social legitimacy in developing economies. Recent empirical evidence
also highlights the mediating role of trust. In a study examining retail
banking customers, Brunner, Ullrich, and De Oliveira (2018) found
that perceived price fairness positively influenced trust, which in turn
predicted loyalty intentions. This mediating effect suggests that ethical
pricing may not directly cause loyalty but does so indirectly through
trust formation. Applying this logic to petroleum retailing implies that
consistent, transparent pricing builds trust, which subsequently
enhances repeat purchase behavior and advocacy.
Furthermore, research on complaint behavior indicates that procedural
justice during price disputes significantly influences loyalty recovery
(Tax et al., 1998). When customers perceive respectful treatment and
clear explanations, they are more likely to forgive pricing grievances. In
fuel retailing, effective resolution of disputes related to pump accuracy
or billing errors may prevent defection.
Empirical Review
This study examined the relationship between advertising and customer
loyalty at Stabex Uganda Limited using a quantitative correlational
cross-sectional survey design complemented by qualitative elements.
The quantitative approach was adopted because the key variables
of the study were measurable in numerical form and suitable for
statistical analysis. The correlational design enabled the researcher
to determine the nature and strength of the relationship between
advertising and customer loyalty without manipulating the study
variables. The cross sectional aspect of the design was appropriate
because data were collected from respondents at a single point in time,
allowing the researcher to capture prevailing perceptions, attitudes,
and experiences regarding advertising practices and customer loyalty
within the organization. In addition, a descriptive research orientation
was incorporated to document and explain the existing conditions
related to advertising strategies and customer loyalty. The descriptive
component made it possible to systematically present respondents’
opinions and views on how advertising influences their loyalty to the
company. Although the study was primarily quantitative, qualitative
data were also collected to enrich the findings and provide deeper
insights, thereby enhancing triangulation and improving the overall
robustness of the study.
The study was conducted at Stabex Uganda Limited. The company
was selected because it was accessible to the researcher and provided
an appropriate context for investigating advertising activities and
their influence on customer loyalty. The target population comprised
employees and customers of the company. Employees were
included because they are directly involved in planning, designing,
and implementing advertising strategies, as well as interacting with
customers on a daily basis. Customers, both retail and commercial
(bulk) clients, were included because they are the recipients of
advertising messages and the ultimate beneficiaries of the company’s
services. Their perceptions and experiences were critical in determining
whether advertising efforts translated into customer loyalty. The total
population consisted of 86 individuals categorized as senior staff, other
staff, retail customers, and commercial customers.
A stratified sampling design was employed to ensure adequate
representation of all key categories within the population. The population
was divided into four strata: senior staff, other staff, retail customers,
and commercial customers. Stratification enhanced representativeness
by ensuring that each subgroup was proportionately included in the
study, thereby improving the reliability and validity of the findings (Lohr,
2021). Within this framework, both probability and non-probability
sampling techniques were utilized depending on the characteristics of
each stratum. Retail customers were selected using convenience and
judgmental sampling techniques due to their unpredictable schedules
Despite extensive global evidence linking ethical pricing to loyalty,
context-specific studies within Uganda’s petroleum sector remain
limited. Most local research focuses on supply chain efficiency,
regulatory compliance, or taxation impacts, with minimal emphasis on
consumer fairness perceptions. This empirical gap underscores the
necessity of examining how ethical pricing dimensions transparency,
fairness, consistency, and measurement honesty predict loyalty
outcomes in Uganda’s competitive petroleum retail environment.
In summary, empirical literature consistently demonstrates that ethical
pricing enhances trust, satisfaction, and long term loyalty. Fair and
transparent pricing reduces negative emotional reactions, strengthens
relational bonds, and improves reputational capital. In industries
characterized by standardized products and frequent price changes,
such as petroleum retailing, ethical pricing may serve as a more
sustainable driver of loyalty than price competition alone.

selection bias (Amin, 2005). Senior staff were selected purposively
because of their strategic positions and in-depth knowledge regarding
advertising decisions within the company. Other staff members were
selected through simple random sampling to ensure fairness and
reduce sampling bias.
The sample size was determined using the Krejcie and Morgan (1970)
table for determining sample size from a given population. From a
total population of 86 respondents, a sample of 80 respondents was
obtained. The sample comprised 10 senior staff selected purposively,
20 other staff selected randomly, 40 retail customers selected
through convenience and judgmental sampling, and 10 commercial
customers selected through random sampling. All 80 questionnaires
distributed were returned, resulting in a response rate of 100 percent.
This response rate was considered satisfactory and exceeded the
acceptable minimum threshold for quantitative research (Mugenda
& Mugenda, 2003). The high response rate was achieved through
continuous follow up and close supervision during the data collection
process.
Both primary and secondary data sources were utilized in the study.
Primary data were collected using semi-structured questionnaires
and key informant interviews. The questionnaire consisted of both
closed-ended and open-ended questions designed in line with the
study objectives. Closed-ended questions enabled quantitative
measurement using a five-point Likert scale, while open-ended
questions allowed respondents to provide additional explanations
and insights. Key informant interviews were conducted mainly with
senior staff and selected respondents to obtain in-depth information
and clarify issues related to advertising strategies and customer
loyalty. The use of questionnaires was appropriate because it allowed
the collection of large amounts of data within a short period, ensured
anonymity, and reduced costs (Amin, 2005). Interviews complemented
the questionnaires by facilitating deeper exploration of complex
issues and enabling clarification of responses. Secondary data were
obtained from textbooks, academic journals, organizational reports,
newspapers, magazines, and credible online sources to provide
theoretical and contextual grounding for the study.
To ensure data quality, both validity and reliability were addressed.
Content validity was established by seeking expert judgment from the
university supervisor and three research experts who assessed the
relevance of the instrument items in relation to the research objectives
and conceptual framework. The Content Validity Index (CVI) was
computed by dividing the number of items rated as relevant by the total
number of items. The resulting CVI of 0.8 exceeded the recommended
minimum of 0.7, indicating that the instrument adequately measured
the intended constructs (Amin, 2005; Kothari, 2004). Reliability was
assessed using the internal consistency method through Cronbach’s
alpha coefficient. Reliability refers to the degree to which an instrument
consistently measures a construct (Thissen, 2000). In line with
established guidelines, alpha coefficients of 0.6 and above were
considered acceptable (Sekaran, 2001). All study variables achieved
acceptable reliability coefficients, confirming the consistency of the
measurement instrument.
Data collection procedures followed formal authorization processes.
The research instruments were pre-tested and approved by the
university supervisor. An introductory letter was obtained from the
university and presented to the management of Stabex Uganda
Limited to seek permission to conduct the study. Upon approval,
questionnaires were administered to the selected respondents, and
interviews were conducted with key informants. Follow-up measures,
including telephone calls and physical visits, were employed to ensure
timely completion and return of the questionnaires.
Data processing involved editing, coding, classification, and tabulation
to ensure accuracy, consistency, and completeness. A computer
was used for data entry and processing to enhance speed and
minimize errors. Quantitative data were analyzed using the Statistical
Package for Social Scientists (SPSS). Descriptive statistics, including
frequencies, percentages, means, and standard deviations, were used
to summarize the data. Inferential statistics, particularly correlation and
regression analysis, were employed to determine the strength and
direction of the relationship between advertising and customer loyalty
and to assess the predictive effect of advertising on loyalty (Cohen
et al., 2013; Sekaran, 2003). Qualitative data were analyzed using
thematic and content analysis techniques. Responses were organized
into categories and themes based on their relevance to the research
questions. Similar responses were grouped together, interpreted,
and integrated with quantitative findings to provide a comprehensive
understanding of the study phenomenon.
Ethical considerations were observed throughout the research process.
Authorization was obtained from the university and permission was
granted by the management of Stabex Uganda Limited before data
collection commenced. Respondents were informed about the purpose
of the study and their role in the research process. Participation
was voluntary, and respondents were assured of confidentiality and
anonymity. Questionnaires did not require respondents to provide
their names, and all information collected was treated with strict
confidentiality and used solely for academic purposes.
The study variables were measured using a five point Likert scale
ranging from 1 (Strongly Disagree) to 5 (Strongly Agree). This scale
provided respondents with a broad range of response options and
enhanced consistency in measurement. Mean scores were interpreted
using predefined ranges to categorize responses from very low to
very high levels of agreement. Despite careful planning, the study
encountered limitations including limited access to up-to-date literature,
reliance on self-reported data, time constraints, and respondents’ busy
schedules. These challenges were mitigated through extensive review
of available secondary sources, inclusion of open-ended questions and
interviews for clarification, effective time management, and consistent
follow-up with respondents to ensure maximum participation.

Overall Interpretation

The study established that ethical product pricing is generally perceived
to be high and positively practiced at Stabex Uganda Limited. The
descriptive statistics revealed an overall mean score of 3.71, indicating
that respondents largely agree that pricing practices are fair, stable,
and customer-oriented. High mean scores for statements such as
price being a critical factor in enhancing customer satisfaction (Mean
= 3.90) and pricing involving consideration of actions that affect others
(Mean = 3.87) confirm that customers associate ethical pricing with
value fairness and responsible corporate conduct. These findings are
consistent with the equity theory of pricing, which posits that customers
evaluate price fairness by comparing what they sacrifice with the value
received. They also align with the findings of Xiaoyan Xia et al. (2004),
who established that perceived price fairness significantly influences
customer satisfaction and repurchase intentions.
The strong positive correlation (r = 0.671, p < 0.01) observed in this study
reinforces the argument that ethical product pricing is closely associated
with customer loyalty. This finding supports previous empirical research
by Valarie Zeithaml (1988), who found that perceived value, strongly
influenced by price perceptions, directly affects customer behavioral
intentions. Similarly, Richard Oliver (1999) emphasized that customer
loyalty develops when consumers consistently perceive fairness and
value in exchange relationships. The present study confirms that when
customers believe that pricing practices are transparent and justifiable,
they are more likely to maintain long term relationships with the firm.
Regression analysis further revealed that ethical product pricing explains
44.3% of the variation in customer loyalty (Adjusted R² = 0.443), with a
statistically significant positive beta coefficient (β = 0.671, p = 0.000).
This indicates that pricing practices are not merely associated with
loyalty but are significant predictors of it. The finding is consistent with
the relationship marketing theory advanced by Leonard Berry (1983),
which suggests that long-term customer relationships are strengthened
through trust-building mechanisms such as fairness and transparency.
Ethical pricing signals honesty and corporate integrity, thereby fostering
trust and commitment.
However, a critical nuance emerged from the descriptive findings:
although respondents generally agreed that pricing is customer-
oriented, many also perceived Stabex pricing as relatively high
compared to competitors (Mean = 4.04). This suggests that while
customers recognize fairness and transparency, price sensitivity
remains a concern. This finding mirrors the observations of Jagmohan
Raju and colleagues (1995), who argued that customers may tolerate
higher prices if they perceive superior value or quality, but excessive
price disparities can weaken loyalty over time. Therefore, ethical pricing
must balance fairness with competitiveness.
The results also suggest that ethical pricing has both direct and indirect
effects on customer loyalty. Directly, fair pricing reduces dissatisfaction
and perceptions of exploitation. Indirectly, it enhances corporate
reputation, trust, and emotional attachment, which are essential
components of loyalty. This supports the findings of Robert Morgan and

the relationship between firm practices and customer retention. In the

literature demonstrating a significant positive relationship between
ethical product pricing and customer loyalty. However, consistent
with previous studies, ethical pricing must remain competitive and

observed positive relationship.
Discussion of Findings
This study examined the effect of ethical product pricing on customer
loyalty at Stabex Uganda Limited. The findings established a strong,
positive, and statistically significant relationship between ethical product
pricing and customer loyalty (r = 0.671, p < 0.01). The regression results
further confirmed that ethical pricing is a significant predictor of customer
loyalty (β = 0.671, p = 0.000), explaining 44.3% of the variation in loyalty
levels among customers.
Descriptive findings revealed that customers generally perceive pricing
practices as fair, responsible, and customer oriented. Respondents
agreed that price plays a critical role in enhancing satisfaction and that
unreasonable price changes create perceptions of unfairness. These
results indicate that customers are sensitive not only to price levels but
also to pricing processes and transparency. Ethical pricing practices
such as consistency, fairness, and clear justification of price changes
significantly influence customer attitudes and behavioral intentions.
However, the study also identified an important concern regarding
perceived high pricing relative to competitors. Although customers
acknowledge fairness and value, price competitiveness remains a key
factor influencing long-term loyalty. This suggests that ethical pricing
must integrate fairness with strategic market positioning to sustain
customer retention.
The study therefore concludes that ethical product pricing significantly
enhances customer loyalty by fostering satisfaction, trust, and perceived
value. However, the strength of this relationship can be further
enhanced by ensuring competitive pricing structures and improved
communication strategies. Ethical pricing should be viewed not only as
a moral obligation but also as a strategic tool for long-term relationship
building and sustainable competitive advantage.

Conclusion and Recommendations
Recommendations
Based on the findings, the following recommendations are proposed to
strengthen the positive impact of ethical product pricing on customer
loyalty at Stabex Uganda Limited:
• Strengthening Pricing Transparency and Communication:
Management should enhance communication regarding pricing
structures, adjustments, and value justifications. Customers should
clearly understand the reasons behind price changes, especially
in response to market fluctuations. Transparent communication
builds trust and reduces perceptions of unfairness, thereby
strengthening loyalty.
• Balancing Fairness with Competitive Pricing: Although ethical
pricing practices are positively perceived, management should
continuously benchmark prices against competitors to ensure
competitiveness. Regular market analysis will help maintain
fairness while preventing customer migration due to perceived
price disparities.
• Implementing Stable and Predictable Pricing Policies: Sudden
and unexplained price fluctuations negatively affect customer
perceptions. The company should establish stable pricing policies
with gradual and well communicated adjustments to maintain
consistency and protect customer trust.
• Enhancing Customer Value Proposition: Ethical pricing should be
complemented by superior service quality, product reliability, and
customer engagement initiatives. When customers perceive that
price aligns with quality and service excellence, loyalty strengthens
even when prices are relatively higher.
• Establishing Customer Feedback Mechanisms: The company
should institutionalize structured feedback systems to continuously
monitor customer perceptions of pricing fairness. Regular surveys
and customer forums would provide insights into price sensitivity
and allow timely adjustments to maintain loyalty.
