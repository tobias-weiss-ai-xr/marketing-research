Abstract. At present, the domestic beauty market has formed a parallel development pattern
of two tracks.The local beauty brands represented by Huaxizi and Mao Geping focus on
traditional Chinese culture, deeply explore the connotation of Chinese aesthetics, and
complete the breakthrough of brand culture empowerment and market.International beauty
brands such as Estee Lauder continue to enter the Chinese market deeply, integrating local
marketing strategies through festival limited series, traditional Chinese packaging design
and traditional Chinese visual symbols it is in line with the aesthetics and preferences of
local consumers. Chinese aesthetic narrative has become a common centre strategy for
domestic and foreign beauty brands to compete for the Chinese consumer market and
establish brand cultural influence.From the perspective of business anthropology as the
basis of this study, choose the local benchmark brand Huaxizi and the leading international
beauty brand Estee Lauder as a case comparison, and use semiotics, cross-cultural
communication and orientalist critical theory to form a four-dimensional analysis
framework.Systematic comparison of the differences in Chinese aesthetic narrative carried
by these two types of brands. This study proves that local brands rely on local culture to
achieve endogenic reproduction, while international beauty brands use superficial cultural
symbols to make profits. There are many problems such as oriental aesthetic stereotypes and
superficial cultural appropriation.This article analyses the intrinsic tension between local
cultural construction and transnational symbol appropriation. In view of the limitations of
existing research institutes based on a single case or a single discipline, it provides a
theoretical and practical reference basis for the fact that local products have not gone to the
world market and international brands to achieve positive cross-cultural integration.
Keywords: Business anthropology, Chinese-style aesthetic narrative, beauty and makeup
brands, cultural appropriation, cross-cultural communication
1. Introduction
Since the full rise of China-Chic in 2018, Chinese aesthetics has broken through the niche circles of
cultural museums and intangible cultural heritage, and has fully penetrated the beauty and makeup
consumption sector. According to the data from the 2025 China-Chic Beauty Industry Consumption
Trend Report, the annual growth rate of traditional Chinese beauty products exceeds 25%. Young
consumers have significantly increased their willingness to pay a premium for products that embody

traditional aesthetics, and Chinese-style narratives have become a core competitive barrier in the
beauty industry [1]. The domestic beauty and makeup market has formed two development

cultural logics are distinctly different. Existing research is often limited to a single perspective of
marketing and communication, lacking the in-depth analysis of cultural power, the original context
of symbols, and consumer identity from the perspective of business anthropology. Furthermore,
there is a lack of systematic horizontal comparison between local and international brands. Business
anthropology focuses on cultural production, circulation, and negotiation in the consumption field,
distinguishing between two modes: the reproduction of local indigenous culture and the
appropriation of foreign capital symbols. It possesses unique analytical advantages. This article
revolves around four major issues: first, the selection of Chinese aesthetic symbols for two types of
brands and the differences in textual criticism logic; second, the distinction between long-term and
short-term frameworks for brand aesthetic narratives; third, the cultural infiltration and traffic
harvesting paths in omnimedia communication; fourth, the different impacts of the two narratives on
the public's perception of Chinese aesthetics and cultural inheritance. The research aims to reveal the
Orientalist dilemma of superficial Chinese-style marketing by international brands, summarize the
mature narrative experience of domestic products, fill the gap in comparative research in this field,
and provide a feasible solution for standardizing cultural narratives in the beauty and cosmetics
industry.
2. Literature review
Existing research is divided into three major areas: local beauty makeup China-Chic, localization of
international brands, and cultural criticism of Orientalism. The following text combines the analysis
of real and searchable literature from CNKI and foreign databases, and points out the shortcomings
of existing research and the innovations of this paper.
2.1. Research on Chinese-Chic aesthetics and the spread of China-Chic in local cosmetics
Taking Huaxi Zi as a core case, it is divided into three categories: new media communication, brand
symbol construction, and DTC marketing. Zhang Yang's Exploring China-Chic Brand Building and
Communication Strategies in the New Media Era: A Case Study of 'Huaxi Zi' is included in New
Media Research. It systematically dissects Huaxi Zi's omni-media matrix, including bilibili
documentaries, short video makeup looks, and offline traditional Chinese style spaces, pointing out
that the brand awakens youth cultural confidence by dismantling perceivable Eastern symbols and
building a channel for the popularization of traditional culture [2]. Chen Xi's article Analysis of DTC
Marketing Model for Domestic Beauty Brands in the Social Media Era published in Marketing
World proposes that domestic brands should directly engage with end consumers through Chinese

2.2. Research on cross-cultural marketing of international beauty makeup localization in
China
Wang S Estée Lauder's Marketing Strategy for Its Beauty Products in the Chinese Market,
confirmed through four years of market data from 2021 to 2024, reveals that Estée Lauder's core
approach to localization involves limited-edition packaging featuring traditional Chinese styles
during festivals and short video placements with local influencers. However, its traditional Chinese
design only incorporates superficial symbols such as red color and zodiac signs, without a long-term
plan for an Eastern aesthetic system [3]. At the domestic industry reporting level, the 2025 Industry
Observation section of People's Daily pointed out that domestic beauty brands are relying on deep
cultural narratives to accelerate their global expansion, while international brands only use
traditional Chinese style as a temporary marketing tool and have not established a sustainable
oriental aesthetic system [7].
2.3. Orientalism and critical study of beauty industry culture
The core theory of Said's Orientalism reveals the Western capital's curious, one-sided, and
instrumental interpretation of Eastern culture, forming a stereotypical impression of a "superficial
and exotic" place [8]. Orientalism in Western Beauty Branding, taking European and American
beauty and fragrance brands as samples, confirms that when Western brands incorporate Eastern
elements, they deliberately strip away the inherent philosophies such as leaving blank spaces and
the harmony between man and nature, only extracting visually complex and festive symbols, which
is a typical appropriation of commercial culture [9]. Domestic scholar Zhou Shangqin, drawing
from semiotics, proposes that the reproduction of indigenous endogenous culture possesses
homologous cultural roots, with symbolic transformation retaining its spiritual core; transnational
symbolic appropriation detaches from the original context, narrowing the public's complete
understanding of Chinese aesthetics through long-term dissemination [10].
2.4. Existing research shortcomings and innovations of this paper
There are four deficiencies in existing research' firstly, there are multiple single-brand cases, but
systematic horizontal comparisons between Huaxi Zi and Estée Lauder are lacking; secondly, there
are multiple perspectives from marketing and communication studies, but analyses from the
perspective of commercial anthropology, cultural power, and field observation are missing; Thirdly,
the critical perspective is weak, with little analysis of the risk of cultural appropriation in the beauty
and makeup industry from the perspective of Orientalism theory; fourthly, there is a disconnect
between theory and industry practice, with no differentiated optimization paths provided for

3. Case analysis
3.1. Basis for case selection
Huaxi Zi is a benchmark for local mid-to-high-end beauty makeup, with its brand foundation
entirely relying on Chinese native culture. It collaborates with intangible cultural heritage craftsmen
and classical makeup artists to complete long-term aesthetic construction, representing a typical
example of local cultural internal production. Several CNKI documents have identified it as a core
sample of China-Chic beauty makeup [2]. Estée Lauder, on the other hand, is a global leading
beauty makeup group with a mature localization layout in the Chinese market. It annually launches
limited-edition products with Chinese style, but its global product research and development, as well
as underlying aesthetics, follow Western standards. Chinese symbols are only used for short-term
festival marketing in China, representing a representative case of transnational commercial symbol
appropriation [3]. The audience, cultural foundation, price and narrative goals of these two brands
are completely contradicted and have strong comparative value.
3.2. Comparison of aesthetic symbols
The symbol of Huaxi Zi originates from four primary domains: the Jiangnan region, makeup styles
from the Tang, Song, Ming, and Qing dynasties, mother-of-pearl/silk intangible cultural heritage,
and the folk customs depicted in the Book of Songs. All visual elements have been pre-researched in
collaboration with experts in literature, history, and intangible cultural heritage [2]. The screening
criteria are "cultural integrity, ancient and modern suitability, aesthetic self-consistency", rejecting
fragment culture; abide by "maintaining the core", completely retaining the core of craftsmanship
and literary spirit, and simply changing the appearance suitable for modern makeup. For example,
the enamel makeup plate retains the inlay technology of Tang Dynasty lacquerware, shrinks the size,
and turns the complex grain into a simple appearance, which is more suitable for daily storage.
Symbols and product efficacy, brand mother narrative are highly unified, and all product line
symbols form a closed loop [6].
The aesthetic symbols used by Estée Lauder are limited to high-visual-impact festive elements
such as the Chinese zodiac signs, auspicious clouds, and red lanterns, with minimal use of deeper
aesthetic symbols such as classical literature, Song-style blank spaces, and intangible cultural
heritage craftsmanship [3]. There is no participation of literary and historical experts in the
screening, and only the marketing department selects according to the annual marketing popularity;
detached from the original context of the symbol's livelihood, there are only simple printing patterns
on the appearance of the product, the product logic and formula are completely produced according
to Western standards, and the symbols are only used as decorations for packaging. The annual
limited series of symbols are cut off, and there is no unified oriental mother narrative [9].
The symbols of Huaxizi are based, systematic, and closely culturally interlocked. The symbols of
Estee Lauder are unfounded, fragmented, and only used to decorate products, which is in line with

3.3. Comparison of narrative logic and periodic architecture
HuaXiZi prioritizes long-term closed-loop narratives in its culture. Its core narrative, "Oriental
makeup, nourishing makeup with flowers," guides the entire product category. It features three sub-
narratives: Jiangnan scenery, Tang and Song Dynasty makeup, and intangible cultural heritage
craftsmanship. Each product is paired with poetry and makeup scenes, forming a three-tier
progressive closed loop. Cultural inheritance takes precedence over short-term sales, with long-term
investments in makeup restoration, intangible cultural heritage documentaries, and collaborations
with traditional Chinese artists, ensuring the narrative has sustainable growth potential [2].
Estée Lauder prioritizes short-term holiday narratives over sales. It does not have a unified
narrative centered around the Eastern Mother, and only sets up a temporary short-term narrative
about the Chinese zodiac 1-2 months before the Spring Festival. Once the holiday is over, it
immediately discontinues related content. Its individual products rely solely on packaging visuals
for superficial expression, without accompanying beauty education or makeup systems. It does not
invest in long-term projects to restore traditional culture, and its narrative relies on short-term gift
box marketing, lacking independent cultural vitality [3, 9].
3.4. Comparison of all-media communication and user interaction
HuaXiZi has established a four-dimensional matrix encompassing long-form video cultural
popularization, short video practical makeup tutorials, immersive offline garden stores, and user-
generated content (UGC) activities such as #Restoring Ancient Flower Adornments#. This approach
integrates cultural education with practical implementation, transforming consumers into secondary
disseminators of Chinese aesthetics and fostering a long-term cultivation of public awareness and
appreciation for Eastern aesthetics [2].
Estée Lauder solely emphasizes the singular characteristics of Chinese aesthetics—joyous,
intricate, and ornate—while deliberately avoiding the core philosophies of simplicity, moderation,
and leaving blank spaces. Its long-term promotion has led the public to equate traditional Chinese
style with red festive packaging, which aligns with the Western imagination of the exotic East,
narrowing the complete connotation of Chinese aesthetics. This approach poses risks of aesthetic
stereotyping and cultural appropriation [8, 9].
4. The core tension between local regeneration and transnational symbol appropriation
4.1. Underlying contradictions between two cultural production models
From the perspective of business anthropology, there exists an irreconcilable tension between local
endogenous reproduction and transnational commercial appropriation in terms of cultural power
[10]. Huaxi Zi adheres to a local model where the cultural subject and the cultural matrix share a
common origin. Its narrative appeal lies in the dynamic inheritance of culture, with complete symbol
verification and retention of the spiritual core through transformation. The brand and consumers
resonate with a shared cultural identity, belonging to sustainable and equal cultural production,
which can expand the boundaries of modern expression of Chinese aesthetics [2, 7]. The foreign-
funded model of Estée Lauder: Western capital serves as the external cultural subject, with the sole
pursuit of short-term profit. It extracts superficial visual symbols in a one-way manner, detaches

4.2. Comparison of the advantages and disadvantages of narrative modes
The advantage of Huaxi Zi lies in its complete Chinese aesthetic system, which has built an
irreplaceable and differentiated competitive advantage, freeing itself from the low-price competition
of domestic products [6]; it integrates intangible cultural heritage and classical makeup to achieve
the popularization and dynamic inheritance of traditional culture [2]; its three-dimensional oriental
narrative awakens the cultural confidence of Generation Z, laying a cultural foundation for the
brand's overseas communication [7]. Its shortcomings lie in the high threshold of ancient books and
heavy intangible cultural heritage narratives, as well as the high cost of understanding for lower-tier
markets and ordinary overseas consumers; some high-end intangible cultural heritage collaborations
overly emphasize craftsmanship and visual appeal, sacrificing portability, affordability, and
practicality, which limits repurchasing by the public.
Est é e Lauder's advantage lies in its festival style packaging that fits the local gift giving scene,
which can boost sales of limited edition gift boxes in the short term and stabilize revenue in the
Chinese market; To a certain extent, expand the global exposure of Eastern visual symbols. The
inherent cultural flaw is the long-term solidification of Orientalist rigid aesthetics with a single
festive symbol, narrowing the public's perception of Chinese aesthetics [8, 9]; Without a long-term
unified Eastern narrative, the brand's local cultural foundation is hollow, and it is easy to be judged
as speculative Chinese style marketing; The lack of professional research on symbols and the misuse
of patterns and folk customs can easily lead to doubts from traditional culture enthusiasts; Only
borrowing cultural resources for profit, with very little investment in intangible cultural heritage
support and aesthetic popularization, resulting in a serious imbalance between commercial income
and cultural investment.
4.3. Industry development optimization route
In terms of aesthetic design based on product line stratification, the mass line adopts simple and
easy-to-understand symbolic elements such as landscapes and flowers, thereby lowering the
understanding threshold for consumers both domestically and internationally. The high-end line
fully embodies the profound narrative of intangible cultural heritage skills and Tang and Song
culture. The brand balances cultural expression with product practicality, prioritizing the basic use
functions of cosmetics, while craftsmanship and visual expression serve as additional value. At the
same time, a unified narrative system tailored for overseas communication is established, extracting
universal aesthetic cores such as the harmony between nature and humanity and natural flowers and
plants, avoiding niche folk customs that are difficult for overseas markets to empathize with, thus
reducing barriers to cultural communication [7]. Invest part of the revenue in the restoration of
intangible cultural heritage and support for traditional Chinese style creators, forming a sustainable
cycle of "material sourcing — monetization — cultural feedback". Establish a textual research team

5. Conclusion
The research perspective of this article is business anthropology. It builds a four-dimensional
analysis framework of aesthetic symbols, narrative logic, communication mode and aesthetic core.
Taking Hua Xizi and Estee Lauder as samples, it compares the differences between the Chinese
aesthetic narratives of local and international beauty brands, and draws the following conclusions in
combination with cases, cultural differences and industry development.
Firstly, there are fundamental differences in the application of Chinese aesthetics between local
and international beauty brands, resulting in two completely different development paths. The local
brand Huaxizi relies on native cultural resources such as Jiangnan scenery and intangible cultural
heritage crafts, and has completed aesthetic modernization transformation through rigorous cultural
research. It has built a coherent and complete brand aesthetic narrative system, combined with
immersive scenes and user co creation and dissemination, conveying the core of traditional Chinese
aesthetics, and enabling consumers to form a deep and stable cultural identity. Estée Lauder only
fragmented the use of visual symbols such as auspicious clouds and zodiac signs during festivals,
without delving deeply into cultural connotations. The Chinese style design relies on short-term
marketing activities, with traffic conversion as the core goal, which can easily create stereotypes of
Chinese aesthetics. The essence of the difference between the two is the underlying division of
cultural production models.
Secondly, there exists cultural power tension in the aesthetic narratives of the two types of
brands, and the primary and secondary positioning of culture and commercial value determines the
sustainability of the narrative. Local brands take cultural inheritance as their core goal, with
commercial value as a derivative outcome. Relying on homologous culture, they construct a virtuous
closed loop between the brand, consumers, and traditional culture, providing space for long-term
iterative growth in narrative. Consumer behavior combines attributes of usage and cultural support.
International brands are solely oriented towards short-term sales, viewing Chinese aesthetics as a
marketing tool. They extract local cultural resources in a one-way manner without cultural feedback,
making it difficult to retain long-term brand favor and resulting in weak user loyalty.
Again, both narrative modes have their own advantages and disadvantages, and need to be
optimized in combination with brand foundation differentiation. Local brands, leveraging a
complete Eastern aesthetic system, have escaped the industry's low-price competition and possess
the potential to go global culturally. However, the heavy and complex cultural narrative has raised
the threshold for understanding for the general public and overseas audiences. Some intangible
cultural heritage products overly emphasize craftsmanship and visual appeal, weakening the
practical attributes of cosmetics and restricting market repurchasing. International brands have a
mature marketing system and can quickly leverage traditional Chinese elements to increase revenue

markets, balance practicality and cultural dissemination, establish a cultural feedback mechanism,
and improve their overseas narrative system. International brands need to abandon short-term
speculative marketing, establish professional cultural research teams, create long-term Chinese style

has certain limitations. In the future, the research samples can be expanded, combined with
