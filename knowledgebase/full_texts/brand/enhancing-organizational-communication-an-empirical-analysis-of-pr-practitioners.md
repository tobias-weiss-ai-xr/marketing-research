Abstract

In today’s dynamic business environment, public relations (PR) plays a crucial role in facilitating effective
two-way communication between organizations and stakeholders. This research examines the impact of PR
on organisational communication, aiming to offer insights for improving communication strategies and
reputation management practices. The study is grounded in the Two -Way Symmetric Communication
Model and Excellence Theory, which emphasise balanced and ethical communication between PR
practitioners and diverse audiences. The Two -Way Symmetric Communication Model highlights mutual
dialogue and feedback, while Excellence Theory positions PR as a strategic management function focused
on buildi ng long -term, trust -based relationships. Together, these theories provide a comprehensive
framework for understanding how PR can enhance organisational communication, reputation management,
and relationships with key stakeholders. A conceptual framework focuses on three critical elements shaping
the role of PR practitioners: interpersonal skills, ethical climate, and social media involvement. As a
strategic management function, PR contributes to building and maintaining a positive reputation, enhancing
brand visibility, and fostering trust with customers, investors, and the public. Data were collected from 270
respondents at the Institute of Public Relations Malaysia (IPRM) using a questionnaire and quantitative
research approach. Descriptive statistics and correlation analysis examined relationships between the
independent variables (interpersonal skills, ethical climate, and social media involvement) and the
dependent variable (the role of PR practitioners in organizations). The findings offer insights into how these
factors influence the effectiveness of PR strategies in organisational communication.

1. Introduction
In the rapidly evolving landscape of contemporary business, organisational communication has
emerged as a critical determinant of success, with public relations (PR) practitioners playing a pivotal role
in bridging the gap between organizations and their d iverse stakeholders (Grunig, 2001). As businesses
navigate increasingly complex communication environments, the effectiveness of PR practitioners has
become paramount in shaping organisational narratives, managing reputations, and fostering meaningful
stakeholder relationships (Ledingham, 2003).
The dynamic nature of modern communication demands a multifaceted approach to PR that extends
beyond traditional communication models. Interpersonal skills, ethical considerations, and social media
engagement have become fundamental components of effective  organisational communication strategies
(Kent & Taylor, 2002). These elements collectively contribute to the strategic management of
organisational relationships, highlighting the transformative role of PR practitioners in contemporary
business contexts.
Interpersonal skills emerge as a critical foundation for effective communication, enabling PR
practitioners to navigate complex stakeholder interactions with nuance and empathy (Berger & Reber,
2006). The ability to communicate effectively, listen actively, and build meaningful relationships represents
a core competency that distinguishes exceptional PR practitioners from their counterparts. These skills
facilitate trust-building, conflict resolution, and strategic dialogue across various organisational interfaces.
Concurrently, the ethical climate within which PR practitioners operate has become increasingly
scrutinized in an era of heightened transparency and accountability (Fitzpatrick, 2006). Ethical
considerations now extend beyond mere compliance, demanding a h olistic approach that integrates moral
principles with strategic communication practices. PR practitioners must balance organisational objectives
with broader societal expectations, navigating complex ethical landscapes with integrity and
professionalism.
The proliferation of social media has fundamentally transformed communication dynamics,
presenting both unprecedented opportunities and challenges for PR practitioners (Breakenridge, 2012).
Digital platforms have democratized communication, enabling real -time interactions, instantaneous
information dissemination, and direct stakeholder engagement. PR practitioners must now possess
advanced digital literacy, strategic social media management skills, and the ability to leverage technological
platforms effectively.
This research seeks to empirically investigate the interconnected nature of these critical elements—
interpersonal skills, ethical climate, and social media involvement—to provide comprehensive insights into
the evolving role of PR practitioners in enhancin g organisational communication. By examining these
dimensions, the study aims to contribute to both theoretical understanding and practical applications in the
field of public relations

communication channels between an organization and its stakeholders. Its primary aim is to promote mutual
understanding, acceptance, and cooperation. According to Harlow (1976), PR involves managing issues,
ensuring organizations stay informed and responsive to public opinion, and emphasizing management’s
responsibility to serve the public interest. Additionally, it plays a key role in efficient change management,

Broom and Smith (1979) categorize public relations practitioners into four main roles: expert
prescriber, problem -solving process facilitator, communication technician, and communication process
manager. The expert prescriber role is held by individuals with advanced expertise who serve as consultants
to management, taking full responsibility for the decisions they provide. The problem -solving process
facilitator guides organizations through issue resolution, often involving managers in the decision -making
process. The communication technician focuses on managing the flow of information between management
and the public, executing tasks on a project basis without engaging in policy-making. The communication
process manager oversees the organization’s overall communication strategy, ensuring messaging aligns
with organisational goals. Acharya (1983) suggests that the environment in which an organization operates
influences the roles of PR practitioners. Managers typically work in more uncertain or high -risk
environments, while technicians tend to work in more stable settings.
The value of public relations within an organization is closely linked to management’s perception
of the PR department. Cutlip et al. (1985) argue that the level of importance PR holds in an organization
depends on how management views it. Grunig (1992) further explains that many managers misunderstand

ensure communication strategies align with the organization’s broader objectives. Kruckeberg (1998)
asserts th at PR is based on societal theories and professional values, promoting ethical practices and
excellence. PR practitioners are critical in building trust and credibility, which are essential for the

effective relationships between organizations and their stakeholders. Through transparency, honesty, and
ethical communication, PR professionals enhance the organization’s public image and credibility.
Guth and Marsh (2006) outline five essential principles that shape the role of public relations. These
include the managerial role, where PR supports the organization’s strategic goals; two-way communication,
emphasizing reciprocal dialogue between the org anization and its stakeholders; purposeful and structured
undertaking, ensuring PR strategies are intentional and well -organized to meet organisational objectives;
research-based, which grounds PR practices in social science and research to ensure effectiv eness; and
societal responsibility, demonstrating a commitment to the public good. Public relations practitioners are
also instrumental in fostering employee engagement and internal communication. Welch (2008) highlights
the importance of effective communication in maintaining employee loyalty, productivity, and morale. By
implementing internal communication strategies such as newsletters, network updates, and staff meetings,
PR professionals help keep employees informed and engaged with the organization’s goals. This
involvement nurtures a sense of commitment, enhancing the organization's culture and employee
satisfaction.

all of which are influenced  by the culture and history of a nation. Public relations practices vary across
countries, reflecting their unique historical and cultural contexts. Lamme and Miller (2010) argue that to

4.2. Interpersonal Skills
Interpersonal skills are central to effective public relations (PR) practice, particularly in building
trust, fostering rapport, and managing stakeholder relationships. According to Harris and Nelson (2008),
strong interpersonal communication enhances the relational dimension of organi sational communication,
which is critical in crisis settings and reputation management. Similarly, Sriramesh and Verčič (2009)
emphasised that PR practitioners must exhibit active listening, empathy, and adaptability in their
communication to align organisational objectives with stakeholder expectations.
Recent studies have also linked interpersonal competencies to internal communication outcomes.
For instance, Men (2014) found that interpersonal credibility significantly influence employees’ trust in
leadership and message acceptance. While these findings reinforce the relational role of PR professionals,
prior research often failed to empirically examine how interpersonal skills directly contribute to strategic
communication role within organizations. This study addresses that gap by examining interperson al skills
not merely as soft attributes, but as measurable factors influencing communication effectiveness in
organisational contexts.
Public relations  is inherently relational, as it focuses on creating, maintaining, and enhancing
relationships between organizations and their audiences. Therefore, PR practitioners must possess strong
interpersonal skills to engage with a variety of stakeholders, such as  clients, media representatives,
employees, and the general public . According to Ledingham and Bruning (2000), trust is a foundational
element in the success of public relations. Trust -building involves effective interpersonal communication,
which requires a PR practitioner to not only convey clear and consistent message s but also to understand
the perspectives and needs. By demonstrating empathy and understanding, PR professionals can establish
credibility and foster long-term, positive relationships.

4.3. Ethical Climate
The ethical climate within an organization shapes how communication is framed, interpreted, and
disseminated. Ethical norms guide decision -making, promote transparency, and ensure message
consistency—a point widely recognized in PR scholarship (Bowen, 2021; Place, 2010). Organizations that
foster an ethical climate encourage openness and accountability, aligning with the principles of the Two -
Way Symmetrical Communication Model proposed by Grunig and Hunt (1984).
Although various studies have explored the ethical dimensions of PR (Fitzpatrick & Gauthier, 2001),
many treat ethics primarily as a normative ideal rather than an operational construct. This study builds on
more recent empirical directions by investigatin g how ethical climate as perceived by PR practitioners
shapes their communication responsibilities. The findings aim to operationalize ethics in measurable terms
and assess its practical impact on organisational messaging.
PR practitioners are often faced dilemmas that require balancing organisational interest with ethical
principles such as honesty, transparency, and fairness. According to Grunig and Hunt (1984), public
relations extend beyond reputation management and involves fostering truthful, bidirectional
communication with stakeholders. Ethical tensions arise when practitioners encounter pressure to distort or
withhold information to protect organi sational interests. Bowen (2021) found that PR professionals
increasingly feel compelled to align personal values with organi sational directives, even when such
directives may conflict with public expectations of ethical behaviour.

One central challenge is the conflict between serving the public interest and maintaining loyalty to

The rise of digital platforms introduces additional ethical complexities, including challenges relate

better positioned to balance advocacy with public accountability and make choices aligned with both
professional ethics and stakeholder trust.
Despite growing awareness of ethical issues in digital PR, guidance remains limited on how these
dilemmas intersect with technological practices such as data use and online engagement. For practitioners
in Malaysia, this gap is compounded by cultural and o rganisational dynamics that uniquely shape ethical
decision-making. In high-context and collectivist cultures like Malaysia’s, where hierarchy and  harmony
are emphasised (Hofstede, 2001), ethical choices may be further influenced by deference to authority or
face-saving norms.  As such, there is a pressing need for context -specific studies that explore how
Malaysian PR practitioners interpret and apply ethical standards in both traditional and digital spheres. Such
research can contribute to developing frameworks that reflect both global ethical expectations and culturally
responsive communication practices.

4.5. Two-Way Symmetric Communication Model
The Two-Way Symmetrical Communication Model is a significant concept in public relations (PR)
that focuses on fostering trust and open dialogue between organizations and their stakeholders, including
customers, employees, and the wider community. This mode l, introduced by Grunig and Hunt (1984),
emphasises the importance of communication as an exchange, where both parties share information and
actively listen to each other’s views. The ultimate aim  is to establish long -lasting relationships based on
trust and mutual respect. The Two -Way Symmetrical Model departs from traditional one -way
communication approaches by promoting balanced dialogue, allowing organizations to engage
meaningfully with their audiences and better understand their needs and concerns.
The Two -Way Symmetrical Communication Model highlights the importance of transparent
communication between organizations and their audiences to foster mutual understanding. In the modern
communication landscape, PR practitioners can leverage this model to enhance credibility, demonstrate
capability, and build enduring relationships with stakeholders. In today’s society, where information flows
rapidly and expectations for responsiveness are high, adopting this model enables PR professionals to
manage relationships effectively. By prioritizing mutual understanding and engagement , PR practitioners
can strengthen an organization’s reputation and credibility, fostering long-term trust with various audiences.
One of the key benefits of the Two -Way Symmetrical Communication Model is its emphasis on
actively listening to feedback and integrating public input into organi sational decision -making. Unlike
traditional one-way communication methods, which only disseminate information from the organization to
the public, this model encourages PR professionals to engage in a dialogue with stakeholders. Social media
platforms have become crucial in facilitating such engagement. For example, PR practitioners can respond
to customer feedback on social media, not only by providing relevant information, but also by engaging in
conversations that identify problems and demonstrate a commitment to addressing concerns transparently.
This interaction helps shape the organization’s communication strategies and enhances the relationship with
stakeholders.
Kent and Taylor (2002) further emphasise that public relations should be rooted in a dialogic
approach, where the goal is to create genuine, reciprocal communication that leads to mutual understanding
and respect. The Two-Way Symmetrical Communication Model, in line with their theory, supports the idea
that organizations should not only communicate with their sta keholders but also engage with them, using
their feedback to inform future decisions. This type of communication goes beyond mere transactional
exchanges and aims to build deeper, more collaborative relationships with stakeholders.
The Two-Way Symmetrical Communication Model is particularly relevant in the context of modern
digital communication, where information flows rapidly in multiple directions. Advances in digital
communication technologies, including social media, have revolu tionized the way organizations interact
with their audiences. In this complex environment, PR practitioners must navigate multiple channels to
ensure that communication is balanced, transparent, and responsive. By applying the principles of
symmetrical communication, PR professionals can manage online conversations more efficiently, address

4.6. Excellence Theory
Excellence Theory, developed by Grunig et al. (2002), is a foundational framework in public
relations (PR) that underscores the importance of strategic communication in fostering organi sational
success. The theory posits that the effectiveness of PR depends on the alignment of communication
practices with organisational goals, the use of two-way symmetrical communication, and a commitment to
ethical communication. Excellence Theory suggests that organisations should not view PR merely as a tool
for managing publicity but as an integral component of the overall management process, crucial for
achieving mutual understanding with various stakeholders.
A key aspect of Excellence Theory is its emphasis on the role of PR practitioners in ensuring that
communication strategies align with organisational objectives and stakeholder interests. According to
Grunig et al. (2002), organizations achieve excellence in PR when they engage in two -way symmetrical
communication, where dialogue between the organization and its stakeholders is balanced and mutually
beneficial. This approach helps organizations build long-term relationships based on trust and transparency.
In this context, PR practitioners act as both strategists and communicators, facilitating open channels of
communication that allow for feedback and engagement.
In the study of PR’s impact on organisational communication, Excellence Theory provides valuable
insights into how communication strategies can influence organi sational reputation and decision -making
processes. By promoting open dialogue and collaboration with stakeholders, organi sations are better

This study adopted a quantitative approach to investigate the impact of public relations (PR) on

 Research framework

4.8. Literature Synthesis and Research Gap
While existing literature affirms the importance of interpersonal skills, ethical orientation, and
digital competencies in public relations, most studies tend to examine these constructs in isolation, often
without accounting for their interrelated influen ce in real organisational settings. Interpersonal skills are
typically explored in relation to relationship management, ethical orientation in the context of normative
frameworks, and digital competencies as technical proficiencies. However, in practice, t hese elements
rarely function independently. Public relations practitioners operate in increasingly complex environments
where ethical decision-making, interpersonal communication, and digital engagement intersect to shape the
effectiveness of internal and external communication strategies.
Despite this reality, there remains a lack of empirical research that integrates these competencies
into a single framework to assess their collective impact on communication performance within
organizations. The existing fragmentation in the literature limits our understanding of how these
competencies jointly contribute to practitioners' roles, particularly in managing stakeholder expectations,
enhancing organisational transparency, and navigating crisis communication.
By integrating these dimensions into a unified framework, this study addresses a critical gap and
aims to provide a more holistic and empirically grounded understanding of communication effectiveness

5. Methodology
The study employed a quantitative method to examine the relationship between interpersonal skills,
ethical climate, and social media involvement, with the role of PR practitioners in organizations. The
respondents were selected from PR practitioners in Malaysia who were registered members of the Institute
of Public Relations Malaysia (IPRM) in 2024. Following Israel's (2006) sample size determination
guidelines, a set of questionnaires (67 items) adopted from Romanian Journal of Communication and Public
Relations (2020) to measure the role of PR practitioners in organization (PRR), Don Hofstrand (2020) to
measure interpersonal skills (IS), Whitehead and Novak (2003) to measure ethical climate (EC), and
Mazurek et al. (2018) to measure the involvement of social media (SMI), were distributed among 270
respondents. The instruments were adapted to better fit the specific context of this study.

7. Discussion and Conclusion
The study found a moderate relationship between PR practitioners' roles and social media
involvement. Social media platforms provide PR professionals with valuable tools to track public
sentiment, manage potential reputation risks, and engage with audience s in real -time. This dynamic
engagement not only enhances brand awareness but also fosters trust and facilitates open communication
between organizations and stakeholders. By strategically using social media, PR practitioners can address
concerns swiftly, maintaining a positive organi sational image. These findings align with Brown et al.
(2018), who emphasi se that responsible social media use promotes transparency, builds trust, and
strengthens an organization's reputation. Similarly, Kietzmann et al. (2021) demonstrated that strategic
social media use enhances brand perception and encourages greater stakeholder engagement.
Beyond confirming positive associations, the findings extend the application of the Two -Way
Symmetrical Communication Model and Excellence Theory in the Malaysian context. Interpersonal skills,
ethical climate, and digital engagement collectively underscor e the need for PR practitioners to adopt
dialogic and ethically grounded approaches that align with organi sational and societal expectations. The
evidence suggests that practitioners who foster a supportive ethical climate and demonstrate strong
interpersonal communication are better positioned to navigate the complexities of real -time digital
communication. This provides theoretical support for positioning PR not only as a technical function but
as a strategic driver of organisational trust and legitimacy.
From a practical perspective, the study highlights three key recommendations for PR professionals
and organisational leaders. First, investment in continuous training for interpersonal communication and
conflict resolution can equip practitioners with the skills necessary to manage diverse stakeholder
expectations. Second, fostering a robust ethical climate throu gh organi sational policies and leadership
practices can help practitioners resolve ethical dilemmas and maintain credibility, particularly in digital
