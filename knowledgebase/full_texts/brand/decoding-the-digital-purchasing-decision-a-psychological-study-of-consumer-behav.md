Introduction: The current study is set amid modern marketing strategies;
whereby digital platforms are employed to shape customer behavior towards
buying products. In particular, the research focuses on analyzing the
psychological processes behind the formation of behaviors related to eating
food products in response to digital stimuli using the stimulus-organism-
response theory.
Methods: Descriptive research strategy was used, and a field survey was carried
out involving 402 people from Egypt, who represented the generations of
millennials and Generation Z.
Results: The study shows that there is an evident change in terms of digital trust,
whereby consumer reviews and rating have become the top determining factors
in making a purchase decision (46.3%) compared to other types of promotions.
This suggests that influencer marketing based on experience ranks second,
thereby emphasizing the importance of the organism state such as trust and
emotional attachment in influencing the behavioral outcomes of advertising
efforts. Despite the fact that 74.6% of surveyed customers stated that they had
purchased goods due to social media ads, their repetitive behavior is relatively
low, which means that there is a gap between increased awareness and brand
loyalty among customers. Additionally, ads containing logic, meaning giving
clear nutrition facts for instance, were preferred to emotional ones.
Discussion: It can be concluded that digital marketing is efficient in the case of
credibility and real-life experience instead of being visually appealing only.

1 Introduction
The digitalization of consumer markets has transformed the
way people search for information and evaluate such information
in making purchase decisions about food products. Social media
platforms have been identified as a means by which marketing
communication converge with consumer behaviour. At present,
social media is more than connecting people, it constitutes a total
marketing environment, in which visual elements, influencer
suggestion and interaction fuse together, driving consumer
behavior (Kumsawat et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2022). Such trend is
also witnessed in food markets that online video platforms such
as Instagram, Tiktok, Youtube have become the center stage for
cooking contents through billions of consumers in an atmosphere
that tells vivid emotional narratives (Truong et al., 2025; Zhang
et al., 2025). The development of content from live eating to food
vlog on social media has opened a new opportunity for marketers
to manipulate customers through psychological understanding
beyond common advertisement format (Bash et al., 2024; Chen
et al., 2024; Guo et al., 2024).
The psychological process by which food marketing in the digital
world affects consumption behaviour has become a critical matter for
both research and business practices. The research evidence so far
show that food content viewed online has an important impact on
the consumer from a psychological perspective and the factors
such as the credibility of and clarity of the food content creator
have been identified as determining factors affecting the
consumer’s behaviour and their tendency to purchase (Deng, 2024;
Fajri et al., 2025; Kumsawat et al., 2025). As the use of social
media as one of an important marketing channel for many firms,
there is an urgent need for applying marketing psychologies
derived from consumer’s psychologies, this is because the success
of running an advertisement using social media platform does not
depend only on its coverage and/or cost saving (in comparison
with other way of advertising) but it also depends on shaping
consumer’s behaviour and influencing their purchasing decision.
Although there has been increasing focus on social media
marketing and consumer behavior, it is necessary to acknowledge
that not enough work has been done concerning how food-
related content affects the purchase decisions of consumers.
Previous studies have identified several psychological paths
through which social media food advertising can affect consumer
attitudes and behavioral intentions including social influence,
visual attention, perceived credibility, and emotions (Kilumile
et al., 2025; Kumsawat et al., 2025). In this regard, studies have
identified the significant role that social influences play in
affecting consumer attitudes and behavioral intentions (Bakshy
et al., 2012). Further, the effect of food advertisements on
consumers can be attributed to various visual attributes like visual
complexity of advertisements (Farhat et al., 2024). Moreover,
researchers have indicated the influence of food-related content
posted on social media platforms on consumers’ visual attention
(Riswanto et al., 2025). On the other hand, food-related content
posted by food influencers on social media platforms has
attracted increasing interest among researchers due to the ability
to influence consumer engagement and purchase intention
(Kilumile et al., 2025; Kumsawat et al., 2025). However, recent
literature reviews indicate that research is still fragmented in that
no comprehensive studies exist on the influence of food-related
social media content on consumers from various cultures and on
various platforms (Truong et al., 2025). There is no research
focused on understanding the effect of social media on
consumers as a psychological platform and not a communication
tool. Similarly, limited research has been conducted on the use of
marketing psychologies in food advertisement in social media
from a developing country, especially Egypt. This study will aim
to fill this gap by analyzing the types of marketing psychology
employed in social media food advertising and their impact on
consumers’ behavioral intentions and purchase decisions.
In this regard, one can say that this research paper is aimed at
filling the existing research gap regarding the insufficient
consideration of psychological and neurological aspects together
with the frequency of purchasing actions in the context of
consumer behavior. In other words, previous studies have paid
much attention to consumers’ intentions and perceptions but
not actual actions and their frequency in the field of food.
The importance of this research consists in its focus on the study
of something else but not advertising effectiveness and savings
related to it. Its main objective is to explain the “psychology of
marketing” and find out how it influences people’s purchasing
actions and meets their deep psychological and mental needs.
Thus, in order to achieve the research objectives, there should
be answers provided to the following main questions: what attracts
people’s attention in terms of marketing campaigns on social
media networks, what kind of marketing and psychology affects
consumers’ purchasing decisions and trends, what role is played
by influencers in relation to consumers’ decision-making process
regarding food products, and to what extent the interaction
between a consumer and a brand helps form a positive
relationship. One of the expected outcomes is to prove that the
S-O-R model will successfully explain the chain of events from a
stimulus to action through psychology.
In order to further the research goals and fill in the existing
gaps, the current research intends to investigate the answers to
the following research questions:
RQ1: What particular types of digital marketing stimuli (visual
elements, social proof) are the best in terms of grabbing
consumer attention on social media platforms?
RQ2: What are the effects of marketing psychology and digital
stimuli on consumer behavior within the S-O-R paradigm?
RQ3: What role do social media influencers play in consumer
decision-making about food products?
RQ4: To what extent can consumer-brand interactions help
create a good relationship and lead to purchases?
One of the key anticipated results in addressing the research questions
will be proving that S-O-R theory correctly accounts for the processes
involved from a stimulus to behavior via psychology.
2 Literature review and theoretical
foundations
2.1 The stimulus-organism-response (S-O-
R) model
The Stimulus-Organism-Response S-O-R model is the more
advanced theory that helps explain consumer behavior on the

basis that the stimulus does not elicit any sort of response. It first
engages a process within the individual’s brain. Modern studies
have shown that the stimulus can have quite an influence on
how a person feels psychologically and cognitively, which
becomes the catalyst of the actual response rather than the
stimulus itself (Hongsuchon et al., 2025; Wang, 2025). In
conclusion, the S-O-R theory acts as a mediation mechanism
where the stimulus is the “spark” which begins the process,
while the organism represents the psychological process itself
and the response is purchasing.
2.2 Digital marketing stimuli (stimuli): visual
content, social proof, and influencer
techniques
In the food industry, marketing stimulus is usually centered
around visuals and sociability. Research suggests that social
media marketing and influence on the marketing of food lead to
increased intake of unhealthy foods among children and young
people (Coates et al., 2019). Seeing attractive visuals causes an
increase in the level of “visual hunger”, which can be described
as the emotional reaction caused by food-related stimuli and
motivating people to replicate what is shown in the visual
(Mohamed Ali, 2025). In addition, people are more focused on
“social proof” (such as customer feedback) rather than
promotional messages (Abadi, 2023). All these marketing
stimuli, including both content and features of the ad (videos),
should affect the consumer’s internal state.
2.3 The organism (organism): mediating
psychological states, trust, and awareness
Organism states act as mediators for digital stimulus to become
emotionally aroused or attracted. Such states involve significant
emotional or cognitive states, such as trust, pleasure, attention, and
emotional bonding (Chou et al., 2024; Kumar and Mishra, 2024;
Yu et al., 2024). For instance, emotional appeals, which arouse
feelings of happiness, have an important part to play in enhancing
viewership and purchase intentions. In addition, emotional
branding love acts as a mediating factor that enhances the effect of
content credibility on purchase intention (Shalaby et al., 2024).
2.4 Behavioral response (response): from
purchase intention to actual behavior
Three main outputs are achieved through this model: purchase
intention, brand loyalty, and impulsive purchases. Influencer
content may create emotional ties capable of predicting
purchase intentions in the near future (Hongsuchon et al.,
2025). While digital marketing campaigns may provoke instant
reactions from the audience, this does not necessarily imply
loyal behavior on behalf of the customers unless they find their
products satisfactory (Yener Ögür, 2022). Indeed, the existing
literature has been paying insufficient attention to actual
behavior in favor of intentions and perceptions analysis, leaving
a gap for researchers to fill.
2.5 Empirical evidence supporting the S-O-
R model in social media consumer behavior
research
In their analyses of consumer behavior in digital space, recent
works by scholars have placed great attention on the impact of
psychological, cognitive, and social variables in forming
consumers’ reactions and purchase behavior. For example, Khan
et al. (2024) establish that the quality of technology service acts
as a stimulus determining users’ satisfaction level. This means
that consumers’ cognitive and affective reactions to a product in
question are driven by its quality. In another work that is
focused specifically on social media, Roy et al. (2025) identify
several behavioral factors such as personal attitude, social
normative beliefs, behavioral intentions and control perceptions,
electronic reviews, celebrity endorsements, and live streaming
which positively affect Generation Z consumers’ purchase
intentions in relation to products promoted via social media and
hence act as a stimulus for their purchasing behavior. Also, Roy
et al. (2024) find out that certain psychological variables
including self-esteem, narcissism, body appreciation, and
exhibitionism can predict users’ digital behavior because of the
complex psychological mechanisms at play and behavioral
mediations involved. Furthermore, Pervin and Khan (2024)
found out that social media marketing strategies impact
consumers’ purchase behavior by promoting increased consumer
engagement and developing trust, which in turn mediates the
relationship between the two phenomena. Thus, the purchase
decision via social media is a product of interactions between
digital and social stimuli on one hand, and psychological and
cognitive variables on the other. All these findings confirm the
central principles of the Stimulus–Organism–Response (S-O-R)
framework applied to digital environments (Khan et al., 2024;
Pervin and Khan, 2024; Roy et al., 2024, 2025).
2.6 Connection of variables and hypothesis
development
In accordance with the S-O-R paradigm, there is a rational
chain of cause and effect that states that digital marketing
stimuli (S) such as visuals and social evidence trigger an
Organism (O) condition of trust, arousal, and perceptual
cognition. In turn, the condition of the individual becomes
responsible for his/her response (R) through purchase intent
and consumer behavior (Almahdi and Alsayed, 2021; Saud,
2026). On the basis of the above-mentioned theory, this study
makes claims that increased exposure to digital stimuli leads to
direct change in perception of consumer psychology (H1 and
H2). Besides, this study suggests that enhanced trust and
awareness conditions in the O are prerequisites to making
purchases (H3 and H4).
Drawing from these relationships in theory, the following
research hypotheses have been developed for this current
research:
H1. There exists a statistically significant correlation between
the degree of exposure to digital marketing stimuli and the
perception/emotion of the consumer.

3 Methodology
3.1 Research design
The research design adopted for this study is that of conclusive
research and quantitative analysis. Such type of research design is
especially fitting in the context of this research, given the need for
the derivation of conclusive results, testing of hypotheses, and
examination of the relationships between variables. The S-O-R
approach serves as a theoretical framework for analysis through
the use of a field survey.
3.2 Sampling and population
• Targeted Population: Egyptian consumers that interact with
the marketing strategies for food products through social
media sites.
• Sampling Method: The researcher used a convenience
sampling method in order to attain representation from
different social media sites (Facebook, Instagram, TikTok)
among the Egyptian population.
• Size of Sample Used: The total number of people who
participated in the research was 402, and this sample is
considered as scientifically sound for representing the
targeted population.
• Demographic Characteristics: The sample had
demographic diversity with more females (76.1%) than
males (23.9%). Moreover, there were more younger
consumers in the age groups ranging between 18 and 25
years (54.2%) and 26–35 years (29.1%), and 75.4% of the
respondents had university-level education.
• Temporal and Geographic Scope: The study has been
conducted from 2024 to 2025 and in Egypt, because of
the unique psychological and cultural aspects of the
Egyptian market.
3.3 Measurement scale and scale
development
The primary tool used for gathering data was an electronic
questionnaire intended to measure the key constructs of the
S-O-R model. These were grouped by different main
components as follows:
• Exposure Intensity: Measuring the level of exposure to food
advertisement on digital media, with regards to intensity
and time.
• Psychosocial and Emotional Perceptions: A Likert Scale 3
points ranging from Agreed, Neutral, and Disagreed
comprising twenty items measuring visual perception,
logical appeal, emotional appeal, and credibility.
• The survey employed a 3-point Likert scale to measure
consumer attitudes. Responses were scored as follows:
Agree = 3, Neutral = 2, and Disagree = 1. The weighted
mean intervals were interpreted as: 1.00–1.66 (Disagree),
1.67–2.33 (Neutral), and 2.34–3.00 (Agree).
• Trust and Social Proof: Measuring levels of trust and social
proof on different mediums as well as consumer opinions
on different products.
• Purchase Behavior: Evaluating both purchase intent and
actual purchase behavior depending on purchase
frequency (daily, weekly, monthly, or rarely).
• Influencers: Determination of trusted influencers and what
influences trust among consumers.
• To ensure the internal consistency of the multi-item
attitude scales (Tables 1, 2), Cronbach’s alpha coefficients
were calculated. The alpha values for the “Advertising
Elements” scale and the “Influencer Credibility” scale
were 0.84 and 0.79, respectively. These values exceed the
recommended threshold of 0.70, indicating high
reliability. Regarding validity, an inter-item correlation
matrix was performed for each construct, confirming that
all items significantly correlated with their respective
scales ($p < 0.05$), thus supporting the psychometric
basis for testing hypotheses H1–H4.
3.4 Data analysis techniques
In order to test the hypotheses and conduct statistical analyses
for the survey data collected, several methods of analysis have been
used:
Descriptive Statistics:
Frequency, percentage, weighted mean and standard deviation
have been employed to summarize the sample and the overall
attitude of the respondents.
Inferential Statistics:
• The chi-squared (X2) test and Coefficient of Concordance
have been applied to determine the significance of
difference in platform use, time investment and level of
trust across gender groups.
• The relationship between exposure to stimuli, perceptions,
trust and purchase behavior (Hypotheses H1–H4) has
been determined through Pearson’s correlation
coefficient r.
• To find any differences in the purchasing factor across
demographic dimensions like gender, age, educational
attainment and income, the t-test and ANOVA methods
have been used (Hypothesis H5).
• Post hoc comparisons (using LSD technique) have
been performed to determine where the source of

5 Discussion
The current paper provides a thorough understanding of the
mental mechanisms involved in the consumer behavior towards
food products on social media of Egyptian consumers. Based on
the S-O-R theory, the research demonstrates how stimulus gets
processed in order to produce certain actions.
One of the main outcomes is the finding about social proof—
consumer comments and reviews—that serves as the most
influential factor behind purchasing decisions made by 46.3% of
participants. It should be noted that this result supports the
claims made by Abadi (2023) stating that consumers tend to
rely on other people’s opinions rather than advertising materials.
As such, the findings emphasize that trust plays an important
role in the S-O-R process acting as an intermediary that
minimizes potential risks associated with food. Our findings
regarding the vital role of social proof, consumer reviews, and
clear product information are strongly corroborated by Roy
et al. (2025). Regarding our results, consumers prefer clarity and
logical information, as noted by Khan et al. (2024).

When analyzing the effect of influencer marketing, it is worth
mentioning that advertisements based on personal experience
have become the second most influential factor, impacting
37.8% of participants. These results are consistent with previous
studies by Coates et al. (2019) and Qutteina et al. (2019). In
addition, these outcomes support the findings made by Al-
Samarrai (2024) about the importance of influence credibility—
trust, charisma, and expertise—as determinants of purchase
intentions. The results also prove that women are more
vulnerable to emotional persuasion, which makes brand love
another intermediary in the S-O-R process.
An interesting outcome is linked to the comparative
evaluation of intellectual and emotional appeal used as
marketing tools. Unlike the traditional viewpoint on Egyptian
culture, it can be stated that advertisements featuring clear
logical structure, benefits, and nutrients prevail among those of
the purely emotional nature. Thus, these results expand the
conclusions by Ng et al. (2025) concerning the necessity of
information provided when advertising food items in collectivist
cultures. However, visual hunger proved to be an effective initial
factor to attract consumers’ attention.
Contribution to literature includes the examination of digital
awareness vs. actual purchasing behavior paradox. As shown by
data analysis, while 74.6% of participants purchased something
thanks to their engagement in social media ads, only half of
those customers make repeat purchases. Such a finding is
consistent with Yener Ögür (2022) who claims that digital
advertising might generate some immediate response; however,
loyalty cannot be ensured by advertising itself without
customers’ continued satisfaction with a product. It should also
be noted that a tendency for passive consumption was
discovered among 42.8% of respondents, showing that
advertisement can be viewed as a kind of media content rather
than as a trigger to action
In addition to that, some demographic characteristics (age)
have proved to be relevant to results (see Table 23). Digital
stimuli have stronger power in terms of influencing Generation
Z customers (Almahdi and Alsayed, 2021; Saud, 2026) who have
a tendency to make impulsive purchases motivated by FOMO.
Summing up, the presented findings confirm the validity of the
S-O-R paradigm as digital stimuli can cause a behavioral
response only if an internal state (O) has been developed first.
The significant behavioral engagement of Generation Z observed
in this study is consistent with Roy et al. (2024), who identified
psychological drivers, such as body appreciation
and exhibitionism.
6 Implications of the study
6.1 Theoretical implications
This research makes a valuable theoretical contribution to
the field of marketing psychology by testing and confirming
the validity of the Stimulus-Organism-R esponse (S-O-R) model
in the context of digital food retailing in an emerging country
such as Egypt. It is shown that any stimuli coming from the
digital environment will not lead to direct behavioral action
but, rather, be mediated by the internal Organismic state,
which will become the most important precursor to the final
response behavior. Furthermore, it differentiates between
emotional and cognitive approaches, proving that in the
FMCG market, logic and information-based (Stimulus)
communication better create the organismic state of trust than
an emotional approach.
6.2 Practical implications
The above study provides various implications from a
practitioner point of view in the field of digital marketing. First,
the use of social proof is necessary since customer opinions and
ratings prove to be the most influential elements (46.3%). The
incorporation of consumer-generated material must be used in
advertisements as a way of reducing perceived risks. Second,
the selection of appropriate influencers should involve food
bloggers who provide experience-based information as the
customers prefer honesty and tests compared to celebrities.
Lastly, the approach adopted must emphasize logic regarding
health and benefits provided through the consumption of
such products.
6.3 Research implications
The research is novel to the existing body of knowledge since
it has gone beyond traditional measurement indicators like
“purchase intentions” and “attitudes”, providing concrete
information related to the act of purchasing and the frequency
of purchases. The research will be helpful because it explains the
difference between high rates of initial purchase action (74.6%)
and low rates of repeat purchases (50%).

7 Conclusion
7.1 Summary
The process of digital decision-making regarding food
purchases of the Egyptian population could be viewed as a
complex psychological process determined by elements such as
trust, social proof, and information clarity. While visual
stimulation and influence in marketing work as a way to attract
the user’s attention, the process of making the purchasing
decision depends mostly on the perception of credibility and
utility provided by the consumer’s mind. The findings of the
paper show that people tend to become increasingly skeptical
about traditional ads while preferring “others’ evidence”.
7.2 Limitations and future directions
Despite the positive aspects, there are some drawbacks in the
current study:
Limited Geographic Area: The research is conducted
only for the Egyptian market where there are particular
cultural and psychological peculiarities which affect the
ability to generalize the findings for different
geographic locations.
Sampling Method: Using the method of convenience sampling
with 402 respondents might fail to reflect all segments of
online consumers.
Variables: Only certain variables are considered within the
S-O-R model.
7.3 Future research directions
Neuromarketing and Eye-Tracking: Further study needs to
employ eye-tracking technology to determine which specific
features of food advertising have the greatest impact on
consumer behavior.
AI and Personalization: There is a need for longitudinal
studies to explore how long-term AI personalization impacts
consumer trust and loyalty.
Health Effects: In view of the impact that food-based vlogs
have on consumers, there is a need for investigation into the
potential health consequences of constant exposure to such
content by the younger generation.
Post-Purchase Behavior: More research needs to be done on
topics like boycotting, cognitive dissonance, and buyer’s
remorse after making an impulsive online purchase.
