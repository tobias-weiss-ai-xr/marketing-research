Introduction
The introduction frames social media in the digital age as a core
communication channel and, in this study’s context, a consulting
platform for marketing and brand communication, especially within
the beauty industry [1,2]. A key claim is that consumers often
prefer influencer guidance because it feels personal, relatable, and
more intelligible than complex corporate marketing jargon, with
research indicating that 82% of consumers value influencer advice.
The article emphasizes Instagram’s role as a visual-first platform
for photo and video sharing and its suitability for marketing via
visual descriptions [3]. It is described as a major visual-oriented
social networking site with “accurate target user positioning” [4].
The authors motivate Kay Yarms as a case because of her high
visibility and perceived credibility in South Africa’s beauty
influencer landscape [5,6,7,8]. She is described as having a
very large following (over half a million Instagram followers
and substantial YouTube subscribers) and as being known for
authentic and relatable facial beauty and lifestyle content [9,10].
The text also cites real-world effects: endorsements linked to
stock shortages and increased product demand, and recognition by
traditional and online publications (e.g., Forbes and Glamour). The
introduction positions influencer-driven marketing as disruptive
to traditional marketing and highlights that this shift challenges
beauty brands to improve communication strategies in the
contemporary environment. Against this backdrop, the main aim
is to understand Kay Yarms’s communication strategies and how
they relate to brand positioning and customer perception. The
study’s objectives are to:
• determine communication strategies used by Kay Yarms to
position products she discusses,
• analyse the effects of these strategies on followers within the
beauty industry, and
• understand influencer performance and compare it to
traditional approaches.
Theoretical Perspective
The study’s theoretical framework integrates social psychology,
marketing, and communication studies via Social Cognitive
Theory (SCT). The authors argue that SCT is relevant because
influencer–follower relationships are a modern, concrete
manifestation of SCT dynamics [11]. Influencers operate as role
models whose behaviours, opinions, and routines are observed
and potentially imitated by followers. Within the SCT framing,
the study foregrounds three components:
• Influencer characteristics such as credibility, expertise, and
attractiveness (as conditions that enable persuasive impact),
• Communication strategies used to convey brand messages
and shape meaning, and
• Customer perceptions that emerge from exposure and
interaction.

ABSTRACT
The study is situated in the wider transformation of marketing communication brought about by social media, particularly in the beauty industry, where
digital content, peer-like endorsement, and influencer credibility frequently substitute for or outperform traditional advertising. The authors argue that
influencer marketing has become an increasingly dominant component of marketing budgets, noting a rise in business investment in influencer marketing
and emphasizing that audiences often perceive influencer recommendations as more relatable and trustworthy than corporate messaging (Influencer
Marketing Hub, 2024; Berger & the Keller Fay Group, 2016; Vrontis et al., 2021). The research uses a qualitative case study approach, employing reflective
(reflexive) thematic analysis on a sample of Instagram content from Kay Y arms. Specifically, the study analyses follower comments on five selected posts
promoting facial beauty products. It aims to clarify which communication strategies Kay Y arms uses to position products, how these strategies affect
followers, and how Kay Y arms’s influencer performance compares to traditional marketing approaches. The study positions itself as contributing to academic
literature on influencer marketing and brand positioning in the South African beauty context, while also offering applied value for beauty entrepreneurs
and brands exploring influencer partnerships in emerging markets.

The authors describe influencer power as enabled by their ability
to reach broad audiences and develop personal connections, which
gives them “unprecedented power to shape consumer attitudes and
behaviours” [12]. Brands are therefore advised (conceptually) to
align communication strategy and positioning with influencer-
promoted values and follower preferences. The study suggests
that analysing follower comments can reveal how influencers
shape attitudes and beliefs about brands and products [11]. SCT
principles are invoked to imply that influencer marketing works
through observation, modelling, vicarious reinforcement, and
self-efficacy: followers see behaviours (skincare routines, product
use), observe positive outcomes, and become more confident that
they too can achieve similar results.
Literature Review
The literature review functions to synthesize existing research
and identify how communication theories and social media
dynamics collectively shape brand perception and consumer trust.
The authors cite Knopf (2006) to clarify that a literature review
involves a considered judgment of what is established, contested,
inconclusive, and missing.
Communication and Strategic Communication
Defining Communication and Communication Strategy
Communication is introduced through Schramm’s framing as
establishing commonness and sharing information, ideas, feelings,
or attitudes; it includes source, message, and destination [13].
Strategic communication is positioned as a purposeful and planned
message use aimed at achieving specific objectives within contexts
like organizations or projects. Communication can be formal or
informal; formal communication tends to be strategic, requiring a
communication strategy described as a carefully planned approach
to communicating with diverse stakeholders [14].
Challenges in Communicating Strategy in the Digital Environment
The literature review claims that only a small fraction of employees
understands organizational strategic direction [15]. It also suggests
that marketing managers face difficulty adapting to digital platforms
and influencers, and may understand traditional media better than
digital media, which complicates decision-making in a shifting
environment [16]. The review includes evidence that consumers
exposed to Instagram influencer post’s view endorsed brands as
more trustworthy, develop more favourable attitudes, experience
greater social presence, and may even feel more envy than those
exposed to regular brand positioning [17]. These insights help
justify the study’s focus on influencer communication as a strategic
tool in brand positioning.
Communication Strategies as Systematic Approaches
The authors caution that communication strategies can be
conceptualized in different ways and argue that it is important
to treat them as part of a systematic approach to achieving
communicative goals rather than isolated tactics. Theoretical
perspectives help categorize strategies by purpose and context,
while Napoli emphasizes the diversity and adaptability of
communication methods across audiences and media [18,19].
Game theory appears as an additional lens for analysing strategic
interactions: Farooqui and Niazi  argue that game theory can help
interpret decision-making in competitive or cooperative settings,
and this can also relate to communication strategy management
[20].
Social Media and Social Media Influencers
Social media is defined as online platforms enabling users to
create, share, and interact with content and each other [21,22].
Platforms support communication, entertainment, and information
sharing in multiple formats [23]. The review also highlights the
marketing utility of social platforms: increasing brand reach,
targeting, and executing social marketing strategy [24]. Instagram
is described as highly influential due to its visual nature and large
user base, providing multiple content formats such as photos,
videos, stories, reels, and live streams [24,25].
Influencers are defined as people with expertise in categories/
items trusted by others who can influence viewpoints, behaviours,
and attitudes via their platforms [26]. Influencers are portrayed
as individuals who invest effort in creating quality content until
recognized as experts [27]. The review distinguishes influencer
marketing from celebrity endorsement: influencer marketing is
likened to word-of-mouth by trusted professionals; celebrity
endorsement relies more on fame and reputation [28]. Carnell  is
cited to argue that celebrities often serve as faces/messengers for
brand-created messages, whereas influencers craft the message
end-to-end, and audiences perceive the message as the influencer’s
own.
Branding and Brand Positioning
Brand positioning is presented as how businesses ensure their
brand is easily identified and distinguished in the marketplace,
shaping customer perceptions to align with intended product
messages [29]. It is described as foundational: strong positioning
is necessary even for good products, and without a communication
strategy, positioning fails [29]. Azmat and Lakhani also argue
that while intuition can help identify opportunities, effective
positioning requires systematic research and analysis of strategic
options, market trends, competencies, and customer needs and
perceptions. Correct positioning helps attract and retain consumers
and influences purchase behaviour [29].
Brand Modelling and Value Creation
Strong brands connect with customers at functional and emotional
levels. By understanding customer-defined ideal experiences
and perceptions, brands can identify unmet needs and claim
“unclaimed territory” [30]. Strong brands add value and stand
out from competitors [31]. Leadership can strengthen market
position by examining weaknesses and aligning services with
customer perceptions, while leveraging actual abilities [32].
For loyalty, the brand must keep promises and be true to itself
[33]. Understanding brand messaging involves goal, strength,
values, R&D, and customer impressions. The review also stresses
influencer–brand value alignment: authenticity and credibility
improve when influencer values match brand values.
Building Trust and Shaping Attitudes Via Influencers
Influencers build trust and loyalty by sharing honest, relatable
content and personal experiences, which can translate into
loyalty if brand experiences meet expectations [34,35]. Brand
experience extends beyond physical products into online searches
and digital interactions [36]. The digital era creates online brand
experiences; providing exceptional experiences online matters due
to information dissemination in electronic environments [37,38].

systematic procedures enhance robustness, reliability, and validity
[39]. The authors stress the value of systematic approaches in case
studies to derive meaningful and trustworthy conclusions [40]. A
case study was selected to enable in-depth analysis of Kay Yarms’s
Instagram brand positioning practices, aligned with SCT’s interest
in influencer–follower relationships. The case study approach is

The study uses a combination of qualitative content analysis
as a data collection technique to detect words, themes, and
concepts in qualitative data and Reflexive Thematic Analysis
(RTA) as defined by Braun and Clarke, for identifying, analysing,
and reporting patterns/themes [42,43]. The sample comprises
Instagram comments from Kay Yarms’s followers. Five posts
promoting facial beauty products were selected using non-
probability purposive convenience sampling [44]. During analysis,
predetermined criteria such as tone, language, and transparency
were applied to comments to assess relevance and significance.
Comments were coded using RTA, and recurring themes were
identified to interpret how communication strategies influence
brand perception and consumer behaviour.
Results
Engagement Metrics and Descriptive Statistics
The analysis covers five Instagram posts across seven months.
Engagement varied widely across posts, with a mean total
engagement of 19,716 ± 14,896 interactions per post, indicating
substantial heterogeneity. The study reports strong positive skew:
one post (Post 5) accounted for 43.8% of total engagement (n =
43,164 interactions), while Post 4 contributed only 6.9% (n =
6,806). Likes ranged from 6,676 to 42,000 (M = 19,175, SD =
14,462), comments from 88 to 395 (M = 240, SD = 145), and
shares from 41 to 806 (M = 302, SD = 322). The authors interpret
this variability as consistent with influencer marketing literature,
suggesting that authentic personal narratives produce stronger
engagement than standardized promotional messages.
Correlation Analysis: Interdependencies Among Engagement
Types
A notable statistical result is a very strong positive correlation
between likes and shares (r = 0.998, p < 0.001, 95% CI [0.987],
[0.999]), suggesting that posts liked more are also shared more,
reflecting a near-linear association and indicating that appreciation
and amplification behaviours co-occur. The correlation between
likes and comments is strong but not statistically significant (r =
0.776, p = 0.123), which the authors attribute to the small sample
size (n = 5) and/or the possibility that commenting is driven by
factors beyond general appreciation. Comments often involve
product inquiries, such as “Hey, what pencil do you use for your
eyebrows?” and “Which concealer did you use?”, implying active
information seeking.
Reflexive Thematic Analysis: Three Dominant Themes
The RTA produced three primary themes with strong evidence:
• Personal Storytelling (95%)
• Audience Engagement (90%)
• Visual Branding (85%)
Personal Storytelling: This theme includes first-person narrative,
sensory descriptions, and relatable framing. The article highlights
an example caption: “Oh, the beauty of an uncomplicated
AM skincare routine, loving this Nivea luminous630 range,
especially the day cream with SPF50, which melts into my skin
like butter!” The authors interpret this as turning endorsement
into a personal experience narrative through sensory metaphor
(“melts into my skin like butter”), which positions the product as
accessible and desirable while maintaining authenticity. The phrase
“uncomplicated AM skincare routine” is read as aligning the
product with contemporary preferences for minimalist regimens.
Visual Branding: Visual storytelling is described as containing
four elements with proportional distribution of close-up photos
(30%), brilliant lighting (25%), before-and-after comparisons
(25%), and aesthetic consistency (20%). Close-ups emphasize
texture and application results, enhancing desirability through near-
sensory representation. Before-and-after comparisons demonstrate
efficacy and provide cognitive cues facilitating observational
learning, reducing perceived risk, and increasing confidence. The
study explicitly links this to SCT: visual documentation supplies
vicarious reinforcement because followers see positive outcomes
attached to product use.
Audience Engagement and Reciprocal Interaction: The theme
of audience engagement focuses on comment activity, follower
questions, and influencer responsiveness. Comments are frequently
product-specific queries (e.g., brows pencil, concealer, lip liner
shades). The article notes Kay Yarms responds with direct
information, transforming passive consumption into active
interaction.
Communication Strategy Detail: “Hashtag Minimalism”
A distinctive finding concerns hashtag usage: only Post 1 used
hashtags (n = 4), while Posts 2–5 used none. Contrary to typical
strategies that rely on hashtags for discoverability, posts without
hashtags produced the highest engagement (e.g., Posts 2 and 5 with
24,800 and 42,000 likes). The authors interpret this as suggesting
that Kay Yarms’s established audience values authenticity and
conversational tone more than algorithmic optimization cues;
the minimal hashtags may signal genuine connection rather than
growth tactics.
Discussion
Social Cognitive Theory (SCT) Mechanisms in the Influencer
Context
The discussion argues that Kay Yarms’s communication strategy
illustrates SCT processes: her posts operate as social modelling
demonstrations where followers observe routines, products, and
outcomes. Kay Yarms is positioned as both consumer and reviewer,
creating identification and relatability distinct from celebrity
endorsement, which can be aspirational but less attainable. The
discussion includes an example caption:
“Love my makeup routine, simple. I am so happy that I can shop
for beauty products on Takealot. Let me know what you think
about this look.”
This is interpreted as combining personal satisfaction (affective
endorsement) with feedback solicitation (interactive engagement),
enhancing self-efficacy by demonstrating accessible behaviours
(shopping availability, simplicity of routine) and inviting follower
participation.
Vicarious Reinforcement and Behavioural Adoption
The discussion emphasizes that consistent positive outcomes
shown in posts create vicarious reinforcement: followers anticipate
similar benefits if they adopt the same routine/products.
Comments such as
“Your skin looks so well taken care of! Need to get my hands on
the mark serum soon, and
You make everything look and feel so luxurious, like your skin
is giving luxury. are treated as evidence of followers attributing
desirable outcomes to products and routines, thus increasing
purchase intent.”

Authenticity and Emotional Connection vs Traditional
Advertising
A central interpretive claim is that Kay Yarms diverges from
traditional marketing by emphasizing authentic personal
connection rather than polished commercial messaging. Traditional
advertising is described as relying on professional models, scripted
endorsements, aspirational imagery, and unidirectional messaging.
Kay Yarms instead uses first-person storytelling, direct interaction,
and relatable demonstrations, positioning products as accessible
solutions. The sensory metaphor “melts into my skin like butter” is
highlighted as a rhetorical technique that communicates experience
without technical jargon, enabling mental simulation and thus
persuasion.
Reciprocal Engagement as Community Building (Reciprocal
Determinism)
The discussion argues that Kay Yarms’s responsiveness to follower
questions and feedback fosters a reciprocal relationship and
transforms the environment of persuasion into a community.
Examples of her responses (e.g., naming specific brow pencil, lip
liner shade) are used to show that she supplies actionable guidance,
building perceived accessibility, and strengthening loyalty. This is
connected to SCT’s reciprocal determinism: follower behaviours
(commenting) influence influencer behaviours (responding and
shaping content), which influences the environment (norms of
participation), which in turn influences follower behaviours further.
Comparative Analysis: Influencer Approach vs Traditional
Marketing Outcomes
The article reports comparative outcomes suggesting Kay Yarms’s
approach produces stronger:
• emotional connection (95% vs 45%)
• audience activity levels (90% vs 30%)
• authenticity perception (95% vs 40%).
These differences are linked to stronger brand loyalty and purchase
intention, supported by follower expressions like, “Need all these
products,” and “Need to get my hands on the mark serum soon.”
Self-efficacy and Behavioural Confidence
Kay Yarms’s focus on simplicity and accessibility (“uncomplicated”
routines) is presented as enhancing follower self-efficacy: followers
perceive fewer barriers and believe they can replicate routines.
The process is described as cognitive: followers mentally rehearse
observed behaviours, judge their ability, and form intentions to
act, leading to adoption and purchase behaviour consistent with
SCT predictions.
Findings
The findings section synthesizes results into a set of core
conclusions, emphasizing patterns across engagement data,
thematic analysis, and SCT interpretation.
Authenticity Drives Superior Engagement
The study argues that authentic storytelling outperforms
optimization tactics like hashtags. Post 5, described as narrative-rich
without hashtags, generated 43.8% of total engagement, whereas
Post 1 with four hashtags achieved only 12.2%. This supports an
inverse relationship between hashtag use and engagement in this
dataset and challenges assumptions that extensive hashtagging
is necessary. The implication is that community quality (loyal,
engaged followers) may matter more than broad discoverability.
Personal Experience Narratives Enhance Desirability
Sensory metaphors and first-person endorsements increase product
appeal and purchase consideration, with the study reporting
product inquiries in 45% of analysed comments. This aligns with
observational learning: followers do not merely receive product
claims but see use, outcomes, and contextual integration into
routines.
Visual Storytelling Supports Cognitive Processing and Risk
Reduction
The balanced use of close-ups, lighting, before-and-after
comparisons, and aesthetic consistency provides cues for
comprehension and retention. Visual evidence of efficacy reduces
perceived risk and supports follower confidence, strengthening
the pathway from engagement to purchase intention.
Reciprocal Engagement Strengthens Loyalty
Two-way interaction is highlighted as a differentiator from
traditional marketing, reinforcing engagement loops consistent
with SCT reciprocal determinism. Follower validation and
responses transform followers into community participants,
fostering loyalty and sustained engagement.
Strategic Simplicity Resonates with Contemporary Consumers
Positioning products within “uncomplicated” routines is argued
to match modern preferences for minimalist beauty approaches.
Mentioning product features like SPF50 is also interpreted
as signalling health-conscious selection and credibility, thus
deepening trust.
Practitioner Implications for Beauty Brands and Entrepreneurs
The study recommends prioritizing authenticity, experiential
language, interactive content, balanced visual storytelling,
simplicity framing, and the use of follower testimonials (user-
generated content) as credible social proof.
Theoretical Contributions
The study claims it extends SCT into influencer marketing
by demonstrating observational learning, social modelling,
and vicarious reinforcement within Instagram endorsements.
It also argues Instagram’s interactive features allow reciprocal
determinism “at scale,” turning parasocial connections into semi-
authentic interactions that enhance influence effectiveness.
Limitations and Future Research
The article acknowledges limitations like (1) a small sample size
(n = 5 posts), which limits statistical power and generalizability,
(2) the single influencer case limits comparative insight; multi-
influencer studies are suggested, (3) reliance on engagement metrics
rather than direct purchase data; future studies should connect
exposure to purchasing through experimental or longitudinal
designs (4) qualitative emphasis could be complemented with
quantitative content analysis to explore reliability and broaden
pattern detection.

Conclusion
The conclusion reiterates that the study examined Kay Yarms’s
Instagram brand positioning strategy for facial beauty products
using Atlas. ti principles, qualitative content analysis, and SCT. It
reports that Kay Yarms’s approach, built on authentic storytelling,
reciprocal engagement, and strategic visual branding, creates
strong engagement and shapes consumer perception and purchase
intention. The study emphasizes that minimal hashtag use paired
with high engagement challenges conventional influencer
marketing assumptions and suggests that authentic community-
building can be more persuasive than algorithmic discoverability
tactics.
The conclusion also returns to SCT, indicating Kay Yarms operates
as a social model whose behaviours followers observe and emulate,
with interactive features enabling reciprocal determinism and
strengthening loyalty. The article ends by encouraging future
research expansions (larger datasets, multi-influencer comparison,
purchase behaviour linkage, and longitudinal analysis of influencer
strategy evolution) [45-122].
