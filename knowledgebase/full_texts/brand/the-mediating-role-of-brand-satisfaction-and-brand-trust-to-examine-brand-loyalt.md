1. Introduction

The smartphone industry is one of the  fundamental industries established in Indonesia. Since there are thousands of
smartphone retailers in Indonesia, customers have many choices to select retailers that suit them the most. Therefore, the
retailer should be able to build a strong brand to win tight competition. Previous stated that creating strong brand added value
in brand loyalty (Ahn et al., 2018). Brands are often developed to differentiate a product from other similar products. However,
brands have become so common that it is difficult for them to function as a mark of differentiation (Sharma & Romero, 2022).
Brand marketing researchers have emphasized that companies are required to focus on the consumer –brand relationship,
which results in favorable consumer attitudes toward brands, brand loyalty, and sales increases (Kuo & Hou, 2017). Researchers
have agreed that “human behavior is heavily influenced by emotions, not solely by reason” (Khatoon & Rehman, 2021). The
importance of emotions has been especially highlighted in the brand –consumer relationship (Shetty & Fitzsimmons, 2022).
That is, consumers’ emotional responses are inseparable from consumer –brand relationships.
Brand love refers to brands with deep emotional connections that are differentiated from typical brand experiences
(Roberts, 2006). This often results in “brand loyalty beyond reason,” which focuses mainly on temporary positive emotions
toward a brand or repetitive purchases. Rodgers (2022) argues that the concept is “emotional communication” associated with
“love-marks”, which could result in improving consumers’ lives with priceless values in the Age of Now (Rodgers, 2022). Love
marks are composed of two major pillars: brand love (i.e., mystery, sensuality and intimacy) and brand respect (i.e., trust,
reputation and performance). The key difference between the two is whether a relationship of love is formed or not (Campbell,
2022).
Consumer items generally fall into four dimensions: products, fads, brands and love marks. Products fall in the areas of
low love and low respect, whereas fads are in the dimension of high love with low respect, which does not provide long-lasting
loyalty. Brands fall into the area of high respect but low love, which creates relatively high brand loyalty. Finally, love marks are
located in the hig h love, high respect area where consumers develop the strongest loyalty. In this case, love marks have a
strong, long-lasting emotional relationship with consumers. For example, Oppo smartphones are considered “brands” with high
Abstract This study investigates how brand image influences brand satisfaction and brand trust, which in turn affect brand
loyalty in the context of smartphone retailing. Additionally, the study examines whether brand love and brand respect
moderate the effects of satisfaction and trust on loyalty. These emotional constructs are drawn from the theory of love -
marks, highlighting the importance of both e motional attachment and perceived credibility in shaping strong brand
relationships. A quantitative survey design was employed to gather data from 200 customers who had purchased Apple
smartphones from retail outlets in Jakarta, Indonesia. Respondents completed a structured questionnaire measuring brand
image, satisfaction, trust, love, respect, and loyalty. Data were analyzed using Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) to assess
the direct and moderating effects among the constructs.The findings reveal that b rand image significantly enhances both
customer satisfaction and trust. In turn, satisfaction and trust positively contribute to brand loyalty. Moreover, brand love
and brand respect were found to strengthen the relationship between satisfaction, trust, an d loyalty, indicating their
important moderating role. These results provide empirical support for extending traditional brand loyalty frameworks by
incorporating emotional dimensions, particularly in high -involvement consumer markets such as smartphones. This
research offers both theoretical and managerial implications. Theoretically, it introduces a novel integration of cognitive
and affective constructs —brand love and respect —into the consumer –brand relationship literature. Managerially, the
findings sug gest that beyond delivering quality and reliability, smartphone retailers should aim to foster emotional
connections with customers through meaningful engagement and personalized brand experiences. By building both love
and respect, brands can cultivate stronger customer loyalty and long-term competitive advantage.

current study, managers can effectively influence their sm artphone retailer brand to create love marks along with a high level
of brand love and brand respect by maximizing their customers’ loyalty.

2. Research Methods

The current study was designed to scrutinize the correlation between BI dimensions and  BS as well as BT, with BV and
BR as moderators. The smartphone industry, specifically the Apple smartphone, was chosen as the study’s object. The research
model is shown in Figure 1. The exogenous variables consisted of BI, BS, and BT, whereas the endogenous variables comprised
BV, BR, and BL. Nonprobability sampling was adopted for this study. Data were collected from customers who bought Apple
from smartphone retailers in Indonesia. Jakarta is known as the most populated city in Indonesia, with approxima tely 10.5
million people at the beginning (BPS Statistics, 2020). This was the main reason that this study was conducted in Jakarta. Of the
300 questionnaires distributed to the customers of the building material retailers, 200 responses were returned, whi ch
represents a response rate of 67%. The main reasons for exploring the smartphone industry in this study are that it is considered
one of the largest contributors to Indonesian GDP (Dehdasht et al., 2022). A questionnaire was developed and sent to customers
of various smartphone retailers in Jakarta, Indonesia. The respondents’ demographic characteristics are presented in Table 1.
The research constructs were measured with a 7 -point Likert scale ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 7 (strongly
agree). Th e measurement indicators of CBBE for this study were adopted (Çifci et al., 2016): brand awareness, brand
identification, staff behavior, lifestyle-congruence, physical quality, and ideal self-congruence. Brand awareness was measured
via three indicators derived from previous research (Sasmita & Suki, 2015). In addition, physical quality was measured with six
indicators (Yoo & Donthu, 2001). Moreover, staff behavior was assessed via three indicators. In addition, lifestyle congruenc e
was gauged with three i ndicators. Moreover, ideal self -congruence was calculated by employing three indicators (Nam et al.,
2011). Brand satisfaction was determined by employing four indicators. In addition, brand trust was computed by employing
four derived indicators (Chaudhur i & Holbrook, 2001). Finally, brand loyalty was evaluated via three indicators (Çifci et al.,
2016).
The researchers employed the structural equation modeling (SEM) technique to measure the study’s hypothesis testing.
SEM was used for the study because of its ability to measure the correlation among latent variables (Ullman, 2006). The current
study investigated the correlations among latent variables such as brand awareness, brand identification, staff behavior,
lifestyle congruence, ideal self-congruence, physical quality, brand satisfaction, brand loyalty, and brand trust. Essentially, the
main benefits of using SEM are the ability to measure causal processes by using a series of structural equations and  the ability
to investigate the structural relations  of conceptual theory (Byrne, 2013). Therefore, this study used Statistical Product and

The results of the confirmatory factor analysis revealed that the factor loading value was > 0.4, with a probability of <
0.5 for all the questions, as shown in Table 2. The measurement of p < 0.05 indicated that all the questions could be measured
significantly. In addition, factor loading with a construct reliability value of > 0.7, as well as the Cronbach’s alpha value, signifies
the consistency of the measurement function (Hair et al., 2019). The overall reliability te st results in Table 2 indicate that the
questionnaire implemented in this study is reliable, with a composite reliability value and a Cronbach’s alpha higher than 0.70.
The statistical measurement for goodness of fit revealed an acceptable fit, with a χ2 o f 888.89; the df equaled 838. GFI (0.698)
and AGFI (0.659) indicated a moderate level, whereas an RMSEA of 0.025 signified a satisfactory fit. Table 3 displays incremental
fit indices for this study at a satisfactory level, with a TLI of 0.974 and CFI of 0.971, whereas an NFI of 0.680 denotes a moderate
level. Owing to the goodness of fit at moderate and satisfactory levels, for all factor loads, they were significant. This si gnifies
that the scales were one-dimensional, and the proposed model was accepted.
Previous research has reported that the acceptable value of composite reliability (CR) is 0.6. Therefore, the average

4. Discussion

The study results revealed that brand awareness was the most significant variable influencing brand satisfaction. This
means that most customers with awareness of specific brands are satisfied with the products. In the retail industry context,
these outcomes are relevant to previous research reporting that brand equity is a key element of brand equity, influencing
brand satisfaction (Nam et al., 2011).
The ability of brand awareness to influence brand satisfaction is triggered by customers’ knowledge about the brand
(Dananjoyo et al., 2024). Customers' ability to recall or recognize a particular brand is recorded as the main factor influencing
brand satisfaction. This indicates that customers are satisfied with a specific product or service while they are aware and familiar
with it. In this context, customers’ familiarity with the use of a specific smartphone brand by the building material retailer relies
on their satisfaction. On the basis of the importance of the research findings, practitioners should generate various promotional
campaigns to increase customers’ brand awareness, increasing customers’ brand satisfaction at building material retailers.
The key value that contributes to strengthening relationships between customers and brands is brand identification (Du
et al., 2007). In this report, customers’ identification with smartphone brands positively influenced brand satisfaction by 33.2%.
This study’s results support those of previous studies, indicating that brand identification significantly influences brand
satisfaction (Chaudhuri & Holbrook, 2001). Once a brand ca n meet or exceed customer expectations, it creates strong brand
identification and finally satisfies the customer itself. An earlier study confirmed that greater identification of organizat ions’
offerings impacts brand satisfaction (Bhattacharya et al., 19 95). Physical quality is defined as a service quality instrument that
influences brand satisfaction with confidence (Brady & Cronin Jr, 2001). In addition, other scholars have proposed that physical
quality influences brand satisfaction (Ryu et al., 2012).  These findings are supported by a recent study that confirmed that
physical quality created value and satisfaction in local and international communities (Bihamta et al., 2017). In the retail
industry context, this examination result supports the earlier finding that physical quality significantly influences brand
satisfaction for retailers’ customers (Çifci et al., 2016).
Consequently, service quality plays a pivotal role in reinforcing relationships with customers (Ekinci et al., 2008). One of
the fundamental factors influencing service quality is staff behavior excellence, such as staff manner, staff willingness to assist,
staff cleanliness, quickness, and great services (Chen, 2008). In addition, the ability of an organization to provide good -quality
staff behavior has an impact on the emergence of brand satisfaction (Nam et al., 2011). Another study verified that staff
behavior positively affects the satisfaction of a brand. These outputs support the study's findings that the staff behavior o f
retailers has a significant correlation with brand satisfaction.
Principally, lifestyle congruence expresses customers’ daily needs and wants. Then, customers pursuing their decision -
making can fulfill their needs on the basis of their lifestyles (Phau et al., 2015 ). This means that lifestyle indicates their living’s
uniqueness, persuaded by their judgments, affairs, interests, and beliefs (Solomon et al., 2019). Since lifestyle congruence is
linked to customers’ intention to purchase, the greater the compatibility of customers’ lifestyles is, the greater the level of
customer satisfaction (Kataria & Saini, 2020). These findings are relevant to the study results, which show that customers’
retailers in the building material retailer directly impact brand satisfaction .
In addition, most customers have the intention to use a specific brand that provides an ideal congruence, which
expresses more value to customers. In this context, a previous study showed that a higher level of ideal congruence significantly
impacts brand satisfaction (Nam et al., 2011). Recent studies have also revealed that ideal congruence is a significant sign of
brand satisfaction in the retail banking industry. Thus, the results of the present study suggest that the ideal lifestyle fo r the
customer of the building material retailer is positively related to brand satisfaction, which aligns with the findings of previous
studies.
Many studies have discussed brand satisfaction as part of the branding concept in several industries, including the retail
industry (Dananjoyo & Udin, 2023). Once a brand's performance satisfies or is beyond customers’ expectations, it will lead to
brand satisfaction. Some research has indicated that the outcome of brand satisfaction is to strengthen brand trust (Şahin et
al., 2 011). A high level of customer brand satisfaction has been found to contribute to a high degree of brand trust from
customers (Chinomona, 2013). This study’s findings revealed that a high degree of brand satisfaction from the customers of
building material retailers initiated greater brand trust. It could be concluded that this study’s result is relevant to the previous
evaluation of brand trust. Hence, the other output of brand satisfaction is brand loyalty (Rather & Sharma, 2016). Recently,
another schola r reported that a high level of brand satisfaction generated a high level of brand loyalty (Jamshidi & Rousta,
2021b). These findings are also relevant to this research result, which confirmed that excellent brand satisfaction created a n
optimum level of brand loyalty in the building material industry.
Most studies have proposed that brand loyalty is a dependent variable of brand satisfaction. Additionally, brand trust is
recognized as a significant sign of brand loyalty. Later, it was reported that brand t rust confidently affects brand loyalty in the
retail industry (Atulkar, 2020). The outcome of this study, which revealed that greater brand trust ultimately contributed to the
high level of brand loyalty for the customers of the building material retailers . Finally, the results of the present study support
the literature by estimating brand loyalty through brand trust.

5. Conclusion

The objective of this study was to measure brand trust and brand loyalty on the basis of CBBE elements (brand
awareness, brand identification, staff behavior, lifestyle congruence, ideal self -congruence, and physical quality) through
satisfaction. The results of the study show that brand awareness positively influences brand satisfaction, which supports the
findings of previous studies. Therefore, brand identification significantly influences brand satisfaction, as mentioned earlier.
The emergence of physical quality positively influences brand satisfaction (Çifci et al., 2016). Therefore, an earlier study claimed
that staff behavior positively influences brand satisfaction, which was supported by this study (Dananjoyo et al., 2020). Thi s
study also revealed that lifestyle congruence influences the satisfaction of customers in the retail industry. These findings  are
relevant to those of a previous study (Susanty & Kenny, 2021). Moreover, the results of this study indicate a positive relationship
between ideal self -congruence and brand satisfaction. This finding was in accordance with an earlier study (Feiz & Moradi,
2020). In addition, brand satisfaction significantly influences the brand trust and brand loyalty of customers. This validate d the
results of a previous study. Finally, this study reveals a positive relationship between brand trust and brand loyalty. These
findings support those of a previous study (Atulkar, 2020).
This report adds to the literature by recommending and checking a model that evaluates the correlations among CBBE,
brand trust, and brand loyalty through brand satisfaction. This study makes thr ee significant contributions. First, the study
confirmed that CBBE antecedents directly triggered the presence of satisfaction with the brand. This study revealed that CBBE
antecedents play a critical role in determining brand satisfaction in the context o f the building material industry. Previous
studies have focused mainly on the hospitality industry and the fashion industry (Çifci et al., 2016). Typically, research in  the
field of the building materials industry has focused on building information modeli ng, procurement, logistic efficiency,
sustainable design, etc. Since this study focused on CBBE in building material studies, the findings of this study contribute  to
the development of the building material industry. This means that this study makes a pos itive contribution to industry.
Second, the study authorizes that CBBE antecedents indirectly caused trust and loyalty to the brand. Previous studies
have not yet empirically investigated and confirmed brand trust in the CBBE measurement (Çifci et al., 201 6). These findings
increase the knowledge of brand -building in the context of CBBE. Third, the current study confirms that the emergence of
loyalty and trust in the brand was directly initiated by satisfaction with the brand. This article postulates that brand satisfaction,
such as customers’ delight, influences customers’ trust and loyalty. Afterward, this study’s findings encourage companies to
build a functional plan for generating brand satisfaction, directly initiating brand loyalty and trust.
Although this study contributes significantly to brand equity in the building material and retail industry, it has limitations.
First, and foremost, the study was conducted in Indonesia as a developing nation, and the results attained in developed nations
may differ from those of the present study, which needs more consideration. Second, future studies should test this conceptual
framework with alternative methodologies such as mixed -method design. By combining both quantitative methods and
qualitative methods, hop efully, the research results can yield more accurate perceptions. Finally, enriching the conceptual
framework using indicators of business performance, such as the profit loss ratio, return on investment, and market share, will
be a challenging effort, thus broadening article contributions to both academia and the industrial sector.
