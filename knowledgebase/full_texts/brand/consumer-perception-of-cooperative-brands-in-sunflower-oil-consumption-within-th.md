Abstract
Purpose: Sunflower oil is one of the staple food products widely consumed in Türkiye. This study aims to
explore consumers’ perceptions of the Trakyabirlik brand within the framework of Aaker’s Brand Equity
Model in the province of Çanakkale.
Design/Methodology/Approach: The study was conducted with 383 consumers living in Çanakkale using a
survey method. The relationships between brand awareness, perceived quality, and brand loyalty were
analyzed with correlation analysis, while the impact of advertising was evaluated through logistic regression
analysis.
Findings: The results of Spearman correlation analysis are as follows: A positive and strong relationship
between brand awareness and brand associations (ρ = 0.821, p < .01); a positive and moderate relationship
between brand awareness and brand loyalty (ρ = 0.446, p < .01); a positive and moderate relationship between
brand awareness and perceived quality (ρ = 0.413, p < .01); a positive and moderate relationship between
brand associations and brand loyalty (ρ = 0.471, p < .01); a positive and moderate relationship between brand
associations and perceived quality (ρ = 0.443, p < .01); and a positive and moderate relationship between
brand loyalty and perceived quality (ρ = 0.524, p < .01). Logistic regression analysis indicated that individuals
who had seen Trakyabir lik advertisements were 3.151 times more likely to use the brand in the future
compared to those who had not.
Practical Implications: The findings suggest that advertising activities have a substantial impact on brand
preference. Improving advertising and promotional strategies could be an effective way for Trakyabirlik to
boost brand loyalty.
Originality/Value: This study is one of the few that examines consumer perceptions of cooperative brands
within the framework of Aaker’s Brand Equity Model. It contributes to the literature by highlighting the
decisive role of advertising in brand preference.
Anahtar kelimeler: Brand loyalty, logistic regression, marketing strategy, perceived quality, purchase intention

INTRODUCTION
Due to global pandemics, wars, political instabilities, and sanctions imposed by countries on one another, food
security has become one of the most critical priorities for nations in recent years. Food demand is continuously rising
due to population growth. Seed oils are widely used in daily nutrition. Sunflower oil, one of the most essential seed
oils, plays a significant role in the food supply chain both in Türkiye and worldwide.
Sunflower (Helianthus annuus L.) belongs to the Asteraceae family and ranks third in global vegetable oil
production and consumption, following soybean and canola oil (USDA, 2025). Originally cultivated as an ornamental
plant, the sunflower is now utilized for various purposes, including the production of edible oil, medicinal applications,
energy, and animal feed. It originated in Central and North America before spreading to Europe and ultimately
becoming a global phenomenon. Sunflower seeds are a nutrient -dense food, containing 8.6% dietary fiber, 21.0%
protein, and 47.5% crude fat. Sunflower oil has a mild flavor and appearance and contains more vitamin E than other
vegetable oils. It is rich in monounsaturated and polyunsaturated fatty acids, vitamins, and minerals (Bhuvaneshwari
and Rumamaheswari, 2013).
Additionally, products derived from oil plant processing are in demand across various industries and do not
face marketing challenges, making them popular among farmers. They first arrived in Europe in the 16th century via
Spain, and commercial cultivation  began in Russia between 1830 and 1840. In Türkiye, it is known that production
first started in Thrace by immigrants from Bulgaria and Romania after World War I and later spread throughout
Türkiye (Meral, 2019; Alonso, 2008; Chivu and Stanciu, 2024; Soare  & Chiurciu, 2018).
Due to unfavorable weather conditions in the central production region of the Black Sea, which caused a
significant decline in yield in the 2024/25 period, global sunflower seed production is expected to increase by 3.9
million tons in 2025/26 compared to the previous year, reaching 56.3 million tons. The harvested area is expected to
show only a slight increase in 2025/26 compared to last year (USDA, 2025).
As of 2024, sunflower production in Türkiye covers a total area of 9,737,224 decares, with 8,549,239 decares
for oil and 1,187,985 decares for snacks. From this area, a total of 2,195,000 tons of sunflower seeds are produced,
including 1,855,000 tons for oil and 340,000 tons for snacks. In 2024, the average yield was 225 kg per decare (Turkish
Statistical Institute, 2025). Oilseed crop s are among the product groups with a high supply deficit in Türkiye. The
country cannot meet its demand for sunflower oil t hrough domestic production and is therefore a net importer of this
commodity. As a result, sunflower prices in Türkiye are directly affected by exchange rates, global prices, and oil
prices. Price formation is also influenced by harvest yields, weather con ditions, consumption trends, exchange rates,
speculators' investment tendencies, cooperatives' purchasing power, and government policies. Although it varies
annually, Türkiye can produce about half of its sunflower oil consumption (Ministry of Agriculture,  Sunflower
Situation Forecast Report, 2024; Ministry of Agriculture, 2024; Semerci and Durmuş, 2021). According to Kadakoğlu
and Yılmaz (2022), the diesel -fertilizer support specifically allocated to sunflower products, which have a supply
deficit, a low self-sufficiency rate, and a dependence on imports, should be increased.
Cooperative enterprises actively address numerous global issues, including climate change, poverty reduction,
food security, and sustainable development, guided by their principles and values. As a result, the United Nations has
declared 2025 the Internati onal Year of Cooperatives to support the achievement of the Sustainable Development
Goals. The goal of designating 2025 as the International Year of Cooperatives is to raise awareness of cooperative
enterprises worldwide and promote comprehensive economic and social progress through the cooperative model. The
theme for the 2025 International Year of Cooperatives is 'Cooperatives Build a Better World' (United Nations, 2025).
Although there are many organizational models in the agricultural sector in Türkiye,  agricultural cooperatives
constitute the most significant part of the economic organization in the agricultural sector (Yercan, 2007).
Cooperatives are essential structures that enable producers to sell their products at fair prices and access inputs at low
costs, and allow consumers to obtain healthy, reliable, and affordable products.
Consumer-based brand equity is a marketing strategy that enables companies to establish a distinct position in
the competitive environment, be perceived as high- quality, and become more competitive than other brands in the
sector by fostering brand loyalty (Koçoğlu & Aksoy, 2017). Brand perception refers to the overall impression that
consumers have about a brand. Brands should not act solely to market the product, as consumers perceive all marketing
tools used; therefore, brand perception and the marketing mix must align. All messages conveyed by the brand are a
crucial part of the brand perception process (Kaptanoğlu et al., 2019).

According to David A. Aaker, brand equity is the perceived power a brand holds over consumers and the
benefit it provides to the business, representing the total value it adds to both customers and the company.
Additionally, consumer loyalty tends to be hi gh for products or services with substantial brand equity, meaning that
even if the product's price is high, consumers might still prefer it. Brand equity is not only a financial asset but also a
strategic advantage. Companies should prioritize long- term e fforts to protect and grow brand equity, rather than
focusing solely on short -term sales goals (Aaker, 1991, 1996). Numerous studies in the literature have explored
Aaker's brand model. Koçoğlu and Aksoy (2017) investigate how consumers perceive brand equi ty overall, the
components of consumer-based brand equity, the levels at which these components are perceived, and the relationships
between the elements that make up brand equity. In his research, Altundal (2019) confirms that a four -dimensional
consumer-based brand equity structure —comprising brand awareness, perceived brand quality, brand associations,
and brand loyalty—is a valid extension of Aaker's model. Taşkın and Akat (2010) found that the service component
has a significant influence on perceived quality in the durable consumer goods sector.
Many factors influence consumers' brand preferences. These include the brand's symbol, logo, perceived
quality, reliability, healthiness, packaging quality, organic status, whether it is a local brand, its origin if it is a foreign
brand, other consumers' reviews, the status of competing brands, and, most importantly, its price. Conscious
consumers often wonder about the stage’s foods undergo during production, the number of additives and residues they
contain, and the extent to which manufacturers comply w ith hygiene standards (Bal et al., 2006). Numerous studies
have been conducted on brand perception in literature. A survey conducted in Kırşehir found that the positive image
of agricultural sales cooperatives affected consumer satisfaction (Sulubulut, 2023). It was determined that consumers
were willing to pay more for Gemlik olives with a geographical indication (Meral & Şahin, 2013). In a study conducted
in Tokat province, it was observed that a positive view of cooperatives led consumers to prefer coope rative brands
they considered high-quality over others; consumers held positive attitudes and behaviors toward cooperatives; they
perceived cooperative-branded products as high- quality and economical; and therefore, they expected more product
options, advertising, and promotional activities from cooperative brands (Yüzbaşıoğlu, 2021). In a study on branding
in cooperatives and the relationship between cooperative consumer products, it was observed that products marketed
under the Torku brand were given Turkish names familiar to the public, such as Banada, Miniki, and Davet, to ensure
that the products remained memorable to consumers (Oğuz & Mete, 2017). In a study conducted on students at
Mehmet Akif Ersoy University regarding the effect of global brand perception on consumer behavior, it was observed
that participants recognized the impact of globalization and responded positively, but that cultural, social, and
economic factors significantly influenced global brand preferences, with local production also pl aying a key role
(Sarıtaş & Duran, 2017). A study in Erzurum found that consumer satisfaction with sunflower oil and its price were
crucial factors influencing their purchasing decisions, and that the quantity purchased was related to the characteristics
of sunflower oil (Topcu et al., 2010). A study in Artvin province on consumers' preferences for sunflower oil revealed
that the most crucial feature was the country of origin, with consumers favoring local products; surprisingly, price was
the second most c ritical factor. Additionally, it was observed that consumers preferred producer brands over
cooperative brands, and branding had limited influence on sunflower oil purchasing decisions (Toklu, 2017).
The study aimed to assess consumers' brand awareness of sunflower oil produced by Trakyabirlik using the
Aaker Brand Equity Model. The following hypotheses were tested in line with the study's purpose:
H1: A significant relationship between brand awareness and brand association.
H2: A significant relationship between brand awareness and brand loyalty.
H3: A significant relationship between brand awareness and perceived quality.
H4: A significant relationship between brand association and brand loyalty.
H5: A significant relationship between brand association and perceived quality.
H6: A significant relationship between brand loyalty and perceived quality.
H7: A significant relationship between consumers' intention to consume the cooperative brand and their
education level.
H8: A significant relationship between consumers' intention to consume the cooperative brand and their
household population.
H9: A significant relationship between consumers' intention to consume the cooperative brand and their income
level.
H10: A significant relationship between consumers' intention to consume the cooperative brand and exposure
to cooperative advertising.

The study surveyed consumers in the central district of Çanakkale province to examine their brand awareness,

In the study, Aaker's (1991) consumer-based brand equity model was used to assess consumers' perceptions of
cooperative brands during data collection. The Brand Equity Model developed by David A. Aaker identifies key
components that determine a brand's val ue in the consumer's mind. According to this model, brand equity includes
four main elements: brand awareness, brand associations, brand loyalty, and perceived quality (Figure 1).

Figure 1: Aaker Brand Equity Model
According to Aaker, these components are factors that directly influence consumers' attitudes, preferences, and
loyalty toward a brand (Aaker, 1991). Aaker's model is regarded as one of the most widely used theoretical
frameworks, especially in measuring consumer -based brand equity. The model enables the evaluation of brand
strategies across various industries and cultural contexts (Aaker, 1996).
In evaluating the study data, basic descriptive statistics, frequency tables with numbers and ratios, and Likert
scale questions were employed. Additionally, Cronbach's Alpha reliability coefficients were calculated to assess the
internal consistency of th e scale used. Spearman correlation analysis was performed to identify the relationships
between scale dimensions, and consumers' future consumption tendencies for Trakyabirlik products were analyzed
using logistic regression.
The survey conducted within the scope of the research was found ethically appropriate by the Çanakkale
Onsekiz Mart University Scientific Research and Publication Ethics Board at its meeting dated 07/12/2023 and
numbered 15/11.

In the study, consumer attitudes toward the Trakyabirlik brand were evaluated using the Aaker Brand Equity
Model criteria and a five -point Likert scale. The average score for the brand awareness criterion "I recognize the
Trakyabirlik brand when I see it" was 3.93. The average score for the criterion "I can distinguish the Trakyabirlik
brand from competing brands" was 3.76. The average score for the brand associations criterion "I remember the
Trakyabirlik brand symbol and logo" was 3.49. The average score for the criterion "I have no difficulty visualizing
the Trakyabirlik brand in my mind" was 3.62. The average score for the criterion "I immediately recognize
Trakyabirlik products" was 3.59. In the study, the average values of the criteria related to brand loyalty ranged from
2.69 to 3.62. The lowest average was 'If Trakyabirlik brand is available, I will not buy other sunflower oil' (2.69). It
was observed that consumers' brand loyalty levels were low to medium. Consumers are more indecisive about brand
loyalty. The standard deviations for the criteria are high (1.34– 1.55), indicating significant differences in consumers'
loyalty levels. The averages for brand loyalty criteria are lower compared to other dimensions. Therefore, strategies
are needed to strengthen brand loyalty.
When examining consumers' perceptions of quality criteria, it was found that the average requirement values
ranged from 3.92 to 4.07, with the highest average being 'Trakyabirlik brand is reliable' (4.07). Overall, consumers
see Trakyabirlik's products as reliable, high-quality, and healthy. The standard deviation values of the criteria are low
(1.09–1.17), indicating that consumers have a more uniform perception of perceived quality. When Likert scales were
averaged across criteria, the strongest dimension  was perceived quality (3.98), followed by brand awareness (3.84),
brand associations (3.56), and brand loyalty (3.03). According to the study by Koçoğlu & Aksoy (2017), the factor
most perceived by consumers is brand awareness/association, followed by perceived quality and brand loyalty. In our
study, perceived quality remains relatively high and consistent. This result shows that the brand has a strong image in
terms of product quality (Table 3). According to a study conducted in Izmir Province, food safe ty, product quality,
product price, and brand awareness are key factors influencing consumers' preferences for cooperative brands (Sapmaz
& Yercan, 2015). According to a study's results, the primary reason consumers prefer cooperative branded products
is their desire to consume higher-quality products (Everest et al., 2018).
Spearman Correlation Analysis was conducted to explore the relationships among the dimensions used in the
study. According to the correlation analysis, there is a positive and strong relationship between brand awareness and
brand associations (ρ = 0.821, p  < 0.01) (H1: Accepted). In other words, consumers' awareness of and associations
with the Trakya Birlik cooperative brand are closely linked. This result suggests that the brand is recognized in
consumers' minds not randomly but with specific, positive, a nd consistent meanings. A significant and moderate
relationship was also identified between brand awareness and brand loyalty (ρ = 0.446, p < 0.01) (H2: Accepted).
Thus, while consumers may be familiar with the brand, this familiarity doesn't necessarily l ead to strong loyalty.
Increasing loyalty requires strategies that extend beyond awareness, such as enhancing product quality, improving the
customer experience, and fostering emotional connections.

Additionally, a significant and moderate relationship was observed between brand awareness and perceived
quality (ρ = 0.413, p < 0.01) (H3: Accepted). The more familiar consumers are with the Trakya Birlik brand, the more
likely they are to evaluate its qu ality positively. However, this weak connection suggests that merely knowing the
brand is insufficient to persuade consumers of its high quality. To improve perceptions of quality, efforts should
extend beyond awareness campaigns by providing concrete evidence and experiences that emphasize quality. A
significant and moderate relationship was found between brand associations and brand loyalty (ρ = 0.471, p < 0.01)
(H4: Accepted). As the associations consumers form about the brand —such as trust, quality, and value —become
stronger, their loyalty to the brand also increases significantly.
Furthermore, a significant and moderate relationship exists between brand association and perceived quality (ρ
= 0.443, p < 0.01) (H5: Accepted). The more positive the associations about the brand, the more consumers believe
in its quality. Lastly, a significant and moderate relationship was observed between brand loyalty and perceived quality
(ρ = 0.524, p < 0.01) (H6: Accepted). As consumers' confidence in the brand's quality grows, so does their loyalty, in
a statistically significant way. When these relationships are viewed through Aaker's Brand Equity Model, they
illustrate that the brand components support one another and help foster a positive consumer perception of the Trakya
Birlik brand (Table 4).

CONCLUSION
When evaluating the research findings overall, it is observed that consumers tend to be middle -aged, highly
educated, mostly female, mostly married, and do not live in very crowded households. Consumers' perceptions of the
Trakyabirlik brand, according to Aaker's Brand Equity Model, are as follows: The results reveal a clear hierarchy
among the dimensions that comprise brand equity. Perceived quality emerges as the most prominent and positive
attribute of the brand in the eyes of consumers, with a score of 3.98. This suggests that consumers have a strong

opinion of the brand's product or service quality, reliability, and performance. Brand awareness scores 3.84, showing
that the brand is well-known and has a high recall within the target audience. Brand associations, with a score of 3.56,
indicate moderate strength, while brand loyalty, with the lowest score of 3.03, is the weakest link in the brand's equity.
This suggests that, despite consumers' positive perception and recognition of the brand, there are some difficulties in
converting these positive feelings into long-term loyalty, highlighting an area for potential improvement.
The correlation analysis results showed strong, positive links among all dimensions. These findings indicate
that individuals with high brand awareness also view the brand as higher quality and are more loyal. Similarly, as
perceived quality increases, brand loyalty tends to rise. All these relationships suggest that consumers hold a favorable
view of the Trakyabirlik brand and trust it. The results reveal that Trakyabirlik has a solid structure, particularly in
terms of brand awareness, brand association, and perceived quality, supporting the notion that these factors play a
crucial role in enhancing consumer loyalty. Therefore, it is recommended to continue activities that boost brand
awareness. Specifically, increasing activity on digital and social media platforms could be beneficial.
Additionally, maintaining and enhancing perceived quality is essential. Focus should be given to elements such
as product packaging, freshness, production process, and content quality. Strategies aimed at strengthening loyalty to
Trakyabirlik brands should  also be developed. In this context, encouraging promotions or special advantages for
cooperative members can help maintain and expand the loyal customer base. Additionally, regularly collecting and
evaluating customer feedback will strengthen brand loyalty. Trakyabirlik's status as a locally based, cooperative brand
can be a benefit, especially for consumer groups sensitive to local production. Campaigns that highlight this attribute
can build further trust in the brand.
In this context, policy recommendations and practical applications can be concretized as follows:
Brand Awareness Strategies: To increase brand awareness, the digital advertising and social media marketing
budget should be increased, and regular and interactive content should be shared with the target audience.
Furthermore, targeted advertising campaigns and promotional activities should be launched in provinces outside the
Thrace region.
Brand Association Strengthening Strategies: A communication strategy that clarifies the brand personality and
establishes an emotional connection with consumers should be adopted. In this context, a "storytelling" approach
should be used that highlights Tr akyabirlik's cooperative history, its mission to support local producers, and the
cultural values of the region.
Perceived Quality Strengthening Strategies: To maintain and strengthen perceived quality, a "freshness
promise" system can be implemented to guarantee product freshness on retail shelves. Packaging should be physically
differentiated from competitors by adopting a sustainable and premium appearance. "Farm -to-table" communication
campaigns should be organized that emphasize the transparency of production processes.
Strategies to Increase Brand Loyalty: A loyalty program that directly targets brand loyalty and rewards repeat
purchases (such as accumulating points, offering one free item for every 10 purchases, and providing special offers to
members) should be launched. Cooperative members should be offered tangible and attractive privileges, such as extra
discounts on grocery shopping or annual profit distributions.
Using Local Emphasis in Marketing: To reinforce the trustworthiness of being a local, cooperative-based brand,
emotionally engaging commercials and social media campaigns with themes like "The Power of the Local" or "The
Abundance of the Cooperative" should be developed.
And finally, the limitations of this study are that the data were collected from consumers in Çanakkale who
had direct interactions with the Trakyabirlik brand. This focused approach enabled an in- depth understanding of the
brand-consumer relationship within a specific context. However, to apply these significant findings to a broader area
and more diverse consumer groups, future research should expand to include other regions, especially Thrace, where
the brand mainly operates.
