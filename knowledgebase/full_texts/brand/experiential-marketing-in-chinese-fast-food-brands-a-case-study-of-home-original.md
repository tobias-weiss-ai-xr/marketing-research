Abstract
In the experience -oriented economy, firms increasingly rely on experiential marketing
strategies to foster customer engagement, satisfaction, and loyalty. This study investigates
the role of experiential marketing in shaping consumer perceptions and brand loyalty in the
Chinese fast-food industry through a case study of Home Original Chicken. Established in 2003
in Anhui Province, the brand has built a strong regional reputation by combining traditional
homestyle cuisine with multisensory dining experiences , including warm restaurant
environments, transparent food preparation practices, and culturally resonant service rituals.
Guided by Bernd H. Schmitt’s experiential marketing framework, the study examines how the
five experiential dimensions —sense, feel, t hink, act, and relate —are implemented and
perceived by customers in the Anhui market. Using a qualitative single -case study design,
data were collected through semi -structured interviews, non -participant observation, and
document analysis involving customers, employees, and industry experts. The findings reveal
that sensory cues, emotional warmth, transparency in food safety, and strong cultural
identification collectively enhance customer satisfaction and strengthen brand loyalty.
However, the research als o highlights challenges in replicating these experiential elements
beyond Anhui due to regional cultural differences and varying consumer expectations. The
study contributes to the limited literature on experiential marketing in China’s fast -food
sector an d provides insights into the portability of culturally embedded experiential
strategies in regional brand expansion.
Keywords: Experiential Marketing, Fast-Food Industry, Consumer Loyalty, China

cleanliness, family hospitality, openness, rites of interaction, and cultural accessibility, might
not be effortlessly replicated in a new regional environment (SohuFood, 2026). The out-of-

company, therefore, has to revisit the portability of its experiential model to other markets.

Limited scholarly interest exists in the role of the discrete experiential qualities, such as

relational engagement (Islam et al., 2024; Nur et al., 2024), in consumer behavior in the fast-
food market in China. It is not clear whether a sense of warmth/safety is based on material

experiential factors can be converted into observable increases in satisfaction and loyalty, as
assumed in other sectors (Lu et al., 2023), is missing. This gap is to be filled by the current
study of Home Original Chicke n in its Anhui core market based on the five experiential
modules proposed by Schmitt: sense, feel, think, act, and relate. The study aims to explain
the induction of competitive advantage and consumer loyalty by examining how experiential
strategies captu re the expression of cleanliness and warmth, transparency by home
metaphor, and how local culture and rituals reinforce the understanding of identity during
the brand development in the fast-food sector in China.

Problem Statement
Rapid growth of Home Original Chicken in Anhui province indicates that the experiential
approach by this company, focused on safety, familiarity and warmth, has proven to be
effective in meeting customer expectations. Patrons in this territory view the bra nd as a
dependable informal family kitchen which creates high levels of affective attachment
(UnicornEarlyNews, 2025). However, some important questions must be posed: Is it possible
to maintain these qualities of experience in conditions of further geographical growth? There
are also the initial signs of difficulties, i.e. the out-of-province shops perform worse, meaning
that the regime factor in Anhui is not going to replicate in the other places (SohuFood, 2026).
The range of differences in the culinary preferences and the cultural background suggests
that the brand might have to modify the experiential propositions. A s a result, one of the
main problems is the identification of which of those experiential elements has universal
validity and which ones depend upon the cultural variables of places.

Limitations
This case -study analysis of Home Original Chicken provides a deep understanding, but it
should be taken with caution when it comes to the external validity. The sample is culturally
and geographically specific to the region and restricts the applicability of the results to the
entire scope of Chinese fast-food businesses or to other provinces. Qualitative data collected
on the basis of company papers, fifteen semi -structured interviews, and observation notes
are all based on a rather small, defined Anhui -focused group, which can possibly be
considered a biased view of non -Anhui consumer views. This homogeneity can distort the
outcomes to have a positive view of Anhui customers.

Constructs which include warmth, channelling, etc., are subjective in nature. Verbatim
quotations and member-checking procedures were utilised to maximise the credibility, and
cross-validation of insights was made possible through facilitated peer-debriefing. However,
researcher bias, which may arise from the researcher's knowledge of Chinese social culture,
cannot be fully eliminated. The qualitative design of the study does not permit statistical
estimation (e.g., the magnitude of the effect of transparen cy on loyalty), which precludes
drawing causal conclusions. In addition, there were issues of social desirability and
observation effects, in that the staff and clients might present the brand positively, and the
presence of the researcher could change behaviour. Anonymity guarantees and unobtrusive
observation were measures that were taken to curb these biases, but some residual effects
can still be present.

Literature Review
Marketing nowadays has shifted into an experience -based paradigm (its feature -centric
paradigm) (Azmi Mohd Ariffin et al., 2026). Contemporary firms now orchestrate
multisensory, affective customer journeys (Schmitt, 2011, Pine and Gilmore, 1998) . Schmitt
also identifies five modules of experience, including Sense (sensory stimuli), Feel (emotional
states), Think (cognitive engagement), Act (physical and lifestyle interplay) and Relate (social
identity). The entire goal is the combination of all these dimensions in the holistic encounter
(Schmitt, 2010). The researchers agree that enriched, multidimensional experiences that are
synthesising senses, emotions, cognitive and social elements are the driving factors that lead
to engagement and loyalty (Gentile et al., 2007, Lu et al., 2023).

Within the foodservice industry, the concepts of experience are especially relevant: as seen
with themed restaurants and performance -driven restaurants, experience and conduct
create a memorable experience (Hossain et al., 2023a; Hossain et al., 2023b; Isl am et al.,

2025; Khaled et al., 2019). Even established fast -food chains are evolving; McDonald’s and
Starbucks are reorganising store layouts into a comfortable setting, and KFC China is
incorporating emotional overtones, the theme of family reunion during the Chinese New Year
to draw consumer relationships. These strategies are moderated by cultural context: the
Western chains in China tend to preload convenience or nostalgia, whereas local brands
incorporate tradition and health awareness. A good example is a baker y based in Shanghai
that utilises the local heritage in its design (Li, 2024). Modern Chinese buyers are more after

The Home Original Chicken is a typical example of the Chinese local fast food. It has been able
to compete with foreign entrants due to its assimilation of the Anhui staple cuisine and fast
service delivery. The chain recorded fast growth in revenue (5.6 b illion RMB in 2023, with
more than 1,400 stores), and had been dubbed the Chinese KFC (Unicorn Early News, 2025).
Its menu is based on Feixi old hen soup and comfort food, which purposely refers to
homestyle cooking. Brand stories predetermine family and l ocal identity. In practice, the
company uses the principles of experience by putting a very strong emphasis on sensory
signals (icy -clean storefronts; enticing aromas), the warm reeds of its employees (use of
kinship language in the dialect), and extreme t ransparency programs, such as an extensive
200,000-word Food Traceability Report naming all suppliers (iMedia, 2026)  and live
nutritional information on digital interfaces. Certain rituals, like unending free refills on the
soup and self -checkout counters, give autonomy to patrons. Furthermore, the community
connection of the company throughout its locations (placing ou tlets in residential
neighbourhoods, providing menu variations related to the region), as well as communal
feasts organized on the company anniversaries, also contribute to the sense, feel, think, act,
and relate alignment, which is further attained through its connection with the Anhui culture.

In spite of these systematic practices, the academic study regarding experiential marketing
as it is applied to Chinese fast food is limited. Existing literature predominantly addresses
other sectors or Western contexts (Lee and Cho, 2024, Alam, 2024). Therefore, the channels
by which loyalty and word -of- mouth accrual is generated by home chains have not been
comprehensively clarified. The proposed study aims to fill this gap by critically examining
Home Original Chicken as a whole based on the five e xperiential modules as presented by
Schmitt in the context of its distinct cultural setting.

Research Methodology
The study is a qualitative, single -case (embedded) study based on the Chinese fast -food
restaurant Home Original Chicken under the provincial setting in Anhui. This is an exploratory
and descriptive design, purposefully developed to provide a subtle focus on the descriptions
of the implementation and perception of experiential marketing, a context in which depth of
context is paramount. Through interactive arrangement of embedded units of analysis, i.e.
customer experience, staff perspectives and store sett ings, documentation of the rapport
between brand strategy and local cultural practice is documented, thus making the case a
sturdy topic of academic testing.

The research employs a triangulated data -collection approach that incorporates semi -
structured interviews, non-participant observation and document analysis in a bid to achieve
strong construct validity. The interview protocol is structured and coordinated  according to
the five dimensions of experience described by Schmitt (1999), namely Sense, Feel, Think,
Act, and Relate, providing a coherent theoretical framework within which the research
objectives are reconsidered. This model guides data collection amo ngst three purposely
chosen groups of stakeholders, namely, customers, employees/managers and industry
experts, hence, encouraging multi -faceted, multi -viewer understanding. The results are
further situated within the solid realities of service delivery an d common discourse through
the observation offered in multiple outlets and brand media analysis.

The study is conducted in accordance with rigorous ethical and procedural guidelines, with
informed consent, anonymity, and strict adherence to observational protocols. Within the
scope of Anhui, where the brand was originally based, one can carry out an ethnographically
grounded interpretation of the power of experiential marketing by the provincial culture and
consumer behaviors. Finally, the methodology is to yield deep, contextualized information on
the overlap of experience aspects that create competitive advantage, as well as discover the
natural ease and challenge of extension of place-sensitive strategy outside its market base.

Data Analysis Plan
The analysis of data was conducted in a systematic and iterative manner of qualitative
methodology, and its aim is to interpret the information related to the experience marketing
of Home Original Chicken in a rigorous manner. It was designed based on the five dimensions
of Schmitt (1999) of the experiential dimensions of a priori deductive framework, offering
initial coding on a priori basis, which was directly related to the objectives of the research. At
the same time, the analysis was essentially open t o an inductive understanding to allow the
development of the salient themes which were not anticipated by the model, i.e. the issue of
value-for-money. The open -ended research approach that was used was a hybrid strategy
that enabled the subtle questioning  of the data, balancing between theoretical rigour and
empirical support.

Data analysis consisted of the careful transcription and translation of interview tapes,
explanation of the field notes and collation of documentary materials, all of which were
strictly organized with NVivo software. The coding procedure was conducted by both the
deductive use of the five main types of core dimensions of experiences - each possessing its
unique sub-codes- and inductively by any shared pattern of the data. This line-by-line coding
was supplemented by the continued production of analytic memo s, which reflected
interpretive annotations and developing conceptual relations to the content, securing an
active and reflexive involvement in the content.

Thematic construction facilitated the development of coded extracts synthesis, through
which higher -order and integrative themes were developed. These themes, including the
Metaphor of Home theme or Transparency and Health Consciousness, summarized the
results of data sources and stakeholder groups. Thereafter, the themes were brought out in
distinct, explicit mapping back to the four research queries, with each query answered in a
logical, evidence-based narrative. This mapping described how experiential s trategies were
implemented (RQ1), customer perception crystallization (RQ2), the elucidation of synergistic
interrelation of dimensions as a source of competitive advantage (RQ3), and the identification
of expansion problems (RQ4).

A multi -faceted validation strategy ensured that methodological rigor was observed. The
triangulation was realized through a comparison of the information provided by customers,
employees or experts and member checks and peer debriefing were used to test and improve
the interpretations. The paper trail on the entire analytical process, including preparation,
codes, and thematic synthesis, has been recorded to ensure there is a good audit trail
preceding the conclusion, and the findings add value to the acad emic discussion on
experiential marketing in localised service situations.

Data Analysis
The analysis shows that the sensory aspect of Home Original Chicken is operationally
designed to create a connotation of safety and comfort by a well-modulated environment of
cleanliness and warmth. The clear, carefully clean interiors are constantly watched and noted,
and the smell of simmering chicken soup that makes everything serves as the main sensory
message that goes beyond even hygiene to create a feeling of emotional comfort. This
designed space, with its heated light, wood-covered furniture, and controlled sound, actively
builds a world of perceived safety and is, in a sense, a distinguishing element compared to
the utilitarian fast-food models and a significant foundation of the whole experience through
its sensory basis.

The brand capitalises on a powerful home metaphor in terms of emotion, which it executes
with the help of family -oriented service scripts, nostalgic food, and spatial iconography.
Workers are systematised to become unrestricted relatives, using language fo rms and
customary generalisations, which make the service encounters relatable social interactions.
This sentiment of family cosiness and nostalgia that is supported by homestyle comfort food
creates a high level of affective attachment that is converted to a very high level of customer
loyalty, making the brand an emotional refuge in the coldness of the impersonal fast -food
environment.

A radically transparent paradigm involving a radical backlash against transparency and a
health awakening discourse competes with customers mentally, so as to be cognitively
attractive to customers, Home Original Chicken. Open kitchens, elaborate food trac eability
reports and availability of nutritional information are a strategic move to develop intellectual
trust and restructure the perceptions about fast foods. This informational transparency gives
the consumer the power, making the brand a viable and he alth-sensitive option, and thus,
meeting an increasing need of consumers to make informed purchases and introduce a new
rational nuance in a perspective of justification of the emotional and sensation -bearing
appeal.

The action dimension is defined by highly tuned -up consumption ceremonies, the most
prominent of which is the self -service soup stand. This straightforward procedure of
replenishing soup serves as a ritual of control and magnanimity, which also provides cl ients
with a feeling of agency and instils a memorable routine that will be a part of the experience.
These other elements of self-service and a balance of service result in the nurturing of a sense
of active participation, which moves the customer, who is  being pursued, into an active
participant and invests in the brand experience again and again.

On the relational level, the brand strength has been based on the extensive embedding into
the community and culture. Having made a necklace of outlets and neighborhood fixtures, to
the menus tailored to local Anhui preferences, and using locally ringing symbols and dialects,
Home Original Chicken is more than a commercial enterprise and turns into a social
institution. It creates a strong sense of group pride and ownership amongst patrons and
develops a defensive, community -based loyalty that is very stron g indeed and has unique
difficulties when it comes to geographic reproduction outside of its cultural core.

Findings and Conclusion
This paper concludes that the overall experiential marketing at Home Original Chicken greatly
affects customer loyalty and brand position. Summarizing by dimension:
Sense: The first impression of the chain is the feeling of being safe and comfortable because
of its clarity, homeliness, and cleanliness. Warm -light and conceptualised soup aroma on
shining surfaces gives reassurance to the diners about the quality of food. The customer
loyalty to Home Original Chicken starts immediately as the customers identify these senses
that make Home Original Chicken stand out from sterile or greasy fast-food shops.
Feel: Special service and nostalgic dishes that feel like a personal and family experience
command a solid emotional connection. Employees refer to customers as family (by names,
family terms), and the brand story highlights the fact that it is a family kitchen.  It is common
to find customers saying that they feel at home when eating. The service, as well as
comforting food, creates this emotional attachment, which results in loyalty. When the chain
is cheaper or more convenient, diners are inclined to use it nevertheless, as it provides them
with true warmth and satisfaction.
Think: The experience is cognitively valuable through the use of radical transparency and
health-oriented communication. Open kitchens and published reports by suppliers are what
make fast food not a mystery, targeting the open kitchen of the restaurant and allo wing the
customer to feel that he takes charge. The stereotypes regarding the unhealthiness of fast
food are eliminated with nutritional information and health partnerships (apps, 14 -day
challenge). This has re -educated the brand to make it more appe aling to health -conscious
people and helped create a sense of trust. Ingredients and processes (we use 180 -day free-
range hens) may be valued by customers because they meet their desire to make an informed
choice and also make the brand image better.
Act: The interactive and self -service aspects approach customers physically. The side -dish
counters and the soup -refill ritual give the customers the option of personalising and
regulating aspects of their dining. These exchanges make the diners active particip ants. It
results in empowerment and pleasure: customers are satisfied with such little things
(selecting dishes, filling soup) as they feel taken care of and autonomous. These rituals are
well-designed rituals that add perceived value but do not make the service complex.

Relate: A brand is closely interwoven with the local social environment. It offers community

symbols/dialect, and communal events. The chain is regarded by Anhui consumers as theirs,
which denotes the local pride (presumably, to a superior level compared to global chains).
Participation in local culture and social good (charity, free meals) would make the customers

These dimensions support each other within a unified system: employees (Feel) provide the

a home -type atmosphere, which has been carefully nurtured. The eff ect of each of these

Recommendations
Keep the Family Culture:  Continue to have the warm and informal service approach as the
brand expands. School and coach employees strictly adhere to the message of the brand:
efficiency in combination with true warmth. Indicatively, allocate senior Anhui employees as
experience ambassadors to mentor new ones on things to do when greeting and how to treat
relatives and care about them. Instil knowledge of local practices in all new locations (e.g.
rudimentary dialect phrases) in a way that even as they proliferate, every one of their stores
would feel like a home. Reward employees constantly who are good examples of the family-
oriented values of the brand.
Fit Local Cultures:  Do not use a one -size-fits-all strategy. Although keeping the essential
identity (chicken soup base, home-cooked concept), the majority of the menu and the interior
should be localised to feature local tastes of each specific market, about 10 -15 per cent. As
an example, you can include a Sichuan spicy soup booth or a Guangdong herbal tea in proper
locations. Apply local design or festivals within every outlet as a sign of reverence towards
the community culture. This happens to be an ind icator that the brand will be a kitchen in
any community and at the same time, not to lose its soul, by adapting to the local taste.
Lead in Transparency and Health:  Keep on focusing on transparency. Frequently update
traceability data and make it available (e.g. QR codes that display live supply -chain maps or
fresh food webcams). Increase the availability of ingredient sourcing and nutrition
information online as a w ay of attacking the tech -savvy customer base. Collaborate with
health/wellness influencers to amplify the message that Home Original Chicken is compatible
with healthy lifestyles. High standards of food safety and their externalization as a progressive
step to win trust should be maintained.
Improve Rituals and Participation: You should maintain rituals and participation that are
stress-free and enjoyable to the customer. Create a few surprises that are enjoyed by
returning clients (a treat of some sort during a season, such as ginger tea at the soup stand in
winter). Ensure that high-volume outlets can accommodate refilling of soup (with additional
stations or personnel when demand is high) to prevent overcrowding and frustration. Think
about the light and optional digital engagements to supplement the physical experience (e.g.
a simple loyalty application or competition) and do not resort to anything that dominates the
