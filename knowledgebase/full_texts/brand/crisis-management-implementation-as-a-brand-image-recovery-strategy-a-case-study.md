Effective crisis management is essential for
restoring organizational reputation and brand
image following a crisis. This study examines the
implementation of crisis management by
Mecimapro following the 2025 DAY6 concert in
Jakarta and its role in support ing brand image
recovery. A qualitative case study approach was
employed using in depth interviews,
documentation and online observation. Data
were analyzed through data reduction, data
display, conclusion drawing and source
triagulation. The findings reveal that Mecimapro
implemented several crisis management
measures including public apologies, refund
processing, official communication and internal
evaluation. However, these efforts were
hindered by delayed responses, limited
transparency and the lingering impact of
previous crises, which weakened public trust.
The study also found that while fans remained
emotionally attached to the artist, their
confidence in the promotor had declined.

INTRODUCTION
With the advancement of globalization, interactions among nations have
become increasingly intensive, facilitationg the widespread exchange of cultural
values across national borders. This phenomenon has contributed to the growing
influence of foreign cultural aspect of daily life. One of the most prominent forms
of foreign cultural influence particularly among young people is South Korean
popular culture. Commonly referred to as the Korean Wave or Hallyu this
phenomenon has experienced remarkable growth and has spread rapidly across
many countries including Indonesia, over the past several years (Valentina &
Istriyani, 2017).
Jakarta as Indonesia’s economic and cultural hub has become one of the
destinations for international K -pop concert. This is reflected in the strong
enthusiasm of young audiences who consistently fill major concert venues such
as Gelora Bung Karno (GBK), ICE BSD, and JIEXpo. These venues have become
gathering places for K -pop fans from various regions across Indonesia
highlighting the country’s growing demand for international live entertainment
events (Zalianti, 2025).
Music concerts have become one of the most popular forms of
entertainment among various segments of Indonesian society particulary in
urban areas. The rapid development of information technology has made ticket
purchasing more accessible through various on line platforms enabling broader
public participation in live music events. Despite fluctuations in economic
conditions and changes in customers purchasing power, public interest in
attending concerts has continued to grow. This trend suggests that high qua lity
and affordable concert experiences have become an integral part of urban
entertainment consumption. For many concertgoers, purchasing tickets is driven
not only by economic considerations but also by emotional motivations such as
self-reward and the desire to gain unique social and cultural experiences (Anang
Muzaki et al., 2025).
The South Korean entertainment industry has developed into a cultural
force with significant influence in Indonesia. The growing demand for
international concerts has encouraged the emergence of promoters specifically
targeting the Korean entertainment fan  market. PT Melania Citra Permata
(Mecimapro) is one of the major concert promoters that has actively introduced
Korean artists to Indonesia since 2015 through concerts, fan meetings, and
festivals that facilitate direct interactions between artists and fa ns. Mecimapro’s
popularity has been strengthened through collaborations with various South
Korean entertainment agencies and extensive networks within local fan
communities. However, behind its established reputation, the company has
faced several ethical concerns that have raised questions regarding its
commitment and professionalism. The relocation of the DAY6 concert venue in
2025 without adequate explanation, the cancellation of several promised
facilities, and changes affecting consumer rights created uncertainty among
attendees. These issues generated widespread criticism across various social
media platforms, resulting in negative public responses toward the promoter.
Such criticism reflects the challenges faced by entertainment businesses in

balancing economic objectives with the implementation of ethical principles and

events as they provide opportunities to engage directly with their favorite artists.
Throughout 202 5, numerous international artists held concerts in Indonesia,
attracting large numbers of attendees. Although ticket prices were relatively
high, this did not significantly discourage fans from purchasing tickets. The
desire to witness their idols’ perform ances and experience live events remained
a strong motivation for concert attendance. However, the high cost of concert

particularly fans, may still lack adequate protection of their rights. Such

Therefore, stronger and more comprehensive regulations are needed to ensure
consumer protection and accountability within the concert industry(Agustien et

The successful organization of Western and K -pop concerts in Indonesia
cannot be separated from the role of concert promoters as key actors responsible
for coordinating the overall event production process. The growing number of
promoters operating in Indo nesia has resulted in varying levels of service
quality, strengths, and challenges in organizing concerts. As a result, fans often
evaluate and compare different promoters based on their previous experiences
attending various events. These comparisons cont ribute to the formation of
public perceptions regarding the credibility, professionalism, and reliability of
concert organizers (Sabil & Parmitasari, n.d.).
The entertainment industry, particularly international music concerts, is not
immune to the potential emergence of communication crises caused by various
operational challenges during event implementation. Incidents such as sudden
venue changes, seat plan adjustments, and disorganized ticket exchange
procedures can become major triggers of public dissatisfaction. Such situations
may develop into broader crises that directly affect public perceptions and the
brand image of concert promoters. In the context of K-pop concerts in Indonesia,
communication crises are increasingly exposed due to the role of social media in
accelerating the spread of negative opinions among fans.
One notable example is the crisis surrounding the SEVENTEEN “RIGHT
HERE” World Tour in Jakarta, which was also promoted by Mecimapro. During
the first day of ticket exchange, several fans reported unclear procedures,
inadequate staff coordination, and various communication issues that resulted in
losses of time, effort, and audience convenience. The increasing volume of public
complaints caused the issue to escalate rapidly and gain widespread attention on
social media platforms. Although Mecimapro released  an official statement on
the same day, the response was perceived as insufficient by some fans, as it was
considered to emphasize the promoter’s reputation management rather than
providing concrete solutions for affected attendees (Kusumadewi et al., 2025).

LITERATURE REVIEW
Crisis Management
Crisis communication is an ongoing process that must be continuously
implemented throughout the crisis management process. The continuity of crisis
communication should be reflected in every stage of crisis management.
Furthermore, crisis communication inv olves the development and
implementation of appropriate communication strategies to effectively respond
to crisis situations (Juliana Rifka, 2022).
One of the most influential and useful crisis typologies was initially
introduced by Coombs and later refined and further developed by Coombs and
Holladay. This typology is based on crisis responsibility attribution, which refers
to the extent to which an organization is perceived as responsible for the
occurrence of a crisis. Coombs explained that the higher the level of responsibility
attributed to an organization, the stronger the public’s anger and the lower the
evaluation of the organization’s reputation. Therefore, by identifying the type of
crisis at an early stage, crisis managers can anticipate the level of responsibility
that stakeholders may assign to the organization and determine the initial degree
of crisis responsibility that must be addressed . A crisis is often perceived as a
threatening situation because most organizations seek to avoid negative
consequences associated with crisis events. However, crises can also serve as
valuable learning opportunities that enable organizations to improve th eir
preparedness, prevent similar incidents in the future, and develop more effective
response strategies. Efforts to prevent and manage crises while protecting
organizations and their stakeholders can be categorized into three phases of crisis
management: pre-crisis, crisis, and post-crisis (Coombs & Holladay, 2012).
When a crisis occurs, organizations often become the primary focus of
media attention, requiring well-developed management strategies to address the
situation effectively. According to Wilcox, crisis management is a public relations
practice developed thro ugh detailed planning to anticipate various potential
crises that can be predicted within certain limitations. Therefore, the primary
objectives of crisis management are to provide optimal protection against the
impacts of a crisis, ensure rapid and appropriate responses during the crisis, and
prepare recovery and rehabilitation efforts after the crisis has ended (Wilcox,
2005).

Situational Crisis Communication Theory
The Situational Crisis Communication Theory (SCCT) was introduced by
Timothy W. Coombs and Sherry J. Holladay. The fundamental assumption of this
theory is that a crisis represents a negative event that encourages the public to
attribute responsibility to an organization regarding the occurrence of the crisis.
Attribution refers to the public’s perception and interpretation of a crisis event
(Kriyantono et al., 2017).Therefore, SCCT considers attribution as a key aspect in
determining whether an organization’s reputation can be protected or further
damaged. This theory emphasizes the importance of understanding public
evaluations of crisis events and how organizationa l decisions and responses are
formulated to prevent additional negative consequences. Appropriate crisis
response strategies are necessary to avoid creating a boomerang effect that may
further harm the organization’s reputation.

Brand Image
The efforts made by companies to build brand image are essential because
brand image serves as a repository of value for an organization. Through a brand
name, companies indirectly invest through accumulated advertising
expenditures and continuous improvements in product quality. This investment
can be considered a long -term asset that plays an important role in supporting
marketing strategies and creating competitive advantages in the marketplace
(Rekarti et al., 2018).
According to Rangkuti (2008), brand image is understood as a set of
associations that are formed and stored in consumers’ minds. This indicates that
brand image represents consumers’ perceptions of a brand, which are reflected
through the associations embedded in their memory. Meanwh ile, Kotler and
Keller (2008) further explain that brand image refers to the impressions formed
in consumers’ minds through the messages and experiences they obtain
regarding a brand, resulting in a particular perception of the brand from the
consumers’ perspective.
According to Ferrinadewi (2008), one of the factors that contributes to the
formation of brand image is the presence of uniqueness and distinctive appeal
that enables a product to possess characteristics that are difficult for competitors
to imitate. This uniqueness creates a strong and  memorable impression among
customers, thereby strengthening the brand image in consumers’ minds.
Kartajaya (2003) explained that one of the factors influencing brand image
development is the position of the brand in relation to the product itself. A brand
functions as an umbrella that represents and strengthens the value of a product
in consumers’ perceptions. Through the presence of a brand, consumers tend to
compare the actual performance of the product they experience with the
promises communicated through the brand’s slogans and messages.

METHODOLOGY
In line with Yin’s (2003) perspective, the case study approach is considered
relevant for this research because it focuses on an in -depth examination of the
crisis management phenomenon experienced by Mecimapro following the
organization of the DAY6 concert. This phenomenon represents a contemporary
event occurring within a real-life context and involving various actors and social
interactions, including relationships between the company, the public, media,
and other stakeholders. Through a case study approach, this research enable s a
comprehensive understanding of how crisis management is implemented and
how these efforts contribute to the brand image recovery process by utilizing
various relevant sources of data.

RESEARCH RESULT
The Implementation of Crisis Management By Mecimapro
To gain a deeper understanding of this phenomenon, the researcher
categorizes the case into three stages: the pre-crisis, crisis, and post-crisis phases.
This categorization aims to explain how the crisis initially emerged, how it
developed over time, and the efforts undertaken to manage and recover the
situation after the crisis occurred.
The pre -crisis phase represents the initial stage of crisis management, in
which several signs or indications of potential problems begin to emerge before
a crisis fully occurs. During this stage, organizations start to identify early
warnings that may develop into a crisis if they are not recognized and managed
appropriately. Therefore, this phase emphasizes the importance of monitoring
situations, identifying potential risks, and preparing preventive measures to
minimize the possibility of a more severe crisis during the implementation of an
event.
The crisis phase represents the peak of a series of problems, in which the
crisis fully emerges and generates direct negative impacts on the organization.
During this stage, issues that previously appeared as early warning signs
develop into a crisis that attracts significant attention from the public and media.
Consequently, the organization’s reputation becomes increasingly affected as
criticism, complaints, and negative sentiments spread across social media
platforms and public spaces. Therefore, the cri sis phase is considered the most
critical stage, as organizations must respond to the situation quickly,
appropriately, and effectively to manage the impacts and prevent further
damage to their reputation
The evaluation and preventive action phase occurs after the crisis has begun
to subside, in which organizations focus on managing long -term impacts,
restoring their reputation, and reviewing compliance with applicable laws and
regulations.

Mecimapro’s action of issuing a public apology through social media
represents a form of crisis communication aimed at acknowledging the issues
that occurred. From the perspective of brand image, this action has the potential
to improve public perceptions of the company’s responsibility by demonstrating
its willingness to respond to consumer complaints. However, based on interview
findings and research documentation, an apology alone was insufficient to
restore consumer trust without being followed by a com prehensive refund
resolution process. This indicates that brand image recovery is influenced not
only by communication efforts but also by the consistency of organizational
actions in fulfilling its commitments.

DISCUSSION
In this section, the study discusses the research findings by relating them to
the Situational Crisis Communication Theory (SCCT). This analysis aims to
examine how Mecimapro’s crisis communication strategies were perceived by
the public and to explain the  process of brand image recovery based on the
empirical findings. By integrating the research findings with the theoretical
framework, this discussion provides a deeper understanding of the factors that
influenced public responses to Mecimapro’s crisis management efforts.
Based on the research findings, the crisis that occurred during Mecimapro’s
organization of the DAY6 concert exhibits characteristics consistent with the
Preventable Cluster in the Situational Crisis Communication Theory (SCCT). This
category describes crises that are perceived to result from factors within the
organization's control, leading the public to attribute a high level of responsibility
to the organization. The findings indicate that the informants associated the crisis
with several internal factors, including weaknesses in operational management,
the suboptimal implementation of standard operating procedures (SOPs), and
inadequate risk management that failed to anticipate potential challenges
throughout the concert organization process.
However, the findings indicate that Mecimapro’s crisis response was not
entirely consistent with the recommendations proposed by the Situational Crisis
Communication Theory (SCCT). The empirical evidence reveals a discrepancy
between the crisis response st rategies recommended by the theory and the
responses perceived by the informants throughout the crisis management
process.
The findings indicate that Mecimapro’s crisis response was perceived as
relatively slow and, at times, defensive. In addition, the company’s
communication through social media was regarded as largely formal, serving
primarily as an expression of organizational accountability. However, it did not
fully meet public expectations for communication that was empathetic,
transparent, and responsive.
The findings indicate that delays in communicating information and a lack
of transparency during the initial stage of crisis management led the public to
perceive that Mecimapro’s response was inadequate. The informants viewed the
organization as initiatin g its crisis communication efforts only after the issues
had escalated and attracted widespread media attention.

This situation negatively affected the credibility of the organization’s crisis

CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS
This study aimed to explore Mecimapro’s crisis management strategies in
responding to the issues that emerged following the DAY6 concert in Jakarta,
while also identifying the key factors influencing the company’s reputation
recovery. Based on the qualitat ive data analysis and the Situational Crisis
Communication Theory (SCCT), the findings indicate that Mecimapro’s crisis
response—including issuing an official public apology, providing refunds, and
implementing internal improvements —was insufficient to eff ectively restore
stakeholder trust. This outcome was primarily attributed to delayed responses,
limited transparency, inconsistent communication, and the incomplete
resolution of compensation issues.
Overall, the findings suggest that brand image recovery depends not only
on effective crisis communication but also on the organization's ability to
maintain stakeholder trust through transparency, good governance, and concrete
actions that fulfill consume r rights. These findings highlight that sustainable
reputation recovery requires consistency between organizational communication
and corrective actions following a crisis.  Based on the findings of this study,
several practical and academic recommendations are proposed. Practically, event
organizers and concert promoters should strengthen crisis management practices
by prioritizing transparent communication, timely responses, and effective
stakeholder engagement. Academically, future research is encouraged to further
examine crisis management, crisis communication, and brand image recovery
across different organizational and crisis contexts to broaden the understanding
of post-crisis reputation recovery.

ADVANCED RESEARCH
Future research is encouraged to expand the scope of investigation by
examining crisis management and brand image recovery across different event
organizers, industries, or crisis contexts to enhance the generalizability of the
findings. Comparative studies involving multiple organizations or countries may
provide a broader understanding of how cultural, organizational, and contextual
factors influence the effectiveness of crisis communication strategies. In addition,
future studies may adopt mixed-methods or quantitative approaches to examine
the relationships between crisis response strategies, stakeholder trust, and brand
image recovery more comprehensively.
