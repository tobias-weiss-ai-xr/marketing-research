Abstract
The rapid growth of social media has increased the importance of personal branding among digital content creators. However,
many influencers face challenges in maintaining a strong personal brand, especially when controversies negatively affect their
public image and credibility. This phenomenon raises questions regarding how influencers sustain audience trust and maintain
their popularity despite public criticism. This study aims to analyze the digital personal branding strategy of YouTube
influencer IShowSpeed using Montoya and Vandehey’s Eight Laws of Personal Branding framework and to examine how these
principles contribute to the sustainability of his online presence. The study employs a qualitative case study approach
supported by a structured content audit of 50 publicly available digital materials published or circulated between May 2024
and May 2025. The data consisted of YouTube videos, livestreams, and highlights; short-form viral clips; Instagram materials;
online news and feature articles; and supporting platform metrics. The data were analyzed through manual coding based on
the eight personal branding dimensions proposed by Montoya and Vandehey. The findings reveal that all eight dimensions
are present in IShowSpeed's digital personal branding, with Personality, Visibility, Distinctiveness, and Persistence being the
most dominant dimensions. Although several controversies affected his public reputation, his strong audience engagement
and loyal fan community enabled his personal brand to remain influential. The study confirms that Montoya and Vandehey's
personal branding theory remains relevant in explaining the success of contemporary digital influencers, while also showing
that goodwill in social media can be segmented within loyal audience communities.
Keywords: Digital Personal Branding; Content Creator; Social Media; Ishowspeed

INTRODUCTION
The rise of social media has changed how society accesses information. Not only can users consume
information, but now they also have broad opportunities to become content creators themselves. As a result, they
can produce a wide range of content for various purposes, including entertainment, information sharing, and
building relationships with their followers. This ease of access has intensified competition among content creators.
Consequently, content creators must strengthen their branding to stand out and offer unique value that attracts
audiences. In the digital era, personal branding is an individual’s strategy for building and managing their image
through social media, influencing public perception, and expanding career and social networking opportunities
(Wijaya & Nasution, 2022).
One notable example of a content creator who has successfully built strong personal branding is IShowSpeed.
Born Darren Watkins, Jr., he is a YouTuber from the United States. Through his energetic, expressive

The purpose of this essay is to analyze IShowSpeed’s branding using the Eight Laws of Personal Branding
theory developed by Montoya and Vandehey. Through this analysis, this essay aims to map the key factors that
drive the success of personal branding for content creators in the digital media era.
Based on this background, this study addresses the following research question: How does IShowSpeed
construct and maintain his digital personal branding through Montoya and Vandehey's Eight Laws of Personal
Branding in the context of social media visibility, audience loyalty, and public controversy?
The IShowSpeed phenomenon is important to study in communication research because it demonstrates the
success of personal branding among young content creators in fierce competition on popular social media
platforms such as Instagram, YouTube, and TikTok. Influencers with large followings, such as IShowSpeed, can
shape opinions, behavior, and even change the perspectives of their target audiences (Pratiwi & Ramadhani, 2025).
His ability to attract tens of millions of followers in a relatively short time shows the effectiveness of the personal
branding strategies he applies.
IShowSpeed once faced a ban from the online game Valorant for sexist comments during a live stream, which
later led to the cancellation of his collaboration with Sky Sports after an old recording sparked public criticism
(Woods, 2022). In the digital era, a person's reputation is very fragile as it can be built or destroyed in a short time.
Therefore, building personal branding consistently requires individuals to actively monitor public response and
provide appropriate feedback to maintain their reputation (Novita et al., 2024). Controversies in viral content can
impact personal image, so personal branding management strategies must be adaptive and responsive. This
requires awareness of public opinion dynamics and readiness to face various communication risks.
Communication issues regarding personal branding and digital reputation are increasingly important to examine,
especially for influencers and content creators who shape public perceptions in the entertainment industry.
Understanding these dynamics offers both theoretical insight and practical implications for anyone seeking to
build, maintain, or restore their image in the highly open and rapidly changing social media era.
In the digital era, building personal branding has become essential not only for public figures but also for
ordinary individuals who actively engage in social media. The Eight Laws of Personal Branding theory, developed
by Peter Montoya, has become a primary foundation for establishing a credible and recognizable image in the
digital environment (Djeke & Paramita, 2024). This theory consists of eight interconnected principles that help
individuals develop a strong and sustainable personal identity. The first principle, the Law of Specialization ,
emphasizes the importance of focusing on a specific field or area of expertise, making personal branding easier
for the public to recognize and remember (Efrida & Diniati, 2020). By applying this principle, individuals can
highlight their strengths and create unique characteristics that distinguish them from others.
The second principle is the Law of Leadership, which refers to the ability to project an image as a role model
who inspires others and positively influences audiences. According to Montoya, as cited in Dika and Sukmarini
(2023), leadership in personal branding aims to strengthen credibility and increase public trust toward the
individual. Furthermore, the Law of Personality  highlights the importance of originality and unique personal
characteristics displayed in everyday communication activities (Amalia & Satvikadewi, 2020). Individuals with
distinctive personalities tend to appear more authentic, making them more attractive and relatable to audiences.
Another important principle is the Law of Distinctiveness, which refers to an individual's ability to demonstrate
clear differences through their content, visual style, and communication methods. Montoya in Widiastuti (2017)
explains that uniqueness helps personal branding stand out amid increasingly intense competition. In addition, the
Law of Visibility emphasizes the importance of maintaining a consistent presence in the public sphere as part of
personal branding efforts (Salam, 2020). The more frequently individuals appear and interact with audiences, the
greater the opportunity to build strong relationships and enhance public recognition.
The sixth principle is the Law of Unity, which stresses the harmony between public image and real-life values
and behaviors (Djeke & Paramita, 2024). Consistency between what individuals present on social media and their
actual behavior is essential in building audience trust. Furthermore, the Law of Persistence  underlines the
importance of maintaining personal branding despite challenges and changing trends. According to Montoya in
Amalia and Satvikadewi (2020), persistence enables personal branding to remain relevant and sustainable over
time. The final principle, the Law of Goodwill , focuses on building a positive reputation through genuine
contributions, empathy, and responsiveness toward the public (Widiastuti, 2017). A positive reputation serves as
the foundation for establishing audience loyalty and long-term trust toward an individual.
The Eight Laws of Personal Branding theory is relevant to analyzing personal branding phenomena in the
digital era because its principles provide a comprehensive and applicable framework. Each law addresses aspects
of building self-image, including authenticity, consistency, and reputation, which are essential on social media.
The theory helps analyze personal branding systematically and thoroughly, and can capture communication
dynamics and challenges amid growing competition.

RESEARCH METHODS
This study used a qualitative approach with a single-case study design. The qualitative approach was chosen
because the study aimed to understand the meaning, pattern, and communication logic behind IShowSpeed's
digital personal branding, rather than measuring it statistically. Qualitative research allows the researcher to
examine social phenomena in a contextual and flexible way, especially when the object of study is closely related
to meaning-making, identity construction, public perception, and symbolic communication practices (Taylor et
al., 2016; Yin, 2016; Salim & Syahrum, 2012).
The study focused on IShowSpeed as a single case because he is a highly visible young digital content creator
who has successfully built a strong global personal brand through social media. His case is relevant because his
popularity is shaped not only by the number of followers but also by his expressive communication style, viral
live-streaming practices, fan community, public controversies, and ability to maintain visibility across platforms.
A case study design is appropriate when a researcher wants to examine a contemporary phenomenon in depth and
within its real-life context (Yin, 2016). In this study, IShowSpeed's personal branding was treated as the main
case, while his social media content, public appearances, interviews, media coverage, and audience responses
served as the primary data sources.
The data in this study were collected from secondary sources. These sources included IShowSpeed's publicly
available social media content, live-streaming clips, interviews, online news articles, digital audience responses,
and prior studies on personal branding and influencer communication. The use of secondary data was considered
suitable because IShowSpeed's personal branding is mainly constructed, circulated, and evaluated in digital public
spaces. In qualitative research, documents, visual materials, online texts, and public records can be treated as
meaningful data when they are relevant to the research focus and examined systematically (Given, 2008; Taylor
et al., 2016).
To make the data analysis more transparent, this study also applied a structured content audit. The audit was
conducted on publicly available digital materials related to IShowSpeed's online activities between May 2024 and
May 2025. The period was selected because it is consistent with the follower data used in the study and captures
IShowSpeed's development as a global livestream entertainer through several major moments, including his travel
streams, public appearances, viral short-form clips, and media coverage of controversies.
The structured content audit covered 50 digital materials. The corpus consisted of YouTube videos,
livestreams, and highlights; short-form viral clips; Instagram profile materials, posts, and reels; online news and
feature articles; and platform analytics or profile metrics. These materials were purposively selected because they
directly showed IShowSpeed's personal branding practices, such as expressive communication style, audience
interaction, public visibility, controversial moments, content consistency, and fan loyalty.

The coding process was conducted manually. Each material was reviewed to identify the presence of one or
more indicators from Montoya and Vandehey's Eight Laws of Personal Branding. A dimension was coded as
present when the material provided observable evidence related to operational indicators. For example, content
showing loud emotional reactions, spontaneous responses, and hyperactive performance was coded under
Personality. Content that appeared repeatedly across YouTube, TikTok, and Instagram was coded under the
Visibility category. After coding, the researcher compared patterns across platforms and data sources to identify
dominant and less dominant dimensions.

The use of this structured audit helped the researcher separate observable findings from interpretation. The
Research Results section presents the patterns found in the digital materials, while the Discussion section
interprets these patterns by connecting them to personal branding theory, digital audience loyalty, and parasocial
relationships.
The researcher focused only on materials that directly showed IShowSpeed's personal branding practices, such
as his communication style, repeated expressions, content formats, public performances, audience interaction,
collaborations, visibility across platforms, and controversial moments. This purposive selection helped the
researcher avoid collecting unrelated digital materials and kept the analysis aligned with the research objective.
In qualitative research, purposive selection enables the researcher to select data sources that are rich, relevant, and
capable of explaining the phenomenon under study (Sugiyono, 2013; Yin, 2016).
The data collection process was conducted through online observation and document review. First, the
researcher observed IShowSpeed's public digital content to identify recurring patterns in his self-presentation,
verbal style, emotional expression, and interaction with audiences. Second, the researcher reviewed online articles
and interviews to understand how media outlets represented his public image, career development, controversies,
and audience reception. Third, the collected data were organized according to Montoya and Vandehey's Eight
Laws of Personal Branding, namely specialization, leadership, personality, distinctiveness, visibility, unity,
persistence, and goodwill. This process enabled the researcher to connect the empirical materials to the study's
theoretical framework.
The data were analyzed using qualitative thematic analysis. The researcher read and reviewed the collected
materials repeatedly to gain a deeper understanding of IShowSpeed's branding practices. After that, the data were
coded according to the eight dimensions of personal branding. For example, materials related to his energetic live-
streaming style were connected to the law of personality and distinctiveness, while his frequent appearances across
platforms were connected to the law of visibility. The researcher then compared the coded data across different
sources to identify patterns, consistencies, contradictions, and critical findings. This analytical process follows the
logic of qualitative analysis, which requires the researcher to organize data, reduce irrelevant information, identify
patterns, interpret meaning, and draw conclusions (Sugiyono, 2013; Given, 2008; Yin, 2016).
To strengthen the study's credibility, the researcher employed source triangulation by comparing information
across different data types, including social media content, online news articles, interviews, audience responses,
and previous literature. Triangulation was used to reduce dependence on a single source and to make the
interpretation more balanced. The researcher also maintained analytical transparency by clearly linking each
finding to the Eight Laws of Personal Branding. This was important because qualitative research relies heavily on

This study has several limitations. Since the study relied on secondary data, it did not include direct interviews

through visible online activities, media narratives, and audience engagement. For that reason, this study offers a
contextual and interpretive understanding of how IShowSpeed's digital personal brand is built, maintained, and
challenged in the social media environment.

they recur across livestreams, short-form clips, Instagram materials, and online media coverage.

The first dimension identified is the Law of Specialization. The analysis shows that IShowSpeed consistently
positions himself as a digital entertainer whose primary focus is delivering highly energetic and interactive
livestream content. Although he initially gained popularity through gaming-related broadcasts, particularly NBA
2K and Fortnite, his content has gradually expanded to include reaction videos, football-related activities, real-
life challenges, and international travel streams. Despite this diversification, a clear thematic consistency remains
visible throughout his content. Most broadcasts emphasize spontaneous reactions, audience engagement, humor,
and unexpected situations. This specialization became increasingly evident through a series of international
livestreams from Indonesia, Malaysia, China, and Denmark, where he interacted directly with local communities
while introducing cultural experiences to a global audience. The recurring appearance of these entertainment-
oriented elements indicates that his specialization extends beyond gaming and has evolved into a broader identity
as a livestream entertainer.
The second dimension, the Law of Leadership, is reflected in IShowSpeed's ability to influence audience
behavior and digital culture. The findings reveal that several expressions and behavioral patterns displayed during
his broadcasts have been adopted and replicated by followers across different social media platforms. One notable
example is the widespread use of the catchphrase “SUI,” which has become strongly associated with his online
persona. The influence of his content is also evident through audience responses to games, trends, and topics
featured in his broadcasts. Several digital trends experienced increased popularity after being highlighted in his
content, suggesting that his large audience base allows him to shape attention and engagement within online
communities. These observations indicate that his leadership is not formal in nature but emerges through
influence, visibility, and audience imitation.
The Law of Personality appears as one of the strongest dimensions in IShowSpeed's personal branding.
Throughout the observed content, he consistently presents himself as spontaneous, expressive, emotional, and
highly energetic. Unlike many content creators who carefully manage and curate their online personas,
IShowSpeed frequently displays unfiltered reactions that include excitement, frustration, surprise, and humor.
These emotional expressions occur naturally during livestreams and form a recognizable pattern throughout his
content. The consistency of these behaviors creates an impression of authenticity among viewers, making
audiences perceive him as genuine and relatable. As a result, personality functions are a central component of his
branding because audience engagement is often driven not only by the activities he performs but also by how he
responds to them emotionally.
The Law of Distinctiveness is evident through the unique characteristics that differentiate IShowSpeed from
other content creators. Analysis of his content reveals a recurring pattern of unpredictable actions, unconventional
challenges, exaggerated reactions, and spontaneous interactions with people around him. These characteristics
distinguish his content from creators who rely on structured production, scripted performances, or highly curated
visual aesthetics. His willingness to engage in unusual situations and take unexpected actions creates anticipation
among viewers, who often watch his broadcasts to experience surprise and entertainment. Consequently,
distinctiveness is a major factor contributing to audience recognition and memorability.
The Law of Visibility is reflected in the frequency and consistency of IShowSpeed's online presence. The
findings show that he maintains active engagement across multiple social media platforms, including YouTube,
Instagram, TikTok, and other digital channels. His content is distributed regularly through livestreams, short-form

The final dimension, the Law of Goodwill, emerges as the most complex aspect of IShowSpeed's personal
branding. The findings reveal the coexistence of two contrasting audience perceptions. On one hand, some public
audiences criticize his behavior and consider certain actions controversial. On the other hand, his core fan
community remains highly supportive and actively participates in his broadcasts and online activities. Evidence
of goodwill can be observed through frequent interactions with followers, expressions of appreciation toward fans,
and the strong sense of community surrounding his content. These findings suggest that goodwill in digital
environments may not necessarily require universal approval. Instead, maintaining trust, loyalty, and positive
relationships within a target audience segment can be sufficient to sustain a strong personal brand.
Based on the structured content audit and the analysis of the Eight Laws of Personal Branding, the main
findings of this study can be summarized as follows:
1. All eight dimensions of Montoya and Vandehey's personal branding framework are present in
IshowSpeed's digital activities.
2. Personality, Visibility, Distinctiveness, and Persistence are the most dominant dimensions in his
personal branding.
3. Visibility is strengthened through multi-platform activity, viral content circulation, international
livestreams, collaborations, and public appearances.
4. Personality and authenticity contribute significantly to audience engagement because audiences
recognize IShowSpeed through spontaneous and emotional performance.
5. Goodwill is segmented. Criticism from the wider public coexists with strong loyalty from his fan
community.
6. Controversy increases visibility but also creates reputational risk, especially for platforms, brands,
and professional collaborations.

Discussion
The results show that IShowSpeed's personal branding is not built solely on a polished image. His brand grows
from repeated exposure, spontaneous performance, emotional expression, and continuous interaction with
audiences. This is important because digital personal branding is shaped not only by what a creator says about
themselves, but also by what audiences repeatedly see, discuss, share, and reinterpret across platforms. In this
sense, IShowSpeed's personal brand is formed through many digital touchpoints, including livestreams, short-
form clips, news coverage, fan responses, and public controversies.
Personality and Distinctiveness become the strongest dimensions, because IShowSpeed's audience not only
watches the activity in his content. They also watch his reactions. His loud voice, sudden emotional shifts, jokes,
anger, surprise, and physical gestures make the content recognizable even when the format changes from gaming
to travel, football, or public interaction. This explains why his personal brand remains consistent despite the
variety of content he produces. This finding is consistent with Montoya and Vandehey's idea that a strong personal
brand must be both recognizable and distinctive.
Visibility also plays a central role. The data show that IshowSpeed's visibility is strengthened by repeated
appearances across platforms and countries. His livestream in Indonesia attracted significant public attention and
reportedly reached more than 17 million viewers (Fimela, 2024). His China livestream episodes also generated

However, visibility also creates risk. The more visible IShowSpeed becomes, the more likely his actions are

The Law of Goodwill is the most complex dimension in this study. IShowSpeed does not always receive

closeness with the audience, repeated interaction, and fan loyalty.
This condition can be explained through parasocial relationships. Audiences who repeatedly watch livestreams

and entertaining persona they already expect from him.
However, the findings should not be read as proof that controversy is a safe branding strategy. Controversy

collaborations. IShowSpeed's case shows that a digital personal brand may survive controversy when supported
by a loyal community, but its long-term sustainability still depends on the creator's ability to balance authenticity,
entertainment value, and reputational control.

CONCLUSION
Based on the analysis, IShowSpeed’s personal branding strongly reflects Montoya’s Eight Laws of Personal
Branding. He successfully applies key principles such as specialization, leadership, authenticity, visibility, and
consistency across multiple platforms. His unique personality and strong engagement with audiences have
contributed significantly to his global popularity. Although Montoya emphasizes the importance of maintaining
a positive reputation, IShowSpeed demonstrates that strong personal branding can survive controversy when
supported by a loyal fan community. In the digital media era, audience loyalty can become a stronger foundation
for branding than general public opinion.
Overall, this study confirms that Montoya’s personal branding theory remains relevant in understanding the
success of digital influencers. The findings also suggest that personal branding on social media should focus not
only on building a positive image, but also on maintaining authentic connections with specific audience
communities. Furthermore, this study provides opportunities for future research into the relationship between
community loyalty, influencer culture, and personal branding in the digital era.
The structured content audit also shows that Personality, Visibility, Distinctiveness, and Persistence are the
most dominant dimensions in IShowSpeed's personal branding. Meanwhile, Goodwill appears as the most
complex dimension because it operates mainly within his loyal fan community rather than through universal public
approval.
