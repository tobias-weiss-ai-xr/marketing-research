Abstract
This major objective of the study is to distinguish the impact of (1) the customer-based brand equity (CBBE) on brand
satisfaction (BS); (2) BS on brand trust (BT) and brand loyalty (BL); and (3) BT on BL. The examination of CBBE
employed a survey for 286 retailers of building material in Jakarta, Indonesia. The research objective is to scrutinise
the CBBE antecedents’ influence on BT and BL in the building material industry. The investigation of the study
employed Structural Equation Modelling (SEM), AMOS version 26. The findings of the study exhibited that CBBE’s
antecedents - brand awareness (BAW), brand identification (BI) , physical quality (PQ), staff behaviour (SB), lifestyle
congruence (LC), and ideal self -congruence (IS) directly and significantly i nfluenced BI, while BS directly and
significantly influenced BT and BL. In addition, BT significantly influences BL. The principal contribution of this study
is to improve that the measurement of CBBE affecting BS, BT, and BL for building material retailer. Nevertheless, no
literature assesses the correlation of the CBBE, BS, BT as well as BL in the building material industry.
Keywords: customer-based brand equity (CBBE), brand satisfaction, brand trust, brand loyalty, building material industry

1. Introduction
One of the core industries formed in Indonesia is the building
material sector. Small -scale family businesses dominate the
majority nationwide. Customers have a extensive variety of
choices when selecting building material stores in Indonesia, as
there are many of them. In the context of retail which facing the
fierce competition, a strong brand must be developed. Strong
brand development contributed value to CBBE (Jiseon, Jung
Kun, & Hyowon, 2018). Customers' views were used to measure
CBBE in the brand eq uity measurement associated with the
financial outcome measurement (Chatterjee, 2018). The main
goals of CBBE is to measure brand equity (BE) from the
standpoint of services marketing (Pike & Bianchi, 2013).
Therefore, assessing the CBBE from the perspecti ve of the
consumer will reveal a profound comprehension of how brands
affect brand satisfaction and loyalty. Secondly, the objective of
the this study was CBBE in order to measure BL and BS.
Brand equity is primarily measured based on consumers'
perceptions, hence taking CBBE ideas into account is essential
(Elliot, Percy, & Pervan, 2018). Different models with distinct
brand equity components have been proposed since the
emergence of CBBE concepts over the past thirty years.
Therefore, Aaker (1991) presented the first brand equity model,
which included five elements of BE, PQ, BL, BAW, brand
associations (BAS), and other intellectual assets.  Furthermore,
Keller clarified that the CBBE concept takes effect as soon as
consumers become familiar with the bran ding and adopt an
alternative of strong, distinct, and favourable opinions about it
(Keller, 1993). These ideas recognize BAW and brand
knowledge (BK) as the components of BE. The other researcher
defined the components of BE as PQ, BAW and BL (Yoo &
Donthu, 2001). In addition, SB, PQ, IS, BI, and LC are disclosed
by the CBBE model substitute (Nam, Ekinci, & Whaytt, 2011).
Moreover, BE denotes to the advantage that customers
characteristic to a brand based on their assumptions of its worth,
service, quality, and items. Several factors provide to a brand's
engagement and impact on satisfying customer (Torres,
Parraga, & Zamora, 2008). Furthermore, customers'
contentment with their selected brand and its ability to live up to
their expectations can be used to determine brand satisfaction
(Cuong, 2020). Accordingly, the construction material retailing
industry's core component for measuring customer relationships
is brand trust, according to today's researchers. These
phenomenon are relevant with the findings of  the earlier
research, which described BT as a mediating variable to quantify
BE and brand-customer connection (Alam & Yasin, 2010). BT is
then acknowledged as a crucial component that fosters BL. It
implies that a devoted client will assess the overall BE, BT, BL,
and contentment in order to minimize risk. In light of this, it is
crucial to examine the relationship between CBBE, BS, BT, and
BL in the building material retail sector (Tung &
Suthinoparatanakul, 2019).
Many studies have investigated the assoc iation among BS
and BL, but none have looked into the impact of the CBBE on
these three variables. The prior study examined the significance
of CBBE in relation to the following industries: automotive
(Adetunji, Rashid, & Ishak, 2018); fashion retail (Çifc i, et al.,
2016); hospitality (Nam, Ekinci, & Whaytt, 2011); insurance
(Chatterjee, 2018); tourism (Saeed & Shafique, 2020);

2. Literature Review
BE is seen as the most important concept for both business
and academic research. Furthermore, the majority of industries
can strengthen their competitive edge through the use of
powerful bra nds. Additionally, good brand equity can be
produced by customers' insights of the features, quality, cost,
and level of service of the product (Singh, Singh, & Islam, 2017).
Through integrated marketing initiatives, it can be reflected in
the attitudes, b ehaviours, and ideas of the customer. These
facts suggest that brand equity is a crucial intangible asset that
adds to the financial and psychological worth of the company.
As stated by Aaker, brand equity encompasses both brand
liabilities and assets that  yield incremental value for the
customer (Aaker D. , 2009). CBBE is a subset of BE that
includes PQ, BAW, BA, BL, and other intellectual assets like a
patent, distribution channel, and trademark (Aaker D. , 2009).
Consequently, CBBE was the main focus of this research.

2.1 Customer-based brand equity (CBBE)
More than thirty years, CBBE model measures and
dimensions have increased (Raji, et al., 2020). PQ, BAW, BL,
and BA are the CBBE dimensions that are most frequently
employed (Aaker D. A., 1991). Next, it is determined that the
brand knowledge dimensions of brand image and brand
awareness affect how consumers react to a company's
marketing (Keller, 1993). The other researcher combined brand
knowledge and brand personality as a CBBE construct (Pappu,
2005). Furthermore, the formation related to of customers'
views, mindsets, associations, and recollections towards a
brand is often the emergence of brand image (BIM) and BAW
(Christodoulides & Chernatony, 2010). The main goals of CBBE
measurement are to stre ngthen financial consequences,
determine the effectiveness of integrative marketing actions,
increase brand power, and improve brand effectiveness.
Consequently, it may be claimed that the most crucial objective
for researchers and practitioners alike is t o measure CBBE
(Baalbaki & Guzman, 2016).
Additionally, the direct and indirect technique is the primary
method used to measure CBBE (Christodoulides G. ,
Chernatony, Furrer, & Shiu, 2006). Therefore, the indirect
method discusses the core of building bra nds based on
consumer behaviour and responses, the direct approach
demonstrates how integrative marketing communication is
implemented (Adetunji, Rashid, & Ishak, 2018). Numerous
indicators, including BIM, PQ, BAW, brand sustainability (BSU),
and BAS, can be used to measure the responses of customers
(Schivinski & Dabrowski, 2015). There is no conclusion to
measure CBBE using a similar model, despite the fact that the
majority of measurement models created are relied on the
theoretical models developed by Aaker and Keller (Keller, 1993).
Yoo and Donthu proposed the CBBE model, which centred
on goods dominant logic, based on industry suitability (Yoo &
Donthu, 2001). The fashion industry (Çifci, et al., 2016), food
industry (Vukasovic, 2016), cosmetics indust ry (Heinberg,
Ozkaya, & Taube, 2018), automotive industry (Adetunji, Rashid,
& Ishak, 2018); (Langga, Kusumawati, & Alhabsji, 2020), IT
industry (Tran, VO, & Dinh, 2020), and retail industry
(Dananjoyo & Udin, 2023) were the main topics of discussion
during the CBBE discussion. As a result, CBBE model
concentrated on service -dominant logic. Numerous research,
including those on the hotel business (Nam, Ekinci, & Whaytt,
2011), bank industry (Myint & Kohsuwan, 2019), insurance
industry (Chatterjee, 2018), tourism industry (Tasci, 2020), and
marketplace industry (Kodua, Blankson, Panda, & Nguyen,
2022), have examined the concept of service dominating logic.
The construction material industry does not mention CBBE.
Green building, durability (Chouhan, Kalla, Na gar, & Gautam,
2019), distribution channel (Wang, Nguyen, & Dang, 2020),
financial consideration (Dananjoyo, Ghaffarianhoseini, Hosam,
& Riyadh, 2020), material properties (Koh & Kraniotis, 2020),
and refurbishment (Okakpu, Ghaffarianhoseini, Tookey, & Haar,
2020). These are the main topics of discussion regarding the
building material industry. Consequently, this study helps to
close the information gap about brand equity. Next, the CBBE
model as presented by Çifci  is being used in this investigation
(Çifci, et al., 2016). These models are employed to look at how
customers see the construction material retailer's customer
service. There is no study has looked into service quality (SQ)
in the building material sector. It might be this study's primary
addition to our understanding of the building materials sector
(Dananjoyo, 2018).

2.2 Brand awareness
The capability of a prospective consumer to identify and
recall a specific brand is related with a specific category of
product is known as BAW (Aaker D. A., 1991). Therefore, BAW
is considered as the ultimate aspect affecting customers
perceptions of a brand (Ukpebor & Ipogah, 2008). Brand recall
(BRE) and brand recognition (BRC) are fundamental aspects of
BAW (Keller, 1993). BRE is the process of a customer retrieving
a brand from their memory, whereas BRC is used to measure
the customer's capacity to validate a prior interaction with the
brand (Sürücü, Öztürk, Okumus, & Bilgihan, 2019).
Furthermore, increased brand acceptance and awareness are
signs of a healt hy demand for a certain brand (Seetharaman,
Mohd Nadzir, & Gunalan, 2001). Brand equity is the first factor
that should be considered while building great brand equity (Buil
& Martinez, 2013). Then, it suggests that a dominating brand is
created by a sophi sticated level of BAW (Gutiérrez, Herrero -
Crespo, & De los Salmones, 2018).
Furthermore, BAW is the primary element that boosts
customer satisfaction (CS), which in turn strengthens BL (Aaker
D. , 1992). Then, it is known that BS and BAW have a positive
connection (Nam, Ekinci, & Whaytt, 2011). The previous study
posited a favourable correlation between BS and BAW within the

2.3 Brand identification
When acquiring a product or service, the main activity of
customers is to detect their perceptions and approaches from
any organisation or people. Therefore, it indicate social identity
theory, stating that recognition can be described as a viewpoint
of interrelation among the people and the corporations (Chou,
2013). In additions, BI is defined as customers’ assumptions of
attributes that effect their responsibility and significant
association toward the product and service. The main variable
used to explain BI are cognitive, evaluative, and emotional (He,
Chen, Wang, & Lee, 2017).
Furthermore, the previous research argued that customers’
participation in character behaviours in the community
influenced individual identity (Schau, Muniz Jr, & Arnould, 2009).
The central concept of this BI is to clarify that individuals are
prepared to differentiate it from the other concepts (Papista &
Dimitriadis, 2012). Then, BI influenced BS by empowering the
observed achievement and designing an touching attachment to
the brand (Zhou, Zhang, Su, & Zhou, 2012) . It means that brand
satisfaction takes place at the time brand performance meets or
is beyond expectations (Yi & La, 2004). In this research’s
context, BI is recognized through an intense association among
customers and retailers. Finally, the examin ations have
stimulated the recent researchers to offer this hypothesis:
Hypothesis 2. There is a positive connection between BI and
BS in the building material retailer industry.

2.4 Physical quality
According to their facilities, furnishings, supplies, and
designs, the physical quality represents the retailers' brand
(Nam, Ekinci, & Whyatt, 2011). Extrinsic and intrinsic qualities
are the two aspects that determine perceived quality (Zeithaml,
1988). According to previous research, the former discussion
focuses to the product's non -physical attributes like pricing,
branding, servicing, and packaging. Then the latter is tied to the
physical attributes like form, colour, appearance, and essence.
In addit ions, customers' assumptions of quality are primarily
derived from their prior use of the goods and services. However,
customers assumptions of quality are influenced by brand name,
price, and advertising in addition to prior experiences (Dodds,
2002). As a result, according to Aaker and McLoughlin, the
factors that determine perceived quality are as follows: goods’
requirement, achievement, attractive strategy, accessibility,
customer assistance, and requirement (Aaker & McLoughlin,
2010). Product quality is considered as a crucial component of
the mindset of building material retailers during the purchasing
decisions process.
Previous study has noted and founded a positive connexion
among BS and PQ. Both were thought to have a favourable
impact on patron satisfaction in the hotel sector. Furthermore, a
positive connections was observed among PQ and CS in the
hotel and restaurant sector, indicating that physical quality is a
crucial factor of CBBE (Nam, Ekinci, & Whaytt, 2011). The
recent study found that in the retail sector, physical quality has
a major impact on brand satisfaction (Çifci, et al., 2016). Next,
this research put out this theory:
Hypothesis 3. There is a positive connection between PQ
and BS in the building material retailer industry.

2.5 Staff behaviour
The material characteristics in combination with the retailers'
facilities, tools, supplies, and workforce convey their brand. In
essence, employee behaviour at a retailer is influenced by their
competency, hospitality, responsiveness, a nd service.
Accordingly, the success of brand equity depends on
employees' capacity to satisfy customers' expectations and
strengthen their bonds with them (Sürücü, Öztürk, Okumus, &
Bilgihan, 2019). The earlier study has indicated that the
development of positive employee performance has a main
effect on retailers' satisfaction (Dananjoyo, Ghaffarianhoseini,
Hosam, & Riyadh, 2020). Related to those earlier research, staff
behaviour had a favourable effect on consumer satisfaction. The
recent research intentions to examine the correlation among the
BS of building material retailers and the behaviour of their staff.
To that end, the study hypothesis is as follows:
Hypothesis 4. There is a positive connection between SB
and BS in the building material retailer industry.

2.6 Lifestyle-congruence
Customers who buy retail products typically do so based on
their perceptions of and beliefs about certain brands. Customers
will feel more positively about a retailer if their perceptions
toward the retailer align be autifully. This phenomenon can be
defined as the self -congruence notion. Thus, there is a
fundamental impact of self -congruence BS and BL (He &
Mukherjee, 2007). Additionally, consumers research the
advantages of the product in light of their self -congruence,
including their identities, habits, values, and way of life
(Kressmann, Sirgy, Hermann, & Huber, 2006). Here, customers'
attitudes, beliefs, and goals are linked to their lifestyle, which is
regarded as a crucial aspect of their everyday existence
(Solomon, Dahl, White, Zaichkowsky, & Polegato, 2017). In the
context of brand performance, LC considered as the customers’
preference based on their beliefs, curiosities, and action (Nam,
Ekinci, & Whaytt, 2011). Therefore, LC has also been
deliberated as a s ign of brand attributes (Çifci, et al., 2016).
Accordingly, consumer satisfaction in the oral sector industry
was positively and considerably impacted by lifestyle (Kataria &
Saini, 2019). Furthermore, in the retail sector, customer
satisfaction was simila rly impacted by LC (Susanty & Kenny,
2015). Based on the recent phenomena, the proposed
hypothesis can be defined as follows:
Hypothesis 5. There is a positive connection between LC
and BS in the building material retailer industry.

2.8 Brand satisfaction
Customers' assessment, which demonstrate that the brand
can live up to their expectations and that they are happy with the
brands they have selected, are what are known as brand
satisfaction results (Cuong, 2020). According to a prior study,
brand satisfaction affected consumers' perceptions of and

Furthermore, previous studies have covered the connection
between BI and BT in the  retail sector, the presence of BI
facilitated the growth of BT (Chinomona R. , 2013). In addition,

strong relationship among BL and BS (Jamshidi & Rousta,

based on previous empirical studies:

2.9 Brand trust
In order to develop BT, there is a vigorous belief in a certain
brand, service, and product (Park & Kim, 2016). Customers will
hence likely purchase a good or service from a reputable
company. The increase in BT has a beneficial effect on
customers' confidence and BL. Hence, BT has a positive
correlation with v alue, differentiation, perceived quality, and
satisfaction in addition to being favourably correlated with BL
(Thompson, Newman, & Liu, 2014). According to Atulkar's 2020
research, there was a considerable impact on brand loyalty
when a high -quality brand was suggested by brand trust
(Atulkar, 2020). Consequently, the researchers has intention to
develop the hypothesis as follows:
Hypothesis 9. There is a positive connection between BT
and BL in the building material retailer industry.

6. Conclusion
This study's goal was to quantify BL and trust using
satisfaction as a proxy for the CBBE aspects (BAW, BI, SB, LC,
IS, and PQ). The findings of the study indicate that BAW has a
favourable impact on BS, which is consistent with a prior study
by Bilal and Malik (Bilal & Malik, 2014). Thus, this study validated
an earlier study by Dananjoyo et al. that stated staff behaviour
positively increases BS (Dananjoyo, Ghaffarianhoseini, Hosam,
& Riyadh, 2020). Then, this study discovered that CS in the retail
sector was impacted by LC. Furthermore, the study's findings
show a favourable correlation between BS and ideal self -
congruence. It was in line with a previous investigation by
previous study (Feiz & Moradi, 2020). Additionally, client BL and
BT are strongly influenced by BS. It was confirming earlier
research findings (Jamshidi & Rousta, 2020). Finally, this study
establishes a favourable correlation between BL and BT. It
provided support for earlier research that was started by Atulkar
(Atulkar, 2020).
The main contribution of the article to the knowledge is by
endorsing and validating a model that assessed the connection
among CBBE, BS, and BT and BL. This article mostly
contributes three important things. Initially, the research verified
that the presence of contentment on the brand was directly
caused by CBBE antecedents. According to the study, trust and
loyalty to the brand were indirectly caused by CBBE
antecedents. Third, the present study affirms that BS was a
direct cause of the rise in BL and BS. The function of BS, such
as consumers' delight in influencing customers' trust and loyalty,
is supported by this article's hypothesis. In addition, the findings
of the study are to motivate the businesses and improve a
workable strategy for producing BS, which immediately starts to
foster BL and BS.
This research has limits, despite the fact that it makes a
considerable contribution to BE in the retail industries. Firstly,
the findings of study carried out in Indonesia as a devel oping
country. It is important to give further thought to how the results
of this study compare to those of industrialized countries.
Secondly, this conceptual framework should be tested in future
research using different methods, namely mixed method.
Hopefully, by combining qualitative and quantitative approach,
the outcome of the study would be able to acquire different
perceptions. Last but not least, enhancing the conceptual
framework with measures of BI and BL will improve the
contribution to the knowledge and the business world.
