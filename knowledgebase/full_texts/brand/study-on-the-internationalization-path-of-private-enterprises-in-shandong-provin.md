  Abstract
Against the background of global value chain restructuring and domestic industrial
upgrading, resource constraints have become the core obstacle restricting Chinese
private enterprises from moving from product export to brand internationalization.
Drawing on the OLI paradigm an d the LLL model, this study takes private enter-
prises in Shandong Province as the research subject, builds a “motivation –behav-
ior–capability” analysis framework , and verifies the framework through a case
study of Haier Group’s internationalization practice. The study finds that, through
market-seeking cross-border mergers and acquisitions (M&A), Haier has system-
atically opened up a progressive path of “connection –leverage–learning”: through
M&A, Haier first established overseas channel and brand connections. It then lev-
eraged synergistic effects through management output and channel reuse. Ulti-
mately, it upgraded its capabilities through the internalization of tacit knowledge,
thereby completing the leap from OEM export to a global brand. However, Haier’s
“leapfrog” model depends heavily on its ownership advantage and financing capa-
bility, which makes it difficult to directly replicate among the broader population
of small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs). Accordingly, this study proposes a
tiered approach: leading enterprises can draw on Haier’s experience to carry out
strategic-asset M&A, whereas small and medium -sized private enterprises should
prioritize leveraging cluster ecosystems and platform channels to expand overseas
in a gradual manner and accumulate specialized advantages in niche segments. The
thesis enriches the application of the LLL model in the context of late -developing
enterprises and provides a theoretical basis for Shandong Province to formulate dif-
ferentiated policies supporting the internationalization of private enterprises.

1. Introduction
In the context of profound adjustments in economic globalization, internationalization has become an inevitable
choice for Chinese enterprises to break through domestic demand bottlenecks and acquire global factors. As a major
economic province, Shandong Province hosts a large number of private enterprises, yet these firms generally face the
dilemma of being “unwilling to go global, daring not to go global, and not knowing how to go global ” in the course
of their internationalization. This predicament is exemplified by the fact that over 80% of traditional manufacturing

Existing research mostly examines the motivations and obstacles to the internationalization of Chinese enterprises

insufficient. In view of this, this thesis takes Shandong Province as its regional focus and Haier Group as a case study,
a benchmark enterprise that has successfully achieved globalization. It then seeks to answer the following question:
as resource-constrained late-movers, how can private enterprises in Shandong learn from the experience of a leading
local firm to choose and follow an internationalization path that suits them? The thesis aims to fill the gap in micro -
path research on regional private enterprises.
2. Theoretical Framework: Motivational Explanation and Process Mechanism
Among the classic theories explaining firm internationalization, Dunning’s (1980) OLI (Ownership, Location, Inter-
nalization) paradigm answers why firms can invest overseas: they need to possess ownership advantages, locational
advantages, and internalization advantages at the same time. For Chinese late- developing firms, however, the moti-
vation for internationalization is often not to exploit existing advantages but to make up for gaps in brands and tech-
nology, showing a strong market-seeking and strategic-asset-seeking orientation.
However, the OLI paradigm cannot fully explain how firms internationalize under resource disadvantages. The
LLL (Linkage-Leverage-Learning) model proposed by Mathews (2006) fills this gap. According to this model, late-
comers can connect with external resou rces, use organizational flexibility to unlock these resources , and finally in-
ternalize external knowledge into core competitiveness through learning.
The OLI and LLL models are complementary rather than alternatives: the former reveals the starting point and
motivation of internationalization, while the latter describes the dynamic process of breaking through resource con-
straints. On this basis, this th esis builds an analytical framework: the OLI paradigm is used to analyze the initial
conditions and strategic motives of firm internationalization, while the LLL model is used to track its “linkage-lev-
erage-learning” evolution mechanism. This allows for a systematic examination of the internationalization paths of
Haier and Shandong’s private enterprises.
3. Realistic Constraints and Theoretical Attribution for the Internationalization of Private En-
terprises in Shandong Province
The overall lag in the internationalization of private enterprises in Shandong is not caused by a single factor but by
the combination of multiple resource constraints. Based on the theoretical framework above, these constraints can be
clearly attributed to the absence of OLI advantages and the breakdown of the LLL cycle.
First, the ownership advantage is weak. Manufacturing in Shandong is mostly concentrated in traditional fields
such as chemicals, machinery, and agricultural product processing. Most enterprises mainly engage in contract man-
ufacturing or the production of intermediate goods, and lack independent brands and international marketing experi-
ence (Chen, 2025), which confines them to the bottom of the “smile curve” in the global value chain, leaving them
unable to obtain a brand premium; that is, lacking the key “O” advantage.
Second, the ability to identify and use locational advantages is insufficient. Most private enterprises in Shandong
have only a limited understanding of the legal, cultural, and business environments of overseas markets (Wang, 2026).
Faced with complex institutional differences, they are often discouraged by high search costs and transaction risks;
even where locational advantages exist in principle, these enterprises struggle to make effective use of them.
Thirdly, the internalization advantage hasn ’t been established. Narrow financing channels, a shortage of interna-
tional talent, and weak internal management capabilities prevent companies from building efficient operating entities
overseas (Chen, 2025). The case of Yuncheng County shows that, without  a professional foreign trade team, enter-
prises cannot internalize their domestic manufacturing advantages into competitive advantages in overseas markets.
As a result, they can only rely on intermediaries, which squeezes their profits.
From the perspective of the LLL model, the difficulty of Shandong private enterprises lies in the fact that their
internationalization cycle hasn’t started. Because they lack initial linkage channels (such as overseas customers and
partners), they can’t obtain the resources needed to leverage advantages, let alone learn and build capabilities in later
steps. Therefore, they fall into a vicious cycle of “ low-end exports-low profits-no capacity to invest in brands -con-
tinuing low-end exports”.

4. Empirical Analysis of Haier’s Internationalization Path
As a model of manufacturing internationalization in Shandong and even China, Haier ’s success wasn ’t achieved
overnight. In fact, it’s a dynamic evolution that precisely followed and expanded the logic of “ connection-leverage-
learning”. The value of Haier’s experience lies in its systematic solution of the common constraints that Shandong’s
private enterprises face, as discussed above.
Breaking the “zero connection”: strategically acquiring external resources. Although Haier had manufacturing and
quality management advantages (ownership advantages) at home in its early stage, it lacked brand awareness and
channel support in developed markets in Europe and America. As  a result, its locational advantages could not be
realized. At that point, Haier’s motivation for internationalization had shifted from the earlier goal of earning foreign
exchange through exports to a strategic search for markets and for brands.
Haier’s breakthrough was to build high -level connections through cross -border acquisitions. In 2011, it bought
Sanyo’s white-goods business in Japan and gained mature channels for entering the Southeast Asian market; in 2012,
it acquired Fisher & Paykel of New Zealand and obtained experience in operating high -end brands as well as R&D
resources. In 2016, it further acquired GE Home Appliances for USD 5.58 billion, directly plugging into the main-
stream channel system of the North American market. On the whole, these acquisitions were a typical move of trading
capital for time and resources for market access. They broke the “zero connection” between Haier and overseas end
markets and laid the foundation for the leveraged operations that followed.
Unleashing “leverage”: resource restructuring and synergy. Establishing connections is only the first step; the key
is how to leverage and amplify these acquired external resources. Haier’s leverage effect shows itself in three aspects:
First, channel leverage. Through GE Home Appliances’ mature network, Haier’s own-brand products quickly en-
tered mainstream US stores, combining the cost-performance advantage of Chinese manufacturing with the market-
coverage advantage of US channels to achieve rapid market expansion.
Second, management leverage. Haier didn’t merely accept the existing systems of the acquired companies; instead,
it actively proposed the “RenDanHeYi” management model to its overseas subsidiaries. By using digital platforms
(such as the Uhome platform) to coordinate the global supply chain, it shortened its domestic order -response cycle
by 13%. In this way, it used its own management strengths to stren gthen the overseas assets. Chinese private enter-
prises, by relying on their industrial chain advantages and governance mechanism advantages, have shown stronger
adaptability and market competitiveness during internationalization (Yang, 2025). It is able to break through cross -
border business barriers with digital tools, quickly adapt to the market rules and su pply chain environments of dif-
ferent countries, and thereby improve overall
Third, financial leverage. The successful mergers and acquisitions (M&A) brought steady growth in revenue and
profit (overseas revenue of RMB 143.814 billion in 2024) and improved cash flow and the asset -liability structure.
This formed a cycle of “ M&A-integration-profitability-refinancing” and created ample financial room for the com-
pany’s follow-up strategic planning (Lu et al., 2025). It is precisely this that many companies lack after falling into
trouble through excessive debt and failed integration.
Achieving “true learning”: internalizing tacit knowledge and upgrading capabilities. The sustainability of the lev-
erage effect depends on whether companies can turn the external knowledge obtained through M&A into their own
long-term innovation capability to achieve learning. Thro ugh internal knowledge transfer, outward foreign direct
investment (OFDI) can acquire more tacit knowledge, which belongs to experience, practical skills, and organiza-
tional culture that are difficult to codify (Wang et al., 2025) . What makes Haier distinctive is that it has built a two -
way knowledge flow mechanism.
On the one hand, through the “Overseas Thousand Talents Program,” it has sent many of its leading domestic staff
to overseas subsidiaries. This has created a legitimate spillover of management experience (Wang et al., 2025) and
ensured that Haier’s culture and management system take root overseas. On the other hand, it actively absorbs the
tacit knowledge of GE and Fisher & Paykel in product development, high- end manufacturing, and quality control
(for example, experience and skills that are hard to codify), and combines it with Haier’s own efficient supply chain.
In 2024, its R&D investment exceeded 11.2 billion yuan, and the coordinated setup of 10 global R&D centers con-
firms Haier’s commitment to turning external technical knowledge into continuous innovation capability. This deep
learning and knowledge transformation has ultimately shown itself in a steady rise in gross margin and the continuous
emergence of best-selling products, marking Haier’s shift in nature from a “global buyer” to a “global player.”

5. Conclusions and Implications
By integrating the OLI paradigm and the LLL model, this paper conducts an in-depth examination of Haier Group’s
internationalization process. The evidence shows that Haier’s success is not simply about scale expansion, but rather
a clear market-seeking, cross-border M&A-driven path. Starting from identifying advantages within the OLI frame-
work, and through the progressive mechanism of the LLL model, it systematically breaks through the resource con-
straints of latecomer enterprises. It ultimately achieves a leap from “Made in China” to “Global Brand.”
However, for the many private enterprises in Shandong Province, simply copying Haier ’s model is dangerous;
learning Haier’s logic is the key. Based on this, this thesis proposes differentiated implications at different levels:
Leading companies, on the premise of sound finances, can learn from Haier’s leapfrog-style M&A experience and
conduct strategic asset- seeking overseas M&A around brands, channels, and core technologies. The government
should provide support in areas such as M&A financing and legal consultation to help them grow into leading global
enterprises.
The vast majority of small and medium-sized private enterprises should follow a path of going global that combines
gradual and cluster-based approaches. When they don’t possess ownership advantages, they shouldn’t blindly make
large-scale overseas investments. The following options are recommended as priorities: First, enterprises can join the
overseas ecosystem built by leading companies and platform companies to reduce contact costs. For example, they
can carry out supply- chain supporting services by rely ing on the overseas parks or channel networks of companies
such as Haier. Second, they can deepen their work in specialized, refined, and innovative niche markets (Zhao & Cao,
2025), establish irreplaceable specialized advantages in specific niche markets through continuous product and tech-
nology iteration, and then gradually promote the internationalization of their own brands.
Governments and policymakers should abandon a “one-size-fits-all” approach. They should provide differentiated
and precise public services to private enterprises at different stages of development and in different industries. The
key points are as follows: First, regional overseas resource- sharing platforms (such as pub lic overseas warehouses
and joint marketing centers) should be built, so as to reduce the fixed costs for SMEs to go global independently.
Second, export credit insurance and exchange-rate risk hedging tools for SMEs should be improved, so as to alleviate
their financing constraints. Third, coordination mechanisms of industry chambers of commerce should be established,
so as to avoid vicious competition and internal resource consumption among enterprises of the province in overseas
markets.
In conclusion, Haier’s internationalization practices provide a valuable strategic reference for Shandong enterprises,
and their greater implication lies in the fact that breaking through resource constraints doesn’t have to rely solely on
large-scale M&A. The key is to find contact methods that suit their own endowments and internalize external re-
sources into irreplaceable capabilities through continuous learning. The internationalization of private enterprises in
Shandong Province should be a multi-level and multi-model collaborative exploration, rather than a blind following
of a single benchmark. Future research can further test the applicability boundary of the hierarchical insights proposed
in this thesis across different industries and enterprise sizes through large-sample empirical evidence.
