Influencer marketing involves using people with an established audience to communicate product-related messages. De Veirman, Cauberghe, and

Lou and Yuan (2019) studied how the value and credibility of branded influencer content relate to trust and purchase intention. Their work highlights

2.4 Expectation–Disconfirmation and Consumer Satisfaction
Expectation–disconfirmation theory, associated with Oliver (1980), explains satisfaction as a judgement formed by comparing prior expectations with
perceived performance. Expectations may be met, exceeded, or fall short after a consumer uses or receives a product. The comparison is important
becausecustomersdonotevaluateaproductinisolation;theyalsoconsiderwhattheythoughttheyweregoingtoreceive.
When a product performs as expected or better, the consumer may feel satisfied. When the experience falls below expectations, negative
disconfirmation can occur and dissatisfaction may follow. The size of the difference and the importance of the product feature can influence the
response. For example, a small shade difference may be acceptable to one customer, while an inaccurate fit or noticeably poor fabric may be more
seriousforanother.
In influencer-led fashion purchases, expectations may be shaped by styling videos, close-up shots, creator reviews, promotional captions, and
statements about quality. If the delivered clothing looks or feels substantially different, customers may judge the experience negatively. This theory
offersausefulbasisfortestingwhethertheperceivedinfluencer–realitygapisrelatedtosatisfactionand,indirectly,tolaterbrandchoices.
Thetheorythereforeprovidesasuitablefoundationforexaminingtherelationshipbetweentheinfluencer–realitygapandbrandloyalty.
2.5 Brand Trust and Brand Loyalty

customermaytrustabrand’squalitywhileoccasionallybuyingelsewhere,ormaycontinuebuyingoutofconvenienceevenwhentrustislimited.
Research on influencer credibility and fast-fashion brand loyalty has examined how the perceived credibility of an influencer can contribute to brand
trust and loyalty. For example, research published in the Journal of Fashion Marketing and Management in 2025 examined the relationship between
influencersourcecredibility,brandtrust,andfast-fashionbrandloyalty.
When promotional content gives an accurate picture of a garment, the customer’s experience may reinforce confidence in the brand. By contrast,
repeated mismatches can createdoubt about whether future productclaims should be believed. The outcome may vary accordingtothe seriousness of

6. Conceptual Background
The conceptual foundation of this study combines three perspectives: expectation–disconfirmation theory, source credibility theory, and the stimulus–
organism–responseframework.
6.1 Expectation–Disconfirmation Theory
Expectation–disconfirmationtheory explains howconsumers evaluate products bycomparing expected performance with actualexperience. When the
product meets or exceeds expectations, consumers may experience satisfaction. When the product falls below expectations, negative disconfirmation
mayleadtodissatisfaction.
In the context of fashion influencer marketing, promotional content can shape expectations before a consumer purchases clothing. If the delivered
product differs substantially from the promotional presentation, the resulting negative disconfirmation may reduce satisfaction and weaken the
consumer'srelationshipwiththebrand.
6.2 Source Credibility Theory
Source credibility theory emphasizes the importance of trustworthiness, expertise, and attractiveness in evaluating a communicator. Ohanian's (1990)
frameworkisparticularlyusefulforunderstandinghowconsumersevaluateinfluencers.
An influencer who provides realistic demonstrations, acknowledges product limitations, and communicates honestly may maintain credibility even
when a product has imperfections. Conversely, consistently exaggerated promotional claims may reduce perceived trustworthiness and influence
consumers'willingnesstorelyonfuturerecommendations.
6.3 Stimulus–Organism–Response Framework
The stimulus–organism–response (S-O-R) framework explains how external stimuli influence internal evaluations and subsequent behavioural
responses.
Inthisstudy:
 Stimulus:Theperceiveddifferencebetweeninfluencer-promotedclothingandtheactualproductreceived.
 Organism:Consumersatisfaction,brandtrust,andperceivedauthenticity.
 Response:Brandloyalty,repurchaseintention,andrecommendationbehaviour.
The framework suggests that consumers do not necessarily move directly from a product mismatch to disloyalty. Their reactions may depend on how
theyinterpretthemismatch,whethertheytrustthebrand,andhowsatisfiedtheyarewiththepurchase.
6.4 Brand Loyalty in Fashion Retail
Brand loyalty can include both attitudinal loyalty and behavioural intentions. Attitudinal loyalty refers to a consumer's preference and emotional
commitmenttoabrand,whilebehaviouralintentionsincludeplanstorepurchaseandrecommendit.
Aconsumermaybedisappointedwithoneclothingpurchasebutremainloyal becauseofpreviouspositiveexperiences,convenientreturns,orastrong
preferenceforthebrand.Alternatively,repeatednegativeexperiencesmayencourageswitchingtocompetingbrands.
Thestudytherefore treats brandloyaltyas anoutcomeinfluencedbymultiplefactors, rather thanassumingthatevery productmismatchautomatically
resultsinlostcustomers.

8. Practical and Theoretical Implications
8.1 Practical Implications for Fashion Brands
Fashion brands can reduce uncertainty by making promotional content consistent with the product that customers receive. Images and videos should
represent colour, fit, fabric, and design as accurately as reasonably possible. Detailed size charts, clear material descriptions, realistic demonstrations,
care instructions, and easy-to-understand return policies can help customers make informed decisions. Brands should also make sure that the product
informationsharedwithinfluencersisaccurateanduptodate.
Customer reviews and complaints can help brands identify recurring problems that advertising alone may not reveal. If buyers repeatedly mention
inconsistentsizing, thinfabric,colour differences, or poorstitching, these patterns should be investigatedas possible productor quality-control issues.
A response that only increases advertising may not solve the underlying problem. Addressing product quality and explaining how complaints are
handledcanhelpprotecttrustovertime.
8.2 Implications for Fashion Influencers
Influencers can maintain credibility by combining engaging content with practical and honest information. They can describe how a garment fits,
explain what the fabric feels like, show the item in different lighting where practical, and mention limitations that matter to buyers. Paid or gifted
partnerships should be disclosed clearly, and claims about quality should reflect genuine experience rather than assumptions. This approach does not
meaneveryreviewmustbenegative;itmeansgivingfollowersarealisticbasisfordecidingwhethertobuy.
