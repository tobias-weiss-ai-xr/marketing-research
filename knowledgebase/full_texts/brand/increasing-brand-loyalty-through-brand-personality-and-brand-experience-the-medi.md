 Background: Indonesia's expanding Muslim fashion market has
intensified competition between brands, increasing the importance of
retaining loyal customers. Previous research has produced inconsistent
findings on the relationship between brand experience and brand loyalty,
indicating that the psychological process connecting the two requires
further examination.
Objective: This study examines the effects of brand personality and brand
experience on brand loyalty and assesses the mediating role of brand trust
in the relationship between brand experience and brand loyalty among
Indonesian consumers of Muslim fashion branded garments.
Methods: The study used a quantitative approach with purposive
sampling techniques . Data from 148 consumers of Muslim fashion
branded garments were obtained through purposive sampling using a
five-point Likert-scale questionnaire and were analyzed with Structural
Equation Modeling (SEM) in AMOS.
Results: The results indicate that brand personality positively affects
brand loyalty (β = 0.632, p < 0.001). Brand experience also has positive
effects on brand loyalty (β = 0.324, p = 0.004) and brand trust (β = 0.444,
p < 0.001). In addition, brand trust positiv ely affects brand loyalty (β =
0.500, p < 0.001) and partially mediates the relationship between brand
experience and brand loyalty.
Conclusion: Brand loyalty is shaped by both brand personality and brand
experience, while brand trust explains part of the pathway through which
brand experience strengthens loyalty. In terms of Stimulus -Organism-
Response (SOR) Theory, findings show that brand -related stimuli can
contribute to loyalty through consumer trust. The study is limited to the
Indonesian context and examines brand trust as the sole mediator.

INTRODUCTION
Brand loyalty is an important indicator of how effectively an organization maintains
lasting relationships with consumers. Loyal consumers are more likely to make repeat purchases,
and this continuity can support business sustainability as market competiti on becomes more
intense. For this reason, maintaining loyalty is especially relevant to Indonesia's Muslim fashion
branded garment sector, where industry growth is accompanied by stronger competition among
brands.

As the Muslim fashion sector expands, retaining consumers and maintaining loyalty have
become increasingly strategic. According to the State of the Global Islamic Economy Report
(SGIER) 2024/2025, Indonesia remains among the largest Muslim consumer markets for modest
fashion products. In 2023, Muslim consumer spending across six major sectors reached USD 2.43
trillion and is projected to rise by 5.3% to USD 3.36 trillion by 2028. Data from the Central
Statistics Agency Statistik (2024) also indicate that the textile and apparel industry continues to
contribute to Indonesia's national processing industry. In addition, Indonesia's modest fashion
exports to member countries of the Organization of Islamic Cooperation (OIC) reached USD 990
million in 2023, up from USD 540 million in the previous year. Taken together, these
developments point to substantial potential in the Muslim Fashion Branded Garment market.
This market opportunity is accompanied by increasingly strong competition. In 2024, the
Indonesian Ministry of Industry recorded approximately 594 thousand small -scale apparel
enterprises employing roughly 1.2 million workers. As more businesses enter the market,
consumers gain wider choices in design, quality, price, and marketing concepts, which makes
brand loyalty more difficult to maintain. Challenges are also evident in consumer retention and
switching behavior. Khairullah et al. (2025a)  reported that approximately 40% of Muslim fashion
consumers switched brands within a 12-month period, while the Indonesia (2024) reported that
retention rates for branded Muslim fashion garments remained below 50%. These conditions
indicate that product attributes alone are insufficient and that companies need to understand the
factors and psychological processes that sustain brand loyalty.
The theoretical foundation of this study is the Stimulus -Organism-Response (SOR)
framework introduced by Mehrabian and Russell ( 1974) and Roessler et al. ( 2022). The SOR
framework proposes that environmental stimuli influence an individual's internal psychological
state before producing a behavioral  response. In this research, Brand Personality and Brand
Experience represent brand-related stimuli encountered by consumers during their interactions
with brands. Brand Trust is positioned as the organismic component because it reflects
consumers' internal psychological confidence in the brand, whereas Brand Loyalty represents the
behavioral response expressed through continued commitment to the brand. The framework
therefore provides a basis for explaining how external brand cues can shape loyalty through  an
internal psychological process. In applying SOR Theory to consumer -brand relationships, this
study places Brand Trust as the mechanism connecting brand-related experiences with loyalty.
Based on the market conditions, research gap, and theoretical perspective described
above, this study examines the effects of Brand Personality and Brand Experience on Brand
Loyalty among Indonesian consumers of Muslim Fashion Branded Garments. It also tests the effect
of Brand Experience on Brand Trust, the effect of Brand Trust on Brand Loyalty, and the mediating
role of Brand Trust in the relationship between Brand Experience and Brand Loyalty. The study
is expected to contribute theoretically by clarify ing the psychological pathway connecting brand
experience with loyalty within the SOR framework. From a practical perspective, the findings are
expected to guide Muslim fashion companies in developing experience -based differentiation
strategies that strengthen Brand Trust as a foundation for lasting Brand Loyalty.
This study uses the Stimulus -Organism-Response (SOR) framework introduced by
Mehrabian and Russell ( 1974) and Roessler et al. ( 2022) as its theoretical foundation. The
framework explains that stimuli from the surrounding environment first influence an individual's
internal or psychological state (the organism) and subsequently lead to a particular behavioral
response. In consumer behavior, brand loyalty refers to a continuing commitment to repurchase
and maintain a consistent preference for a particular brand even when alternative brands are
available (Dan Dey, 2022; Oliver, 1999).
Within this study, brand experience and brand personality are treated as stimuli that
shape consumer psychology, while brand trust represents the organism and brand loyalty
represents the response. Loyalty can therefore emerge through internal psychologica l processes
triggered by the stimuli consumers receive. Brand experience develops through the various
interactions and experiences consumers have with a brand (Hyun & Marsden, 2024 ). Those
experiences may influence consumers' internal psychological state in the form of brand trust,
which can then encourage a behavioral response expressed as brand loyalty. Accordingly, SOR
Theory is used to explain how brand experience and brand trust contribute to the formation of

Brand Personality is defined as the set of human characteristics associated with a brand
Aaker (1997) and Dobre et al. (2025). These human-like attributes allow consumers to perceive a
brand as having particular traits, values, and distinctive qualities (Dobre et al., 2025 ). A strong
brand personality can support emotional attachment, personal identification, and a long -term
relationship with the brand, which may ultimately encourage the development of brand loyalty.
According to Brakus et al. ( 2009) and Lesmana et al. ( 2023), brand experience includes
the sensory, affective, cognitive, and behavioral responses generated by brand -related stimuli
such as brand design, identity, packaging, marketing communication, and the commercial
environment. The concept extends beyond product  attributes to include the emotional
experiences and interactions associated with purchasing and consuming products (Lesmana et
al., 2023). Accordingly, brand experience develops across the various points of contact consumers
have with a brand, rather than only through product use.
According to Chaudhuri and Holbrook (2001) and Sang and Cuong (2025a), brand trust
reflects a customer's willingness to rely on a brand in pursuing expected outcomes. It captures the
psychological confidence consumers place in a brand's ability to meet their expectations
consistently (Selvakumar et al., 2025 ). This confidence is important in sustaining long -term
relationships between consumers and brands. As consumers become familiar with a product, they
may increasingly depend on the brand's competence to fulfill expectations, which can encourage
favorable attitudes and stronger trust (Ibrahim & Abd Ghani, 2025).
Brand loyalty refers to a strong consumer commitment to continue repurchasing or
resubscribing to a preferred product or service in the future despite situational influences or
competing marketing efforts that may encourage switching (Dan Dey, 2022 ; Oliver, 1999). It is
also regarded as a strategic organizational asset because it can support customer retention and
sustained profitability through consumer trust and satisfaction (Pal, 2025). Therefore, companies
need positive brand experiences, strong brand characteristics, and consumer trust to encourage
loyalty over time.
This study has both theoretical and practical implications. Theoretically, examining Brand
Trust as a mediator within the SOR framework contributes to understanding the psychological
process that connects external brand stimuli with consumer responses. Pra ctically, for Muslim
fashion companies operating in intense competition, understanding how positive brand
experiences are converted into trust and ultimately loyalty can guide differentiation strategies
that extend beyond product attributes alone.
Previous research has frequently linked Brand Personality, Brand Experience, and Brand
Trust with Brand Loyalty. Brand Personality refers to human -like characteristics associated with
a brand and can strengthen consumers' emotional attachment to it (Dobre et al., 2025 ). Brand
Experience covers sensory, affective, cognitive, and behavioral responses that arise when
consumers interact with a brand (Brakus et al., 2009; Sang & Cuong, 2025a). Brand Trust, in turn,
reflects consumers' willingness to rely on a brand and their belief that it will fulfill its
commitments (Chaudhuri & Holbrook, 2001 ; Sang & Cuong, 2025a ). Conceptually, these
constructs help explain how enduring consumer -brand relationships are developed. However,
empirical findings do not consistently show that Brand Experience is directly translated into
Brand Loyalty. Jabar et al. (2026) found no significant direct effect of Brand Experience on Brand
Loyalty, whereas Khairullah et al. ( 2025a)  reported a positive relationship. This difference
suggests that an underlying psychological mechanism may help explain how Brand Experience
contributes to Brand Loyalty.
Brand Trust offers a theoretically relevant explanation for this inconsistency. Favorable
experiences with a brand can strengthen consumers' confidence in its reliability and
trustworthiness, and this confidence may encourage them to continue their relationship with the
brand. Prior studies have reported positive associations between Brand Exp erience and Brand
Trust as well as between Brand Trust and Brand Loyalty (Khairullah et al., 2025b; Leoparjo et al.,
2025; Sang & Cuong, 2025b). However, these relationships have often been examined separately
or in different sectoral contexts. Among Indonesian consumers of branded Muslim fashion
garments, the process through which Brand Experience influences Brand Loyalty through Brand
Trust therefore remains insufficiently examined. The research gap addressed here concerns not
merely the inclusion of Brand Trust, but a clearer explanation of the psychological process

To address this gap, this study examines Brand Trust as a mediating variable between
Brand Experience and Brand Loyalty, while Brand Personality is examined as a direct predictor of

influence Brand Loyalty through the development of Brand Trust. The study contributes to the
literature by empirically examining the mediating role of Brand Trust in the context of Indonesian
consumers of branded Muslim fashion garments. Rat her than simply introducing an additional
variable, this study investigates how Brand Trust helps explain the relationship between Brand
Experience and Brand Loyalty.

Research Hypothesis
The Influence of Brand Personality on Brand Loyalty
Brand personality is expected to contribute to stronger brand loyalty. Kusumadewi et al.
(2025) found that brand personality has a positive and statistically significant influence on brand
loyalty in products. Chariri et al. (2025) likewise show that consumers are more likely to maintain
relationships with brands whose attributes are attractive, superior, and consistent with their
preferences. Lu et al. ( 2025) reported that distinctive brand characteristics can reinforce the
connection between consumers and brands and consequently increase loyalty. Kochhar et al.
(2025) also explained that brand personality contributes to brand loyalty through the relationship
developed between consumers and brands. Taken together, these findings suggest that stronger
brand personality is associated with higher consumer brand loyalty. Based on these findings, the
following hypothesis is proposed:
H1: There is a positive influence of Brand Personality on Brand Loyalty.

The Influence of Brand Experience on Brand Loyalty
Brand experience is also expected to strengthen brand loyalty because favorable
experiences can improve consumers' perceptions of a brand and reinforce their commitment to it
(Jibran et al., 2025 ). Prastiwi et al. ( 2025) found that brand experience has a positive and
significant effect on brand loyalty through the development of emotional attachment and
confidence in the brand. Jaya and Saragih (2025) likewise showed that experiences obtained while
using products can deepen consumers' attachment to a brand and encourage loyalty.  Jun et al.
(2025) reported that positive brand experiences can support customer retention, customer
loyalty, and long-term relationships between consumers and brands. These studies indicate that

brand experience can enhance consumer loyalty. Because brand experience reflects the

H2: There is a positive influence of Brand Experience on Brand Loyalty.

The Influence of Brand Experience on Brand Trust
Positive experiences during interactions with a brand can strengthen consumers'
confidence that the brand is capable of meeting expectations and providing the promised benefits
(Leoparjo et al., 2025). Sang and Cuong (2025b) found that the sensory, affective, intellectual, and
behavioral dimensions of brand experience positively influence brand trust. Indriani & Waskito
(2025) further showed that favorable experiences during information search, purchasing, and
service use can increase consumers' trust in brands. Haq et al. ( 2025) similarly reported that
experiences formed during brand interactions can reinforce trust and support the development
of Brand Trust. Collectively, these findings suggest that more positive brand experiences are
associated with greater trust in the brand. B rand Experience reflects what consumers encounter
during their interactions, whereas Brand Trust represents confidence in the brand's competence
and trustworthiness. Based on these findings, the following hypothesis is proposed:
H3: There is a positive influence of Brand Experience on Brand Trust.

The Influence of Brand Trust on Brand Loyalty
Previous empirical research generally indicates a positive relationship between brand
trust and brand loyalty. Khairullah et al. ( 2025b) found that consumers with stronger trust in a
brand are more likely to make repeat purchases and maintain long -term relationships with that
brand. Abeywickrama et al. ( 2025) likewise reported that brand trust can reinforce consumer -
brand relationships and strengthen customer loyalty. Deng et al. ( 2025) found that consumers
who trust a brand are more inclined to develop brand attachment and are less likely to switch to
competitors. Tran et al. ( 2025) also identified brand trust as one of the main factors supporting
the development of brand loyalty. These findings suggest that greater confidence in a brand's
dependability and integrity is associated with a stronger commitment to continue choosing and
using the brand. Based on these findings, the following hypothesis is proposed:
H4: There is a positive influence of Brand Trust on Brand Loyalty.

The Role of Brand Trust Mediation in the Relationship of Exprience Brand to Brand Loyalty
The effect of brand experience on brand loyalty may operate through an internal
psychological process. Sang and Cuong (2025b) identified Brand Trust as a mediating variable in
the relationship between Brand Experience and Brand Loyalty. Liaquat et al. (2025) also showed
that experiential value accumulated through brand interactions can build trust, which
subsequently reinforces consumer loyalty. Kim et al. (2026) described brand trust as an important
element in sustaining long -term consumer-brand relationships and supporting loyalty. Saktiana
(2025) further found that brand experience does not directly increase loyalty but operates
through the prior development of brand trust. Taken together, these findings support the view
that Brand Trust explains how favorable brand experiences can be translated into stronger Brand
Loyalty. Based on these findings, the following hypothesis is proposed:
H5: Brand Trust mediates the influence of Brand Experience on Brand Loyalty.

METHOD
This study applied a quantitative methodology with a causal-explanatory research design
to test the proposed relationships among Brand Personality, Brand Experience, Brand Trust, and
Brand Loyalty, including the mediating role of Brand Trust between Brand Experience and Brand
Loyalty. The research was conducted in four stages: instrument development and validation, data
collection, preliminary assessment of data quality, and hypothesis and mediation testing. The
target population consisted of Indonesian mal e and female consumers who had previously
purchased Muslim Fashion Branded Garments. Participants were drawn from Jakarta, Surabaya,
Bandung, and Medan, which represent important markets for Muslim fashion branded garments.
Purposive sampling was applied using predetermined inclusion criteria. Respondents

were required to have purchased Muslim Fashion Branded Garments within the previous six
months, be between 18 and 60 years old, have experience with at least one Muslim fashion brand
in Indonesia such as Buttonscarves,  Benang Jarum, BHS Sarong, Napocut, or a similar brand, and
have purchased the brand at least twice. Eligibility was checked through an initial screening
question in the Google Forms questionnaire before respondents proceeded to the main survey.
Following Hair et al. ( 2021, 2022), the required sample size was determined at five to ten times
the number of research indicators. With 25 statement items, the minimum sample requirement
was 125 respondents. The final sample consisted of 148 respondents who met the predetermined
criteria.
Primary data were collected electronically through Google Forms using a structured,
closed-ended questionnaire. The instrument contained 25 statement items measuring Brand
Personality, Brand Experience, Brand Trust, and Brand Loyalty and was validated through expert
review before data collection. Responses were recorded on a five-point Likert scale ranging from
1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). Validity and reliability were first assessed with SPSS,
after which Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) was used to test the hypothesized relationships
among the constructs and the mediating role of Brand Trust.
The selection of SEM is based on research objectives that focus on testing predictive
relationships between variables as well as the development of conceptual models involving
mediating variables. Data analysis was carried out using analysis of moment stru ctures (AMOS)
software.

Discussion
The Influence of Brand Personality on Brand Loyalty
The H1 test showed an estimated coefficient of 0.632 with a P -value of 0.000, which is
below the 0.1 significance; therefore, H1 is supported and the effect is significant. This result
indicates that a stronger brand personality can contribute to higher customer loyalty. The finding
is consistent with Kusumadewi et al. (2025), Chariri et al. (2025), Lu et al. (2025), and Kochhar et
al. (2025), whose studies also support a positive influence of brand personality on brand loyalty.
When consumers perceive a match between their own personality and the personality of a brand,
self-compatibility and trust can increase, which ultimately supports stronger loyalty to the brand.

The Influence of Brand Experience on Brand Loyalty
The H2 test resulted in an estimated coefficient of 0.324 and a P-value of 0.004, both below
the 0.1; thus, H2 is supported with a significant effect. This result indicates that a more positive
brand experience contributes to stronger brand loyalty. The fi nding is consistent with empirical
studies by Jibran et al. (2025), Prastiwi et al. (2025), Jaya & Saragih (2025), and Jun et al. (2025).
A strong brand experience can reinforce emotional connections, improve perceptions of service
quality, and build trust, which in turn supports customer loyalty.

The Influence of Brand Experience on Brand Trust
The H3 analysis showed an estimated coefficient of 0.444 and a P-value of 0.000, which is
below the 0.1 significance level; consequently, H3 is supported and the effect is significant. The
result indicates that stronger brand experience is associated with greater brand trust. This finding
is consistent with Leoparjo et al. (2025), Haq et al. (2025), Sang and Cuong (2025b), and Indriani
and Waskito (2025), whose empirical results also show a positive influence of brand experience
on brand trust. Experiences formed during interactions with brands can strengthen consumer
confidence by shaping the relationship between the brand and its customers and creating a n
engaging experience.

The Influence of Brand Trust on Brand Loyalty
The H4 test showed an estimated coefficient of 0.500 with a P -value of 0.000, which is
below the 0.1 significance; therefore, H4 is supported with a significant effect. This result shows
that higher brand trust is associated with higher brand loyalty. The finding is consist ent with
Khairullah et al. ( 2025b), Abeywickrama et al. ( 2025), and Tran et al. ( 2025), whose studies
likewise demonstrate a positive influence of brand trust on brand loyalty. Brand trust directly
affects brand loyalty and can also influence customer satisfaction, which subsequently contributes
to loyalty.

CONCLUSION
Practitioners in the Muslim fashion industry should prioritize differentiation strategies
based on consumer experience while simultaneously strengthening brand trust before expecting
loyalty to develop. Rather than managing brand experience and brand personality as sep arate
dimensions, companies should integrate them through consistent and authentic brand
communication, omnichannel customer experiences, and quality assurance mechanisms that
progressively reinforce consumer trust. The study is limited to the Indonesian c ontext, which
restricts generalization to other Muslim markets or industries, and Brand Trust is the only
mediator examined. Future studies should therefore consider additional mechanisms such as
brand love, customer satisfaction, and brand engagement, ext end the model to other industries
including halal cosmetics, skincare, and food, employ longitudinal designs to assess mediation
effects over time, and examine moderating variables such as cultural values or digital engagement
patterns in increasingly online-driven fashion markets.
The findings of this quantitative study show that consumer loyalty in the Muslim fashion
branded garment sector is influenced by several factors and includes a psychological mediation
process. All five hypotheses were supported. Brand Personality produced the strongest direct
effect on loyalty (β=0.632, p<0.001), while Brand Experience significantly influenced loyalty both
directly (β=0.324, p<0.01) and indirectly through Brand Trust (indirect effect=0.222). Brand
Trust also functioned as a critical mediati ng mechanism linking brand experiences with loyalty
commitment (β=0.500, p<0.001). From a theoretical perspective, the study extends Stimulus -
Organism-Response Theory by showing how brand personality and experience operate as
differentiated stimuli whose e ffects converge through the psychological organism of brand trust
to produce the loyalty response. This mediation pathway helps explain previous research
inconsistencies and clarifies the psychological process underlying Muslim fashion consumer
behavior.
