Abstract
The growing competition among educational institutions and the increasing use of digital
media have made it important for value-based schools to develop distinctive and credible
brand images while maintaining their educational and moral values. However, limited
attention has been given to how creative marketing integrates digital communication,
customer retention-oriented engagement, and parental consumer insights within value-
based educational contexts. This study explores how creative marketing contributes to
brand-image formation, focusing on digital creative execution, customer retention-
oriented practices, and consumer insight alignment as an interpretive mechanism. A
qualitative case study was conducted at Labs School Kaizen, Indonesia. Data were
collected through in-depth interviews with 15 informants comprising management
representatives, marketing staff, and parents, complemented by observation and analysis
of 124 social media posts over six months. Interview data generated 286 coded
segments; value-based positioning accounted for 78 coded segments (27.3%), indicating
a recurring emphasis on the integration of spiritual and academic values. Social media
analysis used individual posts as the unit of analysis, with student storytelling comprising
35 of 124 posts (28%), parenting education 27 posts (22%), and parent testimonials 22
posts (18%). Triangulation of interview, observation, and documentation data indicated
that these communication practices were associated with ongoing parent engagement,
trust-building, and institutional advocacy. Consumer insight alignment, particularly
regarding parental concerns about moral development and aspirations for academic–
spiritual integration, functioned as an interpretive link between institutional positioning and
brand perception. The findings suggest that, within this case, value-based positioning,
digital creative execution, relational engagement, and consumer insight alignment operate
as interconnected processes in institutional brand formation. The study contributes to
educational marketing communication by offering an in-depth case interpretation of trust-
based branding in value-based education.
Keywords: Creative Marketing, Brand Image, Consumer Insight Alignment, Customer Retention, Value-
Based Education.
Introduction
The rapid growth and diversification of Indonesia’s education sector have intensified
competition among educational institutions, particularly private and value-based schools that
combine academic achievement with moral, character, and spiritual development. Competition
increasingly extends beyond curriculum, tuition fees, and infrastructure toward institutions’
ability to establish distinctive identities, communicate their values, and build favorable
perceptions among parents and communities. Institutional branding has therefore become
increasingly important as parents evaluate schools not only based on educational outcomes
but also on trust, reputation, values, emotional compatibility, and perceived capacity to

institutional attributes influence school-choice decisions, while word-of-mouth strengthens
credibility through family experiences and recommendations (Mu'minah et al., 2024; Eriani et
al., 2025; Hidayati et al., 2025).
Marketing communication plays a strategic role in shaping institutional brand image and
influencing parents’ perceptions of educational institutions. Unlike conventional commercial
brands, schools provide intangible and relational services that affect children’s long-term
academic, social, moral, and personal development. Educational branding therefore needs to
communicate both functional benefits, such as academic quality, curriculum, facilities, and
learning outcomes, and symbolic benefits, including character formation, spirituality,
belonging, and personal development (Keller, 2016; Jufry, 2025). Creative marketing
strengthens this process by translating institutional positioning and values into relevant,
distinctive, and meaningful communication. Through Integrated Marketing Communication,
institutions can maintain consistency across channels and stakeholder experiences (Batra &
Keller, 2016; Keller, 2016). Digital platforms further enable schools to communicate identity
through social media, websites, videos, testimonials, and interactive engagement (Kotler et al.,
2021; Dwivedi, 2024).
Digital creative execution represents a critical component of this communication process
because it allows educational institutions to demonstrate rather than merely claim their values.
Content presenting classroom activities, student achievements, teacher interactions, parenting
programs, institutional events, and parent testimonials can provide tangible evidence of how
institutional positioning is enacted in everyday educational practices. However, frequent
exposure to promotional content alone does not necessarily create a strong brand image. The
relevance, authenticity, consistency, and emotional resonance of digital communication are
important in determining whether social media activities generate meaningful engagement and
favorable brand-related outcomes (Novita et al., 2025; Yaqin & Aini, 2025; Putri et al., 2025).
Storytelling further strengthens this process by transforming abstract educational values
into concrete and relatable experiences. Stories about students, teachers, parents,
achievements, learning experiences, and institutional programs can help parents understand
how a school's values are implemented in practice. Digital marketing should therefore be
understood not merely as a mechanism for promoting educational programs but also as a
means of communicating institutional identity and facilitating interaction with stakeholders.
Social media has increasingly become a strategic platform for creating engagement,
encouraging interaction, and strengthening relationships between organizations and their
audiences (Appel et al., 2020; Dwivedi et al., 2021; Godey et al., 2016; Ibrahim et al., 2025;
Pawar, 2025). These relationships are particularly significant in education because parents
simultaneously function as service consumers, stakeholders, evaluators, community
members, and participants in the educational process.
The relational nature of education also makes customer retention an important component
of institutional branding. Relationships between parents and schools may continue for several
years and involve participation in school activities, parent communities, educational programs,
consultation, and other forms of institutional engagement. Consequently, the relationship
between an educational institution and its parents should not end after the initial enrollment
decision. Relational communication, community building, parenting education, webinars,
consultation, and continuous engagement can reinforce trust and institutional loyalty over
time. Sustained customer relationships and engagement are increasingly recognized as

important components of contemporary marketing strategies (Ismail, 2017; Dwianjani et al.,

parental experiences frequently influence school evaluation and choice (Mu'minah et al., 2024;

Although marketing and branding have become increasingly important in education,
previous studies remain divided into several research streams that are only partially
connected. The first stream examines digital and social media marketing, highlighting the role
of digital communication in generating engagement and strengthening brand-related
outcomes. However, much of this literature focuses on general consumer and business
contexts, providing limited explanation of how creative digital communication conveys the
distinctive values of educational institutions (Appel et al., 2020; Dwivedi et al., 2021; Dwivedi,
2024; Ibrahim et al., 2025; Pawar, 2025). The second stream addresses school branding,
institutional image, and school-choice decisions. Studies show that school brands and
institutional image influence how parents evaluate educational alternatives, while brand image,
education costs, word-of-mouth, promotion, and positioning contribute to parental school-
choice decisions (Eriani et al., 2025; Hidayati et al., 2025; Mu'minah et al., 2024). Brand equity
has also been identified as a mechanism linking promotional and positioning activities with
parents’ decisions.
The third stream focuses on parental insight and engagement throughout the school-
selection journey. Recent studies indicate that social media content can support parental
awareness, consideration, decision-making, and subsequent engagement (Nofrizal et al., 2024;
Yaqin & Aini, 2025). Nevertheless, these studies generally examine parental responses,
school-choice determinants, digital marketing, or branding outcomes separately. Limited
attention has been given to how educational institutions interpret parental expectations,
translate these insights into creative communication, maintain relationships after enrollment,
and integrate these activities into a coherent institutional brand narrative. This gap is
particularly relevant to value-based educational institutions, where brand image is closely
associated with academic quality, moral education, character development, religious or
spiritual formation, and alignment with family expectations. Effective communication therefore
requires institutions to understand not only what they seek to communicate but also what
parents value, expect, and aspire to provide for their children. This study conceptualizes this
process as consumer insight alignment, referring to the alignment between institutional
marketing communication and parents’ concerns, aspirations, expectations, and values
(Hidayati et al., 2025).
Consumer insight alignment is positioned in this study as an interpretive and relational
mechanism rather than a statistically tested mediating variable. It describes how educational
institutions interpret parental expectations and translate these insights into strategic
positioning, creative content, relational communication, and institutional experiences.
Consumer insight therefore serves not merely as a measurable antecedent of brand image but
as an interpretive link that enables institutional communication to become more relevant and
meaningful to parents.
Creative marketing is conceptualized as an integrated process comprising interconnected
dimensions. Strategic positioning establishes the distinctive value an institution seeks to
occupy in parents’ perceptions, while digital creative execution translates this positioning into
stories, visual content, educational information, testimonials, and other communication forms.

Relational communication and customer retention maintain interaction with existing parents

Their interaction may contribute to perceptions of institutional credibility, distinctiveness,
trustworthiness, and emotional relevance (Jufry, 2025; Ibrahim et al., 2025; Elhuria et al.,

Despite growing interest in educational marketing, previous studies often examine
marketing communication, digital marketing, brand image, customer engagement, and
customer loyalty as separate constructs rather than interconnected institutional processes
(Rauschnabel et al., 2019; Dwivedi, 2024; Ibrahim et al., 2025; Pawar, 2025). Research on
educational brand image has primarily emphasized reputation, school choice, service quality,
and brand equity, with limited attention to the creative processes through which institutional
identity and values are communicated. Customer retention has likewise been examined mainly
in relation to satisfaction and loyalty rather than as part of ongoing institutional brand
formation. More importantly, limited research has examined consumer insight alignment as an
interpretive mechanism linking strategic positioning, digital creative execution, relational
communication, customer retention, and brand-image formation in value-based educational
institutions in Indonesia. Accordingly, this study explores how creative marketing contributes
to brand-image formation through these interconnected dimensions and addresses four
questions concerning creative marketing, digital creative execution, customer retention, and
consumer insight alignment.
The novelty of this study lies in developing an integrative conceptual explanation of
educational branding that connects strategic positioning, digital creative execution, relational
communication, customer retention, and consumer insight alignment. Rather than treating
consumer insight alignment as a statistically tested mediation effect, the study interprets it as
a relational and interpretive mechanism through which educational institutions understand
parental expectations, translate those insights into marketing communication and institutional
experiences, and contribute to the formation of brand perceptions. Digital marketing, customer
relationships, consumer insight, and brand image are therefore examined as interconnected
processes rather than as isolated constructs within the specific context of a value-based
educational institution.
The qualitative case study approach is particularly appropriate for this purpose because it
enables the study to investigate the meanings, practices, interactions, and communication
processes through which these relationships are developed in an actual institutional setting.
Such an approach provides insight into processes that may not be fully captured through
quantitative measurement of predetermined variables. The study therefore contributes
theoretically to educational marketing communication literature by extending understanding of
how institutional brand image is constructed through creative, relational, and insight-driven
processes. Practically, it provides educational managers with a framework for understanding
how authentic storytelling, digital creative execution, parent engagement, customer retention,
value-based positioning, and consumer insight can be integrated into sustainable and trust-
based institutional branding strategies.

Method
Research Design, Case Selection, and Research Setting
This study employed a qualitative approach using a single-site case study design to
explore how creative marketing strategies contribute to brand-image formation within a value-
based educational institution. A qualitative case study was considered appropriate because the
study examined organizational processes, stakeholder interpretations, communication
practices, and institutional relationships in their natural context rather than statistically testing
predetermined causal relationships (Yin, 2018; Mishra & Dey, 2021; Greenhalgh, 2025;
Susilawati et al., 2023; Khairiyah et al., 2026). The study was conducted at Labs School
Kaizen, Indonesia, which was purposively selected as an information-rich case due to its
implementation of value-based positioning, digital marketing communication, creative social
media content, and continuous engagement with parents. The case incorporated perspectives
from school management, marketing personnel, and parents as key stakeholders in
institutional brand communication. Fieldwork was conducted from January to June 2026 to
capture communication practices over time. Data collection included stakeholder interviews,
repeated observations of marketing activities, and documentation of digital content. Particular
attention was given to recurring and changing patterns in content themes, creative formats,
value-based messages, parent-oriented communication, and stakeholder engagement. This
extended observation enabled the study to identify consistent communication patterns and
distinguish them from activities associated with specific promotional events.
Research Participants and Instruments
The study involved 15 informants comprising five school management representatives
(33%), four marketing staff members (27%), and six parents (40%). Informants were selected
through purposive criterion sampling based on their direct knowledge, involvement, or
experience with institutional marketing communication and brand-building activities (Guha et
al., 2021; Ahmad & Wilkins, 2025). Management representatives provided insights into
institutional positioning and strategic communication, marketing staff contributed perspectives
on content development and dissemination, while parents provided experiences regarding
school choice, expectations, institutional values, and brand perceptions. This composition
enabled comparison between the intended institutional brand narrative and its interpretation by
parents, emphasizing information richness rather than statistical representativeness (Stenfors
et al., 2020). The researcher served as the primary instrument for data collection and
interpretation, supported by a semi-structured interview guide, observation guide, and
documentation checklist (Yoon & Uliassi, 2022). Interviews addressed strategic positioning,
digital creative execution, customer retention, and parental needs and expectations.
Observations focused on communication practices, digital content, stakeholder interactions,
and representations of institutional values, while documentation covered promotional materials
and social media content based on their purpose, message, format, and relevance to the study
dimensions (Husband, 2020; Magaldi & Berler, 2020; Kayesa & Shung-King, 2020). Audio
recordings, transcripts, field notes, and digital archives were retained to support systematic
analysis and comparison.
Data Collection
Data were collected through three complementary techniques: semi-structured in-depth
interviews, observation, and document analysis (Khairiyah et al., 2026; Rakhmat et al., 2026).
Interviews were conducted with all 15 informants to examine institutional positioning, the

translation of educational values into marketing communication, digital content development,
parent relationship management, and the influence of parental expectations on communication

marketing communication as implemented in practice, including digital communication
activities, content characteristics, parent engagement, and representations of value-based

Document analysis covered institutional promotional materials, relevant communication

(21 posts), and events and experiential activities (19 posts) (Orth et al., 2020; Saikia &
Barman, 2023). Analysis considered both the distribution of content categories and the ways
in which institutional values, educational experiences, stakeholder relationships, and brand-
related messages were represented through digital creative execution.
Data Analysis
Data were analyzed using the interactive qualitative analysis framework comprising data
condensation, data display, and conclusion drawing and verification (Miles et al., 2018;
Rakhmat et al., 2026). Analysis proceeded iteratively through comparison of interview
transcripts, observation notes, and documentary materials. The initial stage involved open
coding of statements relevant to the research questions, generating 286 coded statements
from the interview data. Initial codes captured recurring issues concerning value-based
positioning, emotional storytelling, parental concerns, digital retention activities, religious-
academic identity, customer relationships, and brand perceptions (Braun & Clarke, 2021;
Byrne, 2022).
During data condensation, related codes were grouped into broader analytical categories
and progressively refined through comparison across participants and data sources (Braun &
Clarke, 2021; Byrne, 2022). The analysis produced interconnected themes encompassing
value-based positioning, emotional storytelling, parental moral concerns, digital retention
strategies, religious-academic image, digital creative execution, and consumer insight
alignment. In the data-display stage, coded information was organized into thematic tables and
comparative matrices to examine patterns across stakeholder groups and evidence types. For
instance, parental concerns identified through interviews were compared with themes
represented in digital content, while statements concerning institutional values were examined
alongside observed communication practices and documentary evidence.
Conclusion drawing and verification involved repeatedly checking emerging interpretations
against the original transcripts, observation notes, and documentary materials. Themes were
retained when they demonstrated conceptual coherence, relevance to the research questions,
and sufficient evidentiary support across the case. This iterative verification process enabled
the analysis to move from individual coded statements toward integrated interpretations of
how creative marketing processes contributed to institutional brand-image formation (Stenfors
et al., 2020).
Trustworthiness and Triangulation
The credibility and trustworthiness of the findings were strengthened through source
triangulation, technique triangulation, temporal comparison, continuous comparison with

original data, and maintenance of an audit trail (Aguilar Solano, 2020; Lemon & Hayes, 2020;
Stenfors et al., 2020; Carcary, 2020). Source triangulation involved comparing perspectives
from school management, marketing staff, and parents to examine differences between
intended institutional branding and stakeholder interpretations. Technique triangulation
compared evidence from interviews, observations, and documentary analysis, including the
124 social media posts examined during the study period (Rakhmat et al., 2026). Temporal
comparison was conducted throughout the six-month fieldwork period to identify consistent or

evidence supported credibility and confirmability. An audit trail documenting data collection,
coding, categorization, and analytical decisions was maintained to enhance dependability
(Carcary, 2020). Thus, triangulation served to examine convergence, complementarity, and
discrepancy across evidence rather than merely confirming predetermined interpretations
(Lemon & Hayes, 2020).
Results

representatives, marketing staff, and parents, revealed several recurring patterns in how
value-based creative marketing contributed to the institution's brand image. Rather than
interpreting the number of participants or coded statements as indicators of quantitative
prevalence, the analysis focused on the meanings, relationships, and patterns that emerged
across stakeholder perspectives. Through open coding of 286 relevant statements, several
major themes were identified, including value-based positioning, emotional storytelling,
parental concerns, digital retention strategies, and the construction of a religious-academic
image.

The findings are consistent with the customer-based brand equity perspective, which
emphasizes meaningful, distinctive, and relevant associations in shaping brand knowledge
(Keller, 2017). Brand differentiation is strengthened when organizations communicate unique
values that are consistently recognized by stakeholders (Aaker, 2018; Hidayati et al., 2025). In
value-based education, institutional values therefore represent an important source of
differentiation. The findings show that Labs School Kaizen does not position itself solely
through academic achievement but consistently combines academic development with moral
and spiritual formation. This pattern is reflected in the religious-academic image theme,
indicating that stakeholders perceive the institution through an integrated academic and value-
based identity. Such positioning strengthens institutional identity by providing a clear and
distinctive value proposition (Aaker, 2018; Keller, 2016).
The findings further indicate that the institution's value proposition is constructed through
the integration of academic and spiritual dimensions rather than treating spiritual education as
an additional program. This positioning is closely connected to parental concerns, particularly
moral degradation, negative peer influence, and children's exposure to uncontrolled digital
environments. The parental moral concerns theme generated 64 coded citations (22.40%),
demonstrating that institutional communication responds to issues considered important by
parents. Brand associations become more meaningful when they correspond with stakeholder
needs and expectations (Keller, 2017; Hidayati et al., 2025). The institution's emphasis on
moral and spiritual values therefore functions simultaneously as a differentiation strategy and
as a means of establishing relevance with parental expectations (Eriani et al., 2025; Nofrizal et
al., 2024).
The 49 citations (17.10%) related to digital retention strategies further show that creative
marketing extended beyond attracting prospective parents to maintaining relationships with
existing stakeholders. This finding aligns with relationship marketing and customer
engagement perspectives emphasizing continuous interaction and long-term relationships
(Hollebeek et al., 2017; Ibrahim et al., 2025). Digital platforms facilitate ongoing interaction,
community development, and value exchange (Appel et al., 2020; Dwivedi et al., 2021;
Dwivedi, 2024). Overall, the findings indicate that brand-image formation is an ongoing
process shaped by five interconnected themes: value-based positioning, emotional
storytelling, parental moral concerns, digital retention activities, and religious-academic
identity. Creative marketing therefore operates through both identity construction and
relationship development (Batra & Keller, 2016; Jufry, 2025).

Parenting education accounted for 27 posts (22%) and addressed parenting challenges,
child development, educational issues, and family concerns. The content positioned the
institution as a source of knowledge and parental support beyond its primary educational
service. Providing relevant informational content can create functional and relational value,
thereby supporting continuous stakeholder engagement (Hollebeek et al., 2017; Dwivedi,

educational service. As social and peer-based communication can enhance credibility and
trust, testimonials functioned as complementary credibility signals alongside institutional

storytelling and parenting content by providing informational and experiential dimensions.
Overall, the digital creative execution reflected three complementary functions: emotional
communication through storytelling, informational communication through parenting and
academic content, and credibility-oriented communication through testimonials and
experiential activities. This pattern aligns with integrated marketing communication, which
emphasizes complementary and consistent messages across communication activities (Batra
& Keller, 2016; Keller, 2016). Digital media therefore functioned as an extension of institutional
identity, repeatedly making its positioning, educational experiences, and core values visible to
stakeholders (Eriani et al., 2025; Jufry, 2025).

The Mediating Role of Consumer Insight Alignment
The findings suggest that creative marketing strategies become more meaningful when
they align with parental insights and expectations. Interviews with six parent informants
revealed several recurring concerns that shaped their considerations when selecting a school.
In this study, consumer insight alignment is therefore interpreted as a linking mechanism that
helps explain how institutional positioning and creative communication become relevant to
parents. Rather than representing a statistically tested mediation effect, this mechanism
reflects the process through which parental concerns, expectations, and aspirations are
interpreted and incorporated into institutional communication and brand meaning.

Discussion
Value-Based Positioning and Digital Creative Execution
The findings indicate that creative marketing at Labs School Kaizen operates through
interconnected practices involving value-based positioning, digital creative execution,
customer retention-oriented engagement, and consumer insight alignment. The case data
show that the institution consistently communicates academic achievement alongside moral
and spiritual development, uses digital content to make these values visible, maintains
ongoing interaction with parents, and aligns communication with parental concerns. These
patterns suggest that institutional branding is constructed through both communication
practices and sustained stakeholder relationships (Hidayati et al., 2025; Ibrahim et al., 2025;
Eriani et al., 2025).
Value-based positioning emerged prominently in the interview data, represented by 78
coded segments. Moral, spiritual, and character-oriented values were consistently associated
with institutional identity and differentiation, while academic achievement was commonly
presented together with these dimensions. The religious-academic image accounted for
24.10% of coded citations, indicating that the institution communicates a distinctive
educational identity rather than academic services alone. This pattern can be interpreted
through customer-based brand equity theory, which emphasizes meaningful, favorable, and
distinctive associations in brand knowledge formation (Keller, 2017). Institutional values may
also function as signals for parents evaluating an intangible educational service and its
compatibility with family expectations (Nofrizal et al., 2024).
The relevance of this positioning is reinforced by parental concerns. Moral anxiety
represented 22.40% of coded statements, while 83% of parent informants expressed concerns
regarding children's moral development. This correspondence suggests that institutional
positioning becomes meaningful when it addresses stakeholder concerns. Value-based
branding therefore involves not only communicating institutional values but also connecting
those values with parental expectations (Hidayati et al., 2025).
Digital creative execution subsequently translated this positioning into visible and
emotionally meaningful communication. Student storytelling constituted 28% of the 124
analyzed posts, followed by parenting education (22%) and parent testimonials (18%). The
prominence of these formats indicates reliance on narrative, educational, and experience-
based communication rather than conventional promotion alone (Appel et al., 2020; Dwivedi et
al., 2021; Dwivedi, 2024). Student stories transformed abstract claims about character,
achievement, and personal development into concrete representations of educational

Parenting education further demonstrates that digital communication provided value

Digital Creative Execution, Customer Retention, and Consumer Insight Alignment
The findings indicate that digital creative execution extends beyond promotional
communication to support stakeholder engagement and institutional brand formation.
Parenting content, which provided informational and relational value, created opportunities for
parents to interact with the institution beyond enrollment-related communication. This finding
is consistent with the customer engagement perspective, which emphasizes meaningful
interactions that provide value (Hollebeek et al., 2017). Parent testimonials, representing 18%
of digital content, further provided experience-based evidence that could complement
institutional claims and reduce uncertainty surrounding an intangible educational service. Such
peer-generated communication may strengthen credibility and trust because stakeholder
experiences are often perceived as more authentic than organizational messages alone (Appel
et al., 2020; Dwivedi et al., 2021; Fitrianingsih et al., 2025).
Academic information (17%) and events and experiential activities (15%) complemented
storytelling, parenting education, and testimonials. Collectively, the content reflected three
functions: emotional communication through storytelling, informational communication
through parenting and academic content, and credibility-oriented communication through
testimonials and experiential activities. This combination is consistent with integrated
marketing communication, which emphasizes complementary and consistent messages
across communication activities (Batra & Keller, 2016; Keller, 2016). Digital media therefore
functioned as an extension of institutional identity by repeatedly presenting students, parents,
educational experiences, and institutional values (Hidayati et al., 2025; Pawar, 2025).
The institution's three parent WhatsApp groups involving 248 members, six parenting
webinars, and sustained digital interaction indicate communication continuity beyond school
selection. However, communication channels alone do not constitute direct evidence of
retention or loyalty. Relational outcomes were more clearly reflected in 49 coded statements
concerning trust and 37 concerning parental loyalty. These findings suggest that continued
interaction may strengthen relational ties when parents perceive the institution as responsive,
supportive, and consistent. The finding that 64% of newly enrolled parents learned about the
school through word-of-mouth further indicates that existing parent relationships may
contribute to advocacy. Retention is therefore better understood as a relational process
involving communication continuity, trust, community participation, loyalty, and advocacy
(Hollebeek et al., 2017; Ismail, 2017; Ibrahim et al., 2025).
Consumer insight alignment provides an interpretive link between these practices and
brand-image formation. Parental concerns regarding moral development, digital exposure,
peer influence, and character education corresponded with the institution's value-based

Conclusion
This study concludes that creative marketing strategies contribute substantially to the
formation of brand image in value-based educational institutions through the integration of
value-based positioning, digital creative execution, customer retention, and consumer insight
alignment. The findings show that value-based positioning, particularly the integration of
academic achievement with moral and spiritual development, provides a distinctive foundation
for institutional differentiation. Digital creative execution further strengthens this positioning
through student storytelling, parenting education, and parent testimonials, which enhance
emotional engagement, relevance, and institutional credibility. Customer retention activities,
including parent communities, parenting webinars, and continuous relational communication,
reinforce trust, loyalty, and positive word-of-mouth. Consumer insight alignment also
functions as an important explanatory mechanism by connecting institutional communication
with parental concerns regarding moral development, digital influence, and character
education.
The findings suggest that educational managers may consider integrating authentic value-
based positioning, relevant digital content, and sustained parent engagement when developing
institutional marketing strategies. In the context of Labs School Kaizen, these practices
appeared to support the communication of institutional values and the development of
stakeholder relationships; however, their effectiveness should not be assumed to apply
universally across educational institutions. Continuous stakeholder insight analysis may also
help institutions align marketing communication with parents’ expectations and concerns. This
study is limited by its single-case qualitative design and relatively narrow stakeholder
coverage, which restricts the transferability of the findings to other educational contexts.
Future research should examine multiple value-based institutions through comparative case
studies and mixed-method or quantitative approaches to assess whether similar patterns
emerge across different settings. Future studies may also include students, alumni, teachers,
and prospective parents to provide a broader understanding of educational brand-image
formation and its long-term implications for institutional competitiveness.
