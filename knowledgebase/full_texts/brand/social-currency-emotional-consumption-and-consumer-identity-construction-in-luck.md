Abstract. In recent years, emotional consumption has emerged as a prominent driving force
shaping the consumption patterns of young Chinese consumers. Instead of making purchase
decisions solely based on products' functional value, consumers are increasingly motivated
by pursuits of emotional satisfaction, social interaction, and identity expression. Against this
trend, Luckin Coffee has sparked extensive social media participation and consumer
interaction by launching co-branding marketing campaigns with popular IPs, cultural
symbols, and trending public topics. This study aims to explore how Luckin Coffee builds
social currency through co-branding marketing and how such social currency further
stimulates emotional consumption and strengthens consumer identity recognition. Adopting
a qualitative case study approach, this research synthesizes evidence from academic
literature, corporate official reports, media coverage, and social media content for analysis.
The findings demonstrate that Luckin Coffee transforms ordinary coffee consumption into a
symbolic social experience, which effectively enhances consumers' brand attachment and
deepens consumer-brand relationships.
Keywords: Co-branding Marketing, Social Currency, Emotional Consumption, Consumer
Identity, Luckin Coffee
1. Introduction
Digital media has profoundly reshaped consumer behavior. Young consumers increasingly pursue
emotional experiences and social identity through consumption, shifting from functional satisfaction
toward symbolic and emotional participation [1]. Meanwhile, social media platforms such as
Xiaohongshu, Weibo, and TikTok have accelerated the dissemination of brand-related content,
turning social currency into a critical marketing resource [2]. Co-branding marketing has thus
become a widely adopted strategy for brands to attract consumer attention and boost customer
engagement, supported by customer engagement theory [3, 4]. Luckin Coffee offers an ideal
research context for investigating this trend. Since 2022, the brand has launched extensive co-
branding campaigns with partners including Guizhou Maotai, Hello Kitty, and international sports IP
[5-7]. These campaigns have generated extensive online discussions and consumer participation,

2. Theoretical foundations
2.1. Emotional consumption perspectives
Emotional consumption refers to consumption behavior driven primarily by consumers' emotional
needs, emotional experience, and psychological satisfaction, rather than based on functional utility
or rational calculation [1]. Schmitt's Strategic Experiential Modules (SEMs) within the experiential
marketing framework emphasize that brands must create sensory, emotional, and cognitive
experiences that resonate with consumers [1]. This perspective indicates that consumption is not
merely a transactional exchange but a holistic experience involving emotional engagement, which
echoes the discussion of value co-creation in service-dominant logic [8].
The rise of emotional consumption in China is closely tied to broad social and economic changes.
As young consumers secure basic material security, their consumption focus shifts toward symbolic
value and experiential value. Brands that successfully evoke feelings of pleasure, nostalgia,
excitement, or sense of belonging can establish deeper emotional connections with consumers [9]. In
the context of co-branding marketing, emotional consumption often manifests as consumers'
emotional responses to symbolic elements such as IPs, cultural symbols, and nostalgic experiences.
2.2. Social currency theory
Berger defines social currency as the social value individuals gain from sharing brand or product
information, which helps improve their social image and strengthen interpersonal interactions [2].
Products and ideas that make people appear more favorable, intelligent, or well-informed are more
likely to be shared and discussed. Social currency thus can be viewed as a form of social value,
which individuals accumulate and exchange through conversations and recommendations.
In the digital age, social currency takes on new dimensions. Social media platforms allow
consumers to broadcast their consumption choices to broader social networks, turning daily
purchases into public statements about taste, identity, and social affiliation [4]. Co-branded products
tend to generate stronger social currency, as they combine the brand equity of two partnering brands
to create a sense of novelty and uniqueness worth discussing and sharing [10].
2.3. Consumer identity construction perspective
The consumer identity construction perspective examines how individuals use consumption to
construct, express, and convey their identity. Belk's theory of the extended self points out that

2.4. Research gap and analytical framework
Although existing literature has examined emotional consumption, social currency, and consumer
identity, respectively, their interactive mechanism in digital co-branding contexts remains relatively
underexplored, especially in the context of China's co-branding marketing landscape [1, 2, 11, 14].
Previous discussions on Luckin Coffee have mainly focused on its brand development, marketing
innovation, and consumer engagement, while the mechanism linking co-branding activities to social
value creation and identity construction requires further exploration [5-7]. This study proposes a
three-stage analytical framework: co-branding activities generate social currency through symbolic
value creation, novelty effect, and social media amplification; social currency further strengthens
consumers' emotional engagement and stimulates emotional consumption; and emotional
consumption promotes consumer identity construction and enhances brand attachment [15, 16].
3. Development of Luckin Coffee's co-branding marketing strategy
3.1. Overview of Luckin Coffee's brand development
Luckin Coffee has achieved rapid growth in China's coffee market through store expansion, digital-
first operations, and competitive pricing strategies [6]. Founded in 2017, it provides affordable
coffee products through a mobile application-based business model and quickly established a strong
market presence. In 2023, Luckin Coffee surpassed Starbucks China in quarterly revenue, marking
an important milestone in China's coffee industry [9, 10].
Beyond operational efficiency, Luckin Coffee has demonstrated a deep understanding of young
Chinese consumers' preferences, especially the pursuit of novelty, social participation and emotional
resonance. Therefore, its marketing strategy has shifted from price-oriented promotion towards
experience-based engagement, with co-branding campaigns becoming an important component of
its brand development strategy [1, 3].
3.2. Strategic logic of co-branding campaigns
Its frequent co-branding campaigns are based on several strategic considerations, influencing its
selection of partners and campaign design [7].
First, co-branding enables audience expansion through cooperating with brands and IPs that
possess different consumer groups. Second, co-branding creates novelty and maintains consumer
attention in a highly competitive market. Limited-edition products, customized packaging, and
exclusive merchandise provide consumers with fresh experiences and encourage short-term

3.3. Representative co-branding cases
This section introduces three typical co-branding campaigns of Luckin Coffee, focusing on their
launch time, cooperative partners, product design, and market performance.
Case 1: Maotai Latte (2023): In 2023, Luckin Coffee partnered with Guizhou Maotai to launch
the "Sauce-flavored Latte", priced at 38 yuan per cup. The product sold 5.42 million cups on its first
day of release, with first-day sales exceeding 100 million yuan [5]. The campaign attracted extensive
discussion on social media platforms, supported by limited-edition packaging and collectible
promotional materials.
Case 2: Hello Kitty Co-branding (2026): In May 2026, Luckin Coffee collaborated with the IP
Hello Kitty to launch the limited-edition "Hello Apple Jasmine" beverage and related merchandise,
including cup sleeves, tote bags, stickers, and dolls. With the theme of "Return to Childhood", the
campaign reached its peak on the eve of Mother's Day. Co-branded merchandise sold out within
hours of release, and themed offline stores became popular check-in spots for young consumers.
Related topics accumulated tens of millions of views on platforms such as Weibo and Xiaohongshu.
Case 3: Spain & Portugal National Football Teams Co-branding (2026): From May to June 2026,
Luckin Coffee announced a cooperation campaign with the Spanish and Portuguese national football
teams in China. The campaign included themed beverages, team-logo cup sleeves, and related
merchandise. By integrating football elements into its marketing activities, Luckin expanded its
communication reach among sports enthusiasts and the football community.
4. Social currency creation through Luckin Coffee's co-branding campaigns
Building on co-branding practices discussed in Chapter 3, this chapter analyzes the mechanism
through which Luckin Coffee's co-branding campaigns generate social currency, from three
dimensions: symbolic value transformation, social media viral communication, and user-generated
content co-creation.
4.1. Symbolic consumption and emotional value creation
Luckin Coffee's co-branding campaigns create social currency by transforming functional coffee
consumption into a symbolic cultural and emotional experience [2]. Through partnerships with
brands and IPs from different cultural domains, co-branded products acquire additional meanings
beyond their basic consumption value. These symbolic associations allow consumers to express
personal preferences, cultural interests, and social identities through consumption choices.
Specifically, the Maotai collaboration frames coffee consumption as an encounter with luxury
and national cultural prestige, the Hello Kitty partnership infuses consumption with cuteness,
innocence, and childhood nostalgia, the sports co-branding links daily coffee drinking to sports
enthusiasm, team loyalty, and football culture.These symbolic associations enrich the product's
emotional value beyond its basic functional utility [1, 13].

Co-branded packaging, peripheral merchandise, and in-store themed displays jointly construct a

creation is also an accumulation process of brand equity, as co-branding continuously enriches the
brand's overall meaning and symbolic connotation [10]. Products with such symbolic added value
become share-worthy social tokens, laying the foundation for social currency formation.
4.2. Social media sharing and viral communication
Social media platforms serve as essential channels through which Luckin Coffee's co-branding
campaigns convert consumer experiences into social currency. After purchasing co-branded
products, consumers are often motivated to share their experiences on platforms such as
Xiaohongshu, Weibo, and TikTok, driven by factors including product novelty, visual appeal,
cultural relevance, and trending topic participation [2].
Hollebeek et al. pointed out that social media-based brand engagement includes three
dimensions: cognitive, emotional, and behavioral, and Luckin Coffee's co-branding campaigns
stimulate consumer participation across all three dimensions simultaneously [4]. User-generated
content triggered by co-branding far exceeds the reach of paid media, forming a virtuous cycle:
social sharing generates more social currency, which in turn drives further sharing. This mechanism
effectively reduces customer acquisition costs and improves brand awareness on a large scale [2, 9].
For instance, the Maotai Latte's stark contrast between mass-market coffee and premium liquor
drove spontaneous sharing among millions of consumers, pushing related topics to top social media
hot lists within hours.
4.3. User-generated content and community participation
Beyond passive sharing, Luckin Coffee's co-branding campaigns actively encourage user
participation and community building [3]. Elements such as collectible stickers and merchandise,
offline themed check-in spots, and social media interactive activities transform consumers from
passive recipients of marketing information into active co-creators of brand meaning [8].
User-generated content not only extends the reach of co-branding campaigns, but also deepens
consumers' emotional investment in the brand. When consumers invest time and effort in creating
content related to co-branded products, the social value of these products is further strengthened, and
the identity attributes linked to the products become more distinct [11, 12].
In this process, social currency is no longer a value unilaterally created by the brand but a
collective asset jointly constructed by the brand and consumer communities, which enhances its
sustainability and recognition among target groups.
5. Emotional consumption and consumer identity construction
This chapter further analyzes how social currency generated by co-branding stimulates emotional
consumption and promotes consumer identity construction, forming the second half of the influence
mechanism.
5.1. Emotional drivers of participation
Social currency generated by co-branding campaigns encourages consumer participation by
providing emotional experiences beyond functional consumption.

Novelty and curiosity are key drivers. The unexpected combination of mass-market coffee and
premium liquor in the Maotai Latte campaign stimulated strong curiosity among consumers,
prompting millions of purchases and trial experiences on the first day of release [5]. The desire for
novel experiences drives early adoption behavior and subsequent social sharing, which is a core
emotional motivation for consumers to pursue social currency [2].
Nostalgia also serves as a key emotional trigger. The Hello Kitty co-branding evokes collective
childhood memories among consumers, delivering emotional satisfaction of returning to innocence.
Consumers participate in the campaign not only for the product itself, but also to obtain the
emotional value associated with nostalgic symbols, which is a typical manifestation of emotional
consumption [1].
Team belonging and group identity drive participation in sports-themed co-branding. Football
fans express their support and loyalty to their favorite teams by purchasing co-branded products, and
such consumption carries strong group identity attributes. Meanwhile, the sense of social belonging
and fear of missing out further amplify participation willingness, as consumers want to stay aligned
with the topics and experiences shared by their social circles [11]. In addition, when co-branding
activities are highly consistent with consumers' self-concept, they are more willing to participate in
sharing, and the intensity of emotional attachment directly affects their participation willingness
[16].
5.2. Identity expression and symbolic consumption
By participating in co-branding activities and interacting with the resulting social currency,
consumers express and construct their personal identities [11, 12]. Choosing to purchase and share
co-branded products conveys signals about one's cultural awareness, taste preferences, social
connections, and alignment with specific values.
Aaker's brand personality theory points out that brand personality traits (sincerity, excitement,
competence, sophistication, ruggedness) can be internalized by consumers as part of their self-
concept [13]. Co-branded products act as identity markers, indicating affiliation with specific
reference groups [12]. For example, sharing the Maotai Latte signals participation in mainstream
cultural trends and mature consumption taste, posting about Hello Kitty co-branded products
conveys a cute, innocent, and warm personal image, sharing sports co-branded merchandise
demonstrates passion for football and team loyalty. These identity signals are further amplified
through social media dissemination.
Fournier's brand relationship theory indicates that the type of relationship consumers form with
brands reflects their self-identity and social roles [17]. Symbolic consumption through co-branding
is consistent with the logic of the extended self [11]: the higher the consistency between brand
personality and consumers' self-image, the more inclined they are to express their identity through
the brand [13].
5.3. Brand attachment and loyalty formation
Emotional consumption and identity expression jointly contribute to the formation of brand
attachment and consumer loyalty [14, 15]. Park et al. conceptualized brand attachment as the
strength of the emotional bond between consumers and the brand, and Thomson et al. developed a
scale to measure the strength of consumers' emotional attachments to brands [15, 16]. Co-branding
campaigns create memorable emotional experiences for consumers, strengthening the emotional

6. Discussion
6.1. Key findings
This study yields three core findings. First, Luckin Coffee creates social currency by transforming
functional coffee products into symbolic and socially meaningful consumption objects [2, 10].
Through strategic partner selection, symbolic association transfer, and social media dissemination,
co-branded products become shareable social resources that encourage consumer interaction and
participation. Second, social currency stimulates emotional consumption by tapping into consumers'
emotional needs for novelty, nostalgia, social belonging, and identity expression [1, 11]. Different
types of co-branding partners activate different emotional drivers, thereby covering diverse
consumer groups and enriching the brand's emotional connotation. Third, emotional consumption
further facilitates consumer identity construction. Co-branded products function as symbolic
resources depends not only on market attention but also on the symbolic fit between partner brands
and target consumer's identity expectations [12-14].
6.2. Theoretical contributions
This study makes several theoretical contributions to the existing literature. First, it integrates
emotional consumption, social currency, and consumer identity construction into a unified analytical
framework, demonstrating their interactive mechanism in the context of co-branding marketing, and
responding to the value co-creation perspective in service-dominant logic [1, 2, 8, 11, 14].
Second, it extends social currency theory to the Chinese market context, illustrating how social
media platforms and local cultural dynamics shape the creation and exchange of social currency in
brand co-branding practices [2, 4].
Third, by examining co-branded products as resources for consumer identity construction, this
study supplements research on brand attachment and expands the application boundaries of sports
marketing in consumer behavior research [12, 13, 15, 16].
6.3. Managerial implications for Chinese domestic brands
The findings provide practical insights for local consumer brands [9, 10]. First, brands should select
co-branding partners whose symbolic meaning aligns with their own brand positioning and target
consumers' identity expectations, to create associations that trigger emotional resonance [13, 14].
Second, campaign design should center on social shareability. Brands can create visually
appealing packaging, collectible merchandise, and shareable content to encourage user-generated
content and amplify the spread of social currency [2, 4].

7. Conclusion
This study examines how Luckin Coffee's co-branding marketing practices generate social currency
and influence consumer behavior through emotional consumption and identity construction. Using a
qualitative case study approach, the research demonstrates that co-branding enables brands to move
beyond functional product value by creating symbolic meanings and facilitating consumer
participation.
The findings suggest that co-branding operates through a sequential mechanism: strategic
partnerships create social currency, social currency stimulates emotional consumption, and
emotional experiences provide consumers with resources for identity expression. Through this
process, co-branded products become not only consumption objects but also symbols of social
interaction and personal identity.
This study also highlights certain limitations. Over-reliance on co-branding marketing may bring
long-term sustainability challenges, as consumer participation driven mainly by novelty and partner
attractiveness may not translate into lasting brand loyalty. Although the qualitative case study
method provides rich contextual insights, its findings cannot be statistically generalized to broader
scenarios, and the research relies on secondary data sources.
Future research can be carried out in multiple directions. Quantitative methods with larger sample
sizes can be adopted to test the relationships identified in this study. Cross-brand comparative
studies can identify the boundary conditions that influence the effectiveness of co-branding
strategies. Longitudinal research can examine whether emotional attachment persists over time and
translates into stable brand loyalty. Cross-cultural comparisons can help clarify how cultural
differences shape consumers' responses to co-branded products and the construction of social
currency.
