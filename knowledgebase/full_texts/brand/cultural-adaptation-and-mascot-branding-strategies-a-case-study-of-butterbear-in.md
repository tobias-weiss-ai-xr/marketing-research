Introduction

Mascots have been widely employed as a communication tool in both business and
marketing, at national and international levels. They serve as memorable and approachable
symbols that effectively connect brands with their target audiences (Cayla, 2013). Acti ng as
representatives or distinctive symbols, mascots help organizations, events, and activities
establish unique identities. Typically designed as characters with distinctive features— such as
animals, objects, or human figures with appealing and friendly traits—mascots play a significant
role in fostering audience engagement and creating an enjoyable atmosphere at events and
promotional activities (Brown & Ponsonby-McCabe, 2014).

In addition, mascots can be used to create a connection with the brand, making it easier
for consumers to recall products or services. Beyond visual appeal, mascots can also foster an
emotional bond between the brand and consumers. They are often utilized in promotional and
marketing activities —such as participating in events or producing advertising content— to
increase consumer engagement and product appeal (Oh & Kim, 2025). Furthermore, developing
distinctive narratives and personalities for mascots serves as an effective strategy to enrich their
character design, making them appear livelier and more relatable to consumers. This process
strengthens emotiona l attachment, thereby increasing brand memorability and long -term
consumer loyalty (Deligöz & Ünal, 2021).
An illustrative example is the mascot ButterBear, which, despite being designed in the
form of a bear, demonstrates notable differences in both visual appearance and character
development for communication with target audiences. In the Chinese market in particular, the
bear holds deep cultural significa nce, making the design and characterization of ButterBear
especially meaningful for Chinese consumers (Li, 2022).
The mascot ButterBear represents the bakery brand under the Coffee Bean by Dao
franchise and first gained widespread recognition through short video clips circulated on TikTok.
Its distinctive cuteness and charm quickly captivated audiences, leading Chinese social media
users to share the content further and thereby expanding ButterBear’s popularity across China
(Sanook, 2024a). According to scholarly analysis, ButterBear’s character reflects the attributes
of a young girl—adorable, gentle, and endearing—while also embodying politeness and modesty.
These qualities evoke protective and nurturing instincts among consumers, fostering emotional
attachment to the mascot (Thammasat University, 2024).
The growing popularity of ButterBear in China led to the organization of the “China Fan
Meeting” at Jingfeng Shopping Center in Nanjing, Jiangsu Province, which attracted a large
number of Chinese fans (MGR Online , 2024 ). Beyond such events, the brand strategically
collaborated with local Chinese companies to expand market presence and increase consumer
awareness. A notable example was its partnership with Luckin Coffee to launch two co-branded
beverages, Little Butter La tte and Little Butter Americano, which were highly successful.
Between September 30 and October 11, 2024, sales of these beverages on the Douyin platform
exceeded one million orders, with the Little Butter Latte alone accounting for over 700,000
orders, ra nking among Luckin Coffee’s best- selling products of the year (Krungthep Turakij ,
2024). Furthermore, ButterBear’s pop-up store at Grand Gateway 66 in Shanghai demonstrated
the brand’s strong marketing potential. The event increased the shopping mall’s overall sales by
more than 36% and boosted member spending by 40%, underscoring the mascot’s capacity to
generate widespread popularity and significant economic value in the Chinese market (Hang
Lung Properties, 2025).
This study employs ButterBear —a bear -shaped mascot that has gained social media
presence China—as a case study to investigate how mascots can effectively penetrate the
Chinese market. Characterized by its cute, endearing, and childlike image, ButterBear provides
an illustrative example for analyzing both the features of mascots and the strategies that enable
them to achieve cultural relevance and consumer acceptance in China. The research aims to
identify the cultural and marketing factors that have contributed to ButterBear’s success,
thereby offering insights and practical guidelines for leveraging mascots as strategic tools for
future expansion of brands into the Chinese market.

Research Gap and Objectives
While prior studies have explored the role of mascots in domestic branding contexts—
particularly in Japan, China, and Western markets —there is limited research addressing the
success of foreign mascots entering the Chinese market. Existing scholarship has rarely
examined how cultural adaptation, nostalgia, and digital engagement strategies can be
integrated to enhance cross- cultural branding effectiveness. To address this gap, the present
study focuses on ButterBear as a unique case of a Thai mascot brand that successfully
penetrated the Chinese market. The study pursues three key objectives: (1) to examine the
branding strategies employed by ButterBear in entering the Chinese market, (2) to analyze how
cultural adap tation and emotional branding foster consumer engagement, and (3) to identify
practical implications for businesses and policymakers seeking to leverage mascots for
international branding.

Significance of the Study
This research contributes to the literature by extending the theoretical understanding of
mascots as cultural marketing tools beyond their symbolic function, demonstrating how they
can bridge cultural differences and create sustainable consumer relationships. In practical
terms, the findings provide actionable insights for international businesses aiming to enter
culturally complex markets such as China and offer guidance for policymakers on utilizing
mascots as instruments of cultural diplomacy and soft power.

Review of the Literature

Moe Culture
Moe Culture (萌文化), derived from the Japanese concept of kawaii, emphasizes cuteness
and admiration. This cultural phenomenon has influenced Chinese society in product design,
marketing, and brand communication, with the purpose of fostering emotional connections and
consumer loyalty (Zhang, 2024). The term 萌  conveys a sense of affection toward fictional
characters and inspires consumers to participate in content creation, such as fan art or fiction,
thereby strengthening online communities (Wu, 2022).
Moe Culture began to exert influence in China in the 1990s through anime and manga,
significantly impacting internet-native youth. The cuteness embodied in characters responded
to consumers’ psychological needs for escapism and stress relief. Brands have increasingly
incorporated Moe Culture to attract customers and build emotional relationships (Shao, 2020),
while also stimulating artistic innovation and product design, which form an essential part of the
digital economy (Wu, 2022).
In China, Moe Culture has been localized and adapted to fit social and cultural contexts.
Products and services employing “cute” imagery tend to meet diverse consumer needs and are
frequently used to create strong emotional bonds between brands and their audiences (Liu,
2021). Moreover, the popularity of Moe reflects shifting values, particularly among youth seeking
emotional warmth and psychological freedom (Zhang, 2021). Characters designed as children or
cute animals attract attention because they embody innocence and trigger protective instincts
(Liu, 2021). In fast -paced, stressful societies, such imagery helps alleviate anxiety and fulfills
consumers’ emotional needs (Feng, 2019).

The case of ButterBear exemplifies how Moe Culture can be effectively applied in

Cultural Adaptation Theory
Cultural Adaptation Theory refers to the process through which organizations or
individuals adjust their practices to align with different cultural contexts in business
environments. The theory emphasizes the importance of achieving an appropriate level of
adaptation to build trust and foster effective collaboration. Such adaptation is considered crucial
for organizational success and long -term sustainability, as it reduces conflict, strengthens
stakeholder relationships, and enhances operational efficiency (Li et al., 2023).
Within marketing, cultural adaptation highlights the necessity of tailoring advertising and
promotional strategies to resonate with the values of target audiences. By aligning with the core
cultural norms of consumers, businesses can enhance credibility an d improve marketing
effectiveness (Hornikx et al., 2023). In this sense, cultural adaptation is not merely a practical
adjustment but a strategic approach to bridging cultural differences, facilitating consumer
acceptance, and ensuring brand success in new markets.
The case of ButterBear provides a compelling example of how cultural adaptation theory
can be operationalized in practice. Beyond its initial appeal as a cute and endearing character,
ButterBear’s success in China derives significantly from its ability to align with local cultural
norms and consumer expectations. The mascot has been portrayed wearing traditional Chinese
attire, participating in cultural festivals, and even performing Chinese songs,  thereby signaling
respect and familiarity with local traditions. Such adaptive strategies not only increased brand
credibility but also strengthened emotional ties with Chinese audiences, consistent with the
premise that cultural alignment fosters trust a nd long-term consumer engagement ( Hornikx et
al., 2023; Li et al., 2023). In this sense, ButterBear exemplifies how cultural adaptation can
transform a foreign mascot into a culturally resonant symbol, facilitating market entry and
sustained growth in a competitive international context.

Nostalgia Marketing
 Nostalgia marketing is a strategy that leverages consumers’ memories and feelings of
longing for the past to create emotional connections with brands. This is often achieved through
stories, atmospheres, elements that once held cultural significance , such as songs, toys, or
childhood films (Bansal, 2022 ; Cui, 2015; Holbrook et al., 1991 ). By evoking these memories,
brands can generate positive attitudes, stimulate purchase intentions, and enhance brand
loyalty. Nostalgia provides a bridge between past experiences and present emotions, enabling
consumers to develop strong bonds and long-lasting attachments to brands (Bansal, 2022).
 The case of ButterBear illustrates the effectiveness of nostalgia marketing in building
emotional resonance among Chinese consumers. The mascot evokes feelings of childhood
innocence and simplicity, often compared to the comfort of childhood toys such as teddy bears,

Electronic Word of Mouth
 Electronic Word of Mouth (eWOM) refers to the digital extension of traditional word of
mouth, whereby consumers share opinions and information about products or services through
online platforms such as social media and review websites. These communications may include
both positive and negative experiences (Skiera et al., 2010). In the digital era, eWOM has become
highly influential in shaping consumer purchase decisions, as it enhances brand awareness,
broadens audience reach, and fosters consumer trust while establishing long-term relationships
quickly and effectively. As a result, eWOM has emerged as a widely adopted marketing practice
(Erkan & Evans, 2016; Skiera et al., 2010). Despite its digital format, eWOM retains the same
purpose as traditional word of mouth—persuading others through shared experiences—though
it differs in terms of communication channels, which have shifted to digital platforms (Litvin et
al., 2008).
 The case of ButterBear demonstrates how eWOM can accelerate brand recognition and
consumer engagement in international markets. Chinese users on platforms such as Weibo,
Douyin, and Xiaohongshu actively shared videos, images, and reviews of the mascot, generating
viral popularity and amplifying ButterBear’s visibility far beyond its original promotional
channels. This widespread digital dissemination not only created organic publicity but also
enhanced trust in the brand, as consumers often perceive peer- generated content as more
authentic than corporate advertising. Consequently, eWOM played a crucial role in transforming
ButterBear from a localized Thai mascot into a widely recognized cultural phenomenon within
the Chinese market.

Omni-Channel
 Omni-channel marketing refers to the integration of technology and marketing strategies
that combine both online and offline consumer touchpoints, such as in- store purchases and
online shopping, to provide seamless access to products and services. This app roach enables
brands to engage with customers comprehensively and deliver consistent interactions across
platforms, thereby enhancing customer experience (Verhoef et al., 2015 ). Moreover, omni -
channel strategies allow businesses to analyze customer data, personalize offerings, and align
products or services with consumer preferences. These practices not only drive sales but also
strengthen customer satisfaction and loyalty (Massi et al., 2023).
The ButterBear brand has effectively utilized omni -channel strategies to extend its
presence in the Chinese market. Although the mascot does not yet have permanent retail outlets
in China, it has leveraged pop -up stores in major shopping centers, such as Jingfeng in Nanjing
and Grand Gateway 66 in Shanghai, alongside collaborations with established local retailers.
Simultaneously, ButterBear products have been made available through leading online
platforms such as Taobao, JD.com, and Xiaohongshu , ensuring broad accessibility to
consumers. This integration of physical and digital channels not only enhanced brand visibility

Business Alliance
 A business alliance refers to the collaboration between organizations aimed at
enhancing business opportunities, gaining competitive advantages, and achieving mutual
benefits. Such partnerships are established when multiple firms combine resources and
expertise to accomplish shared objectives, such as market expansion, access to new
technologies, or product development. By pooling their strengths, alliances improve operational
efficiency and competitiveness in the marketplace (Mowla, 2012)
 Business alliances also create opportunities for innovation and high- quality product
development, while reducing production and marketing costs. They enable firms to expand their
markets and customer bases through diverse distribution channels. Moreover, s haring
resources such as finance and technology reduces risks and enhances product quality.
Collaborative partnerships therefore support long-term growth and adaptability, while providing
opportunities for mutual learning (Musa, 2022).
The success of ButterBear in the Chinese market is partly attributed to strategic business
alliances with local partners. A prominent example is its collaboration with Luckin Coffee,
resulting in co-branded beverages such as Little Butter Latte and Little Butter Americano, which
quickly achieved record-breaking sales. These partnerships allowed ButterBear to leverage the
market reach, resources, and consumer trust of established Chinese brands, thereby
accelerating its acceptance in the competitive Chinese market. By forming such alliances,
ButterBear not only reduced the risks associated with entering a f oreign market but also
enhanced its brand credibility and cultural resonance, demonstrating the critical role of
collaborative strategies in international branding.

Influencer Marketing
 Influencer marketing refers to the use of influencers’ reputation and credibility as a
promotional strategy to enhance brand image and increase brand awareness (Mulyono &
Sunyoto, 2025 ). Content shared by influencers can shape consumer perceptions, encourage
acceptance, and ultimately influence purchase decisions (Liu & Zheng, 2024). By selecting
influencers whose personality and lifestyle align with the brand, companies can achieve mor e
authentic communication, which strengthens brand –consumer compatibility and positively
impacts consumer attitudes (Belanche et al., 2021).
Moreover, the personal identity and credibility of influencers play a significant role in
consumer evaluations of brands. Trusted influencers can effectively persuade followers,
enhance brand trust, and foster long -term relationships between the brand and its target
audience (Mulyono & Sunyoto, 2025). As a result, influencer marketing has become an
increasingly important tool for building authenticity and relatability in digital brand
communication.
In the case of ButterBear, influencer marketing has been crucial in amplifying its visibility
within the Chinese market. Collaborations with Chinese social media influencers on platforms
such as Douyin and Xiaohongshu enabled ButterBear to reach younger audiences who are highly
responsive to digital content. By associating the mascot with influencers whose personas align
with its playful and heartwarming image, the brand cultivated authenticity and relatability,

Research Methods

Research Design
This study adopts a qualitative research design with an emphasis on content analysis.
The approach was chosen to examine the marketing strategies of the mascot ButterBear in the
Chinese market, focusing on cultural adaptation and consumer perceptions. Content analysis
enables the systematic exploration of textual and visual data from diverse sources, allowing the
researcher to identify recurring themes, strategies, and cultural patterns related to the mascot’s
success.

Scope of the Study
 The scope of the study is defined across 3 dimensions:
  1. Content Scope: The study focuses on marketing strategies and cultural factors that
influenced the success of ButterBear  in China. It also analyzes consumer responses on digital
platforms, aiming to capture both brand-driven strategies and organic audience engagement.
2. Time Scope: The data collection covers the period from 2023 to 2025, corresponding
to the time when ButterBear began to gain significant popularity in the Chinese market.
3. Geographical Scope: The study is limited to the Chinese market, with primary attention
to Chinese social media platforms and digital consumer spaces.

  The main research instrument was a content analysis coding sheet developed based on

shares, reposts, and audience responses. The collected evidence was subsequently interpreted
through the theoretical dimensions presented in the conceptual framework. Data sources were

ButterBear is the mascot of the Thai dessert brand under the Coffee Beans by Dao
franchise, launched in 2023. The character was designed to embody cuteness, warmth, and
friendliness. ButterBear quickly gained widespread popularity through viral TikTok videos that
showcased its distinctive and playful dance gestures. These clips rapidly attracted attention and
were widely shared, extending the mascot’s reach to international audiences, particular ly in
China (Sanook, 2024b).
The growing popularity of ButterBear in China generated a fan movement in which
Chinese followers traveled to Thailand specifically to meet the mascot. This phenomenon
culminated in a mass gathering at the EmSphere shopping mall in Bangkok, famously described
as “breaking the mall” due to the overwhelming crowds (Daily News, 2024). Today, ButterBear
has evolved beyond being a mere symbol of a dessert brand. It has expanded its role into the
Chinese market through collaborations with leading Chinese brands, promotional events across
major cities (Krungthep Turakij , 2024), and even participation in campaigns promoting Thai
tourism to Chinese consumers  (Thansettakij, 2024 ). An analysis of the brand’s strategies
indicates that ButterBear’s success in China is attributed to the effective integration of multiple
marketing approaches.

and innocence. In China, Moe Culture has become an influential phenomenon in marketing and
brand communication, as it fosters emotional connections and consumer loyalty (Zhang, 2024).
Characters designed with childlike qualities or portrayed as cute animals are particularly
effective i n attracting consumer attention, as they evoke a sense of purity and stimulate
protective instincts (Liu, 2021). Within the fast- paced and often stressful context of modern
Chinese society, these elements of cuteness serve as emotional relief, reducing anx iety and
fulfilling consumers’ psychological needs (Feng, 2019).

simplified lines that emphasize softness and tenderness. Third, smallness is represented in
childlike body proportions that enhances the perception of cuteness. Fourth, innocence is
reflected in playful gestures and behaviors —such as raising arms, jumping, or performing
unexpected actions— that are typically associated with children rather than adults. Fifth,
sweetness is emphasized through the use of bright or pastel colors and delicate design details

contemporary Chinese society, where many individuals e xperience stress and fast- paced
lifestyles, ButterBear’s cuteness also provides a form of emotional comfort and psychological
relief. Importantly, this indicates that Moe Culture were not only design features but also a
deliberate marketing strategy that enhanced consumer loyalty and differentiated the brand in a
highly competitive market. Thus, Moe Culture can be identified as one of the key cultural and
marketing drivers of ButterBear’s success in China, directly supporting the study’s objective of
examining strategies behind its rapid rise in popularity.

Cultural Adaptation Theory
      The strategy of adapting to local cultures and values is a critical factor that enhances the
effectiveness of consumer engagement. Since culture significantly influences consumption
behavior, understanding the cultural context of the target audience allows businesses to design
marketing strategies that align with consumer expectations and preferences. This alignment not
only increases brand relevance but also fosters long -term relationships between the brand and
its customers (Hornikx et al., 2023; Li et al., 2023).
Findings from this study indicate that ButterBear has strategically employed cultural
adaptation to strengthen its relationship with Chinese fans. A prominent example was
ButterBear’s 1st Fan Meeting: Adventure Awaits!, held in Bangkok. During the event, B utterBear
performed the classic Chinese song “The Moon Represents My Heart” (月亮代表我的心) (Douyin,
2024), which was warmly received by Chinese participants. Beyond live events, ButterBear has
continuously adapted its image and content to reflect Chinese cultural values. For instance,
promotional materials featured the mascot reading Chinese texts to express interest in Chinese
language and culture.
ButterBear also participated in traditional Chinese celebrations, such as wearing
Chinese costumes and joining rituals during Lunar New Year festivals, actions that were highly
praised by Chinese fans. Another significant strategy was the production of merchandise a nd
souvenirs reflecting Chinese cultural symbols, including red envelopes (Xiaohongshu , 2024,
2025a,b), couplet decorations, and other festive ornaments associated with Lunar New Year.

In summary, ButterBear’s  deliberate use of cultural adaptation —through music,

Nostalgia Marketing
 Nostalgia marketing is a strategy that facilitates emotional connection by evoking
sentimental memories of the past, thereby enhancing consumers’ sense of engagement with a
brand. This approach uses feelings of nostalgia as a psychological tool to foster a ttachment,
encouraging consumers to bond with brands or products that remind them of childhood or earlier
periods in life when they felt warmth and emotional security (Bansal, 2022; Cui, 2015; Holbrook,
1991). This strategy has proven particularly effective for ButterBear in capturing the attention of
Chinese consumers. As Li Ye’s research suggests, Chinese consumers have long -standing
familiarity with and emotional ties to bears—both historically and symbolically.
Such associations are evident in Chinese classical literature, including Shan Hai Jing (山
海经), an ancient text that records various legends, some of which involve bears. Additionally,
Boom Thanawan, the founder of ButterBear, stated that the brand concept was inspired by a
desire to evoke childhood nostalgia. She envisioned ButterBear as a symbol of simple happiness
from one’s youth —full of dreams, innocence, and emotional warmth —similar to a childhood
teddy bear that comforted many children during formative years (The People , 2024 ). This
foundational narrative enabled ButterBear to emerge as a mascot that feels both emotionally
familiar and nostalgically comforting to Chinese consumers, evoking memories of happier times
and strengthening emotional engagement with the brand.
In an increasingly competitive market environment, emotional connection and consumer
attachment are critical elements that help a brand stand out. ButterBear is not merely a
commercial product but has become a “living memory” in the minds of consumers. The research
findings reveal that the mascot effectively stimulates positive emotions and creates symbolic
connections to joyful moments from the past. This emo tional resonance has led to deeper
consumer affinity and enhanced brand likability. As a result, nostalgia marketing plays a vital role
in shaping ButterBear’s emotional image and perceived value among Chinese consumers.

Omni-Channel Strategy
           Omni-channel marketing refers to the integration of technology and strategic approaches
that combine both online and offline consumer touchpoints. This includes in- store purchases
and online shopping, with the goal of providing seamless access to products and services
(Verhoef et al., 2015). Such integration allows customers to interact with brands conveniently
and consistently across platforms, thereby enhancing overall consumer experience and
satisfaction (Liu & Hassan, 2024).
The ButterBear brand has effectively utilized both online and offline channels to reach
consumers. While ButterBear does not yet operate permanent retail stores in China, it has
established a strong physical presence through pop -up stores in major cities —such as at
JingFeng Center in Nanjing  (SocialBeta, 2024 )—and collaborative pop -up events like the
CASETiFY X BUTTERBEAR exhibition in Hangzhou (Sohu, 2025). Additionally, ButterBear products
are distributed through leading retail chains such as Miniso and KKV (Xiaohongshu), making them
physically accessible to a wide range of customers.
On the digital side, ButterBear’s merchandise is available through popular Chinese
e-commerce platforms, including Xiaohongshu, Taobao, and JD.com. The product range is
diverse, featuring items such as bear- shaped cookies, plush toys, keychains, stickers,
notebooks, tote bags, water bottles, and other themed merchandise. This comprehensi ve
distribution strategy ensures that ButterBear fans can conveniently access the brand's products
through both physical and digital channels, thereby reinforcing customer engagement and
supporting brand expansion in the Chinese market.

Selecting influencers whose personalities and lifestyles align with a brand’s identity is a
critical factor that ensures authenticity in brand communication and strengthens consumer trust

Findings and Discussion

Key Findings
This study investigated how ButterBear, a Thai mascot -based brand, successfully
penetrated the Chinese market through culturally adaptive and emotionally driven strategies.
The findings reveal a multifaceted approach combining Moe Culture, cultural adaptation,
nostalgia marketing, electronic Word of Mouth (eWOM), influencer marketing, business
alliances, and omni-channel distribution.
One of the key findings lies in the strategic alignment of the mascot with the concept of
Moe Culture —aestheticized cuteness associated with innocence and emotional comfort—
which allowed ButterBear to build strong affective ties with Chinese consumers. In parallel, the
use of nostalgia marketing served to evoke warm childhood memories, fostering natural brand
familiarity and emotional attachment.
The brand's ability to localize content in accordance with Cultural Adaptation Theory —
through language choices, traditional attire, and participation in Chinese cultural festivals—
further deepened consumer-brand resonance. This cultural sensitivity not only enhanced brand
relevance but also established ButterBear as a “familiar outsider” within the Chinese social
context.
From the findings, ButterBear’s  cultural adaptation can be understood as a process
involving five observable components: localized communication, culturally familiar visual
symbols, participation in Chinese festivals and events, collaboration with local business
partners, and engagement through Chinese digital platforms. These components show how the
brand adapted not only its visual presentation but also its communication channels, cultural
references, and consumer experiences to align with Chinese audience expectations.
From a communication perspective, ButterBear utilized a blend of eWOM, influencer
marketing, and pop -up retail strategies to create immersive brand experiences. Its
collaborations with local brands (e.g., Luckin Coffee, Miniso) and presence on major Chines e
platforms (Weibo, Douyin, Xiaohongshu, JD.com) positioned the brand at the intersection of
online virality and offline tangibility.

Theoretical Contributions
This study contributes to the evolving literature on cultural branding, character- based
marketing, and emotional engagement by highlighting the underexplored role of mascots as
cultural intermediaries in global markets. By integrating Moe Culture, Cultural  Adaptation,
Nostalgia Marketing, eWOM, Omni -Channel Strategy, Business Alliance, and Influencer
Marketing into a single analytical framework, the study demonstrates how multiple branding
strategies can operate collectively to support consumer engagement, cultural acceptance, and
market expans ion. The findings further suggest that emotional resonance and cultural
localization are complementary rather than independent processes in the development of
successful mascot branding across cultural contexts.

By synthesizing theories from emotional branding, cultural adaptation, and visual
communication, the study positions mascots not as mere marketing symbols but as cultural
marketing tools capable of translating brand identity into localized emotional meanin g.
Especially in China’s collectivist and sentiment -driven consumer environment, mascots that
embody culturally resonant symbols and behaviors can serve as anchors of emotional familiarity
and trust.
Furthermore, the study strengthens the conceptual understanding of transcultural
branding, offering a framework where emotional universality (e.g., cuteness, innocence,
nostalgia) intersects with local specificity (e.g., language use, visual symbolism, cultural values).

Practical Implications
The insights generated from the ButterBear case hold practical value for foreign brands
seeking to enter culturally complex markets like China. The study underscores that:
• Cultural adaptation should extend beyond surface -level localization; brands should
engage in cultural co-creation with local audiences.
• Mascot design should strategically reflect emotionally salient traits that resonate
within the target culture (e.g., “childlike warmth” in Chinese consumer psychology).
• Nostalgia can serve as a bridge between foreignness and familiarity, especially when
tied to shared generational icons or collective memories.
• Multi-channel integration— across digital and physical spaces —enhances brand
presence and consumer immersion.

Importantly, the study offers a roadmap for building emotional soft power through
mascots, especially in a digital landscape where visual storytelling and symbolic intimacy are
central to brand success.

Limitations
Despite its contributions, this study relies primarily on qualitative data from online news,
brand communications, and user- generated content. It did not include direct interviews with
ButterBear’s creators or management team; therefore, the findings reflect observable branding
practices rather than direct evidence of strategic intentions. As a single -case qualitative study,
the findings should be interpreted within their specific context. Future research may incorporate
interviews and quantitative approaches to extend and validate these findings.

Directions for Future Research
Future studies could extend this work by incorporating quantitative methods such as
surveys, experiments, or big data analytics to measure the statistical significance of emotional
and cultural strategies. Longitudinal research would also help assess the d urability and
evolution of mascot branding in dynamic consumer markets.
Moreover, comparative studies across other Asian markets—such as Japan, Vietnam, or
South Korea—would reveal how cultural variables influence the success of mascot strategies.
Further research could also examine how mascots function within AI -generated environments,
virtual influencers, or metaverse branding, which are increasingly relevant in global digital
marketing ecosystems.

Conclusion

 This study has demonstrated the strategic potential of mascots as integrated tools for
emotional, cultural, and communicative branding, particularly in culturally nuanced and
competitive markets such as China. By analyzing the case of ButterBear, a Thai mascot brand
that has gained significant popularity among Chinese consumers, the research reveals how a
culturally adapted mascot —grounded in emotional storytelling, nostalgic appeal, and cultural
symbolism—can transcend national boundaries and build deep consumer-brand relationships.
Through the synthesis of seven strategic dimensions —Moe Culture alignment, cultural
adaptation, nostalgia marketing, Electronic Word of Mouth, influencer collaboration, omni -
channel distribution, and business partnerships— ButterBear effectively positioned itself not
merely as a foreign product, but as a culturally resonant experience. These strategies allowed
the brand to construct familiarity, trust, and affective engagement, which are essential factors in
a market where consumer behavior is shaped by collective memory and symbolic meaning.
The findings contribute to the growing body of scholarship on cross-cultural branding by
positioning mascots not only as visual or promotional elements, but as cultural ambassadors
capable of embodying brand values, negotiating cultural boundaries, and fostering emotional
connectivity. Moreover, the research provides practical insights for brands seeking sustainable
international expansion: emotional relevance, cultural attunement, and platform integration
appear to be important strategic considerations for international brand expansion.
Ultimately, this case study affirms that when branding strategies are carefully aligned
with cultural expectations and emotional undercurrents, mascots may serve as valuable agents
of transnational branding success. The lessons drawn from ButterBear offer a valuable blueprint
for other emerging -market brands aiming to navigate the complexities of global consumer
culture in an era of digital convergence and cultural sensitivity.
