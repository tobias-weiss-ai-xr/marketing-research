Abstract. Generation Z has gradually become one of the most influential consumer groups
in the world today. Generation Z's communication and selection process for brands have
changed significantly since they have been growing up in an era of easy access to the
internet, mobile phones and social media. Thus, the reasons for consumers' loyalty at
present may not be the same as those reported in prior studies of consumer behaviour. Based
on the previous research and cases of well-known brands such as Nike, Spotify and
Glossier, this paper investigates the reasons for Generation Z's brand loyalty. Instead of
using a survey to learn about the behaviour of young people, this paper will investigate
which brands have succeeded in attracting their attention and why they have achieved this.
Based on the above studies, the reasons for customer loyalty are not limited to high-quality
products and frequent purchases. Generation Z consumers will be more loyal to a brand if
they feel that the brand is in line with their values, provides personalised services, fosters a
sense of community and communicates well online. Based on the results of the above study,
this paper puts forward a four-part system to investigate how the aforementioned factors
work together to promote stable brand loyalty. The system will help enterprises better
understand the needs of Generation Z consumers and adapt their marketing strategies to a
digital era in this study.
Keywords: Generation Z, Brand Loyalty, Consumer Behavior, Digital Marketing, Brand
Authenticity
1. Introduction
1.1. Research background
Over the years, Generation Z has become one of the most influential consumer groups in the world
today. Generally speaking, people born between 1997 and 2012 are now about 32% of the world's
population [1, 2]. As more members of Generation Z reach their prime and become financially
independent, many enterprises have begun to pay closer attention to where the young people's
money goes.
Generation Z has never known a time without mobile phones, social media and high-speed
internet; they are not the older generation. Now, people use these technologies to communicate and
learn about all kinds of goods and services, and then they choose to purchase them. Many

Therefore, the idea of brand loyalty has changed. Loyalty used to be associated with customer

1.2.Research purpose and significance
What kind of Generation Z people are in the current digital market, and how does it affect brand
loyalty? In addition to the purchase intention, other factors that may influence consumer emotions
towards a brand over time in this study include authenticity, personalisation, social values and online
participation.
The Study has certain academic and practical values. From an academic standpoint, it
investigates whether the old ideas of brand loyalty are still appropriate for a generation that has
grown up in an era of digital technology. The company will learn some ways to motivate the new
generation of young people to join it and remain with the company longer. Many previous studies
have described the characteristics of Generation Z, but few have examined how these qualities
directly affect long-term loyalty in different areas and abroad.
1.3. Research methods
A Qualitative Research Approach Was Used in This Study. Based on the above, this paper will
examine what has been done previously and compare the three prominent brands of Nike, Spotify
and Glossier. Research papers, market reports and industry publications released from 2015 to 2025
were studied to learn about the general trends in Generation Z's consumption behavior and brand
loyalty.
The three cases were selected because they showed different ways of involving young consumers.
Nike promotes values through the brand; Spotify offers personalisation for users; Glossier has built a
community of customers. Compare the above companies to find some common features and also
learn how different brands have been developed.
The six sections of this paper are as follows. Section 2 introduces the traits of Generation Z
consumers and reviews prior studies on brand loyalty. Section 3 introduces the three case studies,
and in Section 4, a new framework is proposed based on the results. Section 5 proposes future
marketing plans, and Section 6 presents the main contents of this paper and suggests possible
research directions.

2. Generation Z consumer characteristics and brand expectations
2.1. Defining Generation Z
Although different researchers often use slightly different birth years, Generation Z is generally
referred to as people born between 1997 and 2012 [1].
The first difference among this generation is their age, but more importantly, it is also the kind of
environment in which they have grown up. Generation Z has always had access to 3C devices, the
Internet, smartphones and social media since the previous generation. Digital technology has been
around Generation Z their whole lives, and now they use it to talk and learn [6].
Research on Generation Z has shown several characteristics of consumers in this generation
compared with older generations. In general, the trait of these people is a lack of trust in advertising.
Generation Z has grown up in an environment full of online fake news, sponsored content and
influencer marketing, and because of all this, they are often suspicious of a brand's truthfulness and
sincerity. Generation Z does not believe advertisements immediately, and before purchasing any
goods, they will gather information from multiple places [7]. Other studies have found that
Generation Z consumers are also very concerned about a company's values and behaviour. They are
more likely to be concerned about how the company handles social problems and environmental
protection, and such behaviour may change whether Generation Z continues to support the company.
Deloitte's 2021 Global Millennial Survey reported that 57 per cent of Generation Z consumers know
what a company does to give back to society and help the environment before they buy something
[8]. Therefore, the results show that a brand's reputation and values are strong predictors of purchase
intention, and in some cases, the company's values may have a greater impact than the product itself.
Generation Z's Media Consumption is not the same as older generations'. Do not use a single or
two platforms; regularly switch between various devices, social media sites and applications. Many
Generation Z consumers use multiple screens simultaneously and generally obtain information in
short bursts rather than continuously over a long period [3]. As people are in a hurry, the brand does
not have much time to impress them.
Generation Z wants more personalised online life. TikTok, Spotify, Netflix and Instagram all
recommend content according to the users' interests and past behaviour, and algorithms are used to
determine what kind of content each user will likely be interested in. Generation Z is regularly
recommended personalised products online, so they have become accustomed to personalised online
services. Now, people of Generation Z expect more than just fun from brands and products; they
want personalisation as well [9].
2.2. Rethinking brand loyalty for Generation Z
Characteristics of Generation Z affect both their shopping behaviour and the construction of brand
loyalty. Many older consumers developed a sense of loyalty gradually due to habit, convenience,
familiarity and years of good experiences. For example, an older customer may continue to shop at
the same supermarket or use the same bank because it is familiar and trustworthy. Generation Z is
exposed to many other brands and choices online, and as they are simple to switch between, some
members of Generation Z will leave a brand that no longer meets their expectations relatively easily.
They may leave for another company that can provide them with better customer service, more
suitable products and services, or better values alignment. A typical case is that a Generation Z
consumer who receives poor customer service posts about the experience on social media, warns
other consumers, stops supporting the company, and chooses to switch to another brand. Generation

Given the changes in the value system for brand loyalty among Generation Z that have been
noted above, a new research problem has arisen: Are the old theories of brand loyalty still applicable
in explaining how Generation Z maintains a long-term relationship with brands?
Many traditional theories of brand loyalty have been proposed. Oliver's model is one of the most
well-known traditional theories. The four stages of the model for the development of loyalty are
cognitive loyalty, affective loyalty, conative loyalty and action loyalty. Based on the above model,
consumers gradually develop a sense of brand loyalty through repeated positive experiences with
that brand [4]. Oliver's framework is still valid, but Generation Z's sense of loyalty or disloyalty has
been affected by the digital environment at a different speed.
Social media spreads news and opinions of the company very rapidly. A company can face
widespread condemnation within a few hours after taking actions inconsistent with its own values,
and thus rapidly lose the confidence of the public. At the same time, a brand can also form a strong
connection with Generation Z by speaking honestly, openly responding to criticism and feedback,
and supporting causes that matter to this generation [10].
Now, Generation Z's loyalty is no longer solely about customer satisfaction and repeated
purchases of a single brand. Other reasons are that they are real, have shared values and meaningful
online interactions; thus, people feel more connected to the brand emotionally. Although the
traditional theories of loyalty still provide a basic foundation, they need to be extended to cover the
expectations and behaviour of the new generation of Z.
3. Case studies in Generation Z brand engagement
3.1. Nike
Nike is a representative case of a brand that has successfully connected with Generation Z in recent
years. Although the company is most famous for its sportswear, its popularity among young people
stems from many other sources. Nike has always promoted the concept of values and stories to
resonate more deeply with young people and thus gain the support of younger generations.
One of Nike's well-known 2018 campaigns that gained widespread attention around the world
was the one featuring former NFL quarterback Colin Kaepernick. Kaepernick had become a public
figure after protesting racial injustice, so many people thought the company would suffer bad
publicity. Nike's online sales among people under 35 in the week after the campaign rose by 31%
instead [11]. Although some customers were dissatisfied with Nike's selection, many young people
were glad to see that the company would support a cause close to their hearts publicly.
This is a typical case of Generation Z. Recently, people have begun to think about whether a
company's behaviour is consistent with its values, rather than just how good the products are and
how cheap they are. A brand that is real rather than fashionable will gain the trust of the people.
Nike is also using many ways to interact with consumers in the new digital space after they buy
the products. Nike By You can be used to customise a product according to one's own wishes, and
the Nike Training Club app offers free exercise instructions and training plans. The above attributes
will enable the development of an ongoing relationship and expand the scope of the connection
beyond a single purchase. Many Generation Z consumers feel that this continuous interaction makes
the brand feel more personal and relevant.

3.2. Spotify
Spotify builds its user base primarily through a good user experience, not Nike. Based on how a user
listens, the system will recommend other popular songs in a playlist. "Discover Weekly", "Daily
Mixes" and "Release Radar" are all intended to make each user's account unique.
Spotify Wrapped is one of the most popular trends each year from around October to December.
At the end of each year, this function will show a summary of a user's listening history. Spotify has
created many colourful and interactive charts to present the data, which are also easy to share on
social media, rather than using a dull table. At the end of each year, millions of users voluntarily post
their Wrapped results online to spread the word through word-of-mouth without the need for
traditional advertising.
Generation Z wants to be able to show a part of their identity, and Spotify Wrapped can do that.
Music is close to people's personalities, and sharing listening habits has become one of the ways
many Generation Z consumers connect with friends online. Spotify does not inform the public
directly; rather, it has had users participate in the promotion of the service.
According to the 2023 Annual Report of Spotify, Generation Z makes up more than 30% of its
monthly active users and has a higher retention rate than many older age groups [12]. Therefore,
personalisation can motivate people to continue using the service for a long time. Rather than
making it difficult for users to leave, Spotify offers them various reasons to stay and return
frequently through relevant and enjoyable experiences [13].
3.3. Glossier
Glossier is not the same as Nike and Spotify. Instead of promoting large-scale advertising or high-
tech solutions, the beauty brand has inspired consumers to participate in the development of the
brand.
Glossier did not use famous people for promotion at the beginning and featured real users
instead. Influencers and other users of Glossier products have posted about their experiences and
beauty routines on Instagram, shared pictures of the products, makeup tutorials and skincare
routines. As it was based on actual users' experiences rather than professional promotion, it appeared
more genuine and trustworthy to Generation Z.
Thus, the two goals were achieved. By 2019, Glossier had been spreading the word about itself
through word-of-mouth for about 90% of its revenue [14]. Instead of investing a large sum in
traditional advertising, the company had happy customers spread the word for the products naturally
in their own online communities.
Glossier is also popular because many customers feel that the company is developing along with
them. As audiences interact on social media and in daily life, they have been able to share their
thoughts and concerns about the future of Glossier with the company. Thus, they will be more likely
to participate in future activities as they feel acknowledged by the company.
For Generation Z, purchasing a product from a brand may also be about something else. It is also
to join a group of people who have the same interests and values. Glossier knows now that, to
interest the public for a longer period, it should build a participatory culture.

4. A revised framework for Generation Z brand loyalty
Based on the above literature analysis and case studies, the traditional theory of brand loyalty does
not fully explain how Generation Z relates to brands. Traditional factors are still to some extent
relevant, such as customer satisfaction and repeat purchases; however, these two factors fail to
explain why Generation Z is loyal to a particular brand, as they value trust, shared values,
meaningful interactions with a brand, and personalised experiences more highly. This paper puts
forward a new system for investigating the sense of brand loyalty among Generation Z. Traditional
frameworks generally judge brand loyalty based on whether customers continue to purchase the
products, but the new framework considers four key reasons why young people are still supported
by a brand: genuine values alignment, personalised experience, digital fluency and community
participation.
The first is alignment with actual values. Generation Z hopes that a brand will be truthful, open
and consistent; therefore, the brand's marketing messages should align with its actual behaviour.
Generation Z can easily find online information about the company's business, public opinion of the
company, responses to social and environmental problems, and efforts in social responsibility. This
information helps Generation Z decide whether the company supports the values it promotes in a
short amount of time. If a company promotes certain values but does not live up to them, Generation
Z will feel that it is inauthentic and thus lose trust in that brand.
The second is community integration. Many Generation Z consumers want to feel a connection
with a brand, and they do not simply wish for a relationship with a company centered on product
consumption. Build a brand community and motivate users to share their experiences with the
product and post reviews. Glossier is a typical case. Glossier has invited its customers to join the
brand and create some content. This participation gives the consumers a stronger sense of belonging
and also fosters a greater sense of community, thus extending their loyalty for a longer time.
The third is personalisation of experience. Generation Z wants more personalisation now.
Younger consumers frequently use digital platforms that recommend content based on four factors:
algorithmically derived data, individual interests, previous behaviour and personal interests. Spotify
is a typical case of a personalised-use brand. Spotify will recommend music and other content based
on the user's listening habits to give that person a sense of recognition and understanding. If the
brand can meet all the demands of different groups of people, these people will continue to be
customers.
The fourth is digital literacy. Generation Z has grown up using digital devices and online
platforms, and the young people in this generation spend a considerable amount of time on social
media and other websites daily. Therefore, brands need to know how to use the various channels on

5. Future development suggestions
Generation Z is becoming economically and financially stronger gradually. Brands that want to gain
the trust of this generation need to be prepared for changes in their expectations in the future. These
strategies can be used together with the four-part system in Section 4.
First, the brand can use artificial intelligence to create a personalised shopping experience and
inform the user that it is powered by technology. Research shows that Generation Z consumers are
more willing to be included in AI-powered shopping experience improvement or modification
programmes by brands. Customers are reluctant to use AI because they are worried that it may be
too persuasive, controlling, invasive or secretive [6]. For example, a brand can say that a
recommendation is based on what the customer has bought previously. This explanation is more
suitable; it will make the consumer feel that the recommendation is based on their own past
purchase history and is therefore less intrusive than new data collection. Transparency can turn the
convenience of an algorithm into consumer trust; otherwise, without transparency, personalisation
would be met with suspicion.
Second, Sustainability needs to be incorporated into the brand's core product plan rather than
only used for promotion. Generation Z's willingness to buy technology and lifestyle products is now
determined by three factors: the long-term stability of the brand, the trust that has been established
in the brand, and the quality of its online experience. Sustainability is not a sufficient reason for
purchase [15]. Rather than adding a "sustainable" label to the product, the brand can build a reuse-
and-recycle system in the production process, use environmentally friendly packaging, and promote
green sourcing. Provide credible data to support the environmental claims of the company, such as
third-party certifications and open-source supply chain reports.
Third, involve Generation Z consumers in the production of products and stop treating the
younger generation as passive recipients. A typical case is Glossier's community-based model, and it
has shown the advantages of customer participation. Companies in other industries have also
involved Generation Z in the initial stages of product development. Possible co-creation activities
include accepting designs and ideas submitted by customers, holding a design competition, and
letting customers vote for a limited-edition product. The above activities can help consumers feel a
sense of belonging to the group of users and fans of the brand, thereby boosting their loyalty.
Fourthly, Generation Z has expanded the forms of media consumption, platforms, devices and
applications considerably. Therefore, the brand should keep the same identity at all times, and the
following four elements need to be consistent across all platforms: tone, values, core messages, and
visual systems. A customer may use the same brand across mobile apps, AI-driven shopping
assistants and short-video platforms, and these different experiences should still feel cohesive and

6. Conclusion
Generation Z is the new group of consumers. The three reasons for its strong brand loyalty are:
growing up in an era of digital technology, a deep sense of social and personal values, and a desire
for community. Nike, Spotify and Glossier are the three brands of the research subjects. The old
loyalty model still provides some references, but it cannot fully explain the all-encompassing
connection between Generation Z and brands.
The four main reasons are community participation, authentic value congruence, personalised
experience, and digital literacy. This system provides a theoretical foundation for Gen Z's brand
loyalty and offers some practical references for all brands around the world looking to win over the
younger generation. The brand will suffer damage if it believes that loyalty is a type of consumer
behaviour subject to regulation. Generation Z will be more receptive to brands that can evoke a
sense of connection through honesty, purpose, community spirit and shared values. Therefore, a
brand that can build these relationships effectively will be well-received by Generation Z.
This study has the following deficiencies. It is only in terms of quality, and no significant
quantitative data for testing the new framework are available. The three case studies are brands that
originated in Western countries, so the results may not fully represent Generation Z consumers from
all over the world or in different countries.
Future research will explore the loyalty of Generation Z in Asia. Possible areas of research
include the impact of WeChat, LINE, K-pop culture and idol culture on brand relationships in the
Asia-Pacific region. This study may show that the sense of loyalty among Generation Z varies by
culture.
