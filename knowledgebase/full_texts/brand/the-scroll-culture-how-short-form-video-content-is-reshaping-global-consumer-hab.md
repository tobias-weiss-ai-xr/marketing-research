Abstract
The rapid ascent of short -form video (SFV) platforms, such as TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube
Shorts, has catalysed a fundamental shift in digital engagement, giving rise to what is now termed "Scroll
Culture". This research explores how hyper-personalized algorithmic feeds are reshaping global consumer
habits across three multidisciplinary domains: business, cinema, and tourism. Adopting a positivist
research philosophy and a mixed -methods approach, the study utilizes a multi -stage stratified random
sampling strategy to analyze a microcosm of 100 digital consumers. The primary objective is to investigate
the transition from passive media consumption to active consumer decision-making, specifically focusing
on impulse purchase triggers and the "Time Distortion" effect caused by dopaminergic reward loops.
Preliminary findings suggest that the global nature of these algorithms creates universal behavioura l
patterns, where high algorithmic trust directly correlates with increased purchase intent and travel
aspirations. This paper provides a critical roadmap for understanding the future of the creative economy,
highlighting the tension between viral content sustainability and long-term brand equity in a high-velocity
digital marketplace.

Keywords: Scroll Culture,  Short-Form Video (SFV), Algorithmic Trust, Consumer Behavior, Digital
Tourism

1. Introduction
The digital landscape of 2026 is defined by a singular, pervasive behaviour: the "Scroll". Over the past
decade, the transition from long -form cinematic content to hyper -personalized, short-form video (SFV)
has fundamentally rewired the circuitry of global consumer habits. Platforms like TikTok, Insta gram
Reels, and YouTube Shorts have transcended their roles as mere entertainment hubs, evolving into
sophisticated algorithmic engines that dictate what consumers buy, where they travel, and how culture is
perceived. This study investigates this shift by examining the psychological and commercial impacts of
these platforms on contemporary users.
1.1 The Global Context
The rise of SFV represents a "dopaminergic revolution" in media consumption. Unlike traditional cinema,
which requires sustained attention a nd a "fixed -seat" commitment, the scroll culture thrives on high -
frequency novelty and low-friction interaction. Current industry data suggests that the average global user
now spends significant portions of daily life within these algorithmic loops. This shift has massive

1.2 The Problem Statement: Localized Realities of a Global Tool
While the technology behind the "scroll" is global, its impact is felt most acutely at individual and local
levels. There is a critical research gap in understanding how localized cohorts acting as microcosms of the
global population respond to these universal algorithms. Most existing literature focuses on the technical
architecture of platforms, yet few studies explore the direct behavioural transition from "scrolling" to
"spending" within a specific, measurable sample. This research addresses this gap by analyzing a focused
group of 100 respondents to serve as an empirical snapshot of the broader global shift.
1.3 Research Objectives
The primary objective of this study is to decode the mechanics of the scroll culture across three
multidisciplinary pillars:
1. Consumer Behaviour: To measure the frequency of impulse purchases triggered by SFV content.
2. Psychological Engagement: To evaluate the "Time Distortion" effect and the strength of the
dopaminergic reward loop.
3. Cultural & Tourism Impact: To determine how cinematic short -form content influences travel
aspirations and the perception of cultural heritage.
1.4 Significance of the Study
By investigating the intersection of Business, Cinema, and Tourism, this paper provides a roadmap for
marketers and cultural curators in the 2026 digital economy. It moves beyond theory to provide empirical
evidence on whether the "Scroll" is a sustainable driver of economic growth or a contributor to the
"disposable" nature of modern cultural trends.

2.1 The Research Gap
1. The "Authenticity Paradox": While Silva et al. (2025) emphasize authenticity, and Alnoor et al. (2024)
emphasize parasocial bonds, there is a gap in understanding how automated, AI-driven algorithms can
maintain "Human Authenticity" as the creative economy evolves.
2. Sector Silos: Most existing studies focus on either Retail (Business) or Psychology (Culture). There is
a distinct lack of research connecting Short -Form Video (Cinema) directly to Destination Selection
(Tourism) in a single, data-driven framework.

3. Research Methodology
3.1 Research Philosophy and Design
This study adopts a Positivist Research Philosophy, emphasizing empirical observation and quantitative
mapping of consumer behavior. The research desi gn is a Cross -Sectional Explanatory Study, utilizing a
Mixed-Methods approach to explain the underlying mechanisms behind digital consumption shifts.
3.2 The Probability Sampling Strategy
To ensure statistical representativeness and eliminate selection bias, a Multi -Stage Stratified Random
Sampling technique is employed.
Stage 1: Stratification (Platform-Based): The population is divided into strata based on primary platform
usage (e.g., Stratum A: TikTok users, Stratum B: Instagram Reels users, Stratum C: YouTube Shorts
users). This ensures that the unique algorithmic behaviors of each platform are represented.
Stage 2: Simple Random Sampling: Within each stratum, participants are selected using a random number
generator from digital consumer databases and academic mailing lists.
Sample Size: N = 100.
3.3 Detailed Research Procedure
The research follows a five-step rigorous procedure:
Step 1: Pilot Testing: The questionnaire is pre -tested with 20 users to ensure face validity and content
validity.
Step 2: Digital Circulation: The survey is distributed via secure API links to the randomized sample.
Step 3: Data Cleaning: Responses with straight -lining or incomplete entries are purged to ensure data
integrity.
Step 4: Quantitative Analysis: Data is analyzed via Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) to test the
relationship between scroll frequency and purchase intent.
Step 5: Qualitative Validation: Thematic coding is used to cross -reference survey results with current
digital media trends.

4.1 Platform Dominance and Consumption Patterns
As illustrated in Figure 3, Instagram Reels is the most utilized platform, accounting for 82% of respondent
activity, followed by YouTube Shorts (18%). This high concentration in Reels suggests a "Cinema -to-
Scroll" transition where users favour high-aesthetic, visually rich cinematic fragments.
In terms of engagement volume, 77% of respondents spend more than one hour daily on these platforms,
with 14% exceeding three hours. This confirms the S -O-R (Stimulus -Organism-Response) framework
proposed by Hewei & Youngsook (2024), where the seamless scrolling interface acts as an environmental
stimulus triggering prolonged consumption.
4.2 The Psychological Mechanics of the Scroll
The data shows a mean score of 3.22/5.0 for Algorithmic Trust ("The app knows my interests better than
I do"). This indicates a moderate to h igh reliance on AI -curated feeds. Interestingly, the correlation
between novelty excitement and perceived addiction was positive, aligning with the dopaminergic loops
discussed in the introduction.

4.3 Consumer Behaviour and Purchase Intent
A critical finding of this study is the "Trust Gap". While users trust the algorithm to recommend content,
trust in viral products stands at 2.93/5.0. Only 12% of respondents reported a purchase in the last 30 days
directly from a video. This supports the "Authenticity Paradox" identified in the research gap: as
algorithms become more automated, consumers may perceive a lack of human authenticity, as noted by
Silva et al. (2025).

4.4 Tourism and Cultural Impact
The impact on the tourism sector is profound. It is confirmed that 46% of respondents added a destination
to their "travel bucket list" due to a short -form video. This validates Vazquez et al. (2024) regarding
narrative involvement: even a 15 -second story arc is sufficient to trigger long -term travel aspirations.
However, a mean score of 3. 20 on cultural accessibility suggests that while global cultures feel more
reachable, there is a rising sentiment that viral trends make traditional cultures feel disposable.

5 Discussion
The results indicate that the "Scroll Culture" is reshaping the Creative Economy by prioritizing immediacy
over long -term brand equity. The weak correlation between addiction and purchase speed (r = 0.16)
suggests that being hooked on content does not automatically translate into economic value for brands.
This aligns wit h Ahmad et al. (2024), who noted the risk of digital backlash. Brands must navigate the
"Scroll" not just by being viral, but by ensuring an image fit that feels authentic to the user. The dominance
of Instagram Reels in this study further suggests that fo r the cinema industry, the future lies in micro -
narratives that can capture the 15-second attention span of a global audience.

5.1 Key Contributions
1. For Business: Immediacy is the primary driver. Brands must focus on parasocial interaction (Alnoor
et al., 2024) to convert scrollers into buyers.
2. For Cinema: The traditional movie format is being challenged by micro -narratives that influence
cultural identity.
3. For Tourism: Short-form video serves as a primary driver of destination selection for contemporary
consumers.

6 Limitations
1. Geographical Scope: The study utilizes a localized sample of 100 respondents as a microcosm. While
representative of scroll behaviors, findings may vary across different global regions with diverse
digital infrastructures.

7 Conclusion
This study concludes that "Scroll Culture" has rewired global consumer habits by leveraging algorithmic
precision and dopaminergic rewards. However, the transition into a sustainable economic model remains
challenged by the Authenticity Paradox.

7.1 Key Findings
1. Platform Dominance and High -Intensity Usage: Instagram Reels is the primary driver of the scroll
culture in this sample, with 82% of participants identifying it as their most -used app. 77% of users
spend more than one hour daily on these platforms, and 14% are power users spending over 3 hours
per day.
2. Algorithmic Trust vs. Commercial Skepticism: Users generally beli eve the algorithm understands

converting to buyers in the last 30 days.
3. Psychological Impact: The addictiveness of short -form video was rated at a mean of 3 .16/5.0,

4. Multidisciplinary Impact: 46% of respondents added travel destinations to their bucket list due to short
