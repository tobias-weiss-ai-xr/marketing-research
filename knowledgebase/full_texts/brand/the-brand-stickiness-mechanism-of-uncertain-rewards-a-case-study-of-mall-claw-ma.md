Jiarui Lin

Abstract. In experiential retail, uncertainty reward is becoming an important factor to
promote consumption. This study takes the shopping mall claw machine as a case to analyze
the psychological path of repeated consumption and the mechanism of establishing brand
dependence. Qualitative methods were used to conduct in-depth interviews with 15 players
to reveal their participation motivation and cognitive bias. The analysis shows that player
behavior is mainly affected by the illusion of control and the near-cause winning effect. The
former enables players to attribute random results to their own technology, while the latter
enables near-misses to stimulate dopamine release and strengthen the urge to try again;
these psychological effects are also enhanced by offline environment and onlooker factors.
Studies have shown that brand stickiness depends on the behavior cycle driven by
psychological expectations, rather than the product function itself; the research conclusions
can provide reference for experiential business strategy design and consumer rights
protection.
Keywords: Uncertain Rewards, Brand Stickiness, Illusion of Control, Near-Winning Effect,
Behavioral Economics
1. Introduction
A common consumption phenomenon in shopping malls is that many people are willing to throw a
lot of coins into the claw machine to catch plush toys, but the retail price of these toys is often only a
small part of the amount invested; a small doll with less than ten yuan often allows players to spend
hundreds of dollars to catch. It is worth noting that when they repeatedly fail to catch, the player will
not walk away directly, but will try again and again. This kind of high-frequency input consumption
is not consistent with the rational man hypothesis of traditional economics, because according to that
theory, consumers should stop when the cumulative cost exceeds the expected return, but the actual
behavior is not the case, which shows that in these consumption scenarios, people 's satisfaction not
only comes from the toy itself, but also a large part from the psychological level in the consumption
process. Grab the baby machine is actually just a concrete manifestation of the big phenomenon of
uncertainty reward economy.
In addition, blind boxes, card games and promotional sweepstakes on live platforms are also
typical examples of this type; according to the data released by the industry, the market size of the
grab doll machine has exceeded 30 billion yuan in 2023, and has maintained an annual growth rate

In the existing behavioral economics and consumer psychology research, some scholars have
proposed that unfixed reinforcement arrangements and uncertainty of results can indeed improve
consumers' participation enthusiasm to a certain extent. However, there are still many gaps in the
understanding of how these temporary psychological states gradually transform into long-term
dependence and stickiness on brands in a real, multi-sensory offline business environment.
The core issue to be examined in this study is what kind of internal psychological mechanisms do
uncertainty rewards promote consumers' repeated consumption, and how these mechanisms generate
continuous brand stickiness in the physical retail environment; in order to answer these questions,
the researchers used a semi-structured interview method in qualitative research to carefully examine
the various characteristics of players in the decision-making process, the trajectory of emotional
changes in the participation process, and their various perceptions and evaluations of their own
consumer behavior.
From a theoretical point of view, the significance of this study is mainly to integrate the relevant
knowledge of behavioral economics and cognitive psychology to build a relatively coherent
analytical framework, so as to better describe the evolution process from uncertainty to brand
loyalty. From a practical point of view, this study can also provide some valuable references for
business operators. On the one hand, it can help them design more attractive consumption
experience. On the other hand, it can help them identify the boundary conditions that need to be paid
attention to, and avoid the problem of excessive induction or even predatory operation in mechanism
design.
The overall structure of this article is arranged in this way. The second part makes a systematic
review and review of the existing research on uncertainty incentive, cognitive bias and
reinforcement mechanism. The third part gives a detailed account of the qualitative research design
adopted in this study and the screening methods of participants. The fourth part presents some
findings obtained through empirical analysis, specifically related to the two aspects of cognitive
attribution and the reinforcement effect at the neural level; the fifth part mainly discusses the
possible enlightenment and significance of these findings from the perspectives of theory and
practice. The sixth part is a summary of the whole research, and also puts forward some
corresponding regulatory suggestions, and makes a prospect for the future research direction that can
be further explored.
2. Literature review
If you want to understand the reason why uncertainty has that kind of incentive effect, you must first
examine the classic behavioral psychology, behavioral economics, and modern consumer research in
these different areas of some of the relevant theories; the existing literature has actually provided a
more critical theoretical basis for explaining a phenomenon. This phenomenon is that those results
are not guaranteed in advance, and are often more likely to cause consumers to participate and invest
in a higher degree than those predictable fixed rewards.
2.1. Uncertainty as a motivational driver
In those classical economic models, risk and uncertainty are usually regarded as a kind of negative
utility, that is to say, rational actors generally try to avoid them, or they need to be compensated to
be willing to bear these things [1]. However, some results of behavioral research have shown that

under the condition of that specific low risk, uncertainty can actually play a relatively strong
incentive role. Hsee and Shen pointed out in their research that uncertainty itself has an inherent
'incentive attribute' [2]. In their experiment, they compared the two situations of fixed reward and
uncertain reward, and found that those participants who were assigned to uncertain reward
conditions were significantly higher in the two indicators of behavioral persistence and effort. The
amount of output they finally completed is almost twice that of those participants in the fixed reward
group [2]. This research result also means the psychological excitement and arousal state stimulated
by uncertainty. In fact, it has nothing to do with the actual value of the reward itself [2].
On the basis of this research, Goldsmith and Amir further pointed out the role of the
psychological phenomenon of 'optimistic bias' in the promotion activities. In their research, they
found that when consumers are faced with uncertainty rewards with lower risk, they tend to
overestimate the possibility of winning the prize at the system level [3]. This cognitive bias will
make consumers ' evaluation of those uncertain promotion activities far better than the level that
objective probability should support. Moreover, the actual effect of this promotion method is often
not worse than that of the more valuable but determined reward method, and sometimes it can even
reach almost the same level [3].
2.2. Reinforcement schedules and behavioral persistence
The theoretical origin of the behavior persistence that can be observed in the environment of
uncertain reward can actually be traced back to the principle of operant conditioning established by
Skinner [4]. Skinner once pointed out in his research that the reinforcement arrangement of variable
ratio (VR), specifically, is that the reward appears randomly after the participant makes an indefinite
number of responses. Compared with the two reinforcement methods of fixed ratio or fixed interval,
the response frequency caused by VR is the highest, and the participant's resistance to behavior
regression under this arrangement is also the strongest [4].
In contemporary marketing practice, Silk once made a corresponding assessment of the variable
reinforcement methods used in consumer loyalty programs [5]. He concluded that the random and
unpredictable reward structure, compared with those fixed reward programs that set clear thresholds
and the results can be known in advance, can often maintain consumers' enthusiasm for participation
for a longer time, and can also keep them active for a longer time in these programs [5]. The
psychological mechanism behind this is actually that there is a sense of expectation that the
participants will continue to have a 'next attempt may win'. It is this persistent expectation that
makes it difficult for the target behavior to fade [5].
2.3. The near-miss effect and dopaminergic response
There is a relatively core cognitive phenomenon in the uncertainty reward mechanism, that is, the
so-called 'near-winning effect'; according to Reid 's definition, nearly winning a prize means that a
result is very close to winning at the visual or perceptual level, but in the end it is not really
rewarded [6]. Psychological research and neurobiological research have shown that the human brain
does not treat these near-winning events as a complete failure when dealing with them, but will
understand such events as a signal that is gradually advancing towards success [6, 7].
According to the situation revealed by the neuroimaging study, the result of almost winning the
prize can actually trigger the activation of dopamine in the striatum of the midbrain, and the
intensity of this activation is basically the same as the level of neural activity recorded in the actual
winning time. In some cases, it is even higher than the latter [7, 8]. Therefore, the result of nearly

winning the prize does not make the participants feel discouraged or want to withdraw. On the

documented and confirmed in commercial gambling devices, such as slot machines, which are often
intentionally adjusted by designers in terms of mechanical configuration and lottery settings. The
purpose of this is to make the frequency of near-winning events significantly higher than the level
that the natural statistical distribution should have been [6, 8].
2.4. Theoretical synthesis and research gap
Although the existing literature has made a relatively full record of the various specific levels
involved in uncertainty, such as variable enhancement, optimistic bias, and the dopamine response to
the nearly winning prize, etc., there are still two major limitations in the current research status:
Most of these existing studies are carried out in a controlled laboratory environment [2, 3]. In
fact, there is no way to completely copy the complex and multi-sensory real scene in the physical
commercial space, because the latter usually includes background music, changing lighting effects,
social gaze from people around them, and the actual physical interaction between consumers and
equipment [9].
Another limitation is that most of the current empirical research focuses on the immediate
response of short-term, single-participation behavior, so the theoretical explanation of how these
temporary psychological states gradually transform into long-term brand stickiness and habitual
consumption is relatively limited [9, 10].
The previous literature review work actually provides the corresponding theoretical basis and
direction guidance for the analysis framework of this study. This study did not choose to examine
these mechanisms separately, but integrated the control illusion, the dynamic process of proximate
cause winning, and the environmental cues at the sensory level in the specific scene into the same
analysis model. By constructing such an integrated framework, we can better explain the real-time
psychological arousal generated in the physical retail space, and what kind of path is gradually
transformed into long-term retention and continuous patronage of consumers.
3. Methodology
In order to make up for the shortcomings of previous studies based on laboratory environment, this
study adopts the method of semi-structured interview in qualitative research to carry out specific
investigation.
3.1. Methodological rationale
The core theoretical concepts involved in this study, such as 'perceptual control', 'emotional intensity
brought by nearly winning the prize' and 'subjective brand attachment' and so on, are biased towards
qualitative, experiential and highly related to specific situations in nature. If we use that kind of pure
quantitative questionnaire survey, researchers can only measure those variables that have been
defined in advance, and there is no way to capture the cognitive attribution process and subtle
emotional changes experienced by consumers in the process of participation.
By using the qualitative research method of semi-structured interview, this study can use the
players ' own words to present their thoughts and feelings in the process of consumption decision-

questionnaire, but chose the design path of in-depth qualitative research [11].
3.2. Participant selection and sampling logic
When selecting participants, this study uses a combination of purposeful sampling and snowball
sampling. The purpose of this study is to find individuals who have rich and repeated experience in
catching claw machines in shopping malls. In order to ensure that the collected data has a certain
richness and fullness, the study also set up some basic screening conditions. Participants need to
maintain the frequency of playing catch machine at least twice a month for the past six months, and
their total consumption amount in places such as commercial game halls or shopping mall counters
also needs more than 100 yuan.
Finally, 15 participants were interviewed, including 8 females and 7 males. Their age distribution
ranged from 18 to 32 years old. The number of samples is not randomly determined, but based on
the standard of data saturation to make a judgment, that is to say, when the interview to a certain
extent, the new interview has no way to provide new theoretical insights or new themes on
participation motivation, cognitive attribution or brand retention. At this time, it can be considered
that the sample size is enough.
3.3. Data collection procedure
The interview was conducted in two ways. One was to do face-to-face communication in a quiet
place near the game hall, and the other was to complete through online voice calls. Each interview
lasted about 30 to 45 minutes.
The whole interview outline is mainly designed around four themes. The first theme is the initial
driving factor, mainly to understand how the players first came into contact with this thing and what
the reasons for their participation are; the second theme is cognitive evaluation, which focuses on
the real-time cognitive response and emotional changes of players in the face of different results
such as win, failure and proximate win. The third topic is about the attribution of skill and luck, that
is, to explore the player 's own views on the relationship between operational skills and probability
results, which also corresponds to the evaluation of the concept of illusion of control; the last theme
is the formation mechanism of brand loyalty, which mainly focuses on what factors will prompt
players to repeatedly patronize a specific claw machine point or game hall brand.
All interviews were recorded with the participants ' prior explicit consent, and then these
recordings were transcribed verbatim into text materials, eventually forming more than 120,000
words of original text data, which will be used for subsequent coding and analysis. In the process of
data analysis, the researchers used the method of thematic analysis to systematically process the
post-transcriptional text materials. The specific operation steps were based on the six-stage analysis
framework proposed by Braun and Clarke [12].
The whole analysis process can be roughly divided into several steps. The first step is to be
familiar with the materials, that is, the researchers first read the transcribed text content in order to
record some preliminary observations and impressions; the second step is to generate the initial
coding. The specific method is to openly encode the representative citation fragments in the original
text, such as 'complaining about the claw angle', 'feeling a little worse', 'like those clean machines'

4. Findings
Through the thematic analysis of the empirical interview data, the researchers identified a number of
specific psychological and situational driving factors, which play an important role in the formation
of continuous consumption behavior and brand stickiness.
4.1. Skill misattribution and the illusion of control
A core finding emerging from qualitative data is that players tend to systematically attribute the final
results to their own operational skills, rather than to the probability program set by the machine
itself. In fact, the operation mechanism of the common claw machines in shopping malls is mainly
controlled by a fixed reward algorithm. Specifically, the strength of the claw will be electronically
adjusted through an internal counter. For example, only once in every N time will a complete grip be
maintained. However, despite the fact that the machine is such a reality at the structural level, 14 of
the 15 participants showed a strong belief that their operation will directly determine the final
outcome. During the interview, participants described in detail a number of specific operational
'tricks', such as creating an inertial effect by swinging a rocker (they call this 'swinging claws'), or
aiming at certain points of force on the plush toys to lower the claws, or precisely controlling the
timing of the paw drop button. When a certain capture fails, participants almost always attribute this
failure to their own operational errors, rather than to the probability setting of the machine itself; a
26-year-old female participant once said: 'I knew the angle was right at that time, but I was half a
beat slower when I pressed the button. If I adjust the timing after the next coin, and then flick the
claw to the left a little bit, I should be able to tick that label. This cognitive level of error attribution,
in fact, constitutes the basis for the generation of the phenomenon of illusion of control [13]. It is
precisely because consumers believe that the fluctuation of results is dominated by controllable
skills, rather than by random probability, so failure in their eyes is not an insurmountable economic
loss, but only temporary operation shows a little problem. In this case, they will not choose to leave
directly after the failure, but will re-adjust their operation strategy, and then immediately go to do
the next attempt.
4.2. Dopaminergic reinforcement of the near-miss
From the respondents' answers, it can be seen that the most intense moment of emotional response in
the whole game is not when the prize is actually captured, but the scene that is almost captured, that
is, the claw has successfully grabbed the doll and moved in the direction of the exit, but fell off at
the edge of the hole; many interviewees mentioned that at that moment there was a clear sense of
increased physical and psychological tension and excitement. Almost winning doesn't make them
feel frustrated and choose to leave. On the contrary, almost everyone understands the experience as a
visually evidence that the machine is about to win or that victory is in sight. A 25-year-old male

would definitely push it down. From the cognitive point of view, the proximate win is actually to
redefine the absolute failure as a state of ' almost succeed', because the visual confirmation of the
doll 's close to the hole will make the player feel that success is indeed achievable; this
neurobiological feedback loop keeps the motivation level of participants at a relatively high level,
thus prompting them to continue to invest and consume.
4.3. Social embedding and commercial environmental influences
From the qualitative data, it can be seen that the psychological cycle at the individual level, that is,
the strengthening effect of control illusions and near-cause wins, will be significantly amplified in
the physical environment of the game hall; the interviewed players emphasized the influence of
various factors in the space, such as bright lights, victory sounds, stacked dolls, and onlookers.
These factors together create a stronger sense of immersion. At the same time, the existence of
companions or bystanders also brings the dimension of social identity. A 22-year-old male player
said that when someone stopped to look, if he sent the doll to the edge of the hole and then walked
away, it was as if he gave the victory to someone else. It feels good to catch the doll in front of
others, but failure at the hole will feel the responsibility to get it out; this kind of social attention
actually turns every coin into a public display of personal skills, both worried about the machine
they have fed being picked up by others and eager to be recognized by people around them.
Together, these two kinds of psychology greatly strengthen the player's investment behavior from
playing to success.
The above findings illustrate how the short-term psychological arousal brought by uncertainty
reward becomes continuous stickiness to specific places and brands over time. Consumers'
attachment to the brand not only depends on whether they can catch the doll, but also comes from
the perception of fairness and the evaluation of environmental experience. In the interview, players
will distinguish between ' fair ' and ' predatory ' game halls. The former has reasonable claw power
and often wins by proximate cause, while the latter has loose claws and basically cannot catch.
Brands that are well balanced in probability design and have a high frequency of near-cause wins
can better cultivate players ' repeated patronage habits. A 23-year-old female gamer said that she
went to a game room because the machine was not desperate, could not catch at least move, felt that
there was still a chance, although it cost about fifty dollars each time but experienced stimulation, so
she and her friends went to a fixed place every week.
In this industry, the formation of brand stickiness is fundamentally dependent on the
predictability of the experience level provided under the uncertain results [14]. When a game hall
brand can continuously provide that kind of high-awakening near-winning experience in a more
attractive environment, and it is also equipped with good-quality physical prizes, consumers will
gradually form a dynamic brand preference, and then establish the habit of fixed consumption every
weekend.
5. Conclusion
This research has made some progress in the two directions of consumer behavior and uncertainty
reward mechanism; it constructs an integrated analytical framework linking behavioral economics
and offline experiential retailing, showing how individual-level cognitive biases such as control
illusions and near-cause wins, together with physical sensory environments, promote repeated

consumption. The study also shifted the focus from the laboratory to the real game hall scene,
indicating how multi-sensory variables and social variables amplify the effect of the change rate
reinforcement arrangement; at the same time, it reveals the formation mechanism of brand
stickiness, and points out that brand loyalty in experiential consumption depends more on the
structural calibration of uncertainty itself, such as maintaining the appropriate frequency of near-
cause wins, rather than relying on traditional product utility or determined returns.
For people who do experiential retail and gamification operations, these findings can provide
some design references. For example, the machine setting should avoid the binary result of non-win-
lose, but let the player see the obvious proximate win process by adjusting the parameters, so as to
maintain the psychological arousal and retain the perception of fairness; in the aspect of prize
display, we should highlight those physical prizes with good quality and attractive appearance, so as
to reduce consumers ' regret after consumption; in the layout of the venue, the gathering needs of
onlookers should be considered, because the space design that can accommodate bystanders helps to
enhance the sense of participation at the social level. However, although the uncertainty reward
mechanism can effectively establish consumer participation, there are also certain ethical risks. If
the goal of experience design changes from creating surprises to taking advantage of cognitive
weaknesses, the long-term accumulated brand reputation and value may be seriously damaged.
In order to ensure the sustainable development of the industry and the basic rights and interests of
consumers, we can consider referring to the regulatory practices of Japan and other countries and
regions on the blind box and card-drawing mechanism in terms of probability transparency, and
require operators to clearly mark the approximate probability of winning the prize or related
parameters on the machine body or screen ; in terms of consumption ceiling, a digital display screen
can be installed on the device to display the duration of the game and the number of coins in real
time, so as to help consumers understand their own expenses and avoid excessive consumption; in
terms of operational fairness, relevant regulatory agencies can set some minimum mechanical
performance standards to prevent 'zero-grab' machine configurations that have no grasping ability at
all.
This study also has some methodological limitations. The sample range is limited to the claw
machine, so the research results may not be fully extended to those non-entity digital uncertainty
platforms; although qualitative interviews can deeply understand the psychological attribution at the
individual level, they cannot accurately quantify the specific threshold between the frequency of
near-cause winning and consumer frustration. Future research can be expanded in two directions.
One is to use longitudinal quantitative tracking to measure the long-term value changes of
consumers under different types of uncertain reward forms. The other is to do cross-category
comparative research, such as comparing offline entity blind boxes, mobile card games and
commercial lottery activities on live broadcast platforms, so as to establish a relatively complete
classification system.
