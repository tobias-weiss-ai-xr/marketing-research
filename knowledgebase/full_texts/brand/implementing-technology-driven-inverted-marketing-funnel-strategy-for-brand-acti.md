INTRODUCTION
The global MICE industry is undergoing a profound transformation
driven by digital disruption. Trade exhibitions, once reliant on physical presence
and traditional marketing collateral, are increasingly mediated through digital
ecosystems that span social media platforms, e -commerce, and influencer
networks (Getz & Page, 2016; Tafesse & Wood, 2021). For exhibiting brands,
participation in large-scale expos is no longer merely a physical showcase it is a
multi channel event where digital engagement before, d uring, and after the
exhibition directly determines commercial outcomes.
In the Indonesian context, this shift is particularly pronounced. With over
200 million active social media users and a rapidly growing e -commerce sector,
Indonesian consumers increas ingly rely on digital touchpoints to make
purchasing decisions including at physical exhibition events (We Are Social &
Hootsuite,
2024). The Indonesia Maternity, Baby & Kids Expo (IMBEX) 2025, held at
Jakarta International Convention Center (JICC) from 28 –30 November 2025,
exemplifies this hybrid event model, where exhibitor success is contingent not
only on booth design and product quality but on the strategic of digital
marketing strategy deployed across the consumer journey.
Imboost, a leading immune su pplement brand under PT SOHO Global
Health Tbk one of Indonesia's largest pharmaceutical companies with over 70
years of industry presence participated in IMBEX 2025 with a deliberate strategic
objective to reposition the brand as a family oriented supplem ent (not just adult
suplemeny) and introduce Imboost Extra Kids to a highly targeted audience of
parents and expecting mothers. The brand deployed an integrated digital
marketing strategy structured around the Inverted Marketing Funnel
progressing from Awa reness through Consideration, Conversion, and
Trial/Repeat.
Despite commendable execution, the overall sales realization reached 70%
of target, with offline booth sales contributing a mere 15% against 55% from
online pre -order channels. Post -event evaluati on further revealed that adult
products outperformed children's products a finding that challenges the initial
brand communication hypothesis. This gap between strategic intent and
commercial outcome provides a rich empirical basis for examining the
effectiveness of digital marketing in MICE exhibition contexts.
This paper makes two primary contributions. First, it provides an
empirical analysis of digital marketing strategy implementation in an exhibition
setting through the lens of the Inverted Funnel fra mework. Second, it identifies
structural limitations in audience segmentation and digital communication that
constrained conversion rates, offering strategic recommendations applicable to
exhibitor brands in similar MICE contexts.

THEORETICAL REVIEW
Digital Marketing in MICE Events
The integration of digital marketing into MICE event strategy has been a
growing subject of academic inquiry. Tafesse and Wood (2021) demonstrated

that exhibitor brands leveraging pre -event digital campaigns achieved
significantly higher booth traffic and lead generation compared to those relying
on onsite promotion alone. In the MICE literature, digital tools are increasingly
categorized as 'pre -event amplifiers,' 'during -event activators,' and 'post -event
retainers' (Roger s, 2021). Pre -event digital campaigns create anticipatory
demand; during-event digital activations (live streaming, QR code engagement,
real-time promotions) stimulate onsite conversion; and post -event re -targeting
campaigns sustain brand recall and drive repeat purchase. This three -phase
model aligns closely with the funnel framework applied in this study.

The Inverted Marketing Funnel Framework
The traditional AIDA (Attention–Interest–Desire–Action) model has been
progressively supplanted by more granula r funnel models in the digital
marketing literature. Kotler et al. (2021) proposed the '5As' framework (Aware –
Appeal–Ask–Act–Advocate), which acknowledges the recursive and non -linear
nature of the modern digital consumer journey. The Inverted Marketing Funnel,
as applied in this study, adapts this structure to event marketing contexts,
compressing the consumer journey into four stages: Awareness, Consideration,
Conversion, and Trial/Repeat.  Critically, the 'inverted' metaphor implies
progressive narrowing not all consumers who become aware of a brand will
convert to purchasers.
  Digital marketing's role is to reduce friction at each stage while
maximizing the proportion of consumers who progress down the funnel. For
MICE exhibitors, this means deploying stage-specific tactics broad-reach content
at Awareness, credibility -building content at Consideration, urgency -driven
promotions at Conversion, and relationship marketing at Trial/Repeat (Chaffey
& Ellis-Chadwick, 2022).

Social Media Marketing and KOL Engagement
Social media platforms particularly Instagram, TikTok, and Facebook
have become dominant channels for pre -event brand communication in
Southeast Asia. Hudders et al. (2021) established that KOL (Key Opinion Leader)
endorsements on social  media yield significantly higher engagement and
purchase intent among niche audiences (e.g., parenting communities) compared
to conventional celebrity advertising. KOLs derive their persuasive power from
perceived authenticity and audience alignment, factors that are especially salient
in health and wellness product categories (Lou & Yuan, 2019).
In the Indonesian pharmaceutical and supplement market, KOL marketing has
emerged as a primary driver of brand awareness among millennial parents
precisely the de mographic targeted at IMBEX 2025. Research by Sokolova and
Kefi (2020) found that micro -influencers (10,000–100,000 followers) in parenting
niches generate higher conversion rates than macro -influencers, due to elevated
audience trust and perceived relevance..
Online Pre-Order as a Conversion Mechanism
The use of online pre -order mechanisms in conjunction with physical
events represents an emerging hybrid commerce model that is particularly well-

suited to the Indonesian digital commerce landscape. Indonesia 's e -commerce
sector is among the fastest-growing in Southeast Asia, with consumer familiarity
with pre-purchase and pre-order systems deeply embedded in platforms such as
Tokopedia, Shopee, and Lazada (Databoks, 2023). This infrastructure makes
online pre-order a natural extension of event marketing strategy, enabling brands

The phenomenon of jastiper (jasa titip, or 'titipan service') whereby
personal shoppers purchase exh ibition products on behalf of remote buyers —
further illustrates how Indonesian consumers have indigenously adapted digital-
physical commerce to overcome geographic barriers. Ramadhani and Handayani
(2022) analyzed jastiper behavior in Indonesian online marketplace communities
and found that trust, promotional exclusivity, and product scarcity are the
primary motivators for consumers engaging jastiper services at trade events,
underscoring the importance of well-communicated, event-specific promotional
offers as a prerequisite for this conversion channel's effectiveness.
However, pre-order and jastiper effectiveness are contingent on the clarity
of promotional communication. Research by Kusumawati et al. (2021) on e -
commerce promotional framing in Indonesia found that specific, price-anchored
promotional messages (e.g., 'Rp89.000 dari Rp120.000') genera ted purchase
intention scores up to 37% higher than generalized percentage -discount claims
('diskon hingga 40%'). This has direct implications for digital pre -event
campaigns where promotional ambiguity reduces the consumer's ability to make
an informed pre-commitment decision.

Consumer Behavior at Exhibition Events
Consumer behavior at trade exhibitions is shaped by a distinctive
combination of experiential motivation, social influence, and promotional
sensitivity (Kirchgeorg et al., 2021). Studies on exhibition attendance in Southeast
Asia indicate that purchasing decisions at family -oriented expos are heavily
influenced by promotional value, product trial availability, and peer
recommendations—factors that intersect with all four stages of the digital funnel
(Han & Verma, 2022).

METHODOLOGY
This study employs a case study approach supported by descriptive
quantitative analysis to examine the implementation of an inverted marketing
funnel strategy in Imboost’s participation at IMBEX 2025. The case was selected
because it provides a relevant co ntext for analyzing digital marketing practices
within a healthcare brand activation program conducted in a large -scale MICE
exhibition environment.
Primary data were collected through direct participation and observation
during the author's internship as a Brand Marketing Intern at PT SOHO Global
Health Tbk. Data collection instruments included structured sales reporting
(target vs. realization by channel and phase), observational field notes, and
participation in internal post -event evaluation meetings. S econdary data were

  Quantitative data were analyzed using descriptive statistics through

The study focuses specifically on digital marketing initiatives

journey. As a single-case study, the findings reflect a specific organizational and
event context and therefore have limited generalizability. Additionally, the
analysis is constrained by the availability of campaign performance data and the
absence of comprehensive platform-level digital analytics.

Pre-Event Phase: Awareness and Consideration via Digital Channels
The pre -event phase (approximately four weeks prior to IMBEX 2025)
constituted the Awareness and Consideration stages of the funnel. Key digital
tactics deployed included: (1) organic social media content on Instagram and
Tiktok promoting the Imboost Extra  Kids product line and announcing IMBEX
participation; (2) paid advertising (Meta Ads) with audience targeting
parameters aligned to parenting demographics; (3) KOL partnerships with
micro-influencers in the parenting and health niches; and (4) preorder sy stem
was implemented using Google Forms. The effectiveness of the awareness stage
can be seen from the pre-order realization rate, which reached 55% of the target.
This result indicates that the campaign was able to generate consumer interest,
although the  overall conversion remained below expectations. One possible
reason is that the promotional content focused more on general discount
messages rather than providing clear product prices and specific savings
information. As a result, consumers may have been  interested in the promotion
but lacked sufficient motivation to complete a purchase.
The use of parenting focused KOLs also helped increase brand visibility
and reach the target audience before the event. However, the actual contribution
of these influencers to pre-order performance could not be measured due to the
lack of engagement and conversion tracking data. Without metrics such as click-
through rates, link visits, or influencer -specific conversions, it is difficult to
determine which content or influencer generated the highest impact.
These findings suggest that future digital marketing campaigns for MICE
events should combine influencer marketing with clearer promotional
communication and stronger performance measurement systems. The use of
tracking links, dedicated landing pages, and campaign -specific analytics would
provide more accurate insights into consumer behavior and campaign
effectiveness.

During-Event Phase: Conversion Through Omnichannel Integration
The during event phase operationalized the Conversion stage of the
funnel through a dual channel approach: offline transactions at the exhibition
booth and continued online sales facilitated through the jastiper (personal
shopper) program. Total realizatio n during this phase reached 70% of target,
comprising 55% online and 15% offline.
  The disproportionate contribution of online versus offline channels (55%
vs. 15%) warrants critical examination. However, Imboost data suggests that pre-
established digital  intent (consumers who had already encountered the brand
online and pre -committed to purchase) outperformed in -event discovery as a
conversion driver. This finding is consistent with Bonfanti et al.'s (2023)
argument that in hybrid exhibition models, pre-event digital campaigns function
as the primary conversion mechanism, with booth presence serving an
experiential reinforcement rather than primary sales role.
The 15% offline realization is partly attributable to structural event factors
specifically, booth location in Hall A, the venue's lowest-traffic zone. Research on
exhibition design confirms that booth location is one of the strongest predictors

Post-Event Phase: Evaluation and Strategic Implications
Post event evaluation revealed three key findings that fundamentally
challenge the strategic premise of Imboost IMBEX 2025 participation:
First, the best selling product was Imboost Extra Vit C & D3 Effervescent
an adult-oriented supplement rather than any  product from the Imboost Extra
Kids line, which was the primary communication focus. Parental attendees at
IMBEX, while interested in children's health products, may have prioritized their
own supplement needs when confronted with compelling Buy 1 Get 1
promotions on adult products.
Second, the Buy 1 Get 1 promotion was identified as the most effective
conversion mechanism—a finding that validates price-sensitivity as a dominant
purchase driver in the Indonesian health supplement market. This aligns with
consumer behavior research by Ailawadi et al. (2021), which established that
bundle promotions consistently outperform percentage discount offers in
generating trial purchases for wellness products, particularly in price conscious
markets.
Third, the dominance of adult products over children's products suggests
a misalignment between the brand's positioning objective (introducing Imboost
Extra Kids) and the product -communication strategy deployed. While IMBEX's
audience profile (parents, expecting mothers) i s theoretically receptive to
children's health products, the communication strategy did not sufficiently
address the consideration gap the persuasive work required to shift a consumer
from 'aware of Imboost Extra Kids' to 'motivated to trial/purchase Imboost Extra
Kids' in an event setting.

Structural Analysis: Why Digital Strategy Partially Underperformed
Synthesizing the above findings, the digital marketing strategy at IMBEX
2025 demonstrated effectiveness at the Awareness and Consideration stages but
encountered structural friction at Conversion and Trial stages. Four
interconnected factors explain this pattern:
1) Promotional Communication Ambiguity: The deployment of generalized
discount claims ('up to 40%') across social media reduced the strategy's
ability to generate pre -committed purchase intent. Chaffey and Ellis -
Chadwick (2022) specifically identify price ambiguity as a high -dropout
factor at the Consideration -to-Conversion transition. Future strategies
should present specific, product -level promo tional offers supported by
price anchoring and scarcity messaging.
2) Audience-Product Misalignment: While the brand targeted a parenting
audience, the product introduced (Imboost Extra Kids) was newer and less
established than adult variants. Consumer adoption of new supplement
products requires a higher consideration investment than re -purchase of
established products (Kotler et al., 2021). The digital content strategy
should hav e allocated greater weight to product educationincluding
clinical benefit communication, safety credentials for children, and trial
sampling mechanisms to close the consideration gap.
3) Booth Location and Offline -Digital Integration: The structural
disadvantage of Hall A placement underscores the need for tighter
integration between digital and physical strategies. Brands in low -traffic
zones should deploy geo -targeted digital advertising within the event
venue itself (e.g., IMBEX app notifications, WhatsApp broadcast to
registered attendees) to drive directed traffic to their booth —a tactic that
was not evidently deployed in this instance.
4) KOL Impact Measurement Deficit: Without trackable attribution metrics
(UTM parameters, influencer -specific discount codes), it is impossible to
quantify the KOL strategy's contribution to pre -order conversion. This
measurement gap represents both a strategic blind spot and a research
gap. Future MICE digital marketing deployments should implement
integrated marketing measure ment systems that enable attribution
modeling across all digital touchpoints.

Theoretical Contribution: The Digital-MICE Funnel Model
This study's findings support the development of what we term the
Digital-MICE Funnel Model—a conceptual extension of the  Inverted Marketing
Funnel adapted to exhibition event contexts. The model proposes that in hybrid
MICE events, digital channels function as the primary conversion engine (rather
than secondary support), with physical booth presence serving as experiential
validation for digitally pre -committed consumers. This represents a significant
departure from traditional exhibition marketing theory, which frames the booth
as the primary point of commercial exchange.
The Digital -MICE Funnel Model has four implications  for exhibitor
brands: (1) digital pre -event campaigns should be conversion -oriented, not

outset, not reactively; and (4) measurement infrastructure (analytics, attribution)
should be embedded in strategy design, not retrospectively assessed.

CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS
This paper examined the implementation and effectiveness of Imboost's
digital marketing strategy at IMBEX 2025 through the analytical lens of the
Inverted Marketing Funnel. The study demonstrated that digital marketing —
encompassing social media, paid advertising, KOL partnerships, and online pre-
order mechanisms—contributed significantly to overall event sales performance,
accounting for 55% of total realization. However, total performance reached only
70% of target, reflecting structural challenges at the Conversion and Trial stages of
the funnel.
Critical analysis identified four primary constraints on performance:
promotional communication ambiguity, audience-product misalignment, booth
location disadvantage, and inadequate digital measurement infrastructure. These
findings carry implications not only for Imboost's future exhibition strategy but for
the broader field of MICE event marketing, where the convergence of digital and
physical engagement increasingly demands a digital-first, data-driven approach.
According to the results, various strategic actions are suggested to enhance
the efficiency of upcoming exhibition involvement. Product offerings in various
price ranges must be systematically organized and assessed using quantifiable
metrics, such as visitor engagement, lead generation, conversion rates, and sales
results. Distinct QR codes must be integrated into every promotional medium to
monitor visitor reactions and produce dependable information on promotional
efficiency. For derivative products, in itial marketing strategies should highlight
product knowledge, especially its features, advantages, and competitive
strengths. WhatsApp broadcasts can also be used to share pertinent information
and advertise programs during exhibitions, as long as the rec ipients have
consented.
Regular assessments ought to be carried out via surveys with current and
prospective customers. The obtained data can aid in future choices regarding
exhibition tactics, market placement, and customer interaction. Additional
studies are suggested to explore the impact of QR code tracking, product
education, and communication via WhatsApp on visitor engagement, purchase
intention, conversion rates, and the overall effectiveness of the exhibition.

FURTHER STUDY
Future research should empirically test the Digital-MICE Funnel Model in
the setting of healthcare and consumer brands involved in family -focused
exhibitions. Experimental or quasi-experimental approaches could be utilized to
assess the effectiveness of specific product promotions against general incentives,
especially Buy One Get One (BOGO) deals and percentage -off discounts. The
evaluation of the model must utilize quantifiable measures throughout the

marketing funnel, such as message reach, click -through rate, booth attendance,
conversion rate, average transaction value, and repeat purchases over the 30, 60,
and 90 days post -exhibition. This long -term evaluation would offer a deeper
insight into both the short-term and lasting impacts of exhibition-centered digital
marketing techniques on consumer behavior.
Additional studies should investigate the impact of Key Opinion Leaders
(KOLs) by employing trackable links and distinct promotional codes to connect
consumer reactions and purchases to specific promotional so urces. Factors
related to KOLs—such as the number of followers, engagement rates, alignment
between the brand and KOL, characteristics of the audience, and type of
content—must be examined to assess their potential in predicting purchase
conversions. Futur e research could also explore consumer trust, perceived
credibility, and purchase intention as mediators, along with product category
and consumer traits as moderators. These research avenues would enhance the
empirical credibility and general applicability of the Digital-MICE Funnel Model
while aiding in the creation of more quantifiable, focused, and impactful
exhibition marketing approaches.
