ABSTRACT: In this literature review, the important role played by frontline workers in promoting brand loyalty in the services
industry is assessed. The problem being investigated is the absence of research evidence illus trating how frontline employees
affects brand loyalty and the degree of customer satisfaction in the process. It is suggested that frontline employees have a great
effect on customer satisfaction, which in turn affects brand loyalty. A thorough examination of relevant literature sources w as
carried out, cons idering research studies that focus on the analysis of frontline employees' interaction with customers,
emphasizing the importance of service quality and employee involvement.
According to the analysis of existing studies, frontline employees play a crucia l role in the development of good customer
experience, which increases customer satisfaction and loyalty. Besides, the behaviors of frontline workers that promote brand
engagement were identified, including empathy, communication, and problem -solving skill s. These results suggest that the
investment in frontline workers should be considered an important factor in building brand credibility and integrity.
Based on the results obtained, it can be concluded that frontline employees need to be prioritized in or ganizational development
practices.

INTRODUCTION
Brand loyalty plays a major role in business success by helping companies keep customers, build brand value, and increase pro fits
(Chaudhuri & Holbrook, 2001). While product quality and price are still important, recent studies highlight the value of huma n
interaction, especially the role of front -line employees (Hennig -Thurau, 2004). Customer satisfaction is a key driver of brand
loyalty, and front-line employees who work directly with customers are crucial in shaping how satisfied customers feel (Bitner e t
al., 2019). As the main representatives of a brand, front -line staff are important points of contact and have a strong influence on
how customers see and experience the brand (Zeithaml, Bitner, Gremler, 2017). Their skills in providing great service, bui lding
relationships, and solving problems can have a big effect on customer satisfaction and loyalty (Parasuraman, Zeithaml,
Berry,1988).
           Even though front-line employees are known to play a big part in customer satisfaction, there has not been much focus on
how they directly affect brand loyalty. There is still a gap in understanding how the actions and attitudes of front -line employees
help keep customers loyal over time, since most research has looked at branding strategies, product features, and customer
opinions (Kandampully, Zhang, Jaakkola, 2018). This gap is even more noticeable in service industries, where personal
interactions have a bigger effect on customer choices (Wirtz Lovelock, 2016).
              As it becomes harder for companie s to stand out through their products alone, marketers are focusing more on customer
experience to keep customers loyal (Lemon Verhoef, 2016). Front -line staff are key to shaping this experience because they often
interact with customers first and most oft en (Bitner, 1990). Their ability to show company values, make experiences personal, and
quickly meet customer needs helps fight negative views of the brand (Homburg, Jozić, Kuehnl, 2017). The impressions customers
get from these interactions can shape how they see the brand overall, especially now that social media and online reviews have a
big impact on decisions (Verhoef, 2003). Because of this, front -line staff do more than just provide service —they also build
relationships that encourage long -term brand  support and loyalty (Kandampully et al., 2018).
              Front-line employees help build brand loyalty and also shape how customers see the brand’s trustworthiness and
authenticity. What they do has a big impact on whether customers feel the brand li ves up to its promises (Morhart et al., 2015).
For many customers, their only direct contact with a company is through its staff, so these moments are important for creatin g
emotional and psychological bonds.

I. Research Problem: Since frontline employees are a customer's first point of contact with a business, they bear a great deal of
responsibility for shaping the customer's opinion of the brand. Additionally, it is demonstrated that these employees play a
significant role in raising con sumer satisfaction levels, which in turn is recognized as a crucial mediator in the processes of brand
loyalty. There is a dearth of empirical data demonstrating the role of frontline staff in brand loyalty, despite the fact tha t studies
have demonstrated a substantial correlation between service quality and consumer happiness. To what extent do frontline staff
members contribute to brand loyalty? Does the link between frontline staff and brand loyalty depend on customer satisfaction?
II. Research Objective: The primary goal of this study is to investigate and evaluate how frontline staff affect brand loyalty. The
essay will give readers a thorough grasp of the crucial role frontline staff play in providing services and the relationship between “
frontline employees, quality of service, customer satisfaction, brand engagement and loyalty.”
III. Research Variables:  Independent variable: Frontline employee  Dependant variable: Brand loyalty  Mediator: Customer
satisfaction
IV. Research Questions:In this study, the researcher will write a literature review to answer the following questions:
- What is the relationship between the frontline employee and brand loyalty?
- What is the level of customer satisfaction that leads to engagement and loyalty?

A. Frontline Employee:
Services are often closely linked to the people who provide them. In many industries, the service provider is a person. When
something goes wrong, frontline employees act as the link between clients and the company. These staff members should be
directly responsible for producing, delivering, and value co -creating (VCC) because they work where the organization meets the
customer.
     Value co-creation (VCC) happens when employees and customers work together to create value by sharing their resources, like
knowledge and skills. While these abilities help during service interactions, they are not  enough on their own. Everyone involved
needs to be motivated to use their knowledge and skills together, since these resources do not create value by themselves.
Research shows that people are often motivated to take part in service interactions because o f their own needs or experiences.
Expectancy theory says that people put in effort when they expect to get meaningful rewards (Amin, Shamim and Bin Ghazali,
2021).
B. Customer Satisfaction:
In this context, integrity means how someone thinks or feels when responding to a rating procedure. This sense of integrity comes
from what is most important to the person, which is influenced by their expectations, experiences, and what they use for
comparison. The timing of their reaction also affects the integrity of their response (Guido, 2015).
C. Brand Loyalty:
Loyalty shows how strongly consumer attitudes connect to their regular buying habits. It includes purchase loyalty, which mea ns
returning to the same store or buying the same brand, and attitude loyalty, which i s a deeper commitment to a brand or retailer.

LITERATURE REVIEW
I. The Role of Frontline Employees in Shaping Customer Experiences
Frontline employees represent the company w hen they interact with customers and often handle public relations tasks. They
provide services, share information, show how to use products, handle complaints, apologize when needed, suggest solutions, a nd
collect feedback. These actions are crucial for t he company’s success now and in the future. How frontline staff respond to
customer questions is one of the most effective ways to communicate. Customers often trust information from frontline employe es
more than from any other source (Cambra-Fierro, Vázquez-Carrasco, Melero-Polo, 2014).
     In service industries, how frontline employees communicate with clients strongly shapes how customers see the quality of
service and how satisfied they feel. This is partly because services can’t be separated from the people who provide them, and
frontline staff both deliver the service and communicate with customers. Because of this, organizations should focus on manag ing
performance, since frontline employees play a key role in how services are judged. It is also impo rtant to support and encourage
positive attitudes among these workers (Cambra-Fierro, Vázquez-Carrasco, Melero-Polo, 2014).
     Customer satisfaction relies on factors like polite and respectful service, employee effort, product knowledge, clear
communication, empathy, and good problem -solving. To keep customers happy, service should meet or go beyond what they
expect. When something goes wrong, the ways companies handle these issues are called 'service failure processes.' In these
moments, how frontline e mployees act is very important. They need to handle complaints well, apologize, and suggest solutions.
The speed, energy, and sincerity they show can make a big difference in how satisfied customers feel, even if the outcome is not
perfect. Research shows there is a positive link between how employees behave and how customers rate their satisfaction
(Cambra-Fierro, Melero-Polo, Vázquez-Carrasco, 2014).
     Even with major advances in technology, telecommunications, and virtual services, employee -customer e ngagement is still
crucial and shapes the customer experience. Many people still prefer face -to-face interactions over virtual ones, especially when
seeking information, which shows how important personal contact remains. Since human resources are key part s of the value
chain, organizations should pay attention to how people work together and coordinate across functions to achieve the best res ults
and keep customers happy. In marketing, frontline staff have many chances to connect with customers. These mome nts are not
just about how services are provided, but also about learning what customers need, checking their satisfaction, and building
relationships. This feedback is very important for the company’s future growth (Cambra -Fierro, Melero-Polo, Vázquez-Carrasco,
2014).
     As discussed above, employees often represent the company and their attitude can encourage customers to give positive
feedback, recommend the business, or stay involved. When service contracts last a long time, customer satisfaction migh t not be
clear until the contract ends or if problems show up early. In these cases, how employees interact with customers plays a big  role
in whether customers stay engaged. So, in service businesses, a positive attitude from employees can make a real dif ference in
customer satisfaction (Cambra-Fierro, Melero-Polo, Vázquez-Carrasco, 2014).

II. The Mediating Role of Customer Satisfaction
The theory suggests that consumers need to feel satisfied enough to stay with a company, show loyalty, speak well of it, and
recommend it to others. These actions show their involvement and commitment (Cambra -Fierro, Melero-Polo, Vázquez-Carrasco,
2014).
    A consumer’s existing attitude shapes what they expect from a service. After using the service, they compare their expectatio ns
to what actually happened. If there i s a difference, it leads to either positive or negative feelings. Customers then try to figure out
why there was a difference, which affects how satisfied they feel. Depending on what they think caused the outcome, they may
change their level of dissatisfa ction and how they act next. Recent studies show that people make these attributions after noticing
a difference and before feeling dissatisfied. Unlike older models, new research supports a sequence where attribution comes
before feelings and behavior. Fi nally, satisfaction with the service shapes how customers see the overall quality, which then
influences actions like recommending the service, switching providers, or staying loyal (Bitner, 1990).
   Engagement is a recent term in the relational marketing  and customer management literature. The term is conceptualized as the
actions of the customer towards the company, which is read beyond buying and rebuying of products and services. In particular ,
non-transactional behaviour - or disengaged customer behav iour - with a long -term impact on business performance should be
charged to the sales transaction. The strength of the customer's behavioral commitment to the business is known as engagement .
With this bond, this knot helps to both acquire new customers an d to retain the existing ones. Since the customer -company
relationship and the relationship between these are the core of engagement, engagement requires ongoing and persistent
communication, such as across time. Words like absorption, involvement, connect ion, and participation are utilized. In business
interaction, participatory experience and co -creation of the customer are also highlighted. (Cambra -Fierro, Melero-Polo, Vázquez-
Carrasco,2014)
In services, we believe that employees play a key role in both customer satisfaction and how engaged customers feel with the
company (Cambra-Fierro, Melero-Polo, Vázquez-Carrasco, 2014).
   We can therefore say that a positive attitude on the part of the employees in the employee --customer interface in the service
industry setting can generate customer satisfaction. Customer satisfaction has a positive, significant effect on the level of
engagement of the customers, which may yield tremendous value to the company at a non -transactional basis. Finally, we
analyzed if there was any direct relationship between the attitude of the employees and the level of customer engagement. This is
important because it indicates that their interaction with customers directly influences the level of engagement. (Cambra -Fierro,
Melero-Polo, Vázquez-Carrasco, 2014).
III. The Impact of Frontline Employee Behaviours on Brand engagement and Loyalty
When products are very similar and price is the main difference, authentic human interactions can give businesses an edge. A
sincere and personal a pproach can turn a simple contract into a meaningful relationship. Seeing customers as people first helps
organizations meet their needs more effectively (Bowen, 2015).
    Lately, there has been more focus on the value of real human interactions, especial ly when staff are honest during important
moments with customers. Researchers call this “calculated honesty,” which creates a unique way for employees to connect with
customers. Studies show that closeness and genuine care from employees help build custome r loyalty. Losing frontline staff, who
set businesses apart, can hurt performance. High turnover among service workers also leads to worse customer experiences and
can affect how people see the brand and future profits (Bowen, 2015).
      In hospitality, service is key to meeting client expectations and keeping the business running. Customers often spend more
time with service providers here than in industries like retail or banking. In hotels, guests usually stay more than one nigh t and
interact with many  staff members. Research shows that these employee –customer interactions strongly shape the overall
experience, affecting satisfaction and whether customers return (Kandampully, Zhang, Jaakkola, 2018).
     It has been studied that social cues which incorp orate the image of the retail brand act as organisms that positively influ ence
the customer’s cognition . Studies show that social cues tied to a retail brand’s image can positively shape how customers think
and feel about the brand, making them more likely  to engage. In daily life, people form relationships with brands, which then
become active parts of their lives. This relationship grows through ongoing interaction. Many retailers use their store envir onment
to express their brand’s identity (Braxton and Lau-Gesk, 2020)
       Brand personification helps consumers understand complex or unfamiliar ideas, making it easier for them to build strong
connections with brands, much like they do with people. Companies have long used both human and non -human mascots to create
these personified brands. Marketers often use anthropomorphism, which means giving human traits to non -human things, to make
brands feel like social companions. Studies show that people who feel socially excluded tend to prefer brands that seem more
human, likely because they want to feel like they belong. This kind of social connection can make customers feel included,
improve how satisfied they are, and increase their trust in the brand. Brand personification also applies to employees who in teract

IV. CONCLUSION
To sum up, the status of service employees in organizations changed in the early 2000s, especially when value creation became  a
key measure. This change points to a decline in employee status as technology takes over many traditional service rol es. Also,
research in employee studies, management, and HR has rarely focused on how individuals affect customer service efficiency.
While the idea of service quality has expanded, it is still mainly discussed in marketing, where it is linked to customer s atisfaction,
service quality, client experience, and loyalty(Bowen,2015).
