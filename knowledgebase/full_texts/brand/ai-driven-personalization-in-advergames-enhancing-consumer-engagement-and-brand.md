Artificial Intelligence (AI) has the potential to transform advergames by enabling
more adaptive, personalized, and data-driven interactions between brands and
consumers. While previous research has established the effectiveness of
advergames in generating consumer engagement and brand-related
responses, less attention has been given to the distinction between user-
directed personalization and algorithmic, AI-driven personalization in branded
gaming environments. This study examines the intersection of personalization,
interactivity, brand integration, and AI in contemporary advergames, with
particular attention to consumer engagement, brand recall, and ethical
considerations. The study employs a qualitative comparative case study
approach. Four prominent branded gaming campaigns NIKELAND, Coca-Cola
Zero Sugar Byte, Gucci Garden, and Wendy’s Food Fight/Keeping Fortnite
Fresh were examined using publicly available secondary sources, including
company and platform materials, campaign reports, news and industry
articles, performance statistics, and relevant academic literature. The cases
were comparatively analyzed across four dimensions: game design and
interactivity, brand integration and advertising strategy, consumer engagement
and publicly reported performance, and ethical considerations related to
personalization and data use. The analysis indicates that successful
advergames create engagement primarily through the integration of branded
content with interactive gameplay, participation, social interaction, and
immersive experiences. The cases demonstrate different forms of user-
directed customization, social participation, exclusivity, digital collectibles, and
brand integration. These features can strengthen consumer involvement and
brand associations, but the publicly available evidence does not establish that
all four campaigns employed AI-driven personalization or algorithmic
adaptation. The findings therefore distinguish between personalization as a
design feature and AI-driven personalization based on real-time behavioral
data and algorithmic adaptation. The analysis also highlights privacy,
transparency, and consumer autonomy as important considerations when
personalization relies on the collection and analysis of player data. The study
contributes to the literature by conceptualizing AI-driven personalization in
advergames as an intersection of personalization, interactivity, immersion, and
algorithmic adaptation rather than treating all forms of customization as AI.
The findings suggest that brands can strengthen advergame effectiveness by
aligning gameplay mechanics with brand identity and providing meaningful
opportunities for participation, while the responsible use of AI requires greater

1 Introduction
In today’s rapidly evolving digital landscape, marketers are
continually exploring innovative ways to capture consumer
attention and foster brand loyalty. Traditional advertising
methods, such as television commercials, print media, and even
digital banner ads, are becoming increasingly ineffective at
engaging modern consumers. The overwhelming volume of
content and advertisements available online has made it more
challenging for brands to stand out. As a result, brands are
turning to more interactive and immersive strategies, with one
of the most promising approaches being advergames video
games specifically designed to promote a product or brand.
Unlike traditional forms of advertising, advergames engage
consumers by offering an interactive, entertaining experience
that allows them to actively participate with the brand. This
form of advertising has proven to enhance brand recall, build
positive associations, and foster long-term engagement.
At the forefront of this shift is the integration of Artificial
Intelligence (AI) into advergames, which is transforming the
way these games interact with players. AI technologies,
including machine learning, natural language processing, and
predictive analytics, allow advergames to offer more dynamic,
personalized experiences than ever before. Through AI,
advergames can analyze player behavior and preferences in real
time, adjusting in-game elements such as narrative, difficulty
levels, and even the types of in-game advertisements based on
the player’s unique actions and choices. This personalization
ensures that each player’s experience is distinct, engaging them
in a way that traditional advertising cannot. The ability to tailor
content to individual players enhances user engagement and
amplifies the effectiveness of the brand message. Personalization
is becoming increasingly critical in today’s marketing strategies,
as consumers demand experiences that are customized to their
specific interests and needs. AI plays a central role in this shift,
enabling advergames to continually learn from player
interactions and adapt accordingly. For instance, AI can track
how players progress through the game, their preferences, and
how they respond to in-game challenges. With this data, AI can
adjust gameplay to suit each individual, ensuring that the
content remains relevant and engaging. This type of
personalized interaction not only leads to longer engagement
with the game but also strengthens the emotional connection
between players and the brand, fostering stronger brand loyalty
and improving brand recall.
While AI-driven personalization offers significant advantages,
it also brings ethical concerns into focus. The use of AI in
advergames relies on the collection of vast amounts of player
data, including personal information and behavioral patterns.
This raises concerns about data privacy and the potential for
exploitation. Players may be unaware of how their data is being
used, and there is a risk that this information could be used to
manipulate consumer behavior in ways that do not align with
their best interests. It is essential, therefore, for game developers
and marketers to carefully address these ethical concerns,
ensuring that data collection and usage align with privacy
standards and respect for consumer autonomy. Although there
is growing interest in AI-driven personalization within digital
marketing, research on the specific use of AI in advergames
remains limited. While studies have examined the broader role
of AI in digital marketing, few have explored its application in
advergames and how it enhances player engagement and brand
recall. This gap in literature highlights the need for a deeper
understanding of how AI can be harnessed to optimize the
advergame experience and drive greater marketing success.
This study explores the integration of AI in advergames, with a
focus on its impact on personalizing the player experience,
increasing user engagement, and improving brand recall. The
research also examines the ethical considerations associated with
the use of AI in personalized marketing, especially in regard to
data privacy and consumer autonomy. By investigating the
practical applications of AI in advergames, this research will
provide valuable insights into how brands can enhance user
experiences and strengthen their marketing strategies. The
findings will offer practical recommendations for marketers and
game developers on how to incorporate AI to maximize the
effectiveness of advergames while maintaining ethical standards.
To provide a clear analytical focus, this qualitative case study is
guided by four research questions. First, it examines how AI-
driven personalization and adaptive game features shape player
engagement in contemporary advergames (RQ1). Second, it
investigates how the integration of brand elements within AI-
driven and interactive advergames influences brand recall and
consumer perceptions (RQ2). Third, it explores the design and
engagement strategies that distinguish AI-driven advergames
from traditional or static advergames in terms of player
retention and consumer-brand interaction (RQ3). Finally, it
examines the ethical considerations associated with AI-driven
personalization, particularly regarding data privacy,
transparency, and consumer autonomy (RQ4). These research
questions provide the analytical framework for the comparative
examination of four prominent advergame cases and guide the
assessment of their game design, brand integration, engagement
characteristics, consumer effects, and ethical implications. AI-

2 Literature review
2.1 The concept and evolution of
advergames as a marketing tool
Advergames, or branded video games, are a subset of digital
media specifically designed to promote a product, service, or
brand through interactive gameplay. These games represent a
fusion of entertainment and advertising, offering a unique
platform that allows brands to engage with consumers in ways
that traditional advertising methods cannot. The fundamental
appeal of advergames lies in their capacity to create an engaging
and immersive environment where the user is not merely
exposed to a brand but actively interacts with it within the
context of the game. This interactive experience shifts the
consumer from a passive recipient of the brand message to an
active participant in the advertising process, creating a deeper,
more personal connection with the brand.
The rise of advergames can be traced back to the late 1990s,
when brands began to experiment with digital technologies to
capture the growing attention of internet users. The first
generation of advergames typically took the form of simple
Flash-based games, often hosted on company websites or
distributed via CD-ROMs. These games, while relatively basic by
today’s standards, were seen as groundbreaking at the time for
their ability to combine entertainment with brand messaging in
a way that was both innovative and cost-effective. Early
examples of advergames include campaigns from brands like
Coca-Cola and McDonald’s, who used simple, casual games to
promote brand awareness and engage users. At this point, the
main goal was to create brand recognition by offering a fun,
lighthearted experience that subtly incorporated the brand into
the gameplay.
Over the years, the concept of advergames has evolved
dramatically, thanks in large part to advancements in digital
gaming technology and the rise of mobile and social gaming. In
the early 2000s, with the advent of broadband internet and
more sophisticated game development tools, advergames began
to feature enhanced graphics, improved interactivity, and more
immersive storytelling. Brands started creating more engaging
narratives within their games, allowing players to experience the
brand’s story through the lens of interactive gameplay. As these
games became more intricate, they moved beyond simple
promotional tools and began to serve as full-fledged
entertainment experiences that competed with traditional video
games for consumer attention. As mobile gaming emerged in
the late 2000s, advergames experienced another significant shift.
With the proliferation of smartphones and the rapid growth of
app-based gaming platforms, brands found new opportunities to
reach a broader and more diverse audience. Mobile advergames
could now be easily distributed through app stores, enabling
brands to engage with consumers directly through their mobile
devices one of the most personal and constantly accessible
platforms in modern marketing. Furthermore, mobile games
allowed for the integration of location-based marketing, where
in-game experiences could be personalized according to the
user’s geographic location, further enhancing the engagement
potential of the game (Hudson and Hudson 2006).
With the rise of social media platforms such as Facebook,
Twitter, and Instagram, the evolution of advergames took
another leap forward. Social gaming became a powerful tool for
brands to reach a vast audience by leveraging the social
networks of individual users. Social advergames encouraged
players to invite friends, share achievements, and even engage in
multiplayer experiences that spread brand messages through
peer networks. These games were not only promotional but also
acted as viral marketing tools, with the potential to reach
millions of users in a short period of time. The integration of
social features into advergames led to new forms of
gamification, where users could earn rewards, unlock
achievements, and share in-game progress with their social
circles, all of which contributed to enhancing brand awareness
and consumer engagement (Deterding et al., 2011).
Today, the evolution of advergames continues, fueled by the
advancements in augmented reality (AR) and virtual reality
(VR) technologies. AR and VR have opened new possibilities for
brands to create highly immersive, interactive advergaming
experiences that transport players into entirely new worlds,
where the brand is seamlessly integrated into the game
environment. For example, brands like Nike and IKEA have
begun exploring VR advergames that allow consumers to
explore virtual stores or test out products in a fully immersive
digital environment. These advanced technologies enable brands
to offer consumers a richer and more captivating experience,
blurring the lines between entertainment and advertising in
ways that were previously impossible (Hudson and Hudson,
2006). As these technologies continue to develop, the potential
for advergames to create highly personalized, engaging
experiences will only increase, positioning advergames as a
central component in the future of digital marketing.
In terms of their effectiveness, advergames enhance brand
recall, generate positive emotional connections with consumers,
and improve attitudes toward the brand. This is particularly
significant in a world where consumers are increasingly
becoming resistant to conventional advertising methods, such as
television commercials and pop-up ads. By providing an
entertaining, interactive alternative, advergames have the ability
to break through the clutter of traditional advertising and create
lasting impressions that build stronger relationships between
brands and consumers.
Despite the success of advergames, there remain challenges
regarding their ethical implications, particularly concerning data
privacy and consumer manipulation. As the personalization of
advergames becomes more sophisticated, marketers are
increasingly able to track players’ behaviors, preferences, and
purchasing patterns to optimize the in-game experience. While

2.2 The role of artificial intelligence (AI) in
advergames
The integration of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in advergames
represents a significant leap in the way brands can interact with
consumers, offering unprecedented levels of personalization and
immersion. As digital marketing continues to evolve, AI
technologies have increasingly become a central tool in crafting
personalized experiences for users, enabling advergames to move
beyond simple entertainment to become highly dynamic,
tailored interactions that adapt to individual player preferences,
behavior, and engagement patterns. Advergames, by design, are
intended to entertain while subtly promoting a brand.
Traditional advergames employed static game structures where
the player’s interaction was limited to a predefined set of rules
and experiences. However, the incorporation of AI into
advergames has shifted this model toward a more personalized,
adaptive experience. With AI, advergames are now capable of
responding to the individual player’s actions, preferences, and
even emotional responses, providing a more engaging and
immersive experience. This personalized experience is critical in
the digital age, where consumers increasingly expect content
tailored to their preferences and needs.
At the heart of AI’s role in advergames is the ability to analyze
player behavior in real time and adapt gameplay elements
accordingly. AI algorithms can track various player metrics,
such as game progress, choices made within the game, response
times, and in-game purchases, to create a profile of the player’s
behavior. This information is then used to adjust the game’s
content, difficulty, challenges, and even the in-game
advertisements based on the player’s individual profile. For
instance, if a player exhibits a particular preference for certain
game elements or demonstrates a higher skill level, AI can alter
the game’s difficulty to maintain an appropriate level of
challenge (Bleier and Eisenbeiss, 2015). This adaptability ensures
that the game remains engaging, preventing players from feeling
bored or frustrated, which can lead to disengagement.
AI can also enhance the narrative and storyline within
advergames. In traditional advergames, the storyline is often
fixed and does not change based on player choices. With AI,
however, the narrative can evolve dynamically based on the
player’s interactions. For example, AI can adjust the plot’s
direction based on the player’s decisions, such as offering
multiple branching paths or altering the outcome of the game
based on in-game choices. This creates a more interactive,
“choose-your-own-adventure” style experience, where players
feel that their decisions have a direct impact on the game world
and the brand experience. This degree of personalization not
only heightens engagement but also fosters a deeper connection
to the brand, as players associate the dynamic experience with
the brand itself.
The use of AI can also improve player retention and
engagement. AI-driven personalization encourages players to
return to the game because the experience evolves alongside
their actions. As the game adapts to the player’s choices and
actions, it keeps the experience fresh and exciting, motivating
players to continue playing. This continuous engagement is
essential for brands, as prolonged interaction with the game
translates into increased exposure to the brand message. For
example, AI can identify when a player has become disengaged
such as if they stop progressing through levels or show less
interest in in-game elements and adjust the experience to
reignite their interest. This dynamic adaptive nature ensures that
the game remains appealing to a wide range of users, from
casual players to more hardcore gamers, expanding its reach and
maximizing the potential for brand recall. Moreover, AI allows
for more targeted in-game advertising, where the ads presented
to the player can be tailored based on the player’s preferences or
behavior within the game. By analyzing player data, such as
their interaction with certain brands or products, AI can deliver
personalized ads that are relevant to the player’s interests,
increasing the likelihood of purchase or brand engagement. For
instance, if a player spends significant time customizing their
character with certain accessories, AI can present ads for related
products or services that the player is more likely to find
appealing. This form of contextual advertising has been shown
to improve ad recall and click-through rates compared to non-
targeted, static ads.
While the benefits of AI in advergames are clear, it is
important to acknowledge the ethical considerations associated
with its use. As AI technologies become more integrated into
advergames, concerns surrounding data privacy and consumer
manipulation have become more prominent. AI-driven
advergames rely heavily on data collection, tracking players’
every move within the game, from their choices to their
spending habits. This data collection raises questions about how
personal information is being used and whether players are fully
aware of the extent to which their behavior is being monitored.
Additionally, the use of AI to manipulate player behaviors such
as by adjusting game difficulty or offering personalized rewards
could be seen as a form of psychological manipulation, raising
ethical concerns about the extent to which players are being
influenced to make purchasing decisions.
Ethical standards must therefore be carefully considered in the
design and implementation of AI in advergames. Developers must
ensure that players’ personal data is handled responsibly, with

2.3 Advergames and brand engagement:
the impact of interactive gaming on
consumer behavior
Advergames have emerged as a novel and highly effective form
of marketing that combines entertainment with advertising,
providing brands with a powerful platform to engage consumers
in an interactive and immersive manner. This convergence of
gaming and advertising allows brands to connect with
consumers in a way that is not possible through traditional
advertising channels like TV, radio, or print. The interactive
nature of advergames provides consumers with a dynamic
environment where they are actively involved in the brand
experience, which enhances engagement, emotional connection,
and brand recall.
2.3.1 The role of interactivity in brand
engagement
At the core of advergames lies interactivity the feature that
differentiates them from passive forms of advertising. In
traditional media, consumers are passive recipients of messages,
but in advergames, they are active participants, making decisions
and influencing outcomes. This interactivity leads to a higher
degree of engagement, as players invest time and effort into the
game’s mechanics. This deeper level of involvement helps create
a more emotionally engaging experience. Studies have shown
that consumers are more likely to form positive emotional
associations with a brand when they are personally involved in a
game that incorporates that brand. For example, when a
consumer is required to solve a puzzle or achieve a goal that
involves a branded product or logo, the brand becomes more
than just a passive object of attention; it becomes part of the
consumer’s journey and experience. This emotional connection
significantly enhances brand recall, as positive emotions are
linked to memory retention, especially when they are tied to
enjoyable or rewarding experiences (Bleier and Eisenbeiss, 2015).
2.3.2 Advergames as an engaging brand
communication tool
Advergames can serve as a multi-sensory communication tool.
By integrating visuals, audio, and tactile gameplay elements,
advergames create an immersive experience where players
interact with both the brand and its narrative. For instance, a
sports drink brand might develop an advergame where players
must overcome physical challenges, such as running or
swimming, which subtly promotes the product’s benefits related
to hydration and energy. These sensory experiences strengthen
brand presence in a way that a simple advertisement on a
billboard or TV cannot match. In contrast to traditional ads,
which often interrupt a consumer’s activity or offer a one-sided
communication flow, advergames invite the player to become a
co-creator of their experience. This allows for a more tailored
and engaging interaction that fosters stronger consumer-brand
relationships. Advergames provide consumers with control over
their interaction with the brand, leading to a deeper sense of
ownership and connection. This interactive narrative structure,
where the consumer participates in shaping the story or
gameplay, increases the likelihood of brand recall because the
consumer is actively involved in the brand’s message.
2.3.3 Brand recognition through repetition in
gameplay
Advergames not only enhance brand engagement through
interactivity but also through repetition. In an advergame, the
brand is often repeatedly presented in various contexts, whether
as part of the game’s backdrop, within character designs, or
through the challenges the player faces. Unlike a traditional
advertisement, where a viewer may only encounter a brand once
in a short period, advergames allow brands to be consistently
present throughout the course of gameplay. This repeated
exposure increases the likelihood of brand recognition and
creates a higher chance of the brand becoming top-of-mind.
The frequency with which a brand is encountered, combined
with the emotional connection fostered by the game’s interactive
nature, reinforces the consumer’s memory of the brand.
Repeated exposure through advergames is particularly effective
at reinforcing positive perceptions of a brand, especially when it
is paired with a rewarding experience or challenge.
2.3.4 Advergames and long-term brand recall
One of the major advantages of advergames over other forms
of marketing is their ability to influence long-term brand recall.
Traditional advertisements may generate immediate attention,
but they do not always have lasting effects. In contrast, the
interactive and personalized nature of advergames ensures that
consumers remain engaged with the brand long after they have
stopped playing. As players continue to interact with the game,
their relationship with the brand deepens, potentially leading to
long-term loyalty. In addition, advergames have the unique
ability to encourage users to share experiences with others. With

2.3.5 Advergames and consumer behavioral
influence
Advergames are not only about engagement and brand
recognition; they can also influence consumer behavior in
significant ways. As advergames allow players to experience a
brand in a more engaging context, they provide an opportunity
for the brand to shape consumer attitudes and purchasing
intentions. The level of engagement, satisfaction, and perceived
enjoyment derived from playing an advergame often translates
into an increased likelihood of a consumer purchasing the
advertised product or service (Bleier and Eisenbeiss, 2015). The
behavioral influence of advergames extends to how players
perceive the product itself. By incorporating branding into the
gameplay, advergames create an indirect yet persuasive narrative
where the consumer’s actions within the game are meant to
represent those of the real world. For example, an advergame
featuring a car brand may have the player take part in a race,
where the vehicle’s performance is a reflection of the car’s
capabilities in real life. Through this direct interaction,
consumers begin to associate the brand with positive attributes
like speed, reliability, or innovation, which can influence their
decision to purchase the product outside of the game.
2.4 AI in gaming: machine learning, natural
language processing, and predictive
analytics in game adaptation
The integration of Artificial Intelligence (AI) into gaming has
transformed how interactive experiences are delivered to players,
particularly in the context of advergames. The ability to
personalize game dynamics based on individual player behavior,
preferences, and interactions has introduced a new era of
adaptive gaming. AI technologies such as machine learning
(ML), natural language processing (NLP), and predictive
analytics are not just enhancing gameplay but are also reshaping
the way brands can engage consumers through interactive
advertising experiences.
2.4.1 Machine learning (ML) and game
personalization
Machine learning, a subset of AI, enables games to learn from
player data and adapt accordingly, creating a highly personalized
gaming experience. In advergames, ML algorithms can analyze
player behaviors such as choices, playstyles, frequency of actions,
and even emotional reactions through biometric data (e.g., facial
recognition or voice tone analysis) to tailor in-game content.
This capability allows the game to evolve dynamically, ensuring
the challenges, rewards, and narratives stay relevant to each
player’s unique preferences and skill level. For example, an
advergame for a clothing brand might adjust the type of
clothing items offered to the player based on their past in-game
preferences. If a player consistently chooses certain styles or
colors, the game could present more of those options, subtly
reinforcing the brand’s offerings in a highly relevant context.
Similarly, ML can be used to adjust the difficulty level of the
game in real-time to keep players engaged without frustrating
them or making them feel the game is too easy. This dynamic
adaptation makes the game feel more intuitive and enjoyable,
while also enhancing brand immersion. ML can also predict a
player’s likely behavior based on past interactions. This
predictive capability allows advergames to create more engaging
narratives that align with what the player is most likely to enjoy
or respond to. Brands can use this data to craft tailored content,
ensuring that every player has a more immersive and
emotionally engaging experience with the game.
2.4.2 Natural language processing (NLP) and
brand interaction
Natural language processing (NLP) is a branch of AI that
enables computers to understand and process human language
in a way that allows for interactive communication. In
advergames, NLP can be used to enhance player-brand
interactions by enabling the game to respond to player
commands or inputs in natural language. For example, a player
could verbally interact with a brand’s character, and the game
could understand and respond appropriately, making the
interaction feel more organic and immersive. NLP can be
particularly valuable in creating more personalized dialogues in
advergames, in some cases, NLP-powered chatbots are used
within advergames to interact with players, offering brand-
related information, answering questions about products, or
guiding players through the game’s storyline. These chatbots
could act as virtual brand ambassadors, providing real-time,
personalized responses that mimic human interaction. The use
of NLP ensures that the interaction feels authentic and
engaging, as the player is able to communicate with the game
and the brand in a way that mirrors real-world conversations.

2.6 Comparing AI-driven vs. traditional

Traditional advergames often rely on static content and

In contrast, AI-driven advergames continuously analyze player
data to adjust content dynamically, creating a more responsive
and engaging experience. Machine learning algorithms allow AI-

game advertisements based on the player’s interactions. This
dynamic adaptation not only keeps the game fresh and engaging
but also ensures that the experience remains relevant and
personalized, which can increase the likelihood of continued
gameplay. For example, if a player is struggling with a particular
challenge, the AI may reduce the difficulty or offer tips,
preventing frustration and increasing retention.

games consistently show that AI-driven advergames result in

2.7 Ethical considerations: AI, data privacy,
and user autonomy in personalized gaming
While AI in advergames brings numerous benefits in terms of
personalization and enhanced user experiences, it also raises
several important ethical concerns, particularly in relation to
data privacy and user autonomy. The collection and analysis of
vast amounts of personal data from players, including behavioral
patterns, demographic information, and even emotional
reactions, raise questions about privacy, informed consent, and
the potential for manipulation.
AI-driven advergames rely on continuous data collection to
personalize content, such as tracking a player’s gameplay
choices, in-game purchases, and interactions. This data is often
used to create detailed consumer profiles that help brands
tailor advertising and gameplay experiences to individual
players. While this data-driven approach increases engagement,
it also puts personal information at risk. Data breaches,
hacking, and unauthorized sharing of user data are significant
concerns that can harm the reputation of both game
developers and brands involved (Krasnova et al., 2010).
Moreover, the use of AI to personalize advergames raises
questions about user autonomy. Personalized experiences can
lead to more persuasive advertising, where the game influences
players’ decisions based on their preferences and past
behaviors. While this can enhance user engagement, it also
raises concerns about manipulation and exploitation. For
example, if a game consistently adapts to present products or
advertisements at moments when the player is most vulnerable
or emotionally engaged, the game could inadvertently push the
player toward purchasing decisions they might not
otherwise make.
Research by Davenport et al., 2020 highlights the potential
for AI-driven manipulation in digital marketing, where
personalized ads may lead to the exploitation of users’
psychological triggers without their full understanding of the
implications. To mitigate these risks, it is crucial for game
developers and brands to implement transparent data practices
that ensure users are informed about how their data will be
used and to allow them to opt-in or opt-out of data collection
(Seymour et al., 2020). Additionally, providing players with
greater control over their data and in-game choices can help
protect their autonomy and foster a sense of trust in the brand.
As AI continues to evolve in advergames, there will be
increasing pressure on developers to balance personalization
with ethical data practices. Ensuring that players are fully
aware of how their data is being used and providing them with
the ability to control their experiences is essential in
maintaining a positive and ethical gaming environment. In the
long run, ethical practices in data privacy and user autonomy
will not only protect consumers but also enhance brand loyalty
and consumer trust, which are essential for long-term success
in the digital age.

4 Findings
The comparative analysis of Nike’s NIKELAND (2021), Coca-

4.1 AI-driven personalization and adaptive
features
The cross-case analysis reveals that personalization and
interactivity are consistently present across the four advergame
campaigns, but their relationship with AI-driven adaptation is
uneven. NIKELAND, Coca-Cola Zero Sugar Byte’s Pixel Point,
Gucci Garden, and Wendy’s Food Fight all incorporated
mechanisms that allowed players to interact with, customize,
explore, or participate within branded digital environments.
However, the available evidence does not demonstrate
equivalent use of AI-based behavioral analysis or algorithmic
adaptation across the cases. This distinction is analytically
important because the presence of personalization does not
establish the use of artificial intelligence.
NIKELAND primarily employed user-directed customization
through avatars, virtual products, and interactive activities.
Coca-Cola’s Pixel Point emphasized social participation and
digital collectibles, while Gucci Garden relied on customization,
virtual products, and exclusivity. Wendy’s Food Fight

These cases therefore represent different forms of
personalization and adaptive experience design rather than a
uniform model of AI-driven personalization.
The comparison suggests that the strongest common
mechanism across the cases is not necessarily AI itself, but the
movement from passive exposure toward participatory and
individualized brand experiences. AI has the potential to extend
this model by dynamically responding to player behavior,
preferences, and interactions; however, the publicly available
evidence examined in these cases does not consistently
demonstrate that such AI-based adaptation was implemented.
The finding therefore identifies a distinction between the
theoretical potential of AI-driven personalization and its
documented implementation in contemporary advergames.
4.2 Brand integration, personalization, and
consumer engagement
The comparison of the four cases indicates that effective brand
integration depends less on the visibility of the brand than on the
extent to which the brand becomes part of the player’s activity and
experience. NIKELAND incorporated Nike products and brand
identity into an explorable virtual environment; Pixel Point
embedded Coca-Cola within a social and collectible experience;
Gucci Garden transformed the brand into an immersive virtual
environment characterized by customization and exclusivity; and
Wendy’s integrated the brand into competitive gameplay. Thus,
although the execution differed substantially, all four cases
moved beyond conventional advertising exposure by embedding
brand elements within activities requiring player participation.
An important comparative pattern is that personalization
strengthens brand integration when it gives the player a reason
to interact with the branded environment rather than simply
displaying the brand. In NIKELAND and Gucci Garden,
personalization was particularly visible through avatar and
virtual-item customization, whereas Coca-Cola and Wendy’s
placed greater emphasis on social participation and gameplay.
These differences suggest that personalization does not operate
through a single mechanism. Instead, it can function through
identity construction, social interaction, exclusivity, or gameplay
participation, depending on the campaign design.
The findings therefore suggest that the value of personalization
in advergames lies not simply in making content individually
relevant, but in increasing the degree to which the player
actively incorporates the brand into the gaming experience.
4.3 AI-driven vs. Non-AI interactive
mechanisms
The cases reveal that engagement can be generated through
mechanisms that do not necessarily require AI-driven
personalization. Digital collectibles, exclusive virtual items,
avatar customization, competitive gameplay, and social
participation all created incentives for continued interaction.
This finding complicates the assumption that increasing
technological sophistication necessarily produces greater
engagement. Rather, engagement appears to emerge from the
interaction between personalization, game mechanics, social
participation, and the perceived value of the branded experience.
At the same time, the cases illustrate the distinction between
static personalization mechanisms and potentially adaptive AI-
driven mechanisms. User-directed customization allows players
to select among predetermined options, whereas AI-driven
personalization could potentially infer preferences from player
behavior and dynamically modify content, rewards,
recommendations, or gameplay. The four cases provide
considerably stronger evidence for the former than for the latter.
This distinction represents an important finding of the study.
Contemporary advergames can deliver highly individualized and
immersive experiences without necessarily demonstrating the
algorithmic adaptation associated with AI-driven
personalization. Consequently, the effectiveness of an advergame
cannot be attributed to AI solely because it incorporates
personalization or interactive technologies.

4.5 Ethical implications of AI-driven
personalization
The ethical analysis of the four cases reveals a significant gap
between the theoretical concerns associated with AI-driven
personalization and the transparency of publicly available
information concerning how personalization is implemented.
Across NIKELAND, Coca-Cola Zero Sugar Byte’s Pixel Point,
Gucci Garden, and Wendy’s Food Fight, players were provided
with opportunities for customization, participation, social
interaction, or competitive engagement. These mechanisms
demonstrate varying degrees of user control over the immediate
game experience. However, the cases provide considerably less
information concerning user control over the behavioral data
potentially generated through these interactions.
A central finding is therefore the distinction between
experiential control and data control. Players may have
considerable agency in selecting avatars, activities, virtual
products, collectibles, or modes of participation, while having
limited visibility into how information generated through these
interactions may be collected, analyzed, stored, or used. This
distinction becomes particularly important when personalization
is AI-driven because algorithmic adaptation may depend on
behavioral information that is not directly visible to the player.
Data transparency represents the first major ethical issue
identified across the cases. The publicly available evidence does
not consistently provide sufficient information to determine
whether player behavior was analyzed for personalized
advertising or algorithmic adaptation. The absence of
documented information does not establish that such practices
did not occur; rather, it limits the ability to assess the
transparency of the campaigns’ data practices.
Consent represents a second concern. Because advergames
combine entertainment and advertising, users may participate
primarily for the gaming experience rather than with an explicit
awareness of the commercial and data-related dimensions of
their participation. Where behavioral information is
subsequently used for personalization, meaningful consent
requires that users understand the relevant data practices rather
than simply agreeing to participate in the game.
User control and autonomy constitute a third concern. The
cases demonstrate considerable control over aspects of gameplay
and personalization, but the available evidence is insufficient to
determine whether players had equivalent control over data
collection or algorithmic personalization. This distinction is
particularly relevant because increasingly adaptive
personalization may increase the persuasive capacity of
advertising by responding to individual behavioral patterns.
The cross-case findings therefore suggest that ethical
evaluation of AI-driven advergames should extend beyond the
question of whether personalization is effective. It should also
consider whether users understand when personalization occurs,
what information contributes to that personalization, whether
meaningful consent is obtained, and whether users retain
meaningful control over their participation and data.

5 Discussion
The findings of this study provide a more differentiated
understanding of personalization, interactivity, and artificial
intelligence within contemporary advergames. Across the four
cases, personalization and interactive engagement were
consistently evident, but the extent to which these experiences
were demonstrably driven by artificial intelligence varied
considerably. This distinction is important because existing
literature frequently discusses AI-driven personalization,
interactivity, and consumer engagement as closely
interconnected processes, while the empirical evidence examined
in this study suggests that personalization can be achieved
through user choice, customization, social participation,
immersive environments, and game mechanics without
necessarily involving documented AI-based adaptation.
The findings suggest that the relationship between AI and
advergame engagement should not be conceptualized simply as
AI → personalization → engagement. Instead, engagement
emerges through the interaction of several mechanisms, with AI
potentially functioning as an additional adaptive layer within an
already interactive and personalized experience. This
interpretation provides the basis for addressing the four research
questions and developing the study’s theoretical contribution.
5.1 AI-driven personalization, adaptive

The first research question examined the extent to which

technologically advanced. Rather, AI should be understood as
potentially enhancing existing engagement mechanisms by

contribution of AI therefore depends on how effectively it is
integrated with game design, player agency, social interaction,
and brand relevance.

5.5 Theoretical contribution: the AI-
enabled advergame personalization
continuum
The principal theoretical contribution of this study is the
development of a conceptual distinction between user-directed
customization, interactive personalization, and AI-driven
personalization. Existing research has established important
relationships between personalization, interactivity, engagement,
and artificial intelligence, but these concepts are frequently treated
as closely connected without sufficiently distinguishing the
technological mechanisms through which personalization
is produced.
Based on the cross-case findings, this study proposes the AI-
Enabled Advergame Personalization Continuum:
User-Directed Customization → Interactive Personalization →
AI-Driven Adaptive Personalization → Personalized Engagement
→ Brand and Consumer Outcomes
At the first level, user-directed customization occurs when
players actively select or modify elements of the advergame
experience, including avatars, virtual products, collectibles,
activities, or other available options. At the second level,
interactive personalization emerges through participation.
Players construct individualized experiences through exploration,
social interaction, competition, immersion, identity expression,
and other game mechanics. Personalization at this level is
produced primarily through player agency and interactive
design. At the third level, AI-driven adaptive personalization
introduces algorithmic responsiveness. AI systems have the
potential to analyze behavioral or preference-related information
and use these signals to dynamically modify content,
recommendations, rewards, gameplay, or advertising experiences.
The continuum therefore conceptualizes AI not as a
prerequisite for personalization but as an adaptive technological
layer that can extend interactive personalization. This distinction
represents an important theoretical refinement because it
prevents all forms of customization and interactivity from being
categorized as AI-driven personalization. The findings further
suggest that movement along this continuum does not imply
that greater technological sophistication will automatically
produce greater engagement. Instead, the effectiveness of AI-
driven personalization depends on its integration with
meaningful gameplay, player agency, social interaction, and
brand relevance. AI may enhance responsiveness, but it does not
replace the fundamental role of game design in creating an
engaging advertising experience.
The study consequently proposes four theoretical
propositions:
Proposition 1: Personalization in advergames does not
necessarily require artificial intelligence; AI represents an
additional technological layer that enables personalization to
become dynamically adaptive.
Proposition 2: The potential contribution of AI-driven
personalization to player engagement depends on its
integration with meaningful gameplay, player agency, social
interaction, and brand relevance rather than on technological
sophistication alone.
Proposition 3: Personalization is more likely to strengthen
brand engagement when personalized or adaptive features are
embedded within the core gameplay experience rather than
presented as a separate advertising layer.
Proposition 4: As personalization becomes increasingly
dependent on behavioral data and algorithmic adaptation, its
potential engagement benefits are accompanied by greater
requirements for transparency, privacy protection, and
preservation of consumer autonomy.
Together, these propositions extend the theoretical discussion of
AI-driven advertising by positioning artificial intelligence within,
rather than outside, the broader process of interactive
advergame personalization.

Existing research has established relationships between
advergame characteristics, game involvement, gamification,
experiential satisfaction, brand engagement, memory, and
consumer responses. At the same time, the integration of
artificial intelligence into advertising creates new possibilities for
adaptive personalization that remain insufficiently examined
within the specific context of advergames. The present study
therefore identifies a conceptual and empirical gap between the
established literature on advergames and the emerging literature
on AI-enabled personalization.
The comparative analysis further suggests that the effectiveness
of contemporary advergames cannot be attributed to interactivity or
personalization alone. Rather, engagement appears to emerge from
the alignment of game mechanics, brand identity, user
participation, social interaction, and the degree to which the
branded experience provides meaningful value to the player. This
finding extends the discussion of advergaming beyond the
assumption that greater technological sophistication necessarily
produces greater advertising effectiveness. Instead, technology
appears most valuable when it supports a coherent and relevant
consumer experience. The study also identifies important ethical
considerations associated with the potential development of AI-
driven advergames. Greater personalization may require the
collection and analysis of increasingly detailed behavioral
information, raising concerns regarding data transparency,
informed consent, privacy, algorithmic transparency, and
consumer autonomy. The case analysis reveals an important
distinction between control over the game experience and control
over the data generated through that experience. Although players
may have considerable freedom to customize avatars, participate
in activities, select digital items, or interact with other users, the
available public evidence does not consistently establish how
behavioral data are collected, analyzed, or used for
personalization. This limitation underscores the need for greater
transparency as AI-enabled advertising becomes more adaptive.
From a theoretical perspective, the study proposes that AI-
driven advergames should be understood not simply as a
technological extension of traditional advergames but as an
intersection of personalization, interactivity, immersion, and
algorithmic adaptation. The value of this perspective lies in
distinguishing observable interactive and personalized features
from AI-specific mechanisms and in recognizing that
technological adaptation must be evaluated alongside consumer
engagement and ethical considerations. From a practical
perspective, brands seeking to develop AI-enabled advergames
should prioritize the alignment of adaptive technologies with
meaningful gameplay and authentic brand narratives rather than
adopting AI solely as a technological enhancement.
Personalization should provide recognizable value to users while
maintaining transparency concerning data practices and
preserving meaningful consumer control.
The study is subject to several limitations. First, the analysis
relies on publicly available secondary sources, including company
materials, campaign reports, news coverage, platform information,
and academic literature. Consequently, internal algorithmic
processes, proprietary data practices, and campaign-level
personalization mechanisms could not be independently verified.
Second, the qualitative comparative case-study design does not
permit statistical generalization or causal claims regarding the
effectiveness of AI-driven personalization. Third, the selected
cases represent prominent campaigns and therefore may not
capture the full diversity of contemporary advergaming practices.
Future resear ch should address these limitations through
empirical investigation of AI-enabled advergames, including
experimental studies comparing AI-adaptive and non-
adaptive experiences, longitudinal resear ch examining
engagement and retention, and user studies investigating
percep tions of personalization, transpar ency, privacy , and
autonomy. Future research should also examine how these
effects vary across cultures, age groups, platforms, and levels
of technological familiarity. Such resear ch would help
establish whether and under what conditions AI-driven
personalization provides measurable advantages over
conventional advergame design while ensuring that
technological innova tion remains consistent with responsible
and consumer-centered advertising practice.
