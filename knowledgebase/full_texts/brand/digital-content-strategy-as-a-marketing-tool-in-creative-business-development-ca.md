 Abstract: This study aims to analyze Merona Spa ce's
digital content strategy, identify factors influencing
participants' decisions to attend workshops, and examine
its role in creative business development. The study used a
qualitative approach with a case study method involving 12
workshop participant s and 1 –2 representatives from
previously collaborated venues, selected purposively based
on data saturation. Data were collected through in -depth
interviews, participant observation, and documentation,
and analyzed using the interactive model of Miles,
Huberman, and Saldaña, supported by source
triangulation, technical triangulation, and expert
validation. The results show that Merona Space's digital
content strategy involved planning, implementation, and
evaluation, with Instagram as the main platform supported
by TikTok, Threads, and Instagram Ads. Participants'
decisions were influenced by friend recommendations,
digital content, workshop benefits, price, personal
motivation, and participation experience. Digital content
supported participants' interest and confidence and
contributed to collaborations and community
strengthening in creative business development.

Introduction
Technological advancements have made social media a crucial marketing tool for creative
businesses. According to We Are Social (2024), Indonesia has over 167 million active social media
users, with Instagram being the most widely used platform by consumers, accounting for 92% of users
(Databoks, 2025). This demonstrates Instagram's strategic potential as a marketing medium due to its
ability to engage and influence consumer decisions through visual content (Chaffey & Ellis-Chadwick,
2022).
The growth of the creative workshop industry post-pandemic has been supported by government
policies and increasing public interest in skills development. In this context, Merona Space in Bandung
City utilizes Instagram as its primary marketing medium and has hosted approx imately 14 workshops
with over 250 participants since its inception in April 2025.

Instagram account remains relatively low compared to similar creative spaces, both in terms of follower

This study aims to analyze Merona Space's digital content strategy, identify factors influencing

Merona Space's creative business development.

Research methods
This research uses a qualitative approach with a case study method to gain an in -depth
understanding of Merona Space's digital content strategy as a marketing tool for workshops and creative
business development. Qualitative research was chosen because it is based on natural conditions, with
the researcher as the primary instrument for data collection and analysis  (Sugiyono, 2022). The case
study was used because the research focuses on one specific case, the Merona Space brand, to
comprehensively understand the phenomenon of digital content utilization in workshop-based creative
businesses in a real -life context ( Yin, 2018). The research approach is inductive, so the analysis is
conducted based on field findings which are then interpreted using relevant theories.
The research subjects were selected using purposive sampling based on the consideration that
informants have direct experience and involvement with the phenomenon under study (Sugiyono, 2022).
The number of informants was determined based on the principle of data saturation (Creswell & Poth,
2018). The informants consisted of 12 Merona Space workshop partic ipants representing the Makeup
Class, Baking Class, and Creative Class, as well as one to two venues that had collaborated with Merona
Space. The research object was the digital content strategy of the Instagram account @merona_space as
a marketing medium for the workshop and supporting the development of creative businesses.
The primary research instrument was the researcher (human instrument) supported by semi -
structured interview guidelines, observation sheets, a recording device, a camera, field notes, and
research documentation (Sugiyono, 2022; Moleong, 2021). Data were collected through in -depth
interviews, participant observation, and documentation (Moleong, 2021; Yin, 2018). Interviews were
conducted with workshop participants and venue staff to expl ore perceptions regarding digital content
strategies and factors influencing the decision to attend the workshop. Observations were made of the
workshop implementation and the Instagram content of @merona_space, while documentation included
Instagram posts, promotional materials, activity photos, and other supporting documents.
Data analysis used Miles et al.'s (2020) interactive analysis model, which includes four stages:
data collection, data condensation, data presentation, and conclusion drawing or veri fication. Analysis
was conducted continuously throughout the research process by grouping data based on the research
focus, namely digital content strategy, factors influencing participants' decisions to attend workshops,
and the role of digital content in creative business development. Data validity was strengthened through
source triangulation, technical triangulation, and expert validation to ensure the credibility of the
research results (Sugiyono, 2022; Creswell & Poth, 2018).
The research procedure be gan with determining the research focus and selecting informants,
followed by data collection through interviews, observation, and documentation. The collected data
were then analyzed in stages using the Miles et al. (2020) model. Verification was then con ducted
through triangulation and expert validation before drawing conclusions that answered the research
objectives regarding digital content strategy, participants' decision -making factors for attending the
workshop, and the role of digital content in the development of Merona Space's creative business.

Results and Discussion
Merona Space's Digital Content Strategy for Workshop Marketing
1. Digital Content Strategy Planning
Planning is the initial step Merona  Space undertakes before developing and publishing
digital content. Based on interviews with key informants, the content strategy planning process isn't
a spontaneous process, but rather begins with defining the workshop concept. Determining the
workshop theme serves as the basis for developing content, identifying target participants, selecting
promotional media, and crafting the message to be conveyed to potential participants.
Based on interviews with Key Informants (IK), the workshop planning process was carried
out jointly by the two founders, Sofina Fahlan and Ahya. Although Merona Space does not yet have
a complex organizational structure, tasks are divided according to each party's responsibilities.
Sofina focuses on managing participants, collaborating with partners, and administering the event,
while Ahya handles digital content development, promotion, and social media marketing.
“So, there are only two of us on this team. Ahya and Sofina handle all aspects of the role
and organization, from planning, implementation, to evaluation, all together. But, we usually like to
divide up the tasks. First, for example, we make the planning together. For example, I'm the one
who looks for brand partners, Ahya is the one who looks for venues. So, later on, we'll work together
through social media. Once we connect with marketing friends or friends from the venue, from the
brand, we contact them. Then, we collaborate, determine the place and date. Then, after that, Ahya
usually creates the content itself on social media. And, Ahya also does the marketing. So, ads. We
use ads to promote our event. Later, I'll manage the participants during registration. So, I'll handle
the registration, payment, and registration process myself. Later, during the implementation, we'll
both carry out the implementation. And, during the evaluation, we'll both be evaluators.” (IK
Sofina)
Based on this statement, it can be seen that the digital content strategy planning process is
an integral part of the overall workshop planning. Despite limited human resources, a clear division
of tasks ensures that each promotional phase runs smoothly. This demonstrates that the digital
content strategy is not only prepared for publication needs but also tailored to the workshop's
specific requirements.
In addition to assigning tasks, the informant also explained that the biggest challenge in the
planning stage wasn't content creation, but rather finding the right participants for the workshop's
target audience. Therefore, each piece of content created wa s always tailored to the theme of the
event.
"Then the most complicated thing, in my opinion, is finding people who want to join,
because that's why we're like, yeah, of course, every time there's an event, we think about, how are
we going to get participants, get people who want to join, who are int erested in us, like what kind
of content to create, that too, that's based on who we are, what our theme is like." (IK Ahya)
This statement demonstrates that digital content development at Merona Space begins with
understanding the characteristics of the w orkshop being marketed. The workshop theme serves as
the basis for determining the content format, promotional message, and communication approach
used with potential participants. Therefore, the content planning process is not random but tailored
to the objectives of the event being promoted.
The findings of this study align with the Plan stage in the RACE Planning Framework theory
proposed by Chaffey and Ellis -Chadwick (2022). At this stage, the organization first establishes
communication objectives, identifies the target audience, and designs a digital marketing strategy
before publishing content. Although Merona Space does not yet have a content calendar or formal
planning, the research results indicate that the planning process still involves internal discussions,
task allocation, participant targeting, and content adjustments based on the workshop theme being
promoted.
2. Digital Content Strategy Implementation
Digital content strategy implementation is the implementation phase of the previously
developed plan. At this stage, Merona Space began communicating information about the workshop
to potential participants through various social media platforms. Based on interviews with key
informants, the social media platforms used for promotion were Instagram an d TikTok.

does not rely solely on a single social media platform, but utilizes multiple digital platforms
simultaneously to reach potential workshop participants. The use of Instagram as the primary
platform is supported by TikTok and Threads as supporting platforms to expand promotional reach.
Furthermore, the use of Instagram Ads demonstrates Merona Space's efforts  to increase content
visibility despite not yet having a dedicated digital marketing team. These findings also indicate that
the strategy implemented remains adaptive through trial and error, reflecting the experience gained
throughout the business.
In add ition to selecting promotional media, Merona Space also employs a copywriting
strategy to ensure the published content captures the audience's attention. Interviewees explained
that each piece of content begins with a hook, or opening sentence, that piques  the curiosity of
potential participants. The content then continues by explaining the various benefits participants will
receive from attending the workshop.
"Usually we give out hooks that are indeed like, are you free on the 30th? Something like
that, well, those are usually the busy ones or I say like guys, makeup class already got a goodie bag,
and getting this much food is worth it or not?"
The interviews revealed that Merona Space's digital content development focuses not only
on delivering information about the workshop schedule but also on how the message is delivered to
the audience. The use of a hook aims to capture the attention of social media users from the first
moment they see the content, while conveying the benefits of the workshop is intended to increase
potential participants' interest in the activities offered.
In addition to focusing on the information presented, Merona Space also emphasizes visual
aspects in every published piece of content. Based on interviews with key informants, promotional
content not only showcases the workshop location but also focuses on the event atmosphere,
participant activities, and the experiences gained during the workshop.
"Actually, the aesthetics of the venue are the first priority, of course. It's definitely there. But
we're more focused on the atmosphere of the event, the activities. So, we want viewers of our videos
to see what we're doing there." (Key Informant – Ahya)
Information obtained from informants indicates that it is understandable that the vi sual
strategy implemented by Merona Space does not only emphasize the aesthetics of the workshop
venue, but rather prioritizes conveying the experiences felt by participants during the event. Through
documentation of participant activities, the workshop process, and interactions that occur during the
event, prospective participants can get a more realistic picture of the activities offered. This strategy
shows that digital content is used as a medium to build perceptions and interest of prospective
participants through the experiences presented, not only through the visual appearance of the venue.
This finding aligns with Pulizzi's (2014) theory of content marketing, which explains that
effective content not only serves to convey information but also provide s value and experience to
the audience. In this study, Merona Space utilized digital content to showcase the workshop
experience so that potential participants could visualize the activities they would undertake if they
participated.

3. Digital Content Strategy Evaluation
Evaluation of Merona Space's digital content strategy cannot be done solely from the
perspective of the management. Source triangulation in this study revealed a gap between what the
owner believed and what workshop participants actuall y experienced, particularly regarding how
potential participants first learned about Merona Space.
Ahya, as a key informant, is quite certain that Instagram and TikTok are the main sources.
"Mostly from TikTok and Instagram." (IK Ahya)
Interviews with ten participants revealed a different story. The majority (six people)
admitted they first learned about the Merona Space workshop from an invitation or a friend's story,
not from scrolling through Instagram. The owner never directly inquired about this during  the
registration process, so it's understandable that the role of word of mouth hasn't been identified by
the management.
Interestingly, the six participants who learned about it from friends still opened the
@merona_space account before finally deciding to join. So, Instagram content isn't the first entry
point, but rather a confirmation or strengthening of intention. Mapped to the AIDA model, the
Attention stage is more often triggered by social media, while Instagram plays a strong role in the
Interest and Desire stages through researching information about the Merona Space Instagram
account before signing up.
Another gap emerged in content performance. Ahya herself acknowledged that some
content generated significant comment traffic, but the number of applicants was actually low.
"We're really busy with that content, there are even people who comment "want it, want it,"
and we DM-in each person who comments "want it," but there are very few people who register, and
there are also things like that." (IK Ahya)
When viewed through the RACE framework, this means the Act stage doesn't automatically
lead to the Convert stage. Comments aren't the same as actual signups, and Merona Space doesn't
yet have a systematic way to follow up with people who initially exp ressed interest but didn't
register.
These two findings share a common thread: a gap between management perceptions and
the reality on the ground. Merona Space is quite good at planning and executing content, but lacks
a measurement tool that truly capture s the role of WOM or converts curiosity into actual
registrations.
Based on the evaluation results, it can be concluded that Merona Space has been able to
build engagement through digital content. However, the evaluation process still needs to be
developed to identify participants' primary sources of information and optimize the conversion of
digital interactions into workshop registrations. These findings further inform the discussion of
factors influencing participants' decisions to attend the workshop.

Factors Influencing Participants' Decisions in Attending the Merona Space Workshop
1. Information Sources and Attention Stages
Participants' initial information sources were divided into two categories: friend
recommendations and direct digital content. Of the ten participants, six learned about the program
through friends' recommendations, and the remaining four learned about it through Instagram or
TikTok.
This friend referral pathway is inextricably linked to the Merona Growth Pulse community,
an informal community formed by the owner that involves people outside the core team to assist
with each workshop. Members of this commun ity indirectly help disseminate information to their
respective networks.
There's also the price factor. Merona Space has several times offered two-person packages,
encouraging participants to bring friends.
"We have several workshops that we've created, l ike a program that says it's cheaper for
two people, for example, one person costs 110 thousand, but for two people it's 200 thousand,
meaning it's only 100 thousand per person." (IK Ahya)
So, WOM at Merona Space isn't purely coincidental. Price incentives  and community
involvement drive it, even though it hasn't been formally designed as a referral program. The

2. Interest, Desire, and Action Factors
Nine out of ten participants said they were attracted by the content, which showcased the
workshop itself, not just the venue. This aligns with previous findings that Merona Space
deliberately emphasizes the atmosphere of the event, not just the aesthetics of the venue.
Tangible benefits were also a consideration. Five out of ten participants cited things like
free PCAs, certificates, product offerings, or food as reasons for attending.
"At Merona, you usually get a free meal or s omething like that, and the goodie bags are
definitely cute, which we really didn't expect. Oh, it's worth it." (IU)
Additionally, there were personal motivations, such as wanting to fill spare time and
improve skills (mentioned by five out of ten participants), and affordability (mentioned by four out
of ten). Price wasn't the dominant factor, but it was still an additional consideration. All of this
suggests that the decision to participate in the Merona Space workshop wasn't the result of a single
factor, but rather a combination of content, benefits, and personal needs, which aligns with Pulizzi's
idea of the importance of tangible value in content.
3. Participant Loyalty and Engagement
One thing that was most consistent among the ten participants was their loyalty. All of them,
without exception, expressed their desire to attend the next Merona Space workshop. This
demonstrates that the workshop experience, from the concept of healing wi th productivity to the
personal interactions with the organizers, successfully retained participants, regardless of the route
they initially learned about Merona Space. Linked to the Engage stage of RACE, this suggests that
hands-on experience plays a greater role in retaining participants than digital content alone.
4. The Role of Digital Content in the Development of Merona Space's Creative Business
Merona Space's digital content not only plays a role in driving registrations but also
expanding its network o f collaborations with partner venues and brands. One venue that has
collaborated with the company stated that the relationship is mutually beneficial in terms of
exposure.
"We benefit each other, people know Merona Space from those who know Hotel Santika,
and vice versa, people know Hotel Santika from that, Merona Space booms." (V enue)
Collaborating with venues isn't just about the space; it's also about expanding each other's
visibility through tagged and shared content. Both venues interviewed reported sm ooth and timely
communication and implementation of the collaboration with Merona Space, which is crucial for
building trust for future collaborations.
The Merona Growth Pulse community also plays a dual role here. Besides acting as a WOM
channel, as discussed previously, this community also acts as a flexible workforce, assisting with
workshop operations, from documentation and venue setup to acting as liaison officers. This model
is what keeps Merona Space running and growing, even though its core team co nsists of just two
founders.
From a female entrepreneurial perspective, Merona Space's approach to growth through
collaboration and community is quite distinctive: gradual growth, relying on networks and trust,
rather than aggressive expansion. This patter n aligns with what Cardella et al. have proposed
regarding the characteristics of female entrepreneurship. Therefore, digital content plays a dual role
here: expanding exposure through business collaborations while simultaneously strengthening the
identity of the young women's community that is Merona Space's hallmark.

Conclusion
 This study shows that Merona Space's digital content strategy has been implemented through
planning, implementation, and evaluation stages oriented towards promoting experience-based
workshops. Content planning was carried out by adjusting the workshop theme, target participants, and
division of tasks among the founders. The strategy implementation utilized Instagram as the main media
supported by TikTok, Threads, and Instagram Ads, with an emphasis on presenting the workshop
experience through compelling visuals and copywriting. The results also show that participants'
