Abstract
This study examines the influence of Social Media Marketing Activities
(SMMA) dimensions on Purchase Intention through Brand Equity and
Customer Engagement among premium smartphone consumers.
Therefore, this research aims to analyze the effects of Entertainment,
Interaction, Trendiness, Electronic Word -of-Mouth (E -WOM), and
Advertising on Brand Equity and Customer Engagement, as well as their
implications for Purchase Intention. This study employed a quantitative
cross-sectional design using purposive sampling. Data were collected
from 175 iPhone users in the Greater Jakarta area through structured
online questionnaires measured using a five -point Likert scale. Data
analysis was conducted using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation
Modeling (PLS -SEM) with SmartPLS 4.0. The results indicate that
Interaction and E-WOM significantly influence both Brand Equity and
Customer Engagement, while Entertainment, Trendiness, and
Advertising show limited  influence on Brand Equity. Furthermore,
Customer Engagement demonstrates the strongest direct effect on
Purchase Intention, exceeding the influence of Brand Equity. The
mediation analysis also confirms that Customer Engagement acts as the
dominant mediating variable in the relationship between SMMA and
Purchase Intention.The novelty of this study lies in the introduction of
the concept of Interactive Community -Based Brand Valuation, which
emphasizes that brand value in digital environments is increasingly
shaped through consumer interaction and engagement rather than
conventional promotional activities. The findings imply that companies
should shift their digital marketing strategies from content -oriented
promotion toward engagement -driven communication and  community
interaction to enhance consumer purchase intention in highly
competitive digital markets.

INTRODUCTION
The rapid advancement of digital technology has significantly transformed the way consumers
communicate, interact, and make purchasing decisions. The increasing penetration of internet access and

smartphone usage has accelerated the development of digital consumer behavior, particularly through
social media platforms. In today’s digital environment, consumers are no longer passive recipients of
marketing information; instead, they actively particip ate in information -sharing, interaction, and value -
creation processes through online communities and social networking platforms (Kaplan & Haenlein,
2010; Dwivedi et al., 2021). Consequently, social media has become one of the most influential marketing
channels in shaping consumer perceptions and behavioral intentions.
This phenomenon is highly relevant in Indonesia, where the rapid growth of internet users and
smartphone adoption has created one of the largest digital consumer markets in Southeast Asia. The
Greater Jakarta region (Jabodetabek), as the economic and techn ological center of Indonesia, represents
a highly competitive digital market characterized by high purchasing power, intense technology adoption,
and strong exposure to global consumption trends. In this context, premium smartphone products such as
the iPhone are not merely technological devices but also symbols of lifestyle, prestige, and self -identity
(Belk, 1988; Keller, 1993). As competition among premium smartphone brands intensifies, companies
are increasingly required to develop more effective digita l marketing strategies to maintain consumer
engagement and stimulate purchase intention.
Among various digital marketing approaches, Social Media Marketing Activities (SMMA) have
emerged as an important strategic tool for enhancing consumer relationships and influencing purchasing
behavior. SMMA enables companies to interact directly with cons umers, distribute information
efficiently, encourage user participation, and facilitate electronic word-of-mouth communication (Godey
et al., 2016; Chen & Lin, 2019). Previous studies have suggested that SMMA contributes positively to
brand-related outcomes, including brand equity, customer engagement, and purchase intention (Alalwan,
2018; Bilgin, 2018). However, despite the growing body of literature, the effectiveness of SMMA remains
inconclusive and fragmented.
Several important research gaps can be identified from previous studies. First, earlier studies
have produced inconsistent findings regarding the impact of SMMA on purchase intention. Some studies
argue that SMMA directly enhances purchase intention, while others indicate that its effect is indirect and
mediated by psychological and relational factors such as trust, engagement, and brand perception
(Ebrahim, 2020; Dwivedi et al., 2021). This inconsistency indicates the need for further investigation into
the underlying mechanisms through which SMMA influences consumer behavior. Second, most previous
studies tend to conceptualize SMMA as a single unified construct, without examining the distinct effects
of its individual dimensions, such as Entertainment, Interaction, Trendiness, Electronic Word -of-Mouth
(E-WOM), and Advertising ( Godey et al., 2016). As a result, the specific contribution of each SMMA
dimension to consumer behavioral outcomes remains insufficiently understood. In reality, different
dimensions of SMMA may generate different levels of cognitive, emotional, and behavi oral responses
among consumers. Third, prior research has predominantly focused on general consumer products or low-
involvement goods, while studies investigating premium high -involvement technological products
remain limited, particularly in emerging economies. This limitation is import ant because consumer
decision-making processes for premium products are more complex and involve stronger emotional
attachment, symbolic value, perceived risk, and brand association (Zaichkowsky, 1985). Therefore,
findings derived from low -involvement prod ucts cannot be fully generalized to premium technology
products such as smartphones.
In addition, the increasing dominance of interactive digital communication has created a
significant shift in marketing paradigms. Traditional content -based promotional strategies are gradually
becoming less effective in influencing consumer decisions comp ared to engagement -oriented
communication approaches. Consumers today tend to rely more heavily on peer interaction, user -
generated content, online reviews, and community participation when evaluating products and making
purchasing decisions. This phenomen on highlights the urgency of understanding how customer
engagement and brand -related perceptions operate within digital marketing ecosystems, particularly in
highly competitive and technology-driven markets.
To address these gaps, this study adopts the Stimulus –Organism–Response (S-O-R) framework
proposed by Mehrabian and Russell (1974). In this model, Social Media Marketing Activities function as
external stimuli, Brand Equity and Customer Engagement represen t the organismic internal states, and
Purchase Intention serves as the behavioral response. Unlike previous studies that mainly focus on
cognitive evaluations such as brand equity, this research simultaneously examines both cognitive
processes (Brand Equit y) and affective -behavioral processes (Customer Engagement) as mediating
mechanisms linking SMMA to Purchase Intention. The novelty of this research lies in three main aspects.

First, this study analyzes the individual dimensions of SMMA rather than treating SMMA as a single
construct. Second, this study investigates the relationship between SMMA, Brand Equity, Customer
Engagement, and Purchase Intention within the context of pre mium smartphone consumers in an
emerging market. Third, this study introduces the concept of “Interactive Community -Based Brand
Valuation,” which emphasizes that brand value in digital environments is increasingly co-created through
consumer interaction, e ngagement, and online community participation rather than solely through
traditional promotional activities.
Therefore, this study aims to:  analyze the effects of SMMA dimensions on Brand Equity and
Customer Engagement;  examine the mediating roles of Brand Equity and Customer Engagement in
influencing Purchase Intention; and identify the most dominant pathway influencing Purchase Intention
in the context of premium smartphone consumers.
RESEARCH METHOD
In this study, a quantitative research design will be used, employing a cross -sectional approach.
Quantitative research will be utilized because it allows for a more in-depth investigation of relationships
among variables and for hypothesis testing. In thi s research, the Stimulus -Organism-Response (S-O-R)
model will be employed, with Social Media Marketing Activities as the stimulus, Brand Equity and
Customer Engagement as the organism, and Purchase Intention as the response.  Data collection was
conducted from September to October 2025. The study was conducted in the Greater Jakarta area
(Jabodetabek), Indonesia, comprising Jakarta, Bogor, Depok, Tangerang, and Bekasi. This place was
chosen because it is the center of economy and technology in Indonesia, where there is a high percentage
of internet usage, purchasing power, and ownership of premium smartphones.
The target population of this study consists of individuals residing in the Greater Jakarta area who
are either current iPhone users or potential iPhone buyers. The sampling technique used is non-probability
purposive sampling, with the following criteria: Respondents aged between 18 and 60 years; Active users
of social media; Have used an iPhone for at least one year or intend to purchase one ; Have a minimum
monthly income of IDR 1,500,000.
A total of 175 valid responses were obtained and used for data analysis. This sample size is
considered adequate for PLS -SEM analysis and meets the recommended minimum for the model’s
complexity. With a sample size of 175, this study satisfies the “10 -times rule” and achieves adequate
statistical power (>0.80), indicating sufficient capability to detect medium -to-strong effects (Hair et al.,
2022).
The research involved different phases as follows:  Phase 1: Conceptualization by identification
of the research variables through a literature review process and development of hypotheses utilizing the
S-O-R model; Phase 2: Instrument Development involves developing an elaborate questionnaire based
on valid constructs from prior works by other scholars; Phase 3: Pilot Test. A pre-test was conducted on
the developed instrument to assess the consistency of the measurement items ; Phase 4: Data Collection
by Distributing online questionnaires through social media platforms ; Phase 5: Data Screening by the
identification of missing values, outliers, and inconsistencies in the data ; Phase 6: Data Analysis with
Analysis of data through PLS-SEM.
Data were collected via an online survey, enabling efficient access to respondents active in digital
environments relevant to the research context. The instrument used in this study is a  structured

The research instrument was adapted from previous validated studies related to Social Media
Marketing Activities, Brand Equity, Customer Engagement, and Purchase Intention. All indicators were

Prior to the main data collection, a pilot test was conducted to evaluate the clarity, validity, and
reliability of the research instrument. The pilot study involved 30 respondents who met the same criteria
as the target population, namely active social me dia users aged between 18 and 60 years who had used
an iPhone for at least one year or intended to purchase one. The pilot test aimed to ensure that all
questionnaire items were understandable, relevant, and capable of accurately measuring the intended
constructs. The instrument consisted of measurement items related to Social Media Marketing Activities
(Entertainment, Interaction, Trendiness, Advertising, and E -WOM), Brand Equity, Customer
Engagement, and Purchase Intention.
Reliability analysis was performed using Cronbach’s Alpha coefficient. The pilot test results
showed that all constructs achieved Cronbach’s Alpha values above the recommended threshold of 0.70,
indicating satisfactory internal consistency reliability. Specifically, the Cronbach’s Alpha values ra nged
from 0.781 to 0.902 across all constructs, suggesting that the measurement items consistently represented
their respective variables.

Data analysis in this study was conducted using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation
Modeling (PLS-SEM) with SmartPLS 4.0 software. This method was selected due to its suitability for
complex models, predictive analysis, and relatively small sample sizes. The analysis consists of two main
stages: 1). Measurement Model Evaluation (Outer Model), This stage evaluates the reliability and validity
of the constructs: Convergent Validity: factor loading (> 0.70) and Average Variance Extracted (AVE >
0.50); Discriminant Validity: assessed using the HTMT criterion (< 0.90); Reliability: Cronbach’s Alpha
and Composite Reliability (> 0.70). 2). Structural Model Evaluation (Inner Model); This stage examines
the relationships between variables ; Path Coefficient: to test hypotheses ; R-Square (R²): to measure
explanatory power ; Effect Size (f²): to assess impact strength ; Predictive Relevance (Q²): to evaluate
model predictive capability.
Statistical analysis was performed using the Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
The results are presented using the PLS -SEM procedure, including measurement and structural
model evaluations to answer the research objectives.

Descriptive Statistics
A total of 175 valid responses were analyzed. The respondents were predominantly aged 21 –35
years (62%), followed by 36 –50 years (24%) and others (14%). Regarding usage behavior, 68% of
respondents had used an iPhone for more than 1 year, indicating sufficient experi ence to evaluate the
product.

Measurement Model Evaluation
The measurement model was evaluated to ensure the reliability and validity of the constructs
prior to hypothesis testing. This evaluation includes convergent validity, discriminant validity, and
internal consistency reliability, following the guidelines of PLS-SEM analysis.

Convergent Validity
The convergent validity of the measurement model was assessed by examining the outer loadings
of all indicators and the Average Variance Extracted (AVE) for each construct, in accordance with PLS-
SEM criteria (Hair et al., 2022). Only indicators with outer  loadings ≥ 0.70 were retained to ensure
sufficient reliability and validity. Indicators with loadings below this threshold were removed to enhance
the overall quality of the measurement model.
In this study, Brand Equity initially included six items (BE1 –BE6), but BE5 and BE6 had
loadings below 0.70 and were excluded from the analysis. Similarly, Customer Engagement originally
consisted of six items (CE1 –CE6), but CE5 and CE6 were removed for th e same reason. After these
adjustments, all remaining indicators for each construct had loadings above 0.70, and AVE values ranged
from 0.621 to 0.701, exceeding the recommended minimum of 0.50. This indicates that each construct
explains a sufficient proportion of the variance in its indicators, confirming that the measurement model
achieves satisfactory convergent validity and is suitable for subsequent evaluation of the structural model.

Effect Size (f²)
The effect size (f²) was evaluated to assess the strength of the influence of exogenous constructs
on endogenous variables. According to Cohen (1988), f² values of 0.02, 0.15, and 0.35 indicate small,
medium, and large effect sizes, respectively. The findi ngs reveal that Customer Engagement has a large

dimensions, E-WOM and Interaction show medium -to-strong effects, whereas Entertainment,
Trendiness, and Advertising exhibit relatively weak effects.

Predictive Relevance (Q²)
Predictive relevance (Q²) was estimated using the blindfolding technique to assess the model's
predictive power on an out -of-sample set. The Q² scores of Brand Equity, Customer Engagement, and
Purchase Intention were all above zero, suggesting that the mod el has sufficient predictive relevance. In
line with PLS-SEM standards, the Q² score should be above zero to conclude that the model has predictive
relevance for the specific endogenous construct (Hair et al., 2022; Henseler et al., 2009). Moreover, the
Q² score for Purchase Intention highlights excellent predictive validity, demonstrating that the model
accurately predicts the consumers' actions in the investigated scenario. This result is supported by prior
literature indicating that models integrating bo th cognitive (Brand Equity) and affective -behavioral
(Customer Engagement) constructs exhibit better predictive performance in understanding consumers'
decisions (Shmueli et al., 2019).
The highly significant predictive power of behavioral observations in this study also strengthens
the generalizability of the S -O-R model, which posits that external stimuli, such as SMMA, affect the
internal state and then elicit a response (Mehrabian & Russell, 1974). In this regard, the model's predictive
power for Purchase Intention confirms the importance of Brand Equity and Customer Engagement as key
constructs for explaining consumer behavior in digital marketing environments. In summary, this study's
findings show that the proposed model is both explanatory and predictive, making it highly valuable for
theoretical and empirical studies of consumer behavior. Both theoretical development and practical
application are involved in understanding consumer behavior.

the 5 cutoff, indicating no collinearity and making our estimates reliable. This outcome conforms to the
PLS-SEM standards, in which VIFs below 5 indicate tha t the predictor variables exhibit no
multicollinearity (Hair et al., 2022).

In contrast, although Brand Equity is a relevant predictor, its effect on Purchase Intentions is
relatively less significant, suggesting that consumer -based assessments, such as brand awareness and
perceptions of product quality, are no longer sufficient t o drive consumer decisions in digital
environments. These findings reflect the emerging marketing paradigm in which the focus shifts from a
brand-oriented strategy to a customer -centered approach (Vargo & Lusch, 2008; Dwivedi et al., 2021).
These findings support the use of the Stimulus -Organism-Response (S-O-R) theory, in which SMMA
represents the stimulus environment, Customer Engagement and Brand Equity represent the organismic
state, and Purchase Intention corresponds to the response behavior (Mehrabian  & Russell, 1974).
However, the current study shows that the affective -behavioral pathway, represented by Customer
Engagement, carries greater weight than the cognitive pathway, represented by Brand Equity, especially
for high -end, expensive products such as premium smartphones. In general, the model suggests a
fundamental shift in consumer behavior, in which purchasing decisions are guided not only by brand
cognition but also by engagement and interaction.

significantly associated with Purchase Intention (β = 0.276, p < 0.001), indicating that a more positive

suggesting that customer engagement is a major factor driving consumer behavior in digital media (Brodie
et al., 20 13; Hollebeek et al., 2014). Overall, the findings suggest that the different dimensions of the
SMMA model do not have equal effects on consumers' reactions. Engagement -related variables,
especially Interaction and E-WOM, are more important than entertainment and advertising.

robust and significant mediator in the relationship between SMMA constructs and Purchase Intention.

Purchase Intention is influenced more strongly by Customer Engagement than by Brand Equity. Thus,

serves as a stimulus, Brand Equity and Customer Engagement as internal organism states, and Purchase
Intention as a behavioral response (Mehrabian & Russell, 1974; Loureiro et al., 2019). The study results
indicate that, in the case of luxury goods, affective and behavioral processes (Customer Engagement)
influence purchase decisions to a greater extent than cognitive processes (Brand Equity). As many experts
have emphasized , modern consumers are value creators who actively participate in value creation
processes (Vargo & Lusch, 2008).

Extended Discussion
Conclusions from the current research shed further light on the impact of social media marketing
activities on consumer behavior, especially in purchasing decisions for premium technological items. In
general, one can state that not all aspects of SMMA affect brand equity and purchase intention to the same
extent, implying changes in the evaluation of online marketing efforts. Among other conclusions, the
current analysis allows saying that Entertainment and Advertising have no significant impact on Brand
Equity. These facts suggest th at even visually attractive or entertaining content will not improve
consumers' perceptions of the brand as premium.
Such findings are corroborated by previous studies that indicate a limited influence of marketing
stimuli on brand equity, especially for high -involvement products that require greater consumer
thoughtfulness (Bilgin, 2018; Keller, 2021). When buying smartphones, consumers rely more on deeper
informational and experiential stimuli than on superficial ones. At the same time, E-WOM and Interaction
are the two strongest factors in the developed model. First of all, the strong and significant influence of
E-WOM indicates the extent to which people trust peer -generated information, including
recommendations and opinions. The theory of electronic word -of-mouth can explain this, as consumers
view their peers’ information as more believable than that generated by firms (Hennig-Thurau et al., 2004;
Cheung et al., 2019).
Similarly, the major role of Interaction underscores the relevance of two -way communication in
digital marketing strategies. The more consumers interact with companies through communication,
feedback, or even social networks, the stronger their feelings of  involvement in the interaction become.
These findings align with relationship marketing principles, which hold that continuous interactions help

create strong relationships with customers and boost their engagement (Gligor and Bozkurt, 2021).
Moreover, one should note that Customer Engagement is a key mediator rather than Brand Equity. In
other words, nowadays consumers rely less on perceptions and  more on affective and behavioral factors
when making purchasing decisions.
Theoretically, this research contradicts the conventional belief that brand equity is the primary
mechanism through which marketing efforts affect purchase intentions. The findings demonstrate that
interaction- and engagement-based processes are far more important in today's digital age. Furthermore,
the results of this research are compatible with the Stimulus –Organism–Response (S -O-R) model.
According to this model, SMMA represents stimuli; customer engagement and brand equity are internal
processes; and purchase intention is the response to both internal and external influences. However, the
affective-behavioral process is much stronger than the cognitive one, especially when dealing with high-
involvement goods.
Taken together, these findings suggest that the process of forming purchase intent for premium
technology products is increasingly influenced by interactive and community -related experiences rather
than by mere marketing communications. This represents a paradigm shift in consumer behavior, in which
value is created collaboratively through engagement rather than being perceived as passive consumers
(Vargo & Lusch, 2008). In this regard, the findings contribute to the extant literature by demonstrating
that engagement-related factors are more influential than perceptual factors in digital marketing practices.
Contrary to earlier studies that focused on brand equity as the main driver, this study finds that customer
engagement is more pronounced and more impor tant for premium products. This represents a paradigm
shift in consumer behavior, in which value is created collaboratively through engagement rather than
being perceived as passive consumers (Vargo & Lusch, 2008). In this regard, the findings contribute t o
the extant literature by demonstrating that engagement-related factors are more influential than perceptual
factors in digital marketing practices. Contrary to earlier studies that focused on brand equity as the main
driver, this study finds that custome r engagement is more pronounced and more important for premium
products.
The findings of this study have important practical implications for marketers, particularly those
operating in highly competitive, digitally driven markets. Companies should reconsider their digital
marketing strategies by shifting their focus from purely promotional, content-driven approaches to more
engagement-oriented ones. Specifically, organizations are encouraged to: develop and nurture interactive
digital communities; stimulate user-generated content and peer discussions; leverage authentic customer
reviews and testimonials (E -WOM); enhance responsiveness and real -time interaction with consumers .
Such strategies are more effective in building meaningful relationships with consumers, strengthening
engagement, and ultimately driving purchase intention. This implies that marketing success in the digital
era is less about visibility and more about participation and connection.
In this research, a new theoretical framework, Interactive Community-Based Brand Valuation, is
presented. The idea here is that brand valuation cannot be based solely on conventional marketing
practices like advertising and promotion. Rather, it has to be created jointly by consumer engagement
within interactive online communities. This contribution presents an innovative approach to creating
brand value in the age of the internet and digital technology, particularly of luxury goods.  Despite the
contributions made in this study, it suffers from certain limitations. To begin with, the scope of this study
is confined to the Greater Jakarta region only. As a result of this limitation, the findings of this study
cannot be generalized to any other region with different socio-economic factors. Secondly, the study has
focused only on one product category: the iPhone. Therefore, the results of this study may not apply to
other industries or product types. Further research should be conducted wi thin the same scope, across
different regions and products. Also, it would be interesting to consider additional variables such as Brand
Trust, Perceived Value, and Social Influence.
CONCLUSION
In conclusion, it is clear that not all dimensions of SMMA affect purchase intentions equally.
Interaction and E-WOM are key drivers, primarily through Customer Engagement, which is the strongest
predictor of Purchase Intention. The results show that engagement-based strategies have a greater impact
on consumers' buying intentions than cognitive strategies, especially for premium products. First, this
paper makes theoretical contributions by introducing the concept of Interactive Community-Based Brand
Valuation. The essence of this concept is that brands are now valued co -creatively through interactions
within communities.  In practice, the findings suggest that companies should prioritize engagement -
