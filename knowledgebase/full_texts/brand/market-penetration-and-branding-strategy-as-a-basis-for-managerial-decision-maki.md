The bottled drinking water market in Indonesia is highly competitive,
prompting PT Sinar Sosro Gunung Slamat to strengthen Prim -A
mineral water as a strategic diversification product amid declining
ready-to-drink tea sales. Despite contributing Rp42.7 bill ion in
Bandung in 2025, Prim -A ranked only 30th nationally in bottled
water distribution, leading management to target a doubling of sales
by 2026. This study aims to formulate an integrated distribution and
branding strategy to increase Prim-A sales in Bandung. A qualitative
approach was employed using data triangulation through semi -
structured interviews with six key informants, observations at 15
retail outlets, and internal distribution data from 1,013 active outlets
during January–December 2025. Data were analyzed using Numeric
Distribution, All Commodity Value, Keller’s Customer-Based Brand
Equity (CBBE) framework, Pareto analysis, and SWOT analysis. The
findings reveal two major challenges. First, Prim -A’s Numeric
Distribution reached only 3.91%, indi cating substantial untapped
market potential, while Pareto analysis showed revenue concentration
in a limited number of outlets and significant contributions from
wholesale channels. Second, the CBBE analysis identified
weaknesses across all brand equity d imensions, particularly in the
trial stage, as consumers who had never tried Prim-A tended to prefer
more established competitors. The association between Prim -A and
Sosro was also insufficiently communicated. Based on these findings,
an integrated 12-month strategy was developed, focusing on selective
retail expansion, food service development, wholesale channel
optimization, targeted product sampling, stronger in -store branding,
and consistent communication of the Prim-A–Sosro relationship. The
projected implementation is expected to achieve 189.2% of the 2025
revenue, exceeding the company’s double-sales target.

INTRODUCTION
In the Fast-Moving Consumer Goods (FMCG) industry, distribution is a key element that
determines the success of a product or brand in the market  (Ali & Mehmood, 2023; Derqui et
al., 2022; Famili Sarvinehbaghi et al., 2025; Holloway, 2024; Kourav & Sharma, 2023; Tsey
et al., 2023). Even high-quality products cannot compete effectively if they are not widely and
consistently available across various points of sale. Distribution not only functions to move
goods from producers to consumers but also shapes perceptions of product availability, which
ultimately influence consumer purchasing decisions. Therefore, understanding distribution
systems and their measurement is essential in developing effective marketing  strategies for
FMCG companies (Fareniuk, 2022; Holloway, 2024; Patel et al., n.d.; Stupak, 2024; Tsey et
al., 2023).

246
According to Guissoni, Neves, and Moraes (2020), there are two main distribution
indicators commonly used to measure product performance: Numeric Distribution (ND) and
Weighted Distribution (WD). Numeric Distribution (ND) measures the percentage of outlets
selling a brand compared with the total number of outlets operating within a specific product
category. In other words, ND reflects the extent of a product’s market presence without
considering the size or sales contribution of each outlet. Meanwhile, Wei ghted Distribution
(WD) provides an additional perspective by considering the contribution of outlet sales to the
overall product category. WD is generally calculated using two approaches: All Commodity
Value (ACV), which is based on total sales of all pro ducts within an outlet, and Product
Category Value (PCV), which is based on total sales within a specific category (Farris, Bendle,
Pfeifer, & Reibstein, 2010). Therefore, WD measures not only the breadth of product
distribution but also the strategic quality of distribution through outlets that significantly
influence category performance.
The relationship between distribution and market share has been widely demonstrated in
academic literature. Products with broader and more strategic distribution networks tend to
achieve higher market shares than products with limited availability. Researc h by Guissoni et
al. (2020) found that both ND and WD significantly influence market share achievement,
although their relevance varies depending on market structure and distribution channel
characteristics. In developing countries with fragmented retail m arkets, such as Brazil and
Indonesia, Numeric Distribution is often more relevant because a large proportion of sales
occurs through general trade channels, including traditional stalls, grocery stores, and local
markets. Conversely, in developed markets d ominated by supermarkets and hypermarkets,
Weighted Distribution becomes more representative because a small number of large outlets
may contribute substantially to total category sales.
In addition to distribution indicators, Nielsen, as a leading market intelligence provider,
emphasizes the importance of evaluating product performance through various dimensions,
including market share, sales performance, and Retail & Trade Insights. Mark et share can be
measured through two dimensions: value share (market share based on sales value in currency)
and volume share (market share based on the number of units or liters sold). Meanwhile, sales
performance includes indicators such as total sales, average sales per outlet, and product sales
velocity. Furthermore, Nielsen reports provide Retail & Trade Insights that offer deeper
information regarding market dynamics, including distribution channel analysis (differences in
product performance between modern trade and general trade), geographic performance, and
promotional effectiveness (Nielsen IQ, 2022). This information is essential to understanding
not only the extent of product distribution but also how products perform and comp ete at the
point of sale (Purnomo, 2023; Sharabati et al., 2024; Sudirjo, 2023).
Within the Indonesian context, the Bottled Drinking Water (Air Minum Dalam Kemasan
or AMDK) market has experienced significant growth. Increasing public awareness of health,
urbanization, and demand for practical beverage solutions have contributed to the expansion
of this industry. According to a Validnews report (2022), the Indonesian bottled water market
reached a value of US$10.24 billion, equivalent to approximately Rp152 trillion, in 2022 and
was projected to grow by 26.5% to reach US$12.95 billion, o r approximately Rp194 trillion,
by 2027. This growth positions bottled water as one of the most promising segments within the
beverage industry.
However, competition within this category remains highly intense. Major players such
as Aqua (Danone) and Le Minerale (Mayora) dominate a significant portion of the market,

247
while Prim-A, a brand owned by PT Sinar Sosro Gunung Slamat, ranks only 30th in the bottled
water industry according to Nielsen research. This ranking is based on Nielsen Retail Audit
measurements, which evaluate national brand performance through value sh are and volume
share within the bottled mineral water category across modern trade and general trade channels
in 2024. This position is primarily influenced by Prim-A’s limited Numeric Distribution, which
reached only 5.8% nationally, meaning that only approximately six out of every 100 available
outlets sell Prim -A products. In comparison, leading brands achieve Numeric Distribution
levels of approximately 85 –90%. These conditions indicate substantial market opportunities
that have not yet been optimally captured by Prim-A.
Prim-A’s relatively weak market performance is primarily driven by two major
challenges. First, limited distribution restricts product availability compared with major
competitors. As emphasized by Kotler and Keller (2016), effective distribution is a crit ical
component in the consumer goods industry, particularly in Indonesia, where geographical
markets are extensive and highly fragmented. Second, insufficient brand differentiation
prevents Prim-A from developing a strong value proposition in consumers’ mi nds. Based on
Aaker’s (1996) brand equity framework, Prim -A has not yet established strong brand
associations and perceived quality capable of influencing consumer preferences within th is
highly competitive category.
The urgency to strengthen Prim -A’s performance has increased as PT Sinar Sosro
Gunung Slamat’s ready -to-drink tea segment has experienced slower growth (Euromonitor,
2023). Under these circumstances, diversification into the bottled water segment represent s a
strategic opportunity for the company to sustain future growth. However, successful expansion
requires the company to address the fundamental challenges of distribution capability and
brand differentiation that have constrained Prim-A’s performance.
According to Porter (1985), companies can improve their competitive position through
generic strategies, including cost leadership and differentiation. In the context of Prim-A, cost
leadership can be achieved through production and distribution efficiency, allowing the product
to compete more effectively in terms of price. Meanwhile, differentiation can be developed by
emphasizing distinctive attributes, such as water quality, environmentally friendly packaging,
or health-oriented brand messages aligned with consumer trends.
Furthermore, digital transformation provides additional opportunities for business
growth. Chaffey and Ellis-Chadwick (2022) highlight that e-commerce and social media have
transformed how consumers interact with brands. With increasing digital penetration  among
Indonesian consumers, particularly younger generations, Prim -A can utilize digital platforms
to enhance brand visibility, strengthen consumer engagement, and  expand product
accessibility.
PT Sinar Sosro Gunung Slamat faces the challenge of strengthening Prim -A Mineral
Water’s position as one of its strategic products amid increasingly intense competition in the
bottled drinking water market. Therefore, the company prioritizes two main initi atives:
optimizing its distribution network to increase market penetration and expand product
accessibility across modern and traditional trade channels, and strengthening brand positioning
through effective marketing communication strategies to build positive brand associations and
clear differentiation from competitors. As part of the Sosro Group, PT Sinar Sosro Gunung
Slamat is recognized as a pioneer of ready -to-drink tea beverages in Indonesia through
Tehbotol Sosro and carries the philosophy of “Good Intentions Good Results.” Beyond tea
products, the company continues to develop Prim -A Mineral Water, which still has

248
considerable growth potential. In 2025, the company’s revenue in Bandung reached IDR42.7
billion, with Prim -A contributing 12.97%, while management established a target to double
Prim-A sales by 2026. Nevertheless, limited distribution penetration, weak br and positioning
compared with competitors, and declining company revenue remain critical issues requiring
strategic intervention. This study aims to identify opportunities for distribution expansion,
determine effective branding strategies to increase bran d awareness, and formulate an
integrated distribution and branding strategy capable of increasing Prim -A sales in Bandung.
The research scope focuses on Prim -A’s distribution and branding strategies in Bandung
through sales data analysis, consumer surveys, and marketing strategy evaluation. The study is
limited to the Bandung area and does not examine production processes or product innovation
aspects.

METHOD
Research Design
 The research design served as a systematic roadmap for effectively addressing the
research questions posed in this study. It provided a structured framework that guided the
research process and facilitated a comprehensive understanding of the study procedures.

Figure 1. Research Design Framework
 The first step was to identify the key business issues faced by the company. The
company experienced a 10% decline in ready-to-drink tea sales, while its mineral water product
had significant market potential. However, Prim -A faced challenges related to lo w brand
awareness and limited distribution coverage, particularly in Bandung. Management targeted a
twofold increase in Prim-A sales compared with the previous year. Based on these issues, the
research questions and objectives were formulated to examine distribution strategies, effective
branding strategies, and an integrated approach to increase Prim-A mineral water sales.
 To address the research questions, a literature review was conducted to support the
analysis. The review covered brand equity to evaluate the current product position, All
Commodity Value to assess distribution performance, market penetration strategies to develop
recommendations, SWOT analysis to identify internal and external factors, customer
engagement to examine consumer relationships, and Porter’s Generic Strategies to determine
appropriate competitive strategies.
 The next stage involved data collection using primary and secondary data sources to
support the analysis. The collected data were analyzed to generate insights and formulate
strategic recommendations. The final stage focused on developing and evaluating st rategic
recommendations to ensure that the proposed strategies were feasible, evidence -based, and

249
applicable to the company’s objectives. The study concluded with research findings and
recommendations for future strategic implementation.
Data Collection Method
Data collection in this research consists of both primary and secondary data to provide a
comprehensive understanding of the current branding and distribution strategies.
Primary Data
Primary Data are collected directly from the sources related to the company’s operations
and market presence. The methods include:
1. Semi-structured interviews: Conducted with 4 company executives (brand manager, sales
distribution manager, distributor owners, salesman) and 2 end user. The aim is to
understand perceptions about brand positioning, distribution challenges, customer
engagement practices, and competitive strategies.
2. Observations: Observations 15 outlet of distribution processes, in-store branding execution,
and point-of-sales activities to assess alignment with intended brand strategies
Secondary Data
Secondary data include internal company reports, market analysis reports, sales and
distribution performance data, branding campaigns evaluations, competitor analysis reports,
and industry publications. These data sources provide quantitative and qualitati ve evidence to
support primary findings and allow for triangulation.
Data Analysis Method
Qualitative data analysis is carried out using thematic analysis to identify recurring
patterns and insights. According to Braun and Clarke (2006), thematic analysis involves
systematically coding data, identifying themes, and interpreting relationships. T he steps
include:
1. Transcriptions and Familiarization: Transcribing interview recordings and reading through
observation notes to gain familiarity with the data.
2. Coding: Labelling important statement and concepts that relate to branding effectiveness,
distribution performance, and strategic fit.
3. Theme development: Grouping codes into broader themes, such as brand equity drivers,
distribution challenges, customer engagement gaps, and strategic opportunities.
4. Interpretation and Conceptualization: Interpreting the themes in the context of the research
objectives to develop strategic insights and recommendations.
5. Framework development: Developing a conceptual framework or strategic map that links
findings to theoretical concepts such as Keller’s brand equity pyramid, SWOT matrix, and
market penetration strategies.
The integration of both primary and secondary data allows the researchers to draw
comprehensive conclusions and ensure that recommendations are relevant, practical, and
aligned with market realities.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
SWOT Analysis
After an in-depth analysis of Numeric Distribution, Value Per Outlet, and brand equity
analysis was carried out using Keller's customer -based brand equity. The analysis is then
carried out using SWOT to find out more about the Prim -A brand in terms of its strengths,
weaknesses, potentials and threats. The SWOT analysis was conducted using a structured
synthesis of three complementary data sources. First, semi -structured interviews with six key

250
informants were held on April 6-9, 2026. Second, using internal distribution data of PT. Sinar
Sosro Gunung Slamat Bandung City for the period of January -December 2025. Third,
observations in the field by researchers as many as 15 general trade retail outlets in the city of
Bandung. The use of these three data sources allows for triangulation that increases the
credibility of the findings, in accordance with the recommendations of Saunders, Lewis and
Thornhill (2019) for qualitative research. Each SWOT factor identified is explicitly supported
by at least one verifiable data source, which is consistent with the evidence -based SWOT
approach according to David and David (2023).

After the formulation of the distribution strategy and also the branding strategy, these
two strategies cannot be carried out separately. These strategies have dependences on each
other. The right distribution strategy will determine the visibility of consumers to find Prim-A
products, while branding strategies will determine whether consumers will choose Prim -A
when they find their products. If the two strategies do not synergize, there is a risk of failure,
namely massive distribution without branding support will make visibility not valid in outlets
because the product will not be chosen by consumers. Meanwhile, strong branding without
adequate distribution will result in brand awareness that cannot be converted into sales.
The results of the interview with the distributor owner's informant, Janny, described the
real conditions in the field, namely:
“If consumers have asked the store about Prim -A, then half of our work is done. The
store will definitely repeat orders because they already know that there are consumers who
buy. The obstacle is if we have to convince the store that there will be consumers who buy, even
though they have never seen the demand” (Distributor Janny, I-04, Interview 7 April 2026)
The statement confirms that effective branding by conducting direct activation and
sampling will accelerate the conversion of new outlets by the sales team, so that the two
distribution and branding strategies are complementary and must be executed in a coordinated
manner.

CONCLUSION
This research addressed three research questions related to distribution strategies,
branding strategies, and the integration of both approaches in increasing Prim -A sales in
Bandung. The findings revealed that the main challenge was the low Numeric Distri bution
(3.91%), indicating substantial untapped market potential. Meanwhile, Pareto analysis showed
that most revenue was generated from a limited number of high -performing outlets, with
wholesale channels making a significant contribution despite their relatively limited number of
outlets. The Customer-Based Brand Equity (CBBE) analysis also identified weaknesses across
all dimensions of brand equity, particularly the low level of product trial and insufficient
communication of the relationship between the  Prim-A and Sosro brands. Based on these
findings, an integrated 12-month strategy was formulated, including selective retail expansion,
food service and wholesale channel development, brand strengthening through activation of
the Prim-A–Sosro relationship, targeted sampling programs, in -store branding optimization,
and periodic performance monitoring. The projected implementation of this strategy indicated
a potential revenue increase of up to 189.2% compared with 2025, bringing Prim -A closer to
the company’s target of doubling sales. This study confirmed that distribution and branding
strategies are mutually reinforcing factors in increasing market penetration and sales
performance. However, the research was limited to the Bandung area, relied on internal
company data, and did not include direct comparisons with competitors’ distribution
performance. Therefore, future research is recommended to expand the geographical scope,
incorporate third -party retail audit data, and conduct quantitative evaluations of strategy
implementation and changes in brand equity after program execution.
