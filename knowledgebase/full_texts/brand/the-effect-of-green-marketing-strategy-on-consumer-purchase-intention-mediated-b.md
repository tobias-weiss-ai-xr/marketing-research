Abstrak
Kesadaran konsumen terhadap isu lingkungan telah mendorong perusahaan untuk mengembangkan
strategi pemasaran hijau. Dalam industri perlengkapan kegiatan luar ruang, perusahaan semakin dituntut
untuk menawarkan produk yang tidak hanya mampu memenuhi kebutuhan fungsional, tetapi juga
mencerminkan nilai-nilai keberlanjutan. Penelitian ini bertujuan untuk menganalisis pengaruh strategi
pemasaran hijau terhadap minat beli, baik secara langsung maupun  tidak langsung melalui citra merek
dan ekuitas merek dalam konteks produk EIGER Green Label. Penelitian ini menggunakan desain
kuantitatif eksplanatori yang dilaksanakan di Kabupaten Sidoarjo. Penelitian melibatkan 160 responden
yang dipilih melalui teknik purposive sampling berdasarkan kriteria yang telah ditetapkan. Data
dikumpulkan menggunakan kuesioner berskala Likert dan dianalisis dengan metode Partial Least
Squares–Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM) menggunakan perangkat lunak SmartPLS. Has il
penelitian menunjukkan bahwa strategi pemasaran hijau berpengaruh positif dan signifikan terhadap
citra merek ( β = 0,914; p < 0,001) dan ekuitas merek ( β = 0,857; p < 0,001), tetapi tidak memiliki
pengaruh langsung yang signifikan terhadap minat beli  (β = 0,083; p = 0,343). Citra merek tidak
berpengaruh signifikan terhadap minat beli dan tidak mampu memediasi hubungan tersebut. Sebaliknya,
ekuitas merek berpengaruh positif dan signifikan terhadap minat beli (β = 0,916; p < 0,001) serta secara
signifikan memediasi pengaruh strategi pemasaran hijau terhadap minat beli ( β = 0,785; p < 0,001).
Temuan ini menunjukkan bahwa strategi pemasaran hijau lebih efektif dalam memengaruhi minat beli
melalui ekuitas merek. Oleh karena itu, EIGER perlu memperkuat nilai, kualitas, keunggulan kompetitif,
dan diferensiasi produk Green Label untuk meningkatkan minat beli konsumen.

Abstract
Consumer awareness of environmental issues has encouraged companies to develop green marketing
strategies. In the outdoor equipment industry, companies are increasingly expected to offer products that
meet functional needs while also reflecting sustainability values. This study aims to analyze the effect of
green marketing strategy on purchase intention both directly and indirectly through brand image and
brand equity in the context of EIGER Green Label products. A quantitative explanatory design was
employed in Sidoarjo Regency. The study involved 160 respondents selected through purposive sampling
based on predetermined criteria. Data were collected using a Likert -scale questionnaire and analyzed
using Partial Least Squares–Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM) with SmartPLS. The results show
that the green marketing strategy has a positive and significant effect on brand image ( β = 0.914; p <
0.001) and brand equity (β = 0.857; p < 0.001), but no significant direct effect on purchase intention (β
= 0.083; p = 0.343). Brand image does not significantly affect purchase intention and does not mediate
the relationship. In contrast, brand equity has a positive and significant effect on purchase intention ( β
= 0.916; p < 0.001) and significantly mediates the effect of the green mar keting strategy on purchase
intention (β = 0.785; p < 0.001). These findings indicate that the green marketing strategy influences
purchase intention more effectively through brand equity. Therefore, EIGER should strengthen the value,
quality, competitive advantages, and differentiation of its Green Label products to enhance consumers’
purchase intentions.

S-O-R Theory
The S-O-R Theory, developed by Mehrabian and Russell (1974), explains how individuals' responses
are formed through environmental stimuli. Unlike the Stimulus–Response (S-R) model, S-O-R introduces the
organism as an internal process occurring before a response is generated (Hochreiter et al., 2022). This process
involves receiving, interpreting, and evaluating stimuli before behavioral decisions are made (Kıymalıoğlu et
al., 2024).
The theory consists of three components. Stimulus refers to external cues such as product attributes,
marketing communications, and brand information that attract consumers' attention (Duong, 2023;
Kıymalıoğlu et al., 2024). Organism represents consumers' internal cognitive and emotional evaluations,
including perceptions, beliefs, attitudes, and emotions, which may differ among individuals (Duong, 2023;
Tortosa-Edo et al., 2025). Response refers to the resulting behavior , such as purchase decisions, loyalty, or
willingness to pay (Chaudhuri et al., 2026). Therefore, S -O-R Theory provides a relevant framework for
explaining consumer responses in sustainable marketing contexts.
Green Marketing Strategy
A green marketing strategy integrates environmental sustainability into product development, pricing,
distribution, and promotional activities while satisfying consumer needs (Tan et al., 2022). As environmental
awareness increases, companies are expected to implement sustainability consistently throughout their
marketing activities.  According to Nokhehan  and Barzegar (2024), green marketing consists of four
dimensions: green product, green price, green place, and green promotion. Green products emphasize
environmentally friendly materials and production processes (Liu & Kim, 2025). Green pricing balances
product value with sustainability benefits (Darberazi et al., 2025). Green place promotes efficient distribution
with lower environmental impact (Liu & Kim, 2025), while green promotion emphasizes transparent
environmental communication and avoids greenwashing (Nguyen et al., 2025). This study measures green
marketing strategy using these four dimensions to examine its influence on brand image, brand equity, and
purchase intention toward EIGER Green Label products (Nokhehan & Barzegar, 2024).
Brand Image
Brand image refers to consumers' overall perceptions and associations toward a brand based on their
experiences and information received. In sustainable marketing, brand image reflects not only product quality
but also a company's environmental commitment,  making it an important determinant of consumer trust and
preference (Muliawan & Nurhasanah, 2026).
Jonathan and Tunjungsari  (2025) explain that brand image develops when environmental claims are
supported by consistent business practices. The construct is measured through brand superiority, perceived
quality, brand reputation, and brand prestige (Jonathan & Tunjungsari, 2025).  In this study, brand image
reflects consumers' perceptions of EIGER Green Label based on these dimensions.
Brand Equity
Brand equity is the added value of a brand derived from consumer perceptions, experiences, and
relationships, enabling the brand to create competitive advantages through trust, loyalty, and positive

Purchase Intention
Purchase intention refers to consumers' willingness to purchase a product after evaluating available
information and perceived benefits (Zhuang et al., 2021). In green marketing, purchase intention reflects
consumers' intention to buy environmentally frien dly products promoted through sustainable marketing
strategies (Krisdayanti & Widodo, 2022).
Purchase intention is influenced by consumers' perceptions of green marketing credibility, brand image,
and brand equity (Pratistha & Prabawani , 2025). Positive evaluations of green products, prices, distribution,
and promotions also strengthen consumers' intentions to purchase environmentally friendly products (Liu &
Kim, 2025). The construct is measured using purchase probability, purchase inte ntion, and purchase desire
(Cao et al., 2025).
Interrelationships Among Variables
Green Marketing Strategy → Purchase Intention
Previous studies consistently show that green marketing strategy positively influences purchase
intention. Testa et al. (2024), Liu et al. (2025), Pratistha and Prabawani (2025), and Astuti et al. (2024) found
that green marketing activities increase consu mers' intention to purchase environmentally friendly products.
Therefore, green marketing strategy is expected to positively influence purchase intention for EIGER Green
Label products.
Green Marketing Strategy → Brand Image
Green marketing strategy strengthens brand image by enhancing consumers' trust and perceptions of
environmental responsibility. This relationship is supported by Tan et al. (2022), Ha et al. (2022), Novitasari
and Mangifera (2024), and Gunistiyo et al. (20 25). Accordingly, green marketing strategy is expected to
positively affect EIGER Green Label's brand image.
Green Marketing Strategy → Brand Equity
Green marketing also contributes to stronger brand equity by increasing perceived value and consumer
trust. This relationship is supported by Yonalia and Bahri (2025), Sohaib et al. (2023), Nguyen et al. (2025),
and Ha et al. (2022). Therefore, green marketing strategy is hypothesized to positively influence brand equity.
Brand Image → Purchase Intention
A favorable brand image enhances consumers' confidence and willingness to purchase. Jonathan and
Tunjungsari (2025), Vanessa and Ariestya (2024), Pratistha and Prabawani (2025), and Wijaya and Setyawan
(2026) consistently found a positive relationship between brand image and purchase intention.
Brand Equity → Purchase Intention
Strong brand equity encourages consumers to purchase through greater awareness, trust, and loyalty.
This relationship is supported by Sohaib et al. (2023), Yonalia and Bahri (2025), and Naldy and Nuvriasari
(2026), indicating that brand equity positively influences purchase intention.
The Mediating Role of Brand Image
Brand image mediates the relationship between green marketing strategy and purchase intention because
green marketing strengthens positive brand perceptions that subsequently encourage purchasing behavior. This
mediating role has been confirmed by Tan et al. (2022), Pratistha and Prabawani (2025), and Ha et al. (2022).
The Mediating Role of Brand Equity
Brand equity also serves as a mediator between green marketing strategy and purchase intention by
translating sustainability initiatives into greater brand value. This relationship is supported by Sohaib et al.
(2023), Yonalia and Bahri (2025), and Naldy and Nuvriasari (2026).

of green marketing strategy on purchase intention, mediated by brand image and brand equity. Data were
collected once through an online questionnaire distributed to individuals who had purchased or were familiar

The research was conducted in Sidoarjo, with questionnaires distributed online to communities of

sustainable marketing strategies, strengthening brand image and brand equity, and supporting corporate
policies that are more adaptive, effective, and aligned with consumer needs and sustainability principles.
Data analysis was performed using SmartPLS following the SEM-PLS procedure recommended by Hair
et al. (2019). The analysis began with preliminary data processing by screening questionnaires for
completeness, respondent eligibility, and response consistency. Responses that did not satisfy the sampl ing
criteria or contained incomplete information were excluded, while valid responses were coded according to
the five -point Likert scale before further analysis. Descriptive statistics, including frequency, percentage,
mean, and standard deviation, were s ubsequently employed to summarize respondent characteristics and
describe perceptions of green marketing strategy, brand image, brand equity, and purchase intention.

Common Method Bias
Because all variables were measured using a single cross-sectional questionnaire, common method bias
(CMB) could potentially occur (Podsakoff et al., 2003). To reduce this risk, respondent anonymity was
maintained, questionnaire items were clearly worded, and indicators for each construct were separated.
Following Kock (2015), a full -collinearity assessment with VIF values ≤ 3.3 indicates that common method
bias is not likely to threaten the validity of the findings.

affect purchase intention ( β = −0.089, p = 0.450). In contrast, brand equity has a strong positive effect on
purchase intention (β = 0.916, p < 0.001). Furthermore, brand image does not mediate the relationship between
green marketing strategy and purchase intention, whereas brand equity serves as a significant mediator ( β =
0.785, p < 0.001).
Discussion
The Effect of Green Marketing Strategy on Brand Image
Green marketing strategy integrates environmental aspects into products, pricing, distribution, and
promotion to build positive brand perceptions (Tan et al., 2022). The results support H1 (β = 0.914, t = 77.734,
p = 0.000), indicating that green marketing  strategy has a positive and significant effect on EIGER Green
Label’s brand image. The R² value of 0.835 and f² of 5.056 indicate a very strong explanatory and effect size.
Consistent with the S-O-R Theory, green marketing strategy acts as a stimulus that shapes consumers’ internal
evaluations, resulting in a stronger brand image. Respondents, predominantly aged 17 –25 and exposed to
EIGER through official social media, perceived the brand’s environmental initiatives positively. These findings
are consistent with Tan et al. (2022) and Rehman et al. (2025), confirming that consistent green marketing
strengthens brand image.
The Effect of Green Marketing Strategy on Brand Equity
The findings support H2 (β = 0.857, t = 46.073, p = 0.000), demonstrating that green marketing strategy
significantly enhances brand equity. The model explains 73.5% of the variance in brand equity (R² = 0.735),
with a very large effect size (f² = 2.768). According to the S-O-R Theory, green marketing serves as a stimulus
that shapes consumers’ evaluation of brand value. Brand equity also recorded the highest mean among all
variables, suggesting that respondents highly valued EIGER Green Label. These findin gs support previous
studies by Ha et al. (2022) and Yonalia et al. (2025), indicating that consistent green marketing strengthens
brand value and competitive advantage.

The Effect of Brand Image on Purchase Intention
The results reject H4 (β = −0.089, t = 0.756, p = 0.450; f² = 0.004), indicating that brand image does not
significantly influence purchase intention. According to the S -O-R Theory, brand image reflects consumers’

Although respondents generally held positive perceptions of EIGER Green Label, purchasing decisions were
more strongly influenced by product quality, functional benefits, price, and overall brand value. Therefore, a
favorable brand image alone is insufficient to increase purchase intention.
The Effect of Brand Equity on Purchase Intention
The findings support H5 (β = 0.916, t = 10.378, p = 0.000; f² = 0.653), demonstrating that brand equity
has a strong positive effect on purchase intention. Under the S -O-R framework, brand equity represents
consumers’ internal evaluation that leads to purchasing behavior. Brand equity achieved the highest mean score
and exerted a substantially stronger influence on purchase intention than either green marketing strategy or
brand image. These findings agree with Ha et al. (2022) and Yonalia et al. (2025), highlighting brand equity as
a key driver of consumers’ purchase intention. Therefore, maintaining product quality, differentiation, and
brand value is essential for strengthening purchase intention.
The Mediating Role of Brand Image
The results do not support H6 ( β = −0.081, t = 0.755, p = 0.450), indicating that brand image does not
mediate the relationship between green marketing strategy and purchase intention. Although green marketing
significantly improves brand image, brand imag e itself does not significantly affect purchase intention. In
terms of S -O-R Theory, the internal evaluation formed through brand image is insufficient to produce a
purchasing response. Consumers continue to prioritize factors such as quality, value, price, and trust over brand
image alone. Consequently, brand image is not the primary mechanism linking green marketing strategy to
purchase intention for EIGER Green Label.
The Mediating Role of Brand Equity
The findings support H7 ( β = 0.785, t = 9.983, p = 0.000), confirming that brand equity significantly
mediates the relationship between green marketing strategy and purchase intention. Consistent with the S -O-
R Theory, green marketing acts as a stimulus th at strengthens brand equity, which subsequently encourages
purchase intention. The insignificant direct effect of green marketing and the significant indirect effect through
brand equity indicate full mediation, suggesting that brand equity is the primary pathway through which green
marketing influences consumers’ purchase intention. These results are consistent with Ha et al. (2022) and
Yonalia et al. (2025), emphasizing the importance of strengthening brand value alongside implementing
consistent green marketing strategies.

CONCLUSION
Based on the findings of this study, it can be concluded that green marketing strategy has a positive and
significant effect on both brand image and brand equity among EIGER Green Label consumers, indicating that
consistent implementation of green products , green pricing, green promotion, and green place strengthens
consumers’ perceptions of the brand. However, green marketing strategy does not directly influence purchase
intention, suggesting that environmental marketing activities alone are insufficient to encourage consumers to
purchase EIGER Green Label products. Similarly, brand image does not significantly affect purchase intention,
indicating that a favorable brand image alone is not enough to stimulate consumers’ purchasing intentions. In
contrast, brand equity has a positive and significant effect on purchase intention, demonstrating that consumers
are more likely to purchase EIGER Green Label products when they perceive stronger brand value.
