This paper takes the Chinese-themed packaging of Modern China Tea Shop as the research object, analyzes the
brand positioning and the embodiment of traditional Chinese cultural elements in its brand packaging design, and
mainly analyzes the characteristics  of traditional Chinese culture and symbols such as painting, text, and color in
the packaging design. This paper explores the creative design and application of packaging with traditional
Chinese elements in its brand touch points through the analysis method in culture code brand design and points
out that the packaging design of Modern China Tea Shop is close to consumer psychology,  and the Era Z has
gradually become the main force of Chinese consumption.  The brand accurately grasps the consumer psychology
in the era of Gen-Z so as to formulate corresponding  marketing strategies. Combined with the analysis of brand
trends and consumers, it is clear that the packaging design of Chinese style is not a simple superposition  of
traditional elements and modern elements, but the integration and innovation of various cultural elements based
on the current market and consumers. Furthermore, the paper summarizes the ways of traditional Chinese elements
to create commercial value and provides a feasible reference for the brand positioning and packaging design of
other tea products in China.
Keywords: Modern China Tea Shop, semiotics, traditional Chinese cultural elements, brand packaging design

Semiotic Analysis of the Application of Traditional Chinese Cultural
Elements in Brand Visual Touchpoints
At the brand’s inception, it was positioned to specialize in Chinese tea, with a visual identity design
reflecting a commitment to traditional Chinese aesthetic styles. Both in product development and visual design,
the incorporation of elements from traditional Chinese culture was emphasized. The promotion of Chinese
traditional culture itself carries significant commercial value for this indigenous Chinese brand. Coupled with a
profound cultural connotation, it holds a considerable advantage in competition within the industry. Consequently,
it has garnered attention, leading to numerous imitations by smaller enterprises, including attempts to mimic the
brand’s visual identity and name. However, this also serves as evidence of the brand’s competitiveness and the
accuracy of its positioning.
In order to deeply understand how Modern China Tea Shop conveys the fusion of traditional culture and
modern brand through its visual design, Pierce’s ternary symbol model provides an effective analytical
framework. By treating the visual elements of a brand as symbols, we can use this model to deconstruct the
symbol system of a brand in more detail and reveal how it interacts meaningfully with consumers. Peirce’s ternary
model, consisting of representamen, objects, and interpretants, can help analyze how symbols in brand visual
design convey cultural values.
Representamen: The visual symbols in a brand’s packaging, such as traditional patterns, text, and colors,
that form the visual language of the brand.
Object: These symbols refer to traditional cultural connotations, such as Chinese tea culture, the five-
element color system, and calligraphy art, and represent the cultural and historical information that the brand is
trying to convey.
Interpretant: The process of consumers’ understanding and decoding of these symbols, that is, how they
perceive and interpret the meaning of these symbols from the brand design, thus generating brand identification
and emotional connection.
By using Pearce’s ternary symbol model, we are able to systematically  analyze how brands build cultural
connections with consumers through visual touches and maintain their uniqueness and cultural value in a
highly competitive market through symbolic design strategies. The application of this model not only helps to

Traditional Painting Elements
The application of traditional Chinese painting elements in the brand design of Modern China Tea Shop is
particularly prominent, with the packaging of the tea cups standing out as a major highlight. In 2018, the
introduction of the “Ancient Paintings Series” packaging involved the brand acquiring the rights to use a
collection of paintings from major museums across the country. Paintings such as “A Thousand Miles of Rivers
and Mountains”, “Dwelling in the Fuchun Mountains”, “Night Revels of Han Xizai”, and “Auspicious Cranes”
(see Figure 1) underwent image enhancement and redesign. Building upon these renowned artworks, the brand
adjusted and combined content relevant to its brand attributes, using a modernized redesign to convey the charm
of traditional Chinese culture.
For example, when using a portion of “A Thousand Miles of Rivers and Mountains” as the background, a
combination of extensive green and purple hues was chosen to emphasize the beauty of the distance and space
between heaven and earth. In the case of selecting “Auspicious Cranes” as the background, the original depiction
of cranes flying in the air was retained, while diminishing the details of the palace eaves below. This adjustment
aimed to highlight the graceful flight of the cranes, conveying a sense of wealth and auspiciousness.
Simultaneously, it communicated the auspicious symbolism to consumers.

Traditional Character Element
The text design of Modern China Tea Shop draws inspiration from Chinese characters while incorporating
innovative elements. Calligraphy, a unique form of artistic expression in China, stands as one of the quintessential
symbols of the “Chinese style”. The brand’s text-themed packaging, especially in the creative series,
predominantly features auspicious phrases. This creative choice aligns with the psychological needs of consumers,
blending traditional calligraphic patterns with modern design, resulting in a strong ethnic style. The packaging
and poster designs of the text-themed series (see Figure 2) introduced by the brand are not a mere direct
application of calligraphy characters. Instead, they are a fusion of various considerations, including the brand’s
products, regional culture, tea culture, and more. The designs integrate these elements to create a harmonious and
culturally rich visual representation, showcasing a thoughtful and comprehensive approach to modern design
while staying true to the brand’s roots.

Figure 2. The application of text in brand packaging design (by the official website of the brand).
Traditional Color Element
In terms of the application of traditional colors, Modern China Tea Shop creatively designs based on the
core concept of the traditional Chinese color theory known as the “Five Colors”. The traditional five colors in
Chinese color theory are red, yellow, blue/green, black, and white. These colors were derived from minerals and
plants, and their selection was influenced by the ancient people’s extraction of pigments as well as their
understanding of the natural world, evolving gradually into a comprehensive color system based on observational
insights. Brand not only considers the visual aspects in applying the traditional five colors but also effectively
utilizes the cultural connotations behind each color.
Application of red, black, and white colors: Red and black were widely used colors in early China, such as
the red and black colors seen on pottery. In traditional Chinese color schemes, the combination of red and black
is common, emphasizing the balance between warmth and coolness. Similarly, the pairing of black and white
follows a similar principle, where white represents yang and black represents yin, creating a harmonious balance
between the two opposing forces.

windows (see Figure 3). The stark contrast between the black hair, white fan, and attire creates a visually striking

The Role of Traditional Chinese Element Symbols in the Interaction
Between Brand Vision and Consumers
Through the semiotic analysis of the brand visual touchpoints of Modern China Tea Shop, it is clear that the
brand has cleverly utilized traditional Chinese cultural elements as distinctive touchpoints. By employing visual

symbols, it has constructed a unique brand identity and cultural value. The tea beverage market in China is fiercely
competitive, and Modern China Tea Shop has gained significant prominence among numerous brands. Through the
analysis of the application of Chinese traditional cultural elements in the design case of this brand, we can outline
and summarize the ways in which these elements strategically manifest commercial value in brand design.
Modern China Tea Shop, as a tea brand, excels particularly in Derivative Functions, providing it with a unique
brand positioning, commercial value, and competitive advantage in the Chinese tea beverage market.
In the face of the vast tea beverage market, ensuring that one’s products stand out from similar ones and
appeal to a younger audience is crucial. Beyond delivering high-quality products, visual design becomes
extremely important. The brand’s Derivative Function is prominently manifested in its packaging design.
Through differentiation, symbolism, and image creation, the brand has successfully constructed its unique
identity. In comparison to other brands of the same type, it not only emphasizes the quality of the product but
also places a strong emphasis on cultural experiences. By incorporating elements of Chinese traditional culture,
such as ancient paintings and poetry, as brand symbols, it cleverly positions itself in a differentiated space within
the market. Through the sensitive use of cultural symbols, a unique and recognizable brand image has been
created. The brand was also a pioneer in introducing cultural and creative products into the tea beverage industry.
Through the use of these products, consumers gradually feel and understand the core values of the brand. If
consumers appreciate the brand’s cultural and creative designs, it indicates resonance between the brand’s values
and those of the consumers, leading them to become loyal customers.
The brand positioning primarily focused on Derivative Functions endows the brand with higher commercial
value. By emphasizing cultural experiences and introducing cultural and creative collaborations, it has
successfully shaped the brand’s added value, leading a new trend in the tea beverage market. This value even
surpasses the fundamental attributes of tea beverages themselves. Consumers are not just purchasing a cup of
milk tea; they are immersing themselves in a cultural experience that blends tradition with modernity, art with
life. This unique commercial value enables the brand to attract consumers seeking richer consumption
experiences, thereby achieving a higher level of added value for the brand. The brand releases limited-edition
beverages each season, characterized by unique packaging with distinct limited-edition features. Such visual
designs attract a large number of consumers, with many purchasing these items for collection purposes or
engaging in secondary modifications. On various online platforms, one can observe many individuals using these
items as traditional-style flower vases or as contemporary Chinese-style table lamps. This subtle influence on
consumer aesthetics further strengthens brand loyalty among consumers.
The consumption behavior of the young generation is gradually showing characteristics of symbolism and
socialization. Taking the tea beverage market, with Generation Z as the main consumer group, as an example,
young people drinking tea and purchasing tea beverages are not only for their food attributes but also as a new
socializing method. Modern China Tea Shop has established a social circle around the tea brand. By enhancing
interaction rituals through packaging, cultural, and creative products, the brand taps into individual desires for
group identity within the social circle, turning its products into symbols of gifts exchanged within the community.
Simultaneously, it has sparked trends like the “first cup of milk tea in autumn” on social media. This branding
strategy has given Modern China Tea Shop an advantage in the highly competitive tea beverage market. By
emphasizing differentiation and cultural symbols, the brand stands out, attracting consumers who are curious,
value rituals, prioritize brand experiences, and appreciate cultural heritage. This positions the brand with stronger
attractiveness and loyalty in the competitive landscape, solidifying its position in the market.

Conclusion
The application and innovation of Chinese traditional cultural elements by Modern China Tea Shop have
disrupted the monotony of brand design in the tea beverage market, capturing consumers’ attention and standing
out. In the utilization of traditional cultural elements, the focus extends beyond mere commercial objectives;
instead, the goal is to apply Chinese style for brand innovation, ensuring differentiation from other competitive
brands. By integrating semiotic theory, particularly Peirce’s Triadic Model of Semiotics, it becomes evident how
the brand’s visual symbols serve as effective carriers of meaning, contributing to both brand identity and cultural
resonance.
Through a profound understanding of Chinese traditional cultural connotations and design methods, the
brand effectively balances traditional charm with contemporary aesthetics. Using Peirce’s model, the visual
elements of the brand—such as colors, patterns, and typography—act as the representamen, while the object they
refer to is the rich history and cultural symbolism of Chinese tea culture. Consumers, as the interpretants, decode
these symbols based on their own cultural understanding and life experiences, creating a strong emotional
connection with the brand. This process not only helps the brand stand out in a competitive market but also
deepens consumer loyalty by reinforcing cultural identity and emotional resonance.
By utilizing Chinese traditional elements as a theme, the brand unveils the ethnic culture behind visual
symbols, truly driving and developing business through culture, achieving a dual success in cultural heritage and
commercial benefits. The semiotic analysis highlights how the brand’s packaging and design do more than just
appeal visually—they function as symbols that engage consumers on a cultural and emotional level, adding layers
of meaning and value to the brand experience.
Therefore, in future business design, it is essential to place a greater emphasis on the integration of
Chinese traditional cultural elements with modern brand design, using semiotic frameworks to better
understand how visual symbols communicate with consumers. This involves preserving tradition while
fostering innovation and utilizing modern technology for creative advancements.  The success of the Modern
China Tea Shop brand demonstrates the effectiveness of incorporating traditional elements cleverly into
modern design concepts and contemporary lifestyles. By employing semiotic principles, the brand has been
able to disseminate cultural values with a fresh brand image in the tea beverage market, injecting new vitality
into both the brand and the market.
