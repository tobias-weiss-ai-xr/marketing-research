Abstract

This study focuses on developing a marketing strategy following the rebranding of the
“Cage-Free Fish Farmers Association Network” to the “Alangkan  Agricultural Group Network”
by investigating how consumer perceptions of the brand interact with marketing
communication efforts, and how these combined elements influence consumer buying
behavior toward agricultural products.  Particular emphasis is plac ed on the role of
demographic factors and the extent to which new brand perceptions and marketing
communications influence consumer behavior through online distribution platforms. Using a
multiple-case study design, qualitative data were collected from 11 farm owners through focus
groups and 45 consumers via semi-structured interviews. This research method allowed for a
deep understanding by examining individual cases and using multiple sources of data, which
helped guide the development of the rebranding strategy.
As part of this rebranding initiative, a new slogan, “Experience Kanchanaburi intimately,
elevate its grandeur,” was introduced to strengthen and clarify the group’s brand image. This
initiative was guided by the 3i Marketing Strategy Model, con sisting of Brand Image, Brand
Identity, and Brand Integrity. The analysis of consumer behavior on online platforms identified
four key factors influencing purchase decisions: age, occupation, Component 1 —particularly
the emotional connection to the logo, which remained intact even after the rebranding effort
(X10)—and Component 7, specifically, the decisive role of sales personnel in providing
consultative advice on products featuring the new brand logo (X29). These factors highlight a
combination of demogr aphic characteristics (age, occupation) and psychological and
emotional drivers (logo connection, consultative advice from sales personnel) that impact
how consumers buy online.

1. Introduction
 Thailand’s 13th National Economic and Social Development Plan (2023–2027) outlines
the strategic directions aimed at enhancing infrastructure and digital readiness in key economic
zones and urban centers. Central to this approach is the continuous development of
standardized and robust digital systems, along with the promotion of research and
development activities with commercial application potential. The Plan also emphas izes the
importance of leveraging digital technologies to enhance business value, while encouraging
entrepreneurs to adopt digital -driven business models (Office of the National Economic and
Social Development Council, 2022). The key objective is to build the capacity of community
enterprises to effectively utilize technology, data, and digital platforms as tools for value
creation. This strategic initiative is anchored in the 20 -Year National Strategy (2018–2037) and
is aligned with the National Research and Innovation Strategy. Specifically, it reflects Strategy
1, which focuses on research and innovation as drivers of economic prosperity through the
utilization of digital content. This strategy emphasizes the application of research and
innovation for commercial benefit, particularly in supporting the development of small and
medium-sized enterprises (National Research and Innovation Policy Council, 2017).
 The Cage-Free Fish Farmers Association Network has demonstrated moderate capacity
for digital market ing, as identified during the initial phase of the current research project,
according to the evaluation criteria developed by the Community Enterprise Promotion
Division (2017). Key challenges facing the Network include inconsistent production content
and the absence of a strategic digital marketing framework to ensure customer retention under
the brand “VGet: Safe Agricultural Products” (Katepan, 2023). The Network is undergoing a
rebranding process to strengthen its brand identity and improve market reco gnition. This
strategic overhaul involves updating elements like its name, logo, messaging, and marketing
strategy to create a new, differentiated identity in the minds of customers, investors, and
competitors. The goal is to modernize the brand, clarify its positioning, attract new customers,
and build stronger brand equity. Rebranding poses significant challenges for agricultural
community enterprises due to their limited resources, uneven digital capabilities, and strong
socio-cultural attachments to tra ditional practices. Their dependence on localized markets
and long-established trust relationships often leads to resistance to change, while insufficient
marketing expertise and strategic frameworks further constrain the effective integration of
rebranding initiatives with digital platforms. Consequently, these enterprises face difficulties in
implementing coherent and sustainable rebranding strategies that align with evolving
consumer expectations.

2. Literature Review
 2.1 Concepts and theories related to rebranding
  Rebranding is more than just a visual makeover; it represents a shift in a company’s
overall image, impacting how consumers perceive and interact with the brand, ultimatel y
influencing their attitudes and experiences through targeted marketing efforts, thereby
increasing brand awareness and recognition. Thus, rebranding extends beyond visual or
symbolic changes; it embodies organizational perception and enables the brand to effectively
engage with its target audience. Furthermore, it influences consumer attitudes and experiences
through targeted advertising and interactions with products or services, thereby enhancing
brand visibility and recognition (Smith & Lee, 2021). Wit hin this framework, building brand
awareness is essential for business development and strategic growth. It emphasizes the
management of brand equity to secure sustainable consumer recognition of the new brand
identity. This transformation positions the brand as a valuable intangible asset, allowing firms
to command premium pricing, improve profitability, and increase market valuation.

Consequently, rebranding contributes to long-term business stability and sustainability (Smith
& Lee, 2021).
 2.2 Concepts and theories related to brand perception
  Barajas-Portas (2015) defines consumer brand perception as a key factor that stimulates
consumers’ expectations regarding product performance. The author identifies several
dimensions of brand perception as follows: (1) Functional Perception, influencing perceptions
of product quality, pricing, cultural relevance, and brand personality; (2) Affective Perception,
referring to the emotional responses associated with the brand; (3) Brand Experience, affecting
the consumers’ perception of risk; (4) Brand Reputation, shaping perceptions of the brand’s
user image; and (5) Interaction, referring to the level of consumer engagement and
communication with the brand.
 2.3 Relevant research
 The study by Poomalee (202 2) focuses on the relationship between consumer
personality and the personality of high -end fashion brands. The research employs in -depth
interviews with 30 participants and reveals the following key findings: (1) Consumers whose
lifestyle, preferences, an d personality align with a brand’s image are more likely to make
repeat purchases, which may lead to long -term brand loyalty. (2) Segmenting target groups
based on personality traits, interests, lifestyle, and behavior is more effective than
segmentation based on demographic factors. (3) When building a new brand or repositioning
an existing one to achieve rapid and lasting brand recognition, using a celebrity whose public
image aligns with the brand can be an effective strategy. The celebrity’s prominence serves
as a powerful driver of consumer awareness and brand perception.
 Chokphitakkul and Anantachat (2017) measure consumer-based brand equity in small
and medium enterprises (SMEs) under two objectives: (1) to measure consumer -based brand
equity in small and medium enterprises (SME) based on brand knowledge and perception of
brand relationship quality; and (2) to examine the relationship between SME brand equity and
three consumer response factors, namely brand preference, brand loyalty, and word of
mouth. These researchers use self-administered questionnaires to collect data from 400 male
and female respondents. The findings show that After You scores higher than Cookies Crust
on the consumer-based brand equity scale. Moreover, the After You and Cookies Crust’s brand
equity scores demonstrate positive correlations with all consumer response factors for their
own brand at a higher level in comparison to the other brands under study.

of consumers to purchase Honda cars; and 3) to study the relationship between brand equity
and the purchase decisions of consumers toward Honda cars. The sample group consists of

 3.2 Population and sample sizes
 The population for this study comprised two groups: (1) farmers who were members
of the Cage -Free Fish Farmers Association Network in Kanchanaburi Province, totaling 20
individuals; and (2) consumers who purchased products from this Network.
  The study sample consisted of: (1) 11 farm owners or their designated representatives
who were actively involved in farm operations and members of the Cage -Free Fish Farmers
Association Network; and (2) consumers of agricultural products from the Network, located
throughout Kanchanaburi Province. However, due to limitations in accessing the consumer
group, data were collected from only 45 respondents. The selection of these 45 respondents
was based on the following inclusion criteria: (1) individuals of any gender; (2)  aged between
20 and 60 years; and (3) consumers who had purchased agricultural products from the Network
through online channels. Nevertheless, this research constituted a pilot study, constrained by
limited access to the Network’s customer base, with data obtained from only 45 participants.
 The study received ethical approval from the Kasetsart University Research Ethics
Committee, Bangkok, Thailand (COA No. COA67/012).
 3.3 Data collection
  Data collection in this study involved two distinct groups and  methods: focus group
discussions with 11 farm owners from the Cage -Free Fish Farmers Association Network and a
semi-structured questionnaire given to 45 customers who had previously bought products
from the Network, selected through purposive sampling. This dual-method approach provided
insights from both the supply side (farmers) and the demand side (customers).
  A six-point Likert scale from strongly disagree (1) to strongly agree (6) was used to
measure how brand perception and marketing communication affected customer responses
to products from the Alangkan Agricultural Group Network after a rebranding effort.  This
method allowed respondents to express the extent of their agreement with statements,
providing data on the effectiveness of the rebranding in influencing their views and behaviors
toward the products by dividing them into six levels based on an interval width of 0.83 as
follows: (1) a mean score of 5.16–6.00 was interpreted as “highest”; (2) a mean score of 4.33–
5.15 as “high”; (3) a mean sco re of 3.50–4.32 as “moderate”; (4) a mean score of 2.67 –3.49
as “relatively low”; (5) a mean score of 1.84–2.66 as “low”; and (6) a mean score of 1.00–1.83
as “lowest.”
 3.4 Data analysis
 This study used a mixed-methods approach, whereby both qualitative and quantitative
research techniques were combined to gain a deeper understanding of a
topic. Specifically, focus group discussions (a qualitative method) were used to develop a
strategy for enhancing new brand awareness, with quantitative methods employed to analyze
the factors that influence customer responses.

 Furthermore, the brand personality of the Alangkan  Agricultural Group Network was

  - Brand Image : Conveying attributes such as sincerity, humility, excitement,

  - Brand Identity : Reflecting the group’s commitment to offering only certified

the promotion of local “unseen” attractions, and the organization of interactive workshops
designed to share and exchange local knowledge and expertise.

5. Conclusion, Discussion, and Recommendations
   According to the findings, this research study on a strategic model, designed to increase
recognition for the rebranded Alangkan Agricultural Group Network, led to lasting consumer
loyalty by involving customers in the brand’s growth through participatory engagement.
   The findings revealed that the development of a strategic model aimed at enhancing
brand awareness of the rebranded Alangkan Agricultural Group Network successfully fostered
sustainable consumer brand loyalty through participatory engagement. A key element  of this
strategy was the introduction of a new slogan —“Experience Kanchanaburi intimately,
elevate its grandeur ”—which aimed to enhance and clarify the group’s brand image. This
rebranding initiative was further refined using the 3i Marketing Strategy Mod el, comprising
Brand Image  (the external perception of the brand), Brand Identity  (the internal and
intended representation of the brand), and Brand Integrity (the consistency and credibility of
the brand). Collectively, these efforts contributed to a clea rer and more memorable brand
image for the Network. In addition, a sustainable process for building new brand awareness
and creating promotional media content was developed using the 5Ws Pyramid: Insight Finder,
focusing on the core products of this agricultural group network.
   Following rebranding, age, occupation, Component 1—particularly retained emotional
connection to the logo (X10) —and Component 7, especially consultative advice from sales
personnel, were significant factors influencing online purc hasing behavior and played a
decisive role in consumers’ purchasing decisions regarding products featuring the new brand
logo (X29). Therefore, the Alangkan Agricultural Group Network should implement a “Bridge
Campaign” to transfer consumers’ existing emotional attachment from the former Cage-Free
Fish Farmers Association Network  to the new brand identity, rather than abandoning it

entirely. This approach would preserve existing emotional bonds with the previous logo and
strengthen future repurchase intentions. Additionally, age and occupation are personal factors
that influence consumers’ purchasing decisions and online channel choices. Accordingly, the
Network should consider age and occupational structure as key determinants of aggregate
purchasing behavior, so that firms can better align their marketing strategies with contemporary
consumer behavior in digitally connected markets.
 Future research
   To guide future research, it is important to address the limitations of the current study
by expanding sample diversity to include a greater variety of demographics and agricultural
contexts, with a cross-regional study to understand the cultural and geographic influences on
consumer responses and marketing strategies, while acknowledging that the results may differ
in regions with distinct regulatory and cultural environments.
