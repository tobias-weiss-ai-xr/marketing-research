Abstract
Food tourism is a growing niche within the travel industry that merges gastronomy, culture, and destination branding. Its
potential to contribute to environmental sustainability and social development has gained increased scholarly interest,
especially in relation to the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). Th is article presents a systematic
review of 139 peer -reviewed publications (2020 –2024) indexed in the Web of Science, aiming to evaluate how food
tourism and corporate communication strategies —particularly public relations —intersect with sustainability obje ctives.
The study applies bibliometric methods and the PRISMA framework to identify dominant trends, geographical hotspots,
key stakeholders, and the most frequently addressed SDGs in this field. Findings reveal that most research focuses on
SDG 9 (Industry, Innovation, and Infrastructure), with limited holistic coverage of all 17 goals. Furthermore, companies
within the tourism and food sectors are emerging as key corporate actors implementing environmental management
practices such as renewable energy use , food waste reduction, and sustainable sourcing. Public relations play a critical
role in shaping stakeholder perceptions, enhancing corporate social responsibility (CSR) efforts, and legitimizing
sustainability strategies. This review highlights the need  for stronger integration of communication, governance, and
sustainability frameworks to achieve long-term environmental and social goals in tourism. Future research should explore
applied assessments of SDG implementation across tourism-related business ecosystems.
Keywords: food tourism, sustainable development goals, corporate social responsibility, environmental management,
systematic review

1. Introduction
Tourism has increasingly been recognized as a vehicle for sustainable and resilient development. The
forthcoming International Year of Sustainable and Resilient Tourism in 2027 highlights the sector’s potential
to drive inclusive growth and opportunity, while underscoring the need to ensure that tourism’s expansion
remains environmentally and socially sustainable (UNWTO, 2024). Within this broader context, sustainable
tourism is defined as optimizing the use of environmental resources, maintaining essential ecological
processes, conserving natural and cultural heritage, and ensuring long -term socio-economic benefits that
are equitably distributed among stakeholders (UNWTO, n.d.). These principles have become a central focus
in both policy agendas and academic research (Antolini et al., 2024; Moreira dos Santos et al., 2024).
Among tourism’s various segments, food tourism has emerged as one of the fastest -growing niches. It
connects gastronomy, culture, and local identity, becoming a significant driver of economic activity and job
creation while also raising complex questions about sustainability and corporate responsibility (BBVA, 2022;
Yeoman & McMahon -Beatte, 2016). As travelers increasingly seek authentic culinary experiences, local
products, and cultural immersion, food tourism represents more than a leisure activity: it is  a vehicle for
cultural exchange, regional development, and destination branding (Andersson, Mossberg & Therkelsen,
2017). However, the environmental pressures associated with agriculture, food production, and hospitality

— including water consumption, greenhouse gas emissions, and waste generation —highlight the urgent
need for more sustainable practices and governance frameworks (Fraser et al., 2023; Alonso -Muñoz et al.,

Corporate social responsibility (CSR) and environmental management have thus become crucial in shaping
the practices of tourism and food enterprises. Recent studies show that the adoption of environmental,
social and governance (ESG) strategies in the hosp itality and tourism industry is positively associated with
improved sustainable performance and reduced corporate risk (Martínez-Falcó et al., 2025; García, Herrero
& Morillas-Jurado, 2024). Yet, within much of the tourism sector, especially hospitality, t he Sustainable
Development Goals (SDGs)  — and particularly environmental sustainability —remain peripheral in CSR
initiatives (Moyeen & Mehjabeen, 2024). At the same time, the expectations of travelers and other
stakeholders around sustainability are rising, creating both a reputational challenge a nd an opportunity for
companies to differentiate themselves.
Public relations (PR) strategies play a pivotal role in this transformation. PR connects businesses with their
audiences and shapes the narratives that frame sustainability initiatives in a digital and multidirectional
communication environment (Palau -Sampio & López -García, 2022; Kretschmer & Winkler, 2024). When
effectively deployed, PR enhances transparency, educates stakeholders, and legitimizes corporate actions
on sustainability, reinforcing the brand’s long -term reputation and its contribution to envi ronmental
awareness (Marino, 2023; SYNC, 2023). This makes PR an essential component of the governance and
communication frameworks needed to advance the SDGs in tourism.
Despite the growing recognition of public relations as a strategic governance and legitimacy mechanism in
sustainability transitions, its empirical integration into food tourism research remains limited. This review
therefore not only maps sustainability -related practices, but also critically examines the visibility  — and
absence — of PR and communication within SDG -oriented food tourism scholarship. Few studies have
comprehensively analyzed how food tourism is positioned within sustainability frameworks an d how its
corporate communication strategies intersect with the SDGs. Existing research is fragmented, with a
technical emphasis on specific sustainability practices but little holistic assessment of the social good and
stakeholder engagement perspectives (Wu et al., 2018; Dube & Nhamo, 2021). In order to provide a wider
perspective on the relationship between the food business associated with tourism and the 17 SDGs, it is
essential to systematically analyze the sector’s impact on each of them and identify linkages between
benefits and drawbacks.
This study addresses this gap by presenting a systematic review and bibliometric analysis of academic
research on food tourism, sustainability, and public relations between 2020 and 2024. Specifically, it seeks
to: (1) detect whether there is an emerging l ine of research on sustainability and food tourism and its
magnitude; (2) identify where the growth of interest in food tourism and sustainability is occurring
geographically and thematically; and (3) determine which SDGs are most frequently addressed in f ood
tourism research, with particular attention to the role attributed to communication and public relations in
sustainability-oriented strategies. By synthesizing this body of literature, the paper aims to contribute a
strategic framework for integrating sustainability communication, stakeholder engagement, and CSR
practices within food tourism enterprises.

2. Theoretical framework
Corporate social responsibility (CSR) has become a strategic imperative for tourism and food -related
businesses responding to growing sustainability challenges. In these sectors, CSR is no longer viewed merely
as reputational or philanthropic but rather as a governance framework guiding environmental stewardship,
social equity, and ethical conduct (Sheykhan et al., 2024; Moyeen & Mehjabeen, 2024). Companies have
increasingly adopted sustainability practices aligned with global goals, including the reduction of food waste
(Biber-Freudenberger et al., 2020), local sourcing (Andersson, Mossberg & Therkelsen, 2017), and green
marketing strategies aimed at reinforcing brand legitimacy (Sahin et al., 2017).
Despite this evolution, engagement with the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) remains fragmented
across the tourism literature. Much of the focus has been placed on technical or environmental dimensions—
especially SDGs such as Industry, Innovation and I nfrastructure (SDG 9), Responsible Consumption (SDG
12), and Climate Action (SDG 13) —but little attention has been paid to how CSR is communicated or
integrated across multiple goals (Wu et al., 2018; Dube & Nhamo, 2021). Moreover, critiques persist around
the tendency of some tourism CSR efforts to remain superficial, failing to embed systemic change or include
holistic stakeholder engagement (Moyeen & Mehjabeen, 2024; Cavano, 2020).
In this regard, public relations (PR) has emerged as a vital tool in sustainability governance. PR helps
organizations translate internal efforts into externally visible commitments, enhance transparency, and
promote behavioral change among consumers and other stakeholders (Palau-Sampio & López-García, 2022;
Marino, 2023). In the case of campaigns such as “The Scarecrow” by Chipotle, PR became a vehicle for
raising awareness about food sustainability through emotionally compelling narratives (Swensen et al. ,
2016). Similarly, PR plays an increasing role in digital reputation management, helping tourism firms
navigate negative reviews related to food and service quality (Sahin et al., 2017), while contributing to
broader ESG positioning (Martínez-Falcó et al., 2025).
Food tourism, situated at the intersection of gastronomy, cultural heritage, and environmental resource use,
offers a relevant setting for the integration of CSR and PR. As a sector, it fosters economic development
and destination branding (Yeoman & McMaho n-Beatte, 2016; BBVA, 2022), but it also raises sustainability
concerns linked to agricultural pressure, food waste, and over -consumption (Fraser et al., 2023; Shi et al.,
2022). Scholars have pointed to the potential of circular food systems and bioeconomy strategies to mitigate
these impacts and promote responsible production (Alonso-Muñoz et al., 2022; Rinn et al., 2023).
At the same time, food tourism is deeply embedded in consumer culture, which complicates efforts to drive
behavioral change. Visitors are key stakeholders, yet their environmental awareness and sense of
responsibility often remain limited (Paskova & Zelenk a, 2019). This highlights the importance of effective
sustainability communication that can engage consumers emotionally and cognitively, fostering more
responsible food-related travel behaviors (Colistra & Passalacqua, 2019; Kretschmer & Winkler, 2024).
Taken together, the literature suggests a growing but still insufficient integration of CSR, PR, and
environmental governance in food tourism. While there is increasing attention to ESG metrics and
sustainability performance in hospitality (García et al., 2024), systematic reviews of how these strategies
intersect—especially in terms of communication, legitimacy, and stakeholder engagement —remain scarce.
This study aims to address that gap by synthesizing recent scholarship and mapping out the connections
between food tourism, sustainability practices, corporate communication, and the SDGs.

3. Methodology
To map the academic landscape of food tourism in relation to sustainability, public relations, and the
Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), this study applies a systematic review methodology based on the
PRISMA framework. The review combines qualitative s ynthesis with bibliometric analysis to provide an
overview of key research trends, influential contributors, and thematic priorities.
The Web of Science (WoS) Core Collection database was selected as the primary source of academic
literature due to its coverage of high -impact journals and multidisciplinary indexing. The search strategy
included combinations of keywords such as “food tour ism,” “sustainability,” “public relations,” “CSR,” and
“SDGs,” applied to the title, abstract, and keyword fields. To ensure relevance, the query was limited to
peer-reviewed articles and reviews published in English between January 2020 and March 2024. Wh ile the
Web of Science (WoS) Core Collection was selected due to its rigorous indexing standards, high -quality
peer-reviewed coverage, and compatibility with bibliometric tools such as VOSviewer, this choice also
ensures consistency in citation and metadat a analysis. However, this decision may exclude relevant
contributions indexed in Scopus, Google Scholar, or practitioner -oriented outlets, particularly in regional or
applied tourism research. Therefore, results should be interpreted as representative of high-impact academic
production rather than the entirety of global scholarship.
After initial retrieval, duplicate entries were removed. Records were screened based on title and abstract
content to exclude publications unrelated to the thematic scope. The final sample consisted of 139
documents meeting the inclusion criteria (1) explicit reference to food tourism or gastronomic tourism; (2)
a focus on sustainability or CSR-related practices; and (3) relevance to communication, public engagement,
or stakeholder interaction. Studies focused purely on food science, nutrition, or unrelated tourism segments
were excluded.
For the bibliometric component, the dataset was exported in plain text format compatible with VOSviewer
software. Co-occurrence analyses were performed on keywords, authors, and countries to visualize the
intellectual structure and geographic distribution of research activity. Thematic clusters were identified based
on keyword proximity, while citation mapping highlighted the most influential works and journals.
This mixed-method approach allows for both depth and breadth in analyzing how food tourism research
engages with sustainability discourses and corporate practices. It also enables the identification of research
gaps and emerging areas of inquiry relevant t o CSR, environmental management, and communication
strategies within the tourism and food sectors.
The article selection process followed the PRISMA methodological framework, as shown in Figure 1. From
an initial set of 367 documents retrieved from the Web of Science database, duplicate and irrelevant studies
were removed through a multi-stage selection process (identification, screening, and eligibility). Only articles
containing at least one of the defined keywords and explicitly addressing one or more Sustainable
Development Goals (SDGs) were included in this review. As a result of this process, a fin al sample of 139
peer-reviewed articles met all the established inclusion criteria.
Although this review identifies communication -related keywords, a more granular sub -analysis focusing
exclusively on PR-oriented studies could further refine understanding of communicative strategies in food
tourism sustainability. This represents a promising avenue for future research.

3.1. Bibliometric Analysis
To complement the systematic review, a bibliometric analysis was conducted to map the intellectual
structure and research dynamics of food tourism, sustainability, and corporate responsibility. This method
allows the identification of thematic clusters, influential authors, and collaboration networks, offering a
quantitative overview of the field in addition to the qualitative synthesis provided by the review.
The metadata from the 139 selected articles were exported from the Web of Science Core Collection in plain
text format and analyzed with VOSviewer. This software was chosen because of its capacity to generate
visualizations of co-authorship networks, keywo rd co-occurrence, and citation mapping, which are widely
used in bibliometric research across the social and environmental sciences.
For the keyword analysis, a minimum occurrence threshold was established to ensure that only the most
relevant terms were included in the maps. Terms with fewer than five co -occurrences were excluded.
Similarly, for co-authorship and citation networks, thresholds were applied to highlight authors, institutions,
and countries with significant contributions to the field. These parameters were defined to balance
comprehensiveness with interpretability, avoiding excessive fragmentation of clusters.
The bibliometric analysis produced three main types of networks:
● Keyword co-occurrence maps are used to detect thematic clusters and emerging topics.
● Co-authorship and country collaboration maps, which identify leading contributors and
international research hubs.
● Citation and co-citation networks, which highlight the most influential works and journals
shaping the field.

5. Discussion
This systematic review confirms that the food industry within tourism has the potential to act both as a
driver and as a constraint in the achievement of the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). As previous
studies emphasize (Leyva & Parra, 2021; Tarashka r et al., 2024; Sheykhan et al., 2024), food -related
businesses can contribute to sustainable transitions when adopting models inspired by the bioeconomy,
reducing resource dependency, and embedding sustainability into value creation. Territories that adopt these
approaches are more likely to strengthen resilience and unlock new development opportunities. In this
context, public relations (PR) emerges as a strategic tool to raise awareness, foster stakeholder engagement,
and reinforce the legitimacy of sustainability-orientated initiatives.
The results of this review also demonstrate that the agri-food sector linked to tourism plays a largely positive
role in advancing the 2030 Agenda, yet gaps remain in terms of scope and integration. Echoing Romero
Martín et al. (2023), this study extends p rior knowledge by combining theoretical and empirical insights to
clarify the obstacles and opportunities of embedding sustainability in food tourism. The role of tourism and
food enterprises as corporate agents of sustainability is particularly significan t: by influencing extensive
supply chains, these firms are in a position to implement ESG-aligned practices such as local procurement,
circular food systems, reduction of single -use plastics, and investment in green infrastructure. Recent
evidence confirms that hospitality and tourism firms adopting robust ESG strategies experience reduced
corporate risk and greater financial resilience, in addition to reputational benefits (García et al., 2024;
Martínez-Falcó et al., 2025).
To further explore the role of communication within food tourism sustainability, a sub -analysis of the
reviewed literature was conducted focusing on the types and functions of public relations (PR) and
communication strategies.
The findings reveal three dominant communication approaches. First, awareness -oriented communication,
which aims to inform stakeholders about sustainability issues through campaigns, storytelling, and branding
strategies. Second, behaviour -change communica tion, which seeks to influence consumer practices, for

Most studies emphasize external communication directed at consumers and stakeholders, particularly

rather than transformational, highlighting a critical gap between communication practices and sustainability
outcomes.
Nonetheless, few studies explicitly link food tourism practices to specific SDGs. While SDGs 2 (Zero Hunger),
3 (Good Health and Well-Being), 8 (Decent Work and Economic Growth), 12 (Responsible Consumption and
Production), and 13 (Climate Action) are relatively well covered in the literature (Dolnicar, 2023), other goals
such as SDG 4 (Quality Education), SDG 5 (Gender Equality), and SDG 16 (Peace, Justice, and Strong
Institutions) remain marginally addressed. These findings point to the fragmented treatment of the SDGs in
tourism research, with greater attention paid to environmental and economic dimensions than to issues of
equity, governance, or institutional strength. As Buczacki et al. (2021) argue, systemic perspectives are
essential for understanding  interdependencies among SDGs and for designing strategies that go beyond
isolated interventions.
Beyond the academic analysis, the implications for practice are substantial. Tourism and hospitality firms
that integrate gastronomy into their core offerings act as facilitators of the SDGs when they embed
sustainability into business operations —through s ustainable sourcing, reduction of packaging waste, or
transparent communication of ESG commitments. These businesses influence wide value chains, impacting
not only environmental outcomes but also rural development, food security, and social equity (Scheyvens &
Laeis, 2021; Moreno Alarcón & Cole, 2019). Embedding sustainability in corporate strategies and reinforcing
these commitments through authentic PR narratives strengthens organizational legitimacy and
accountability in sustainability discourse.
For instance, several hospitality businesses have implemented food waste reduction programmes aligned
with SDG 12, using digital monitoring systems and consumer awareness campaigns to minimise surplus.
Others have adopted local sourcing strategies, support ing rural producers and contributing to SDGs 2 and
8 by strengthening local economies and reducing supply chain emissions.
In terms of environmental management, initiatives such as the adoption of renewable energy systems or
the reduction of single -use plastics have been increasingly integrated into tourism operations. From a
communication perspective, companies are employing storytelling-based branding, sustainability reports,
and social media campaigns to communicate their ESG commitments and engage stakeholders. These
examples illustrate how the integration of CSR practices and communication strategies can translate
sustainability principles into tangible business actions.

The interconnections identified in this review highlight both opportunities and blind spots. While the
economic goals (SDG 8 and SDG 12) and ecological goals (SDG 14 and SDG 15) are recurrent in food tourism
research, social goals such as SDG 5 and SDG 10 remain underexplored.
Finally, partnerships (SDG 17) are mentioned but not systematically assessed. This uneven coverage
confirms the need for interdisciplinary approaches that capture the systemic role of food tourism across the
17 goals, bridging environmental management, CSR, and stakeholder communication.
The sustainability practices identified in the reviewed literature —such as food waste reduction, local
sourcing, renewable energy adoption, and circular food systems —can be systematically aligned with
established CSR and ESG frameworks. Environmental practices correspond primarily to the “E” dimension of
ESG, while stakeholder engagement and transparency relate to governance and social responsibility.
However, the limited role assigned to communication suggests that governance mechanisms are often
operationalized without a parallel communicative strategy.
Rather than constituting a limitation, the low visibility of communication and public relations within the
reviewed literature should be interpreted as a significant finding. It reveals a structural gap in food tourism
sustainability research, where governance and operational practices advance more rapidly than the
communicative mechanisms needed to legitimize, scale, and institutionalize them.
This gap calls for a more integrative perspective that connects operational practices with communication
and governance mechanisms. Building on the previous discussion, and particularly on the identified gap
between operational sustainability practices and  communication mechanisms, a conceptual framework is
proposed to illustrate how food tourism, corporate social responsibility (CSR), environmental management,
and public relations contribute to the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) . The model conceptual ises
food tourism as a driver of CSR and environmental management practices, including sustainable sourcing,
waste reduction, and energy efficiency. Public relations and communication strategies mediate and amplify
these practices, playing a critical role in shaping stakeholder perceptions, trust, and engagement.
In this framework, public relations acts not only as a communication tool but as a governance mechanism
that translates corporate sustainability actions into socially legitimised practices. Effective communication
can foster behavioural change among consum ers and reinforce accountability, thereby enhancing the
contribution of tourism activities to the SDGs. The model also incorporates contextual factors such as
governance structures, cultural dynamics, and consumer awareness as moderating variables that influence
the effectiveness of these relationships. By integrating operational, communicative, and institutional
dimensions, this framework provides a holistic perspective on how food tourism can contribute to sustainable
development.

6. Conclusions
This study demonstrates that food tourism is not only a driver of cultural and experiential value but also a
significant vector of sustainability transitions. By linking gastronomy to the material and immaterial heritage
of destinations, food tourism contributes to the achievement of several SDGs, particularly those related to
innovation (SDG 9), climate action (SDG 13), sustainable communities (SDG 11), health and well-being (SDG
3), and responsible consumption (SDG 12). The bibliometric evidence—139 articles published between 2020
and 2024—confirms that interest in sustainable food tourism and its communicative dimensions is rapidly
expanding, with strong contributions from China, the United States, and Spain. These findings highlight the
emergence of research communities addressing the intersection of tourism, food systems, and sustainability.
The analysis also underscores the role of food and hospitality companies as corporate actors with wide -
reaching impacts on sustainability. By embedding ESG-aligned practices—such as sourcing local ingredients,
reducing single -use plastics, and investing in  green infrastructure —firms in the sector can reduce
environmental footprints while reinforcing resilience and competitiveness. Public relations plays a strategic
role in this process, not only by amplifying sustainability messages but also by embedding th em in
governance frameworks that connect corporate practice with institutional expectations. Transparent

as responsible actors within the 2030 Agenda.
At the same time, the review reveals important gaps. Research continues to emphasize environmental and
economic dimensions of the SDGs, while goals related to equity, education, and institutional strength (e.g.,
SDG 4, SDG 5, and SDG 16) remain underexplor ed. This uneven coverage points to the need for more
holistic approaches that consider social justice, governance, and systemic interconnections among SDGs. In
particular, the integration of food tourism into multi -stakeholder partnerships (SDG 17) remains  an open
field for future inquiry and practice. This uneven coverage is a reflection of the fact that in current literature
the study of some SDGs predominates over others, and this is one of the findings that we wanted to show.
While the search and selection process identified a total of 139 articles that met the established inclusion
criteria, a subset of 80 studies was selected for the literature review. This decision was based on the need
to ensure an in -depth and conceptually coherent analysis, prioritizing articles with greater thematic
relevance, methodologic al clarity, and substantial contribution to the study's theoretical framework. In
particular, studies that explicitly addressed the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) in relation to the
analyzed topic were considered, as well as those that presented theoretical or empirical approaches directly
linked to the research objectives. The remaining articles, although relevant from a bibliometric perspective,
were excluded from the narrative review because they presented a tangential treatment of the topic or a
limited contribution to the study's conceptual development.
This study is limited by its reliance on the Web of Science database, which, although comprehensive,
excludes relevant contributions from other sources. The keyword strategy may also have restricted
coverage, as the use of “PR” as an abbreviation for public relations may have omitted some relevant studies.
Furthermore, the analysis focused on food tourism, but similar approaches could be applied to related
sectors such as green or adventure tourism. Future research would benefit from mixed methodologies that
include consumer and citizen perspectives across cultures, as well as longitudinal analyses of SDG-oriented
interventions at territorial and institutional levels. Such extensions would enrich our understanding of how
food tourism can contribute to both c orporate responsibility and environmental governance. Additionally,
this work identifies future lines of research that could focus on analyzing how gastronomic tourism influences
gender relations and perspectives within local communities, especially in ter ms of economic participation,
labor distribution, and cultural representation. It would also be relevant to study whether the increased
tourist demand for local products affects the availability and accessibility of nutritious and affordable food
for the r esident population, potentially generating social and food inequalities. Furthermore, it would be
pertinent to explore how public policies and tourism development strategies can simultaneously contribute
to the preservation of gastronomic heritage and the achievement of the Sustainable Development Goals
related to gender equality, the reduction of inequalities, and food security.
This review demonstrates that public relations is not a peripheral complement to sustainability in food
tourism, but a central governance mechanism for aligning corporate practices with the SDGs. The systematic
underrepresentation of PR in existing research highlights an urgent need to integrate communication more
explicitly into sustainability assessment frameworks. Advancing the 2030 Agenda in food tourism will depend
not only on what companies do, but also on how they communicate, legitimize, and co-create sustainability
with stakeholders.
