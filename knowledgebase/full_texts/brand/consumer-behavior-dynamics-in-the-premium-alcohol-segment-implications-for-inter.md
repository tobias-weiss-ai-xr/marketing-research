Abstract
This empirical study examines the dynamics of consumer behavior in the premium alcohol industry and its relevance to
the positioning strategies of international brands in the emerging markets environment. Based on primary survey data
collected from 452 con sumers living in the city of Lagos, Nigeria, and using partial least squares structural equation
modeling (PLS -SEM), the study evaluates an integrated model comprising of factors such as social group influence,
luxury value perception, mediation through br and equity, materialism/prestige seeking behavior, risk/benefit
perceptions, and the moderation effect of market volatility. It is found that social group influences and brand equity
have the greatest impact on purchase intention, whereas brand equity medi ates completely the impact of luxury value
perception on brand loyalty. The moderating effect of market volatility significantly enhances the positive effect of
prestige seeking on purchase intention.
Keywords: premium alcohol, consumer behavior, brand equity, emerging markets, market volatility, brand positioning,
prestige-seeking, PLS-SEM
1. Introduction
Premium alcohol covering high -end spirits, fine wines, and craft beers —has emerged as one of the fastest -growing
segments in the global alcoholic beverage market (Ameyibor et al., 2022). This growth is largely driven by increasing
consumer aspirations for status, exclusivity, and experiential consumption. The trend is especially strong in emerging
markets (EMs), where a rapidly expanding middle class often uses luxury purchases to signal upward social mobility. At
the same time, these markets are marked by significant volatility, including economic instability, currency depreciation,
inflation, regulatory changes, and socio-political uncertainty, all of which strongly influence consumer decision -making
(Kaunda & Ndoro, 2023). In such contexts, international brands must go beyond traditional preference analysis and
account for the interaction between economic turbulence and aspirational consumption.
Consumer behavior in the premium alcohol segment is shaped by a complex mix of psychological, social, and cultural
factors that influence purchase intention, brand loyalty, and long-term brand equity. Social group influence is particularly
important in collectivist EM contexts, where consumers often rely on peer validation and reference groups to guide
purchasing decisions and signal status (Cunningham, 2023). This social influence is further shaped by generational
differences and shifting lifestyle preferences, which affect how various age groups perceive and engage with premium
brands.
Luxury value perception is another central driver. Functional, individual, and social dimensions of luxury such as quality,
self-expression, and prestige play a key role in shaping preferences for premium beer and spirits. Evidence from Latin
America shows that consumers often balance exclusivity with value -for-money considerations in volatile environments
(Thomé  et al., 2016). Similarly, in Asian EMs, wine consumption reflects a nuanced evaluation process involving
price-quality cues, brand reputation, and sensory expectations, underscoring the importance of adapting global brand
strategies to local preferences (Mehta & Bhanja, 2018).
Risk and benefit perceptions add further complexity, particularly under uncertain economic conditions. For example,
consumers in India assess health, financial, and social risks alongside the perceived prestige and enjoyment of wine
consumption, which dire ctly shapes their attitudes and intentions (Gupta & Sajnani, 2020). In volatile markets, these

Cross-cultural evidence reinforc es the importance of context. In China, a major premium alcohol market, consumption

remain underexplored.
This study addresses these gaps through an empirical investigation in Lagos, Nigeria, a representative example of a
volatile yet high -potential emerging market. Using primary survey data from urban consumers and analyzed through
partial least squares struct ural equation modeling (PLS -SEM), it develops and tests an integrated model of consumer
behavior in the premium alcohol segment. The findings offer both theoretical insights into the interaction of key drivers
and practical guidance for international brands seeking to strengthen their positioning in uncertain environments.

outlines the methodology; Section 4 presents the empirical findings ; Section 5 discusses the results; Section 6 provides

2. Literature Review
2.1 The Premium Alcohol Segment in Volatile Emerging Markets
The premium alcohol segment comprising high-end spirits, fine wines, and craft or super-premium beers priced well above
mass-market options has become a fast -evolving and rapidly growing part of the global beverage industry. In volatile
emerging markets (EMs), this categor y is expanding even more quickly, fueled by rising middle -class aspirations,
urbanization, and a strong desire for status-driven consumption (Ameyibor et al., 2022; Kaunda & Ndoro, 2023). Countries
such as Nigeria, India, South Africa, Vietnam, China, Brazil, and Mexico exemplify this trend, while also facing economic
instability, currency fluctuations, inflation, and regulatory uncertainty. These conditions create a setting where consumer
behavior is shaped by both aspiration and heightened sensitivity to risk (Macinko et al., 2015; Xie et al., 2025).
To understand consumer behavior in this segment, it is necessary to adopt a multidimensional perspective that
incorporates psychological, social, cultural, and economic influences. Unlike more stable developed markets where brand
loyalty tends to be relatively consistent volatile EMs intensify the importance of external signals such as social validation,
perceived luxury value, and brand equity. At the same time, market instability acts as a moderating force th at reshapes
purchase intentions and loyalty patterns (Cunningham, 2023; Thomé  et al., 2016). This review brings together existing
empirical findings, evaluates key drivers, and highlights gaps that this study aims to address.
2.2 Social and Psychological Drivers of Premium Alcohol Consumption
Social group influence is one of the strongest determinants of purchase intention in EMs. Consumers often depend on
peers, family, and broader social networks for validation, especially in collectivist societies where conformity and status
signaling enhance perceived social standing (Cunningham, 2023). These dynamics are further shaped by generational
differences. For instance, younger consumers in India show distinct beer preferences influenced by digital culture and
evolving lifestyle aspirations compared to older cohorts (Rai, 2024).
Luxury value perception is another critical factor, encompassing functional (quality and utility), individual
(self-expression and enjoyment), and social (prestige and exclusivity) dimens ions. In Brazil’s premium beer market,
consumers heavily rely on these values to signal higher social status despite economic uncertainty (Thomé  et al., 2016).
Similar patterns are observed in emerging Asian wine markets, where consumers carefully evaluate  attributes such as
origin, price, and sensory qualities (Mehta & Bhanja, 2018; Payini et al., 2022). Notably, Indian women consumers often
prioritize experiential and social attributes over purely functional ones (Payini et al., 2022).
Risk and benefit perceptions further complicate decision-making. In uncertain environments, consumers weigh potential
social gains such as status enhancement against financial, health, and social risks. This balance directly influences
attitudes and intentions toward premium  alcohol consumption (Gupta & Sajnani, 2020). During periods of heightened
volatility, these considerations become even more pronounced, leading to more cautious or strategically compensatory
consumption behaviors.

2.3 Brand Equity, Positioning, and Loyalty Mechanisms
Brand equity plays a central mediating role in the premium alcohol sector. Strong brand positioning enhances
performance largely through building brand equity, which reduces perceived risk and strengthens customer loyalty
(Ameyibor et al., 202 2). Evidence from Ghana shows that brand equity fully mediates the relationship between
positioning strategies and firm outcomes, while studies in Vietnam confirm its strong influence on customer loyalty even
in unstable environments (Do Huynh, 2023).
Additional research highlights the role of consumer skepticism as a moderating factor, emphasizing the importance of
trust-building strategies for international brands (Ewur et al., 2025; Francioni et al., 2022). Customer-focused positioning
further reinforces  brand equity by aligning offerings with local expectations (Dressler & Paunovic, 2021).
Prestige-seeking behavior, rooted in Veblen’s theory of conspicuous consumption, also strengthens these dynamics, as
consumers use premium products to signal social mobility (Lee et al., 2019; Kaunda & Ndoro, 2023).
2.4 Cross-Cultural and Contextual Influences
Consumer preferences vary significantly across cultural contexts. In China, wine consumption reflects a mix of traditional
values, Western influences, and rapid e conomic development, resulting in patterns distinct from Western markets
(Camillo, 2012; Lockshin et al., 2017; Wang, 2017). Regional differences within China also shape beer consumption
drivers, highlighting the need for localized strategies (Xie et al., 2025). In Latin America, loyalty in wine and beer markets
is influenced by both experiential factors and price sensitivity (Leyva-Herná ndez et al., 2025; Haddad et al., 2023), while
broader consumption patterns reflect underlying socio-economic conditions (Macinko et al., 2015).
Country-of-origin effects remain relevant, though their importance varies across products and segments (Guardato et al.,
2023; Canziani & Byrd, 2017). Conjoint analyses of whiskey attributes provide detailed insights into consumer trade-offs,
supporting more precise positioning strategies (Patwardhan et al., 2025). Emerging trends, such as interest in low - and
no-alcohol products among South African millennials, further illustrate evolving consumption dynamics (Steinhobel et al.,
2024). Additional factors like experiential consumption, brand love, and sustainability considerations also contribute to
loyalty formation (Gevers, 2019; Hoang et al., 2026; Drennan et al., 2015).
2.5 Synthesis, Research Gap, and Conceptual Framework
Although existing studies provide valuable insights into individual drivers, the literature remains fragmented. There is a
clear lack of integrated empirical models that simultaneously examine social group influences, luxury value perceptions,
brand equity mediation, prestige-seeking behavior, risk-benefit assessments, and the moderating role of market volatility
on purchase intention and loyalty. This gap is particularly evident in frontier markets like Nigeria, where volatility is
especially pronounced.

2.6 Aim and Objectives of the Study
This study aims to empirically analyze co nsumer behavior in the premium alcohol segment and derive strategic
implications for international brand positioning in volatile emerging markets, using Nigeria as a representative case.
The specific objectives are:
1. To examine the direct effects of social group influence, luxury value perception, prestige -seeking, and
risk/benefit perceptions on purchase intention and brand loyalty.
2. To evaluate the mediating role of brand equity between luxury value perception and both purchase intention and
loyalty.
3. To analyze how perceived market volatility moderates the relationship between prestige -seeking behavior and
consumer outcomes.
4. To provide actionable recommendations for international brands seeking effective positioning in turbulent EM
environments.
By addressing these objectives through primary data and PLS -SEM analysis, the study contributes new theoretical
insights and practical guidance to the fields of consumer behavior, international marketing, and brand strategy.
3. Methodology
3.1 Research Design
In this empirical study, a quantitative, cross -sectional survey design was applied to analyze the dynamics of consumer
behavior within the premium alcohol product category. The deductive approach has been employed to validate the
theoretical assumptio ns proposed based on the reviewed literature. Partial least squares structural equation modeling
(PLS-SEM) was chosen as the primary data analysis method, as it is highly suited for complicated analysis models that
incorporate mediation and/or moderation e ffects; is appropriate for predictive research; can effectively be applied to
non-normal data; and is suitable for analysis of consumer behavior (Hair et al., 2022).
3.2 Study Context
The research has been carried out in Lagos, Nigeria, which is considered  an archetypal example of a volatile emerging
market experiencing high levels of economic instability and fluctuations, including currency depreciation, inflation, and
fast urbanization. Nigeria can be regarded as one of the biggest and fastest -developing alcohol markets in Africa, where
the premium segment experiences premiumization amid the socioeconomic instability (Ameyibor et al., 2022). As the
largest city and the commercial capital of Nigeria, Lagos is an ideal place to examine the urban consumer beh avior
towards premium alcoholic beverages (spirits, wines, and beers priced above ₦15,000 per bottle). Therefore, it allows
providing generalizable conclusions for the analysis of similar market conditions faced by foreign international brands in
such frontier markets.
3.3 Population, Sample, and Sampling Procedure
The study's target population included urban adult consumers aged 25–55, residing in Lagos, Nigeria, and purchasing and
consuming premium alcohol products (spirits, wines, or premium beer) at least once during the previous year. The chosen
age limit represents the economically active part of the population that is responsible for premiumization in developing
countries (Kaunda & Ndoro, 2023).
Purposive sampling and snowballing have been used as sam pling methods and supplemented by the quota sampling
controls for age, gender, and income levels. An online survey was conducted using structured questions distributed via
business networks, social media pages, and consumer panels between October and Decem ber 2025. 682 questionnaires
have been gathered in total, and 452 valid surveys were obtained after data cleaning (response rate = 66.3%). The
chosen sample size exceeds the recommended minimum threshold for PLS -SEM (at least 200 -300 samples) and
provides enough statistical power to detect the medium effect size.

3.7 Data Analysis Methods
The data collected will be subjected to analysis using the SmartPLS 4.0 program. Measurement model analysis is the first
step where Cronbach’s Alpha and Composite Reliability (should exceed 0.70), Convergent Validity (average variance
extracted exceeding 0.50) and Discriminant Va lidity (HTMT ratio below 0.85) will be tested. Path analysis, mediator

analyzed based on standardized root mean squared residual (SRM R should be lower than 0.08) and predictive relevance
(Q² higher than 0). Harman’s single factor test and the full collinearity approaches will be used to verify common method
biases.

4. Results
This chapter presents the empirical results of the PLS -SEM analysis carried out using the software SmartPLS 4.0.
Before testing the hypotheses, the data were examined for missing values, outliers, and potential common method
variance. No major issues were identified, as Harman’s single -factor test accounted for 38.4% of the total variance,
which is below the 50% cut-off.
The analysis proceeded in stages, beginning with the assessment of the measurement model, followed by the structural
model, and then the mediation and moderation effects. All estimations were performed using 5,000 bootstrap resamples.

5. Discussion
Overall, this study makes several unique contributions to the academic literature regarding consumer behavior dynamics
in the premium alcohol category in an unstable emerging market environment. The application of an integrative
PLS-SEM model to the survey responses of 452 urban consumers in Lagos, Nigeria allowed for the confirmation of
four out of seven hypotheses, including the critical role of social group influences, luxury value perceptions, and brand
equity as well as prestige -seeking and perceived market volatility as moderating fac tors. Notably, the findings indicate
that brand equity serves as the key mediator between perceived luxury value and purchase intention and brand loyalty,
making this construct even more central to premium alcohol purchasing decisions.
5.1 Interpretation of Key Findings
Social group influences emerged as the most significant direct determinant of purchase intention (H1), indicating that
reference groups retain their essential influence over premium alcohol consumption. Specifically, in collectivist cultures
prevalent in emerging markets, consumers' choices are heavily driven by the need to gain approval from their families,
friends, and broader social networks when selecting premium products. This finding is consistent with the view of
premium alcohol as a form of social capital and status signaling.
Furthermore, perceived luxury value proved to be a strong antecedent of brand equity (H2). The strong mediating effect
of brand equity (H4) suggests that perceived luxury alone does not directly translate into hi gher purchase intention or
loyalty; instead, it operates through the development  of durable brand associations. This highlights the importance of
sustained brand equity investment in environments characterized by economic instability and recurring shocks.
Finally, materialism and prestige -seeking behavior were identified as additional drivers of purchase intention (H5).
Importantly, perceived market volatility acted as a significant moderator, strengthening the influence of
prestige-seeking on purchase inte ntion (H7). This indicates that in conditions of economic uncertainty, consumers may
increase their consumption of premium alcohol as a means of status signaling and psychological compensation. In

5.2 Theoretical Contributions
From a theoretical perspective, this study contributes to consumer behavior and interna tional marketing literature in
several ways. First, it integrates social group influences, luxury value perceptions, and prestige -seeking within a unified
framework, demonstrating their interconnectedness through brand equity as a central mediating mechani sm. By
confirming full mediation, the study refines earlier models that treated these constructs as independent predictors of
purchase behavior.
Second, the introduction of perceived market volatility as a moderating boundary condition represents a novel
theoretical extension. It demonstrates how macroeconomic instability intensifies prestige -driven consumption, enriching
theories of conspicuous consumption and status signaling by linking them to structural economic conditions rather than
purely psychological drivers.
Third, by focusing on Nigeria, the study extends the geographic scope of brand equity theory into under -researched
African emerging markets. It shows how brand equity operates under conditions of currency depreciation and inflation,
thereby enhancing the generalizability of existing theory beyond relatively stable emerging economies.
5.3 Comparison with Prior Empirical Research
The strong effect of social group influences supports prior findings that reference groups are especially influential i n
collectivist cultures when consumers evaluate high-involvement luxury products.
The mediating role of brand equity aligns with research from Ghana and Vietnam, where brand equity similarly drives
performance and loyalty. This study extends those findings  by demonstrating full mediation in a more volatile frontier
market context.
Luxury value perceptions and prestige-seeking effects are consistent with evidence from Brazil and South Africa, where
social and symbolic dimensions of luxury consumption are pro minent. However, this study adds that such effects are
intensified under conditions of economic volatility, a contribution not captured in earlier work.
Risk/benefit perceptions show a weaker influence compared to findings in India, suggesting contextual d ifferences in
decision-making priorities. In Nigeria’s premium segment, social signaling appears to dominate risk considerations,
likely due to the symbolic role of premium alcohol during economic uncertainty.
6. Conclusion
This empirical research provides a thorough investigation into consumer behavior dynamics within the premium alcohol
segment in the context of highly volatile emerging markets, where Nigeria was used as a representative setting. Primary
data collected via survey questionnaires from a sample of 452 respondents in urban Lagos revealed a significant impact of
peer social influence, luxury value perceptions, and status -oriented materialism on purchase intention and brand loyalty.
Brand equity turned out to be an integral mediating factor, wh ich entirely accounted for the luxury perception effects.
Perceived market volatility was found to serve as a boundary condition which significantly increased the effect of prestige
on behavioral intentions.
Several theoretical contributions can be highlig hted concerning the current state of consumer behavior, international
marketing, and brand management literature. First, the study integrates fragmented concepts into a cohesive framework,
allowing us to explore the relationships between social influences, luxury perceptions, brand equity, prestige, risk/benefit
assessment, and market volatility simultaneously in the context of premium alcohol in a frontier emerging market.
Specifically, the complete mediating role of brand equity in the examined scenario is confirmed, offering insight into how
sustainable brand outcomes are formed under unstable conditions. The role of market volatility as a moderator further
extends theories of conspicuous consumption and status signaling, particularly in environments wher e instability is
structural rather than temporary.
From a managerial perspective, the findings suggest clear implications for premium alcohol brands operating in volatile
emerging markets. Consistent investment in brand equity and the use of social proof s trategies can help convert
aspirational demand into long -term loyalty, even under economic uncertainty. Marketing communications should
therefore emphasize prestige, identity, and social standing rather than focusing solely on functional or price -based
appeals. These insights are especially relevant as global alcohol companies increasingly expand into fast -growing but
economically unstable regions. For instance, during periods of currency depreciation, international brands could
implement dynamic loyalty pr ograms with tiered pricing or run targeted social media campaigns featuring local
Nigerian influencers that portray premium alcohol consumption as a symbol of resilience, aspiration, and social success.

Although the study offers meaningful contributions, s everal limitations should be acknowledged. The cross -sectional
design, single-country focus (Nigeria), and the sample limited to urban consumers in Lagos recruited primarily through
online channels and snowball sampling restrict the ability to make strong causal claims and limit broader generalization
to rural or non-digital populations in other emerging markets.Future research could employ longitudinal designs to track
changes over time or conduct cross -country comparisons involving markets such as India, Vietnam, South Africa, and
Brazil. Further refinement of the model could also include additional moderators such as cultural orientation and digital
social engagement. In addition, qualitative or mixed -method approaches could provide deeper insight into co nsumer
motivations in volatile economic environments.
In conclusion, this research highlights the complex interaction of psychological, social, and contextual forces shaping
premium alcohol consumption behavior. It demonstrates how brand equity and prestig e motives interact with market
volatility to influence consumer decisions, offering both theoretical advancement and practical guidance. Ultimately, it
shows that success in volatile emerging markets depends not only on product quality or global branding, but on a deep
understanding of how consumers use luxury consumption to navigate uncertainty and construct social identity.
