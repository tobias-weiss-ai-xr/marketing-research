# Decoding hotel brand loyalty in the digital era: A multidimensional analysis of online feedback, service quality, and customer engagement in western Thailand

Abstract
  The study aims to elucidate the impact of online guest feedback on hotel
selection in Western Thailand. Its objective is to increase understanding of how
internet users’ reviews influence satisfaction, engagement, and loyalty in the
luxury hotel sector. Based on a comprehensive literature review, the research
examines key factors affecting online guest reviews: guest feedback, hotel
management response, and customer decision-making processes. These factors,
in turn, impact levels of service quality, customer satisfaction, and engagement
in the hotel and tourism industry in Western Thailand. The practical problem
arises when there is a decline in customer engagement and loyalty. Hotel
management particularly faces challenges in addressing negative comments in
the online market. The quantitative method tested the validity of measurements
and the correlation of constructs through GSCAM and NCA tools. Four hundred
and three respondents participated in the survey, primarily associated with
the hotel and tourism profession. The results demonstrate that the customer’s
decision-making process and guest online feedback have the most significant
impact on overall service quality, which in turn influences customer satisfaction
and engagement behaviors. The study’s implications are to foster luxury hotel
management and sustainable tourism by effectively managing online reviews
in developing regions. Limitations and suggestions for future research are
also discussed to contribute to an overall understanding of existing and future
hospitality studies.

Introduction
 In the current digital era, the internet has become a
crucial source of information, significantly influencing
consumer decision-making across various industries,
particularly in the hotel and tourism sectors (V o et al.,
2019a; V o et al., 2019b). The rapid growth of information
technology has compelled most hotel businesses to adapt
by developing their own websites and collaborating with
online travel agencies (OTAs) as effective tools for sales
and marketing. Online reviews from previous guests have
emerged as a critical factor influencing tourists' decisions
to choose hotel services. Korfiatis et al. (2012) found that
reading reviews greatly impacts decision-making, while
Malik and Hussain (2018) highlighted that the content of
reviews is highly effective for information seekers.
 Previous studies have confirmed the importance of
online reviews in consumer decision-making processes.
Erkan and Evans (2016) found that electronic word-of-
mouth (e-WOM) on social media significantly influences
consumers' purchase intentions. Furthermore, Ukpabi
and Karjaluoto (2017) noted that user reviews on OTA
websites have a substantial impact on consumer behavior.
This is consistent with Sparks et al. (2016), who affirmed
that consumers actively engage with online reviews
when evaluating hotels. However, Ahmad and Sun
(2018) cautioned that unreliable reviews can undermine
consumer trust.
 The response of hotel management to online feedback
plays a crucial role in guests' decision-making processes.
Sparks et al. (2016) demonstrated that management
responses can mitigate the risk of readers misinterpreting
negative feedback, particularly in cases where negative
comments are made. Li et al. (2017) found that the
frequency, promptness, and length of responses influence
the quality of interactions with customers, reflecting the
complexity of communication between hotels and guests
in the digital era.
 The western region of Thailand is a significant
tourist destination, with a diverse range of hotels
and accommodations. Understanding the factors that
influence guests' decisions to choose hotel services based
on online information in this specific regional context is
therefore essential. This study aims to develop and test
an integrated model that explains the interaction between
online customer feedback, hotel management responses,
decision-making processes, service quality, customer
satisfaction, and customer engagement behaviors, which
ultimately impact hotel brand loyalty and sustainable
tourism in the western region of Thailand. The study
employs quantitative analysis and Necessary Condition
Analysis (NCA) to identify key factors and necessary
conditions for enhancing the sustainability of the hotel
and tourism industry in the digital age.
 This research is expected to provide valuable insights
for hotel operators in the western region of Thailand,
aiding in a deeper understanding of online consumer
behavior. The findings will help operators to develop
effective digital marketing strategies, increase customer
satisfaction, promote engagement, and build long-
term brand loyalty. Additionally, the study will benefit
academics and policymakers by offering insights into
consumer behavior trends in the digital era, leading to
the sustainable development of the tourism and hotel
industry in the western region of Thailand.
Literature Review
 This literature review examines relevant theories and
research to inform the study of hotel selection decisions
by guests in the western region of Thailand based on
online information.
Guest Online Feedback (GOF)
 Online reviews have a significant influence on hotel
selection decisions, particularly in the western region
of Thailand. Casaló et al. (2015) presented empirical
evidence supporting the credibility of online reviews,
which aligns with the current study's finding that guest
online feedback (GOF) has a positive impact on the
perception of overall service quality (OSQ). Mauri and
Minazzi (2013) demonstrated a positive relationship
between online reviews and service quality and customer
satisfaction, while Fuentes (2016) found that customers
are willing to pay more for services that receive positive
reviews. However, Li et al. (2017) emphasized the
negative impact of unfavourable reviews on a hotel's
reputation and booking decisions. Casaló et al. (2015)
showed that hotels with high rankings in reputable online
communities tend to receive more bookings. The current
study confirms the importance of GOF, identifying it
as a necessary condition for hotel brand loyalty and
sustainable tourism (HBST). Although social media is a
key communication channel, Li et al. (2017) warned of
the severe consequences of negative reviews. Effective
management of online reviews is therefore crucial for
hotel operators in the western region.

Hotel Management Response (HMR)
 The response of hotel management to online feedback
plays a crucial role in the decision-making process
of guests. Sparks et al. (2016) provided evidence
that management responses can mitigate the risk of
misinterpreting negative feedback, particularly when
dealing with negative comments. Li et al. (2017)
found that the frequency, promptness, and length of
management responses significantly affect the quality
of interactions with customers, reflecting the complexity
of communication between hotels and guests in the
digital era. For hotel operators in the western region of
Thailand, Kim and Busser (2018) recommend monitoring
online discussions and real-time reviews across multiple
platforms to maintain their reputation and respond
promptly to customer needs. Effectively managing
online reviews, both positive and negative, is therefore
a crucial strategy for hotel operators in the western
region. Such efforts not only enhance credibility but also
attract new customers through online channels. In an
era where digital communication is paramount, timely
and appropriate responses to customer feedback are key
factors in gaining a competitive advantage in the hotel
industry (Kim & Busser, 2018; Li et al., 2017; Sparks et
al., 2016).
Decision-Making Process (DMP)
 The decision-making process for selecting hotel
services is a complex phenomenon, influenced by both
internal and external factors. The development of internet
technology has significantly transformed how consumers
choose and book hotels, particularly in the western
region of Thailand. Numerous studies have demonstrated
a positive relationship between online reviews and
booking decisions (Chan et al., 2017; Mauri & Minazzi,
2013). Additionally, electronic word-of-mouth (e-WOM)
communication is widely recognized as an effective tool
for attracting new customers (Reimer & Benkenstein,
2018). However, there is a caution that consumers are
often more influenced by negative information in the
early stages of decision-making, which can impact their
choice of services. In contrast, online reviews from
reputable travel communities tend to increase booking
intentions (Casaló et al., 2015). For hotel operators
in the western region of Thailand, understanding the
decision-making process of consumers and the influence
of online information is crucial for developing effective
marketing and service strategies. The key challenge
lies in managing both positive and negative reviews
effectively to build a strong reputation and attract new
customers while balancing the benefits of e-WOM with
the potential negative impacts of unfavourable reviews.
Overview of Service Quality and Customer Satisfaction
 The relationship between service quality and
customer satisfaction in the service sector has garnered
extensive attention from researchers worldwide.
Numerous factors have been found to influence customer
satisfaction and loyalty (Rianthong et al., 2016). Online
reviews and management responses play a crucial
role in shaping customer expectations and decisions
(Li et al., 2017; Mauri & Minazzi, 2013). Moreover,
service quality is a fundamental determinant of hotel
performance (Rianthong et al., 2016; Zhao et al., 2018).
Perceived service quality forms the basis for overall
quality perception and customer behavior prediction.
When service quality aligns with customer satisfaction,
customers are more likely to rely on online reviews
and recommend the hotel to others (Berezina et al.,
2016; Mauri & Minazzi, 2013). The impact of online
customer ratings on hotel performance is significant,
with a mere 1 percent increase in ratings potentially
leading to a substantial increase in sales (Ogut & Tas,
2012). Therefore, hotels must prioritize service quality
and customer satisfaction in their operational strategies
to maintain competitiveness and sustainability in the
market.
Customer Engagement Behaviors (CEB)
 Customer Engagement Behaviors (CEBs) play a
crucial role in decision-making processes for hotel
services in the digital age. Zhao et al. (2018) define
CEBs as behaviors that extend beyond transactional
interactions, focusing on brand or company engagement.
In the context of the hotel industry, online reviews are
a key form of CEBs that significantly influence other
consumers' decision-making (Li et al., 2017). Key factors
influencing repeat purchases in the online context include
trust, perceived ease of use, perceived benefits, and
satisfaction, all of which are reflected in online reviews
and impact consumer decisions. Moreover, service quality
is directly related to the generation of positive or negative
reviews, which in turn affect the decisions of those
seeking information. Service recovery measures, such
as refunds, can positively impact satisfaction and CEBs.
However, failure to address customer dissatisfaction may
lead to customer attrition to competitors. Therefore, when
studying the factors influencing hotel selection decisions

Methodology
Measurement and Data Collection
 The present investigation employed a quantitative
methodology, utilizing both Structural Equation
Modeling (SEM) and Necessary Condition Analysis
(NCA). The operationalization of variables was derived
from established measurement scales in extant literature
(Chan et al., 2017; Fuentes, 2016; Li et al., 2020; V o
et al., 2022). The conceptual framework of this study
comprises several constructs, each operationalized
through multiple indicators. Guest Online Feedback was
assessed using a four-item scale, while Hotel Management
Response was measured via a three-item instrument. The
Decision-Making Process construct incorporated four
items, and both Overall Service Quality and Overall
Customer Satisfaction were evaluated using three-item
scales. Customer Engagement Behaviors were captured
through a five-item measure, and Hotel Brand Loyalty
Sustainability was gauged using a three-item scale. All
measurement items underwent contextual adaptation to
align with the study's specific focus on factors influencing
hotel service decisions among guests in Thailand's
western region, based on online information sources.
 Data collection was conducted in July 2024, with
the assistance of research students who distributed
online questionnaire links to the target sample group. A
convenience sampling method was employed to select
participants who had used hotel services within the past
12 months. A total of 403 responses were collected and
subsequently analyzed using statistical methods.
 Descriptive data revealed that 52.6 percent of
respondents were female, 35.0 percent were male, and
12.4 percent chose not to disclose their gender. The
majority of respondents were young adults, with 44.2
percent aged between 16 and 25 years, followed by
those aged 26 to 35 years (32.8%), and individuals over
45 years accounted for 10.4 percent. More than 50% of
respondents reported using the internet for more than 4
hours per day. The most popular booking channel was
online travel agencies (OTAs), utilized by 47.5 percent
of respondents, with 42.9 percent having used OTAs 1
to 3 times. Additionally, the findings indicated that the
majority of respondents were students (38.0%) and had
a monthly income of less than or equal to 12,000 THB
(34.2%).

Analytical Procedures
 This study followed the methodology of Richter
et al. (2020) to conduct a combined analysis using
Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) and Necessary
Condition Analysis (NCA), similar to the approach taken
by Wattanacharoensil et al. (2024). The data were divided
into two sets: a training sample comprising 80 percent of
the data and a testing sample comprising the remaining
20 percent. Although specific measures were not applied
to assess the reliability and validity of the latent variables
within the NCA, the study model was evaluated using
SEM criteria as previously outlined by Richter et al.
(2020).
 Component-based SEM was employed in this study
due to its ability to create appropriate linear composite
scores for subsequent analysis (Chumwichan et al., 2023;
Leruksa et al., 2023; Rasmidatta, 2023). Following the
recommendations of Hwang et al. (2017), GSCAM was
selected as the primary estimator. GSCAM addresses
both the common and unique aspects of indicators,
similar to how factor-based SEM handles measurement
errors. After determining net effects and individual
necessary conditions, the analysis revealed distinct
conditions that were not previously identified in the SEM
analysis. These conditions are necessary and/or sufficient
for the occurrence of the outcome (HBST).
Results
Assessment of the Measurement Model and Net Effects
 In this study, the GSCAM approach was employed
to assess the measurement model, with the results
summarized in Table 1. The validation process confirmed
internal consistency by verifying that common reliability
estimates, such as Dijkstra-Henseler’s rho_A, exceeded
the threshold of 0.7. Convergent validity was affirmed
through all component loadings and average variance
extracted (A VE) values, both of which were above the 0.5
benchmark (Fakfare et al., 2023; Meeprom & Fakfare,
2023). To further assess discriminant validity, the
advanced Heterotrait-Monotrait Ratio (HTMT) criterion
was applied, revealing that all measured values were
below the recommended cutoff of 0.85, thus confirming
discriminant validity. Collinearity was evaluated by
examining the Variance Inflation Factor (VIF), with
all VIF scores generally below 5 (Fakfare et al., 2020;
2021; Hair et al., 2011), indicating no significant issues.
Additionally, the fit measures suggested an acceptable fit
with the data (SRMR = 0.06, GFI = 0.979), confirming
that the measurement model demonstrated both validity
and reliability.

Discussion and Implications
 The primary objective of this study was to
understand the relationships between various factors
influencing overall service quality (OSQ) and overall
customer satisfaction (OCS) within the hotel industry.
The factors examined include Guest Online Feedback
(GOF), Hotel Management Response (HMR), Decision-
Making Process (DMP), Overall Service Quality (OSQ),
Customer Engagement Behaviors (CEB), and Hotel
Brand Loyalty/Sustainable Tourism (HBST).
 The analysis revealed that the Guest Online Feedback
(GOF) model component has a significantly positive
influence on the perception of Overall Service Quality
(OSQ), which aligns with the established hypotheses
(Fuentes, 2016; Li et al., 2020). However, Hotel
Management Response (HMR) did not show a significant
impact on OSQ, suggesting that customers’ perceptions of
hotel management responses may not substantially affect
their evaluation of overall service quality in this context.
In contrast, the Decision-Making Process (DMP) had a
strong influence on OSQ, underscoring the importance
of decision-making in customers’ assessments of service
quality (Mauri & Minazzi, 2013).
 Further analysis also revealed that OSQ has a
significantly positive influence on both Overall Customer
Satisfaction (OCS) and Customer Engagement Behavior
(CEB), underscoring the crucial role of service quality in
fostering customer satisfaction and engagement (Casaló
et al., 2015; Reimer & Benkenstein, 2018). Additionally,
OCS was found to positively influence CEB, and CEB,
in turn, has a significant impact on Hotel Brand Loyalty
and Sustainable Tourism (HBST). This demonstrates
that customer engagement behaviors contribute to long-
term brand loyalty and support for the brand. This study
thus highlights the importance of enhancing customer
satisfaction and engagement to promote brand loyalty
and sustainable tourism (Casaló et al., 2015; Mauri &
Minazzi, 2013).
 In the context of hotel services, customer satisfaction
(OCS) is often influenced by the perceived overall service
quality (OSQ), which aligns with the theory that when
consumers receive high-quality service, they are more
likely to feel satisfied and inclined to return. Moreover,
the perception of service quality is linked to customer
engagement behaviors (CEB), indicating that customers
who perceive the service they receive as high quality are
more likely to engage in behaviors such as posting online
reviews or recommending the service to others. These
behaviors play a key role in fostering brand loyalty and
promoting sustainable tourism (HBST) (Li et al., 2020;
Reimer & Benkenstein, 2018).
 The relationship between service quality and customer
satisfaction is a critical indicator that underscores the
importance of guest online feedback (GOF). This study
demonstrates that GOF has a significant impact on the
perception of overall service quality, allowing consumers
to express pride or dissatisfaction when providing
feedback online. Although Hotel Management Response
(HMR) does not directly affect the perception of service
quality in this context, it can help alleviate any feelings
of guilt that may arise when customers recognize their
role and responsibility in the service process. This finding
highlights the complexity of the interaction between
customers' emotional responses and their decision-
making process when selecting hotel services (Fuentes,
2016).
 Further research reveals that the Decision-Making
Process (DMP) plays a crucial role in shaping customers'
perceived service quality. This aligns with the concept that
when customers are involved in a clear and transparent
decision-making process, they are more likely to feel
confident and perceive service quality more positively.
These feelings can lead to increased satisfaction, which
ultimately enhances Customer Engagement Behaviors
(CEB)—a key factor in fostering brand loyalty and
sustainable tourism (Casaló et al., 2015; Mauri &
Minazzi, 2013).

 Additionally, for the development of Hotel Brand
Loyalty and Sustainable Tourism (HBST), this study

Decision-Making Process (DMP), Overall Service

Theoretical Implications
 This study offers key contributions to both academic
research and practical applications in the hospitality
sector, particularly in luxury hotels. Academically, it
contextualizes the dynamics between Overall Service
Quality (OSQ) and Overall Customer Satisfaction (OCS),
incorporating variables such as Guest Online Feedback
(GOF), Hotel Management Response (HMR), and
Decision-Making Processes (DMP). It further extends
the frameworks of Customer Engagement Behavior
(CEB) and Hotel Brand Loyalty and Sustainable Tourism
(HBST), especially in the context of sustainable tourism.
Additionally, by using Necessary Condition Analysis
(NCA), the study identifies essential conditions for
HBST, enriching the analytical methods available to
researchers.
 From a practical perspective, the research offers
actionable insights for hoteliers and policymakers. It
emphasizes managing online feedback to build trust and
satisfaction, while also highlighting the importance of
improving service quality to foster customer engagement
and brand loyalty. The application of NCA helps hotel
managers design more precise strategies for promoting
loyalty and sustainability. Overall, the findings bridge
theoretical insights with practical implications, providing
a comprehensive framework to enhance service quality,
customer satisfaction, and brand loyalty in the evolving
landscape of sustainable tourism.
Limitations and future research
 This study has several limitations. First, the cross-
sectional design captures data at a single point in time,
limiting the ability to observe changes over time. Future
research should consider longitudinal studies for a
more dynamic analysis. Second, the study focuses on
specific constructs like Guest Online Feedback (GOF)
and Hotel Management Response (HMR), which may
not capture all relevant factors. Future studies could
incorporate additional variables or alternative models,
such as the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM).
Lastly, the use of GSCAM for analysis, while effective,
could be complemented by other estimation methods
like Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) in future
research to verify robustness.
