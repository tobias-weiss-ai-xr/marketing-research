ABSTRACT

Objective: The purpose of this study is to analyze the influence of content marketing strategies
on the improvement of trust, word-of-mouth, and potential customer purchase intentions

Theoretical Framework: This study discusses the influence of content marketing interventions
on trust, word of mouth, and purchase intent of potential customers in wedding organizers in
Malang Raya, this is due to the high use of social media which opens up opportunit ies for
businesses to utilize marketing content to increase customer engagement.

Methods: This study uses a quantitative approach with an experimental survey method. The
quantitative approach was chosen because it allows the measurement of the cause -and-effect
relationship between the independent variable, namely the "Wedding Organizer" advertising
intervention, and the dependent variable, namely trust, word of mouth, and purchase intent.
Experimental survey methods are used to control certain variables an d observe changes that
occur as a result of advertising interventions.

Results and Discussion:  Results showed a significant increase in trust, word -of-mouth, and
purchase intent in the group that received the content marketing intervention compared to the
control group. In conclusion, content marketing is effective in increasing the elements that
affect the purchase intention of potential customers for wedding organizers.

Research Implications: The implication of this study is that consumers are expected to be more
selective in choosing a wedding organizer and consider ads that are informative and have
positive testimonials from other users. Second, wedding organizers need to strengthen their
marketing strategy by creating ads that are not only visually appealing but also informative and
able to build consumer intent. Including testimonials from previous customers can increase the
trust and intent of potential customers. Finally, for future researchers, it is recommended to
conduct longitudinal research to look at the long -term impact of advertising on consumer
behavior and explore other variables such as loyalty and customer experience that may have a
significant influence on purchasing decisions.

Originality/Value: These findings help in designing more ef fective marketing strategies and
understanding how content marketing can affect customer intent, as well as how it can be used
to improve performance and competitiveness in a competitive market.

2 THEORETICAL FRAMEWORK

2.1 ELABORATION LIKELIHOOD MODEL (ELM))

This study uses the Elaboration Likelihood Model (ELM) to describe
customer acceptance. ELM states that individuals who are able and willing to
process messages are more likely to use central concepts to consider persuasive
information. In other words, they are more likely to generate their own
thoughts in response to persuasive information. ELM states that individuals who
are unable or unwilling to process messages are more likely to use peripheral
concepts to consider persu asive information. In other words, they are more
likely to generate mental shortcuts in response to persuasive information.

3 METHODOLOGY

The research method used is an experimental method based on the
positivism paradigm, using experimental and survey research. Experimental
research is used to prove the first and fourth research hypotheses, which are
then tested again through the survey met hod using questionnaires to answer
the three hypotheses that have been proposed at the beginning. Experimental
research is a type of research that emphasizes the causal relationship between
research variables. The word 'experiment' has its roots in the Lat in
experimentum which means to try (Shadish, et al., 2002). The Merriam Webster
Dictionary gives the old definition of experiment as “an operation or procedure
used under controlled conditions to discover new laws, test hypotheses, or
illustrate establishe d laws”. The population in this study are prospective
customers who use the services of wedding organizer in Malang City.
Prospective consumers are often chosen as research subjects in psychology and
marketing experiments. This practice cannot fully genera lize the findings
because responses from customers for a limited number of purchasing behaviors
may not be entirely representative of the general population of people
(Kantowitz et al., 2009). In obtaining the experimental sample, this study will
control conditions through randomization, which randomly distributes samples
to the experimental group and control group. The experimental group is the
