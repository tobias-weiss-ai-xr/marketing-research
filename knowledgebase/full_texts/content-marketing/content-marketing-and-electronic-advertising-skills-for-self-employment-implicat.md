ABSTRACT
The study assessed content marketing and electronic advertising skills required of Business Education
students for self-employment in universities in South -South, Nigeria. Two research questions and two
null hypotheses guided the study. The study adopted descriptive survey research design. Population of
the study comprises 199 Business Educators (102 males and 97 females) in public universities in South-
South, Nigeria. The instrument was validated by two experts in Business Education programme, one in
Measurement and Evaluation from Michael Okpara University of Agriculture, Umudike, Abia State with

reliability coefficients of 0.83 and 0.81 respectively. Structured questionnaire titled “Content Marketing
and E-Advertising Questionnaire” (CMEQ) was used for data collection. The Instrument consists of 20
questions based on four -point scale of Very Highl y Required, Highly Required, Moderately Required,
and Not Required. Mean was used to answer research questions, standard deviation to determine
closeness of the responses. Null hypotheses were tested using t-test analysis at .05 level of significance.
Findings revealed that content marketing and e-advertising skills are required in Business Education in
South-South universities for self-employment. It was concluded that graduates should be equipped with
these skills, to be successful in entrepreneurial endeavour in this digital era. It was recommended among
others that educational administrators should promote digital entrepreneurship to provide real -world
exposure, internships, and hands-on experience to students

Keywords: business education; content marketing; electronic advertising skills and self -employment.

1 INTRODUCTION
Business Education is a vocational programme designed to equip students with
a set of competencies to develop them into informed and principled professionals who

development of the society. Falobi, Ishola, & Owodolu (2023) also viewed that

1.1 STATEMENT OF THE PROBLEM
The rising rate of youth unemployment in Nigeria, particularly among university
graduates, has become a critical national concern. Despite the establishment of
Business Education programs in universities aimed at equipping students with relevant
vocational and entrepreneurial skills, many graduates still struggle to create or sustain
self-employment ventures in today’s digital economy. This points to a possible
mismatch between the training provided and the demands of the modern business
environment. In rec ent years, content marketing and electronic advertising (e -
advertising) have emerged as essential tools for business visibility, customer
engagement, and sustainable entrepreneurship. These skills are especially valuable
for small-scale startups and self -employed individuals seeking low -cost but effective
marketing strategies in a highly competitive digital marketplace. Yet, the extent to
which these competencies are integrated into the Business Education curriculum
remains largely unclear. If Business Educ ation students are to transition into
successful self-employed individuals, they must possess not only traditional business
knowledge but also up -to-date digital marketing skills tailored to current market
realities. The absence or insufficiency of such training could severely limit their capacity
to build and sustain profitable ventures. Therefore, this study sought to assess the
content marketing and e-advertising skills required of Business Education students for
self-employment in universities in South-South Nigeria

3 METHODOLOGY
The study adopted a descriptive survey research design. This design is deemed
suitable because it enables the researcher to identify existing conditions, gather and
analyse current data regarding Business Educators' perceptions while providing
valuable data for informed decision -making. As Two  research questions guided the
study and two null hypotheses were tested at 0.05 level of significance. The population
of the study comprised all 199 Business Educators (102 males and 97 Females
Business Educators) in all the twelve (12) federal and state universities offering
Business Education in South -south, Nigeria. There was no sample because the
population was manageable, hence census sampling was adopted. The instrument for
data collection was a 20 -item structured questionnaire titled: Digital Market ing Skills
Assessment Questionnaire (DMSAQ). The instrument was validated by three experts
two in Business Education and one from Measurement and Evaluation, all in College
of Education, Michael Okpara University of Agriculture, Umudike, Abia State. The
reliability of the instrument was determined using test -retest method and their
responses were analysed using Cronbach Alpha Coefficient statistic which yielded an
overall index of 0.85. Five briefed research assistants helped in the administration and

ISSN 1696-7348, v. 24, n. 119, 2026
collection of the instrument. . Out of 199 copies of the instrument administered, 189
copies representing 95% were returned and used for data analysis.
The data collected were analysed using mean to answer the research questions
and standard deviations to determine the closeness of their responses. The t -test
statistics was used to test the null hypotheses at a 0.05 level of significance. The data
analysis was done using Statistical Package for Social Science (SPSS). The decision
rule for the research questions was based on the principle of real limits of the mean so
that the researcher can get the actual response limit of the respondents or clear -cut
decisions, objective decision-making, control over type 1 error rate among others: Very
Highly Required (VHR) -3.50 - 4.00, Highly Required (HR) - 2.50 - 3.49 Moderately
Required (MR)-1.50 - 2.49Not Required (NR) -1.00 - 1.49 The null hypotheses were
tested using t-test analysis at .05 level of significance and at the appropriate degree of
freedom. The decision rule for the hypotheses were that when the calculated p -value
is equal to or greater than .05, the null hypothesis was accepted, but when the
calculated p-value is less than .05, the null hypothesis was not accepted or was not
significant. These served as a criterion for making inferences for the data collected to
enable the researcher provide answers for the research questions raised and
hypotheses formulated that are meant to guide the study.

4 DISCUSSION
Findings of the study further revealed content marketing skills required in
business education for self -employment in universities in south -south, Nigeria.
According to the respondents, ability to develop marketing content strategies that
aligned with busi ness goals, ability to create various types of content for online
audience (blogs, articles, videos), understanding of content optimization for different
platforms, ability to plan content calendar management, and ability to measure and
analyze content per formance metrics among others are all content marketing skills
required in business education for self -employment in universities in south -south,
Nigeria. These findings align with the study of Adebisi and Tamunonengiyeofori (2023),
whose findings revealed that there is no significant difference between the extents of
acquisition of digital marketing skills among Business Education students from tertiary
institutions in Rivers State. The study further found out that there was no significant
difference between the mean responses of male and female business educators on
the content marketing skills required in business education for self -employment in
universities in south-south, Nigeria. This implies that gender does not play a significant
role in determining the content marketing skills required in business education for self-
employment in universities in south -south, Nigeria. This also implies that content
marketing skills helps in developing and sharing valuable, educational, and engaging
materials across v arious digital formats with the purpose of attracting potential
customers and maintaining relationships with existing ones.
Findings of the study also revealed e -advertising skills required in business
education for self-employment in universities in south-south, Nigeria. According to the
respondents, ability to create pay -per-click advertising campaigns, ability to develop
targeted e-advertising campaigns using audience segmentation techniques, ability to
run paid social media advertising campaigns, ability to analyze and interpret key
