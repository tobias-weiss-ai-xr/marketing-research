ABSTRACT

As social media becomes central to tourism marketing, small
service providers must use digital content to communicate
experiences and build recognition. This study examines the
content strategy of Lost in Jogja, a cycling -based private tour
business in Yog yakarta, through McPheat’s  four content -
marketing dimensions: Educates, Informs, Entertains, and
Creates Trustworthiness. Using a descriptive qualitative design,
the research drew on observations of social media content, in -
depth interviews with the owner and a tour guide, and
documentation supported by a literature review. The findings
show that educational posts introduce alternative cycling routes
and local experiences, while informational posts describe tour
activities, routes, and services. Entertainment is conveyed
through sho rt videos, relatable themes, humor, and platform -
specific trends. Trustworthiness is communicated through
spontaneous documentation of actual tours and alignment
between promotional messages and service delivery. Instagram
primarily supports marketing comm unication and customer
interaction, whereas TikTok offers opportunities to reach wider
audiences. The study’s contribution is to demonstrate how these
four communicative functions can be integrated into an
experience-based tourism business through authenti c local
storytelling and platform adaptation. Practically, the findings
highlight the importance of content relevance, credibility, and
publication consistency. Because the study is based on a single
qualitative case, it documents strategic practices rathe r than
establishing causal effects on brand awareness or business
performance.

INTRODUCTION
Technological advances have transformed how businesses communicate with consumers and market
their products and services. In tourism, digital content marketing has become increasingly important as
consumers use online platforms to gather information, compa re options, and assess experiences before
making travel decisions. Images, videos, captions, and stories enable tourism businesses to communicate not
only service information but also the experiences and values associated with their offerings. Marketing
communication has therefore shifted from conventional promotion toward more interactive, visual, and
experience-oriented approaches. Social media enables businesses to build brand awareness, establish
audience relationships, and encourage engagement through content that reflects consumer interests and
behavior.
The growth of digital tourism has reinforced the role of social media in tourism marketing. Prospective
travelers routinely use the internet to explore destinations, attractions, accommodation, food, and activities. In
Indonesia, the Indonesian Internet Se rvice Providers Association (APJII, 2024) reported an internet
penetration rate of 79.5% in 2024, representing approximately 221.56 million users. This environment creates
opportunities for tourism businesses to disseminate information, showcase experience s, and interact with
potential customers through Instagram and TikTok. Beyond promoting destinations, digital tourism involves
representing experiences that may shape tourists’ perceptions and choices. Through social media, consumers
can assess visual depictions of activities and settings before purchasing tourism services.
Yogyakarta offers a relevant setting because its tourism experiences combine local culture, community
life, creative activities, and urban exploration. Lost in Jogja, a private tour business, offers cycling -based
experiences along routes in and around the city. The business established its digital presence on Instagram
and subsequently expanded its communication to TikTok. Its posts feature cycling activities, local settings,
community interactions, and customer experiences through photographs, short videos, Instagram Stories, and
Reels. It also reshares user-generated photographs and videos, presenting tourism experiences from both the
business’s and customers’ perspectives. This approach is pertinent to private tours because their value
extends beyond transportation or an itinerary to local exploration, interpersonal interaction, and personal
memories. Figure-1 presents the business’s Instagram profile. By identifying cycling -based private trips and
displaying booking information, the profile serves as an initial point of reference through which prospective
customers can recognize the brand and locate its services.

However, the growing volume of tourism content on social media makes it difficult for businesses to
secure attention and establish a distinctive identity. Visually appealing posts do not necessarily help audiences
understand the services offered, sustain interest, or develop trust. Tourism businesses therefore need content
strategies that educate, inform, entertain, and build credibility. This need is particularly relevant to Lost in Jogja
because prospective customers must assess routes, activities, facili ties, atmosphere, and expected
experiences before booking. Effective digital communication consequently depends not only on posting
frequency but also on whether content is designed to support brand awareness and business development.
Previous studies have addressed social media marketing, user -generated content, customer
engagement, and memorable tourism experiences. Informative, interactive, and visually appealing content can
support customer engagement (Novyanti & Christin, 2024), wh ile user-generated content may offer authentic
accounts of travel experiences and influence consumer perceptions (Akehurst, 2009; Lu & Stepchenkova,
2015). Studies of memorable tourism experiences highlight refreshment, hedonism, novelty, local culture,

communication across platforms. The case offers insights for communication and tourism-marketing research
on the digital representation of local experiences.

LITERATURE REVIEW
Digital Content Strategy
Digital content comprises information distributed in electronic formats, including text, images, audio,
video, and infographics. In digital marketing, content is not simply a vehicle for information delivery but a
strategic communication resource through w hich brands communicate value and sustain consumer
relationships. Businesses use such content to convey product information, engage audiences, and strengthen
their market presence. Nugroho (2024) suggests that digital content can also help organizations re ach
consumer segments that are difficult to access through conventional marketing channels.
Content serves different communication purposes. Abdullah (2021) identifies six categories: (1)
informative content, which communicates information about products, services, or organizations; (2)
educational content, which expands audience knowledge; (3) c ontent marketing, which offers relevant
information and value; (4) interactive content, which encourages participation through polls, quizzes, live
streams, and questions and answers; (5) storytelling content, which builds emotional connections; and (6)
content development, which involves ongoing evaluation and optimization. These categories illustrate how
formats and messages can be adapted to specific audiences and communication objectives.
Effective digital content requires attention to both message and delivery. Kingsnorth (2025) describes
seven characteristics of strong content: credible, shareable, useful or entertaining, interesting, relevant,
distinctive, and consistent with the brand. Together, these qualities help content attract attention, encourage
engagement, and support trust and marketing objectives.
Content marketing is central to digital content strategy because it enables brands to communicate value
through sustained engagement. McPheat  (2011) describes content marketing as publishing material that
empowers, engages, educates, and connects readers. This perspective treats content as more than direct
product promotion: it emphasizes meaningful exchanges that provide value to audiences. In  social media
settings, the approach can be examined through four interrelated dimensions: Educates, Informs, Entertains,
and Creates Trustworthiness.
McPheat’s perspective is especially relevant to social media because audiences can respond to and
redistribute content rather than merely receive it. Content must therefore offer value while facilitating interaction
and conveying brand credibility. Shelyci a and Dewi (2025) found a positive relationship between Instagram
content marketing and brand trust in the context they studied, reporting a contribution of 63.6%. Although the
setting differs from tourism, the finding illustrates the potential relevance of  content quality to consumer
confidence.
In this study, McPheat’s  (2011) framework is operationalized through four dimensions. Educates
concerns content that provides useful knowledge and improves audience understanding. Informs concerns
clear, accurate information about services, activities, and the brand. Entertains c aptures the enjoyable or
engaging qualities of content, including creative formats and storytelling. Creates Trustworthiness refers to the
consistency, authenticity, and credibility through which content may foster confidence in a brand. The
dimensions provide complementary analytical categories for examining Lost in Jogja’s digital communication.

Brand Awareness
Brand awareness refers to consumers’ ability to recognize or recall a brand within a product or service
category. Aaker (1991) associates brand awareness with recognition and remembrance, while Keller (1993)
distinguishes brand recognition from brand recal l. Recognition involves identifying a brand when presented
with a cue; recall involves retrieving it from memory when considering a category. Consistent digital
communication can support both processes by making a brand more familiar. Social media marketin g may
also contribute to awareness alongside brand engagement, trust, and loyalty (Salsabila & Hati, 2024).

METHOD
This study used a descriptive qualitative approach to examine how Lost in Jogja manages digital
content. The approach was appropriate because the research sought to understand the experiences and
practices of individuals responsible for planning, producing , publishing, and evaluating content on the
business’s Instagram account. A descriptive design permits a systematic account of practices in their existing
context without manipulating conditions (Fadli, 2021).
Informants were selected purposively according to three criteria: (1) direct involvement in Lost in Jogja’s
social media activities; (2) knowledge or experience of Instagram content planning, production, publication, or
evaluation; and (3) the ability to p rovide information relevant to the study. Two informants met these criteria:
the business owner, who determines digital marketing and content strategy, and a tour guide, who supports
tour operations and supplies information and visual documentation. Their roles provided perspectives on
strategic decisions and operational implementation.
The unit of analysis was digital content management on the Lost in Jogja Instagram account, specifically
planning, creation, publication, and evaluation. The researchers observed content types, themes, presentation
formats, captions, posting activities, an d audience responses. In -depth interviews explored the reasons,
experiences, and practices behind these activities. Published posts and other relevant materials were
documented to complement the observational and interview evidence.
Data collection involved observation, in -depth interviews, documentation, and a literature review.
Observation focused on the account’s content management practices; interviews examined decisions about
content planning, production, publication, and evaluat ion; and documentation supplied relevant examples of
published content. The literature review informed the study’s conceptual and theoretical interpretation.
The analysis followed the interactive model of Miles and Huberman (1984): data collection, reduction,
display, and conclusion drawing and verification. Interview, observation, and documentary materials were
organized and compared to identify recurring patt erns in content management. Source triangulation involved
comparing statements from the owner and tour guide with observed Instagram posts and other documentation
to strengthen the credibility of the findings.

awareness and business development. The business operates almost daily in Yogyakarta and offers cycling -
based private tours to adults and children, including families and groups booking special packages. Customers
come from Yogyakarta and other Indonesian cities, including Jakarta, Malang, Bandung, Semarang,
Palembang, and Lampung, as well as Malaysia, Japan, and Brunei Darussalam. The business is also

Lost in Jogja uses Instagram and TikTok as its principal digital platforms. Instagram is the primary

Educates
The Educates dimension appears in content that introduces alternative ways of experiencing
Yogyakarta. Instead of focusing solely on established tourist attractions, Lost in Jogja documents local
exploration and community interaction. For example, its “Blu sukan” concept presents everyday life in local
neighborhoods, introducing audiences to settings and activities that are less visible in conventional destination
promotions.
Educational content also informs the business’s plan to publish an e -book featuring alternative travel
routes in Yogyakarta. Although the e -book remains a planned product, it reflects an intention to share
destination knowledge beyond conventional attracti ons. Published content likewise connects tourism
experiences with familiar subjects such as friendships, relationships, and working life, making information
accessible through everyday contexts rather than exclusively promotional messages.
Informs
The Informs dimension is evident in posts communicating Lost in Jogja’s services and activities.
Instagram primarily supports marketing and sales, while TikTok broadens exposure. Photographs, Reels, and
short videos present cycling routes, activities, and the local environments encountered during tours.
The business also monitors social media trends, particularly on TikTok, by observing topics discussed
by users and videos appearing on the For You Page (FYP). It adapts relevant trends to its services, enabling
tourism information to be presented in formats familiar to social media audiences.
Visual design also supports the presentation of information. Lost in Jogja uses Avenir and Arial
typefaces, colorful graphics, and photographs and videos of tourism activities. Smartphones and cameras are
used for documentation, while InShot and Canva support editing and graphic design. Alongside explicit service
descriptions, visuals communicate routes, destinations, activities, and the likely atmosphere of a tour.

accompanying service information show how digital content helps prospective customers distinguish among
available packages before making an inquiry.

Creates Trustworthiness
The Creates Trustworthiness dimension is reflected in the use of authentic tour documentation and the
alignment between online representations and services provided. Lost in Jogja emphasizes spontaneous
recording of activities to avoid the impression that customer experiences are staged. Its photographs and
videos depict activities and interactions that occurred during tours.
Tour guides contribute to this documentation and accommodate customers’ preferences, allowing
images and videos to reflect individual experiences. The business also attends to bicycle quality, comfort, and
safety. Before tours, staff check tire pressure and brakes, lubricate moving parts, clean bicycles, and address
minor defects when necessary. These operational practices support consistency between the experience
portrayed online and the service delivered. In this sense, credible communication is complemented by service
quality.
Supporting Factors and Constraints in Content Management
The implementation of McPheat’s four dimensions is affected by organizational resources. The owner
handles bookings, invoices, administration, social media management, and content production. Heavy
demand for tours can also cause staff fatigue, making regular posting difficult.
The findings indicate that consistent content marketing depends not only on creative skills but also on
the human resources available for ongoing production. These constraints do not prevent the business from
producing educational, informational, entertain ing, or trust -building content, but they affect publication
frequency and consistency.
To address these constraints, the owner plans to develop skills in copywriting, digital content analysis,
and campaign planning. The business also intends to monitor consumer behavior more systematically. These
plans position content management as an ongoing process of planning, production, audience observation, and
evaluation.

Discussion
Tourism experiences are increasingly communicated through visual documentation. Photographs and
videos function not only as personal records but also as content through which tourism businesses represent
experiences and establish a presence on social media . In Lost in Jogja’s case, spontaneous tour
documentation connects the lived experience of customers with the brand messages encountered by
prospective visitors.
Visual content can communicate information efficiently when imagery, color, layout, and other design
elements reinforce the intended message. For Lost in Jogja, the value of these elements lies not simply in their
aesthetic appeal but in their ability to c ommunicate the character of its cycling tours. This interpretation is
consistent with the role of relevant, engaging material in digital content strategy.
The business’s use of Instagram and TikTok illustrates the shift from conventional promotion toward
participatory digital communication. Both platforms allow the company to turn customers’ travel experiences
into shareable narratives, although each platfor m serves a somewhat different function. Instagram primarily
supports marketing communication, while TikTok extends potential audience reach.
The Educates dimension illustrates how accessible messages can introduce audiences to experiences
outside conventional tourist itineraries. By documenting local routes and “Blusukan” activities, Lost in Jogja
communicates practical and cultural knowledge w hile distinguishing its service from other cycling -tour
offerings. The findings suggest that educational value does not require a formal teaching format; it can be
conveyed through understandable narratives grounded in real experiences.
Content themes are also informed by emerging social media conversations. The business connects
familiar issues to local tourism and incorporates aspects of Yogyakarta’s community identity. This practice
links audience relevance with place-based differentiation: posts encourage interaction while portraying the tour
as a cultural and interpersonal experience rather than merely a commercial itinerary.
These practices can also be considered in relation to emotional branding. Gobé’s  concept of emotional
branding (2005, as cited in Prasetyo & Febriani, 2020) describes the development of meaningful connections

Yogyakarta’s food, local culture, and community interactions offer recurring subjects for Lost in Jogja’s

The Informs dimension complements these narratives by providing details relevant to travel planning.
Audiences need reliable descriptions of routes, services, and activities to evaluate a tour. Consequently,
informative content should be assessed not only for visual attractiveness but also for its clarity, relevance, and
consistency with actual service provision.
This interpretation is broadly consistent with research linking tourism -related information to tourists’
perceptions, trust, and intentions (Sutrisno et al., 2024). Other studies cited in the manuscript also associate
digital content quality and destinatio n image with trust and visit intentions (Li & Wang, 2022; Zhang et al.,
2020). These comparisons should be treated cautiously because the present qualitative study examines
content practices rather than measuring their causal effects on consumer behavior.
The business also responds to audience interest in trending tourism destinations. The cited GoodStats
(2025) infographic identifies Yogyakarta as a popular holiday destination among Indonesians. Lost in Jogja
uses this wider interest to present its tours through stories that make destination information more engaging.
Although the manuscript discusses fea r of missing out (FOMO), the available interview and observation
evidence does not establish its effect on purchasing decisions; it is best understood here as a possible
contextual influence.
Storytelling provides another perspective on these findings. Pulizzi (2012) discusses storytelling as a
marketing approach that can engage audiences, while McKee and Gerace (2018) emphasize narrative in
marketing communication. Lost in Jogja’s portrayal of  local environments and travel experiences illustrates
how destination information can be framed as a story rather than a sequence of promotional claims.
Copywriting contributes to this narrative approach through concise and persuasive captions, scripts, and
other messages. In a crowded digital environment, tourism businesses must present service information in
ways that compete for attention without obscuring its meaning. Ningrum (2022) discusses communication and
tourism branding, a relevant context for the business’s efforts to combine visual material, written captions, and
spoken narration.
The Entertains dimension is visible in the business’s adaptation of popular formats and themes.
Audience comments and other interactions provide feedback that may inform future content decisions.
However, engagement with an individual post should not be eq uated automatically with sustained brand
awareness or business growth. Publication consistency and the relevance of entertainment to the core service
remain important considerations.
The results further indicate that maintaining a coherent digital presence requires a manageable
publication schedule, continued collaboration, and appropriate service innovation. Consumer expectations are
influenced in part by information encountered befor e booking, making coordination between online content
and tour operations important for brand credibility.
Creates Trustworthiness is therefore closely connected to both communication and delivery. Authentic
tour footage can support credibility when it reflects the experience that customers actually receive. Yet the
business’s limited personnel and multiple ope rational responsibilities constrain the frequency of content
creation and its capacity to adopt new digital practices.
Taken together, the findings suggest that the four dimensions operate as complementary functions
rather than isolated content categories: education and information explain the experience, entertainment
attracts attention, and authenticity supports credibil ity. This integration offers a useful perspective on
experience-based tourism marketing, while the single -case qualitative design limits claims about its wider
effectiveness or causal impact on brand awareness.

CONCLUSION
Lost in Jogja integrates educational, informational, entertaining, and trust -building messages into its
digital content strategy. Instagram functions mainly as a marketing and customer -communication channel,
while TikTok extends potential audience reach. A cross both platforms, local exploration, spontaneous tour
documentation, and accessible narratives help communicate the distinctive features of its cycling -based
private tours. The study contributes a contextual illustration of how McPheat’s  four content -marketing
dimensions can be applied to an experience-based tourism business.
These findings are limited to a qualitative case involving two informants and observations of the
business’s digital content; they do not establish a causal increase in brand awareness or sales. Practical
priorities include consistent posting, attention to  audience feedback, and alignment between promotional
representations and service delivery. Future research could compare multiple private tour operators and
incorporate audience interviews or platform analytics to examine how content characteristics relat e to brand
recognition, trust, and booking decisions.
