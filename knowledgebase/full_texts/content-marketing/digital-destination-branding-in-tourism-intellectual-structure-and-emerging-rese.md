Abstract
The development of digital communication technology has rapidly
revolutionised the destination branding techniques in the tourist industry,
especially with the growing role of social media and user -generated content.
Although digital destination branding is increasingly attracting scholarly
interest, research in the field remains scattered among multiple issues, including
destination image, social media marketing, tourist engagement, narrative, and
digital communication. Hence, this study intends to analyse the intellectual
structure, theme progression, and upcoming research trends in digital
destination branding. A bibliometric method was carried out based on data
extracted from the Scopus database. The search term TITLE -ABS-KEY
("destination branding" AND ("social media" OR "digital")) retrieved
publications between 2019 and 202 5. After applying document type and
language criteria, 77 journal articles in English were selected for study.
Biblioshiny in RStudio and VOSviewer were used for the analysis of publishing
trends, influential authors, country scientific production, citation  structures,
theme clusters, and keyword co-occurrence networks. The results show a large
rise in research production after 2023, showing increased academic interest on
digital tourism communication. The main topics identified in the field were social
media, destination image, tourist experience, storytelling and sustainability. The
study shows that destination branding research has progressed from
conventional advertising strategies towards interactive and digitally mediated
tourist ecosystems. This work adds by offering an extensive bibliometric
mapping of the subject and highlighting future research opportunities on
artificial intelligence, immersive tourism experiences, and sustainable
destination communication.
Keywords: bibliometric analysis; destination branding; destination image;
digital tourism; social media; tourism marketing

INTRODUCTION
The rapid advancement of digital
technology has significantly
transformed the tourism industry,
particularly in the way destinations
communicate, compete, and establish
their identities in increasingly global
and interconnected tourism markets.
Traditional destination promotion has
gradually shifted toward digitally
mediated communication ecosystems
that incorporate social media
platforms, online reviews, user -
generated content, digital storytelling,
and influencer marketing.  Recent
studies indicate that de stination
branding increasingly depends on the
ability of destinations to communicate
territorial values through digital
platforms and social media
ecosystems, enabling destinations to
strengthen competitiveness and global
visibility (Francisco et al., 2024) .
Digital communication has become
increasingly important in shaping
destination visibility and
competitiveness, while branding
functions not only as a promotional
instrument but also as a mechanism
for creating differentiation and
strengthening destination image
(Habiburrahman, et al., 2024;
Sakuntalawati et al., 2024)
Consequently, destination branding
has evolved from a promotional
activity into a dynamic and
decentralized communication process,
where destination image is
increasingly shaped by authentic
digital narratives, user -generated
content, and interactions amon g
diverse tourism stakeholders (Simeli et
al., 2025)
Social media has become one of
the most powerful tools in modern
destination branding. The growing
popularity of short-video platforms
such as TikTok and Douyin has further
transformed destination branding
practices by influencing travel
planning and behavioral intentions
through user-generated content and
electronic word-of-mouth (Wang et al.,
2022). Tourists utilise social networks
such as Instagram, TikTok, Facebook,
YouTube and TripAdvisor to search
for information, rate places, share
experiences and influence the travel
choices of other users. Social media has
been found to have an important role
in the construction of destination
image, visitor interaction, destination
attractiveness and travel intention
(Tran & Rudolf, 2022). Furthermore,
user-created content is becoming seen
as more real and trustworthy than
traditional promotional campaigns,
thereby making travellers active
players in the co -creation of
destination brands. This finding
validates the increasing importance of
digital marketing and social media in
enabling involvement, enhancing
brand awareness, and affecting
consumer views in modern
marketplaces (Habiburrahman et al.,
2024).
The growing integration of
tourism and digital communication
has stimulated substantial academic
interest in destination branding
research. During the last decade,
studies related to destination image,
social media marketing, electronic
word-of-mouth, tourism storytelling,
tourist engagement, and digital
communication have expanded
considerably within tourism and
hospitality literature (Zhang &
Ramayah, 2024). Recent research
suggests that tourists increasingly

examined publication trends, citation

production, conceptual relationships

destination branding research using a
bibliometric approach.
Bibliometric analysis is a
systematic method to examine the
history of scientific knowledge and to
figure out publication trends,
influential authors, prolific countries,
top journals, citation networks and
thematic evolution in a research
domain. Bibliometric techniques
provide quantitative information on
intellectual structures and research
trends and allow a visualisation of
conceptual linkages among major
themes as contrasted to traditional
literature evaluations (Donthu et al.,
2021); Aria & Cuccurullo, 2017) .
Therefore, bibliometric analysis is
highly suited to investigate the
heterogenous and dynamic nature of
research on digital destination
branding.

approached from diverse perspectives
in recent studies  For example,

destination branding extends beyond

the communication of territorial

participation of multiple stakeholders.
They further emphasized the
importance of destination identity,
stakeholder collaboration, and value-
based communication in
strengthening destination
competitiveness and brand
positioning. Likewise, Tuyen (2025),
found destination branding,
participation of stakeholders,
emotional experience and digital
interaction as significant thematic
clusters. However, the overall

METHOD
Data Collection
This paper used a bibliometric
approach to explore the evolution of
digital destination branding research.
Bibliometric data were extracted from
the Scopus database in January 2026,
using the search phrase TITLE-ABS-
KEY ("destination branding" AND
("social media" OR "digital")). The
search scope was limited to journal
articles published from 2019 to 2025 to
cover the recent innovations in digital
destination branding and tourist
communication. Scopus was chosen as
it is one of the largest and most
comprehensive multidisciplinary
scientific databases, containing high-
quality bibliographical information for
bibliometric analysis.

Study Selection
The article selection process
followed the Preferred Reporting
Items for Systematic Reviews and
Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) framework
to ensure transparency and
reproducibility. The initial search
identified 163 documents.
Subsequently, document type and
language filters were applied. Only
journal articles published in English
were retained for analysis. After the
screening process, a final dataset of 77
articles was selected for bibliometric
analysis.

Data Analysis
Bibliometric analysis was
conducted using Biblioshiny, an R -
package interface of Bibliometrix, and
VOSviewer. Performance analysis was
used to examine annual scientific
production, source productivity,
author productivity, citation
performance, and country scientific
production. Science mapping
techniques were applied to identify
conceptual structures, thematic
clusters, keyword co -occurrence
networks, and emerging research
trends.
Biblioshiny was used to generate
descriptive bibliometric indicators,
including annual publication trends,
most relevant authors, most relevant
sources, and thematic evolution.
VOSviewer was employed to visualize
network relationships among
keywords and research themes. The
integration of these two software
packages enabled a comprehensive
understanding of the intellectual
structure and thematic development of
digital destination branding research.

Research Framework
The analytical framework of this
study consisted of four stages: data
collection, study selection,
bibliometric performance analysis,
and science mapping analysis. The
results were interpreted to identify
dominant themes, influential
contributors, and future research
opportunities in digital destination
branding.

RESULT AND DISCUSSION
This part provides the results of
the bibliometric study of digital
destination branding research
included in the Scopus database in the
period between 2019–2026. The main
bibliometric indicators, annual
scientific production, nation scientific
production, influential authors and
sources, network of co-occurrence of
keywords, thematic structures, trend
themes and thematic evolution are
presented and analysed. Finally, the
findings are reviewed to figure out the
conceptual framework and future
study directions of digital destination
branding studies.

Main Information
Bibliometric analysis was
conducted using Biblioshiny in
RStudio and VOSviewer to investigate
the development of digital destination
branding and social media tourism
studies indexed in the Scopus
database. The dataset consisted of 77
journal articles published between
2019 and 2026, originating from 56
academic sources, involving 228
authors, and supported by 5,255 cited
references. The average citation per
document was 13.21, while the annual
growth rate reached 6.67%.
International collaboration accounted
for 28.57% of total publications,
indicating the growing involvement of
global scholars in tourism branding
and digital communication research.

CONCLUSION
This study examined the
evolution of digital destination
branding research in the Scopus
database from 2019 to 2025 with a
bibliometric approach. The data
indicate a rising trend in publications,
which suggests an expanding
academic interest in digital destination
branding as an emerging research area
within tourism studies. The
investigation revealed the following
main themes that shape the intellectual
structure of the sector, namely,
destination branding, social media,
destination image, visitor experience
and digi tal communication.
Furthermore, the results suggest that
study in this area is increasingly
multidisciplinary, including research
streams from tourism, marketing,
communication and digital
technology. The conceptual structure
analysis highlighted the high
correlations between destination
branding, social media engagement,
tourist behaviour and digital
communication, as well as the
significant significance of digital
platforms in the formation of
destination competitiveness and
tourists’ views. Furthermore, the trend
topic research shows a change from
traditional brand management
attitudes to topics more related to
technology, such as digital
engagement, sustainability, and online
tourism experiences. The findings
imply that digital change still drives
destination branding strategies, as
well as tourism research goals.
This study contributes to the
literature by providing a
comprehensive bibliometric overview
of digital destination branding
research and identifying emerging
research opportunities. Future studies
are encouraged to explore topics
related to artificial intelligence,
immersive tourism experiences, digital
storytelling, social media engagement,
and sustainability -oriented
destination branding to further
advance theoretical understanding
and practical applications within the
field.
