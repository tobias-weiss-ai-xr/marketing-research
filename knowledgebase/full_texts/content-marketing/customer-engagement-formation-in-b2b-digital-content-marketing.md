Abstract
This study conducts a systematic review to examine how digital content marketing (DCM)
in business -to-business (B2B) contexts shapes customer engagement (CE) among
organizational customers and how such engagement translates into various outcomes. The
findings reveal that B2B DCM stimulates CE through multiple factors, including content and
customer characteristics, and different stages of the customer journey (CJ). The resulting
engagement responses evolve sequentially from cognition to emotion and ultimate ly to
behavior. Furthermore, CE is shown to generate multilayered outcomes, including relational
trust, increased relationship value, partnership development, sales productivity, revenue and
profitability, and broader organizational -level performance. More over, several important
research gaps are identified, such as the need to refine the mechanisms underlying CE
formation, integrate the inherent complexity of B2B contexts, and clarify the causal pathways
linking CE to performance outcomes. Overall, this st udy advances an integrative
understanding of B2B DCM and CE while providing directions for future theoretical
development and practical applications.

I. Introduction
The advancement of digital technologies has significantly transformed customer
touchpoints and sales processes in business -to-business (Business -to-Business: B2B)
transactions. Traditionally, B2B marketing—particularly in the manufacturing and industrial
goods industries—has relied heavily on relationship -based sales activities grounded in face -
to-face interactions and personal relationship building by sales representatives. In recent
years, however, a wide range of B2B firms, including those in industrial machinery, IT/SaaS,
professional services, pharmaceuticals, and medical devices, have experienced a shift in
customer behavior whereby organizational buyers independently search for information and
complete substantial portions of their purchasing decision-making process before contacting
sales representatives. Against this background, many B2B firms have increasingly focused on
digital content marketing (Digital Content Marketing: DCM), utilizing white pape rs,
webinars, case studies, social media, and marketing automation to build relationships with
customers prior to direct sales interactions.
The importance of DCM in B2B contexts lies not only in its role as a promotional tool but
also in its function as a strategic communication approach that supports customers’
purchasing decision -making processes  (Järvinen & Taiminen, 2016; Terho et al., 2022).
Compared with business-to-consumer (Business-to-Consumer: B2 C) purchasing, B2B
purchasing is characterized by the involvement of multiple decision-makers, longer evaluation
periods, and higher decision-making risks. Consequently, short-term sales or lead generation
alone are insufficient to fully capture marketing performance . Moreover,  customer
engagement (Customer Engagement: CE) —which reflects customers’ cognitive, emotional,
and behavioral involvement during the prepurchase stage—has become an increasingly crucial
performance indicator (Hollebeek & Macky, 2019). Furthermore, because customers’ needs
and interests change across the purchasing process in B2B contexts, providing content that
aligns with the customer journey (Customer Journey: CJ) is considered critical for fostering
CE (Salonen et al., 2024; Terho et al., 2022). In other words, the essence of B2B DCM lies in
achieving alignment regarding what type of content should be delivered, to which customers,
and at what timing.
Nevertheless, research on DCM in B2B contexts remains underdeveloped. Factors such as
content characteristics, customer characteristics, and the CJ have largely been examined
separately and in a fragmented manner. Additionally, many existing studies have focused on

II. Theoretical Background
The theoretical foundation of this study is based on three concepts: DCM, CE, and the CJ.
Rather than discussing these concepts separately, this section organizes them as an integrative
framework within a B2B digital context. The relationships among the ma jor concepts
addressed in this study on B2B DCM are shown in Figure 1. Specifically, this study positions
DCM as the starting point for CE formation, while its effectiveness is viewed as being
conditioned by content characteristics, customer characteristic s, and purchasing contexts
along the CJ.
First, DCM refers to marketing activities through which firms provide value to customers
via digital content while supporting relationship-building and purchasing processes (Rowley,
2008). Particularly in B2B contexts, content functions not only as a promotional tool but also
as a knowledge resource that supports organizational customers’ problem understanding and
decision-making processes (Järvinen & Taiminen, 2016). CE mediates the outcomes of DCM
by reflecting customers’ cognitive, emotional, and behavioral involvement. Therefore, it is
positioned as an intermediate performance indicator that links marketing activities to firm
performance outcomes (Hollebeek & Macky, 2019).
However, B2B DCM does not uniformly generate CE under all circumstances. Existing
studies have indicated that its effectiveness varies depending on both content and customer
characteristics. For example, content characteristics such as message appeal, info rmational

III. Methodology
This study adopts a systematic review methodology to comprehensively examine the effects
of B2B DCM on CE (Lim et al., 2022).
Literature search was performed in accordance with the PRISMA protocol (Moher et al.,
2009). Using Scopus and Web of Science, searches were performed by combining keywords
related to “B2B,” “digital content marketing,” and “engagement,” targeting peer -reviewed
English-language articles published between 2008 and 2025. After duplicates were removed
and titles, abstracts, and full texts were reviewed, a final sample of 37 empirical studies was
selected for analysis (Figure 2).
To structurally integrate the findings of prior research, this study draws on the
Antecedents–Decisions–Outcomes (ADO) framework (Paul & Benito, 2018), while
reconstructing it into an analytical framework consisting of antecedents, formation processes,
and outcomes to better fit the context of B2B DCM research. Specifically, content
characteristics, customer characteristics, and the CJ are positioned as antecedents; the
cognitive, emotional, and behavioral dimensions of CE are conceptualized as the formatio n
process; and outcomes are categorized into relational outcomes and performance outcomes.
In this study, the objectives, methodologies, and key findings of each study are
systematically organized, and common conceptual patterns are identified to provide a
structured understanding of CE formation in B2B DCM contexts.

IV. An Integrated Mechanism of the CE in B2B DCM
1. Antecedents of CE in B2B DCM
The process through which B2B DCM fosters CE can be primarily explained by three
antecedents: content characteristics, customer characteristics, and the CJ. These antecedents
do not operate independently; rather, they interact with one another to shape the emergence
and deepening of the CE.
（1） Content Characteristics
Content characteristics refer to factors related to the  types of content, formats, and
purposes that are presented, and they function as the starting point for CE formation.
Through the integration of prior studies,  content characteristics can be broadly categorized
into the following three dimensions.
First, emotional and rational appeals influence CE formation. Content stimulates CE by
evoking customers’ emotional resonance or cognitive understanding (Balaji et al., 2023; Deng
et al., 2021; Deng et al., 2025; Gu et al., 2023; Hariyana et al., 2024). In particular, emotional

（2） Customer Characteristics
Customer characteristics refer to the factors that explain why different responses emerge
toward the same content and play a moderating role in the manifestation of CE. Prior studies
have suggested that customer characteristics can be categorized into the following three
dimensions.
First, attribute -related characteristics influence CE formation. Firms’ levels of digital
maturity and organizational characteristics affect their responses to digital content
(Guesalaga, 2016; Iankova  et al., 2019; Li et al., 2021; Shaltoni, 2017; Zhang & Du, 2020).
Additionally, the roles and experiences of purchasing decision -makers shape their interests
and evaluation criteria (Andersson & Wikström, 2017; Keinänen & Kuivalainen, 2015).
Second, attitudinal characteristics are important determinants. Customers’ cognitive and
emotional orientations influence how content is interpreted, thereby affecting the
manifestation of CE (Deng et al., 2021; Deng et al., 2025; Garner & Mady, 2023; Meire et al.,
2022). Furthermore, attitudes toward brands and learning orientation are associated with the
intensity of engagement (Wang et al., 2019).
Third, involvement level affects customer engagement. Customers with greater
involvement in information search tend to interact more actively with content, leading to
deeper CE (Jánská & Žambochová, 2021; Rose et al., 2021; Sombultawee & Wattanatorn,
2022).

（3） Customer Journey (CJ)
The CJ provides a framework through which CE formation progresses over time and across
stages. Prior studies have revealed the following three findings.
First, customer needs change across purchasing stages. Customers seek information in the
early stage, focus on comparison and evaluation in the middle stage, and require support for
decision-making in the later stage (Salonen et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2019).
Second, the role of content varies depending on the stage of the CJ. Content facilitates
awareness and understanding in the early stage, supports trust formation and evaluation in
the middle stage, and drives decision -making in the later stage (Järvinen & Taiminen, 2016;
Juntunen et al., 2020; Taiminen & Ranaweera, 2019).
Third, the CE deepens progressively across stages. Beginning with initial interest, the CE
develops through evaluation and trust formation and ultimately evolves into relational
engagement and purchasing behavior (Balaji et al., 2023; Mora Cortez et al., 2 023; Yaghtin
et al., 2020). Nevertheless, many existing studies have only partially examined differences in
effectiveness across purchasing stages, resulting in a limited, integrative understanding of
how CE formation mechanisms evolve throughout the entire CJ in B2B contexts.

2. CE Formation Process in B2B DCM
In B2B DCM, CE is understood not as a single response but as a process that progressively
develops from cognition to emotion and ultimately to behavior (Hollebeek  & Macky, 2019).
Unlike general advertising responses, which primarily focus on short -term attitude change,
the CE formation process in B2B DCM is characterized by its emphasis on supporting
customer decision-making and fostering long-term relationships.
（1） Cognition
CE first emerges as a cognitive response among customers. Prior studies have indicated
that content exposure generates three major cognitive outcomes: understanding, attention,
and perceived expertise.
First, understanding serves as the foundation of the CE. Informational and emotional
content induce different cognitive processing mechanisms and influence the formation of
customer understanding (Deng et al., 2021; Gu et al., 2023). Moreover, digital content
facilitates customers’ understanding of problems and solutions, thereby contributing to the
development of knowledge necessary for decision -making (Huotari et al., 2015; Wang et al.,

（2） Emotion
Once cognitive understanding has been established, the CE deepens into emotional
responses. At this stage, customers develop trust, favorable attitudes, and emotional
resonance toward firms and brands.
First, trust constitutes the core of the CE. Useful and consistent content contributes to trust
building (Rose et al., 2021; Taiminen & Ranaweera, 2019). Social interaction further
strengthens trust (Sombultawee & Wattanatorn, 2022).
Second, favorable attitudes reduce psychological distance. Linguistic style matching and
positive messaging promote favorable evaluations (Deng et al., 2025; Garner & Mady, 2023;
Meire et al., 2022). Furthermore, demonstrating expertise strengthens brand f avorability
(Magno & Cassia, 2019).
Third, emotional resonance represents the deepest form of engagement. Emotionally
appealing content generates strong emotional connections (Deng et al., 2021; Gu et al., 2023).
Such resonance forms the basis of relational engagement.
（3） Behavior
After progressing through the cognitive and emotional stages, the CE ultimately manifests
as customer behavior. These behaviors move beyond simple viewing responses and
progressively develop into sales-related interactions and purchasing advancement behaviors.
In the initial stage, basic responses such as clicks and continued viewing occur (Singh et al.,
2023; Sundström et al., 2020). Sharing behavior and electronic word -of-mouth (eWOM)
subsequently emerge, extending CE into customer networks (Kwon et al., 2022;  McShane et
al., 2019; Swani et al., 2017).

3. Outcomes Generated by Enhancing the CE through B2B DCM
CE formed through B2B DCM extends beyond cognitive, emotional, and behavioral
responses and ultimately contributes to mid - to long -term firm outcomes through the
mediation of customer knowledge development, problem understanding, and perceptions of
expertise and trust toward firms. This suggests that B2B DCM is a marketing activity that
generates long -term firm outcomes by supporting customer learning and relationship
building. The integration of prior studies indicates that these outcomes can be broadly
categorized into relational outcomes and performance outcomes.
（1） Relationship
The most important relationship-level outcomes are relational trust, relationship deepening,
and partnership formation.
First, relational trust represents the most fundamental outcome of CE in B2B contexts.
Useful and sincere content enhances organizational customers’ trust and leads them to
perceive firms as knowledge providers and problem -solving partners (Rose et al., 20 21;
Taiminen & Ranaweera, 2019). Transparent messaging and continuous communication
further strengthen trust formation (Garner & Mady, 2023; Sombultawee & Wattanatorn,
2022).
Second, trust formation leads to relationship deepening, including increased satisfaction,
commitment, and relationship value. Useful content and thought leadership content enable
organizational customers to recognize continuous value, thereby strengthenin g their
evaluations of the relationship (Magno & Cassia, 2019; Taiminen  & Ranaweera, 2019).
Furthermore, interactive content and interactions with firms reduce psychological distance
and promote relationship commitment (Meire et al., 2022; Rose et al., 2021).

（2） Performance
The trust and commitment established at the relationship level subsequently connect to
performance-level outcomes. Prior studies have mainly identified improvements in sales
productivity and contributions to revenue and profitability.
First, CE promotes both the efficiency and effectiveness of sales activities. In particular,
customers who have already developed knowledge and problem understanding through B2B
DCM possess higher levels of decision-making readiness at the time of sales interaction. This
improves the quality and speed of sales representatives’ interactions, leading to enhanced
sales performance (Guesalaga , 2016; Terho et al., 2022). Furthermore, timely content
delivery and appropriate content design contribute to improvements in conversion efficiency
and sales KPIs (Juntunen et al., 2020; Salonen et al., 2024). These findings indicate that CE
has practical value for improving the productivity of the entire sales process.
Second, improvements in sales performance ultimately contribute to revenue and
profitability. CE facilitates opportunity creation and sales progression, which in turn enhance
profitability and overall firm performance (Mora Cortez & Ghosh Dastidar, 2022; M ora
Cortez et al., 2023). Additionally, prior studies have reported that enhanced relationship value
is linked to financial outcomes (Magno & Cassia, 2019), suggesting that CE functions as a
bridge that transforms relational outcomes into economic outcomes.
Third, CE contributes to organizational-level outcomes. Recent studies have suggested that
CE strengthens competitive advantage and enhances brand equity through the establishment
of professional positioning and thought leadership in the marketplace. For e xample, CE has
been shown to reinforce market leadership and improve firm performance (Kethuda & Ayoubi,
2025). Furthermore, engagement across multiple platforms contributes to organization-wide
value creation and the enhancement of brand equity (Bourguignon et al., 2025).
Although, prior studies have provided insights into the pathways through which CE
contributes to firm outcomes, the mid - to long -term causal mechanisms underlying the
complex decision -making structures specific to B2B contexts remains insufficiently
understood.

V. Discussion and Implications for Future Research
This study reveals that the mechanisms through which B2B DCM forms and deepens CE
can be understood as a staged and multi -layered structure. Specifically, CE progresses
sequentially from cognition (understanding, attention, and perceived expertise) to emot ion
(trust, favorability, and emotional resonance) and then to behavior (clicks, sharing,
information search, inquiries, and sales opportunities) following exposure to content.
Ultimately, CE extends to relational trust, commitment, and partnership formati on,
eventually contributing to sales outcomes, firm performance, and even organizational -level
value. These findings suggest that CE functions not only  as an attitude or behavior, but also
as an integrative mechanism that connects psychological responses to firm outcomes (Figure

VI. Conclusion
The theoretical contributions of this study can be summarized in three main points. First,
this study reconceptualizes CE as a dynamic process consisting of three stages —cognition,
emotion, and behavior—and clarifies the mechanisms underlying its formation and deepening.
Second, it integrates three key antecedents—content characteristics, customer characteristics,
and the CJ—and presents a perspective focused on what type of content is effective, for whom,
and at what timing. Third, it organizes the outcome s of CE as a hierarchical structure
extending from relational -level outcomes to performance -level outcomes, thereby clarifying
the mechanism through which CE is transformed into economic value through the formation
of relational assets.
From a practical perspective, this study provides several important implications for B2B
firms. First, CE contributes not only to short-term behavioral outcomes but also to long-term
relationship building and firm performance.  Hence, firms should not neglect engagement at
the cognitive and emotional stages and should instead design content according to each
purchasing stage. Second, because providing the right content at the right timing advances
customers’ decision -making proce sses, integrated engagement management between
marketing and sales functions is essential. Third, DCM should be positioned not only  as an
information dissemination activity, but also as a strategic tool that coordinates the

B2B contexts. Third, although this study aims to integrate existing theories, it does not
empirically test the causal relationships within the proposed framework.
Despite these limitations, the findings and research issues presented in this study are
expected to contribute to the development of more sophisticated and systematic research on
B2B DCM and CE.
