Abstract
This study examined how emotional consumer insight shapes purchase intention among female skincare users
and whether brand trust and emotional attachment explain this relationship. A quantitative explanatory -
correlational design was applied to 200 women aged 18 -30 years who used skincare, had purchased the
products used at least twice during the previous six months, actively used social media, and had encountered
skincare-related information or promotions online. Data were collected using Likert -scale instruments and
analyzed through mediation testing with JASP. Reliability testing showed satisfactory internal consistency for
emotional consumer insight (α = .889), purchase intention (α = .861), brand trust (α = .839), and emotional
attachment (α = .880). The findings indicate that emotional consumer insight positively predicts purchase
intention and brand trust. Brand trust significantly mediates the effect of emotional consumer insight on
purchase intention, whereas emotional attachment does not provide a significant mediating effect. These results
suggest that female skincare users rely more on trust in brand credibility and dependability than on emotional
attachment when forming purchase intention.

Introduction
The skincare industry in Indonesia has grown rapidly, especially among young women
who actively use social media. Competition among skincare brands encourages companies
not only to emphasize product quality, but also to build emotional connections with
consumers through emotional consumer insight. This approach seeks to make consumers
feel understood, cared for, and emotionally close to the brands they use. In the skincare
context, emotional aspects are important because beauty product use is often associat ed
with self-confidence, self-care, and women's personal identity (Mulyana & Layman, 2025).
The increasing use of social media platforms such as TikTok and Instagram also shapes
consumer behavior in skincare purchase decisions. Consumers no longer evaluate products
only by functional benefits; they also consider the emotional experience provided by the
brand. Mulyana and Layman ( 2025) show that perceived emotional value can increase
consumer interest in skincare products and support the emergence of purchase intention.
Purchase intention is also influenced by consumers' level of trust in a brand. For
skincare products, brand trust is critical because the products are applied directly to the
skin and may carry risks when quality does not meet expectations. Consumers who trust a
brand tend to feel safer and more confident in making purchases. The direct positive role of
brand trust in purchase intention has been shown by Jolie and Panjaya ( 2024) and
Thessalonian and Prasetyo ( 2025) in studies of local skincare consumers. Subarkah et al.
(2025) also found that brand trust significantly mediates the relationship between content
marketing and purchase intention among users of The Originote skincare.
Beyond trust, consumers' emotional attachment to a brand may also increase purchase
intention. Skincare consumers who feel compatible with, comfortable using, and positively
experienced with a brand may develop stronger emotional bonds. In this study, brand trust
and emotional attachment are positioned as mediating variables because both are expected
to explain the psychological mechanism through which emotional consumer insight affects
purchase intention. Consumers who feel emotionally understood by a bran d may develop
trust in that brand and, over time, emotional attachment that encourages purchase
behavior.
Studies on skincare purchase intention have been conducted, but much of the previous
work has focused on marketing factors such as social media marketing, brand image,
electronic word of mouth, and celebrity endorsers. Research specifically discussing the
influence of emotional consumer insight on purchase intention remains relatively limited,
particularly among female skincare users. In the beauty industry, emotional factors deserve
attention because consumers buy not only for product function, but also fo r emotional
experience and perceived connectedness with a brand.
The novelty of this study lies in the use of two mediating variables, brand trust and
emotional attachment, within one model. Prior studies generally used a single mediator,

The Effect of Emotional Consumer Insight on Purchase Intention
Emotional consumer insight refers to a brand's understanding of consumers' needs,
feelings, and emotional experiences. In the skincare industry, consumers not only consider
functional product benefits, but also whether a brand understands their emotional
condition. When consumers feel understood by the brand, they may experience comfort,
interest, and emotional closeness that increase their desire to purchase. According to Gobé
(2001), emotional relationships built through emotional branding can create consumer
connection to brands and encourage purchasing behavior. Kim and Sullivan ( 2019) also
show that emotional connection between consumers and brands positively affects
purchase intention.  Purchase intention refers to the tendency of consumers to buy a
product after forming a positive evaluation of the product or brand ( Kotler, Keller, &
Armstrong, 2019). In this study, purchase intention is reflected in four aspects: desire to
buy the product, interest in trying the product, plans to make a purchase, and possibility of
repurchase.
The emotional consumer insight variable is based on Gobé's  emotional branding
concept, which explains that emotional relationships between brands and consumers are
formed through deep understanding of consumer emotions. Its aspects include the brand's
understanding of consumers' emotional needs, consumers' emotional closeness to the
brand, relevance of brand messages to consumers' feelings, and consumers' perception of
being cared for and understood by the brand.  Therefore, the higher the emotional
consumer insight felt by consumers, the higher their purchase intention for skincare
products. H1: Emotional consumer insight has a positive effect on purchase intention.
The Influence of Emotional Consumer Insight on Brand Trust
Brands that understand consumers' emotional needs tend to gain consumer trust more
easily. Consumers may perceive that the brand is not solely focused on profit, but also cares
about their needs and comfort. In the skincare context, emotional understanding from a
brand can make consumers feel safer and more confident in product quality. Delgado -
Ballester (2004) explains that trust in a brand emerges when consumers believe that the
brand can meet expectations and has good intentions toward consumers. Rather et al.
(2019) also explain that consumers' emotional experiences influence the formation of
brand trust. Brand trust has two main aspects: brand reliability, namely the ability of the
brand to fulfill the value or promise offered so consumers perceive it as dependable; and
brand intentions, namely consumers' belief that the brand will consider consumers'
interests and well-being when unexpected problems occur.  Thus, the higher the emotional
consumer insight, the higher consumers' brand trust in skincare brands. H2: Emotional
consumer insight has a positive effect on brand trust.

The Effect of Emotional Consumer Insight on Emotional Attachment
A brand's emotional understanding can create a closer psychological connection
between consumers and the brand. Consumers who feel emotionally understood may find
it easier to build emotional attachment to a particular brand. In skincare use, this
relationship may arise because consumers feel comfortable, compatible, and positively
experienced with the brand. Thomson et al. ( 2005) describe emotional attachment as an
affective bond formed through meaningful emotional experience between consumers and
brands. Therefore, the higher the emotional consumer insight felt by consumers, the
stronger their emotional attachment to skincare brands. H3: Emotional consumer insight
has a positive effect on emotional attachment.
The Influence of Brand Trust on Purchase Intention
Brand trust is an important factor in increasing purchase intention, especially for
skincare products that are used directly on the skin. Consumers tend to buy products from
brands perceived as safe, high quality, and trustworthy. When consumers believe that the
brand can deliver the expected benefits, they become more confident in making a purchase.
Delgado-Ballester (2004) states that brand trust can reduce uncertainty and perceived risk
in purchase decisions. Jolie and Panjaya ( 2024) and Thessalonian and Prasetyo ( 2025) also
show that brand trust has a positive effect on purchase intention among local skincare
consumers. Therefore, the higher consumers' brand trust in skincare brands, the higher
their purchase intention. H4: Brand trust has a positive effect on purchase intention.
The Effect of Emotional Attachment on Purchase Intention
Consumers who have emotional attachment to a brand tend to show stronger loyalty
and desire to buy. Emotional attachment makes consumers feel close, comfortable, and less
willing to switch to other brands. In the skincare context, positive experiences and a sense
of belonging to a product can strengthen consumers' emotional connection with the brand.
Thomson et al. ( 2005) explain that consumers with emotional attachment to a brand are
more motivated to maintain their relationship with the brand through purchasing
behavior. Chen and Yang ( 2023) also show that emotional attachment significantly affects
purchase intention.
Emotional attachment refers to an emotional bond between consumers and brands
formed through deep affective experience. Its aspects include feeling close to the brand,
feeling comfortable using the brand, feelings of belonging to the brand, difficulty switching
to other brands, and emotional connection with the brand.  Thus, the higher consumers'
emotional attachment to skincare brands, the higher their purchase intention. H5:
Emotional attachment has a positive effect on purchase intention.

Brand trust acts as a mediator because emotional understanding provided by a brand
can increase consumer trust, and this trust can encourage purchase intention. Consumers
who feel emotionally understood by brands may have more confidence in the quality and
goodwill of the brand, making them more confident in buying skincare products. Subarkah
et al. ( 2025) also found that brand trust significantly mediates the relationship between

The Role of Emotional Attachment as a Mediator
In addition to building trust, emotional consumer insight can also create consumers'
emotional attachment to brands. Consumers who feel emotionally connected to a brand
may become more motivated to buy and use products continuously. Khuong et al. ( 2023)
show that consumer knowledge or insight about products combined with emotional
interest can trigger purchase intention. Therefore, emotional attachment is expected to
mediate the relationship between emotional consumer insight and purchase intention
because it may strengthen consumers' desire to buy skincare products.  H7: Emotional
attachment mediates the effect of emotional consumer insight on purchase intention.
Research Method
This study used a quantitative approach with an explanatory -correlational research
design. The study focused on the effect of emotional consumer insight on purchase
intention with brand trust and emotional attachment as mediating variables among female
skincare users. Purposive sampling was used because participants needed to meet specific
criteria relevant to the research topic. The sample criteria were: (1) women; (2) aged 18-30
years; (3) currently using skincare products; (4) had purchased the skincare products used
at least twice in the previous six months; (5) actively used social media such as TikTok,
Instagram, or Facebook; and (6) had seen information, promotions, reviews, or content
about skincare products through social media.
The number of participants in this study was 200. Data were collected using
psychological scales with a Likert response format to measure emotional consumer insight,
brand trust, emotional attachment, and purchase intention. Hypothesis testing was
conducted using mediation analysis with JASP version 0.95.4.0. Respondent characteristics
are presented in Table 1.

Results
The loading factor values for each indicator show the relationship between the
indicator and the latent variable it measures. For emotional consumer insight, the loading
factor values ranged from 0.75 to 0.88, indicating that all indicators contributed adequately
to the construct. Purchase intention had loading factor values of 0.70 to 0.82, brand trust
had values of 0.72 to 0.78, and emotional attachment had values of 0.75 to 0.83. Overall, all
indicators had loading factor values above 0.50 and were there fore considered valid in
representing their latent variables.
The analysis of relationships among variables showed that emotional consumer insight
had a positive and significant effect on purchase intention (β = 0.802; p < .001), so H1 was
accepted. Emotional consumer insight also had a positive and significant effect on brand
trust (β = 0.810; p < .001), so H2 was accepted. However, emotional consumer insight did
not significantly affect emotional attachment (β = 0.130; p = .062), so H3 was rejected.
Brand trust had a positive and significant effect on purchase inten tion (β = 0.527; p < .001),
so H4 was accepted. Emotional attachment also had a positive and significant effect on
purchase intention (β = 0.084; p = .020), so H5 was accepted, although the effect was
relatively small. In the mediation test, brand trust significantly mediated the effect of
emotional consumer insight on purchase intention (β = 0.527; p < .001), so H6 was
accepted. In contrast, emotional attachment did not mediate the relationship (β = 0.011; p
= .143), so H7 was rejected.

Discussion
The results of mediation analysis indicate that emotional consumer insight positively
influences purchase intention both directly and indirectly through the mediating variables.
This means that the higher consumers' emotional understanding of a skincare brand, the
higher their intention to buy the product. Consumers tend to gravitate toward brands that
can understand their needs, feelings, and emotional experiences.
The results also show that brand trust has a stronger mediating role than emotional
attachment. This can be seen from the coefficient of the effect of emotional consumer
insight on brand trust (β = .81) and the effect of brand trust on purchase intention (β = .53).
These findings indicate that skincare consumers consider trust in the brand before
deciding to buy. Trust may arise when consumers perceive that the brand can provide
product quality, safety, and benefits that match expectations. This finding is consistent with
Subarkah et al. ( 2025), who found that brand trust mediates the relationship between
content marketing and purchase intention among skincare users, and with Rather et al.
(2019), who found that emotional experience contributes to brand trust formation.
Meanwhile, the effect of emotional consumer insight on emotional attachment was
relatively low (β = .13), and the effect of emotional attachment on purchase intention was
also small (β = .08). This suggests that emotional understanding from brands is not yet
strong enough to create deep emotional attachment among skincare consumers.
Consumers may focus more on functional aspects such as product quality, skin
compatibility, ingredient safety, and practical benefits rather than on building emotional
bonds wit h one specific brand. Kusumasondjaja ( 2024) also found that emotional
attachment to celebrity endorsers does not necessarily have a direct significant effect on
purchase intention when consumers consider other factors such as quality and brand
attractiveness.

The high trend of skincare consumption and the large number of product choices
available through social media also make it easier for consumers to try new brands. This
condition may make emotional attachment to a single skincare brand less stable.
Consequently, emotional attachment is not a strong mediator in the relationship between
emotional consumer insight and purchase intention. The finding is also consistent with
research suggesting that skincare consumers consider brand reputation and functional
factors more strongly than emotional factors when forming purchase intention ( Claudia &
Ruslim, 2026).
Based on the R² value, the purchase intention variable had R² = .749. This means that
74.9% of the variation in purchase intention can be explained by emotional consumer
insight, brand trust, and emotional attachment, while the remaining 25.1% is influenced by
factors outside the research model. The brand trust variable had R² = .656, indicating that
65.6% of brand trust variation can be explained by emotional consumer insight. In
contrast, emotional attachment had R² = .017, meaning that only 1.7% of its v ariation can
be explained by emotional consumer insight. Overall, brand trust is the psychological factor
that plays the most important role in increasing purchase intention among female skincare
users.
Conclusion and Suggestions
This study concludes that emotional consumer insight contributes positively to
purchase intention among female skincare users. Consumers who feel emotionally
understood by skincare brands are more likely to show buying interest, particularly when
that emotional understanding is converted into brand trust. The results show that brand
trust is a significant mediator in the relationship between emotional consumer insight and
purchase intention, while emotional attachment does not significantly mediate the
relationship. Although emotional attachment has a positive effect on purchase intention, its
role is weaker than brand trust in explaining consumers' intention to buy skincare
products.
Practically, skincare brands should translate emotional messaging into trust -building
cues, such as transparent product information, credible claims, consistent product quality,
safety assurance, and responsiveness to consumer concerns. Emotional communication
should not stand alone; it should be supported by evidence of reliability and consumer -
oriented brand intentions. For future research, additional variables such as perceived
quality, brand reputation, influencer credibility, electronic word of mouth, and perceived
behavioral control can be tested to explain the remaining variance in purchase intention.
Future studies may also compare different age groups, skincare categories, or local and
international brands to improve generalizability.
