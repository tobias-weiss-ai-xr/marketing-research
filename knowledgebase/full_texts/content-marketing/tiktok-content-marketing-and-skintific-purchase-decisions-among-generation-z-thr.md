ABSTRACT
This study examines the influence of TikTok Content Marketing on Purchase
Decision among Generation Z consumers of Skintific  skincare products, with
Perceived Value, Online Customer Reviews, and Customer Engagement as mediating
variables. Grounded in the Stimulus – Organism - Response (SOR) framework, the
research analyses how marketing content as external stimuli affects consu mer
psychological states and subsequent purchase behaviours. A quantitative survey was
conducted, with 359 valid responses from Indonesian Generation Z consumers (born
between 1997 and 2012) who actively use TikTok and have been exposed to Skintific
content. Data analysis was performed using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation
Modeling (PLS-SEM) with SmartPLS -4. TikTok Content Marketing significantly
affects purchase decisions both directly and through multiple mediation mechanisms.
Perceived Value, Online Customer Reviews, and Customer Engagement serve as
significant mediators, with Cu stomer Engagement carrying the strongest indirect
effect (β = 0.258), followed by Online Customer Reviews (β = 0.203) and Perceived
Value (β = 0.186). The total indirect effect (VAF = 79.7%) indicates that
approximately 80% of content marketing's influence  operates through mediating
mechanisms. These findings extend the SOR framework by empirically validating
three complementary mediating mechanisms, offering a more comprehensive model
of how short -form video content marketing shapes Generation Z consumer
behaviour. From a practitioner's perspective, beauty brands targeting Generation Z
should place greater emphasis on TikTok content marketing strategies that
simultaneously strengthen perceived value, stimulate online reviews, and drive
customer engagement.

Introduction
Scrolling short form video have reshaped the way people, in this term we call it consumers, discover
and evaluate products. TikTok has come out as a powerful force in shaping behaviour to purchase among
the younger generation. By 2024, TikTok's global user  base exceeded 1.9 billion, representing a 40%
increase from 2021 (Wei, 2025; Huo & Filieri, 2025). Generation Z accounts for approximately 27.9% of
Indonesia’s population, and 89% of this cohort actively uses TikTok as a primary source of product
discovery (Tang et al., 2025) . In this digital shift, the skincare industry has particularly reshaped, with
hashtags such as #SkincareRoutine, which has accumulated over 50 billion views globally and has become
the commercial potency of beauty content on the platform (Solikhah et al., 2022).
This transformation reflects a broader paradigm shift from interruptive advertising toward value -
driven, audience-centric content marketing strategies (Jacob & Johnson, 2021; Hollebeek & Macky, 2019).
Content marketing creates materials that audiences seek out intentionally, and it is contrary to conventional
promotional approaches, positioning brands as informational authorities instead of mere product vendors
(Holliman & Rowley, 2014). TikTok's unique algorithmic architecture has widely opened brand exposure
and enabled an exceptional level of organic reach, which prioritizes content engagement rather than follower
counts (Wei, 2025 ; Li et al., 2022) . Generation Z consumers (born between 1997 and 2012) exhibit a
particularly strong responsiveness to this model. Defined by digital nativity, value -consciousness, and
heightened scepticism toward traditional advertising, 67% of individuals within this cohort report that social
media directly influences their purchase decisions (Nuzula & Wahyudi, 2022; Tang et al., 2025).
Notwithstanding the rapid rise of TikTok content marketing, meaningful theoretical and empirical
gaps remain. First, the majority of existing research has predominantly focused on established platforms
such as Instagram and Facebook, with limited attention to the unique dynamics of short-form video content
(Wei, 2025; Jacob & Johnson, 2021). Second, previous studies have largely focused on individual mediating
variables such as brand engagement or perceived value in isolation, without integrating multiple
psychological and behavioural mediators into a comprehensive framework (Li et al., 2022); (Nguyen et al.,
2025). Third, content marketing scholarship has been largely concentrated in Western contexts or focused
on mature generational groups, leaving Generation Z consumer behaviour in emerging markets
underexplored (Tang et al., 2025; Nuzula & Wahyudi, 2022). These gaps carry a practical cost of urgency;
beauty brands are committing significant resources to TikTok marketing without evidence, which is based
on frameworks to optimize content strategies for Generation Z audiences (Solikhah et al., 2022).
This study addresses these gaps by examining how TikTok content marketing influences purchase
decisions among Generation Z consumers of Skintific  skincare products, with perceived value, online
customer reviews, and customer engagement serving as mediating mechanisms. Theoretically, it is
grounded in the Stimulus -Organism-Response (SOR) framework (Mehrabian & Russell, 1974) , which
frames content marketing as the environmental stimulus (S), the three mediating constructs as internal
organismic states (O), and the purchase decision as the behavioural response (R). The research objectives
are threefold: (1) to examine the direc t effect of TikTok content marketing on purchase decision among
Generation Z consumers; (2) to investigate the mediating roles of perceived value, online customer reviews,
and customer engagement; and (3) to provide empirical validation for the applicabili ty of the SOR
framework in explaining the effectiveness of content marketing on short-form video platforms.
Therefore, this study spans both theoretical and practical domains. Theoretically, this study enriches
the SOR framework by empirically validating a comprehensive mediation model encompassing cognitive,

Literature Review
Stimulus-Organism-Response (SOR) Theory
The SOR framework originated in environmental psychology, developed by Mehrabian and Russell
(1974) proposed it to explain how external environmental stimuli (S) influence individuals' internal
cognitive and affective states (O), which then drive behavioural responses (R). The framework has been
widely validated across consumer behaviour contexts, inclu ding online shopping, social media marketing,
and e -commerce adoption (Yu & Li, 2023 ; Wei, 2025) . Its highlight that the framework’s explanatory
strength lies precisely in modelling how environmental factors do not directly determine behaviour but
operate through internal psychological mechanisms (Lin & Shen, 2023).
Applied to digital marketing contexts, the stimulus component encompasses both the marketing
communications and the platform’s structural features; the organism component represents consumers'
internal psychological states, including cognitive evaluations (perceived value), affective responses
(engagement), and social considerations (review credibility); and the response component captures
observable behaviours such as purchase decisions (Nguyen et al., 2025; Yu & Li, 2023) . Recent TikTok-
specific applications to social media contexts have proven the framework's particular relevance for content
marketing effectiveness. Wei (2025)  used it to trace how TikTok content attributes influence purchase
intentions through perceived relevance, and Nguyen et al. (2025) utilized it to investigate ephemeral content
marketing through customer engagement mechanisms.
For the present research, the SOR framework declares an explicit attention for examining how
TikTok content marketing as an external (stimulus) influences purchase decisions (response) through
perceived value, online customer reviews, and customer engageme nt (organismic states), enabling
systematic investigation of the psychological processes through which content marketing affects consumer
behaviour (Wei, 2025 ; Huo & Filieri, 2025) . Consistent with Mehrabian and Russell (1974)
Conceptualisation of the organism as the internal cognitive and affective states that intervene between a
stimulus and a behavioural response, online customer reviews are treated here not as the external messages
themselves but as the consumer's internal processing of, and trust toward, those reviews. Specifically,
exposure to TikTok content prompts an internal evaluation in which consumers interpret review valence,
credibility, and consistency, and it is this internalised appraisal and the resulting trust, rather than the reviews
as an external cue, that subsequently shapes their purchase response. This cognitive and evaluative nature
justifies the placement of online customer reviews under the Organism component.
Generation Z Consumer Behaviour
Generation Z, comprising individuals born between 1997 and 2012, is widely described as the first
truly digital-native cohort, but that label understates an important nuance. Their consumption characteristics
are strengthened by digital fluency preference for authentic value consciousness, peer -generated content,

and expectation of seamless omnichannel experiences (Huo & Filieri, 2025; Tang et al., 2025). In Indonesia,
this cohort shows an exceptionally high level of social media adoption rates, conducting extensive online
research before purchases and consulting an average of 4.5 information sources (Nuzula & Wahyudi, 2022).
Unlike older generations, Generation Z naturally integrates product discovery into leisure consumption,
viewing shopping as a social activity (Solikhah et al., 2022). Despite growing recognition of Generation Z's
economic importance, research specifically examining this cohort's response to TikTok content marketing
through several mediating mechanisms remains limited (Huo & Filieri, 2025).
TikTok Content Marketing
As the stimulus (S) in this study, TikTok content marketing refers to the creation and dissemination
of valuable, relevant, and compelling brand-related content on the platform that engages consumers through
informative, authentic, emotional, and entertain ing material rather than direct persuasion (Wei,
2025;Hollebeek & Macky, 2019) . Drawing on prior studies, the construct is operationalised through four
dimensions: informative content delivers useful product knowledge, such as authentic content, which builds
a sense of closeness to the brand, emotional content, which resonates with consumers' feelings, and
entertainment content triggers imagination and curiosity involvement (Wei, 2025; Yu & Li, 2023) . The
selection of these characteristics because Generation Z actively seeks out content that is genuine and
entertaining while rejecting overt advertising, making the informational, affective, and hedonic qualities of
TikTok content a particularly relevant stimulus for this cohort.
Perceived Value
Perceived value, the first organismic state (O), captures a multidimensional construct representing
the consumer's overall evaluation of a product based on the trade-off between perceived benefits and costs
(Blanco-González et al., 2025). The construct encompasses three dimensions of the study's functional value,
reflecting the quality and price worthiness of the product. Emotional value means capturing the positive
feelings derived from the purchase experience, and social value relates t o the status and social acceptance
the product confers (Blanco-González et al., 2025; Nuzula & Wahyudi, 2022). This construct emphasizes
the model because Generation Z is highly value -conscious, weighing functional, emotional, and social
returns before committing to a purchase, so they internalise the value from TikTok content as a key cognitive
pathway to their decisions.
Online Customer Reviews
Online customer reviews (OCRs), the second organic state (O), are consumer-generated evaluations
and comments about products or services on online platforms, serving as a form of electronic word of mouth
whose persuasive impact depends on factors such as valence, quantity, accuracy, completeness, consistency,
and usefulness (Ngo et al., 2025). In this study, the construct is measured through review valence, review
quantity, and review accuracy, which together reflect how consumers internally process and trust the
feedback they encounter (Ngo et al., 2025) . OCRs are particularly important for Generation Z skincare
consumers because product efficacy varies by individual skin type. Authentic peer reviews, therefore, act
as a risk reduction and social proof mechanism, which creates how this cohort evaluates products discovered
through TikTok content.
Customer Engagement
Customer engagement, the third organismic state (O), is a multidimensional psychological state
characterised by cognitive, emotional, and behavioural activity in brand interactions, representing the

consumer's motivational investment in interactive, co-creative experiences with a brand (Sallaku & Vigolo,
2024; Hollebeek & Macky, 2019) . It is reflected through dimensions such as enthusiasm, cognitive
engagement, attention, and dedication, which capture the depth of a consumer's involvement with brand
content (Sallaku & Vigolo, 2024). Engagement is especially relevant for Generation Z on TikTok, which is
used primarily for entertainment and social connection. The platform's interactive features lower
participation barriers and foster habitual engagement, making active involvement a cr itical behavioural-
affective route through which content marketing influences their decisions.
Purchase Decision
Purchase decision, the behavioural response (R) refers to the consumer's intention and willingness
to buy a specific product or brand after identifying needs, evaluating alternatives, and choosing between
options, which represents the purposeful motivation  and subjective likelihood of taking a consumption
action (Yu & Li, 2023 ; Abdul Latip et al., 2025) . It is measured through dimensions such as purchase
intention, repurchase intention, product knowledge, experiential factors, and trust (Abdul Latip et al., 2025).
As the terminal outcome of the SOR sequence, this construct captures how the cognitive, social, and
behavioural states activated by TikTok content translate into the buying behaviour of Generation Z skincare
consumers.
Hypotheses Development
Content Marketing and Purchase Decision
Content marketing describes a value-driven strategic approach focused on creating and distributing
valuable, relevant, and consistent content to attract and retain defined audiences that ultimately drive
profitable customer action (Jacob & Johnson, 2021 ; Holliman & Rowley, 2014) . On TikTok, short -form
video content (15 –60 seconds) is blending entertainment, education, and product information in formats
optimized for mobile consumption (Wei, 2025) . The effectiveness of the platform stems from several
characteristics: its algorithm prioritizes content quality over follower counts, enabling high organic reach;
its integration of user-generated content (UGC) with branded content blurs advertising boundaries, reducing
avoidance behaviours; and its interactive features, such as duet, stitch, and commenting, facilitate
participatory content creation (Li et al., 2022; Hollebeek & Macky, 2019).
Empirically, TikTok content marketing's influence on consumer behaviour. (Wei, 2025) found that
content attractiveness, emotional appeal, and trustworthiness were significantly predict purchase intentions
among Chinese TikTok users. (Solikhah et al., 2022)  demonstrated that TikTok skincare content drove
purchase decisions more effectively than Instagram or YouTube content, attributed to the platform's
authentic aesthetic. (Tang et al., 2025)  showed that TikTok content marketing influences Generation Z
purchase decisions through both rational evaluation and emotional response. Complementing this evidence,
(Rakhmawati, 2023)  highlighted that social media marketing does not significantly affect consumer
purchase decisions on the Shopee marketplace, and emphasized that content strategies incorporating visual
elements, promotional activities, and key opinion leaders need to be o ptimized to truly drive purchase
decisions through social media channels.
Although classical SOR theory discourages a direct stimulus to response path without an
intervening organismic state, contemporary social commerce environments justify modelling a direct effect
alongside the mediated paths. TikTok integrates content discovery with in-app shopping, and its short-form
videos frequently trigger immediate, heuristic, and spontaneous purchasing responses, for example, through
instant checkout features such as the in -app yellow shopping basket ("Keranjang  Kuning"), which allows

consumers to buy while still viewing content and thereby bypass extended cognitive and affective
evaluation. This impulse-oriented, social commerce behaviour bridges traditional SOR theory with present-
day digital consumption habits. Recent empirical work on short-form video and live streaming marketing
similarly reports that platform stimuli can directly drive purchase decisions and impulse buying (Wei, 2025;
Tang et al., 2025), supporting the inclusion of a direct path (S → R) in the present model before the mediation
hypotheses are examined. Based on SOR theory and this evidence, we hypothesize:
H1: TikTok content marketing positively influences purchase decisions.
Content Marketing, Perceived Value, and Purchase Decision
Perceived value represents consumers' overall evaluation of a product's utility based on perceptions
of what is received relative to what is given. Within marketing literature, perceived value has been defined
as the psychological evaluations and affective  judgment consumers have about the perceived benefits of
purchasing a product or service (Hanaysha, 2018). Content marketing enhances perceived value through
multiple mechanisms. First, reducing information asymmetry via detailed product knowledge (Li et al.,
2022; Ngo et al., 2025), Second, it generates hedonic value through entertaining content (Wei, 2025). Third,
trend-embedded content creates social proof through authentic peer recommendations (Nuzula & Wahyudi,
2022; Tang et al., 2025).
Within the SOR framework, perceived value is managed as a critical cognitive organismic state.
Hanaysha (2018) confirmed that perceived value has a significant positive influence on purchase decisions
in the retail context and that marketing programs structured around added value effectively reinforce
consumer purchases. Nuzula & Wahyudi (2022) has found that perceived value mediates the relationship
between brand attitude and luxury product purchase intentions, while Li et al. (2022)  demonstrated
perceived value mediated the relationship between content quality and online purchase intentions. Despite
this empirical support, the perceived value's specific function in TikTok content marketing among
Generation Z consumers remains underex plored. Building on this evidence, TikTok content marketing is
expected to enhance the functional, emotional, and social value that Generation Z consumers perceive in
Skintific products, and this elevated perceived value is in turn expected to drive their purchase decisions.
Therefore:
H2: Perceived value mediates the relationship between TikTok content marketing and purchase
decision.
Content Marketing, Online Customer Reviews, and Purchase Decision
Online customer reviews (OCRs) constitute user-generated evaluative statements about products or
services posted on internet platforms, and represent a prominent manifestation of electronic word-of-mouth
(eWOM) that influences consumer decision -making (Ngo et al., 2025) . According to Baier et al. (2025)
OCRs are characterized as semi -formal evaluations that provide necessary customer feedback and help
improve reviewed products and services. In the skincare industry, where product efficacy varies by
individual skin type, customer reviews provide particularly valuable information about real-world product
performance (Solikhah et al., 2022).
TikTok-based content marketing uniquely stimulates review generation through several
mechanisms: engaging content increases brand awareness and broadens the customer base capable of
generating reviews (Li et al., 2022) . TikTok's interactive features, such as the comment section and duet,
enable immediate review, like, and feedback directly within content (Wei, 2025), and viral trends such as

#TikTokMadeMeBuyIt create social reactions and also movements around product discovery and review
sharing (Solikhah et al., 2022). Ngo et al. (2025) found that multiple dimensions of online customer reviews
(valence, quantity, accuracy, completeness, consistency, and usefulness) mediated the effects of online
customer reviews on online shopping intentions. As Baier et al. (2025) Note, OCRs are persistently available
on digital platforms and serve as a rich knowledge source, making them integral to consumer decision
processes.
From the perspective of the SOR framework, OCRs represent a social organismic state where
content marketing stimuli trigger information -seeking and social comparison processes. Crucially, this
internal state subsequently shapes the purchase response (O → R) for skincare consumers; reading authentic
reviews functions as a risk reduction mechanism that lowers uncertainty about product efficacy and builds
immediate trust, which in turn drives purchase decisions. Hanaysha et al. (2025) confirmed that customer
reviews have both a direct positive effect on online purchase intention and a significant indirect effect
through trust, confirming that reviews build consumer confidence and reduce perceived risk, thereby
increasing the likelihood  of making a purchase. Similarly, Ngo et al. (2025)  demonstrated that multiple
dimensions of online customer reviews significantly enhance perceived trust and perceived value, which in
turn positively drive consumers' online purchase intentions. Therefore:
H3: Online customer reviews mediate the relationship between TikTok content marketing and
purchase decision.
Content Marketing, Customer Engagement, and Purchase Decision
Customer engagement is conceptualized as a multidimensional construct encompassing cognitive,
emotional, and behavioural investment in brand interactions beyond purchase transactions (Hollebeek &
Macky, 2019) . In digital environments, engagement is an investment that is expressed through likes,
comments, shares, content creation, and sustained attention to brand communications. (Nguyen et al., 2025).
Distinct from passive content exposure, engagement reflects active psychological investment, making it a
critical predictor of purchase outcomes. (Li et al., 2022).
Content marketing drives engagement through functional motives (seeking useful information),
hedonic motives (entertainment), and authenticity motives (credibility and connection) as identified by
Hollebeek & Macky (2019). Within the TikTok environment, the short-form video format, algorithm-driven
content discovery, and interactive features create particularly conducive conditions for engagement (Wei,
2025). Recent empirical evidence further provides strong support for engagement's mediating role. Li et al.
(2022) demonstrated that customer engagement mediated relationships between digital content marketing
and purchase intentions, with engagement showing 34% of the total effect.
 Ferdianto et al. (2024)  confirmed that customer engagement serves as a significant mediating
pathway through which content marketing on TikTok translates into purchase intention among Generation
Z consumers, demonstrating that active cognitive and behavioural involvement with ma rketing content,
rather than passive exposure, drives initial transactional responses. Saifudin et al., (2025) showed that viral
marketing through social media significantly generates interest and encourages purchase decisions,
indicating that content strategies with a viral character, as commonly employed in TikTok content
marketing, are effective in influencing consumer purchase decisions through increased engagement and
brand awareness. Furthermore, the result underscores that highly engaged consumers develop stronger
emotional connections, leading to favourable transactional outcomes. Therefore:

Conceptual Framework
Grounded in the SOR theoretical framework, this study develops a comprehensive model examining
how TikTok content marketing (stimulus) influences purchase decisions (response) through three mediating
organismic states, which are perceived value (cognitive) , online customer reviews (social), and customer
engagement (affective -behavioural). Exposure to Skintific  skincare content on TikTok triggers
simultaneous activation of these three mechanisms, which collectively influence purchase decisions among
Generation Z consumers.
Methodology
Research Design
This study adopts a quantitative approach with an explanatory design to test causal relationships
within the SOR model. TikTok content marketing functions as the stimulus (S), while perceived value,
online customer reviews, and customer engagement represen t the organism (O) components reflecting
consumers' internal psychological processes. Purchase decision is conceptualized as the behavioural
response (R). The SOR model has been widely validated in prior consumer behaviour research within digital
and e-commerce contexts (Tang et al., 2025).
 This study population is defined as Generation Z consumers (born 1997 - 2012) who have been
exposed to Skintific content marketing on TikTok and have made purchases of Skintific products.
Generation Z was chosen as the target population because this cohort constitutes the largest and most active
user base on TikTok in Indonesia and is the primary consumer segment for skincare products marketed

Instruments and Measurement
The research instrument was a structured questionnaire design measuring five variables based on
the SOR framework: content marketing (8 items), online customer reviews (12 items), customer engagement
(5 items), perceived value (5 items), and purchase decision (5 items). All items were measured using a 10-
point Likert scale (1 = strongly disagree to 10 = strongly agree) adapted from previously validated studies.
Content marketing items were adapted from (Wei, 2025) and (Yu & Li, 2023); online customer review items
from (Ngo et al., 2025); customer engagement items from (Sallaku & Vigolo, 2024); perceived value items
from (Blanco-González et al., 2025); and purchase decision items from (Abdul Latip et al., 2025). Data has
been collected through online questionnaires distributed via Google Forms throughout January 2026. The
complete variable definitions and indicator items are presented in Appendix A.
Data Analysis
Data analysis was conducted using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM)
with SmartPLS-4. PLS-SEM was chosen due to its capability to handle complex models with multiple
mediators and its suitability for testing SOR-based theoretical models (Hair et al., 2021). The measurement
model evaluation included convergent validity assessed by factor loadings (≥0.70) and Average Variance
Extracted/AVE (≥0.50), discriminant validity evaluated through HTMT (<0.85), and reliability through
Cronbach's Alpha and Composite Reliability (≥0.70) (Henseler et al., 2015). The structural model evaluation
encompassed collinearity checks using (VIF <5), coefficient of determination (R²), effect size (f²), and
predictive relevance (Q²) through blindfolding procedures (Hair et al., 2021). Hypothesis testing employed
bootstrapping with 5,000 subsamples at a 95% confidence level (p < 0.05). Mediation analysis used the
Variance Accounted For (VAF) approach, where VAF <20% indicates no mediation, 20 -80% indicates
partial mediation, and >80% indicates full mediation. Model fit was assessed using SRMR (<0.08).

Discussion
This study shows empirical validation of the SOR framework in explaining how TikTok content
marketing shapes Generation Z purchase decisions through interconnected psychological and social
mechanisms. The structural model accounts for 77.8% of variance in purchase decisions (R² = 0.778),
substantially surpassing the explanatory power typically reported in prior content marketing studies, which
generally achieve R² values between 0.45 and 0.65 (Li et al., 2022; Nguyen et al., 2025; Wei, 2025). Three
features of the present study's design likely explain this superior predictive performance: the integration of

Nur et al., 2026  334

three complementary mediating mechanisms that capture distinct psychological pathways, the restriction of
the sample to a specific generational cohort, which reduces between-subject heterogeneity, and the platform-
specific focus on TikTok, which enables mo re precise measurement within a consistent technological
context. Importantly, 79.7% of content marketing's total effect on purchase decisions flows through the three
mediating pathways rather than directly, strongly confirming the SOR premise that environ mental stimuli
shape behaviour through internal psychological processing, and this also strongly reinforces the SOR
theoretical premise that environmental stimuli shape behaviour through internal psychological processing
rather than direct influence (Mehrabian & Russell, 1974; Wei, 2025).
Content Marketing and Purchase Decision
TikTok content marketing exerts a direct and significant, though comparatively modest, effect on
purchase decision (β = 0.165, t = 2.264, p = 0.024). It’s a small effect size (f² = 0.026), which confirms that
content marketing can directly trigger purchase responses without requiring full mediation through internal
organismic states. Theoretically, this direct path reflects the social commerce reality in which TikTok’s
short-form videos can trigger immediate, heuristic purchasing responses that partly bypas s extended
cognitive and affective evaluation, consistent with impulse buying behaviour in integrated content and
commerce environments. Classical SOR formulations posit that stimuli must be filtered through internal
organismic states before producing beha vioural responses (Mehrabian & Russell, 1974) . Contemporary
digital commerce environments justify the inclusion of a direct stimulus to the response path. TikTok
uniquely integrates content discovery with transactional functionality, enabling consumers to move from
content exposure to purchase in a seamless, low-friction process that can avoid deliberate evaluation.
This finding aligns with prior research on short -form video and social media advertising. Wei
(2025) reported that content marketing characteristics on TikTok directly affect consumer purchase
intentions, and Tang et al. (2025)  similarly pointed out that platform stimuli can directly drive purchase
decisions and impulse buying on short-form video platforms. In the Indonesian MSME specifically, Alfajri
et al. (2026)  confirmed that social media promotion exerts a direct and significant effect on consumer
purchasing intention for food products, and reinforces the notion that digital promotional content functions
as a stimulus capable of directly shaping purchase respon ses even without mediating variables. However,
the modest magnitude of this direct effect (β = 0.165) compared to the combined indirect effects (β = 0.647)
indicates that most of the influence is transmitted through the mediators, rather than directly, consistent with
the SOR framework’s emphasis on internal processing.
Within the Skintific context on TikTok, this direct path can be attributed to the platform's in -app
shopping features, particularly the “Keranjang Kuning” (yellow shopping basket), which allows consumers
to purchase products directly while viewing Skintifi c’s branded content. Engaging product demonstration
videos, limited -time promotional offers, and visually appealing short -form content can prompt some
immediate purchases among Generation Z consumers who encounter Skintific products during their TikTok
browsing sessions. Nevertheless, the results suggest that building perceived value, encouraging authentic
reviews, and driving meaningful engagement remain the dominant routes to purchase for Skintific among
this cohort.
Content Marketing, Perceived Value, and Purchase Decision
Perceived value significantly mediates the relationship between TikTok content marketing and
purchase decision as hypothesized with a significant content marketing to perceived value path (β = 0.807,
t = 25.745, p < 0.001), a significant perceived value to  purchase decision path (β = 0.231, t = 3.363, p =

0.001), and a positive and significant specific indirect effect (β = 0.186, t = 3.352, p = 0.001). Content
marketing explained 65.2% of the variance in perceived value (R² = 0.652), which confirms its strong role
in shaping how Generation Z consumers value perceptions.
From a theoretical standpoint, this pathway illustrates the Stimulus-Organism-Response mechanism
precisely. Content marketing (Stimulus) enhances consumers’ internal cognitive evaluation of value
(Organism), which subsequently drives purchase behaviour (Re sponse). When Generation Z consumers
encounter TikTok content marketing about Skintific, they engage in an evaluative process that weighs the
perceived benefits against expected costs and forms a value judgment. This internal organismic state,
encompassing both rational assessment and affective appreciation of the content, also serves as the
psychological mechanism that transforms external marketing stimuli into behavioural intentions.
This finding builds on and contextualises prior evidence. Jacob and Johnson (2021) established that
digital content marketing creates unique value to customers by facilitating problem-solving and information
assimilation, which subsequently influences purchase decisions. Holliman and Rowley (2014)  similarly
emphasized that content marketing creates value by educating consumers about products and thereby
facilitating the buying decision -making process. The present finding further aligns with Hanaysha (2018)
who demonstrated that perceived value is a significant influence on purchase decisions and that marketing
initiatives associated with added value reinforce consumer purchasing. This study extends these findings by
confirming their applicability to the short-form video content marketing context on TikTok.
In the context of TikTok and Skintific, perceived value is managed through multiple dimensions.
Functionally, Skintific’s TikTok content provides ingredient education, application tutorials, and skincare
routine demonstrations that help Generation Z consumers assess product efficacy and make informed
choices. Emotionally, the entertaining and visually appealing nature of sho rt-form video content creates
positive affective associations with the brand. Socially, participating in trending skincare conversations and
being part of the Skintific  community on TikTok adds social value to the consumption experience. For
Generation Z consumers in the Indonesian beauty category, these multidimensional value perceptions
collectively reduce perceived risk and increase purchase confidence. (Nuzula & Wahyudi, 2022).
Content Marketing, Online Customer Reviews, and Purchase Decision
Online customer reviews significantly mediate the relationship between TikTok content marketing
and purchase decision, with a significant content marketing -to-reviews path (β = 0.806, t = 29.568, p <
0.001), a significant reviews to purchase decision path (β = 0.252, t = 4.205, p < 0.001), and a positive and
significant specific indirect effect (β = 0.203, t = 4.191, p < 0.001). Content marketing explained 64.9% of
variance in online customer reviews (R² = 0.649), demonstrating that effective content market ing
substantially stimulates review -related behaviours. Within the SOR framework, online customer reviews
serve as a social organismic state where content marketing stimuli activate information -seeking and social
comparison processes among consumers. When exposed to Skintific’s TikTok content, consumers do not
passively receive brand messages instead. They actively seek validation from fellow consumers through
reviews, comments, and user -generated testimonials. This social validation process represents an internal
cognitive and social mechanism that filters external marketing stimuli through peer-based trust assessment
before generating purchase responses. For skincare consumers, reading authentic reviews functions as a risk
reduction and social proof mechanism that lowers uncertainty about product efficacy and builds immediate
trust, which in turn drives the final purchase decision.

The finding extends previous eWOM research by demonstrating how content marketing and organic
user conversations integrate within social commerce ecosystems. As Baier et al. (2025) emphasize, online
customer reviews provide a rich and persistently available knowledge source for consumers and play an
evaluative role in shaping purchase decisions. It is particularly salient in contexts where product quality
assessment depends on user-generated feedback. Ngo et al. (2025) similarly found that multiple dimensions
of online customer reviews, including valence, accuracy, and usefulness, significantly enhance perceived
trust and perceived value, which subsequently drive consumers’ online shopping intentions. Hanaysha et al.
(2025) further demonstrated that customer reviews have both a direct positive effect on online purchase
intention and a significant indirect effect through trust, confirming that reviews build consumer confidence
and reduce perceived risk.
On TikTok, the Skintific brand environment uniquely amplifies the review mechanism in several
ways. First, TikTok’s comment sections have functioned as real-time review repositories where users share
immediate reactions to Skintific products, including before and after results, ingredient analyses, and usage
experiences. Second, the #TikTokMadeMeBuyIt hashtag and similar viral trends create dedicated spaces
for authentic user testimonials that reach audiences far beyond the original content creator’s followers.
Third, TikTok’s interactive features, such as the duet and stitch functions, enable direct response and review
creation that integrate seamlessly into the content ecosystem (Solikhah et al., 2022 ; Wei, 2025) . For
Indonesian Generation Z consumers who are particularly sensitive to peer influence, these platform native
review mechanisms offer a trusted information source that significantly shapes their skincare purchase
decisions.
Content Marketing, Customer Engagement, and Purchase Decision
Customer engagement significantly mediates the relationship between TikTok content marketing
and purchase decision with a significant content marketing to engagement path (β = 0.863, t = 30.248, p <
0.001), a significant engagement to purchase decision path (β = 0.299, t = 3.873, p < 0.001), and the strongest
specific indirect effect among all mediators (β = 0.258, t = 3.922, p < 0.001). Content marketing explained
74.5% of the variance in customer engagement (R² = 0.745), the highest explanatory power amon g all
endogenous constructs, with a large effect size (f² = 2.925). Theoretically, customer engagement represents
the most consequential organismic state within the SOR framework as applied to this study. Unlike
perceived value and online customer reviews,  which involve primarily cognitive evaluation, customer
engagement encompasses a multidimensional psychological state involving cognitive attention, affective
connection, and behavioural participation (Hollebeek & Macky, 2019). When Generation Z consumers are
exposed to TikTok content marketing (Stimulus), they develop an immersive and participatory organismic
state characterized by active interaction with the content, emotional investment in the brand, and sustained
behavioural involvement. This deep psychological engagement then transforms into purchase behaviour
(Response) not through calculated deliberation but through the organic emergence of purchase intentions
from sustained participatory experiences.
This engagement dominant pattern highlights a fundamental difference between Generation Z’s
digital consumption patterns and those of previous cohorts. Compared to older consumers who typically use
social media instrumentally for information seeking, Generation Z appears to engage with content primarily
for entertainment and social connection, with purchase decisions emerging organically from sustained
engagement (Huo & Filieri, 2025; Tang et al., 2025). This finding is consistent with the work of Ferdianto
et al. (2024),  who observed that customer engagement significantly mediates the relationship between
marketing stimuli and purchase decisions in social commerce contexts, confirming that active psychological

and behavioural investment is more consequential than passive information processing in driving
transactional outcomes. Furthermore, Li et al. (2022)  demonstrated that content -driven engagement on
social media platforms strengthens brand relationships and subsequently facilitates purchase behaviour, and
Wei (2025) also confirmed that engagement with TikTok content significantly affects consumer purchase
intentions. From a theoretical standpoint, these findings indicate that traditional information processing
models of consumer decision making may fall short in capt uring Generation Z’s behaviour on
entertainment-oriented platforms. Instead, frameworks emphasizing experiential consumption, community
participation, and identity expression may better explain purchase decisions in these contexts (Hollebeek &
Macky, 2019).
Within the TikTok and Skintific environment, customer engagement is amplified by several
platform-specific affordances. TikTok’s algorithm -driven content distribution ensures that Skintific’s
branded content reaches users who have demonstrated interest in skincare, creating a self-reinforcing loop
of content exposure and engagement. The platform’s low participation barriers, including likes, comments,
shares, duets, and stitches, encourage even casual viewers to actively interact with Skintific  content.
Furthermore, challenge-based campaigns that deliver direct questions prompting comment responses, and
featuring user -generated content in Skintific’s  strategy, encourage participatory engagement that goes
beyond passive consumption. Generation Z consumers in Indonesia demonstrate strong peer influence
sensitivity in this emerging market with rapid social media adoption and a youthful population. These
engagement mechanisms create habitual brand interaction patterns that naturally culminate in purchase
decisions.
Conclusion
This study provides robust empirical evidence support that TikTok content marketing shapes
Generation Z purchase decisions through three distinct yet complementary psychological mechanisms.
Those are perceived value, online customer reviews, and customer e ngagement. The findings validate and
extend the SOR framework by demonstrating that approximately 80% of content marketing's influence
operates through internal processing rather than direct persuasion with customer engagement identified as
the dominant pathway driving purchase behaviour.
The engagement dominant mediation pattern represents the study's most significant contribution,
highlighting that Generation Z consumers experience purchase decisions as emerging organically from
participatory social experiences rather than through deliber ate calculated evaluations. This shift from
information processing to participation based decision making carries important implications for consumer
behaviour theory and marketing practice. From theoretical perspectives, these findings highlight the need
for frameworks incorporating experiential consumption, community participation, and identity expression
as central influences. From practical standpoint, optimizing engagement, balancing multiple value creation
mechanisms, and developing platform specific strategies emerge as critical success factors for brands
targeting Generation Z on TikTok.
Comparative research on Millennial and Generation X consumers indicates that perceived value
and review consultation exerting relatively stronger effects than engagement, suggesting instrumental
information seeking rather than experiential participation (Nuzula & Wahyudi, 2022; Huo & Filieri, 2025).
Generation Z's digital nativity, characterized by lifelong exposure to participatory social media
environments. It fundamentally reshapes how marketing influences purchase decisions that shifting from
information transmission to community participation and identity expression (Tang et al., 2025). The

Indonesian context, this effect is further nuanced as this emerging market with rapid social media adoption
and a youthful population demonstrates particularly strong peer influence sensitivity, potentially amplifying
the social mechanisms highlighted in this study.
The findings provide several practical implications for marketing strategy such as brands targeting
Generation Z on TikTok should prioritize engagement driven content strategies over purely informational
approaches. It adopts a multiple pathways approach that simultaneously builds perceived value, stimulates
authentic reviews, and fosters active participation, and invest in community building initiatives that
emphasize authenticity over production quality. Performance measurement frameworks should accordingly
be updated to capture engagement depth and user generated content quality rather than relying solely on
reach-based metrics.
Several limitations constrain generalization. The cross -sectional design limits causal inference
beyond what the correlational SOR structure supports longitudinal or experimental designs would
strengthen causal claims. The single brand focus on Skintific r estricts applicability to other beauty brands
and product categories. Self-reported measures introduce social desirability concerns. And the model does
not account for potentially important moderating factors, including individual differences in digital me dia
habits, specific content characteristics, or platform algorithm dynamics. Future research could address these
limitations through multiple brand comparative studies, cross cultural validation, and behavioural data
integration that combines self-reported psychological measures with actual purchase records and platform
engagement analytics. The latter would be particularly valuable for testing whether the engagement
dominant mediation pattern replicates when behavioural indicators are used, or whether it is partially
inflated by self -report bias. Longitudinal designs would illuminate how the three mediating mechanisms
develop over extended brand exposure rather than capturing them at a single point in time.
Taken together, the present findings advance understanding of how short form video content
marketing operates on TikTok by empirically validating a comprehensive three pathway mediation model
within the SOR framework. More broadly, they highlight through t he engagement dominant result that
Generation Z consumers require a genuinely different theoretical frame, one whose purchase decisions are
embedded in participation and community rather than in evaluation and deliberate choice.
