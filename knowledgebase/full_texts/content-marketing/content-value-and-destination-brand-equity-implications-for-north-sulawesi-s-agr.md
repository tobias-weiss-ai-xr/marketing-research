Abstract. Social media enables tourism micro, small, and medium
enterprises to function not only as service providers but also as
local content producers that shape destination perceptions. This
study examined whether content marketing value strengthens
destination brand equity directly and indirectly through social
media engagement and electronic word of mouth (e-WOM), while
considering its applied relevance for local agrifood and agro -
tourism promotion in North Sulawesi. A quantitative cross -
sectional survey was conducted from June to August 2026 among
audiences exposed to tourism enterprise social media content. Of
350 survey invitations, 285 responses were received and 230 valid
cases remained after eligibility and data-quality screening. Partial
least squares structural equation modeling was performed using
SmartPLS 4.0.9. Content marketing value positively affected
destination brand equity ( β = 0.25, p < 0.01), social media
engagement ( β = 0.55, p < 0.001), and e -WOM (β = 0.40, p <
0.001). Engagement positively a ffected destination brand equity
(β = 0.30, p < 0.01) and e -WOM (β = 0.45, p < 0.001), while e -
WOM positively affected destination brand equity ( β = 0.35, p <
0.001). Both mediation paths and the sequential engagement-to-e-
WOM pathway were significant. The  findings show that valuable
local tourism content builds destination equity more effectively
when it stimulates interaction and peer recommendation, with
practical implications for integrating local food, agrifood
products, and agro -tourism experiences in to destination
communication.

Keywords: content marketing value; destination brand equity;
electronic word of mouth; local agrifood; social media
engagement.

INTRODUCTION
Digital platforms have transformed
destination communication from a
predominantly organization -controlled
activity into a distributed process
involving visitors, communities, local
enterprises, and other place -based actors.
Social media enables these actors to create
and circulate representations of places in
real time, thereby influencing destination
awareness, image, perceived quality, and
loyalty. Reviews of destination -branding
research show that social media
increasingly connects user -generated
content, cognitive and affective responses,
and behavioral outcomes (Tran & Rudolf,
2022). Empirical evidence also
demonstrates that destination -generated
and tourist -generated communication
contributes to destination brand equity,
while integrated and interactive digital
communication remains strategically
important for building and maintaining
that equity (Stojanovic et al., 2022; Qi et
al., 2024). In rural tourism contexts, social
media affordances can shape cognitive
and affective destination images by
enabling interaction, social presence, and
digitally mediated engagement with place
representations (Liu et al., 2024).

This transformation is particularly
relevant to local -resource economies
because micro, small, and medium
enterprises can simultaneously function as
commercial actors and communicators of
the places in which they operate. Digital
marketing is increasingly embedded in
agricultural and agrifood value chains.
Han et al. (2022) show that product
suitability influences farmers' adoption of
social media marketing, indicating that the
characteristics of local products matter
when digital channels are used to reach
markets. In Indonesia, agricultural
retailers are also combining physical
stores with social media, marketplaces,
and other digital channels as part of
broader omnichannel transformation
(Kawet et al., 2026a). These developments
suggest that local enterprises can use
digital communication not only to
promote individual products and services
but also to connect those offerings with
wider place-based value propositions.
The relationship between local
resources and tourism creates an
additional pathway for this digital
transformation. Agro -tourism, agro -
educational tourism, horticultural
attractions, culinary experiences, and
local-product tourism can convert
agricultural resources into visitor
experiences while creating economic
opportunities for local enterprises. Recent
applied research demonstrates t hat agro -
educational tourism can generate
measurable social and economic value for
stakeholders (Razak et al., 2025). In North
Sulawesi, online information has been
used to evaluate agritourism and
horticultural destinations in Tomohon,
showing that digital inform ation is
becoming relevant to destination selection
in resource -based tourism (Tindi et al.,
2026). Local food commodities also
represent an important component of
regional agribusiness potential, and
analysis of production and marketing
dynamics in Tomoho n highlights
opportunities to strengthen the market
visibility of locally produced foods
(Kawet et al., 2026b).
Tourists do not evaluate destinations
solely through scenery or formal
attractions. Local food, agricultural
landscapes, culinary products, traditions,
and locally produced goods can contribute
to perceptions of destination
distinctiveness and experiential  value.
Evidence from Indonesian consumers
indicates that health, emotional,
interaction, and social -media-related
values of local cuisine can contribute to
food destination image and behavioral
responses (Alexandra & Choirisa , 2024).
A recent review similarly shows that
social media is increasingly intertwined
with memorable tourism experiences
involving local food, particularly through
visually oriented platforms such as
Instagram and TikTok (Sartika et al.,
2025). Tourists' evaluations of food
quality, novelty, and healthiness can also
encourage positive travel -experience
sharing through social media (Soonsan  et
al., 2025). These findings support the view
that local culinary and agrifood resources
can become part of a broader digital
destination narrative.
Within this environment, the
effectiveness of tourism enterprise
communication depends not only on
posting frequency but also on the value
audiences perceive in the content. Content
marketing value refers to the extent to
which digital content is perceived  as
informative, attractive, credible, relevant,
enjoyable, and sufficiently useful to
justify attention or interaction. Tourism
content can provide practical information,
represent local resources, generate mental
imagery, stimulate affective responses,
and encourage redistribution. Abbasi et al.
(2023) found that the perceived value of
destination-related social media images
can stimulate consumer engagement and
e-WOM through a transmittal process.
Characteristics of destination -related
Instagram posts al so influence
engagement (Blanco-Moreno et al., 2024),

Destination communication has been

contemporary destination -image research

formation (Wang et al., 2023). Research
on rural travel likewise shows that social

travel intention toward rural destinations
(Azhar et al., 2023), and recent evidence
confirms a mediating role for e -WOM in
linking social -media-induced destination
image with travel intention (Shekhar et al.,

Despite this growing literature, three
gaps remain. First, destination social
media research has concentrated primarily
on destination marketing organizations,
tourists, influencers, or users in general,
while tourism micro, small, and medium
enterprises r emain comparatively
underexamined as producers of
destination-related content. Second,
content marketing value has been
associated with firm -level brand
outcomes, but its extension to destination
brand equity requires further empirical
testing. Third, enga gement and e -WOM
are often examined separately even
though they may operate sequentially as
audiences first interact with content and
then redistribute recommendations. This
gap is particularly relevant in Indonesian
MSME research, where much of the firm-
side digital -marketing literature has
emphasized adoption and platform use
rather than the mechanisms through which
digital content generates higher -order
branding outcomes (Tindi & Silaban,
2025).
North Sulawesi provides an
appropriate context for examining these
mechanisms because tourism promotion is
strongly connected with local enterprises
and place-based resources. The empirical
setting includes audiences exposed to
tourism enterprise content associated with
Batu Angus and other areas in Manado,
Bitung, Tomohon, and North
Minahasa/Likupang. The digital
environment is strongly visual, and local
enterprise posts can represent not only
attractions but also culinary products,
locally produced foods, and other place -
specific offerings. This creates an
opportunity to examine destination -brand
formation while also identifying practical
implications for local agrifood and agro -
tourism promotion without redefining the
empirical population as an agricultural
sample.
Accordingly, this study examines
the relationships among content marketing
value, social media engagement, e-WOM,
and destination brand equity among
audiences exposed to tourism micro,
small, and medium enterprise social media
content in North Sulawesi. It  tests the
direct effects of content marketing value
on destination brand equity, social media
engagement, and e -WOM; the effects of
engagement on e -WOM and destination
brand equity; and the effect of e-WOM on
destination brand equity. It further

MATERIAL AND METHODS
Study Design and Context
This study used a quantitative cross-
sectional survey to examine relationships
among four reflective latent constructs:
content marketing value (CMV), social
media engagement (SME), electronic
word of mouth (e-WOM), and destination
brand equity (DBE). The field survey was
conducted from 1 June to 31 August 2026.
The research context was digital
promotion by tourism micro, small, and
medium enterprises in North Sulawesi,
particularly content associated with Batu
Angus in Bitung and related tourism areas.
Respondents represented Manado,
Bitung/Batu Angus, Tomohon, and North
Minahasa/Likupang. The unit of analysis
was the individual audience member
exposed to tourism enterprise social media
content. The agricultural relevance of the
study was treated as an applied
implication of local -resource
communication, particularly where
tourism content incorporated culinary
products, local foods, or other place-based
resources.
Population, Eligibility, and Sampling
The target population consisted of
tourists and prospective tourists aged at
least 17 years who had encountered social
media content produced by tourism micro,
small, and medium enterprises in North
Sulawesi during the previous six months
and who recognize d Batu Angus or a
comparable destination in North Sulawesi.
Purposive sampling was used, supported
by online network -based dissemination
through tourism communities and social
media groups. A total of 350 survey
invitations were distributed through
Google Forms. The survey yielded 285
initial responses (81.4% of invitations).
Eligibility screening removed 30
responses: 15 respondents were younger
than 17 years and 15 had not been exposed
to relevant tourism enterprise content
during the preceding six months . Data -
quality screening removed a further 25
responses, comprising 10 duplicate
IP/email responses and 15 straight -lining
responses. The final analytical sample
therefore consisted of 230 valid
respondents.

contextualized to tourism enterprise
content and destinations in North
Sulawesi. A pilot test involving 30
respondents was conducted from 15 to 20
May 2026. Two SME i tems were
linguistically revised to improve clarity
and naturalness, while no item was
deleted.

Conceptual Model and Hypotheses
The structural model positioned
CMV as the exogenous construct, SME
and e-WOM as mediating constructs, and
DBE as the endogenous construct. Six
direct relationships were tested: CMV ->
DBE (H1), CMV -> SME (H2), SME ->
DBE (H3), CMV -> e -WOM (H4), e -
WOM -> DBE (H5), and SME -> e-WOM
(H6). Two specific indirect effects were
tested: CMV -> SME -> DBE (H7) and
CMV -> e-WOM -> DBE (H8). Because
SME was also modeled  as an antecedent
of e -WOM, the sequential indirect
pathway CMV -> SME -> e -WOM ->
DBE was evaluated as an exploratory
extension.

Data Collection and Quality Control
Data collection was conducted
online through tourism communities and
social media networks relevant to North
Sulawesi tourism. Screening and cleaning
were completed before model estimation.
The procedure assessed age and content -
exposure eligibility, dupli cate
submissions, and straight -lining.
Responses that failed the predefined
eligibility or quality criteria were
excluded. The analytical dataset was
prepared without personal identifiers.
Data Analysis
Partial least squares structural
equation modeling  (PLS-SEM) was
performed in SmartPLS  4.0.9. PLS-SEM
was selected because the study
emphasized prediction, simultaneous
estimation of multiple latent -variable
relationships, and mediation analysis
(Hair et al., 2022; Hair & Alamer, 2022).
The analysis proceeded through
respondent and construct descriptives,
reflective measurement -model
assessment, and structural -model
assessment. For the measurement model,
outer loadings above 0.708, Cronbach's
alpha and composite reliability above
0.70, average variance extracted (A VE)
above 0.50, and heterotrait-monotrait
(HTMT) ratios below 0.85 were used as
quality criteria. The structural model was
assessed using inner variance inflation
factor (VIF), adjusted R², effect size f²,
predictive relevance using Q²/PLSpredict,
direct effects, and specific indirect effects.
Hypothesis testing used 5,000 bootstrap
subsamples, two-tailed tests, α = 0.05, and
bias-corrected and accelerated confidence
intervals. Full -collinearity VIF was also
examined as a diagnostic for common -
method bias.

Ethical Considerations
Participation was voluntary. Digital

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Respondent Characteristics and Digital
Exposure
The final analytical sample
consisted of 230 respondents distributed
across Manado (80; 34.8%), Bitung/Batu
Angus (70; 30.4%), Tomohon (40;
17.4%), and North Minahasa/Likupang
(40; 17.4%). Respondents aged 17 -25
years represented 45% of the sample,
those aged 26 -35 years represented 35%,
and respondents older than 35 years
represented 20%. Women accounted for
52% and men for 48%. Sixty-five percent
had previously visited Batu Angus, while
35% were prospective visitors. Half of the
respondents travelled one to two times per
year and the other half travelled more than
twice per year.
Digital exposure was concentrated
on visually oriented platforms. Instagram
was the main source of exposure to
tourism enterprise content (60%),
followed by TikTok (30%) and Facebook
(10%). Short-form videos, including Reels
and TikTok posts featuring natu ral
attractions and culinary offerings, were
the most frequently viewed content type
(55%), followed by feed/story
photographs (30%) and reviews or vlogs
(15%). Field observations indicated that
short videos combining Batu Angus
scenery with recommendation s for local
culinary products generated the strongest
'tag-a-friend' interaction pattern. This
pattern is consistent with evidence that
visual characteristics influence
engagement with destination posts and
that short tourism videos can stimulate
destination-related e -WOM (Blanco -
Moreno et al., 2024; Yin et al., 2024).

Structural Model and Hypothesis
Testing
The structural model showed no
problematic multicollinearity, with inner
VIF values ranging from 1.4 to 2.5. The
model explained meaningful proportions
of variance in the endogenous constructs:
adjusted R² was 0.45 for SME, 0.52 for e-
WOM, and 0.61 for DBE . Effect sizes
ranged from f² = 0.15 to 0.35, indicating
medium to large effects, while
Q²/PLSpredict values ranged from 0.25 to
0.40 and were consistently above zero,
demonstrating positive predictive
relevance.
All six direct hypotheses were
supported. CMV had a positive effect on
DBE (H1: β = 0.25, p < 0.01), indicating
that audiences who perceived tourism
enterprise content as more valuable also
evaluated the destination brand more
positively. CMV had its stron gest direct
effect on SME (H2: β = 0.55, p < 0.001),
while SME positively affected DBE (H3:
β = 0.30, p < 0.01). CMV also positively
affected e -WOM (H4: β = 0.40, p <
0.001), e-WOM positively affected DBE
(H5: β = 0.35, p < 0.001), and SME
positively affected e-WOM (H6: β = 0.45,
p < 0.001).

Mediating and Sequential Effects
Both proposed mediation
mechanisms were significant. The indirect
effect of CMV on DBE through SME (H7)
was significant (p < 0.01), indicating that
valuable content contributes to destination
brand equity partly by activating audience
engagement. The indirect effect through e-
WOM (H8) was also significant (p <
0.01), showing that valuable content can
strengthen destination equity by
stimulating recommendation and peer -to-
peer communication. Because the direct
CMV -> DBE effect remained significant,
the patt ern is consistent with partial
mediation.
The exploratory sequential pathway
CMV -> SME -> e -WOM -> DBE was
also significant (p < 0.01). This result
positions engagement and e -WOM as
linked stages in a digital communication
process. Audiences may initially respond
to valuable content by paying att ention,
liking, saving, or commenting; these
responses can then increase the likelihood
of tagging, recommending, or sharing,
which broadens the social visibility of
destination-related information and
contributes to destination brand equity.

Discussion
Content Value as a Destination-Level
Value-Creation Mechanism
The positive CMV -> DBE
relationship demonstrates that audiences
do not evaluate local -enterprise
communication entirely separately from
the destination represented in that
communication. When tourism enterprise
content is perceived as useful, credible,
attractive, and interaction -worthy, part of
that positive evaluation appears to transfer
to the destination brand. This extends the
firm-level evidence of Kolibu et al. (2025)
by showing that content marketing value
can contribute to a geographically broader
branding outcome. The result also
complements Qi et al. (2024), who
emphasize the importance of interactive
and consistent digital communication for
destination brand equity.
This mechanism is particularly
relevant to destinations where local
enterprises are highly visible online.
Tourism enterprises do not communicate
only their own products; they also display
scenery, food culture, routes, visitor
activities, and recognizable  place cues.
Their content can therefore operate as
distributed destination communication.
This interpretation is consistent with
research showing that social media
communication contributes to destination-
brand outcomes (Stojanovic et al., 2022;
Abbasi et al., 2024) and with rural-tourism
evidence that interactive platform
affordances can influence destination
image formation (Liu et al., 2024).
Engagement and e-WOM as Relational
Conversion Mechanisms
The strongest direct coefficient was
CMV -> SME (β = 0.55), suggesting that
audience engagement is particularly
sensitive to the perceived value of the
content itself. The finding aligns with
Abbasi et al. (2023), who show that
perceived value in destinatio n-related
social media images can stimulate
engagement, and with Blanco -Moreno et
al. (2024), who demonstrate that
destination-post characteristics influence
interaction. In the North Sulawesi context,
the dominance of Instagram and short -
form video means that tourism enterprises
compete for attention in highly visual and
rapidly consumed environments. Content
must therefore offer both information and
a reason for users to stop, watch, react,
save, comment, or share.
SME also had a positive direct effect
on DBE (β = 0.30). Active interaction can
increase the cognitive and affective
salience of destination -related
information, reinforcing recognition,
imagery, and perceived quality. The e -
WOM relationships deepen this
interpretation. CMV positively affected e-
WOM (β = 0.40), SME positively affected
e-WOM ( β = 0.45), and e -WOM
positively affected DBE ( β = 0.35).
Recommendation is therefore not only a
direct response to content quality; it can
also emerge after engagement.  This
process corresponds with Hervás-Cortina
et al. (2024), Abbasi et al. (2023), and
recent work showing that e -WOM
mediates the relationship between social -
media-induced destination image and
travel intention (Shekhar et al., 2026).
The observed 'tag-a-friend' behavior
provides a concrete local example of this
mechanism. Short videos that combined
Batu Angus scenery with local culinary
recommendations generated the strongest
tagging pattern. Tagging transforms a one-
to-one content exposure into socially
distributed communication and resembles
the sharing process identified by Soonsan
et al. (2025), who show that positive local-
food evaluations can stimulate social -
media sharing. It also supports the broader
argument that local food and social media
can jointly contribute to memorable
destination experiences (Sartika et al.,
2025).

Applied Relevance for Local Agrifood
and Agro-Tourism Promotion
The findings have practical
relevance beyond general tourism
promotion because local -resource
products can be embedded in destination
communication. This does not imply that
the empirical sample represents farmers or
agro-tourists; rather, the results show a
communication mechanism that can be
applied by agrifood enterprises and agro -
tourism operators when they participate in
destination ecosystems. The digital -
transformation evidence of Kawet  et al.
(2026a) indicates that agricultural and
agrifood retailers increasingly combine
physical and digital channels, while Han
et al. (2022) show that agricultural product
characteristics influence social media
marketing adoption. These studies suggest
that content design and channel use are
increasingly relevant to how agricultural
products reach audiences.
North Sulawesi also offers a clear
place-based linkage between tourism and
local resources. Kawet  et al. (2026b)
identify marketing opportunities for local
food commodities in Tomohon, while
Tindi et al. (2026) show that digital
information can support the evaluation of
agritourism and horticultural destinations
in the same city. Razak et al. (2025)
further demonstrate the measurable value
that agro -educational tourism can create
for stakeholders. When these findings are
considered alongside the present study, a
practical implication emerges: local
agrifood products and agro -tourism
experiences may gain visibility when they
are communicated as part of a coherent
destination story rather than as isolated
products.
The local -food literature reinforces
this implication. Alexandra and Choirisa
(2024) show that social media interaction
and consumption values contribute to food
destination image among Indonesian
consumers. Thus, content that combines a
recognizable place with local culinary
products, farm -based experiences,
horticultural attractions, or food -
production stories can potentially generate
both product visibility and destination
value. The present study's significant
CMV -> SME -> e -WOM -> DBE
pathway indicates why such integratio n
matters: valuable content must first attract
and invol ve audiences, then encourage
interpersonal redistribution, before
broader brand value is strengthened.
Managerial Implications
Tourism enterprises, local food
businesses, agro -tourism operators, and
destination stakeholders in North
Sulawesi should prioritize content that
combines functional information with
vivid place representation. Short videos
can integrate natural scenery, lo cal
culinary products, production stories,
visitor activities, routes, and recognizable
location cues. Content should be designed
to invite interaction through save -worthy
information, questions, tagging prompts,
and clear reasons to share. e-WOM should
also be facilitated deliberately by
encouraging visitors to mention
companions, share itineraries, post
experience-based recommendations, and
connect local products with the wider
destination narrative.
For agrifood and agro -tourism
promotion, cross-promotion is likely to be
more useful than isolated product posting.
A culinary enterprise can link a local food
product with a nearby attraction; an agro -
tourism operator can combine
horticultural activities with nearby
culinary offerings; and destination
stakeholders can coordinate thematic
content around local food, farms,
landscapes, and visitor experiences. Such
integration increases the likelihood tha t
enterprise-level communication
contributes to destination -level value
while simultaneously expanding the
visibility of local resources.

Limitations and Future Research
Several limitations should be recognized.
The cross -sectional design identifies
structural associations but does not establish
temporal causality. The sample was obtained
through purposive online recruitment and
concentrated in selected North Sulawesi
areas, which limits statistical generalization to
all tourists. The study measured perceived
content value and self -reported digital
responses rather than platform -level
behavioral traces. It also did not sample
exclusively from identified agrifood
enterprises, farm-based tourism businesses,
or agricultural producers; therefore, the
agricultural implications should be
interpreted as applied extensions of the
observed communication m echanism rather
than as direct evidence of agricultural -sector
behavior.
Future research can strengthen the
agroecotechnology relevance by sampling
audiences of identified agrifood
microenterprises, farm -based tourism
businesses, horticultural destinations, and
local food producers. Survey data can be
linked with social media analytics, online
reviews, sales, visitation, or prod uct-
demand indicators. Multi -group analysis
could compare visitors and prospective
visitors, platforms, content types, or
locations. Longitudinal and experimental
designs would also help establish how
repeated exposure to local -resource
content contributes  to destination equity
and whether destination -brand effects
translate into measurable market
outcomes for local agricultural products.
CONCLUSION
This study demonstrates that
tourism micro, small, and medium
enterprise content can contribute to
destination brand equity when audiences
perceive the content as valuable and when
that value is converted into social
interaction and peer communication.
Content marketing value had significant
positive effects on destination brand
equity, social media engagement, and e -
WOM. Engagement strengthened both
destination brand equity and e -WOM,
while e -WOM strengthened destination
brand equity. Both proposed medi ation
paths were significant, and the sequential
pathway from content marketing value
through engagement and e -WOM to
destination brand equity was also
significant. The model explained 61% of
the adjusted variance in destination brand
equity and showed positi ve predictive
relevance. For North Sulawesi, the
findings indicate that visually compelling,
useful, credible, and interaction -oriented
content can support destination branding
while also providing a practical
communication pathway for local food,
agrifood products, and agro -tourism
experiences. The most defensible strategy
is to integrate these local resources int o
coherent destination narratives that invite
audiences to engage, tag, recommend, and
share.
