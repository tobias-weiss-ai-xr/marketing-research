 ABSTRACT
 In the era of digital transformation, Digital Content Marketing (DCM) has become a key strategy for increasing B2B customer engagement. Telkomsel Enterprise uses
various digital platforms to distribute educational content, product promotions, and
case studies supporting the customer journey. However, challenges remain in ensuring
that content effectively aligns with customer journey stages to maximize engagement
and business outcomes. This research aims to analyze the effect of content –customer
journey stag e fit on content engagement and firm engagement, while testing the
mediating role of content engagement and the moderating effects of social media
usage frequency and organizational position. This research employed a quantitative
approach using scenario-based online experiments. Data were collected from 420 B2B
customers of Telkomsel Enterprise who had been exposed to the company's digital
marketing content. Analysis was performed using Partial Least Squares Structural
Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM) to examine  direct, mediating, and moderating
relationships. Results show that perceived content –journey fit significantly increases
content engagement, which fully mediates its effect on firm engagement. Social media
usage frequency strengthens the impact of content–journey fit on content engagement,
while higher organizational position weakens the effect of content engagement on firm
engagement.

INTRODUCTION
Business-to-business (B2B) companies increasingly adopt digital content marketing
(DCM) to build customer engagement along the customer journey  (Hidayat et al., 2025) .
Telkomsel Enterprise, Indonesia’s leading telecom operator for B2B solutions, has
implemented DCM strategies since 2021 using channels such as LinkedIn, Facebook,
Instagram, and Twitter (X) to deliver educational content, product promotions, and case studies.
Despite these efforts, the effectiveness of Telkomsel Enterprise’s DCM remains limited.
Report data (Q4 2024) shows high total reach exceeding 300,000 impressions in several
campaigns, but with low conversion rates on key platforms (Choi & Lee, 2020). For example,
LinkedIn recorded an engagement rate (CTR) a s low as 2.01%, while Twitter (X) had only
0.09%. Although Facebook and Instagram achieved higher click numbers, conversion from
engagement to meaningful business interactions remains suboptimal (Kim & Park, 2021).
Moreover, monthly trends in click -to-reach rate vary significantly, from as low as 2.35% in
September to 37.70% in November 2024, highlighting inconsistencies in content appeal and
audience targeting (Jang et al., 2021). This suggests that while awareness and traffic increase
through digital channels, alignment between content types and customer journey stages is often
lacking, reducing effectiveness in driving customer actions such as proposals, negotiations, or
closed sales (Meyer et al., 2022; Wang & Lee, 2020). Understanding customer behavior and
optimizing content strategy can improve conversion rates and engagement in digital marketing
(Zhang et al., 2020; Chung & Lee, 2021).
Prior research by Salonen et al. (2024) emphasizes that perceived fit between content
and customer journey stages is critical to achieving engagement. Content that is misaligned
with B2B buyer stages may fail to generate meaningful interest or facilitate d ecision-making.

Previous research by Järvinen & Taiminen (2016) has also shown that marketing automation
and personalized content according to the stages of the journey can significantly increase
engagement in the context of B2B. This is especially important given that ov er 70% of B2B
customers conduct independent online research before engaging directly with sales teams.
Given these challenges, it becomes crucial for Telkomsel Enterprise to ensure that its
DCM strategy does more than simply push products; it must deliver relevant, high-value content
tailored to different stages of the customer journey. The study aims to in vestigate whether
perceived content –journey fit significantly affects content engagement, and whether such
engagement can, in turn, drive firm-level business interactions (firm engagement).
Furthermore, the research explores moderating effects of social media usage frequency,
which may amplify engagement, and organizational position, which could influence decision -
maker responsiveness to marketing content. By clarifying these relationships, t his study
provides insights to improve the strategic effectiveness of DCM in B2B contexts.
Despite the growing adoption of Digital Content Marketing (DCM) strategies in B2B
markets, there is still limited empirical evidence on how content effectiveness depends on its
fit with the customer journey stages, especially in emerging markets like Indon esia. While
Telkomsel Enterprise has invested in delivering various types of digital content (educational,
product-related, and use-case content) via multiple platforms (LinkedIn, Facebook, Instagram,
and X/Twitter), internal reports show that engagement r ates remain relatively low on key
channels (e.g., 2.01% CTR on LinkedIn, 0.09% on Twitter).
This suggests a potential mismatch between the content delivered and the customer's
information needs at different stages of the buying journey (Salonen et al., 2024). Prior research
emphasizes that content must be timely and tailored to customer journey stages to be effective
(Hollebeek & Macky, 2019; Cortez et al., 2023). Misalignment can result in reduced
engagement and diminished impact on downstream outcomes such as firm -level engagement
or sales interactions (Brodie et al., 2013).
Moreover, most existing studies have focused on overall engagement metrics without
testing whether content engagement mediates the relationship between content fit and firm -
level outcomes (Salonen et al., 2024). Although mediation models have been widely applied in
B2C contexts, for example, brand equity effects on loyalty mediated by customer satisfaction
(Aspari Sugiati et al., 2023), such mediated processes remain underexplored in B2B digital
content marketing. This limits understanding of the specific p rocess through which content
effectiveness is realized. Customer characteristics also add complexity to this process. For
example, buyers with high social media use may have stronger preferences and greater ease in
evaluating digital content (Dwivedi et al ., 2021), while organizational position can influence
how decision-makers interpret and respond to marketing content (Diba et al., 2019). These
moderating effects have rarely been examined systematically in B2B content marketing
research. Addressing these gaps is especially relevant for Telkomsel Enterprise, which operates
in a B2B context with long, multi-stage buying processes. Aligning content with the customer
journey can help improve customer engagement metrics and ultimately drive firm -level
interactions such as proposals, negotiations, and conversions. Based on these considerations,
the study proposes the following hypotheses:
H1. Perceived fit between content and the customer journey stage positively affects content
engagement.
H2. Content engagement positively affects firm engagement, i.e., the intention to conduct
further business interaction with the firm.
H3. Content engagement mediates the relationship between perceived content –journey fit and
firm engagement.

METHOD
Research Design
 This research employed a scenario -based survey design to examine the relationships
between perceived content–journey-stage fit, content engagement, and firm engagement in a
B2B context. An online survey approach was chosen due to its high practicality and efficiency
in reaching B2B decision-makers across multiple organizations (cf. Aguinis & Bradley, 2014).
The research was conducted among customers of Telkomsel Enterpr ise in Indonesia who had
been exposed to Telkomsel’s digital marketing content.
 The scenario was designed to reflect real B2B purchasing situations, where respondents
evaluated different types of digital content (educational, product-related, and use-case content)
in the context of different customer journey stages ( pre-purchase, purchase, post-purchase).
This approach increases realism and external validity by mimicking the actual decision-making
environment in which B2B buyers encounter Telkomsel's digital content.
Measures
 The survey instrument measured the key constructs in the research model:
1. Content engagement was operationalized as the respondent’s intention to interact with
the content, measured through items adapted from prior social media marketing research

(e.g., likelihood of liking, commenting, or sharing). Respondents rated on a 1 –7 scale
their likelihood of engaging with the presented content.
2. Firm engagement  was assessed with a single item measuring the likelihood that, if
contacted by a Telkomsel Enterprise representative, the respondent would agree to
discuss solutions or arrange a business meeting (1 = extremely unlikely, 7 = extremely
likely). This item reflected the firm-level business outcome relevant in B2B sales.
3. Perceived content–journey-stage fit was the key independent variable. Respondents were
asked to evaluate whether the presented content type matched their expectations and
needs at the given customer journey stage. This perceived fit was measured on a Likert
scale (1 = extremely unlikely, 7 = extremely likely). Content types included educational
content, product-related content, and use-case content.
4. Moderating variables included social media usage and job position. Social media usage
was measured through agreement with statements such as "Do you often use social
media to find information related to business needs?" (0 = no, 1 = yes). Job position was
measured by asking respondents to indicate their hierarchical level in the organization
(coded as 1/2/3 for top, middle, or lower management level).

Data Collection and Sample
 Data were collected using an online questionnaire distributed via Telkomsel Enterprise’s
B2B customer database and targeted outreach to customers who had previously interacted with
Telkomsel's digital content. The sampling targeted B2B decision -makers across industries in
Indonesia, ensuring respondents were relevant to the study’s context. A total of 420 usable
responses were collected after cleaning for missing data and quality checks. The sample
included de cision-makers, managers, and professionals from diverse sectors such as
telecommunications, IT, services, and manufacturing. Screening questions ensured that
respondents had experience evaluating B2B digital marketing content  and were involved in
purchasing decisions for their company. Demographic data indicated a broad representation
across organizational levels, with respondents including top-level management (e.g., directors,
owners), mid -level management (e.g., department heads), and first -level management (e.g.,
supervisors, team leads).

Data Analysis
 The data were analyzed using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-
SEM) with SmartPLS 4.0. PLS -SEM was chosen due to its suitability for complex models
involving mediation and moderation and its ability to handle non -normal data distribut ions
(Hair et al., 2022). Measurement model assessments included tests for reliability, convergent
validity, and discriminant validity using standard criteria (e.g., Cronbach’s alpha, Composite
Reliability, AVE, HTMT). The structural model was then evaluat ed to test the hypothesized
relationships among perceived content–journey-stage fit , content engagement , and firm
engagement, including mediation by content engagement  and moderation by social media
usage and job position. Indirect effects were calculated using bootstrapping procedures with
5000 subsamples to assess mediation significance.

The structural model analysis produced strong empirical support for all hypothesized
relationships (Table 4). First, the perceived fit between content and the customer journey stage
(ContFit) had a large, positive, and highly significant effect on content engagement (ContEng)
(β = 0.861, t = 17.48, p < 0.001). This result supports H1, confirming that content perceived as
well-matched to the customer's journey stage leads to higher engagement levels. Second,
content engagement (ContEng) itself showed a subst antial positive effect on firm -level
engagement (FirmEng) (β = 0.697, t = 13.671, p < 0.001), supporting H2. This indicates that

higher customer interaction with content can translate into a greater willingness to engage in
business interactions with the firm. Third, mediation analysis demonstrated that content
engagement fully mediated the relationship between content fit and firm engagement. The
indirect effect of ContFit on FirmEng through ContEng was significant (β = 0.600, t = 12.841,
p < 0.001), supporting H3. This finding suggests that aligning content to the customer journey
indirectly drives firm -level engagement by increasi ng content engagement first. Regarding
moderating effects, social media usage frequency (SMU1) significantly moderated the
relationship between content fit and content engagement (β = 0.127, t = 3.38, p = 0.001),
supporting H4. The positive coefficient ind icates that the impact of content fit on content
engagement is stronger for users with higher social media use. Finally, the customer's
organizational position (POS1) significantly moderated the effect of content engagement on
firm engagement (β = –0.118, t = 2.15, p = 0.032), supporting H5. The negative coefficient
suggests that the influence of content engagement on firm -level engagement is weaker for
customers in higher -level organizational positions. This reflects potential differences in
decision-making styles or responsibilities across organizational levels

CONCLUSION
This study confirms that perceived content–customer journey -stage fit  significantly
enhances content engagement, which fully mediates its impact on firm-level engagement among
Telkomsel Enterprise’s B2B customers. The results highlight that aligning digital content with
specific buying stages is essential for driving meaningful engagement and supporting business
outcomes. Additionally, social media usage  positively moderates the effect of content fit on
engagement, suggesting the value of segmenting strateg ies based on digital behavior, while
higher organizational position  reduces the impact of content engagement  on firm -level
decisions. These findings validate Salonen et al.'s model in the Indonesian B2B
telecommunications context, emphasizing the need for personalized, journey -aligned content
marketing approaches in emerging markets. Future research should explore real -world
behavioral data, cross-industry comparisons, and qualitative insights to deepen understanding
of B2B customer engagement with digital content.
