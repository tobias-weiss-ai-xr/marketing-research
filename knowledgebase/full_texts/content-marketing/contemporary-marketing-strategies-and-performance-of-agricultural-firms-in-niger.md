Abstract
This study, titled the effect of contemporary marketing strategies on the performance of
agricultural firms in Benue State, Nigeria , adopted a quantitative research design to assess how
social media marketing (SMM), relationship marketing (RLM), and content marketing (CTM)
influence firm performance. The population comprised agricultural firms in Benue State, and a
purposively selected sample of 101 respondents was surveyed. Descriptive results showed that
46.5% of the firms were small, 30.7% medium, and 22.8% large. On social m edia marketing
adoption, 55.4% were at a medium le vel, 25.7% high, and 18.8% low. Regression analysis
revealed that SMM (β = 2.396, p < 0.001), RLM (β = 1.652, p < 0.001), and CTM (β = 1.745, p
< 0.001) all had positive and statistically significant effects on firm performance, with social
media marketing exerting the strongest influence. The model was robust, with R² = 0.512, adjusted
R² = 0.496, and F -statistic = 33.859 (p < 0.001). Diagnostic checks confirmed the validity of
results, with no multicollinearity (VIF values close to 1), no heteroscedasticity (LM = 5.347, p =
0.148), normally distributed residuals (JB = 0.283, p = 0.868), and no autocorrelation (Durbin -
Watson = 1.766). The study concludes that contemporary marketing strategies significantly drive
firm performance in Benue State’s agricultural sector. It recommends that firms invest more in
social media marketing to enhance visibility, strengthen relationship marketing to  improve
customer loyalty, and use content marketing to effectively communicate product value. The
findings provide empirical evidence that modern marketing  tools explain over 51% of variations
in firm performance, underscoring their role in promoting competitiveness and sustainability in
Nigeria’s agricultural value chain.

Keywords: Agricultural firms, Social media marketing, Relationship marketing, Content
marketing, Firm performance

Introduction
The significance of the agricultural sector in developed and certain developing economies,
including Nigeria, is widely recognized. Since independence, policymakers have relied on the
agricultural segment to meet national food needs, supply raw materials for manufacturing, generate
employment and income, and contribute substantially to foreign exchange earnings (Adegbuyi,
2011). Since 1962, Nigeria's national development plans have all recognized the potential of
efficient planning to boost economic growth and solve structural flaws that impede development
(Otokiti, 2007).

over time by the creation of additional national research institutes and universities of agriculture.

Since the 1990s, transaction marketing (TM) has drawn a lot of attention in developing nations
due to the rise in customer demand for business exchanges and the resulting intensification of
competitive problems ((Inuwa and Ali, 2024). ). TM operations have the market -driven ability to
produce increased value that surpasses customers' expectations and the firm's offering. Strong
customer ties have apparently been shown to provide a substantial viable benefit and to be
responsive to competitors' direct invitations (Dinis, 2006).

Estimating the future potential of retaining customers, growing market share  and other metrics
remains a complex and imprecise process. Current metrics in marketing productivity primarily
emphasis on former customer behavior and current marketing expenditures, which fail to
accurately assess the input of Transaction Marketing (TM) to firm performance ( (Inuwa and Ali,
2024).). Consequently, there has been criticism of this approach, prompting a reevaluation of
marketing structures and strategies due to TM's perceived unable to show substantial impacts on
firm performance.

In recent times, there has been an increasing focus in research on improving the ability to measure,
predict, and accurately assess marketing performance using integrated technology and marketing
(TM) strategies. Traditionally, studies on marketing productivity have emphasized efficiently and
effectively managing costs, particularly in relation to TM's customer-centered approach. This shift
reflects an increasing desire to understand how TM variables influence customer behavior and
ultimately enhance firm p erformance (Abdelghafar, 2024) . Moreover, the integration of
information technologies (IT) in agro-marketing has been a focus of investigation. IT applications
are reshaping buyer -seller interactions in the field of agriculture , potentially leading to trends
toward monopolization in database administration (Abdelghafar, 2024). This shift has prompted
organizations to develop IT -based marketing strategies, leveraging powerful databases to gain
competitive advantages despite challenges related to human, techn ical, and organizational
complexities. Conceptually, IT is seen as pivotal in introducing innovative models of customer
retention and profitability relationships. Empirical studies highlight IT's positive impact on
organizational performance through amplified sales volume and market share, though its effects
on customer acquisition and retention capacities remain less documented, particularly at the
sectorial level rather than nationally.

2.0. LITERATURE REVIEW
Conceptual Frame work
Contemporary Marketing Strategy
Contemporary marketing strategies have evolved significantly with the advent of digital
technologies, globalization, and changing consumer behaviors. These strategies encompass a
range of approaches, including digital marketing, relationship marketing, soc ial media
engagement, content marketing, and data -driven decision -making. The application of these
strategies has become essential for firms across various sectors, including agriculture, to remain
competitive in the modern market landscape  (Inuwa and Ali,  2024). Agricultural marketing in
Nigeria has traditionally been characterized by a reliance on informal channels, limited access to
markets, and minimal value addition. However, with the increasing recognition of agriculture as a
critical sector for economic development, there has been a shift towards more structured and
strategic approaches to marketing agricultural products. The role of marketing in agriculture
extends beyond merely selling products; it involves creating value, building brand identity, and
establishing long-term relationships with buyers (Inuwa and Ali, 2024)
Digital marketing has emerged as a powerful tool for agricultural firms to reach broader markets,
engage with customers, and promote their products effectively. Studies have shown that digital

Theoretical Frame work
Dynamic Capability Theory
The Dynamic Capabilities Theory was developed by Teece, Pisano, and Shuen in their seminal
1997 paper, "Dynamic Capabilities and Strategic Management." The theory emphasizes a firm’s
ability to integrate, build, and reconfigure internal and external competences to address rapidly
changing environments. It extends the Resource -Based View (RBV) by focusing on the
capabilities that enable firms to achieve new forms of competitive advantage in dynamic markets
(Abdelghafar, 2024). In the context of your literatu re review on agricultural firms in Nigeria, the
Dynamic Capabilities Theory is relevant because it highlights the importance of continuous
adaptation and innovation in marketing strategies. Agricultural firms need to be able to adapt their
strategies to th e changing market conditions, technological advancements, and consumer
behaviors to maintain and enhance their performance. The theory suggests that firms with strong
dynamic capabilities are better positioned to leverage contemporary marketing strategies,
overcome challenges, and achieve long-term success in the competitive agricultural market.

METHODOLOGY
Study Area
Benue State, located in the North-Central region of Nigeria, is widely regarded as the “Food Basket
of the Nation” due to its rich agricultural potential and vast arable land. The state is bordered by
Nasarawa, Taraba, Kogi, Enugu, Ebonyi, and Cross River States, as well as the Republic of
Cameroon to the southeast. Agriculture is the backbone of its economy, with crops such as yam,
rice, maize, cassava, and citrus produced in large quantities. Benue also hosts numerous
agricultural firms, making it a strategic area for studies on agribusiness performance (Ocholi et al.,
2018).

Population of the Study
Benue State, located in the North-Central geopolitical zone of Nigeria, is popularly referred to as
the “Food Basket of the Nation” due to its rich agricultural resources and vast arable land.
According to the 2006 National Population Census, the state had a population of about 4.2 million
people, and recent projections by the National Population Commission and international
development agencies estimate the figure at over 6 million in 2025. The state is predominantly
rural, with farming as the major occupa tion, though urban centers such as Makurdi, Otukpo, and
Gboko are growing rapidly. Benue is ethnically diverse, with the Tiv forming the majority,
followed by the Idoma, Igede, and other minority groups. This large and heterogeneous population
provides both opportunities and challenges in terms of agricultural development, market access,
and policy planning.

Sample and Sampling Technique
The choice of 101 purposively sampled respondents is justified as it ensures the inclusion of
participants with relevant knowledge and experience required for the study, thereby enhancing the
quality of information obtained. Given the focus on agricultural  firms in Benue State, purposive
sampling allows the researcher to deliberately select individuals directly involved in marketing
and firm operations. Although the sample size is modest, it is adequate for capturing key insights,
generating reliable analysis, and aligning with time, cost, and logistical constraints.

Data Collection
For this study, a variety of data gathering techniques were used, including in -person interviews,
questionnaires, phone calls, mail orders, and online resources. At the firm level, a well-structured
questionnaire was used for the respondents. To gather data from participants, copies of the
questionnaire were distributed as part of the survey process, which helped achieve consistent
responses. Additionally, to identify appropriate contacts and confirm addresses for the pilot
project, the researc her conducted  face-to-face interviews with employees from targeted
companies. Advance letters were sent to each identified employee beforehand. Two weeks after
mailing the advance letters, thirty copies of the questionnaire were provided to two selected
establishments for our study.

Discussion of Result
The current study’s findings on the significant role of social media marketing in enhancing the
performance of agricultural firms align with the conclusions of scholars such as Shashidhara and
Chandramma (2018) and Abdelghafar Ahmed (2024), who emphasized the transformative
potential of digital and e-marketing in driving brand awareness and firm growth. Both prior studies
and the present research converge on the argument that digital platforms are no longer optional but
essential tools for competitiveness in modern markets. However, unlike earlier works that largely
focused on global firms or general organizations, the novelty of this study lies in its focus on
agricultural firms in Benue State, offering localized insights into how social media marketing
affects performance in an agrarian economy.

Relationship marketing was also found to have a strong and positive effect on firm performance,
which resonates with the findings of Coviello and Brodie (2001) and Adegbuyi (2011), who
demonstrated the importance of relational practices in business -to-business and agricultural
contexts, respectively. These studies confirmed that trust -building, loyalty programs, and long -
term customer engagement strategies are vital in sustaining firm performance. A divergence,
however, arises in terms of application: while  earlier research often emphasized network and
database marketing as the core relationship -building mechanisms, the current study highlights

5.0 CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS
Conclusion
This study examined the effect of contemporary marketing strategies on the performance of
agricultural firms in Benue State, Nigeria, with emphasis on social media marketing, relationship
marketing, and content marketing. The results showed that all three strategies had positive and
significant effects on firm performance. This finding highlights the importance of adopting
innovative marketing practices to enhance growth, competitiveness, and sustainability in the
agricultural sector, which has often been characterized by traditional and less effective marketing
approaches in Nigeria. In conclusion, the study establishes that contemporary marketing strategies
are essential for agricultural firms aiming to improve performance and remain competitive in an
evolving market environment. Thus, demonstrating the effectiveness of digital engagement,
customer relationship building, and content -driven communication, the research underscores the
need for agricultural firms to shift from conventional marketing to more mo dern, technology -
driven practices. This provides a great perspective for agribusiness managers, practitioners, and
policymakers working to strengthen the agricultural value chain in Nigeria.

Recommendations
i. Agricultural firms in Benue State should invest more in social media marketing to improve
visibility, customer engagement, and brand awareness, as this channel has shown significant
potential in driving performance.
