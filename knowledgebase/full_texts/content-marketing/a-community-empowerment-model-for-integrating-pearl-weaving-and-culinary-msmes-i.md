Abstract: The Senggigi tourism area possesses considerable potential for creative economy
development through pearl artisans, traditional wea ving artisans, and local culinary MSMEs.
However, these businesses continue to face fragmented marketing practices, inconsistent
destination branding, limited digital storytelling capabilities, and weak collaboration among
business sectors, reducing their contribution to the sustainable tourism value chain. This
community service program aimed to strengthen the competitiveness and collaborative capacity
of 36 artisan MSMEs in Senggigi through participatory capacity building, destination branding,
digital st orytelling, mentoring, and collaborative marketing. The program employed a
Participatory Action Research approach involving needs assessment, capacity building,
mentoring and co -creation, and evaluation and reflection. The results indicate improved
participant awareness of destination branding, increased ability to develop product narratives
and digital promotional content, and stronger opportunities for cross -promotion and product
bundling. The program also produced a Community Empowerment Model integratin g
community-based tourism, destination branding, digital storytelling, collaborative marketing,
and a sustainable tourism value chain.

INTRODUCTION
Tourism has become one of the most important drivers of regional economic development,
particularly in developing countries where local communities rely heavily on tourism -related
activities for employment, entrepreneurship, and income generation. Beyond its contribution to
economic growth, contempor ary tourism emphasizes sustainability by integrating economic
prosperity, environmental conservation, and social inclusion. Within this context, Micro, Small,
and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) play a fundamental role because they provide tourism
products and services that reflect local culture, strengthen destination competitiveness, and
distribute tourism benefits directly to local communities (UN Tourism, 2024; OECD, 2020).
The growing emphasis on sustainable tourism has shifted attention from attracting visitors
to creating integrated tourism experiences that generate value for both tourists and host
communities. Such experiences are increasingly supported by local creative industries,
including handicrafts, traditional textiles, culinary products, and cultu ral performances, which
contribute to destination authenticity and differentiation (Richards, 2021; UN Tourism, 2024;
OECD, 2020) Consequently, strengthening local MSMEs is not only an economic strategy but
also an essential component of preserving cultura l heritage while enhancing the sustainability
of tourism destinations (UN Tourism, 2024; Scheyvens & Biddulph, 2018)
Senggigi, located in West Lombok Regency, West Nusa Tenggara Province, is one of
Indonesia's prominent coastal tourism destinations, attrac ting both domestic and international
visitors. The area possesses abundant cultural and creative resources, particularly pearl
handicrafts, traditional weaving products, and local culinary specialties that represent the
identity of the Sasak community. The se artisan MSMEs have considerable potential to enrich
tourists' experiences while contributing to local economic development. Nevertheless, many
businesses continue to operate independently with limited collaboration, resulting in
fragmented product promo tion and underutilization of the destination's collective branding
potential (West Nusa Tenggara Provincial Tourism Office, 2024; Briedenhann & Wickens,
2004)
Despite the quality and uniqueness of local products, several challenges constrain the
competitiveness of artisan MSMEs in Senggigi. Many business owners have limited
capabilities in destination branding, digital storytelling, product presentation, and digital
marketing. Promotional activities are generally conducted individually without integrated
narratives that connect different local products into a unified tourism experience. Furthermore,
collaboration among pearl artisans, weaving artisans, and culinary producers remains relatively
weak, reducing opportunities for cross -promotion, product bundlin g, and collective market
expansion. These limitations hinder the integration of local creative products into Senggigi's
sustainable tourism value chain (Sigala, 2018; Hays, Page, & Buhalis, 2013)
Previous community empowerment initiatives have primarily fo cused on improving
technical production skills, financial management, or general entrepreneurship training.
Although these interventions have contributed to business development, they often overlook
the importance of collaborative branding, destination-based marketing, and digital storytelling
as strategic tools for enhancing tourism competitiveness. Likewise, studies on community -
based tourism have emphasized community participation and local economic empowerment,

To address these challenges, this community service program implemented a participatory
empowerment approach involving pearl artisans, traditional weaving artisans, and culinary
MSMEs in the Senggigi tourism area. The program combined capacity building, collaborative
branding, digital storytelling, mentoring, and evaluation within a Participatory Action Research
(PAR) framework. By emphasizing collaboration rather than individual business development,
the program encouraged participants to co -create destination narratives that strengthen
Senggigi's tourism identity while increasing the visibility and marketability of local products.
The novelty of the proposed Community Empowerment Model lies in its integration of five
complementary perspectives —Community-Based Tourism, Destination Branding, Digital
Storytelling, Collaborative Marketing, and Sustainable Tourism Value Chain —into a single
participatory framework for artisan MSMEs. Unlike previous empowerment programs that
primarily emphasize technical training or individual business development, the proposed model
positions artisan MSMEs as interconnected actors within a destination ecosystem, enabling
collective branding, integrated marketing, and collaborative value creation to strengthen
sustainable tourism competitiveness.
Therefore, this article aims to describe the implementation and ou tcomes of a community
service program entitled "Integrating Pearl, Weaving, and Culinary MSMEs into Senggigi's
Sustainable Tourism Value Chain."  In addition to reporting the implementation process, the
article proposes a Community Empowerment Model that can serve as a practical reference for
universities, local governments, tourism organizations, and development agencies seeking to
strengthen artisan MSMEs while promoting sustainable tourism development in other cultural
tourism destinations.

METHODS
Research Design
This community service program employed a Participatory Action Research (PAR)
approach, which emphasizes the active involvement of community members throughout the
planning, implementation, evaluation, and reflection stages. PAR was selected because it
enables researchers and community partners to collaboratively identify local challenges,
develop practical solutions, implement intervention programs, and evaluate the outcomes.
Unlike conventional training programs, PAR encourages cont inuous interaction between
facilitators and participants, ensuring that the intervention addresses the actual needs of local
MSMEs while promoting sustainable community empowerment.
The program adopted a capacity -building model that integrated Community -Based
Tourism (CBT), destination branding, digital storytelling, collaborative marketing, and
sustainable tourism value chain concepts into a comprehensive community empowerment
framework. This integrated approach enabled participants to collaboratively iden tify local
challenges, co -create marketing strategies, and strengthen the competitiveness of artisan
MSMEs within the Senggigi tourism destination.

Research Location and Duration
The community service program was conducted in the Senggigi tourism area, We st
Lombok Regency, West Nusa Tenggara Province, Indonesia, on 15 July 2026 as part of the
International Conference on Sustainable Economics, Management, and Accounting (ICSEMA)
2026 Community Service Program. Senggigi was selected as the implementation sit e because
it is one of Indonesia's leading coastal tourism destinations, supported by diverse creative
MSMEs specializing in pearl jewelry, traditional weaving, and local culinary products. These

Participants
The participants of this community service program consisted of 36 local Micro, Small,
and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) operating within the Senggigi tourism area. The participants
represented three creative industry sectors closely associated with tourism activities, namely
eight pearl artisans (22.2%), seven traditional weaving artisans (19.5%), and twenty -one
culinary producers (58.3%).
Participants were selected using purposive sampling throug h coordination with the
community service committee and local stakeholders. The selection criteria included: (1)
actively operating a tourism-related MSME in the Senggigi area; (2) producing authentic local
products; (3) being willing to participate throug hout the empowerment program; and (4)
demonstrating commitment to implementing the knowledge and skills acquired during the
mentoring activities.
The diversity of participants enabled the program to encourage collaboration among
complementary tourism-supporting industries and facilitated the development of an integrated
destination-based marketing ecosystem.

Community Service Stages
The program was implemented through four interrelated stages over the course of the
community service activities.

Stage 1. Needs Assessment
The initial stage focused on identifying the participants' existing business conditions
through direct field observations, informal interviews, and interactive discussions. Particular
attention was given to business branding, product packaging, marketing practices, utilization of
digital media, and opportunities for collaboration among pearl, weaving, and culinary MSMEs.

Stage 2. Capacity Building
Based on the identified needs, participants attended an interactive workshop covering:
Destination branding, Digital storytelling, Product value proposition, Smartphone product
photography, Social media content development, Collaborative marketing, and Sustainable
tourism value chain. The workshop combined conceptual presentations with practical exercises
using participants' own products.

Stage 3. Mentoring and Co-Creation
Following the training session, participants received direct mentoring to improve their
marketing capabilities. Activities included developing product narratives, refining brandi ng
elements, enhancing promotional content, and encouraging collaborative marketing initiatives
among the three MSME groups.
Participants also discussed opportunities for cross -promotion and product bundling to
strengthen Senggigi's tourism identity.

Stage 4. Evaluation and Reflection
The final stage evaluated participants' learning outcomes and gathered feedback regarding
the effectiveness of the empowerment activities. Reflection sessions were conducted
collaboratively to identify achievements, remaining challenges, and future opportunities for
sustaining collaborative marketing practices beyond the program.

The initial assessment was conducted through field observation, informal interviews, group
discussion, and review of participants’ existing promotional media. The assessment indicated
that the three MSME groups possessed distinctive products with strong cultural and tourism
value. P earl jewelry represented Lombok’s premium craft identity, traditional weaving
reflected local cultural heritage, and culinary products complemented tourists’ consumption and
souvenir experiences.
The findings indicate that the main constraints experienced by participating MSMEs were
associated with marketing integration rather than product quality. Similar conditions have been
reported in previous studies, which found that tourism -related MSMEs frequently possess
unique products but lack integra ted destination branding, collaborative promotion, and digital
marketing capabilities, thereby reducing their competitiveness in tourism destinations (Pike &
Page, 2014; Hays et al., 2013; OECD, 2020). This confirms that strengthening marketing
capacity is essential for improving the contribution of local enterprises to sustainable tourism
development.
Despite these strengths, the participating MSMEs faced several common marketing
challenges. Product promotion was generally undertaken independently, brand m essages were
not consistently connected to Senggigi’s destination identity, and collaboration across the three
business categories remained limited. Several participants also relied primarily on direct sales,
personal messaging applications, word -of-mouth communication, or individually managed
social media accounts.
The initial condition demonstrated that the main challenge was not product availability, but
the limited integration of these products into a coherent tourism experience. Pearl crafts, woven
products, and local snacks were marketed as separate commodities rather than as
complementary elements of Senggigi’s tourism value chain. This fragmentation reduced
opportunities for cross -promotion, joint product packaging, and the development of a shared
destination narrative.
These findings are also consistent with the concept of community -based tourism, which
emphasizes that tourism value is created not only through individual products but through
collaboration among community stakeholders who jointly crea te authentic visitor experiences
(Scheyvens, 1999; Giampiccoli & Mtapuri, 2015). Therefore, integrating pearl, weaving, and
culinary MSMEs into a unified tourism value chain represents an important strategy for
strengthening destination competitiveness.

Implementation of Collaborative Branding Activities
The intervention was implemented through participatory workshops, practical exercises,
mentoring, and co -creation activities. Rather than treating the three MSME categories as
unrelated business groups, the program positioned pearl products, traditional weaving, and
culinary products as complementary components of Senggigi’s tourism experience.

The participatory approach adopted in this program reflects the principles of Participatory
Action Research (PAR), w here researchers and community members collaboratively identify
problems, design interventions, and evaluate outcomes. Such collaborative learning processes
have been shown to increase community ownership, improve knowledge transfer, and enhance
the sustainability of empowerment initiatives (Reason & Bradbury, 2008).
The first activity introduced the concept of destination -based branding. Participants were
encouraged to identify the unique values of their products and connect them with Senggigi and
Lombok through visual identity, product descriptions, cultural narratives, and customer
experience. The activity emphasized that effective branding should communicate not only what
the product is, but also its origin, meaning, production process, and relev ance to visitors.
Destination branding extends beyond visual identity and promotional slogans. Instead, it
represents the collective image of a destination formed through cultural identity, stakeholder
collaboration, and visitor experiences (Kavaratzis & H atch, 2013). By connecting artisan
products with Senggigi's identity, participants were encouraged to position their products as
cultural experiences rather than isolated commercial goods.
The second activity focused on digital storytelling. Participants p racticed transforming
product information into short promotional narratives suitable for social media, digital
catalogues, and messaging platforms. Pearl artisans were encouraged to communicate
craftsmanship, material characteristics, authenticity, and pro duct-care information. Weaving
artisans developed narratives relating to motifs, production techniques, cultural meanings, and
the role of local artisans. Culinary producers focused on ingredients, local flavors, production
methods, serving suggestions, an d suitability as tourism souvenirs. Digital storytelling has
become an increasingly important tourism marketing strategy because authentic narratives
strengthen emotional engagement and improve tourists' perceptions of destination authenticity
(Sigala, 201 8; Ghaderi et al. (2023) Previous studies also suggest that storytelling increases
product differentiation and customer trust, particularly for cultural and creative industries.
The third activity addressed product photography and digital content developmen t.
Participants used their own products during practical sessions and learned how to improve
lighting, background selection, product arrangement, visual consistency, and caption writing.
This practical format allowed participants to apply the material dire ctly to their business
context.
The fourth activity promoted collaborative marketing. Participants explored opportunities
to combine products through cross -promotion, shared catalogues, recommendation networks,
and product bundling. Potential combinations included pearl accessories accompanied by
traditional woven packaging, curated souvenir packages, and culinary gift sets promoted
together with cultural craft products. The collaborative marketing activities introduced during
the program also support previ ous findings that partnerships among local tourism businesses
enhance destination competitiveness through resource sharing, integrated promotion, and
expanded market access (Fyall et al., 2012).
The participatory format was important because the facilitato rs did not impose a
standardized branding model. Product stories, visual elements, and collaboration ideas were
developed through dialogue with participants to ensure that the outputs remained consistent
with local identity and actual business capacity.

Improvement in Participants' Marketing Capacity
The effectiveness of the community service program was evaluated through direct
observation, participant discussions, mentoring sessions, and documentation of the outputs
produced during the activities. Rather than measuring participants' knowledge using formal pre-
test and post-test instruments, the evaluation focused on observable improvements in branding
practices, digital promotion, collaborative marketing, and participants' engagement throughout
the program.
Following the training and mentoring sessions, p articipants demonstrated improved
awareness of destination branding concepts and greater confidence in communicating the value
of their products through digital storytelling. Many participants actively developed product
narratives, improved product presentation, and explored opportunities for cross-promotion with
other artisan MSMEs. Collaborative discussions also encouraged participants to consider joint
promotional strategies that strengthen Senggigi's tourism identity. These observations indicate
that experiential learning combined with direct mentoring is more effective than classroom -
based knowledge transfer alone. Practical application enables participants to internalize
branding concepts while adapting them to their own business contexts, thereby impr oving
confidence and implementation capacity (Kolb, 1984; Zimmerman, 2000).
These observed outcomes indicate that participatory capacity building can effectively
enhance participants' marketing capabilities while fostering stronger collaboration among pearl
artisans, weaving artisans, and culinary producers. The findings further support the importance
of integrating branding, storytelling, and collaborative marketing into community
empowerment programs for tourism-related MSMEs. Similar outcomes have been documented
in community empowerment programs involving tourism MSMEs, where improvements in
branding capability, digital communication, and stakeholder collaboration contributed to
stronger destination competitiveness and increased community participation (Sari et al., 2024).

into a structured framework for strengthening sustainable tourism through collaborative
community empowerment. The proposed Community Empowerment Model extends previous
empowerment frameworks by integrating five complementary perspectives: community-based
tourism, destination branding, digital storytelling, collaborative marketing, and sustainable
tourism value chain principles. While earlier studies generally examined these concepts
separately, this model demonstrates how they can operate simultaneously within a participatory
empowerment process to strengthen tourism destinations.
The model begins with four foundational principles, namely participation, collaboration,
sustainability, and innovation . These principles emphasize that successful community
empowerment should actively involve MSMEs throughout every stage of the program,

encourage collaboration among stakeholders, promote long -term sustainability, and support

The first operational stage, Understand, focuses on identifying the existing conditions of
participating MSMEs. During this stage, facilitat ors conducted field observations, interviews,
and interactive discussions to assess participants' business characteristics, digital readiness,
branding conditions, marketing practices, and collaboration opportunities. This participatory
assessment ensured that subsequent interventions addressed actual community needs instead of
externally imposed assumptions.
The second stage, Empower, concentrates on strengthening participants' capacities
through interactive training and practical learning activities. Part icipants were introduced to
destination branding, digital storytelling, social media utilization, product photography,
packaging improvement, and sustainable tourism value chain concepts. The objective of this
stage was not merely to transfer knowledge but  to develop practical competencies directly
applicable to participants' businesses.
The third stage, Co-create, represents the central innovation of the empowerment model.
Unlike conventional training programs that focus on individual business improvement,
participants collaboratively developed destination-oriented branding strategies, cultural product
narratives, and consistent promotional messages reflecting Senggigi's tourism identity. Product
storytelling was co -created by integrating local culture, pro duction processes, and authentic
community values into digital promotional materials.
The fourth stage, Connect, emphasizes collaborative marketing among participating
MSMEs. Pearl artisans, weaving artisans, and culinary producers explored opportunities f or
joint promotion through shared digital catalogues, cross -promotion, product bundling, and
collaborative marketing campaigns. This stage strengthens the relationship among different
business sectors while expanding market exposure through collective prom otion rather than
isolated marketing efforts.
The fifth stage, Sustain, focuses on ensuring the continuity of the empowerment
outcomes. Long-term sustainability is supported through institutional networking, partnerships
with tourism stakeholders, continuo us mentoring, product quality improvement, governance
mechanisms, and coordination among participating MSMEs. This stage recognizes that
community empowerment should continue beyond the completion of a single community
service activity.
The sequence of emp owerment stages also reflects continuous capacity development
theory, whereby learning, collaboration, implementation, and institutionalization occur as
interconnected processes rather than isolated training events (Perkins & Zimmerman, 1995;
Zimmerman, 2000). Such an approach increases the likelihood that empowerment outcomes
will be sustained beyond the completion of the intervention. The model further identifies several
enabling factors  that influence successful implementation. These include supportive
government policies, academic knowledge transfer, multi -stakeholder collaboration, access to
digital technology, and business development support. These external factors strengthen the
effectiveness of each empowerment stage while facilitating long-term sustainability.

stronger business networks among participating MSMEs. These outputs represent tangible

Ultimately, the model contributes to several long-term impacts, including increased MSME
competitiveness, stronger destination branding, integrated tourism value chains, improved
community welfare, and sustainable tourism development. The integration of artisan products

CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS
Conclusion
Overall, the findings demonstrate that collaborative empowerment creates greater value
than individual capacity building because tourism products become interconnected through
shared destination narratives. This supports sustainable tourism theory, which argues that
competitiveness increasingly depends on collaboration, innovation, and  community
participation rather than individual business performance alone (UN Tourism, 2024; OECD,
2020; Richards, 2021).
This community service program demonstrated that strengthening artisan MSMEs in
tourism destinations requires a more integrated empow erment approach than conventional
technical training. Through the implementation of participatory capacity -building activities
involving 36 MSMEs—comprising eight pearl artisans, seven traditional weaving artisans, and
twenty-one culinary producers —the pro gram successfully promoted collaborative branding,
digital storytelling, and destination -oriented marketing as complementary strategies for
enhancing MSME competitiveness within the Senggigi tourism ecosystem.  The program
showed that community empowerment should extend beyond improving individual technical
skills. By integrating community-based tourism, destination branding, digital storytelling,
collaborative marketing, and sustainable tourism value chain  principles, participants were
encouraged to reposition their products not merely as individual commercial commodities but
as integral components of Senggigi's cultural tourism experience. This collaborative approach
strengthened participants' understanding of branding, improved their ability to communicate
product value through digital media, and fostered greater collaboration among different creative
industry sectors.
One of the principal contributions of this community service initiative is the development
of a Community Empowerment Model for Integrating Artisan MSMEs into a Sustainable
Tourism Value Chain. The model provides a systematic framework consisting of participatory
needs assessment, capacity building, collaborative co -creation, market integration, and
sustainability mechanisms. Unlike many exist ing empowerment programs that emphasize
isolated technical training, the proposed model highlights the importance of connecting
multiple artisan sectors through a shared destination identity and collaborative marketing
ecosystem. Consequently, the model of fers a practical reference for universities, local
governments, tourism organizations, and development agencies seeking to strengthen local
creative industries while promoting sustainable tourism development.
In conclusion, this community service program c onfirms that sustainable tourism
development depends not only on the quality of tourism attractions but also on the ability of
local communities to collaboratively create, communicate, and sustain the value of their cultural

Limitations
Nevertheless, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the program was
implemented during a one-day community service activity on 15 July 2026 , limiting the
opportunity to observe long-term behavioral and business changes among participants. Second,
the evaluation primarily measured immediate learning outcomes and collaborative o utputs
rather than long-term economic indicators such as sales growth, customer retention, or business
resilience. Third, the program involved MSMEs located only in the Senggigi tourism area;
therefore, the findings may not fully represent the diversity of  artisan communities in other
tourism destinations.

Recommendations
From a practical perspective, the program indicates that collaborative branding and digital
storytelling can become effective instruments for increasing the visibility of local products and
strengthening destination identity. The integration of pearl crafts, traditional weaving, and
culinary products into a common promotional narrative creates opportunities for cross -
promotion, product bundling, and broader market exposure while preserving  the cultural
uniqueness of local communities.
Future community service initiatives should incorporate longer mentoring periods,
continuous monitoring, and stronger institutional partnerships involving local governments,
tourism associations, universities,  and private -sector stakeholders. Further studies are also
encouraged to evaluate the long -term effectiveness of collaborative branding and digital
storytelling using measurable business indicators, including digital engagement, customer
acquisition, market expansion, and sustainable income generation.
