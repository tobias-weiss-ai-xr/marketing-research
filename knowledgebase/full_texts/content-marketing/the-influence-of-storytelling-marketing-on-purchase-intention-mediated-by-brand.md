Abstract
The coffee shop industry in Indonesia is expanding swiftly, although not all establishments can compete effectively. The Mekayo
coffee business has seen variable sales figures. This case study seeks to assist the firm in overcoming the problems posed by
intensifying competition. This study aims to investigate the role of brand image as a mediator in the relationship between narrative
marketing and purchase intention. This study employed a quantitative technique. The sampling approach employed a purposive
sampling technique. The research sample comprised 360 respondents, all of whom were followers of Coffee Mekayo on Instagram.
A survey using the Likert approach was employed for data gathering. The data analysis approach employed was SmartPLS software
version 4. The data demonstrated that narrative marketing has a substantial impact on brand image, brand image significantly
influences purchase intention, and storytelling marketing greatly influences purchase intention. Additional data suggest that brand
image somewhat mediates the impact of narrative marketing on Purchase intention. These findings demonstrate the significance of
narrative marketing in establishing brand image and fostering customer purchase intention.

Keywords:  storytelling marketing, brand image, purchase intention, coffee industry.

1. Introduction
In recent years, coffee shops in Indonesia have expanded rapidly. Experts estimate that by 2023–2024, Indonesia will
have around 10,000 coffee shops, according to the Indonesian Coffee and Chocolate Entrepreneurs Association (APKCI,
2025) Data from a report from Point of Interest (POI, 2026) states that Indonesia is the world's coffee shop king,
reaching 461,991 outlets by the end of 2025. This phenomenon demonstrates the rapid growth of the coffee shop
industry and emphasizes the strong role of coffee shops as easily accessible social spaces.
Coffee Shop Growth in West Java According to statistics publications made by the Central Statistics Agency (also
known as BPS in indonesian), the West Java provincial government reported that in 2024, the number of restaurants,
eateries, and cafes reached 43,853 units (Open Data Jabar, 2024). Coffee shop growth in West Java continues to
increase in line with lifestyle trends and the increasing public interest in local coffee and comfortable hangouts. Due to
their high population density and economic activity, major cities in West Java, such as Cirebon, have become hubs for
coffee shop expansion. The West Java Provincial Tourism and Culture Office reported that by 2024  number of
restaurants, cafes, and restaurants in the Cirebon area was recorded at 1,713 units, covering the period from 2013 to
2024 (Open Data Jabar, 2024).

interesting case study that can help this business navigate the challenges of increasingly fierce competition.

2. Literature Review
2.1. Storytelling Marketing
According to Zimand-Sheiner (2024), storytelling marketing is the activity of conveying meaningful stories that build
relationships with audiences, determined by the depth of the storys meaning, the resulting consumer engagement, and
the dissemination of the story through various communication channels. According to Aaker et al. (2012) in the journal
(Mandala & Aliyah, 2025) , storytelling marketing is a communication tool that combines various elements and uses
narrative to convey a message. In the marketing sector, storytelling stirs the audience's emotions, gives them a range of
feelings, and inspires them to take compelling actions. According to Smeda & Dakich (2012) in (aisyah et al., 2025),
storytelling marketing is a strategy for creating a brand identity through narratives that can trigger emotional reactions
and strengthen the connection between the customer and the company.
 Chang (2025) explains that storytelling marketing not only conveys information but also serves as a tool to create
trust, emotional connections, and customer imagination about a brand, ultimately shaping consumer opinions and
purchasing behavior. According to (Chang, 2025), storytelling marketing can be measured through three dimensions:
1) Credibility, which indicates the degree to which the brand narrative conveyed by the brand is believable, the
information conveyed in the story is credible, and the emotions or feelings evoked by the story are genuine. 2)
Resonance (emotional resonance): The label storyline leaves a deep impact on consumers, the label story can build
emotional bond with consumers, and the story of this brand is interpeted as creative and engaging. 3) Extensibility: The
brand story provides broad scope for consumers' imaginations, helps consumers believe the brand can meet their needs,
and increases consumers' desire to purchase the brand.
Effective storytelling marketing implementation not only serves to build emotional bonds with customers but also
Being part of this process the brand image of a coffee shop. According to research conducted (Thamrin & Santoso,
2025), storytelling marketing substantially influences brand image strengthening through narratives relevant to
consumers' lives. (Rafania et al., 2025) It has been proven that marketing through stories storytelling marketing
significantly and positively influences brand image by using easy-to-understand stories to develop narratives that
influence customer perceptions and help consumers build a positive brand image.

2.2. Brand image
Consumers’ perception of a brand is known as brand image, which is formed from their own experiences,
observations, and data they obtain about the business or product (Ramadhan et al., 2025). in consonance with Kotler
and Keller (2016) in their journal (Ellitan et al., 2022) brand image describes image buyers have of a brand, derived
from the associations they have in their minds with that brand. This image consists of a combination of buyers' emotions
about the product, as well as other indirect factors. No company can maintain a large market share if they do not have
a strong brand image (Ali & Naushad, 2023). According to Elseidi and El-Baz (2016) in the journal (Al-Fatwa et al.,
2024), the general view of a brand is formed by the brand image which is formed from knowledge and information
about the brand. A robust brand image can enhance product attachment and impact greater purchasing decisions.
Furthermore, as stated by Keller (1993) in (Chang, 2025), brand image is interpreted as a collection of combination
stored in consumers' reason about a brand, which form a comprehensive view of the brand.
Based on Keller (1993) in (Shimp, 2020), brand image is formed by three main characteristics of brand associations.
These are: 1) Strength of brand association, how strongly the brand association  firmly embedded in the memory
customers. 2) Favorability of brand association, the extent to which the brand association is viewed positively and
provides value to consumers. 3) Uniqueness of brand association, which describes the distinctive unique association
that distinguishes the brand from other brands.
Previous studies have shown that storytelling marketing contributes to the creation of a positive brand image, which
then has the function strategic role in stimulating consumer purchase intentions (Rafania et al., 2025). A good brand
image can be formed when satisfied customers share their good experiences with the brand with others.  the main
influencing factor on purchase intention is brand image by Y.H. Wang (2014) in (Liang et al., 2024).
2.3. Purchase intention
Based on Shah et al. (2012) in the journal (Joshi et al., 2022), purchase intention that is buyer's The choice to purchase
a brand for a specific reason. Perspectives instill confidence in various features and qualities a brand is trying to market.
Perspectives also reflect how customers perceive something, regardless of how much they like it. Consumer actions in
response to a product that motivates them to purchase it are called purchase intentions Kotler (2016) in (Hidayat et al.,
2023). Warshaw and Davis (1985) also state in (Shanerika Salina & Sudaryanto, 2023), that a customer's initial intention
to purchase a product is known as their purchase intention. Based on Ferdinand (2002) in (Tamba et al., 2025) purchase
intention is measured in the following manner dimensions and indicators 1) Transactional interest: The individual's
tendency to purchase an item. 2) Referential interest: A consumer's willingness to recommend a product they have
purchased to others based on their own consumption history. 3) Preferential interest: A consumer's preference for one
product over another. Only reference products can change these preferences. is damaged. 4) Explorative interest: A
person with exploratory curiosity is always seeking new information, both positive and negative, about their interests.
Anggun Wijayanti et al. (2024) proven that marketing via stories and building a good image of the business
significantly impacts the intention to buy. According to research (Sari et al., 2024), marketing storylines greatly enhance
the likelihood of prospective buyers completing a transaction. Narrative marketing, customer perception of the brand,
and consumer intent to buy all form a positive feedback loop.
By referring to the existing literature, this study formulates the following hypothesis:
H1: storytelling marketing has a positive and significant effect on brand image.
H2: brand image has a positive and significant effect on purchase intention.
H3: storytelling marketing has a positive and significant effect on purchase intention.
H4: brand image mediates the effect of storytelling marketing on purchase intention.

3. Research Methods
3.1. Type and design research
The purpose of this research was to determine the relationship between brand image, purchase intention, and narrative
marketing. A correlational quantitative methodology was used in the study. This method demonstrates how variables
relate to each other by statistically testing hypotheses. (sugiyono, 2021), quantitative research methodology, based on
positivism, aims to validate established theories.
Primary data has been collected directly from participants by sending questionnaires to Mekayo coffee shop followers
who met the research criteria. Secondary data refers collected from various sources, including scientific publications,
data reports from coffee shops, as well as related research on narrative marketing, consumer intent to buy, and
perceptions of established brands.

4.2. Discussion
4.2.1. The influence of storytelling marketing on brand image.

With a T-statistic of 16.858, a P-value of 0.000, and an initial sample value of 0.689, narrative marketing is shown
to significantly and positively impact brand image. As a result, Coffee Shop Mekayo's reputation will rise in tandem
with the caliber of its narrative marketing. On the other hand, a damaged brand image might result from a badly executed
narrative marketing. This is in line with other research that confirmed the positif and significant association between
narrative marketing and brand image (Tamba et al., 2025). Improving brand image is directly proportional to how well
it is implemented.
4.2.2. The influence of brand image on purchase intention.

The initial positive sample value of 0.352, a T-statistic of 5.863, and a P-value of 0.000 all point to a positive and
significant relationship between brand image and purchase intention. This indicates that storytelling marketing not just
enhances brand image but motivates customer to make purchases. These results confirm findings  (Komang et al., 2025),
which shows brand image can significantly and positively influence purchase intention. This implies that purchase
intention will increase in response to a positive brand image and will decrease in response to a negative brand image.
4.2.3. The influence of storytelling marketing on purchase intention.

With a positive path of 0.492 and a p-value of 0.000, narrative marketing clearly influences purchase intention in a
positive and statistically significant way T-statistic 9.495. Generally speaking, customers want to buy from businesses
that employ engaging narrative marketing strategies, according to these findings. According to research (Tamba et al.,
2025), stories in marketing and significantly positively affect consumers' propensity to buy. as proven by the fact that
compelling narratives in advertising may inspire substantial consumer spending.
4.2.4. The influence of brand image mediation between storytelling marketing and purchase intention

Based on the results of this study, brand image acts as a mediator between marketing narratives and consumers'
intent to buy. Positive path coefficient of 0.242, T-statistic of 5.698, and P-value of 0.000 all point to the substantial
influence of this mediation path. To rephrase, storytelling marketing not only has a direct impact but also strengthens
purchase intention through brand image. With a VAF of 33%, we can see that there is some mediation happening. This
suggests that Mekayo consumers' propensity to buy can be increased through enhancing brand image by using
storytelling marketing. A prior research by (Chang, 2025) reached the same conclusion. Brand image has been shown
to significantly and positively mediate the relationship. Storytelling marketing and purchase intention are partially
mediated by brand image.
5. Conclusion
It can be concluded that based on the results of the SMART-PLS analysis obtained through data collection, grouping,
processing, analysis, and interpretation, it can be concluded that storytelling marketing has a positive and significant
influence on brand image at mekayo coffe shop. This finding indicates that a narrative based marketing approach is
effective in shaping consumer perceptions of the brand. The results also show that brand image has a positive and
significant effect on purchase intention. This implies that the more favorable consumers perceptions of the brand, the
higher their likelihood of making a purchase. In addition, customer satisfaction with the brand is positively and
significantly correlated with purchase intention, where positive reviews from satisfied customers tend to encourage
purchasing behavior. Furthermore, the findings reveal that storytelling marketing dir ectly exerts a positive and
significant influence on purchase intention. This suggests a strong relationship between storytelling marketing and
purchase intention, indicating that consumers are highly influenced by narrative-driven marketing campaigns.The study
also demonstrates that brand image serves as a mediating variable in the relationship between storytelling marketing
and purchase intention. Therefore, the more effective the storytelling marketing strategy implemented by Mekayo
Coffee, the more positive the brand image formed in consumers’ minds, thereby increasing their likelihood of making
a purchase.
