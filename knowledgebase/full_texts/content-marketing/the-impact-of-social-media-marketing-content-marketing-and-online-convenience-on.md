Social Media Marketing
Over recent years, social media has grown to gain recognition as a pronounced and paramount
marketing factor guiding the success of a product/service/business (Hawkins and Vel, 2013). SMM
started to gain population in the early 2000s because of platforms like Facebook, then Instagram
and Twitter. According to (Pentina and Koh, 2012), SMM triggers viral communications amongst
consumers across online communities, brand a nd fan pages, and promotion -related content
generated by companies/organizations on popular networking sites such as Twitter, Facebook, and
many others. Social media marketing evolves around creating and sharing engaging content which
includes posts, videos, reels and advertisements that create brand awareness and loyalty which also
helps in connecting with a target audience and driving sales. (WebFX, 2021) reports that 93% of
marketers globally utilize social media for business, while ( Fatima, Khan, & Kous ar, 2024;
Statista, 2019) reports that 37.9% of survey participants claimed they have purchased after viewing
a social media advertisement.
SMM also promotes direct customer engagement, includes influencer partnerships, and enables
real-time interactions t o enhance relationships. Tools such as Facebook Ads and Instagram
Shopping help businesses reach targeted audiences, track campaign performance, and offer smooth
shopping experiences, making SMM an essential element in today’s competitive digital landscape.
(Jara et al, 2014) define SMM as a new -generation marketing tool encouraging higher attention
and participation from consumers through the use of social networks.
Accordingly, (Ananda, A. S., Hernández -García, Á. and Lamberti, L., 2016) the lite rature review
on SMM and strategy provides hints of further development in this area. At the foremost, there is
still a need for formalization and classification of industrial SMM strategies. In particular,
empirical research needs to explore how firms dev elop their SMM strategy and the implications of
SMM strategy on the firm’s marketing activities.

Online Convenience
Post-Covid-19 epidemic in Pakistan, consumer shopping habits underwent a major transformation.
The epidemic era was critical; individuals were isolated and had no options other than online
buying. Over time, individuals become increasingly comfortable with online purchasing,
prompting businesses to enhance the shopping experience for their consumers.
In recent years, online convenience has benefited several individuals in procuring groceries,
medications, culinary products, and apparel. The brands swiftly transitioned to websites to enhance
client convenience. As (Shaqman et al., 2022) mentioned, More and more consumers are turning to
Internet shopping for its convenience since they have less time to devote to shopping and more
time to pursue other interests. Over time, individuals recognized that o nline retailers offer greater
advantages than physical ones, eliminating travel time, the need to navigate around stores, and the
inconvenience of waiting in line to purchase items. They recognized that everything was as simple
as a touch on their smartpho nes. People focus on efficiently completing the shopping experience
and obtaining the product with minimum effort (Kaltcheva and Weitz, 2006). People are more
drawn towards internet convenience since everyday living is getting difficult and their professional
responsibilities are also rising. Retailers increase the value of their market offer, by saving
customers time and energy through convenience improvement (Seiders et al., 2000). Web -based
shopping removes many geographic barriers between customers and merchants, allowing new
distant merchant-customer relationships to emerge (Sylke, Belanger & Comunale, 2004).

Electronic word-of-mouth (Ewom)
Through Web -based consumer opinion platforms (e.g., epinions.com), the Internet enables
customers to share their opinions on, and experiences with, goods and servic es with a multitude of
other consumers; that is, to engage in electronic wordof -mouth (eWOM) communication (Hennig-
Thurau, T., Gwinner, K.P., Walsh, G., Gremler, D.D., 2004). The substantial growth in online
social networks has vastly expanded the potenti al impact of (e -WOM) on consumer purchasing
decisions (P. Duarte et al, 2018). A positive Wom is “the act of doing something for others without
anticipating any reward in return” and a negative Ewom is “to prevent others from experiencing
the problems they had encountered” (Sundaram, Mitra, & Webster, 1998).
Ewom refers to a form of communication that persuades the listener to either purchase or refrain
from purchasing a particular object. Electronic word of mouth manifests in several forms and
platforms, i ncluding comments on Facebook and Instagram, online and in -person reviews,
suggestions, news groups, and others. Applying the work of (Engel et al., 1993, p. 158), eWOM
communication on Web-based opinion platforms may be initiated because of a desire to help other
consumers with their buying decisions, to save others from negative experiences, or both. EWOM
has a critical role in consumer preferences and behavioural intentions (Pedersen et al., 2014; Severi
et al., 2014; Torlak et al., 2014). Electronic w ord of mouth has a significant effect on purchasing
decisions (Prasad et al., 2017).
Consumer involvement in brand -related eWOM is reflected by three sets of behaviours: Opinion
Seeking (OP), Opinion Giving (OG) and Opinion Passing (OP) (Fynn et al., 1996) . Many
individuals just visit websites comment sections or Facebook pages to learn about other people's
reviews, how they find that particular brand's products, how the quality, quantity, or if we discuss
apparel then how is the fabric? (Yuliantoro et al.,  2019) Reviews given by consumers based on
their personal experiences after using them will be more trusted by other consumers and encourage
them to make purchasing decisions. Lastly, (Nur Aisyah & Hapzi Ali, 2023), (Dian Firmansyah &
Ali, 2024), (Saputra & Ali, 2024), are some researchers who found that Ewom has a significant
influence on purchasing decisions.

Consumer Buying Decision Process
The buying decision is a complex process comprising several stages, including need recognition,
information search, appraisal of alternatives, purchase choice, and post -purchase evaluation. When
purchasing online, a buyer considers numerous factors, including what to buy and where to get it.
If a buyer is brand -loyal and possesses awareness, then all is well; nevertheless, for a new
customer, electronic word -of-mouth is the most critical aspect that might influence their
purchasing decision (Nawaz, et al., 2024; Afzal, et al., 2019).
Every digital marketing form (e.g., Social Media Marketing, Content M arketing) or online
shopping convenience affects consumer buying decisions in the modern era since after COVID- 19
everything is going digital and people are now mostly depending on online platforms rather than
physically going anywhere. Brand preference refers to the inclination of consumers to choose a
particular brand over its competitors when making purchasing decisions (Sjahruddin Dan Adif,
2024). Businesses aim to understand and influence this decision -making process by employing
effective marketing strategies, providing relevant information, building brand trust, and creating
positive customer experiences (Zhou et al., 2022). Usually, social media channels help to increase
awareness even before the sales season starts so that consumers might know abo ut their sales and
visit their businesses right away. Companies should start their marketing campaigns under these
online platforms and social media sites since customer impressions and moulding of buying
decisions depend mostly on them.

Methodology
This study employed a survey -based approach to test its hypotheses and validate the proposed
theoretical model. A structured questionnaire was designed in two sections: the first collected
demographic information (gender and age), while the secon d assessed the influence of social
media marketing, content marketing, and online convenience on consumer behavior in the fashion
apparel industry using a 5 -point Likert scale. The questionnaire included 16 items across five
dimensions: social media marketing (4 items), content marketing (3 items), online convenience (3
items), consumer buying decisions (3 items), and electronic word -of-mouth (4 items). The survey
was validated by marketing experts and academics, followed by a pilot test with 30 respo ndents to
ensure clarity and relevance. It was conducted online, targeting individuals aged 16 –25 actively
engaging with fashion apparel brands in Pakistan. After eliminating incomplete responses, 228
valid submissions were analyzed using Partial Least Squ ares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -
SEM), a method chosen for its suitability in explaining and predicting relationships within the
proposed hypotheses.

Discussion
Interpretation of Results
This study was conducted to evaluate the impact of digital marketing strategies (Social media
marketing, content marketing), online convenience on Electronic Word of mouth and consumer
buying decision process. This study attempted to evaluate the results based on a cross -sectional
sample of 228 people with an interest in buying from fashion apparel brands. In today's world,
firms are adopting digital marketing strategies and every brand has its e-commerce website. A total
of seven hypotheses are tested in this study, four of them have direct effects which include H1, H2,
H3, and H5.
Social media marketing, content marketing and online convenience have  a positive impact on the
consumer buying decision process. That means all the direct relationships are supported. People
have a positive impact on Social Media marketing, content marketing and online convenience
because companies use formulas that are cus tomized to each person's tastes. Marketing becomes
more engaging and relevant to consumers. Second, content marketing that provides personal
testimonials, informative blogs, and e -commerce sites that are always available so customers can
get their goods de livered to their door makes things easier. Online offers, discounts, and reviews
simplify decision-making.
On the other hand, electronic word of mouth is an important part of this study. H4a, H4b, and H4c
are the last three hypotheses. EWOM acts as a mediator for social media marketing and the buying
decision process of consumers. H4a is supported by increasing the probability that people will trust
their reviews. Positive electronic word-of-mouth boosts consumer trust and confidence by showing
that others like a product. Second, H4c is supported. Ewom mediates online purchasing
convenience and consumer decision -making. The convenience of online buying saves time.
Electronic word -of-mouth (EWOM) reduces product risks and increases awareness of online
platform convenience and reliability, making purchasing selections easier. It offers numerous
advantages. To send a gift, locate an item from a specific clothing brand online, add it to your cart,
complete the payment, and provide the recipient's address. A lengt hy process was streamlined
from your residence.
Last but not least, H4b is rejected, EWOM acts as a mediator for content marketing and the buying
decision process of consumers. There are many reasons for this, such as spam and paid ratings.
We've all been in situations where we bought something from a clothing brand's winter collection
and didn't like it, but an influencer or popular person on the internet telling everyone that it's the
best collection ever makes EWOM weaker. After that, there may  be things in the content that don't
directly affect buying, like not mentioning the quality of the product or not making the specs of the
items clear, etc.

Conclusion
This study looks at how social media marketing, content marketing, and online convenience have
changed over time and how that has affected people's decisions to buy. The study's goal is to look
at the roles of three platforms: social media marketing, content marketing, and online convenience
and how the fashion apparel marketing ind ustry in Pakistan can use these platforms to boost sales,
with eWOM as a mediating variable.
Recommendations for future Research
The present article acknowledges several limitations that might need to be fixed in further
research. This study did a good job  of showing how social media marketing, content marketing,
and online convenience affect people's decisions to buy, but it has some limitations that should be
thought about for future research.
One of the study's first limitations is its geographical strat egy, which included assessing a variety
of fashion apparel brands in Karachi, Pakistan. The majority of the data was gathered mostly in
Karachi only and not globally, indicating that the data was merely generalized. To compare
research done at different ti mes and to track changes in the community and member participation,
a longitudinal study can be done.
Another limitation lies in the limited number of digital marketing strategies developed (social
media marketing, content marketing) and online convenience  based on data and expert interviews.
The research clearly shows that everything is getting digital these days; additional independent
variables can be included as this is also evident. Therefore, it is difficult to make definitive
statements about the uni versal applicability and effectiveness of these strategies across most
industries. This, however, represents a potential direction for future research.
Finally, the suggested paradigm took advantage of electronic word of mouth as a mediator.
Additional mediators include emotional attachment, brand love, and brand engagement could find
a place in future models. A moderator could also be incorporated in line with brand loyalty,
platform knowledge, price sensitivity, etc.
