ABSTRACT
This inquiry provides an empirical assessment regarding how content marketing and social media
marketing predict the brand awareness of OZ Radio Bali, particularly as contemporary youth audiences
increasingly pivot toward digital alternatives. Operating within a quantitative cross -sectional explanatory
survey design, this study focused on a population consisting of active TikTok followers of OZ Radio Bali.
Utilizing Cochran's formulation alongside a convenience sampling approach, a total of 96 respondents
were selected. Primary data gathered through validated online instruments using a Likert-scale design were
subsequently evaluated via multiple linear regression analyses implemented within the SPSS environment.
The derived statistical metrics reveal that, on a partial basis, content marketing (t = 9.303; p < 0.001) and
social media marketing (t = 8.307; p < 0.001) are positively and significan tly associated with brand
awareness. When evaluated simultaneously, these joint independent predictors establish a significant
relationship with brand awareness (F = 130.114; p < 0.001). Furthermore, an R² value of 0.737
demonstrates that this specific reg ression framework successfully explains 73.7% of the total variance
observed in brand awareness, whereas the residual 26.3% stems from unobserved exogenous factors.
Ultimately, cultivating a deliberate operational harmony between relevant audio -visual prod uction and
responsive digital community interactions remains paramount to preserving institutional visibility within
the current media convergence landscape.
Keywords: content marketing; social media; brand awareness; radio.

1. INTRODUCTION
People's media consumption patterns have been significantly altered by advances in digital
technology over the past five years. The significant shift from conventional media to internet -
based media and digital platforms is driven by digital transformation.  The fact that social media
networks including YouTube, WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, and TikTok have hundreds of
millions of active users in Indonesia demonstrates the shift in contemporary communication
channels. This digital disruption phenomenon is si gnificantly changing consumer behavior. The
modern world, especially Generation Z and Millennials, who are the primary audiences for radio,
have shorter attention spans and tend to reject annoying conventional advertising.
From a managerial perspective, this transition changes the function of radio from a single-
channel broadcaster into a multiplatform media brand. The on -air program is no longer the only
point at which audiences encounter the station. Short videos, presenter clips, event documentation,
playlists, podcasts, live sessions, comments, and user -generated conversations can all become
extensions of the radio experience. Consequently, brand visibility depends on the station's ability
to maintain a recognizable identity across multiple contact poin ts. A fragmented digital presence
may weaken recognition, whereas a coherent combination of editorial style, visual identity, tone of
voice, and audience interaction can reinforce the same brand association repeatedly. This situation
makes digital marketing capability increasingly central to the survival of conventional broadcasters.
To keep up with these changes in consumer behavior, companies must do more than just
display advertisements; they must engage in interactive communication with audiences to
strengthen their brand awareness. According to Dabbous and Barakat (2020), radio broadcasters
must be present in various converged formats, such as audio on air, visuals on Instagram/TikTok,
and text interactions on Twitter/X. Social media marketing strategies must be able to balance the
gap between the airwaves and the vast digital universe (Dabbous & Barakat, 2020).
Brand awareness is especially important in the radio  industry because audience choice is
made in an environment with abundant alternatives and low switching costs. Listeners can move
quickly between terrestrial radio, music -streaming applications, podcasts, video platforms, and
creator-led content without s ubstantial barriers. Under these conditions, a station that is not
immediately remembered may be excluded from the audience's consideration set even when its
programs remain relevant. Content marketing helps create reasons for audiences to notice and
remember the station, while social media marketing sustains the relationship after the initial
exposure. Their roles are related but not identical: content provides the value that attracts attention,
whereas social media interaction helps circulate that value and convert attention into familiarity.
OZ Radio Bali, one of Bali's leading youth radio stations, faces a complex challenge
stemming from external sources. Bali is a global tourism hub with a highly mobile population. As
a result, its audience is highly ex posed to the global digital information flow. OZ Radio Bali
competes not only with other local radio stations but also with global streaming platform
algorithms that continuously deliver personalized content. OZ Radio Bali needs to increase its
visibility through innovative social media content to maintain top-of-mind brand awareness among
local audiences, as physical radio usage has declined among young people.
In terms of the percentage of the population aged 10 and over who listen to radio
broadcasts, Bali Province ranks highest nationwide at 23.64 percent in 2024, based on information
provided by the Central Statistics Agency or Badan Pusat Statistik (BPS). This figure demonstrates
the importance of radio as a mass communication. Conversely, GoodStats (2024) survey reveals a
significant contradiction: 48% of teenagers never listen to conventional radio at all. This is
reinforced by the statement by OZ Radio Bali's HRD management that the biggest challenge today
is competing with digital platforms that are more flexible, more operationally efficient, and attract
more advertising. To address this, the company's main focus is optimizing the synergy between
social media management and con tent marketing to maintain its presence and increase brand
awareness among the internet-based younger generation.

While previous studies have extensively explored digital marketing within the retail, e -
commerce, and fast-moving consumer goods sectors, there remains a noticeable gap regarding its
application within the conventional broadcasting industry. Specifically, there is insufficient
empirical evidence on how traditional radio stations, which are currently transitioning into visual
and digital platforms, leverage digital content to retain their audience. This study bridges that gap
by investigating OZ Radio Bali. The theoretical and empirical significance of this study lies in
extending digital marketing frameworks to the broadcasting sector, while prac tically, it provides
actionable insights for media management to sustain brand equity among digitally native
demographics.
Accordingly, the central research problem concerns whether the digital activities
conducted by OZ Radio Bali are strong enough to pre dict audience brand awareness in a
measurable manner. The study does not merely ask whether the station uses social media, but
whether the quality of its content and the relational management of its social platforms are
associated with higher recognition, recall, consideration, and consumption. The research therefore
aims to examine the partial contribution of content marketing, the partial contribution of social
media marketing, and their simultaneous predictive capacity. This focus is important because
management decisions regarding creative production, platform administration, and audience
engagement require empirical prioritization rather than assumptions based only on follower counts
or posting frequency.
2. THEORETICAL REVIEW AND HYPOTHESIS DEVELOPMENT
2.1. Social Network Theory and Hierarchy of Effects Theory
The theoretical foundation of this research rests on two main concepts: Social Network
Theory and the Hierarchy of Effects Theory. Social Network Theory provides a framework for
how informat ion dissemination is conditioned by the configuration of relationships and
interactions between actors within a social ecosystem. In this context, active engagement on digital
platforms expands the reach of information and fosters deep community bonds. Concurrently, the
Hierarchy of Effects Theory views consumer cognition as a sequential process, moving from initial
exposure to brand recognition and eventual loyalty. By integrating these theories, this study posits
that well -planned messages (content) distr ibuted through interactive networks (social media)
systematically build the cognitive phase of brand awareness.
Social Network Theory explains the circulation mechanism through which a message can
move beyond the station's direct followers. Digital audienc es are connected through visible and
invisible networks of friendship, interest, location, entertainment preference, and platform
recommendation. When users comment, share, save, mention, or recreate a piece of content, they
act as nodes that extend the me ssage to other parts of the network. The value of social media
marketing therefore lies not only in publishing information but also in activating interaction that
increases the probability of repeated exposure. In parallel, the Hierarchy of Effects Theory explains
the cognitive consequence of that exposure. Audiences must first notice the message, connect it
with OZ Radio Bali, and store the association before stronger attitudes or behavioral responses
can emerge.
2.2. Construct Differentiation and Hypotheses
To avoid conceptual overlap, this study distinctly defines its predictor variables. Content
marketing is operationally defined as the creation and distribution of relevant, informative, and
visually engaging materials (e.g., storytelling, visual quality, and  entertainment value). Conversely,
social media marketing focuses on the relational aspect, specifically interactivity, administrator
responsiveness, participation, and online community engagement.
Within this study, content marketing emphasizes the substa ntive and creative
characteristics of what is communicated. Content creation concerns the ability to produce material
that is relevant to the audience and consistent with the station's identity. Content sharing reflects

the ease with which information can be distributed and reused across platforms. Connecting
represents the capacity of content to relate the brand to audience interests, while community
building indicates that recurring content can provide a shared reference for listeners. These
dimensions make content marketing more than promotional copy; it becomes an editorial process
that translates the atmosphere of radio into formats suitable for visual and interactive media.
Social media marketing, by contrast, emphasizes how the platform relationship is managed.
Convenience concerns whether audiences can easily access information and interact with the
account. Trust develops when the station provides accurate, consistent, and credible
communication. Information quality reflects the usefulness and clarit y of posts, while
responsiveness and interactivity demonstrate that the account functions as a two -way
communication channel rather than a static notice board. This distinction is analytically necessary
because an organization may produce attractive content but fail to respond to audiences, or it may
be highly responsive while offering content that is repetitive and weak. Each condition can produce
a different effect on brand awareness.
Brand awareness is treated as a cognitive outcome that ranges from basi c recognition to
active recall and consideration. Recognition occurs when audiences can identify the brand after
seeing its name, logo, presenters, visual style, or other cues. Recall is stronger because audiences
can retrieve the brand from memory without  an immediate prompt. Consideration indicates that
the brand enters the set of media options audiences may choose, while consumption represents
the practical expression of that awareness through listening, viewing, or following content.
Following Keller and Swaminathan  (2020), these layers are important because awareness is not
limited to knowing that a brand exists; it also concerns the accessibility and strength of the brand
in audience memory.
Based on the synthesis of the aforementioned theories and previous empirical findings, the
following hypotheses are proposed:
H1: Content marketing significantly predicts the brand awareness of OZ Radio Bali.
H2: Social media marketing significantly predicts the brand awareness of OZ Radio Bali.
H3: Content marketing and social media marketing simultaneously predict the brand awaren ess
of OZ Radio Bali.

4. RESEARCH METHOD

This study employed a quantitative cross -sectional explanatory survey design. The object
of this study is OZ Radio Bali. The population consisted of active followers on the official TikTok
account of OZ Radio Bali, operationally defined as users who actively engage with, view, or follow
the account during the data collection period (May to June 2026).
The unit of analysis was the individual TikTok follower who had a reasonable opportunity
to encounter OZ Radio Bali's digital communication. The cross -sectional design captured
respondent perceptions at one point in time and was appropriate for estimating statistical
associations among the constructs. Although this design cannot demonstrate long -term causal
change, it enables the study to assess whether variations in perceived content quality and social
media management correspond with variations in brand awareness within the selected audience.
The explanatory orientation also supports the use of hypothesis testing because the relationships
were specified theoretically before the empirical analysis was conducted.
A convenience sampling technique was utilized. While this non-probability approach poses
limitations to broad generalizability, it was se lected due to the highly dynamic, anonymous, and
fluctuating nature of social media user populations, making a strict sampling frame difficult to
establish. Using Cochran's formula (Z = 1.96, p = 0.5, q = 0.5, e = 10%), a sample size of 96
respondents was determined. To prevent duplicate responses, the online instrument required
unique email sign-ins.
The use of convenience sampling was accompanied by procedural controls intended to
improve data quality. Respondents were approached within the relevant digit al audience rather
than from the general population, and unique email sign -ins were required to reduce duplicate
participation. The Cochran calculation provided a transparent minimum sample basis under the
selected precision level. Nevertheless, the sample represents accessible followers who were willing
to participate, so the findings should be interpreted as evidence concerning the surveyed OZ Radio
Bali TikTok audience rather than as a definitive estimate for every radio listener in Bali.
Primary data were gathered through a structured, self -administered questionnaire using a
five-point Likert scale (1 = strongly disagree to 5 = strongly agree). The operationalization of the
variables is presented in Table 1 below, derived from established literature and adapted for the
broadcasting context.
Questionnaire items were adapted to the broadcasting context so that respondents could
evaluate concrete digital experiences rather than abstract marketing con cepts. The five -point
response format provided sufficient variation for statistical analysis while remaining simple for
online completion. Expert review was used to assess the relevance and clarity of the wording,
whereas the pilot test helped identify statements that could be misunderstood by respondents. The
final instrument therefore sought to balance theoretical consistency with contextual readability.
Participation was voluntary, and the responses were analyzed in aggregate so that the interpretation
focused on patterns across the sample rather than on the identity of individual users .

6. DISCUSSION
6.1. Content Marketing as a Predictor of Brand Awareness
The hypothesis testing results (t-test) demonstrate that the content marketing strategy is a
significant positive predictor of the Brand Awareness of OZ Radio Bali. Moving beyond the
statistical output, this significant relationship can be explained by the  psychological engagement
triggered by high -quality, relevant, and consistent audio -visual content. In the current digital

landscape, audiences particularly Generation Z and Millennials possess shorter attention spans and
are highly selective regarding the media they consume. By transitioning from traditional audio-only
broadcasts to a visually stimulating "visual radio" concept, OZ Radio Bali successfully captures
audience attention. These empirical findings support the Hierarchy of Effects Theory,
demonstrating that well -crafted content systematically guides audiences from initial exposure
(cognitive stage) to brand recognition. Furthermore, these results align with studies by Fauzi and
Sarasati (2025) and Abidin et al.  (2025), which assert that engaging, relevant, and consis tent
marketing content can effectively increase audience recall of a brand. When OZ Radio Bali utilizes
communicative captions, clear visuals, and relevant hashtags, it generates substantial cognitive
recognition, thereby strengthening the brand's position  at the top of its listeners' minds without
relying on intrusive traditional advertising.
The finding for content marketing also has an operational interpretation. A radio station
cannot simply transfer lengthy on-air material to social media and expect the same communication
effect. Digital content must be reorganized into concise, platform-appropriate units that retain the
brand's distinctive voice. For OZ Radio Bali, this may involve selecting memorable presenter
moments, translating program themes into short-form videos, maintaining consistent visual cues,
and connecting posts to local youth culture. The coefficient and significance result suggest that
investment in content planning is not merely a creative expense; it is associated with a measurable
branding outcome. Consistency remains crucial because isolated viral posts may generate
temporary reach without establishing durable recognition.
6.2. Social Media Marketing as a Predictor of Brand Awareness
The outcomes of the hypothesis testing (t -test) indicate that the brand awareness of OZ
Radio Bali is positively and significantly predicted by social media marketing efforts. This strong
association occurs because interactive features such as rapid commen t replies, interactive
Instagram stories, and two-way communication foster parasocial relationships between the radio
station and its listeners. When audiences feel heard and valued in the digital space, their emotional
connection to the brand deepens, leading to higher top-of-mind awareness. These empirical results
directly corroborate the theoretical framework of Social Network Theory proposed by Santoso
(2020), which posits that social media helps disseminate information and form close -knit online
communities. By acting as a facilitator of these digital interactions, the radio station cultivates a
loyal listener base that actively partic ipates in information sharing. Furthermore, this aligns with
the research of Ghozahdi and Suharyati (2023) and Ramdani and Fietroh (2025), stating that the
level of brand awareness increases rapidly as a result of the intensity and responsiveness of social
media marketing. Ultimately, active social media management transforms passive listeners into
active brand advocates.
The social media marketing result indicates that awareness is reinforced when audiences
experience the station as accessible and responsive. Interaction can reduce the psychological
distance between broadcaster and listener, particularly when administrators answer question s,
acknowledge comments, invite opinions, and respond to current local conversations. These
practices make the brand more socially present and create additional memory cues beyond formal
promotional messages. However, responsiveness should be managed strategically. Fast replies that
are inconsistent with the station's identity may weaken credibility, while standardized replies may
appear impersonal. The managerial challenge is therefore to combine speed, relevance, and a
recognizable tone of voice across platforms.
6.3. The Simultaneous Prediction of Brand Awareness
The simultaneous hypothesis testing results (F -test) indicate that content marketing and
social media marketing collectively establish a significant relationship with the Brand Awareness
of OZ Radio Bali, with a total variance contribution (R²) of 73.7%. T his empirical finding
highlights the critical importance of implementing Integrated Marketing Communications (IMC)
within the broadcasting sector. In practice, this means that content marketing provides the

substantive, high-quality narrative (the message), while social media marketing provides the rapid,

The simultaneous result provides evidence that the strongest digital branding strategy is
not a choice between creative content and community interaction. These capabilities reinforce one
another. High-quality content gives audiences something worth viewing and sharing, while active
social media management creates the conditions under which that content can circulate and
generate conversation. The R² value of 0.737 is substantial in this contex t and indicates that the

because 26.3% of the variance remains outside the model.
Several limitations should guide interpretation. First, the cross -sectional design measures
perceptions and awareness at a single period, so it cannot establish how brand awareness changes
over time. Second, convenience sampling limits population -level generalization and may
overrepresent followers who are already active on TikTok. Third, the model relies on self-reported
evaluations, which can be influenced by recent exposure or personal preference. Fourth, the study
focuses on two predictors and does not incorporate program quality, broadcaster popularity, event
experience, electronic word -of-mouth, advertising exposure, or platform algorithms. These
limitations do not invalidate the results, but they define the boundari es within which the findings
should be applied.
7. CONCLUSION AND SUGGESTIONS
7.1. Conclusion
By adopting visual radio concepts, OZ Radio Bali has adapted its operational focus to align
with a highly dynamic digital media environment. The station's audience profil e is heavily
dominated by Generation Z and Millennials, specifically the productive age group between 17 and
34 years old. The digitally native characteristics of this demographic group reflect a strong
alignment between the core target market and the station's digital marketing initiatives.
Statistical evaluations confirm that Content Marketing (X1) is a significant predictor of
brand awareness, as evidenced by a calculated t -statistic of 9.303 (p < 0.001). This confirms that
producing consistently relevan t, compelling, and high -quality audio -visual content is strongly
associated with audience recall and brand recognition for OZ Radio Bali.
Social Media Marketing (X2) is likewise confirmed as a significant predictor, with a
calculated t -statistic of 8.307 a nd a significance value below 0.001. This result shows that
accessibility, trust, information quality, responsiveness, and interactivity are associated with
stronger audience awareness of OZ Radio Bali. In other words, the station's digital performance
depends not only on what it publishes but also on how it manages ongoing relationships with
followers.
Simultaneously, the joint integration of content marketing campaigns and active social
media usage demonstrates a significant relationship with the brand aw areness of OZ Radio Bali
(F-count = 130.114; Sig. < 0.001). These two combined predictors account for a substantial 73.7%
of the total variance in brand awareness. Meanwhile, the remaining 26.3% is attributable to
alternative external factors outside the scope of this regression model.

7.2. Suggestion
This study offers practical recommendations for the management of OZ Radio Bali. It is
advised to consistently optimize their visual communication strategy by allocating resources
toward multi -sensory content such  as motion graphics, live streaming, video reels, and visual
podcasts to engage younger audiences effectively. Furthermore, the social media team is
encouraged to enhance online community attachment through responsive communication in the
comment sections. Marketing budgets and upload schedules should be strategically allocated to
platforms that dominate the 17 -34 age demographic, particularly Instagram, TikTok, and
YouTube.
Implementation should be supported by clear editorial guidelines that connect the station's
visual identity, language style, posting rhythm, and response standards. Management may also
conduct periodic audience reviews to identify which programs, presenters, topics, and content
formats generate the strongest recall. For future studies, l ongitudinal designs, probability -based
sampling where feasible, platform analytics, and comparisons across radio stations would provide
a more comprehensive assessment of how digital communication develops brand awareness over
time.
For future researchers, it is recommended to expand the scope of this study by examining
other exogenous variables that may account for the remaining 26.3% variance in brand awareness.
These variables could include the influence of electronic Word -of-Mouth (e -WOM), the
effectiveness of off -air outdoor event activations, broadcaster popularity, and the integration of
holistic marketing strategy evaluations, such as the 7P Marketing Mix model.
