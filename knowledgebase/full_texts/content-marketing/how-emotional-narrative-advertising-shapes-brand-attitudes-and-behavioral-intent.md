Background: Emotional narrative advertising has become increasingly prevalent
in contemporary marketing, emphasizing affective engagement and immersive
experiences rather than purely informational appeals. However, the internal
psychological mechanisms through which emotional storytelling influences
consumer attitudes and behaviors remain insufficiently explored. This study
draws upon the principles of positive psychology and the Stimulus–Organism–
Response (S–O–R) framework to investigate the impact of emotional
advertisements on consumers’ brand attitudes and behavioral intentions. The
study explores how affective and cognitive processes influence these attitudes
and intentions.
Methods: A two-stage experimental design was adopted. The pretest (N = 100)
validated the manipulation of advertising type (emotional vs. functional). The
primary experiment (N = 713) examined the hypothesized relationships by
employing PROCESS v4.1 (Model 4 and Model 6). The independent variable in
this study was advertising type; the dependent variables were brand attitude,
repurchase intention, and word-of-mouth intention; positive affect and
narrative absorption acted as mediators.
Results: The findings indicate that emotional narrative advertising has a
substantial impact on positive affect and narrative absorption, which, in turn,
play a mediating role in the relationship between ad type and brand attitude.
Additionally, narrative absorption exerts indirect effects on repurchase and
word-of-mouth intentions, thereby supporting the broaden-and-build theory
of positive emotion. The findings underscore the presence of two discrete yet
interconnected mediating pathways—affective and immersive processing—in
the context of emotional advertising effectiveness.

1 Introduction
In the contemporary communication landscape, characterized
by media fragmentation and information overload, the functional
boundaries of advertising are continually expanding. The role of
the product information website has evolved beyond its original
function as a mere conduit for disseminating product-related
information. Driven by consumers’ growing demand for
emotional connection and meaningful identification, it is
gradually evolving into a psychological mechanism that
stimulates emotions, constructs meaning, and shapes long-term
brand relationships (Escalas and Bettman, 2003; Pera and Viglia,
2016). Recent research findings indicate that interactive and
story-driven formats can enhance immersion and positive
evaluations in advertising contexts. This underscores the
strategic value of narrative design for consumer engagement (Gu
et al., 2023). Furthermore, recent research in the field of scale
development indicates that immersive storytelling practices are
conducive to fostering emotional engagement and memorable
brand experiences, thereby reinforcing the transition from a
model of “informing” to a model of “immersing” audiences
(Ahmed et al., 2024). Moreover, with the diversification of
communication channels and the increasingly differentiated
nature of consumer needs, the traditional research perspective,
focusing on “what advertising conveys,” is no longer sufficient
to explain the complex impact of advertising on individual
cognition and behavior. Consequently, academic research is
shifting its focus to “how advertising can precisely influence
individuals’ emotional responses and decision-making through
differentiated appeals.” This shift also provides more targeted
theoretical guidance for strategic optimization in
advertising practice.
In this context, the differentiation of advertising appeals is
becoming increasingly evident, gradually forming two typical
paths: functional appeal and emotional appeal. The fundamental
principle of functional appeals is to furnish consumers with an
objective foundation for decision-making by emphasizing the
product itself, conveying rational information, and developing
factual arguments. Emotional appeals transcend the product
level, stimulating the audience’s subjective emotional response
through storytelling, the penetration of emotional resonance,
and a sense of narrative absorption in sensory experience. The
two factors correspond to the two core logics of “rational
persuasion” and “emotional touch” in advertising
communication (Albers-Miller and Royne Stafford, 1999; Batra
and Ray, 1986; Stafford and Day, 1995). Furthermore, the two
types of appeals correspond closely to the dual-channel model
of consumer cognitive processing. Functional appeals focus on
the rational cognitive channel of consumers through the rigor of
logical reasoning and the credibility of the information, thereby
reducing information uncertainty in their decision-making.
Emotional appeals, in contrast, rely on the intensity of
emotional arousal and the depth of psychological immersion to
act on the perceptual cognitive channel. This activates
individuals’ emotional memories and value identification, and
promotes differentiated effects on attitudes and behavioral
responses (Mattila and Enz, 2002; Verhoef et al., 2009).
The present study employs the stimulus-organism-response
(S-O-R) framework as its overarching analytical foundation to
elucidate how external advertising stimuli elicit internal
psychological mechanisms, thereby influencing consumer
behavior (Mehrabian and Russell, 1974). The fundamental tenet
of this theoretical framework posits that external stimuli do not
directly precipitate behavioral outcomes; rather, they act through
the “organism”, a construct comprising internal affective and
cognitive states, to modulate responses. In the context of
advertising, the medium of the advertisement functions as the
primary external stimulus capable of eliciting various internal
reactions, such as emotional arousal, narrative absorption, and
information evaluation. These internal responses, in turn,
determine key behavioral outcomes, including brand attitude,
purchase intention, and word-of-mouth behavior (Donovan
et al., 1994; Jacoby, 2002). Brand attitude is defined as
consumers’ overall evaluation and emotional predisposition
toward a brand. It is a crucial psychological variable that
mediates between consumers’ brand perceptions and their
behavioral responses (Keller, 1993). According to attitude-
behavior theory, an individual’s attitude subsequently influences
their behavioral intentions and decision-making tendencies
(Heilbroner et al., 1980). In the field of advertising
communication research, brand attitude is therefore considered
a key mediating outcome variable that links advertising stimuli
to consumer behavioral responses. Studies have shown that
positive brand attitudes can enhance purchase intentions,
repurchase likelihood, and word-of-mouth behaviors (Spears
and Singh, 2004). Recent studies have extended the S-O-R
model to digital contexts, highlighting that personalized
recommendation stimuli and emotional states mediate the
relationship between advertising stimuli and purchase behavior
(Jeong et al., 2022; Nagano et al., 2023).Accordingly, the present
study focuses on two core psychological mechanisms—positive
affect and narrative absorption—that mediate the relationship
between advertising stimuli and consumer responses.
Specifically, positive affect is generally defined as the positive

emotional states experienced by an individual within a specific
context, such as pleasure, excitement, and enthusiasm (Watson

positive emotions function not merely as immediate affective
responses; rather, they also serve to broaden an individual’s
cognitive and behavioral resources, thereby further influencing
their attitude formation and behavioral decision-making
(Fredrickson, 2001). Consequently, this study posits that positive
affect functions not only as a psychological response elicited by
advertising stimuli; it also serves as a crucial psychological
mechanism that bridges the advertising experience and
consumer behavioral intentions. While the present study
concentrates on the context of beverage brand advertising, it
presents notable implications for comprehending the extensive
question of how narratives affect consumer psychology and
behavior. Specifically, this research reveals how emotional
narrative advertising enhances consumers’ receptiveness to
brand messages and shapes their attitudes through the
mechanisms of positive emotion and narrative immersion.
Positive psychology offers a pivotal theoretical framework for
examining the emotional consequences of advertising. According
to Fredrickson’s Broaden-and-Build Theory, positive emotions are
not merely transient experiences of pleasure; they expand
consumers’ cognitive breadth and attention span, thereby
facilitating the long-term accumulation of psychological capital
and social resources (Fredrickson, 2001). When applied to the
domain of advertising research, this theory suggests that the
effective stimulation of positive emotions (e.g., joy, appreciation,
or warmth) through emotional narrative advertising enhances
consumers’ cognitive flexibility and brand identification. This, in
turn, results in more stable brand attitudes and behavioral
intentions (Fredrickson, 2001; Isen, 2001). This mechanism has
been extensively supported by empirical research in advertising
settings. For instance, positive emotions have been demonstrated
to fortify brand attitudes through the “emotional resonance-
value identification” chain (Bagozzi et al., 1999). A substantial
body of research has demonstrated that emotional arousal plays
a pivotal mediating role in the retention of advertising messages
and the evaluation of brands (Micu and Plummer, 2010). In
essence, positive emotions not only enhance consumers’
immediate experience of advertising but also serve as the core
psychological bridge connecting “momentary emotional
reactions” with “long-term brand relationships.”
Narrative absorption, a fundamental psychological mechanism
that enables emotionally appealing advertising to influence
consumers, plays a pivotal role in advertising persuasion. Green
and Brock, 2000 define it as a state of profound psychological
engagement with the narrative. During this process, audiences
develop emotional empathy and cognitive focus, temporarily
weakening their rational assessment of reality and naturally
accepting the brand values and beliefs implicit in the advertising
narrative (Escalas, 2004; Van Laer et al., 2014). In
contradistinction to the rational information processing pathway
of traditional advertising, Narrative absorption relies on the
“psychological assimulation” of emotion and meaning to achieve
persuasive intent. The primary objective of this narrative
technique is to diminish the audience’s psychological defenses
through the process of immersion in the story. In the context of
advertising, Narrative absorption has been shown to facilitate a
more profound empathetic identification with the characters and
situations portrayed in a brand’s narrative. This phenomenon
not only serves to augment the persuasive efficacy of the
advertisement but also prompts consumers to integrate the
brand’s experiences and values into their personal narratives,
thereby facilitating the establishment of a long-term association
between the brand and their cognitive processes (De Graaf et al.,
2012; Escalas and Bettman, 2005). This psychological process
generally unfolds through three progressive stages: 1. Sensory
entry: Ads capture the audience’s attention through multi-
dimensional sensory cues, such as visual and auditory, laying
the foundation for entry into the story; 2. Emotional projection:
As the narrative unfolds, the audience experiences a profound
connection with the characters’ emotions and circumstances,
gradually becoming fully immersed in the story.3. Cognitive
Integration: The audience’s active internalization of the brand
meaning conveyed by the ad’s narrative constitutes a
fundamental aspect of their self-perception, thereby marking the
transition from “story understanding” to “brand identification”.
Recent narrative research further supports this progression by
showing how consumers use narrative processing to organize
experiences and translate immersion into evaluative outcomes
(Hamby and Escalas, 2024).
A substantial corpus of empirical studies has corroborated the
notion that Narrative absorption exerts a substantial impact on
brand attitudes, concomitantly fostering heightened purchase
intention. A robust correlation has been demonstrated between
heightened narrative engagement and the enhancement of brand
evaluation, which in turn fosters positive advertising attitudes
(Escalas, 2007). A subsequent meta-analysis (Van Laer et al.,
2019) revealed an average effect size of r = .45 for narrative
absorption on advertising persuasiveness. This effect was found
to be significantly moderated by story coherence and emotional
intensity. This finding indicates that narrative absorption plays a
pivotal role in consumers’ emotional processing of advertising,
serving as the psychological conduit that facilitates the
transformation of fleeting emotional responses into enduring
brand behavioral intentions. This transformation elucidates the
comprehensive psychological shift from “emotional impact” to
“behavioral intention,” underscoring the pivotal role of narrative
absorption in shaping consumer responses to advertising.
Conversely, functional appeals primarily influence rational
processing through the diagnosticity of information. When
advertisements provide clear, verifiable, and objective
information relevant to the decision, consumers perceive greater
cognitive credibility, leading to rationally driven brand attitudes
(Lutz, 1985; Purohit and Srivastava, 2001). Recent empirical
work has demonstrated that heightened perceived information
diagnosticity significantly enhances consumer trust and
subsequent brand evaluation in online review and advertising
contexts (Byun et al., 2021). Consequently, functional appeals
embody the cognitive-rational pathway, while emotional appeals
represent the emotion-al-experiential pathway. The integration
of these two approaches unveils the complementary mechanisms
of emotional and cognitive systems in the effectiveness of
advertising (Batra and Ray, 1986; Mattila and Enz, 2002).
Despite the extensive exploration of the applications of
emotional advertising, narrative communication, and the S–O–R
model within the realm of consumer behavior in existing

research, the majority of studies remain primarily focused on the
persuasive effects of advertising or the processes of cognitive
evaluation. Consequently, there remains a paucity of systematic
explanation regarding how positive psychological experiences
interact with mechanisms of narrative immersion to
subsequently influence consumers’ brand attitudes and
behavioral intentions. This discrepancy is especially pronounced
from the standpoint of positive psychology, where research
investigating the influence of positive emotions on advertising
effectiveness — specifically through the sequential psychological
pathway of “emotion-immersion” — remains
comparatively sparse.
Accordingly, the present study integrates positive psychology,
narrative communication theory and the S-O-R model in order to
construct a chain-mediation model centered on the “positive
emotion–narrative immersion” pathway. The overarching
objective of this model is to provide a more comprehensive
elucidation of the underlying mechanisms through which
emotional narrative advertising influences consumers’
psychological and behavioral responses.
The present study is predicated on the theories mentioned
above, and its logical thread progresses from “advertising type”
to “positive emotion/Narrative absorption” to “brand attitude”
to “behavioral intention (repurchase/word-of-mouth).” The
present study integrates the “build-build mechanism” of positive
psychology with the “stimulus-response chain” of the S–O–R
model to construct a systematic advertising psychology model.
The specific research objectives are as follows:
1. The objective of this study is to investigate the hypothesis
that emotional narrative advertising is more effective than
functional advertising in stimulating positive emotion and
Narrative absorption (emotional pathway).
2. The objective of this study is to examine the hypothesis that
positive emotion and Narrative absorption mediate the
relationship between advertising type and brand attitude
(the psychological mechanism pathway).
3. The objective of this study is to analyze whether Narrative
absorption exerts a significant influence on repurchase
intention and word-of-mouth intention, operating
through the medium of brand attitude (the behavioral
pathway).
1.1 Hypotheses
H1. Emotional narrative advertising has been shown to elicit
higher levels of positive emotion and Narrative absorption in
comparison to functional advertising. This suggests that
emotional information is more effective in activating positive
experiences and psychological engagement.
H2. The indirect impact of advertising on brand attitude via
positive emotion, thereby illustrating an emotional
mediation effect.
H3: The role of advertisement type in shaping brand attitude
is examined through the lens of Narrative absorption, which
serves as an indicator of immersion mediation.
H4: The impact of Narrative absorption on repurchase
intention and word-of-mouth intention is further elucidated
through the mediating role of brand attitude, thereby
establishing a chain mediation path.
The objective of this study is to elucidate the manner in which
various forms of advertising influence consumers’ emotional
experiences, brand cognition, and behavioral responses through
positive psychological mechanisms. This study presents a
theoretical framework for integrating research in advertising
psychology, providing a foundation for brand communication
practices that combine emotional and rational strategies. The

the impact of different ad appeal types on consumers’
psychological states and behavioral responses. The present study
employs the stimulus-organism-response (S-O-R) model,
proposed by Mehrabian and Russell, 1974, as its theoretical
framework. The model posits that the external stimulus (ad
type) catalyzes the activation of specific psychological
mechanisms, namely individual positive affect and Narrative

To ensure the effectiveness of manipulating advertising
materials (whether different ad appeal types accurately triggered
the target differences), a pretest was conducted before the main
experiment. A total of 100 participants were recruited for this
study (51% female, average age 27 years). The participants were
recruited through Credamo, an online survey platform that
serves as both a questionnaire distribution platform and a
participant sample provider. Credamo handles the distribution
of questionnaires and the recruitment of participants.
Participants were randomly assigned to either the functional
advertisement group or the emotional advertisement group (50
participants per group). Participants were recruited through an
online survey platform and randomly assigned to either a
functional advertising group or an emotional advertising group
(50 participants in each group). The experimental material
utilized the fictitious beverage brand “Aurora Coffee” as the
carrier. Unrelated variables such as brand logo, presentation
duration, and basic copy structure were strictly controlled, and
only the advertising appeal type was manipulated. Functional
advertising focuses on product attributes and rational
information, while emotional advertising focuses on narratives
and evoking emotions. In the subsequent phase of the
experimental procedure, each participant is randomly assigned
via an online platform to view only one specific advertisement,
thereby ensuring that the advertisement type serves as a single-
stimulus condition. This random presentation mechanism
ensures independence between groups while preventing potential
confounding effects that could arise if participants were
simultaneously exposed to both types of advertisements.
In the pre-test, the effectiveness of the advertising manipulation
was validated by requesting that participants evaluate the
advertisements after viewing them. Specifically, the constructs of
functional judgment, informational sufficiency, emotional appeal,
and content clarity were each measured using multiple-item
scales, with all items employing a 7-point Likert scale
(1 = Strongly Disagree, 7 = Strongly Agree). Among these
measures, “functional judgment” and “informational sufficiency”
were utilized to evaluate the extent to which the advertisement
conveyed objective product information. For instance, the
question posed to participants was, “Was this advertisement
sufficient?” The term “emotional appeal” was utilized to assess
whether the advertisement elicited an emotional response through
the use of emotional expression or narrative context. An example
of this would be the statement, “This advertisement moved me
through its emotional expression.” In conclusion, the concept of
‘content clarity’ was employed to assess the comprehensibility of
the advertisement’s primary message. This was operationalized as
follows: ‘I was able to clearly understand the core content
conveyed by this advertisement.’ The primary objective of this
measurement phase was to verify the distinctiveness of the two
types of advertisements along the functional and emotional
dimensions, thereby ensuring the validity of the experimental
manipulation. The specific measurement items are provided in
the Supplementary Materials S1.
Preliminary findings indicated that the functional advertising
group demonstrated significantly higher performance ratings on
the dimensions of “functionality judgment” and “information
sufficiency” compared to the emotional advertising group (p
values < 0.001, ps < 0.001). The emotional advertising group also
demonstrated a marked superiority on the dimension of
“emotional appeal” (p = 0.001). However, a non-significant
discrepancy was observed between the two groups in terms of
“content clarity” (p = 0.567). These results validated the
effectiveness of manipulating advertising appeal type and
provided a foundation for the reliability and validity of the
subsequent main experiment. The results of the preliminary
examination are displayed in Table 1.

2.2.2 Stimuli and manipulation
The experimental stimuli comprised two print advertisements
for a fictitious brand, Aurora Coffee. The product highlighted in
the brand advertisement under consideration is a caffeinated
beverage. Despite the classification of caffeinated beverages as
everyday consumer goods, extant research suggests that excessive
caffeine intake may have potential implications for sleep, general
health, and specific populations (e.g., pregnant women).
Consequently, this study may also be regarded as an exploration
into the advertising communication effects of a product category
characterized by potentially risky attributes. Participants were
permitted to view the images at their discretion while answering
the subsequent questions. The two advertisements were found to
be analogous in terms of visual layout, color tone, and brand
logo. The distinguishing factor between them was the nature of
the persuasive appeal employed in each. Specifically, one of the
appeals adopted an emotional narrative strategy, while the other
employed a functional, information-based approach. The textual
content was meticulously balanced to ensure equivalence in
length and readability, thereby controlling for potential linguistic
and cognitive biases. This methodological approach aligns with
established practices in advertising-effect research, ensuring that
the observed differences can be attributed to the manipulated
appeal type rather than extraneous design variations (Batra and
Ray, 1986; Escalas and Bettman, 2005; Zhang et al., 2006).
Preliminary testing indicated the efficacy of the manipulation, as
evidenced in Table 1. Consequently, the two advertisements
were utilized as the principal experimental stimuli.

performed to verify the validity of the advertising appeal type

Concurrently, a main effects analysis was conducted using

sampling method, utilizing 5,000 repeated samples and
maintaining a confidence level of 95%. The confidence interval
was utilized as the criterion for judgment. If the confidence
interval did not contain zero, the effect was deemed to be
significant. The significance level was set at α = 0.05 uniformly
throughout the entire analysis.

particularly the layered transmission of positive emotions and
narrative absorption. These findings demonstrate that the impact
of emotional appeals is not direct but rather achieved through
the processing of internal experiences.

narrative absorption theory. Previously, this concept has been
primarily applied to film, television, literature, and game
research. This study employs a case study approach, examining
the application of the concept in the context of advertising. The
study offers new empirical evidence that contributes to our
understanding of the use of storytelling in advertising. This
finding aligns with the prevailing perspective that the structural
tension, character development, and plot progression of

4.2 Theoretical significance: connecting

This study utilizes empirical testing to substantiate the
considerable benefits of emotional narrative advertising in
comparison to functional appeal advertising. The study’s
findings indicate that emotional narrative advertising is more
adept at stimulating positive affect and narrative absorption in
audiences. It further elucidates the dual mediating role of these
two psychological responses in the “advertising stimulus to
brand attitude to behavioral intention” pathway. This finding
provides new empirical support for the classic stimulus-
organism-response model (Mehrabian and Russell, 1974) in
consumer behavior, concurrently, it further expanded the
explanatory boundaries of this model within the context of
advertising communication. In this study, advertising type was
operationalized as the stimulus, positive affect and narrative
absorption as organism variables, and brand attitude and
behavioral intentions as response outcomes. Consequently, the
present study broadens the definition of the “organism”
component by incorporating both affective (positive affect) and
experiential (narrative absorption) processes. However, the
present study corroborates the notion that consumers’ emotional
experiences and psychological immersion collectively form a
dual-dimensional mediating mechanism, thereby functioning as
a pivotal conduit between external advertising stimuli and brand
attitude and behavioral intention.
This study further elucidates that positive emotions are not
merely immediate affective responses triggered by advertising
stimuli. Instead, positive emotions enhance consumers’
psychological openness and emotional engagement with the
brand. This emotional engagement facilitates the formation of
brand attitudes and behavioral intentions. From the perspective
of positive psychology, the study’s findings exhibit a high degree
of congruence with Fredrickson’ 2001 “Broaden-and-Build
Theory.” Specifically, the results of the present study suggest
that positive emotions evoked by affective narratives in
advertisements can have a substantial impact on consumers’
brand attitudes and behavioral intentions, thereby offering
support to the theoretical premise that “positive emotions can
broaden the scope of individuals’ cognitive and behavioral
responses.” In the context of advertising communication, this
mechanism manifests itself as follows: positive emotions evoked
by emotional narrative advertising weaken the audience’s
defensive screening of commercial information, allowing them
to more openly embrace the brand’s value. This process fosters
psychological “active absorption” and emotional resonance,
thereby fostering more positive brand evaluations and behavioral
intentions.This study further reveals that narrative immersion
exerts an independent influence on brand attitude. In addition,
it forms a chain-mediation path in conjunction with positive
emotions, thereby driving consumers from emotional
experiences to brand identification and behavioral intentions.
A subsequent analysis, informed by narrative communication
theory, illuminates the pivotal function of storytelling in the
psychological underpinnings of advertising. According to Green
and Brock’ 2000 “Narrative Transportation Theory.” The present
study found that higher levels of narrative involvement are
significantly associated with more positive brand attitudes,
consistent with existing research findings. Subsequent research
by Van Laer et al., 2014 further confirmed that this “narrative
transportation” state significantly enhances information
acceptance. This study builds on this key finding: narrative
absorption not only independently and positively influences
brand attitudes, but also forms a chain of transmission pathways
with positive emotions. The sequence of events begins with
emotional arousal, followed by psychological immersion, and
concludes with attitude formation. Positive emotions provide the
emotional foundation for narrative absorption (emotional
activation promotes attentional engagement), while narrative
absorption transforms fleeting emotional resonance into more
positive brand attitudes.Furthermore, this study demonstrates
that positive emotions serve not merely as an outcome of
advertising response, but also as a critical psychological
resource-catalyzing mechanism. This mechanism posits that
positive emotions facilitate the accumulation of resources—both
cognitive and affective—within the individual, thereby
establishing a more stable psychological link between brand
attitude and behavioral intention. This perspective, rooted in the
principles of positive psychology, enhances our comprehension
of the psychological mechanisms underlying advertising and
provides a complementary explanation for the dynamic role of
the “Organism” component within the S–O–R model.
Consequently, this study corroborates the communicative
benefits of emotional narrative advertising and further
illuminates how the experience-driven psychological mechanism
—specifically, the pathway from positive emotions to narrative
immersion—influences consumer attitudes and behavioral
responses. By offering novel empirical support for the cross-
theoretical integration of positive psychology, narrative
communication theory, and the S–O–R model, this study makes
a significant contribution to the field.
4.3 Practical implications
This research carries significant implications for the realm of
advertising and brand communication practices. First, it is
imperative to transition from “functional persuasion” to
“experiential persuasion” in advertising strategies. This shift
involves stimulating consumers’ psychological engagement
through an emotional and narrative-driven approach. The
utilization of emotional narrative advertising has been
demonstrated to elicit positive emotions and immersive
experiences among audiences, thereby fortifying brand attitudes
and behavioral intentions. This finding aligns with prior
research demonstrating the mediating role of emotional
responses in advertising persuasion (Batra and Ray, 1986;
Escalas and Bettman, 2003; Holbrook and Batra, 1987). The
impact of advertising stimuli on consumers’ behavioral

Secondly, emotional arousal should be embedded in a
complete narrative context rather than presented in isolation.
The immersive experience is shaped by a combination of
narrative plot structure, character resonance, and multimodal
cues, such as visual atmosphere and musical rhythm (Green and
Brock, 2000; Van Laer et al., 2014). The efficacy of advertising

resonance through storytelling logic, as opposed to the mere
accumulation of sentimental elements. This finding further
demonstrates that evoking positive emotions and creating
narrative immersion must be grounded in a complete and
coherent narrative context.
Thirdly, communication strategies ought to be adaptively
modified to cater to the varied characteristics of the audience.
For audiences with strong emotional responses (e.g., young
consumers), the narrative appeal of a work can be augmented.
For rational decision-makers (e.g., consumers of high-
involvement or high-risk products), functional information can
be appropriately integrated into the emotional framework to
balance emotion and rationality (Purohit and Srivastava, 2001).
The efficacy of this strategy lies in its ability to enhance
consumers’ emotional engagement and brand experience while
ensuring the credibility of the information provided.
In conclusion, the efficacy of advertising should extend to the
behavioral conversion stage. The extant research results
demonstrate that advertising type exerts a significant influence
on repurchase and word-of-mouth intention, with the impact
occurring through the “positive emotion-narrative absorption-
brand attitude” pathway. It is posited that consumer behavioral
conversion is not a direct result of advertising stimuli; rather, it
is the gradual accumulation of emotional experiences and
psychological immersion. It is imperative for brands to design
behavioral touchpoints (e.g., purchase, sharing, and member
interaction) at the peak of emotion to form a closed-loop brand
experience. The sequence of events begins with emotional
arousal, which is followed by an immersive experience. This is
then accompanied by an attitude change, which ultimately
results in a behavioral response. From a broader perspective, the
findings of this study also hold significant social and educational
implications. The mechanisms of emotion and immersion, as
they pertain to the context of brand communication, offer
valuable insights into understanding public emotional responses
and information reception processes within digital media
environments. Furthermore, they serve as a potential reference
for applications in the fields of educational communication and
social marketing.
4.4 Limitations and future research
directions
This study successfully verified the psychological mechanisms
through which emotional narrative advertising influences
consumer responses. However, the study’s findings are subject to
several constraints. Firstly, the short-term experimental
manipulation imposes limitations on the capacity to evaluate the
longevity of emotional and immersive effects. Future studies
may benefit from the adoption of longitudinal or repeated-
exposure designs to capture their long-term dynamics. Secondly,
given that the current sample was predominantly composed of
young adults, the findings may not be fully generalizable to
other demographic or cultural groups. Consequently, subsequent
research endeavors should encompass more heterogeneous
populations to substantiate the robustness of these effects
(Zhang and Shrum, 2009). Thirdly, subsequent investigations
could integrate potential moderating factors, such as advertising
platform, brand familiarity, and individual emotion-regulation
capacity, to identify the contextual boundaries and psychological
contingencies of advertising effectiveness (Purohit and
Srivastava, 2001).
5 Conclusions
The present study lends validation to the psychological
pathway through which emotional narrative advertising
influences brand attitudes and behavioral intentions. This
validation is achieved through a chain-mediated effect of
positive emotions and narrative absorption. This research
contributes to the psychological mechanisms underlying S–O–R
theory and provides a new theoretical basis for the cross-
disciplinary application of positive psychology within the field of
communication. Of particular significance is the demonstration,
provided by this study, of the manner in which advertising
fosters a more profound connection between consumers and
brands. This is achieved through the stimulation of positive
emotions and psychological immersion. This study underscores
the pivotal role of emotional experiences and immersion
mechanisms in contemporary advertising communication,
thereby offering a novel perspective on consumer attitudes and
behavioral intentions within complex media environments. In
consideration of the aforementioned points, novel directions for
future research in brand communication strategies, emotional
marketing, and social psychology are hereby suggested.
