Abstract
This study aims to analyze the effectiveness of using memes in digital marketing and its influence on Attitude
Toward Ad Engagement towards the Gojek Indonesia brand. Specifically, the research examines how cognitive
and affective variables namely Meme Mar keting Type, Narrative Transportation, Brand Knowledge, Meme
Literacy, and Meme Linguistic Characteristics contribute to shaping consumers' positive responses to meme-based
advertising. A quantitative approach was employed by distributing an online questionnaire to 262 respondents who
are active social media users in the Jabodetabek area and have been exposed to Gojek Indonesia's meme marketing
content. Data analysis was conducted using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM) to
test the relationships among variables in the research model. The results indicate that all tested constructs, which
are Meme Marketing Type, Narrative Transportation, Brand Knowledge, Meme Li teracy, and Meme Linguistic
Characteristics, were found to have a positive and significant influence on  Attitude Toward Ad Engagement.
Specifically, Narrative Transportation was identified as the strongest determinant (highest importance) of audience
engagement attitude, confirming that the power of storytelling and emotional connection within a meme is key to
successful brand communication. These findings provide empirical evidence that meme marketing is a highly
effective digital communication  strategy for building emotional and social connections between the brand and its
young audience. The managerial implications of this study suggest that companies, particularly the Gojek creative
team, need to focus on strengthening the relevant and relatable narrative elements in every meme content published
to enhance audience immersion and emotional resonance, thereby encouraging long -term engagement.
Keywords: meme marketing type, narrative transportation, brand knowledge, meme literacy, attitude toward ad
engagement
1. Introduction
Meme marketing has grown rapidly in recent years as entertaining content that significantly
increases consumer engagement; research on this phenomenon shows that it has become one of
the most effective communication strategies in the digital era. According to Razzaq et al. (2024,
meme marketing allows brands to create engaging customer experiences by incorporating their
elements into engaging content. This allows brands to create engaging experiences for
customers, achieving social media engagement rates significantly higher than the 1% achieved
by Google AdWords ads (Vasile et al., 2021). The appeal of memes lies in their ability to create
emotional connections through humor and relevant cultural references. Consumer behavior is
influenced by the key featur es of memes, especially among Generation Z (Arie Bowo &
Marthalia, 2024). Meme marketing has great potential to capture audience attention and build

emotional closeness with a brand, is formed through strong emotional experiences and
positively impacts consumer engagement.
2. Literature Review
Research by Razzaq et al. (2024) shows that memes with high brand cues (brand prominence)
generate more positive attitudes toward the advertisement and higher engagement levels
compared to memes with subtler cues. This effect occurs because clear brand elements make it
easier for consumers to associate the narrative with the brand identity, especially among
consumers with low brand knowledge.
Meme Marketing. As a marketing tool, meme marketing has been shown to increase consumer
engagement through likes, comments, and content sharing due to its entertaining and organic
nature (Malodia et al., 2022; Rathi & Jain, 2024). Vasile et al. (2021) emphasized that meme s
provide a strategic advantage for brands because their format can convey messages in a style
that doesn't feel like direct advertising. Therefore, understanding the forms and characteristics
of meme marketing is crucial in designing digital communication  strategies. Based on Razzaq
et al.'s (2024) framework, there are two main types of meme marketing that differ in how the
brand is presented in the content: brand -prominent meme marketing and brand -subtle meme
marketing.
Narrative Transportation . Narrative transportation describes the state of being completely
drawn into a story, feeling deeply emotionally and cognitively involved in the message.
According to Razzaq, Shao, and Quach (2024), narrative transportation is the primary
psychological mech anism linking marketing meme types to consumer responses, such as
attitude toward the ad and customer engagement. In Narrative Transportation theory, several
key elements shape this immersive experience, including a compelling plo t, relatable
characters, depicted motion, and a narrative voice in the first or second person. The combination

Brand Knowledge. Based on associative network theory, brand knowledge is formed through a
network of associations in consumer memory. Any information about a brand, such as a logo,
user experience, or communication message, is stored as interconnected nodes, and activation
of one node can trigger other related associations (De Toni, Zielke, & Mazzon, 2023). In a
marketing context, the level of brand knowledge determines how consumers process and
evaluate brand messages. Consumers with high brand knowledge have a mature understanding
and can recognize the meaning of messages even when brand elements are conveyed implicitly.
Conversely, consumers with low brand knowledge tend to rely on explicit visual and textual
cues to understand the message, such as a logo , brand name, or a clear product image (Feng,
2024).
Meme Literacy. Meme literacy refers to an individual's ability to understand, interpret, and
assign meaning to the symbols, texts, and socio -cultural contexts contained within memes in
digital spaces. This literacy encompasses not only an understanding of visual structur e and
humor, but also the interpretation of the symbolic values and cultural context inherent in memes
(Ntouvlis & Geenen, 2025). According to Razzaq, Shao, and Quach (2024), meme literacy acts
as a moderating variable influencing the rela tionship between meme marketing type and
narrative transportation. Consumers with high levels of meme literacy tend to better understand
the symbolic meanings conveyed by memes, making it easier to experience narrative
transportation even when brand elements are presented subtly.
Meme Marketing Linguistic Characteristics. Linguistic characteristics in meme marketing refer
to how language is used to convey brand messages, build relationships with audiences, and
encourage engagement on digital platforms. Linguistic elements function not only as
communication tools but also as strategic narrative devices that help create emotional closeness
and personal experiences for audiences. According to Razzaq, Shao, and Quach (2024),
linguistic elements serve as strategic -level moderators that i nfluence the relationship between
meme marketing type and narrative transportation. In this context, the use of personal pronouns,
such as first -person pronouns (I, me) and second -person pronouns (you, your), is crucial
because it can shape the level of pe rsonalization and audience engagement with the narrative.
Deng et al. (2021) explain that using informal, emotional language that aligns with the
audience's communication style has been shown to be more effective at increasing engagement
with brand content  on social media. Light, familiar language helps create an authentic
impression and strengthens the psychological closeness between the brand and the audience.
Research by Rathi and Jain (2024) also confirms that emotionally evocative linguistic elements
such as humor, irony, or wordplay can increase consumers' likelihood of engaging with and
sharing content more widely, as they feel more connected to socially and emotionally relevant
messages. Meanwhile, Surjadeep Dutta et al. (2024) highlight that using l inguistic elements
aligned with digital culture can strengthen brand image and increase customer retention by
creating a pleasurable emotional experience.

Data Analysis Techniques. Instrument testing included a pre -test (Validity, A VE, HTMT) and
reliability (Cronbach's Alpha and Composite Reliability). In the validity test, an indicator is
considered valid if it has an outer loading value ≥ 0.70 and an A VE ≥ 0.50. Indicators with
loading values between 0.40 –0.70 can still be retained if the construct still meets the A VE
criteria. Next, discriminant validity was tested to ensure that each construct is unique compared
to other constructs. This evaluation was carried out using the Forn ell-Larcker Criterion and
HTMT. A construct is declared to meet discriminant validity if the square root of the A VE is
higher than the correlation between constructs, and the HTMT value is below 0.85 –0.90 as
recommended by Hair et al. (2022). The testing p rocess was carried out by running the PLS
Algorithm to obtain the outer loadings, A VE, and HTMT values. If all indicators meet these
criteria, the instrument can be declared valid and suitable for use in the structural model analysis
in the next stage.  According to Hair et al. (2022), a construct is considered reliable if its
Cronbach's Alpha and CR values are above 0.70, indicating that the indicators have good
internal consistency in reflecting the measured variables. Therefore, if the Cronbach's Alpha
and CR values for all constructs exceed 0.70, it can be concluded that the research instrument
has good reliability and is suitable for further analysis. In this study, reliability testing was
conducted using Smart PLS by first running the PLS algorithm.

4. Finding and Discussion
Data collection through an online questionnaire distributed to Gojek service users who actively
use social media in the Greater Jakarta area yielded 262 respondents. The identification results
revealed that the majority of respondents were female (52.3%) and male (47.7%). This finding
suggests that female social media users tend to be more active in interacting with entertainment
content, including meme-based advertising. However, the nearly equal proportion of males also
indicates that meme -based communica tion strategies have cross -gender appeal, as their
humorous elements and social relevance are acceptable to a diverse user group (Malodia et al.,
2022).
In terms of age, the dominant group was respondents aged 21 –25 (51.9%), followed by those
aged 26 –38 (30.5%), and those aged 18 –20 (17.6%). This composition indicates that most
respondents are Generation Z and early Millennials, groups that are highly acti ve on social
media and are attracted to interactive and creative content. This aligns with the findings of
Razzaq et al. (2024), who stated that the effectiveness of meme marketing is highly dependent
on the emotional engagement of young audiences accustom ed to consuming short, visual
content.
Based on their educational attainment, the majority of respondents (52.3%) had a bachelor's
degree (S1), followed by those with a high school/vocational high school (24.4%), a diploma
(16%), a master's degree (6.9%), and a doctoral degree (0.4%). This dist ribution indicates that
the majority of respondents have a high level of education, which is closely related to the ability
to understand symbolism, humor, and social context in digital communication (Ntouvlis &
Geenen, 2025).
In terms of occupation, the majority of respondents were private -sector employees (42.7%),
followed by students (21.8%), entrepreneurs (18.7%), and civil servants (16.4%), while
housewives accounted for only 0.4%. This data indicates that most respondents are
economically and socially active individuals, with high levels of exposure to digital advertising
and daily online activities. This aligns with Wang and Lee's (2020) observation that professional
and productive users tend to engage more frequently with branded content on social media due
to their intense digital routines.
By domicile, all respondents were from the Greater Jakarta (Jabodetabek)) area is considered
representative because it is the center of Gojek user activity and the most active social media
users in Indonesia (Dwivedi et al., 2021a).
Overall, the respondent profile shows that the majority of study participants were young women
aged 21–25, highly educated, working in the private sector, and residing in the Greater Jakarta
area. This group belongs to the urban digital consumer segment wi th high levels of media
literacy and active engagement in online interactions. Therefore, this study's respondents can
be considered representative of the target population, namely young urban users who are the

5. Conclusion
First, the research results indicate a positive and significant effect of Meme Marketing Type on
Narrative Transportation. This finding confirms the importance of narrative immersion as a
mechanism that encourages audiences to evaluate meme ads more positi vely. Furthermore,
Meme Marketing Type also has a significant direct effect on Attitude Toward Ad Engagement,
indicating that meme type can drive engagement both directly and through increased narrative
immersion.

Second, Narrative Transportation has a positive and significant effect on Attitude Toward Ad
Engagement. This finding indicates that the greater the narrative immersion audiences
experience when viewing meme-based ads, the more positive their attitudes toward engagement
with those ads. In the context of Gojek's meme marketing, short, relatable narratives that are
close to the user's experience enable audiences to feel "involved" with the depicted situation.
Third, the research results indicate that Meme Marketing Type has a positive and significant
effect on Attitude Toward Ad Engagement. This finding indicates that the more engaging and
relevant the meme type used in an ad, the more positive the audience's a ttitudes toward
engagement with meme-based ads. In the context of Gojek's meme marketing, the use of humor
and references close to users' daily lives makes advertising messages easier to process and more
relevant. When audiences perceive that a meme "hits"  a familiar situation, ads tend to receive
more positive evaluations and encourage engagement. Therefore, these findings confirm that
selecting and designing the right Meme Marketing Type plays a crucial role in shaping Attitude
Toward Ad Engagement in meme-based ads.
Fourth, the results show that Meme Marketing Linguistic Characteristics do not moderate the
effect of Meme Marketing Type on Narrative Transportation. The tested moderation effect was
not statistically significant, meaning that changes in the linguistic ch aracteristics of the meme
did not strengthen or weaken the relationship between meme type and audience narrative
immersion. These findings indicate that narrative immersion formed through memes is more
influenced by key aspects of the meme type itself, suc h as situational relevance, humorous
strength, and the context's proximity to the audience's experiences, rather than by variations in
linguistic elements. In the context of Gojek's meme marketing, audiences are likely more
responsive to the visual context  and scenarios presented than to the accompanying linguistic
details. Thus, the effectiveness of meme type in building narrative immersion does not
significantly depend on linguistic characteristics as a moderator variable.
Fifth, the results of the study indicate that Meme Literacy does not moderate the effect of Meme
Marketing Type on Narrative Transportation. The tested moderation effect was not statistically
significant, indicating that the audience's meme literacy level did not strengthen the relationship
between the meme type used and perceived narrative immersion. In the context of Gojek's
meme marketing, the memes used are generally easy to understand, so meme literacy is not a
significant differentiating factor. Therefore, increasing narrative immersion depends more on
the quality and relevance of the meme type, rather than meme literacy as a moderator.
Sixth, the results of the study indicate that Brand Knowledge does not moderate the effect of
Meme Marketing Type on Narrative Transportation. The tested moderation effect was not
statistically significant, indicating that the audience's brand knowledge level did not strengthen
the relationship between the meme type used and perceived narrative immersion. The effect of
Meme Marketing Type on Narrative Transportation tends to be stable across different levels of
brand knowledge. This means that audiences with both higher and lower brand knowledge can
still experience narrative immersion when the meme used is engaging and relevant, without any
significant difference in the amplification effect due to variations in brand knowledge. In the

Seventh, the results show that narrative immersion mediates the effect of meme marketing type

When audiences experience narrative immersion, they tend to rate ads as more engaging and

in building narrative immersion. Practical implications: meme marketing stra tegies should
prioritize designing memes that are relevant to the audience's experiences, have an appropriate
humorous context, and construct a concise narrative flow that can draw the audience into the
story. Strengthening Brand Knowledge and Meme Literacy can be selectively implemented as
supporting factors, for example through consistent brand identity and selecting cultural
references that are truly relevant to Gen Z's daily lives. Thus, memes can function not only as
entertainment content but also as an effective persuasive medium for building ongoing audience
connection and engagement with advertisements.
