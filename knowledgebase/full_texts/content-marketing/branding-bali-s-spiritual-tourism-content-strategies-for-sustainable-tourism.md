This study examines the effects of Spiritual Tourism and Content Marketing on
Destination Competitiveness through the mediating role of Competitive Advantage
in Bali’s spiritual tourism destinations. The research seeks to explain how spiritual
and cultural assets can be transformed into sustainable competitive resources in
the digital tourism era. A quantitative explanatory research design was employed
using a survey of 219 domestic and international tourists who had participated in
melukat spiritual tourism activities in Bali. Data were collected through a structured
questionnaire and analyzed using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation
Modeling (PLS-SEM) with SmartPLS 4.1. The results indicate that Spiritual Tourism
and Content Marketing have significa nt positive effects on Competitive Advantage
and Destination Competitiveness. Competitive Advantage also has a significant
positive effect on Destination Competitiveness and partially mediates the
relationships between Spiritual Tourism and Competitiveness , as well as between
Content Marketing and Competitiveness. Among all direct relationships, Spiritual
Tourism demonstrates the strongest influence on Competitive Advantage,
emphasizing the strategic value of authentic spiritual experiences in strengthening
destination competitiveness. This study extends the Resource -Based View by
positioning spiritual and cultural assets as strategic resources capable of generating
sustainable competitive advantage. It also introduces a digital -spiritual alignment
perspective that integrates spiritual authenticity, content marketing, and
destination competitiveness within a single framework. The findings offer valuable
insights for tourism stakeholders seeking to enhance competitiveness while
preserving cultural and spiritual integrity.

1. INTRODUCTION
Tourism has become one of the most dynamic sectors contributing to economic growth,
employment generation, and regional development across the world. In recent years, the tourism
industry has undergone substantial transformation as travelers increasingly s eek meaningful
experiences that extend beyond traditional leisure -oriented activities. This shift has encouraged the
emergence of alternative tourism forms that emphasize authenticity, personal enrichment, cultural
immersion, and well -being. Among these em erging trends, spiritual tourism has attracted growing
attention due to its ability to address travelers’ psychological, emotional, and existential needs (Choe &
O’ Regan, 2020; Chu et al., 2025) . Unlike conventional tourism products that primarily focus on
entertainment and recreation, spiritual tourism offers transformative experiences that facilitate self -
reflection, healing, and inner peace (Chu et al., 2025; Dong et al., 2025) . Consequently, destinations

The growing popularity of spiritual tourism is closely associated with contemporary societal
challenges, including rising levels of stress, mental fatigue, and the search for personal meaning in
increasingly complex modern lifestyles. The post-pandemic tourism landscape has further accelerated
demand for wellness-oriented and spiritually enriching travel experiences that promote holistic well -
being (Karagianni et al., 2025; Putrevu & Mertzanis, 2026) . Many destinations worldwide have
responded by integrating spiritual elements, cultural heritage, and local wisdom into their tourism
offerings to attract visitors seeking transformative experiences. In Southeast Asia, particularly in
Indonesia, Bali has emerged as one of the most prominent spiritual tourism destinations due to its rich
cultural traditions, sacred landscapes, and deeply rooted spiritual practices. Among its unique
attractions, the melukat purification ritual has gained significant recognition among both domestic and
international visitors seeking emotional healing, spiritual cleansing, and cultural engagement. The
increasing popularity of melukat demonstrates how local spiritual traditions can evolve into globally
recognized tourism assets while maintaining their cultural significance.
Despite its growing appeal, the expansion of spiritual tourism presents several challenges for
destination managers and tourism stakeholders. One of the most critical concerns involves balancing
tourism development objectives with the preservation of authe nticity and sacred cultural values. As
spiritual experiences become tourism commodities, there is a risk that commercialization may diminish
their cultural meaning and spiritual integrity (Erwen et al., 2025; Sumpena et al., 2026). This challenge
is particularly relevant in Bali, where spiritual practices constitute an essential component of local
identity and religious life. Excessive promotion that prioritizes commercial interests over cultural
sensitivity may lead to superficial  representations of sacred traditions and reduce their long -term
sustainability. Therefore, destination competitiveness in spiritual tourism cannot be achieved solely
through increased visitor arrivals but must also consider cultural preservation, communit y
participation, and sustainable destination management.
In response to these challenges, digital communication strategies have become increasingly
important in shaping tourists’ perceptions and destination experiences. The rapid growth of social
media platforms, digital storytelling, and user -generated content has transformed how tourism
destinations communicate their value propositions to global audiences (Christou et al., 2025; Lund et
al., 2020) . Among various digital marketing approaches, content marketing has emerged as a
particularly effective strategy because it emphasizes education, engagement, and relationship building
rather than direct promotional messages. High -quality content can commun icate the philosophical
meanings, cultural narratives, and emotional dimensions embedded within spiritual tourism
experiences (Li et al., 2026; Xiao et al., 2026). For destinations such as Bali, content marketing provides
opportunities to present spiritual tourism not merely as an attraction but as a meaningful cultural and
transformative journey. Consequently, understanding how content marketing contributes to destination
competitiveness has become increasingly relevant for sustainable tourism development.
Previous studies have extensively examined spiritual tourism from multiple perspectives, including
tourist motivation, well -being, destination image, visitor satisfaction, and loyalty. Research has
demonstrated that spiritual experiences significantly infl uence tourists’ emotional attachment,
subjective well-being, and intention to revisit spiritual destinations (Kim & Chen, 2021; Nguyen-Viet &
Nguyen My, 2026). Other studies have highlighted the role of spiritual tourism in promoting sustainable
destination development by integrating cultural heritage, religious values, and local community
participation (Mishra & Maheshwari, 2024; Niezgoda et al., 2025). Within the Balinese context, Choe &
Mahyuni (2023)  emphasized the strategic importance of spiritual tourism as a sustainable post -
pandemic tourism development strategy, while (Dewi, 2025) identified substantial opportunities for
strengthening the competitiveness of melukat -based tourism destinations. Collectively, these studies

Simultaneously, a substantial body of literature has investigated the influence of content marketing
on consumer engagement, destination image, perceived value, and behavioral intentions. Studies by J.

been shown to strengthen emotional connections between visitors and destinations whil e improving

studies focus on general tourism destinations, smart tourism, or cultural tourism, with limited attention
devoted to spiritual tourism environments characterized by sacred values and intangible cultural assets.
Although prior studies provide valuable insights into spiritual tourism and content marketing
independently, several important gaps remain unresolved. First, empirical studies that simultaneously
examine spiritual tourism, content marketing, competitive advantage, and destination competitiveness
within a single integrated framework remain limited. Second, existing research largely emphasizes
tourism promotion outcomes without adequately explaining how digital communication strategies can
preserve spiritual authenticity while enhancing destination competitiveness. Third, the mechanism
through which spiritual tourism resources and content marketing activities are transformed into
sustainable competitive advantages has received insufficient scholarly attention.  Finally, research
focusing specifically on melukat -based spiritual tourism in Bali remains scarce despite its growing
international recognition and strategic importance for sustainable tourism development. These
limitations indicate the need for a more comprehensive model that explains how spiritual tourism and
content marketing jointly contribute to destination competitiveness through the creation of competitive
advantage.
Based on these considerations, this study aims to examine the effects of spiritual tourism and
content marketing on destination competitiveness, with competitive advantage serving as a mediating
variable in the context of Bali’s spiritual tourism destinati ons. The study contributes theoretically by
extending the Resource-Based View (RBV) perspective through the conceptualization of spiritual and
cultural assets as valuable, rare, and difficult -to-imitate resources capable of generating sustainable
competitive advantage. Furthermore, it introduces a digital -spiritual alignment perspective that
integrates local wisdom, spiritual authenticity, and content marketing strategies within a sustainable
tourism framework. Practically, the findings are expected to provide guidance for destination managers,
tourism businesses, and policymakers in developing marketing strategies that enhance competitiveness
while preserving the cultural and spiritual integrity of Bali’s tourism assets. By addressing these issues,
the study offers a more comprehensive understanding of sustainable destination development in the
increasingly competitive spiritual tourism sector.
2. METHOD
This study employed a quantitative research approach using an explanatory survey design to
examine the relationships among spiritual tourism, content marketing, competitive advantage, and
destination competitiveness within the context of Bali’s spiritual t ourism destinations. A quantitative
approach was considered appropriate because the study aimed to test theoretically derived hypotheses
and investigate the causal relationships among multiple latent variables. Furthermore, the explanatory
design enabled t he identification of both direct and indirect effects among constructs through a
mediation model. To achieve these objectives, Structural Equation Modeling based on Partial Least
Squares (PLS -SEM) was adopted because of its suitability for predictive resea rch, complex models
involving mediation effects, and latent variables measured through multiple indicators.

The study was conducted in four major tourism areas in Bali Province, Indonesia, namely Denpasar,
Badung, Gianyar, and Tabanan Regencies. These locations were selected because they represent the
primary centers of spiritual tourism activities in Bali and c ontain several well -known melukat
destinations that attract both domestic and international tourists. Among the destinations included in
the study were Tirta Empul Temple, Mengening Temple, Beji Griya Waterfall, Pancoran Solas, Tamba
Waras Temple, Kedatuan Raksa Sidhi Temple, Campuhan Windu Segara Temple, and Dalem Pengembak
Temple. These destinations have been actively promoted through digital platforms and are recognized
for their spiritual, cultural, and healing experiences. Data collection was conducted  over a four-month
period from January to April 2025.
The target population consisted of domestic and international tourists who had participated in
melukat spiritual tourism activities in Bali. Since the exact number of tourists who had experienced
melukat rituals was unknown and continuously changing, the p opulation was considered infinite. A
purposive sampling technique was employed to ensure that respondents possessed relevant experience
related to the research context. To be included in the study, respondents had to meet three criteria: (1)
be at least 18 years old, (2) have participated in a melukat ritual at least once within the previous two
years, and (3) have been exposed to information or promotional content regarding spiritual tourism
through digital media platforms. Individuals who had never partic ipated in spiritual tourism activities
or who submitted incomplete questionnaires were excluded from the analysis. A total of 235
questionnaires were collected, and after screening for completeness and response consistency, 219 valid
responses were retained for final analysis. This sample size exceeded the minimum requirements for
PLS-SEM analysis and provided sufficient statistical power for hypothesis testing
Data were collected using a structured questionnaire consisting of two sections. The first section
gathered demographic information, including gender, age, nationality, educational background, and
frequency of participation in spiritual tourism activities.  The second section measured the study
variables using multiple -item scales adapted from previous tourism and marketing studies.  Spiritual
Tourism was measured using four indicators reflecting self -discovery and healing, connection with
nature and culture,  physical and mental well -being, and detachment from materialism. Content
Marketing was measured through indicators assessing content quality, contextual relevance,
consistency, audience engagement, and value orientation. Competitive Advantage was operatio nalized
through indicators related to destination uniqueness, service quality, innovation capability, and
experiential differentiation. Destination Competitiveness was measured using indicators associated
with destination attractiveness, tourist satisfacti on, destination image, and perceived competitive
position. All items were assessed using a five-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5
(strongly agree).
Prior to the main survey, the questionnaire underwent content validation through expert review
involving three academics specializing in tourism management, marketing, and destination
development. Their feedback was used to improve item clarity, relevance,  and contextual suitability. A
pilot study involving 30 respondents was subsequently conducted to evaluate the preliminary reliability
and comprehensibility of the instrument.
Construct validity was assessed through convergent validity and discriminant validity tests within
the PLS -SEM framework. Convergent validity was evaluated using outer loading values and Average
Variance Extracted (AVE), with acceptable thresholds of 0.70 and 0.50, respectively. Discriminant
validity was examined using cross -loadings, the Fornell –Larcker criterion, and the Heterotrait –
Monotrait Ratio (HTMT). Reliability was evaluated through Composite Reliability and Cronbach’s Alpha
coefficients, with values exceeding 0.70 considered satisfactory. The final measurement model met all
recommended validity and reliability criteria, indicating that the instrument was suitable for hypothesis
testing.

The data collection process was conducted in several stages. First, a comprehensive review of the
literature was undertaken to identify relevant constructs and develop measurement indicators. Second,
the questionnaire was designed and validated through exp ert judgment and pilot testing. Third,
permission was obtained from destination managers and local tourism stakeholders to facilitate data
collection activities. Fourth, questionnaires were distributed both online and offline to eligible
participants at se lected spiritual tourism destinations and through tourism -related social media
communities. Respondents were informed about the purpose of the study and their rights as
participants before completing the questionnaire. Finally, all completed questionnaires were screened,
coded, and entered into a database for statistical analysis.

Figure 1. Research Flowchart
The collected data were analyzed using SmartPLS version 4.1. The analysis followed a two -stage
PLS-SEM procedure consisting of measurement model evaluation and structural model evaluation. In
the first stage, the measurement model was assessed through tests of convergent validity, discriminant
validity, composite reliability, and internal consistency reliability. In the second stage, the structural
model was evaluated by examining path coefficients, coefficients of determination (R²), predictive
relevance ( Q²), and overall model fit.  Hypothesis testing was conducted using the bootstrapping
procedure with 5,000 subsamples to estimate the significance of direct and indirect relationships among
variables. Statistical significance was determined using a p-value threshold of 0.05 and a t-statistic value
greater than 1.96. Mediation effects were assessed by examining the significance of indirect effects
between independent variables and destination competitiveness through competitive advantage.
This study adhered to established ethical principles for social science research. Participation was
entirely voluntary, and all respondents provided informed consent before completing the questionnaire.
Participants were informed about the objectives of the study, the confidentiality of their responses, and
their right to withdraw at any stage without consequence. No personally identifiable information was
collected, and all data were analyzed anonymously. The collected information was used exclusively for
academic purposes and stored securely to ensure data confidentiality and participant privacy.

3.4 Summary of Findings
The empirical findings demonstrate that both Spiritual Tourism and Content Marketing play
significant roles in strengthening Competitive Advantage and Destination Competitiveness. Among the
direct effects, Spiritual Tourism exhibits the strongest influence on Competitive Advantage, highlighting
the importance of authentic spiritual experiences in differentiating tourism destinations. Furthermore,
Competitive Advantage serves as a significant mediating mechanism through which spiritual tourism
resources and content marketing initiatives contribute to sustainable destination competitiveness. The
results provide empirical support for the proposed conceptual framework and emphasize the strategic
importance of integrating spiritual tourism experiences with effect ive content marketing strategies to
enhance the long-term competitiveness of Bali’s spiritual tourism destinations.
The findings reveal that Spiritual Tourism exerts the strongest positive influence on Competitive
Advantage, highlighting the strategic importance of spiritual experiences as a unique tourism resource.
This result suggests that the competitiveness of Bali’s spiritual tourism destinations is not solely derived
from physical attractions or service facilities but is deeply rooted in intangible cultural and spiritual
assets. From the perspective of the Resource -Based View (RBV), resources capable of generating
sustainable competitive advantage must be valuable, rare, difficult to imitate, and organizationally
embedded (Malhotra et al., 2024) . The melukat ritual embodies these characteristics because it is
inseparable from Balinese religious traditions, local wisdom, and sacred cultural practices. The finding
supports previous studies emphasizing that spiritual experiences create destination differentiation and
strengthen market positioning (Carvache-Franco et al., 2024; Jose et al., 2024; Mishra & Maheshwari,
2024). However, this study extends the existing literature by demonstrating that spiritual tourism
contributes not only to visitor satisfaction and well-being but also functions as a strategic resource that
enhances competitive advantage. This perspective advan ces current understanding by positioning
spirituality as a core competitive asset rather than merely an experiential tourism component.
The significant effect of Content Marketing on Competitive Advantage further demonstrates that
destination competitiveness increasingly depends on the ability to communicate intangible value
through digital channels. In contemporary tourism markets, travel ers often construct destination
perceptions before visiting through online content, social media interactions, and digital storytelling.

The positive relationship between Competitive Advantage and Destination Competitiveness
confirms that competitive resources must be transformed into market -relevant value before they can

natural resources, not all are able to convert these resources into sustainable competitiveness. The
findings indicate that competitive advantage serves as a strategic mechanism that translates unique
destination attributes into stronger market positions, visitor attraction, and long -term sustainability.
This result aligns with the arguments of Jiang & McCabe (2021), Ndubisi & Nair (2023), Xu & Au (2023),
who emphasize that destination competitiveness is largely determined by a destination’s ability to
leverage its unique resources effectively. However , the present study adds empirical evidence from the
spiritual tourism context, where competitive advantage emerges primarily from symbolic, experiential,
and cultural values rather than tangible infrastructure. This distinction is important because it broadens
existing destination competitiveness frameworks that often prioritize economic, managerial, and
infrastructural dimensions. Consequently, the findings suggest that intangible cultural assets deserve
greater attention within competitiveness research and tourism policy development.
The direct influence of Spiritual Tourism on Destination Competitiveness demonstrates that
authentic spiritual experiences remain a central determinant of destination attractiveness regardless of
mediation effects. Tourists who participate in melukat ritua ls are not merely consuming tourism
products; they are engaging in transformative experiences associated with self -discovery, healing,
emotional restoration, and cultural immersion. Such experiences generate strong emotional attachment
and contribute to de stination loyalty. This finding supports studies by Garg et al. (2021), Mishra &
Maheshwari (2024), Yuan & Li (2025) , which identify spiritual fulfillment as a significant driver of
visitation intentions and destination loyalty. Nevertheless, the current findings suggest that the
competitiveness effects of spiritual tourism may be particularly strong in Bali because spi rituality is
embedded within everyday social life rather than being artificially constructed for tourism purposes.
This contextual factor differentiates Bali from many other spiritual tourism destinations and may
explain why spiritual tourism exerts a subs tantial influence on competitiveness. Therefore, preserving
authenticity becomes a strategic necessity rather than merely a cultural responsibility.

that destination competitiveness is increasingly shaped by digital communication effectiveness. The
tourism industry has become highly information-intensive, where destination visibility, credibility, and
engagement are largely influenced by online interactions. The findings suggest that effective content
marketing strengthens competitiveness by enhancing destination awareness, shaping favorable
perceptions, and stimulating tourists’ intentions to visit. These results are consistent with previous
research highlighting the role of digital content in improving destination image and tourist engagement

4. CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS
This study examined the relationships among Spiritual Tourism, Content Marketing, Competitive
Advantage, and Destination Competitiveness within the context of Bali’s spiritual tourism destinations.
The findings demonstrate that both Spiritual Tourism and C ontent Marketing significantly influence
Competitive Advantage and Destination Competitiveness. Among the direct effects, Spiritual Tourism
emerged as the strongest determinant of Competitive Advantage, indicating that authentic spiritual
experiences const itute a valuable strategic resource for destination development. Furthermore,
Competitive Advantage was found to significantly enhance Destination Competitiveness and partially
mediate the effects of Spiritual Tourism and Content Marketing. These results confirm that destination
competitiveness is strengthened not only by the presence of unique spiritual assets but also by the ability
to communicate and transform those assets into distinctive competitive value.
From a theoretical perspective, this study extends the Resource  Based View (RBV) by
demonstrating that spiritual and cultural assets can function as valuable, rare, and difficult  to imitate
resources that generate sustainable competitive advantage. The study also contributes to the tourism
literature by proposing a digital spiritual alignment perspective, which explains how content marketing
can support both destination competitiveness and the preservation of spiritual authenticity. By
integrating Spiritual Tourism, Content Marketing, Competitive Advantage, and Destinat ion
