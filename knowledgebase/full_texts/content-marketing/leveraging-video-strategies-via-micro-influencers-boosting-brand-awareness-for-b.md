ABSTRACT
Video content has emerged as one of the most effective formats for engaging social media audiences.  In
Malaysia, MSEs make up 98.3% of all businesses, yet many struggle to grow due to a lack of a structured marketing
approach. For beauty MSEs, leveraging video content through micro-influencers presents a cost-effective strategy to
enhance brand visibility, build consumer trust, and compete in crowded markets. However, little is known
about how vide o content strategies used  by micro-influencers contribute to consum er engagement and
foster MSEs’ brand awareness. This study aims to explore video content structure, styles, and audience
engagement techniques used by micro-influencers to boost brand awareness of MSEs. Grounded in Uses and
Gratifications Theory, the study employed a qualitative design, a descriptive content analysis of 50 Instagram
posts shared by micro-influencers who have been collaborating with beauty MSEs in Malaysia. Findings
revealed three key strategic content approaches: (1) educational storytelling through informative content
and product reviews to establish brand credibility, (2) emotionally resonant narratives often found in lifestyle
content and personal/product reviews to cultivate audience connection, and (3) tactical engagement
methods including hashtags, tagging, and calls to action to drive visibility and interaction. Findings show that short-
form videos are particularly effective in fostering emotional engagement through storytelling, relatability, and
authenticity. Instagram’s visual focus, algorithmic prioritisation of early engagement, and interactive tools make
it especially suited for micro-influencer marketing. This research offers practical insights for MSE/business owners
and policy-makers seeking cost-effective digital marketing strategies.

1. INTRODUCTION
The way businesses communicate with customers is
entirely different now because of social media, and
Instagram is now recognised as a favourite medium for
visual storytelling, particularly when it comes to short-
form video, such as reels and stories.  Regular users
creating materials, algorithms that increase reach, and
a focus on presenting a "real" image of oneself online
have all contributed to the growth of influencer culture.
Amid this shift, video content has gained prominence as
a persuasive and immersive communication tool. Unlike
static posts, video enables multi-sensory engagement,
combining visuals, sound, narrative, and performance
to deliver richer messages. On Instagram, features like
Reels and Stories enable brands and influencers to co-
create dynamic, bite-sized content that resonates with
audiences in real time (Jun, 2022; Altamira et al., 2023).
Influencers are individuals with a strong online presence
and the ability to influence consumer behaviour (Ali
Abbasi et al., 2022; Agustian et al., 2023).
The global influencer marketing market is expected to
exceed US$39 billion by 2025, with an annual growth
rate of 9.37% expected through 2029 (Statista, 2025).
In Southeast Asia, it was valued at $693.70 million in
2024 (Supermom Business, 2024), generating roughly
$15 billion in e-commerce sales (Cube Asia, 2024), with
growth expected at 10.83% over the next five years
(InFluent, 2024). Within Southeast Asia (SEA) countries,
Malaysia stands out as the region’s second most
impactful market (89%) (Cube Asia, 2024; InFluent, 2024).
Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs) are the backbone
of Malaysia. Within that, Small and Medium Enterprises
(SMEs) are an important part of a country’s economy,
contributing to job creation and economic growth
(Azhar & Shakil, 2021), and they make up about 98.3% of

influencers exert a strong influence, reaching 89% of

leaving a significant research gap in understanding how
micro-influencers collaborate with MSEs through video
content to foster brand awareness (Ardiyanti, 2023).
To explore this under-researched area, this study applies
Uses and Gratifications Theory (Katz et al., 1973), which
posits that individuals actively use media, selectively
engaging with content to fulfil particular psychological
and social needs. Therefore, how do beauty micro-
influencers in Malaysia utilise video content strategies
on Instagram to build brand awareness and foster
audience engagement?
This question led to the aim of this research, namely, to
understand how small businesses, specifically beauty
MSEs, can strategically use Instagram video content in
collaboration with micro-influencers to enhance their
digital brand presence. By focusing on content structure,
storytelling techniques, and audience engagement
strategies, this research offers practical insights that are
especially valuable for small enterprises navigating the
challenges of social media marketing in a resource-
constrained environment.
2.  LITERATURE REVIEW
2.1 Brand Awareness in the Digital Age
Brand awareness is a fundamental concept in marketing
that describes how well consumers can recognise and
remember a brand and its associated attributes. It is a
crucial element in building and maintaining a successful
brand presence in the marketplace. Brand awareness
is defined as the knowledge about the brand, or the
descriptive and evaluative information about the
brand stored in consumers' memory (Kotler et al., 2019).
According to Keller's customer-based brand equity
model, brand awareness is the first step in building
strong brand associations and fostering customer loyalty
(Keller, 1993).
In today’s highly competitive and saturated markets,
establishing brand awareness is not just an advantage but
a necessity for survival.  The digital shift has transformed
the way brand awareness is built and maintained.
Traditional methods such as print advertisements, radio,
and television have been largely supplemented, if not
replaced, by digital strategies, especially through social
media platforms. Among these, Instagram has emerged
as a key platform due to its visual nature and high user
engagement. Its user interface and features like Stories,
Reels, and IGTV allow for creative visual storytelling
that enhances brand recognition and consumer
connection (Alwan & Catya, 2023). Instagram’s image
and video-driven layout makes it particularly effective
for building brand awareness, especially in the beauty
industry, where aesthetics and visuals are central to
brand identity (Pratama et al., 2024). A study found
that 74.2% of brand awareness was driven by content
marketing on Instagram (Kalkautsar & Listiani, 2022).
Visual communication is processed 60,000 times faster
than text, enabling viewers to absorb information more
efficiently when visuals such as infographics and data
are used together (Dunn, 2023).

collaborations.
2.2 Micro-Influencers and Small Business Marketing
Micro-influencers are individuals with between 1,000
and 100,000 followers (Gerlich, 2022) who are perceived
as more relatable, trustworthy, and authentic, making
them especially effective in niche markets where
audiences feel connected (Wei et al., 2021). Their
persuasive power lies in subtly integrating product
qualities into personal experiences, making their
content less commercial and more genuine (Kim et
al., 2021b).
Trust is widely recognised as a key mechanism behind
micro-influencers’ effectiveness. Consumers place
a high level of trust in micro-influencers who are
perceived as relatable and knowledgeable about
consumer preferences (Wei et al., 2021; Gerlich, 2022),
especially when content is authentic and aligns with
brand values (Nayak & Das, 2024). This trust leads
consumers to believe these influencers provide reliable
recommendations and are more likely to engage with
branded content. This trust can translate into stronger
brand-consumer relationships (Felix et al., 2024), leading
to greater loyalty, sales up to 40%, and enhanced brand
visibility (Nayak & Das, 2024).
Engagement metrics support this pattern; micro-
influencers consistently achieve higher interaction rates
than macro-influencers (Kusumawati et al., 2021), with a
study reporting up to 60% greater engagement (Ehlers,
2021). On the contrary, Simão (2023) found that both are
viewed as equally trustworthy, as trust depends more
on content and personal traits than follower count;
however, micro-influencers are perceived as more
relatable.
Small businesses increasingly use micro-influencers
across a variety of industries, including food, fashion,
travel, and retail (Nayak et al., 2024). Micro-influencer
partnerships offer a cost-effective alternative to macro-
or celebrity-level collaboration (Nayak & Das, 2024;
Bezhovski, 2025). While many small businesses engage
influencers through nonmonetary incentives, such as
product gifting and affiliate marketing arrangements,
where compensation depends on performance,
aligning business and influencer success (Miloševi ć
et al., 2022; Nilsson et al., 2023; Bezhovski, 2025). This
affordability enables many small businesses to adopt
micro-influencers, recognising the quality interactions
these influencers have with their audience (Wei et al.,
2021; Gerlich, 2022).
Although micro-influencers benefit SMEs, it may be
challenging. Focusing on micro-influencers may limit
the brand's reach and potentially limit brand exposure
(Tian et al., 2024). Scholars suggest mitigating this by
collaborating with multiple micro-influencers, which can
collectively expand reach and increase exposure. This
approach also diversifies campaign risk, ensuring that
if one influencer underperforms, others may still deliver
strong results (Kusumawati et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2022).
While existing studies have examined the effectiveness
of micro-influencers across small businesses, limited
attention has been given to the beauty sector, where
strategic collaboration with micro-influencers plays a
particularly crucial role in driving engagement.

Content strategy is a critical component of modern

and management of content to achieve marketing
goals such as enhancing brand awareness, building
trust, and driving engagement. In the digital age,
influencers are integral to content strategy as they
can amplify brand messages and drive engagement
through their established credibility and reach (Tafesse
& Wood, 2021; Gshayyish, 2025).
Instagram, as a highly visual and interactive platform,
offers fertile ground for content marketing. Strategic
use of content on Instagram can boost brand visibility,
foster trust, especially when aligning content with the
brand’s objectives, tailored to audience preferences,
and created in engaging and relevant formats (Tafesse
& Wood, 2021; Mate & Patil, 2024). However, what counts
as “effective” content is not uniform. While some studies
emphasise that visual appeal combined with storytelling
is central to effectiveness (Skoglund, 2022; Gross et
al., 2023), others suggest that the authenticity and
perceived similarity of content may outweigh aesthetic
quality in sustaining engagement (Chung et al., 2023).
Divergence can be traced to methodological choices;
studies that measure objective engagement metrics
(likes, views) are more likely to capture the impact of
visual appeal, whereas research that uses subjective
outcomes (credibility, purchase intent) captures
authenticity effects.
Different types of content serve different strategic
purposes. Social and entertaining content attracts more
user engagement than informative content (Sharief
& Elsharnouby, 2024). Informative content builds trust
and brand awareness, while authentic endorsements
strengthen consumer response. Product reviews and
tutorial videos were most preferred among beauty
consumers, while promotional content was the least
favoured on Instagram and YouTube (Gregor et al.,
2023), suggesting audiences value authentic and
helpful content over commercial posts.
Instagram’s features and algorithms further shape
influencers’ content strategies. Algorithms are crucial in
influencer marketing where visibility drives success, and
must be carefully studied to grasp their value and power
(Sule & Wani, 2024). Carousels gain the most interaction,
videos the least; shorter captions and minimal hashtags
perform better, while over-posting reduces engagement
(Thorgren et al., 2024). Most interactions occur within 24
hours, indicating the algorithm’s preference for timely
content (Sule & Wani, 2024; Thorgren et al., 2024). This
includes aligning content tone, values, and aesthetics
with the brand identity (Okonkwo & Namkoisse, 2023).
Stories drive higher purchase intention than posts
(López-Barceló & López, 2022), as they are often used
for storytelling, which simplifies complex messages,
enhances relatability and trust, and fosters emotional
engagement (Atiq et al., 2022).
According to Vivas-Peraza (2022), to enhance
engagement, influencers use platform-specific
techniques such as hashtags to expand content reach
(Kumar et al., 2022), call-to-actions (CTAs), tagging,
music, and voiceovers. Clear CTAs can increase
conversion rates by 63.3% through actionable, well-
phrased, and multilingual wording (Al Khaldy, 2024).
These features also align with Instagram’s algorithm,
which favours content that sparks early reactions.
Although prior research offers broad insights into
influencer content effectiveness, limited studies focus
specifically on the effective content types employed
by micro-influencers.
2.4 Video Content and Its Impact on Brand Awareness
Video content has become a dominant format in digital
marketing due to its ability to deliver information visually,
emotionally, and memorably. Compared to static
images or text, videos facilitate stronger storytelling
and viewer retention (Nicoli et al., 2022; Fan et al.,
2023). This strength is particularly relevant to influencer
collaborations, where authenticity and emotional
resonance underpin consumer trust. Especially for
beauty micro-influencers, video formats enable the
sharing of skincare routines or product demonstrations
in ways that appear personal and relatable, thereby
enhancing the perceived authenticity of brand
messages. Such emotionally infused storytelling aligns
with Safitri's (2022) findings that interactive features,
such as Stories and Reels, are effective at engaging
audiences and driving brand recognition.
Platform-specific features further shape the effectiveness
of video marketing. Instagram’s Reels, Stories, and IGTV
offer varied formats with different durations, levels of
interactivity, and reach. Reels and Stories are popular
due to their short duration, creativity, and engagement
that can build trust and awareness among audiences
(Min & Kim, 2025). Reels are also effective because they
can reach the right age group, and longer videos allow
viewers to enjoy clearer, more detailed content (Halim et
al., 2022).   These formats also offer interactive features
such as polls, stickers, and clickable links, encouraging
active audience engagement and boosting brand
recall.
Videos across platforms offer unique advantages for
brands aiming to reach and engage target audiences.
Instagram Reels has been found to generate higher
engagement than TikTok in certain cases, as it allows
longer video durations and more explicit content
delivery (Halim et al., 2022). The literature has shown
that video content significantly influences consumer
behaviour and purchase decisions. Longer videos
were perceived as more informative, entertaining,
emotionally appealing, and having greater advertising
value compared to shorter videos (Morreale et al., 2022),
while videos featuring music can increase sales (Ge

The Uses and Gratifications Theory, proposed by Katz et
al. (1973), posits that individuals actively engage with
media to fulfil specific needs such as entertainment,
social interaction, and identity formation. UGT has been
widely adopted in studies that highlight social media
users’ active roles in determining how to use such media
to seek gratification rather than merely being passive
recipients of its content (Falgoust et al., 2022; Liestia,
2023 ) .   Gr a tific a tions  c an  be  divided  in t o  those  tha t
people expect to gain and those that they actually
experience.
Some recent studies have used UGT as a framework for
describing audiences’ motivations for engaging with
influencers’ content (Yaman & Çakin, 2021; Nguyen et al.,
2025). In this study, a framework is adopted to examine
micro-influencers’ content-sharing experiences, as its
emphasis on individuals’ active fulfilment of needs may
provide a nuanced understanding of this group, in line
with the study objective.
While previous research has extensively explored
influencer marketing in the context of large brands
and macro-influencers, there remains a notable lack of
qualitative studies that investigate how micro-influencers
create video content specifically for small businesses.
Most studies rely on quantitative surveys or focus on
consumer responses, overlooking the strategic content
decisions made by influencers and their collaborating
MSE brands. Moreover, limited attention has been paid
to Instagram video features, such as Reels and Stories,
as tools for brand awareness, particularly in the beauty
sector. This study addresses this gap by applying Uses
and Gratifications Theory (UGT) to examine how micro-
influencers, in collaboration with MSEs, use video as
a rich, immersive medium to communicate brand
values and engage audiences. This study addresses
this gap by combining interviews and content analysis
to examine how beauty MSEs leverage video content
strategies in collaboration with micro-influencers to
enhance audience engagement and brand visibility.

3.  METHODOLOGY
This study adopts a qualitative case study research
design, using an observation technique on Instagram,
to explore the video content strategies micro-
influencers use to build brand awareness and foster
audience engagement. The qualitative method is
used to understand subjective experiences and social
phenomena in depth, rather than quantifying variables,
as said by Creswell (2013). The observation technique
was employed in this study as it enables researchers to
examine behaviours, interactions, and activities within
their natural setting (Creswell, 2013) while also allowing
researchers to examine the meaning and context of
Instagram posts (Krippendorff, 2013). Purposive sampling
was used to select influencer posts directly related to
MSE collaborations. Inclusion criteria required that posts
(a) explicitly feature the partnered MSE brand, (b) be
posted between May 2022 and June 2024, and (c) show
an engagement rate between 1% and 3%, indicating
healthy audience interaction. Engagement rate was
calculated using the formula: (likes + comments) ÷
followers × 100, following benchmarks (Wong, 2026).
Data collection involved extracting text from videos,
audio (music & voice-over), captions, and engagement
metrics such as likes and shares from selected Instagram
posts. In total, 50 Instagram posts were analysed.
Considering that this analysis did not yield substantially
new insights, the content was analysed to the point of
data saturation within the selected posts.
A descriptive content analysis was used to analyse
the observed Instagram posts; the study does not
permit specific inferences and instead seeks to expose
the pattern(s) of a theme in terms of frequencies or
percentages to present an overall case (Dinçer, 2018).
The content analysis was guided by a structured
codebook developed to categorize post types (e.g.,
product reviews, tutorials), media formats (e.g., reels,
carousels), and interaction elements (e.g., hashtags,
CTAs).

4.1 Informative Content: Educating to Build Trust
Informative content emerged as the top-most frequently
used content type, accounting for 25 posts (50%).
Aimed at building brand awareness through product
education, demonstrations (e.g., applying serums or
masks), tutorials, FAQs, tips, and benefits. Table 2 outlines
the common effective video and caption structure.
Among these, demonstration and product benefit
are the most common topics. Most videos followed
a structured format: starting with a visual hook,
humorous scene, or a curiosity-triggering (e.g., “Wait
for it…”), followed by a verbal (voice-over) or textual
explanation of product features(e.g., science-backed
or traditional), usage instructions, or key ingredients, and
concluding with a CTA (e.g., “Check out the link in bio!”,
“Buy now” or “DM to order”), encouraging audience
engagement. The overall tone was predominantly
friendly, enthusiastic, conversational, and humorous,
aimed at building trust and maintaining viewer interest.
Audio elements played a key role in reinforcing the
content's informativeness. Among the 25 informative
videos, 12 used voiceovers only, while 7 incorporated
music without voice. A smaller portion combined both
music and voice, with five combined voiceovers with
background music, and one used an AI-generated
voice. The strong preference for explanatory voice-
overs highlights the emphasis on clarity and instructional
delivery.
The captions generally followed a persuasive and
informative structure. Most began with an attention-
grabbing hook of a question, bold statement (e.g.,
sunscreen game is STRONG), or relatable scenario (e.g.,
“Still struggling with dry skin?”), followed by concise
product benefits, usage instructions, or ingredient
explanations. In several cases, influencers included
personal experiences or endorsements to increase
authenticity and relatability. Captions often concluded
with an action-driven CTA (e.g., “Try it now!”), brand
mentions, promo codes, and hashtags to increase reach
and visibility.
Various audience engagement techniques were
strategically embedded in the captions. Hashtags were
most frequently used, appearing in 22 out of the 25
informative posts (e.g., #skincareroutine, #glowingskin,
and #beautytips), broadening reach to targeted
audiences. CTA were observed in 16 posts, encouraging
purchase or brand followership (e.g., “You should really
try this Brand X sunscreen”, “Have a try!”, “For more
info, you can find it at Brand X”, “Get yours now at
Brand X”). In 14 of the videos, influencers tagged the
MSE’s Instagram account to establish a clear brand
association and facilitate redirection to the product
page. Although no direct links were included in the
videos, several captions referred to the “link in bio” for
more information or to make purchases.
An Instagram post by Jing (2024) features an influencer’s
self-representation image with infographic-style “5 Facts
of Sunscreen” messaging. The post incorporates step-by-
step demonstrations and ingredient explanations that
elicited the strongest audience reactions, especially
when paired with clear visual guidance, explanatory
voiceovers, and detailed captions.

4.2 Product Review: Establishing Credibility Through
Authenticity
Product review content emerged as the second most
frequently used content type, accounting for 13 posts
(26%).  Product review-themed posts adopted a direct
and informative tone, centring the product as the main
subject of the video or caption ( Table 3).
Structurally, it typically began with a personalised
hook, such as a direct introduction to the product, a
specific problem, personal anecdotes, or “before”
visuals (e.g., images of dull or acne-prone skin), to
establish an authentic connection with their audience.
Followed by a detailed main segment where influencers
demonstrate application in real-time while narrating
its texture, key ingredients, and benefits. On-screen
text overlays are frequently used to highlight product
specifications such as “fragrance-free,” “non-stripping,”
or “best for oily skin,” serving as quick-reference guides
for viewers. Videos often incorporate before-and-
after comparisons or close-up shots to emphasise
effectiveness. Videos typically close with the influencer
showing their current appearance with visibly improved
skin or hair condition, followed by a recommendation
or soft CTA (e.g., “Try it out” or “You’ll love this too”, “It
really works”). Occasionally, influencers disclose brand
partnerships or express gratitude to the brand, which
subtly affirms the endorsement while maintaining
transparency. The overall tone remains relatable and
persuasive, with influencers appearing barefaced or
filter-less to boost credibility.
The audio structure commonly uses voice narration to
guide viewers through the review process. Of 13 posts,
5 featured voice-only narration, offering personal
experiences and detailed product explanations. Five
blended voices with background music, while two used
only music supported by text overlays.

5.  DISCUSSION
This study aims to examine how beauty MSEs can
strategically use Instagram video content in collaboration
with micro-influencers to enhance their digital brand
presence. These research findings highlight three major
themes: informative content that builds trust through
educating the audience, product reviews that establish
credibility through authenticity, and lifestyle content
that builds emotional connection and relatability.
Findings reveal that micro-influencers primarily use
informative content to educate their audience about a
product or brand. This reflects distinct content practices
that set these beauty micro-influencers across regions,
unlike beauty micro-influencers in prior studies, who
rely heavily on entertainment content on Instagram
(Koowuttayakorn, 2025). This contrast may be attributed
to differences in study context: prior research examined
global micro-influencers, whereas the present study
focuses on Malaysian micro-influencers, whose regional
content preferences diverge. This aligns with Wei et
al. (2021), who found that micro-influencers support
start-ups by using informative and authentic content to
build awareness and trust. Taken together, these studies
indicate that micro-influencer content strategies may
vary depending on brand maturity, audience familiarity,
and market context. Moreover, credibility, perceived
expertise, and authenticity directly influence consumer
trust and purchase intention (Kim & Kim, 2021).
Additionally, the second significant finding concerns
how the product review content type helps build
authenticity and credibility among the audience.
For example, when influencers share their genuine
personal experiences or struggles related to a product,
it receives strong engagement from the audience.
When consumers see influencers overcome relatable
challenges using the product, it strengthens emotional
identification and positions the brand as a solution
provider (Foos, 2021). These findings align with a

previous study (Nahdiah et al., 2024) on e-commerce
platforms’ customer reviews, which exert an even more
substantial effect on purchase intention than celebrity-

perceive peer reviews as more authentic, trustworthy,
and reflective of real product and service experiences
(Nahdiah et al., 2024). Through electronic word of
mouth, product reviews, and recommendations on
TikTok, these shape consumers’ purchase intentions
(Indrawati et al., 2023). Taken together, these studies
show that across all influencer tiers and social media
platforms in the beauty industry, product reviews serve
as a very important content type, as they are seen
as authentic and boost trust among the audience.
Influencers’ credibility dimension of expertise and
trustworthiness can affect users' behaviours according
to the perceived usefulness they acquire from their
content (AlFarraj et al., 2021; Azhar et al., 2025).
The third most emerging finding is the lifestyle content
type, which builds emotional connection through
relatability. This aligns with Mahlakaarto and Suanse
(2025), who found that consumers engage with
authentic and relatable content because they feel a
sense of belonging within online communities. They
are more likely to trust influencers who reflect their own
lifestyles, appearance, or values, which strengthens the
emotional connection and motivates engagement with
the content. Similarly, lifestyle content is important for
beauty products because it makes recommendations
appear authentic, relatable, and contextually useful,
thereby strengthening audience trust and engagement
(Torjesen, 2021). Research shows that content relatability
that resonates with audience needs and preferences
plays a crucial role in purchase intention and sales
growth (Nayak & Das, 2024).
6.  CONCLUSION
This study set out to explore how beauty Micro and
Small Enterprises (MSEs) in Malaysia strategically utilise
Instagram video content through collaborations with
micro-influencers to build brand awareness and foster
audience engagement. Anchored in the context of
digital marketing for beauty-based MSEs, the research
examined how different video content strategies,
including informative, product review, and lifestyle, are
leveraged to shape consumer perception, trust, and
interaction. Through interviews with MSE owners and
content analysis of influencer posts, the study identified
recurring patterns in how content is constructed,
delivered, and received.
The findings revealed three major insights. First,
MSEs prioritise value-driven content, particularly
informative videos and product reviews, to establish
brand credibility. These content types play a central
role in educating audiences, showcasing product
functionality, and building consumer trust. Second,
lifestyle content and emotionally grounded product
reviews contribute significantly to emotional brand
engagement. These narratives are not just promotional
tools but serve to humanise the brand, foster relatability,
and align with the values and lived experiences of the
target audience. Third, tactical strategies such as calls to
action (CTAs), hashtags, account tagging, and indirect
linking are consistently used to increase post visibility
and drive user interaction on Instagram.
Theoretically, this study contributes to the application
of Uses and Gratifications Theory in the context of
micro-influencer marketing for MSEs. It demonstrates
that audience gratification is not satisfied solely by the
informational or persuasive aspects of content, but
also by the authenticity, emotional relatability, and
credibility conveyed by micro-influencers. Additionally,
this research enriches the content strategy literature by
showing how small businesses can leverage different
content types, namely informative content, product
reviews, and lifestyle content, to build both cognitive
trust and emotional engagement with their target
audience.
In practice, the study offers valuable guidance for
MSEs and micro-influencers seeking to optimise their
Instagram marketing efforts. For beauty brands with
limited budgets, integrating educational value and
emotional storytelling can be a cost-effective way
to boost engagement and brand perception. By
combining authenticity with structured messaging,
MSEs can position themselves as credible, relatable, and
trustworthy, increasing the likelihood of brand loyalty
and consumer action.
However, several limitations must be acknowledged. The
study focused solely on MSEs within the beauty industry
in Malaysia and may not reflect practices in other
sectors or regions. Additionally, the analysis was limited
to Instagram, excluding other emerging platforms such
as TikTok and YouTube Shorts, where similar strategies
may yield different outcomes.
Future research could explore cross-platform
comparisons to understand how content strategies
differ across media environments. Longitudinal studies
could also assess how influencer collaborations impact
long-term consumer loyalty or brand growth. Further
quantitative studies may provide statistical insights
into the direct influence of specific content types on
consumer behaviour, such as purchase decisions or
conversion rates.
