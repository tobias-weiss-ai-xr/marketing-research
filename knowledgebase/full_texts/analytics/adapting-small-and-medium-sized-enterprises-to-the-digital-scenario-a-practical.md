# Adapting Small and Medium-Sized Enterprises to the Digital Scenario: A Practical Framework for Leveraging Sales

Abstract
Small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) were among the organizations most affected by the acceler-
ation of digitalization that followed the COVID-19 pandemic, yet they often lack the resources, skills and
structures needed to adapt their marketing. This narrative review examines how SMEs can adjust their
business and marketing practices to the digital scenario in order to leverage sales. Peer-reviewed studies
and  reference  works  published  up  to  2023  on  digital  transformation,  digital  marketing,  customer
relationship management and marketing analytics were analyzed qualitatively. The literature shows that
successful SME adaptation tends to follow a staged path: diagnosing the customer journey and the firm’s
digital  maturity,  establishing  an  integrated  digital  presence,  developing  relevant  content  and
engagement, structuring customer relationship management and adopting simple performance indicat-
ors that link marketing actions to sales. Rather than large technology investments, the evidence points to
the consistent use of accessible tools, clear priorities and a test-and-learn culture as the main drivers of
results.  Based  on  these  findings,  the  article  proposes  a  six-stage  practical  framework,  summarizing
objectives,  actions  and  indicators  for  each  stage.  The  framework  is  intended  to  help  managers  and
consultants guide the digital adaptation of SMEs in a structured and measurable way.

1 Introduction
Small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) play a central role in employment and economic activity,
but they typically operate with limited financial resources, small teams and less access to specialized
expertise  than  large  corporations.  These  constraints  shape  how  SMEs  respond  to  technological
change. The acceleration of digitalization during the COVID-19 pandemic made this challenge visible:
many SMEs had to move their sales and communication to digital channels almost overnight in order
to continue operating [1, 2].
Digital marketing offers SMEs opportunities that were previously available only to large firms, such
as reaching specific audiences at low cost, measuring the results of each action and maintaining
continuous relationships with customers [3, 4]. However, the mere presence on digital platforms does
not  guarantee  results.  Without  a  clear  strategy,  SMEs  often  adopt  tools  in  a  fragmented  way,
producing activity without corresponding growth in sales.

This study therefore examines how SMEs can adjust their marketing and business practices to the

1.1 Theoretical background
Digital transformation is commonly described as a process in three phases: digitization, in which
analog information is converted into digital form; digitalization, in which digital technologies are
used to improve existing processes; and digital transformation, in which the business model itself
changes [5]. For most SMEs, the immediate challenge lies in the first two phases, but the long-term
competitive advantage depends on reaching the third.
From a marketing perspective, digital technologies affect the entire process of value creation, from
understanding customers to delivering and sustaining value [3]. Customer relationship management
(CRM) provides a complementary lens: it emphasizes acquiring, retaining and developing profitable
customer relationships over time, supported by information on customer behavior and value  [6].
New media have intensified this logic by multiplying the channels through which customers interact
with firms and with each other [7].
2 Materials and methods
2.1 Review design and source selection
This study adopts a narrative literature-review approach. Peer-reviewed articles and reference books
published up to 2023 were selected on four themes: digital transformation of SMEs, digital marketing
strategy, customer relationship management and marketing analytics. Priority was given to conceptu-
al frameworks and empirical studies that address the practical adaptation of firms, particularly in the
context of the COVID-19 pandemic.
2.2 Framework development
The selected sources were analyzed qualitatively to identify recurring stages, practices and indicators
associated with successful digital adaptation. These elements were then organized into a sequential
framework. The framework is conceptual and is intended as a guide for practice; it does not result
from  new  empirical  testing.  No  ethical  approval  was  required,  as  the  study  relies  exclusively  on
published sources.
3 Results and discussion
3.1 Digital transformation paths in SMEs
Empirical evidence from the pandemic period shows that SMEs followed different digital transforma-
tion paths depending on their initial digital maturity and resources [1]. Firms with little prior digital
experience typically began by moving sales and communication to digital channels, such as social
networks,  messaging  applications  and  marketplaces.  Firms  with  greater  maturity  were  able  to
digitalize internal processes and, in some cases, redesign their business models [1, 8].
Studies  of  family-owned  firms  in  Europe  show  a  similar  pattern:  in  the  face  of  the  crisis,  firms
combined  short-term  measures,  such  as  cost  reduction  and  liquidity  protection,  with  a  rapid
adoption of digital tools to maintain contact with customers [2]. These findings suggest that digital

3.2 Common obstacles
The literature identifies recurring obstacles to digital marketing in firms, many of which are more
acute  in  SMEs.  They  include  limited  analytical  talent,  difficulty  measuring  the  return  on  digital
investments and the challenge of keeping up with the speed of technological change [9]. In addition,
digital  transformation  requires  changes  in  organizational  structures  and  capabilities,  not  only  in
technology, which can be particularly demanding for small teams [5].
These obstacles help explain why SMEs often adopt tools without a clear strategy. Opening social
media profiles or launching an online store without defining target customers, value proposition and
success indicators tends to produce limited results. The framework presented below addresses this
problem by organizing adaptation into stages with defined objectives.
3.3 A practical framework for leveraging sales
Based on the literature, the digital adaptation of SMEs can be organized into six stages, summarized
in  Table 1. The first stage is a diagnosis of the customer journey and of the firm’s digital maturity,
identifying how customers search, evaluate and buy, and where the firm currently stands [5, 10]. The
second stage establishes an integrated digital presence, ensuring that the firm can be found and that
customers can move easily between channels [4, 11].
The  third  stage  concerns  content  and  engagement.  Relevant,  useful  content  builds  trust  and
engagement and supports the customer’s decision process, particularly in markets where purchase
decisions require information  [12]. Social networks and partnerships with creators can extend the
reach of this content when they are consistent with the brand [13, 14].
The fourth stage structures customer relationship management. Recording interactions, segmenting
customers and maintaining regular contact through e-mail, messaging and after-sales service help
increase repurchase and customer value over time  [6, 7] . The fifth stage introduces measurement
through a small set of key performance indicators (KPIs) that connect marketing actions to sales,
such as conversion rate, customer acquisition cost (CAC), retention and customer lifetime value (CLV)
[6,  15].  The  sixth  stage  institutionalizes  continuous  improvement  through  testing,  learning  and
reallocating resources to the actions that produce the best results [5].

3.4 Discussion
The framework emphasizes sequence and consistency rather than the adoption of advanced techno-
logies. This is consistent with evidence that SMEs advance through digital transformation in stages
and that early gains come from using accessible tools coherently  [1]. It also reflects the view that
digital  marketing  creates  value  when  it  is  integrated  with  the  firm’s  strategy  and  customer
relationships, rather than treated as a separate communication activity [3, 16].
An important implication concerns measurement. Many SMEs evaluate digital marketing through
activity  metrics,  such  as  followers  or  posts,  which  do  not  necessarily  reflect  commercial  results.
Linking actions to indicators such as conversion, acquisition cost and customer lifetime value allows
managers to decide where to invest limited resources and to justify those decisions [6, 15]. Equally
important is the human dimension: SMEs often compete on proximity and personalized service, and
digital tools should reinforce, not replace, these advantages [16].
3.5 Limitations
The framework is derived from a narrative review and has not been empirically validated across
sectors. SMEs differ widely in size, industry, resources and digital maturity, so the stages may need to
be  adapted,  combined  or  reordered  in  specific  contexts.  Future  empirical  studies  could  test  the
framework and measure its effect on sales performance.
4 Conclusion
SMEs face the digital scenario with fewer resources than large firms, but they can achieve meaningful
sales gains by adopting digital marketing in a structured way. The literature indicates that successful
adaptation follows a staged path that begins with understanding customers and the firm’s own digital
maturity  and  progresses  toward  integrated  channels,  relevant  content,  structured  customer
relationships and measurement linked to sales.
The six-stage framework proposed in this article translates these findings into a practical sequence of
objectives,  actions  and  indicators.  Its  central  message  is  that  consistency,  prioritization  and
continuous learning matter more than the volume of tools adopted. Future research should evaluate
the  framework  empirically  and  examine  how  SMEs  in  different  sectors  can  accelerate  their
progression through the stages.
