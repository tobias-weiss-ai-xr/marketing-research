Abstract

Social media has evolved from a promotional channel into a measurable source of consumer and
campaign intelligence for e-commerce firms. This study examines the impact of social media marketing
analytics on consumer purchase intention, with emphasis on personalized advertising, online reviews and
ratings, influencer credibility, promotional offers, and customer engagement. A descriptive and
analytical, quantitative and applied research design is used. Primary data were collected through a
structured questionnaire from 120 respondents, mainly students and young professionals aged 18 to 30
years who regularly use social media and online shopping platforms. The study uses percentage analysis,
mean-score analysis, ranking, and correlation -based interpretation on  a five -point Likert scale. The
supplied results show that online reviews and ratings have the highest mean score (4.25; Rank 1),
followed by personalized advertisements (4.10; Rank 2), influencer credibility (3.95; Rank 3),
promotional offers (3.88; Rank 4), and customer engagement (3.78; Rank 5). The findings indicate that
social proof and relevant personalized communication are particularly important in shaping purchase
intention. The paper concludes that analytics -driven social media marketing can suppo rt more informed
targeting, campaign monitoring, and customer -engagement decisions when consumer data are used
ethically and transparently.

Introduction
Social media has become a central element of the digital customer journey in e -commerce. Consumers encounter product information,
advertisements, reviews, influencer recommendations, promotional messages, and brand interactions across platforms before rea ching a
purchase decision. For firms, these interactions also generate measurable data. Reach, engagement, click -through activity, conversion
behavior, audience characteristics, comments, reviews, and sentiment can be analyzed to understand how customers re spond to marketing
communication.
The managerial value of social media therefore extends beyond content distribution. Marketing analytics enables e -commerce firms to
compare campaign performance, identify responsive customer segments, personalize messages, and monitor the relationship betw een
communication activity and customer response. This is especially relevant in highly competitive online markets, where firms m ay spend
substantially on digital promotion without knowing which marketing factors have the strongest influence on purchase in tention.
Purchase intention in e -commerce is influenced by a combination of informational, relational, and promotional cues. Online reviews can
provide social proof and reduce perceived purchase risk; personalized advertising can increase relevance; credible influe ncers may affect
brand preference; promotional offers can accelerate decisions; and visible customer engagement can strengthen brand recall an d trust.
Analytics provides a mechanism for evaluating these factors rather than relying only on intuition.
Problem, Significance and Scope
The central problem addressed in this study is that social media investment does not automatically produce effective marketin g outcomes.
Without appropriate analytics, campaign activity can become costly, fragmented, and difficult to evaluate. The study th erefore investigates
how analytics-driven social media marketing activities influence consumer purchase intention and identifies the factors that appear most
important for improving conversion-related decisions.
The study is significant for e-commerce marketers because data-driven decisions can improve audience targeting, message personalization,
campaign monitoring, and resource allocation. Its scope is limited to purchase intention in e -commerce among young cons umers, mainly
students and young professionals who frequently use social media and online shopping applications. The analysis focuses speci fically on
personalized advertisements, influencer credibility, customer engagement, online reviews, and promotional offers.
Objectives, Research Questions and Hypotheses
The study has five objectives: to study the role of social media marketing analytics in e -commerce marketing; identify the major social
media factors influencing consumer purchase intention; analyze the relationship between personalized advertising, engage ment, reviews,
influencer credibility, and purchase intention; understand how business analytics supports digital marketing decisions; and suggest strategies
for improving social media marketing effectiveness in e-commerce.
The research questions examine how social media marketing analytics influences consumer purchase intention, which social medi a factor
has the strongest influence, and how e-commerce companies can use business analytics to improve marketing performance. The hypotheses
are retained as originally specified: H1 states that social media marketing analytics has a positive influence on consumer purchase intention
in e-commerce, while H0 states that social media marketing analytics has no significant influence on c onsumer purchase intention in e -
commerce.
Literature Review and Research Gap
Digital marketing literature positions social media as both a communication environment and a measurable customer -contact channel.
Chaffey and Ellis -Chadwick (2019) provide a strategic framework for digital marketing in which customer interaction and perfo rmance
measurement are closely linked, while Ryan (2020) emphasizes the importance of engaging audiences through digital channels. W ithin e-
commerce, this perspective is relevant because the consumer can move rapidly from exposure and evaluation to transac tion.
Consumer-behavior concepts also help explain why different social media signals may affect purchase intention. Solomon (2020) discusses
the importance of social and psychological influences in buying behavior, while Kotler, Keller, and Chernev (2022) place  customer value,
communication, and market response within the wider marketing -management process. In the present study, online reviews and influencer
recommendations function as forms of social information, while personalized advertising and promotional o ffers represent targeted
marketing stimuli.
The analytics perspective is equally important. Davenport and Harris (2017) emphasize the competitive role of analytics in or ganizational
decision-making, and Malhotra (2020) provides a methodological basis for structured marketing research and quantitativ e interpretation.
Applied to social media, analytics can help firms segment audiences, evaluate campaign performance, monitor sentiment, and co mpare
customer responses across marketing activities.

Research Methodology
Research Design and Data Sources
The study follows a descriptive and analytical research design and is quantitative and applied in nature. The descriptive com ponent
summarizes consumer responses toward selected social media marketing activities, while the analytical component compares the  relative
importance of the selected factors in relation to purchase intention. Primary data were collected through a structured questi onnaire.
Secondary information was drawn from books, research papers, websites, articles, and reports related to digital marketing, social media
analytics, business analytics, and consumer behavior.
Sampling, Instrument and Analysis
Convenience sampling was used to select respondents who actively use social media and e -commerce platforms. The sample size is 120
respondents, mainly in the 18 –30 age group and drawn from the student and young professional segment. The questionnaire conta ins
demographic items and Likert -scale statements related to social media marketing, personalization, influencer credibility, reviews,
engagement, and purchase intention.
The study uses percentage analysis, mean-score analysis, ranking, and correlation-based interpretation. Responses are interpreted on a five-
point Likert scale ranging from strongly disagree to strongly agree. The source document does not report a correlati on coefficient, test
statistic, confidence interval, or p-value; accordingly, this journal version does not introduce any additional inferential statistics and retains
the supplied descriptive interpretation.

Findings and Discussion
The principal finding is that social media marketing analytics is positively associated with consumer purchase intention in the study. Online
reviews and ratings emerge as the strongest factor, followed by personalized advertisements. This ordering is mana gerially meaningful
because it suggests that purchase intention among the sampled young consumers is shaped strongly by both social validation an d message
relevance.
The role of reviews can be interpreted through uncertainty reduction. In e -commerce, customers cannot physically inspect many products
before purchase, so ratings and prior customer experiences can function as decision cues. The high mean score for reviews  indicates that
monitoring review quality, sentiment, and recurring customer concerns should be an important component of social media and e-commerce
analytics.
Personalized advertising also performs strongly. Analytics allows marketers to use behavioral and interest -related information to make
communication more relevant. However, the value of personalization must be balanced with privacy and transparency. Excessive or poorly
governed use of customer data can undermine trust even when personalization improves short -term relevance.
Influencer credibility ranks below reviews and personalized advertising but remains important for brand trust and preference.  The study
indicates that influencer marketing is more effective when the influencer is perceived as credible and authentic. For e-commerce firms, this
implies that influencer selection should be evaluated using audience fit and sentiment rather than reach alone.
Promotional offers can accelerate buying decisions, particularly among young consumers, while engagement contributes to brand recall and
trust. From a business analytics perspective, these factors should not be managed independently. Dashboards and campaig n-monitoring
systems can connect engagement, sentiment, audience segments, promotional response, and conversion-related indicators to provide a more
complete view of marketing performance.
The source paper accepts H1 because the analyzed factors show a positive influence on purchase intention. This conclusion is retained. At
the same time, because the supplied document does not provide the numerical correlation output or an inferential signi ficance test, the
present journal version does not claim a specific effect size or p-value beyond the evidence reported in the study.
Managerial Recommendations
The findings support a data -driven approach to social media marketing. E -commerce firms should use analytics dashboards to monitor
engagement, conversion, and campaign performance; design personalized advertisements around customer interests and browsing behavior;
and actively monitor online reviews and ratings because they are the highest-ranked factor in the study.

Companies should also collaborate with credible influencers whose audience and communication style align with the brand, apply sentiment
analysis to comments and reviews, and target promotional offers to relevant customer segments rather than using uniform offers. Customer
data should be handled ethically with appropriate privacy protection and transparency. These practices can help translate soc ial media
activity into more disciplined marketing decisions and support customer conversion and retention.
Conclusion, Limitations and Future Research
This study concludes that social media marketing analytics plays an important role in understanding consumer purchase intenti on in e -
commerce. Among the five factors evaluated, online reviews and ratings receive the highest mean score, followed by personal ized
advertisements, influencer credibility, promotional offers, and customer engagement. The results therefore emphasize the impo rtance of
social proof, relevance, trust, promotional value, and interaction in analytics -driven digital marketing.
The study is subject to the limitations specified in the source document. The sample is limited to 120 respondents and focuse s mainly on
young consumers; convenience sampling may introduce bias; the analysis is restricted to e-commerce and social media marketing activities;
actual company-level sales and campaign data were not available; and respondent opinions may change over time with platform trends and
promotional conditions.
Future research can use larger and more geographically diverse samples, compare platforms such as Instagram, YouTube, Faceboo k, and
LinkedIn, and incorporate company -level measures including click -through rate, conversion rate, customer lifetime value, and  purchase
history. Advanced approaches such as sentiment analysis, clustering, predictive modeling, and artificial-intelligence-based recommendation
systems may also provide deeper insight into digital marketing performance.
