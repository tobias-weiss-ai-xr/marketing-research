Abstract: This study presents a systematic literature review (SLR) on the evolution of
marketing mix strategies over the past decade, with a focus on how traditional
frameworks have been adapted in response to d igital transformation, sustainability
demands, and shifting consumer behavior. The review follows PRISMA guidelines and
analyzes 54 peer -reviewed articles published between 2015 and 2025, sourced from
Scopus and other reputable academic databases. The anal ysis employs the TCCM
(Theory, Context, Characteristics, Methodology) framework to classify findings across
theoretical foundations, research contexts, thematic focus, and methodological
approaches.The results indicate that classical marketing mix models, particularly the
4Ps and 7Ps, remain dominant in both academic research and practical application,
especially in emerging economies such as China, Indonesia, Malaysia, and Iran.
Quantitative methodologies, particularly structural equation modeling, are sti ll widely
used; however, there is a growing shift toward mixed -method and qualitative
approaches that provide richer contextual insights. Emerging themes include green
marketing, digital marketing, artificial intelligence applications, and sustainability -
oriented strategies, reflecting an expanding research agenda beyond traditional
marketing boundaries.Despite these developments, several gaps remain evident,
including limited theoretical integration, insufficient longitudinal research designs, and
underrepresentation of specific sectors such as agriculture and cultural industries.
This review synthesizes fragmented literature and proposes future research directions
emphasizing theoretical pluralism, integration of Service -Dominant Logic and
Dynamic Capabilities, and methodological innovation through big data analytics and
experimental designs. Overall, this study provides a comprehensive synthesis that
contributes to both academic development and managerial practice in evolving
marketing environments.

INTRODUCTION
The marketing mix has regained significant attention in marketing literature as both a
theoretical framework and a practical business tool relevant in the context of accelerating
digitalization, increasing environmental awareness, and shifting consumer preferences. The initial
concept introduced by McCarthy (1960) through the 4P model —product, price, place, and
promotion—has become the core foundation of modern marketing strategy. Over time, this concept
has expanded into services marketing contexts (Lau, 2015), experience -based consumption (Batat,
2022), and integration within physical and digital spaces (phygital environment). These developments
indicate that the marketing mix is adaptive to changes in a dynamic business environment.
A number of studies show that the marketing mix is also theoretically flexible because it can be
integrated with various other mode ls. Chen (2018) combines the marketing mix with the IS Success
Model to understand mobile commerce behavior, while Houghtaling, Serrano, Dobson, et al. (2019)
apply it in consumer decision -making models in the food retail sector. This demonstrates that the

marketing mix functions not only as a descriptive framework but also as an analytical model for
understanding consumer behavior.
Nevertheless, the development of marketing mix literature still shows a relatively high level of
fragmentation. Previous studi es have largely focused on specific themes such as green marketing
(Mahmoud et al., 2024), digital engagement (Katsikeas et al., 2019), and the metaverse (Yadav et al.,
2024), but have not yet produced an integrated theoretical synthesis. This fragmentatio n is further
reinforced by the dominance of quantitative approaches, which tend to emphasize statistical
generalization but provide limited contextual and temporal depth.
In addition, marketing mix literature is still dominated by studies conducted in deve loped
countries, resulting in a knowledge gap regarding its implementation in developing countries and
different cultural contexts. Fuchs and Köstner (2016) emphasize that geographical context
differences influence marketing strategies, meaning that findin gs from developed countries cannot
always be generalized to developing countries.
On the other hand, developments in digital technology, artificial intelligence, and data analytics
have transformed how the marketing mix is implemented. Pantano et al. (2019 ) show that social
media significantly influences consumer decisions, while Batat (2022) develops the experiential
marketing mix model (7E), which emphasizes emotional experience within a phygital environment.
This highlights that digital transformation ha s expanded the conceptual boundaries of the marketing
mix.
However, the integration of digitalization, sustainability, and theoretical diversity in marketing
mix studies has not yet been systematically established. Therefore, a synthetic approach capable o f
integrating these dimensions comprehensively is needed. This study seeks to address this gap
through a mapping of marketing mix literature using the TCCM framework approach.
Based on this context, this study proposes several key research questions: what are the global
patterns and sectoral differences in marketing mix studies; to what extent can methodological
approaches capture temporal and contextual changes; which theories are dominant and still contain
gaps; and how do digital, green, and experiential  marketing strategies interact with traditional
marketing mix models across various industrial contexts?
By comparing classical approaches such as the 4Ps and 7Ps with modern developments such as
the 7Es and sustainability -based models, this study not only  summarizes the evolution of the
literature but also identifies conceptual tensions and new directions in marketing mix research.
Ultimately, this study is expected to contribute to the development of a more inclusive, adaptive, and
relevant integrative framework in response to changes in the global business environment.

To ensure transparency and rigorous application, the present review was con ducted according
to the PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta -Analyses) guidelines, as
recommended by (Moher et al., 2009). This phase of the work spanned four main steps identification,
screening, eligibility determination and inclusion which were all conducted carefully in terms of
methodological coherence and conceptual transparentness.
The identification stage commenced with a systematic search of the Scopus database, as the
database was confirmed to contain a high standard of peer -reviewed academic work. This choice
was not random, as Scopus provides good protection against duplication and suspect publication
(Ana Rocha. (2023) ; Hariningsih, E., Haryanto, B., Wahyudi, L., & Sugiarto, C. (2024) ; Lasda Bergman
EM (2012) . The search resulted in 67 initial records, which were obtained from the use of the
keywords “Marketing Mix Strategy” and “Strategy Marketing Mix,” whose selection was intentional
for their accuracy and consistency with the resear ch field. A first filter was implemented: 17 articles
were excluded as they did not fit within publication dates from 2015 to 2025; 12 were removed by a
magazine ranking lower than the Q4 cut‐off; and one of them did not present the abstract. Curiously,
no redundant hits were found one of the many manifestations of how well-kept the database is. This
procedure reduced the pool to 37 articles that could potentially be eligible for inclusion.

In the screening phase, the remaining studies were re-reviewed more specifically to judge their
methodological quality and thematic relevance. Although we did not exclude any of them at this
point, a viable limitation appeared; the full text of 14 articles was not obtainable. Rather than leaving
these gaps to undermine the review we tried to be supplementary and proactive with other sources.
We also found 32 further studies by referring to reference harvest, institutional repositories and
citation tracking. These additional sources after validation were included in the reservoir, thus greatly
enhancing the dataset.
The eligibility check consisted of a review of all 54 relevant articles (22 results from Scopus
plus 32 from other sources) using predefined criteria for thematic relevance, methodological
transparency, and theor etical background. Only one paper was excluded after close inspection
because it clearly did not fit the primary aims of the review. Consequently, 54 studies were finally
included. The process of inclusion ended up with a rich and varied corpus of 54 artic les in peer
reviewed journals. This choice represents a trade -off between rigor (on the one side) and
completeness and representativeness (on the other side).
A thematic and interpretive synthesis was completed following inclusion to identify common
themes, theoretical perspectives and methods. This step was facilitated by the Watase Uake System
(Wahyudi, 2020)   to guarantee the coherence of data extraction and the tracking of coding. The
synthesis focused on the provided context which provided greater comprehension of marketing mix
synthesis and of temporal factors in various industries, geographics, and periods.
Furthermore, to grasp recently emerged trends and transitions in research foci, a temporal
trend analysis was conducted to explore changes in publication years, theoretical preferences, and
research methodologies. This review recognized a clear rise in marketing mix studies in the context
of global disruption, with the COVID -19 pandemic particularly evident in the focus on digitalization
and adaptive marketing efforts.
Findings were presented in bar charts, summary tables and conceptual maps to ease
accessibility and interpretation facilitating a clearer understanding of the research questions that
predominate across marketing mix research topics, methods, regions and theories.
This methodological protocol is not only a mechanical PRISMA -compliant piece the series of
reporting items has been planned in a cautious and thoughtful manner for systematic re viewing.
Through strict eligibility criteria and careful supplementation and synthesis, this review provides a
credible and well -built platform to further the scholarly conversation regarding the marketing mix
strategies in the wake of digital acceleration  and sustainability imperatives (Ana Rocha. (2023) ;
Hariningsih, E., Haryanto, B., Wahyudi, L., & Sugiarto, C. (2024) ; Lasda Bergman EM (2012) ; Moher

Interestingly, the Traditional Marketing Mix (4Ps/7Ps) continues to domi nate, appearing in
39.2% of the analyzed articles. This enduring prevalence is particularly prominent in studies focused
on developing markets and sectors such as education, tourism, and healthcare (Dadzie et al., 2017) ;
(Nikbin et al., 2022) . The consistency of this model's application may reflect its practical simplicity,
adaptability, and pedagogical legacy factors that continue to appeal to both academics and
practitioners. Despite frequent criticism for being overly static, its enduring re levance signals a
strong anchoring point in foundational marketing strategy.
Equally noteworthy is the rise of Sustainability -Based Marketing and Green Marketing, which
together comprise approximately 13% of the reviewed studies. These approaches indicate a shift
toward ethically conscious and environmentally responsible marketing philosophies. For instance,
studies by (Tudu & Mishra, 2025)  and (Mahmoud et al., 2024)  reveal how brands are increasingly
aligning their value propositions with sustainability narratives to meet the expectat ions of socially
aware consumers.
Moreover, the growing prominence of Digital Marketing and social media Marketing
collectively accounting for around 11% of the literature highlights the significant digital
transformation of marketing landscapes. Scholars such as (Pantano et al., 2019)  and  (Zhang, 2024)
have shown how digital platforms, data-driven insights, and real-time engagement are redefining the

Although less prevalent, Innovation-Based Marketing and AI-Driven Marketing are emerging as

The evidence suggests that while traditional paradigms remain robust, marketing strategy as a
field is undergoing a notable shift. Scholars and practitioners are progressively integrating
technology, ethics, innovation, and personalization into their strategic frameworks. This ongoing

Analytical Framework
This review applies the TCCM framework as Theory, Context, Characteristics, and
Methodology to systematically classify and interpret the body of literature.
Theoretical Foundations (T)
Approximately 72% of the reviewed studies are grounded in the classical 4Ps and 7Ps models
(Booms and Bitner (1981), n.d.; McCarthy, E.J., n.d.) .  These models continue to serve as the default
theoretical foundation across numerous industries. However, recent studies increasingly incorporate
complementary perspectives, including the Resource -Based View (RBV), the Theory of Planned
Behaviour (TPB), and the Information Systems Success Model. Such integration s aim to address
contemporary complexities ranging from digital interactivity to sustainable value creation (R. Al -
Dmour et al., 2024; Chen, 2018; Giantari & Sukaatmadja, 2021) . Nonetheless, comprehensive
theoretical synthesis remains limited.
Contextual Applications (C)
A pronounced geographic concentration in Southeast and South Asia  notably Indonesia, Iran,
and India suggests that emerging markets have become important testing grounds for marketing mix
frameworks. These contexts often necessitate adaptations focused on accessibility, digital readiness,
and localized promotion strategies (Solimun & Fernandes, 2018). In contrast, studies from developed
economies tend to focus on more advanced themes such as AI -enabled personalization, immersive
digital environments, and customer co -creation (Hermann & Puntoni, 2025; Houghtaling, Serrano,
Dobson, et al., 2019).
Empirical Characteristics (C)
Customer satisfaction, loyalty, and purchase intention dominate as outcome variables.

Discussions
Our aim in this SLR is to offer a comprehensive and nuanced view of how marketing mix
strategies have adapted to fast technological development, requiring new sustainability imperatives
and demanding consumer behavior. Notwithstanding the 4Ps and 7Ps underpinning a lot of
discourse added up to much of the review, there exists a clear indication of a movement towards
hybrid and context-bound implementation of marketing. These developments are critically discussed
in the following, before being located within the broader academic context and their consequences
articulated.
Despite relentless attacks on their stiffness, the classic 4Ps and 7Ps models (McCarthy, E.J.,
1960); Booms and Bitner (1981) still dominate marketing literature, especially in the perspective of
developing countries. This perseverance is not unfounded, these frameworks do provide an
organized direction which is helpful for le ss infrastructure filled and resources lean environments
(Dadzie et al., 2017; Solimun & Fernandes, 2018) . In this sense, th e conclusions of this study are
consistent with (Kotler, P., & Keller, K. L. (2021)  in that the value of such models as a planning  tool
continues to be relevant. But this persistence is also an indication of a reluctance to accept paradigm
change at least in the places where technological change is demanding it.
However, it is noteworthy to consider the geographical breakdown of sear ches which reveals
striking distinctions in strategic approach. At the same time, in developing economies, researchers
typically emphasize accessibility, affordability, and function of organic smallholders mirroring country
context specific economic structure and development policy priorities. In stark contrast, researchers
in developed economies address more advanced topics, including personalization through AI,
immersive brand experiences in the metaverse, and generative AI in customer journeys (Batat, 2022)
;  (Hermann & Puntoni, 2025). This dichotomy highlights the contextual nature of marketing strategy
practice and points to a need for more comparative, cross -regional analysis that stretches beyond
narrow thematic silos.
Methodologically, it is noteworthy that more than half of the included studies (almost 60%) are
quantitative, in particular, to SEM or PLS-SEM. Although these approaches undoubtedly enhance the
empirical robustness and theoretical probing (Mahmoud et al., 2024) , they are not always equipped
to grasp the deeper contextual and longitudinal layers. Efficient as such methodological conservatism
might be, it could serve to stifle innovation. In contrast, very few qualitative and mixed -method
studies were in the sample like (Batat, 2022) and (Hailu et al., 2021)  offer richer, more interpretive
insights, particularly into  new topics such as co -creation, customer experience, and sustainability.

This imbalance illustrates a wider disciplinary tension: how to balance generalizability with contextual

One of the very interesting contributions of this review is this f ocus on theoretical pluralism.
Although most of the research still stick to traditional marketing mix, a significant number are
starting to adopt the Resource -Based View (RBV), Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB), and Game
Theory (Abedian et al., 2022b; R. Al -Dmour et al., 2024; Giantari & Sukaatmadja, 2021) . Yet the
combination of these two views is still in its infancy. In contrast to this previous work that has mainly
reported on these interventions with no critical engagement, this article contends for a more
deliberate and theoretically -grounded synthesis.  For example, integrating TPB and marketing mix
components might help gain new insights on consumer behavior particularly in the ethical or
sustainability context. Second, RBV contributes to marketing conversation by uncovering the internal
capabilities that lead to strategic advantage in a digital environments.
On top of this all, sectoral analysis complicates matters further. Retail and service sectors are
predominating in the literature, which is supposed to be related to the citizen -facing work and the
data access, although there should be an increase of fields such as agriculture and healthcare, which
are still underrepresented (Eggers, W. D., & O’Leary, J. (2017) ; Moraes M, Gountas J, Gountas S,
Sharma P (2019). This is particularly surprising considering their growing imp ortance to global
sustainability initiatives (Hailu et al., 2021; Tudu & Mishra, 2025) . Our results indicate a potential
variation in the marketing mix strategies of these two industries, thus, subsequent investigation
should be directed in this regard. Most importantly, such sectoral imbalances also raise quest ions
about potential biases in funding of research, data accessibility and publication outlet.
Technology has imprinted itself on the marketing mix, but unevenly. E -commerce and social
media are at the heart for the digital strategy, especially for the MSMEs and service providers in the
post-COVID-19 era (Chen, 2018; Nikbin et al., 2022) . However, there is a conceptual dissonance in
developing these platforms within integrated marketing mix frameworks.  Studies may be examining
discreet tactics like Instagram promotions or WhatsApp engagement without taking the time to
thoroughly rewire elements, such as “Place” or “Promotion,” to reflect the omnichannel world we
inhabit. Thus, our review not only adds w eight to such observations but takes them one step further
proposing to develop integrative models that integrate legacy with digital ecosystems.
Consistent with temporal patterns, peaks in number of publications in 2019 and 2021
correspond to temporal global upheavals (ecopolitical unrest and COVID -19 outbreak). Such events
served as a stimulus for innovation and adaptation in marketing strategies, as reflected by the
increased focus on agile and crisis -responsive advertising (Houghtaling, Serrano, Dobson, et al.,
2019; Nikbin et al., 2022). Of course, these temporal peaks also suggest that academic responses do
get "up to speed" regarding real -world change, which is a promising indicator of relevance and
responsiveness.
Using the TCCM framework, this review weaves together disparate scholarship. In relation to
the science, it criticizes the naivety f the dominance of the classical paradigms and calls for the
inclusion of models like Dynamic Capabilities (Teece et al., 1998) and Service-Dominant Logic (Lusch
& Vargo, 2014) . These frames offer a more dynamic and relational lens, well -suited to tumultuous,
tech-powered markets. It hig hlights the geographic and sectoral blind spots of Context, draws
attention to the risk of sampling strategy that is limited and not inclusive enough and raises some
methodological questions.

creative, change-oriented, and downstream marketing effects.
CONCLUSION
The present systematic review aimed at evaluating, in a critical fashion, how marketing mix
strategies has change Although 4Ps/7Ps concepts continue to provide a structural base, their
explanatory force seems to be increasingly limited by the fast pace of technological change, evolving
consumer values and increasing sustainability pressures.
Specifically, the narrative logic of this review, which is reminiscent of (Bolton & Houlihan,
2009), r ather than the fragmented sub -themes concerning digital marketing, sustainability and
diversity of contexts, which has been the focus of earlier syntheses, is what is new, as it is seeking to
weave these into a fluid assemblage that disrupts the formal log ics of classical marketing. In the
process, this review adds a level of nuance to prior evidence by showing how the digitalization and
green-push are not peripheral recalibrations, but rather central drivers reconfiguring strategic
priorities in both sectors and geographies.
The contribution of the review at the theoretical level is thus the demand for a more pluralistic
and flexible model. It urges future researchers to transcend the traditional overreliance on the
marketing mix as a standalone archetype and seek to incorporate untapped views, namely, Resource-
Based View, Dynamic Capabilities Theory and Service -Dominant Logic. This integration may provide
a more comprehensive understanding of how firms mutually create value in turbulent and
information-rich contexts.
In practice, cross -sectionally focused quantitative methods have offered the advantage of
generalizability at the expense of depth and context. Significantly, the increased interest in
interpretive and mixed methods research represents a promisi ng change, albeit an underused one.
