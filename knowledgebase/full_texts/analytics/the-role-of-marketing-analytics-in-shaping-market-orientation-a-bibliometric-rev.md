Abstract
This study explores the intersection of marketing analytics and market orientation in the digital age through a com-
prehensive bibliometric analysis. By analyzing 238 articles published between 2007 and 2023 from the Web of Science
database, we identify trends, patterns, and research gaps in this rapidly evolving field. Our methodology combines
performance analysis and science mapping to provide a systematic review of the way in which marketing analyt-
ics shapes market orientation strategies. The findings reveal a significant increase in research attention over the past
decade but also highlight substantial gaps in general understanding of the ways in which data-driven market orienta-
tion impacts business performance. We identify key research opportunities in areas such as B2B big data analytics,
innovation and internationalization through analytics, and the development of data-driven capabilities. The study
makes both theoretical and practical contributions, synthesizing current knowledge and identifying future avenues
of research. For practitioners, it offers insights into ways of leveraging analytical tools for enhanced market orienta-
tion, while for researchers, it maps unexplored territories in the field. In particular, the analysis emphasizes the need
for a more in-depth investigation into big data analytics capabilities, customer analytics capabilities, and their strategic
value in market orientation. It provides a foundation for understanding the evolving relationship between marketing
analytics and market orientation in contemporary business environments.

Introduction
Market orientation (MO) has been a foundational con -
cept in strategic management and marketing literature
for decades. At its core, MO is a strategic approach
that centers on generating and utilizing market intel -
ligence to understand both the expressed and latent
needs of customers, as well as the capabilities and strat -
egies of competitors [1]. This market intelligence serves
as a foundation for guiding innovation initiatives [2] and
drives sales growth through the cultivation of strong rela-
tionships with key customers and identifying new market
opportunities. According to Narver and Slater [1], busi -
nesses that exhibit a high degree of MO tend to achieve
greater profitability.
Marketing, by contrast, represents the practical imple -
mentation of the MO principles [3]. Thus, MO provides
the philosophical foundations and strategic direction that
shapes marketing activities [4]. It embodies a strategic
customer-centered posture and a set of behaviors focused
on creating greater value [5]. Moreover, an organization’s
capacity to continuously generate intelligence about cus -
tomers’ expressed and latent needs, and then figure out
how to address them, is essential to creating greater cus -
tomer value [6].

Similarly, a market-oriented company adopts an
outside-in approach to formulating and implement -
ing strategy decisions, by embedding an iterative learn -
ing process that begins with external market signals and
works inward throughout the firm [7]. Marketing analyt -
ics (MA) operationalizes this approach by systematically
gathering and analyzing customer data to inform market-
ing decisions and enhance the effectiveness of marketing
strategies and campaigns.
While the foundational concept of MO [1, 8] empha -
sizes customer focus, current interpretations also incor -
porate considerations of new technologies related to
digital and marketing analytics [9]. Within the digital
paradigm, new technology tools play a central role by
enabling marketers to understand customer behaviors,
preferences, and purchase patterns with greater preci -
sion [10]. Scholarship by Sivarajah et al. [11] and De Luca
et  al. [12] has emphasized the role of big data as a key
driver for value generation through strategic orientation
in both business and marketing activities. The impact
of big data has been transformative, reshaping the mar -
keting landscape significantly across both B2C and B2B
domains [13]. However, while big data provides market -
ers with an unprecedented volume of information, MA
assumes a role in filtering, processing, and interpreting
this data to extract actionable insights that drive strategic
marketing decisions.
This study views MA as a critical marketing function,
encompassing systematic data collection, management,
analysis, and reporting in information-rich environ -
ments. The inclusion of data from traditional and digital
marketing enhances analytical potential and advances the
field of MA [14]. MA is currently central to intelligence
generation for marketing performance enhancement,
providing insights to foster customer loyalty, measur -
ing customer lifetime value, and optimizing marketing
investment to maximize customer interactions [15].
Overall, the relationship between big data and MA
is symbiotic. By integrating the two, organizations
can adopt more data-driven marketing strategies that
improve effectiveness and efficiency. Cao et al. [16] show
that big data and MA can serve as an accelerator of MO
implementation, reinforcing their strategic importance
in contemporary marketing practices. Similarly, Gnizy
[17] argues that digital marketing capabilities such as
data-driven decision-making, prediction, and real-time
responsiveness may provide firms with a competitive
advantage that cannot be fully replicated by traditional
approaches.
Substantial development of the MA concept has gen -
erated a large and fragmented body of the literature. For
this reason, a systematic and comprehensive examina -
tion of its evolution and its role within MO framework
is required. This study seeks to provide a comprehen -
sive framework for understanding the current state and
future path of the MA–MO interface. In this context,
we propose the following overall research question:
RQ: How has academic literature evolved regard -
ing the integration of MA within MO frameworks for
strategic decision-making? To address this overarching
question, the study is structured around the following
sub-questions:
RQ1: What trends can be identified in the publication
and citation of MA research within the MO domain?
RQ2: What are the key concepts studied and the
intellectual structure of the intersection between MA
and MO?
RQ3: What is the future research agenda for MA in
the MO domain?
This research contributes to the existing literature
in several ways. Very significantly, it provides a com -
prehensive and systematic review of the rapidly evolv -
ing MA field within the wider MO context. It offers
empirical insights that clarify the conceptual interface
between MA and MO. In particular, it shows whether
MA is treated as a peripheral tool or as a fundamen -
tal component of modern MO. Determining this level
of MA–MO integration is crucial for rethinking MO in
terms of the evolving digital transformation.
This analysis is of key theoretical and practical impor -
tance, since it contributes to a more coherent concep -
tual framework that sets the boundaries and defines the
synergies of these two disciplines, thereby enriching
academic discourse. The study also plots a clear path
for future research, ensuring that forthcoming studies
can build on and extend the current body of knowledge.
The findings of this study should therefore be invalu -
able not only for scholars seeking to advance academic
discourse but also for practitioners wanting to imple -
ment data-driven strategies in real-world business
environments, ultimately guiding both academia and
industry toward a more insightful, efficient, and cus -
tomer-centered future.
We begin by describing the interface between MA
and MO and go on to present the methodology used to
show further fundamental contributions of MA analyt -
ics in the MO field. We then set out the results of these
analyses on the origins and trends of MA in MO and
discuss possible future research. We conclude with
some final remarks and a discussion of the limitations
of our study.
Theoretical foundations
MO and MA
The conceptualization of MO by Kohli and Jaworski [8]
and Narver and Slater [1] launched an abundant corpus

of empirical research. Narver and Slater [1] proposed
three important components for MO, namely customer
orientation, competitor orientation, and inter-functional
coordination.
Rokkan [18] reconceptualizes MO as a strategic typol -
ogy, in which orientation is selected according to specific
market conditions and organizational context. He iden -
tifies three distinct forms of MO: customer-centric (or
customer-oriented), innovation-oriented (aligned with
inter-functional coordination), and competitor-oriented
(or copycat) strategies. Each form reflects a unique com -
petitive logic and strategic focus, and in some cases, one
strategy may complement another. The customer-centric
strategy is fundamentally relational, viewing customer
relationships as sources of mutual value creation in which
close interactions provide market insights that enable
co-creation and shared growth. By contrast, the innova -
tion-oriented strategy emphasizes the creation of value
through new product offerings based on an understand -
ing of emerging or latent customer needs, relying on
cross-functional collaboration and external stakeholder
engagement to generate novel solutions and competitive
differentiation. Finally, the competitor-oriented strategy
operates to a fundamentally different logic, aligned with
cost leadership, where the value proposition centers on
offering basic lower-cost products, replicating or adapt -
ing existing market solutions to meet customer needs.
The three market orientation strategies share sev -
eral foundational principles. First, they are united by
an underlying premise that competitive advantage is
contingent upon the firm’s ability to deliver superior
value to customers. Second, they emphasize the criti -
cal role played by market intelligence—the development
of insights from data for making marketing decisions
[19]—in enabling firms to understand customer needs
effectively. By gathering and analyzing such data, market-
ers can gain deeper insights into customer psychology
and purchasing behaviors, thereby facilitating the devel -
opment of highly personalized and targeted marketing
strategies [20].
Driven by big data and advances in artificial intelligence
(AI), MA has emerged as one of the most significant and
rapidly growing technological innovations in marketing
research [21]. Novel digital technologies have led to the
emergence of new analytical methods that facilitate the
creation, sharing, and utilization of market intelligence,
which are all activities that are foundational to MO [8].
Wedel & Kannan [22] argue that MA is crucial in this
context, because it provides the metrics, tools, and ana -
lytical frameworks needed to enhance data-driven mar -
keting processes and support strategic decision-making.
Likewise, Fan et  al. [19] offer practical guidance for
making marketing decisions based on big data analytics
through a dedicated marketing mix framework for big
data management.
Data serve a dual function, both in shaping marketing
strategies and in evaluating their effectiveness [23]. By
providing insights into competitor activities, consumer
behaviors and patterns, and general market trends, data
can reveal opportunities for creating innovative products
and services or exploring new market segments. Com -
panies can capitalize on these data-driven insights to
develop tailored marketing strategies aligned with identi -
fied opportunities, ultimately driving more favorable out-
comes [24].
This practical approach does more than simply
enhance the customer experience by ensuring that new
products, services, and communications fulfill customer
needs; it also involves using online platforms to gather
direct customer feedback [23]. These resources offer val -
uable perspectives on customer viewpoints and external
expectations, allowing firms to refine their offerings and
marketing methods, thus effectively implementing the
principles of MO.
Methodology
By adopting the mixed bibliometric framework [25], this
study used a blended approach that integrated quantita -
tive bibliometrics with qualitative content analysis. The
quantitative analysis was divided into two main com -
ponents: performance analysis, which examined annual
publication and citation trends, and science mapping,
which visually depicted the field’s intellectual and con -
ceptual structure through keyword co-occurrence analy -
sis and bibliographic coupling.
Bibliometric analysis
Bibliometric methods have proven to be effective tools
for evaluating and monitoring dynamic changes in
research topics within a given field [26, 27]. They offer
a quantitative perspective that facilitates the assessment
of the scientific impact and quality of studies [28–30]
and resources, enabling large bodies of the literature to
be analyzed by focusing on macro-level patterns and
minimizing researcher bias [31]. Moreover, bibliomet -
ric analysis has been successfully applied across various
disciplines—including operations, marketing, customer
behavior research, information technology management,
and innovation management—to map the development
of specific research fields.
A protocol for searching, selecting, and refining rel -
evant research literature is vital for conducting compre -
hensive bibliometric analysis. The current study follows
the guidelines of the PRISMA 2020 flowchart [32] for
bibliometric analysis and systematic reviews (Fig. 1).

The Web of Science (WoS) Core Collection biblio -
graphic database was used because of its consistency,
standardization, and credibility [33]. It is also the most
influential database classifying academic research, pro -
viding access to approximately 22,000 top-tier journals
[31]. The use of Web of Science (WoS) guarantees the
quality of the sample to be used, as all articles in the data-
base meet the four criteria on which quality is measured,
i.e., source-normalized impact per article (SNIP), Cit -
eScore, SCImago Journal Rank (SJR), and h-index. This
review was limited to publications indexed in the SCI-
Expanded, SSCI, AHCI, and Emerging Sources (ESCI) of
the WoS collection.
We performed a search for peer-reviewed articles
published from 2007 to 2023 using the advanced search
function, which includes the use of Topic Search (TS)
and the Boolean operators “OR” and “ AND. ” The fol -
lowing search string was used to explore abstract and
keyword fields in the titles: "* analytics” OR "Big data*"
OR "Data driven*" OR “Digital marketing” OR “Cus -
tomer data mining” AND "Market* orientation" OR
"Customer orientation" OR "Customer insight*" OR
"market* insights" OR "Customer centric*" OR "Market*
intelligence" OR "customer intelligence." The search was
further refined by applying a filter to restrict the publi -
cation year to the period from 2007 to 2023 and limit -
ing it to journal articles and reviews. A starting point
of 2007 was chosen because this is widely recognized
as being the year when the analytical approach in mar -
keting—often referred to as MA—first became consoli -
dated, as a result of Hauser’s [34] seminal work, which
emphasized the growing importance of data-driven
decision-making and analytical rigor in the marketing
field. We also selected articles published in scientific
journals in the field of management and marketing. We
read the titles, abstracts, and keywords carefully and
excluded any documents that did not fall within the
scope of the study, giving a final sample of 238 articles.
The homogenization of keywords and authors’ names
in the database used was another major step, as this is
necessary to obtain a more solid scientific mapping.
As for keywords, the following replacements were car -
ried out: “customers” replaced by ( =) “customer, ” “con-
sumer” = “customer, ” and “supply chain” = “supply chain
management. ”
Performance analysis
The purpose of performance analysis is to evaluate the
publication and citation performance of researchers,
institutions, countries, and journals [35]. In this sense,
performance analysis involves assessing the scientific
output of the retrieved dataset in terms of both quality
and quantity indicators related to each unit of analysis
(e.g., author, journal, country, or institution) within the
studied research field.
Science mapping: keyword co‑occurrence
and bibliographic coupling analysis.
Co-occurrence analysis uses frequently co-occur -
ring terms to pinpoint thematic relationships [28].
We employed this technique because, unlike co-cita -
tion and bibliographic coupling, which tend to yield
broad thematic clusters, co-word analysis offers finer-
grained insights of these themes. Specifically, it helped
us to develop a more in-depth interpretation of our

To facilitate article clustering, we built a bibliometric
network using bibliographic document coupling. This
technique, which is a retrospective measure, was selected
for its inherent stability over time, a key advantage over
the dynamic nature of co-citation analysis. This stabil -
ity enables a broad spectrum of topics and their latest
advancements to be identified [36].
Bibliographic coupling works on the assumption that
publications sharing common references are thematically
related [28]. In this analysis, the weight parameter quan -
tifies the number of shared references between docu -
ments in a cluster, and the total link strength measures
the cumulative intensity of these connections. Conse -
quently, the weight value pinpoints the articles that most
robustly connect the literature in a cluster, serving as
their thematic and intellectual anchors.
The resulting analysis provides a representation of the
current state of the research field on MA integration into
MO.
Accordingly, the use of weight < Links > and
weight < Total link strength > as selection filters methodo-
logically justifies the representativeness and relevance of
the analyzed documents, ensuring the robustness of both
the bibliometric clustering and the derived content anal -
ysis [37].
Content analysis
Finally, to elaborate on the results of the quantitative
bibliographic clustering, we performed a qualitative
content analysis on a sample of 25 articles drawn from
the five main clusters. In accordance with the rigorous
approach outlined by Krippendorff [38], the analysis
involved systematically defining units of analysis and
coding a comprehensive set of constructs from the lit -
erature such as marketing intelligence, big data, AI tools,
technological capabilities, dissemination, value creation,
innovation, and market effectiveness. These coded ele -
ments were then grouped into four primary conceptual
categories: capabilities, marketing analytics, market ori -
entation implementation, and strategic outcomes. This
process allowed us to clearly identify the core themes
addressed by each cluster. Bibliometrix and VOSviewer
were selected to carry out the analysis, as both programs
are well-accepted tools in research studies [39].
Results
In the first place, a descriptive analysis was conducted to
present the performance indicators of scientific perfor -
mance within MA and MO, thereby addressing RQ1. To
this end, bibliometric information was collected from the
subject literature, including publication time trends, top
journals, and geographical distribution of countries that
have contributed to the development of research into the
application of MA for MO between 2007 and 2023. Next,
a scientific mapping analysis combining co-authorship,
keyword analysis, and bibliographic linkage was under -
taken to address RQ2. Finally, based on the results of bib-
liographic clustering, a content analysis was carried out
to extend the quantitative results of the research, thereby
answering RQ2 and RQ3.

The bibliometric analysis (Fig.  4) reveals distinct tem -
poral patterns in the conceptual evolution of the field.
Research activity remained relatively quiet until 2013,
after which there was a marked intensification in schol -
arly activity. Of particular significance is the subsequent
emergence and dominance of data-centric paradigms.
This is clearly shown by the increased frequency of terms
such as “big data” and “big data analytics” in recent litera-
ture. This trajectory suggests a fundamental and recent
shift in the field’s theoretical and methodological orienta-
tion toward data-intensive approaches.
Beyond these dominant streams, a parallel but slightly
delayed acceleration is evident in concepts such as “social
media, ” “digital marketing, ” and “machine learning. ” This
reflects the inclusion of consumer interaction platforms
and artificial intelligence techniques in the discourse of

orientation.

Future research directions (RQ3)
This revised agenda provides a robust road map for
future research by incorporating the conceptual frame -
work into broader theoretical perspectives and address -
ing some emerging ethical challenges.
The current findings strongly indicate that MA and
MO need to be embedded into broader theoretical per -
spectives and move beyond isolated capability studies.
Building on the theoretical foundations proposed by Hos-
sain et  al. [68], who demonstrated how the inclusion of
RBV, KBV, and MO perspectives enables firms to lever -
age customer analytics for competitive advantage, future
research should use such theoretical lenses to enrich this
framework. In this regard, future scholarship should not
only align with but extend this integrative approach to
examine ways in which analytical capabilities interact
with resource, knowledge, and market-based orientation
to foster sustained competitive advantages.
Viewing MA as the intellectual engine enables organi -
zations to interpret and respond to market dynam -
ics effectively. Future studies should explicitly explore
how organizations reconfigure their technological and

Conclusion
This study provides a bibliometric analysis of the MA–
MO interface, comprehensively mapping the intellectual
structure and evolution of this rapidly maturing field.
Our examination of 238 articles establishes that data-
driven market orientation (DDMO) is a distinct and rap -
idly evolving strategic business philosophy, systematically
leveraging MA to replace reliance on intuition with tech -
nically operationalizable, data-driven decision-making.
This marks a fundamental transformation, shifting MO
from a strategic philosophy toward a verifiable, practical
capability.
In methodological terms, this study provides the
foundational intellectual structure of the field, show -
ing accelerating maturity and convergence, particularly
since 2014. The comprehensive analysis confirms the
cross-functional nature of the research domain, drawing
on essential contributions from the fields of marketing,
information systems, and management, with top jour -
nals such as Journal of Marketing and Journal of Busi -
ness Research providing conceptual depth and Electronic
Markets reflecting technological orientation. From the
theoretical perspective, the analysis makes a profound
contribution by establishing MA as the essential tech -
nological engine and enabler of dynamic capabilities for
modern MO. The progression of the seminal works that
have laid the foundation in linking strategy and analytical
capabilities [41] demonstrates that MA powers the DCV.
It enhances the firm’s sensing capability via advanced
data processing, drives opportunity-seizing through
knowledge creation and strategic decision-making, and
facilitates the organizational transformation required for
sustained competitive advantage. This framework firmly
situates the MA–MO relationship within the modern
strategic literature.
The intellectual progression analyzed here is indica -
tive of a broader digital transformation in the organiza -
tion. The evolution reflects thematic clusters that focus
on integrating big data and social media analytics [42, 44,
54] in order to drive innovation and enhance firm per -
formance. This transition is not merely about adopting
technology,it is about embedding data-centric paradigms
into the entire competitive process. Ultimately, the syn -
thesis demonstrates that MA is the critical infrastructure
enabling this transformation, allowing firms to achieve
market agility and a highly personalized customer orien -
tation, thus translating MO philosophy into measurable
competitive advantage.
From a managerial standpoint, this research pro -
vides a clear road map for investment and strategic

full potential of a DDMO requires the necessary invest -

Managers must focus on fostering organizational align -

Final remarks and research limitations
This study had two main limitations. First, we used bib -
liographic coupling as the basis for clustering articles in
our analysis. It is recommended that additional article
clustering methods be used, such as co-citation analysis,
and the results were compared. Second, this research was
based on data collected in the WoS database. Although
this is a comprehensive database for scientific and aca -
demic research, other well-known databases, such as
Scopus, SCImago Journal Rank, and others, could help
to improve the results, due to possible differences in the
content of the databases. The results could be improved
due to possible differences in the coverage of scientific
literature across various fields and disciplines.
Despite these drawbacks, this study provides a deep
understanding of the use of big data and MA to improve
MO and develop competitive marketing strategies.As
businesses continue to navigate an increasingly data-rich
environment, the ability to effectively leverage BDA will
likely become a key differentiator.
