ABSTRACT: This study investigates how artificial intelligence (AI) and predictive analytics can enhance audience engagement in
social media management within the Israeli digital ecosystem. Building on digital transformation and marketing analytics
literature, the research develops and tests a predictive engagement model that integrates three capability dimensions: behavioral
activity (posting frequency), technological capability (AI usage intensity), and organizational infrastructure (data governan ce
maturity). To demonstrate the empirical testing procedure, the study employs dataset of 250 observations and applies correlation
analysis, multicollinearity diagnostics, and multiple regression models with interaction effects. Results show that posting
frequency is a sign ificant predictor of engagement; however, explanatory power increases substantially when AI usage and
governance maturity are included. AI usage intensity emerges as a strong driver of engagement performance, while data
governance maturity also contributes positively, indicating that predictive effectiveness depends on both technological adoption
and organizational readiness. Importantly, the interaction analysis suggests that AI amplifies the marginal impact of posting
frequency on engagement, positioning AI as a performance multiplier rather than a simple add -on. Although findings are based
on synthetic data and therefore do not support population-level inference, the study offers a scalable, context-relevant framework
that can be validated using real engagement data from Israeli firms and public institutions.
KEYWORDS: Artificial intelligence; Predictive analytics; Social media management; Audience engagement; Data governance;
Israel; Digital marketing analytics.

1. INTRODUCTION
Social media has become a very important way for people to talk to each other, allowing big groups of people to talk to each other
in real time. Because of this, social media has a huge audience  6.8 million people in Israel—and is an important place for brands,
agencies, and public-sector organizations to promote themselves and get people involved. To make an organization more visible,
reach more people, and be more relevant, it needs to get more peo ple involved. You may use predictive analytics, which util izes
past data to make predictions about the future, to improve social media engagement by showing you the best time, kind of
content, or frequency to publish. Even while it is clear that anticipating how engaged an audience will be with Israeli organizations
on social media is important, there isn't much knowledge regarding how predictive analytics may be used in this way.
The aim of this study is to evaluate the prospects and obstacles of utilizing predictive analytics to enhance audience engage ment
on social media for organizations functioning in the Israeli market. The primary research inquiries are as follows: (i) What data
sources can be used to use predictive analytics to get people to interact with you on social media in Israel? (ii) What AI technologies
and predictive analytics models can be used to get more people to interact with each other on social media in Israel? (iii) W hat
organizational governance measures are required to use predictive analytics in social media audience engagement for firms  in
Israeli markets? To answer these concerns, we look at a number of issues, such as the theoretical basis of predictive analyti cs in
social media, the state of the Israeli social media ecosystem, and useful tools, approaches, and case studies. This study  highlights
the key significance of predictive engagement in social media within the Israeli environment, demonstrating its capacity to
enhance communication between companies and consumers, facilitate message dissemination, and promote goods or services
more successfully.
This work advances the field by presenting and empirically evaluating a predictive engagement model specifically designed for the
Israeli social media context. The study employs a structured dataset that combines behavioral activity (posting frequency), artificial

2. LITERATURE REVIEW
2.1 Artificial Intelligence and Digital Transformation in Marketing
The rapid advancement of artificial intelligence (AI) has significantly reshaped marketing theory and practice. AI is no long er
viewed merely as a technological tool but as a strategic capability that influences decision -making structures, organizational
performance, and customer engagement (Dwivedi et al., 2021; Shrestha et al., 2019). Within marketing environments, AI enables
firms to process vast amounts of structured and unstructured data, uncover hidden patterns, and generate predictive insights that
support more informed strategic actions (Grewal et al., 2020).
Digital transformation research emphasizes that AI -driven capabilities form a core component of modern competitive advantage
(Verhoef et al., 2021; Kraus et al., 2022). Organizations that successfully integrate AI into their marketing processes often achieve
superior performance outcomes due to improved responsiveness, personalization, and operational efficiency (Chatterjee et al.,
2021). Big data analytics capability, in particular, has been shown to positively influence firm performance through enhanced
decision-making quality and dynamic adaptability (Mikalef et al., 2020; Wamba et al., 2020).
AI in service and marketing contexts extends beyond automation; it transforms customer interaction models. Huang and Rust
(2021) argue that AI systems increasingly participate in service delivery, augmenting human capabilities and enabling data -driven
personalization. Similarly, Kumar et al. (2020) highlight how intelligent agent technologies enhance marketing effectiveness by
predicting customer responses and optimizing communication strategies.
Within this broader digital transformation framework, social media has emerged as a critical domain where AI applications directly
influence audience engagement and brand visibility.
2.2 Social Media Engagement and Strategic Marketing
Social media platforms have evolved into central channels for marketing communication, customer interaction, and brand
building. Engagement on these platforms —measured through likes, shares, comments, and interaction intensity —has become a
key performance indicator for digital marketing effectiveness (Trunfio & Rossi, 2021).
Strategic social media marketing requires more than content posting; it involves structured planning, segmentation, and
performance monitoring (Li et al., 2021; Tafesse & Wien, 2018). Organizations increasingly rely on data analytics to determin e
what content resonates with audiences, when to publish posts, and how to maintain sustained interaction over time.
Predictive analytics plays an essential role in this process. Schoen et al. (2013) demonstrated that social media data can be  used
to forecast behavioral outcomes and trends, highlighting the predictive power embedded within digital interactions. Similarly ,
Kursuncu et al. (2018) showed that social media text and engagement signals can be modeled to anticipate user behavior patterns.
Empirical research also suggests that engagement levels depend not only on content characteristics but also on timing and
interaction dynamics. Spasojevic et al. (2015) emphasized that optimal posting timing significantly influences user response rates.
Rosas-Quezada et al. (2019) further demonstrated that both linguistic style and content structure contribute to engagement
predictability.
These findings suggest that engagement is not random; rather, it can be systematically modeled using predictive techniques.
2.3 Predictive Analytics and Big Data Capabilities
The theoretical foundation of predictive engagement modeling is closely linked to big data analytics capability theory.
Organizations that develop advanced data analytics capabilities are better positioned to extract actionable insights from com plex
datasets (Mikalef et al., 2020). Big data ecosystems facilitate real -time decision-making and adaptive strategy formation (Pappas
et al., 2018).
Brynjolfsson and McElheran (2016) argue that data -driven decision -making improves performance when organizations
systematically incorporate analytics into strategic processes. This aligns with AI adoption research showing that firms integ rating
AI into marketing operations experience improved outcomes (Chatterjee et al., 2021).
From a capability perspective, AI -enhanced predictive systems allow organizations to transition from reactive to proactive
engagement management. Instead of evaluating posts after publication, predictive models estimate engagement outcomes
before release, enabling strategic content optimization.
However, predictive engagement modeling remains underexplored in specific national contexts, particularly within smaller but
highly digitized markets such as Israel.

2.4 AI Governance, Ethics, and Responsible Deployment
While AI offers substantial opportunities for predictive engagement optimization, governance and ethical considerations remai n
critical. AI systems rely heavily on user -generated data, raising concerns related to privacy, transparency, and algorithmic
accountability (Radanliev et al., 2024).
Digital transformation research emphasizes that technological adoption must be accompanied by organizational restructuring and
ethical safeguards (Kraus et al., 2022). Kaplan and Haenlein (2019) note that AI applications in business must balance effici ency
gains with responsible usage to maintain stakeholder trust.
Furthermore, organizations must ensure that predictive models do not unintentionally reinforce bias or create discriminatory
outcomes. Responsible AI deployment requires clear governance frameworks, data minimization strategies, and transparent
algorithmic design.
In the context of social media engagement, ethical AI usage becomes particularly important due to the personal and behavioral
nature of user interaction data.
2.5 Research Gap and Study Positioning
Although extensive research has examined AI adoption, digital transformation, big data capability, and social media engagemen t
independently, there remains limited empirical integration of these domains within the Israeli context.
Existing studies largely focus on global or large -market datasets, leaving smaller yet technologically advanced ecosystems
underexamined. Israel represents a unique environment characterized by high internet penetration, advanced technological
infrastructure, and strong digital adoption rates.
Despite these characteristics, empirical predictive engagement modeling tailored to Israeli social media behavior remains limited.
Moreover, prior literature often conceptualizes AI engagement capabilities at a theoretical level without testing structured
quantitative models that incorporate engagement metrics and posting behavior.
2.6 Contextual Landscape of Israeli Social Media Ecosystem
A diverse range of social media platforms are prevalent in Israel, reflecting general trends with a few notable local feature s.
Facebook dominates among users aged 18 and older, while Instagram, Snapchat, YouTube, and LinkedIn are also among Israel’s
most popular platforms. Facebook is also the leading platform for microblogging and has the highest audience engagement rate.
Nationwide, about 95 percent of the population is online, and widely reported political and social turmoil remains a potentia l
driver of increased online activity (Abeysinghe et al., 2018). Nevertheless, the expansive and interconnected arrangement of all
the social networks discourage user migration between platforms, thus mitigating against a homogeneous ecosystem. Alongside
the widespr ead use of established international platforms, local emerging platforms enjoy a degree of popularity. The most
notable is Telegram, where platform features outpace those offered by WhatsApp and lead to experienced users selecting the
former (Steinfeld & Lev-On, 2018).
The Israeli regulatory environment for social media is permissive concerning user -generated content, in line with the
constitutionally enshrined principle of freedom of expression. Accordingly, efforts to supervise information or to implement
regulatory restrictions at the request of users remain very limited. In line with global trends, social media has gained popularity as
a communication medium among public institutions such as ministries, municipalities, and organizations affiliated with the
national he alth basket. However, past human or technological failures involving online communications have simultaneously
generated general distrust toward the space within the public sector (Lamsal et al., 2022).
2.7 AI Tools and Methods for Audience Engagement
Artificial Intelligence (AI) serves as a supportive component for brands and, indirectly, for users in the social media ecosy stem.
Users receive helpful content based on their interests or preferences without the need for manual searches. Due to the volume
of content uploaded by users, brands encounter difficulties in providing attentionworthy content for users. Furthermore, without
appropriate content, users may lose interest in a brand. AI plays a supporting role by sending reminders to users who have ye t to
consume a brand’s content before the information becomes outdated. When brands share periods in which users are more active,
AI assists brands in choosing a time slot that aligns with their plan to reach a broader audience. AI also plays a crucial ro le in
conversing with users, detecting raw signals, and determining when users display a crisis signal. Informed by the detection
incident, detailed data regarding brand conversations becomes significant to strategise future approaches. Crisis -fixing is critical
as unresolved issues lead to negative impacts on the whole brand.
As social media evolves rapidly, brands find it challenging to maintain an updated understanding of users’ tones and styles f or
effective conversations. The conversation tone employed by brands often determines the level of engagement they receive from
users. Users have diverse styles and preferences, especially in multi -lingual settings. Therefore, it is vital to analyse present

2.7 Content Personalization and Recommendation
Social media platforms leverage sophisticated algorithms to collaboratively filter, recommend, and personalize content for
individual users. The aim is to make the content posted in the newsfeeds more relevant and engaging for its audience. In order to
design such systems, the social media platforms rely on users’ interests, context at the given moment, and their historical browsing
and engagement histories. Algorithms can be grouped into three categories based on the input: collaborative filtering, conten t-
based filtering, and hybrid filtering. One of the critical challenges in this area is the representation of users’ interests.
Content personalization and recommendation enhance reach to relevant target audiences, leading to increased engagement
rates. The underlying premise here is that people discuss topics they are interested in or that lately have been trending amo ng
their peers. Chris and Tuitjer1 show that posts with a lower engagement latency lead to higher interaction rates. Including events
calendars as part of the data can yield predictions on which topics are likely to generate discussions.
2.8 Sentiment and Trend Analysis
Social media sentiment and trend analysis supports understanding of customer attitudes and market transitions to anticipate
shifts in content strategic decision -making. A fill -tier approach addressed customer sentiment toward university services across
Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, and Twitter in Israeli Hebrew (Axhiu et al., 2014) and identified emergent discussion topics to
reveal topic diffusion patterns and underlying sentiments over time (Abeysinghe et al., 2018).
Natural language processing (NLP) enables automated sentiment scoring of Hebrew text using a lexicon -based approach for sub-
and full-sentences grounded in prevailing cultural context (Prasad Gunasekaran, 2023). NLP assessments inform brand affinity and
effective content types for specific themes. Topic modeling algorithms uncover prominent subjects of discussion at selected
timemarks, facilitating identification of active themes surrounding specific campaigns or events. These analyses help generat e
actionable strategic guidance and viable creative options informed by holistic monitoring of engagement, sentiment, and topics
across competing Israeli institutions.
2.9 Predictive Scheduling and Cadence Optimization
Successful audience engagement hinges not only on the content quality but also on timely interaction. The old adage that “timing
is everything” holds especially true in social media (Spasojevic et al., 2015). Predictive analyses can bolster audience outr each by
indicating when, how often, and in which order content should be shared. Optimal scheduling calls for determining the precise
moment to make posts, frequency of message dissemination over time, and sequence of content appearance across platforms.
Visibility corresponds to several factors: peak activity periods, geographical location of an audience and corresponding time zo ne
differences, organization -imposed calendars of events, and prior engagement behavior. Clues from historical data pinpointing
when and how messages have resonated previously can prove invaluable in deriving this intelligence.
Analysis and modeling of similar information enable the definition of a strategy encompassing the cadence of communications
aimed at a specified target group linked to one or more social platforms. Between messaging bursts, diminishing returns generally
apply (i.e., interest in stimuli wanes). Moreover, excessive outreach heightens the risk of generating fatigue or exasperation,
further reducing the likelihood of sustained engagement, retention, or conversion. Consequently, even with predetermined
posting times, prediction of the subsequent publication difficulty remains crucial for maximizing retention.
2.10 Crisis Detection and Reputation Management
Anomaly detection models identify content engagement patterns. When future activity significantly departs from these norms,
they signal a potential crisis requiring scrutiny. These systems incorporate leading indicators, such as negative sentiment, trend
shifts, abnormal usage within subaudiences, and external events (e.g., legal adjudications). Anticipating future occurrences
enables preparations and an accelerated response. Canada’s APMA successfully minimised risk by quickly addressing service
disruptions.
Forecasting models evaluate likely scenarios and impacts of alternative actions. Many Israeli organisations maintain operatio nal
reputations qualitatively exceeding factual accuracy. Institutional risk policies often resist confirmation of genuine crises , leading
the third sector to lose supporters, donors, and media trust.

2.11 Data Governance, Ethics, and Privacy in Israel
Data provenance denotes the process of managing and tracking the movement and transformation of data throughout its lifecycle,
and it seeks to preserve transparency. Permission denotes collective agreements and seeks to protect privacy and the rights o f
the parties involved. Data Minimization involves collecting a minimal amount of data and seeks to prevent collecting excessive
data. Retention pertains to the period the data is stored for use within the organization, during which each party still poss esses
the previously acquired rights. Inter-operability allows for the data collected for one purpose to be used in fulfilment of a different
purpose by another party, and enables integration of diverse data in the pursuit of (Radanliev et al., 2024).
Israel's data governance and privacy aligns broadly with the European Union's General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). The
existing Israeli privacy regulation seeks to grant individuals the principles associated with the GDPR (Latham & Goltz, 2019) . The
actual provisions present a significant difference in approach to developing codes. Since December 2020, organizations generating
educational content using AI are thus obliged to ensure the incorporation of the principles incorporated in the code of ethic s laid
down and not to influence them. Since early 2019, UNISA has advanced drafts for an ethic of care model code.

3. DATA AND METHODOLOGY
This study uses a simulated (synthetic) dataset (N = 250) generated to evaluate the proposed predictive engagement model and
to demonstrate the empirical testing procedure. The simulated data were created with plausible parameter distributions and
controlled noise to reflect realistic variation in posting behavior, AI usage intensity, governance maturity, and engagement
outcomes. The dataset is used for methodological illustration rather than to claim population -level inference about Israel.
The synthetic dataset was generated using controlled random distributions calibrated to theoretical expectations and prior
empirical findings. Error terms were introduced to simulate realistic variance while preserving structural relationships amon g
variables.
This study uses a dataset  (N = 250)  The dataset contains user -level indicators including age, gender, posting frequency, and
interaction metrics (likes, shares, and comments). The dependent variable is an engagement score scaled from 0 to 1.

4. EVALUATION METRICS AND METHODOLOGIES FOR PREDICTIVE ENGAGEMENT
Prediction-support systems present unique operational challenges compared to recommendation -support systems (Trunfio &
Rossi, 2021). The goal of marketing content optimization is to enhance user attention, boost engagement, and drive conversion s.
Metrics such as engagement rate, reach, retention, conversion, and latency are typically used to gauge Predictive engagement
refers to estimating any value associated with a brand’s post or message before its actual release. It includes determining u ser
engagement (e.g., likes, shares, comments), reach (views and impressions), retention (e.g., time spent, saves, visits), conversion
(user journey progression), and the duration between publication and user response (engagement time) (S. Rosas-Quezada et al.,
2019).
Many systems validate predictions postfacto using A/B tests, observing real -world performance, and measuring differences via
statistical tests. Evaluation consequently focuses on difference metrics (e.g., ++%, ––%, +Δ, –Δ) rather than level metrics (e.g.,
thousand units), which depend on various baseline parameters not universally available.
A/B testing requires real -time allocation of historical test data, necessitating development under an experimental design
methodology. Such designs influence the entire data lifecycle, from acquisition to user segmentation and suggestion formulation.
Optimal design selection depends on the nature of the segmentation and the corresponding evaluation measures.
Cross-validation combines many performance evaluations via folds in a systematic manner. For predictive approaches capturing
multiple dimensions, each dimension may require independent cross -validation, and metrics duly averaged across dimensions.

6. DISCUSSION
This study examined the determinants of predictive engagement performance within the Israeli social media ecosystem by
integrating behavioral, technological, and governance-based factors into a unified empirical framework. The findings offer several
theoretical and managerial insights.
First, the results confirm that posting frequency significantly enhances engagement performance , supporting H1. This finding
aligns with established social media engagement theory, which suggests that content consistency increases visibility and
interaction probability. However, the moderate explanatory power of the baseline model (Model 1) indicates that posting activity
alone is insufficient to fully explain engagement variability in contemporary digital environments.
Second, the inclusion of AI usage intensity significantly increases explanatory power , with AI demonstrating a strong and
statistically significant positive effect on engagement. This finding supports H2 and suggests that predictive analytics capa bility
functions as a performance driver rather than merely a supplementary tool. The results reinforce the argument that AI-enhanced
scheduling, personalization, and sentiment detection improve content optimization efficiency.
Third, data governance maturity also positively influences engagement performance , supporting H3. This result highlights an
often-overlooked dimension in social media analytics research: organizational infrastructure. Governance frameworks ensure data
integrity, ethical compliance, and structured analytics deployment, thereby enhancing predictive reliability. In the Israeli context—
where regulatory alignment with GDPR principles is increasing—this finding carries strategic importance.
Fourth, the interaction analysis (Model 3) demonstrates that AI usage amplifies the marginal effect of posting frequency ,
supporting H4. This suggests that AI does not merely act as an independent predictor but functions as a multiplier of behavio ral
strategy. Organizations that combine frequent posting with advanced predictive tools achieve superior engagement performance
compared to those relying solely on activity volume.

7. THEORETICAL CONTRIBUTION
This study makes several theoretical contributions to the literature on social media analytics, predictive engagement modelin g,
and AI-enabled digital strategy.
First, the study extends traditional engagement prediction frameworks by integrating organizational technological capability into
behavioral models of social media performance. Prior predictive engagement research has primarily focused on observable
interaction metrics such as likes, shares, comments, or posting frequency. While these variables explain engagement outcomes at
a surface level, they do not account for the underlying capability structures that enable predictive optimization. By incorpora ting
AI usage intensity as a strategic variable, this research advances engagement modeling beyond behavioral determinism and
toward capability-based explanations.
Second, this study introduces data governance maturity as a structural determinant of predictive performance. Governance has
often been examined within regulatory or ethical frameworks but rarely operationalized as an explanatory variable in engagement
prediction models. The findings demonstrate that governance infrastructure contributes significantly to engagement outcomes,
suggesting that predictive analytics effectiveness depends not only on algorithmic sophistication but also on institutional
readiness. This bridges the gap between digital governance literature and marketing analytics research.
Third, the study contributes to the emerging literature on AI as a performance amplifier by empirically testing a moderation effect.
Rather than treating AI as an isolated independent predictor, the interaction analysis shows that AI usage enhances the mar ginal
returns of posting frequency. This positions AI as a multiplier of behavioral strategy rather than merely a technological add -on.
Theoretically, this finding supports a dynamic capability perspective in which AI strengthens the effectiveness of exist ing
operational activities.
Fourth, the research contextualizes predictive engagement modeling within the Israeli digital ecosystem, characterized by hig h
internet penetration, rapid technological adoption, and evolving regulatory alignment with GDPR principles. By situating predictive
analytics within a localized socio -digital environment, the study contributes to the growing body of research emphasizing the
importance of national and regulatory context in AI deployment.
Finally, methodologically, this study proposes a scalable empirical framework for predictive engagement analysis that integra tes
behavioral, technological, and governance dimensions. Although the dataset is simulated, the modeling structure provides a
replicable template for future empirical research using real platform-derived engagement data.
Collectively, these contributions reposition predictive engagement from a purely behavioral forecasting exercise to a multi -
dimensional strategic capability model that integrates technology adoption, governance maturity, and content strategy within a
unified analytical framework.

Because the dataset is simulated, the results demonstrate model behavior and statistical testing rather than providing eviden ce
