Abstract Artificial intelligence (AI) is transforming marketing from a retrospective reporting function into a pre-
dictive decision system. However, research and policy discussions often treat marketing analytics and economic
competitiveness as separate domains. This study introduces the AIMx framework (AI-integrated marketing analyt-
ics), which integrates marketing mix modeling (MMM), multi-touch attribution (MTA), and incrementality testing
(IT) within a unified AI-driven feedback architecture. Using a design-science methodology combined with explora-
tory system dynamics simulation, the study examines how integrated marketing analytics influence forecasting
accuracy, resource allocation, and organizational responsiveness under volatile market conditions. The results
suggest that coordinated AI-driven measurement reduces decision fragmentation and improves adaptive deci-
sion making. At a broader level, the framework highlights how aggregated marketing intelligence may contribute
to competitive resilience within digital ecosystems. The study contributes a clear conceptual architecture linking
AI-enabled marketing analytics with adaptive competitiveness and data-driven strategic learning.
Purpose This study develops the AIMx Framework to show how AI-driven marketing analytics can move
beyond standalone measurement methods toward an integrated decision system. The paper addresses the gap
between firm-level marketing optimization and broader adaptive competitiveness by specifying how predictive ana-
lytics systems may generate scalable learning effects.
Design/methodology The study adopts a design-science approach to develop and refine the AIMx framework. The
architecture is specified as a recursive feedback system linking predictive measurement, resource allocation, organiza-
tional learning, and market volatility. Its structural behavior is examined using exploratory system dynamics simulation
to analyze adaptive patterns under varying market conditions. The simulation is intended to test the internal logic
of the framework rather than to generate empirical economic forecasts.
Findings The results suggest that integrating marketing measurement models within an AI-driven system reduces
decision fragmentation and improves responsiveness to market fluctuations. Firms using the integrated framework
demonstrate more stable budget adjustments and faster adaptation compared to fragmented approaches. At
a broader level, widespread adoption may generate stabilizing feedback effects, although these system-level implica-
tions remain conceptual.

Introduction
Artificial intelligence (AI) is reshaping how organiza -
tions allocate resources, forecast performance, and make
strategic decisions. In marketing, AI has evolved from
an operational support tool into a central component of
predictive analytics, budget allocation, and performance
measurement. Algorithms that determine how much to
invest, where to allocate spending, and how to respond
to consumer behavior increasingly influence firm-level
competitiveness.
However, limited understanding remains regarding
how firm-level marketing analytics systems influence
broader economic performance and competitive dynam -
ics. This gap represents a missed opportunity to examine
how marketing intelligence contributes to market inno -
vation and adaptive capacity beyond the firm. Studies
of digital transformation typically emphasize efficiency
gains or technology adoption, while macrolevel analyses
rarely consider how algorithmic marketing decisions may
generate cumulative systemic effects. As global advertis -
ing expenditure exceeds USD 1 trillion annually [45], and
AI-related investments are projected to surpass USD 500
billion in the coming years [44], business executives, and
policymakers face mounting pressure to justify market -
ing and AI expenditures not only in terms of return on
investment but also in terms of long-term strategic value.
At the same time, organizations operate under condi -
tions of structural volatility. Inflationary pressures, tech -
nological disruption, and rapidly changing consumer
expectations challenge conventional measurement
approaches. Traditional attribution models struggle to
capture complex omnichannel customer journeys, while
real-time optimization and predictive analytics increas -
ingly define competitive differentiation. Younger con -
sumer segments place growing emphasis on authenticity
and participatory engagement in brand relationships [33].
Marketing effectiveness therefore depends on integrating
data analytics, computational tools, and managerial judg -
ment within adaptive decision systems.
AI and machine learning accelerate this transformation
by uncovering behavioral and contextual relationships
that conventional analytics often overlook. AI-enabled
platforms integrate data across touchpoints to connect
engagement metrics with revenue outcomes [39]. Mar -
keting analytics has evolved from descriptive reporting
toward predictive and prescriptive decision intelligence.
Empirical research shows that AI-driven personaliza -
tion strengthens customer loyalty [18], anthropomor -
phic AI influences brand–consumer relationships [14],
and digital marketing adoption enhances organizational
adaptability [10]. Together, these findings suggest that
AI-enabled marketing analytics increasingly functions as
a driver of strategic responsiveness rather than merely an
operational enhancement.
Building on this literature, this study introduces the
AIMx framework (AI-integrated marketing analytics),
a conceptual architecture that integrates marketing mix
modeling (MMM), multi-touch attribution (MTA), and
incrementality testing (IT) within an AI-orchestrated
feedback system. Rather than treating these models as
independent measurement tools, AIMx specifies how
their coordinated integration can improve forecasting
accuracy, resource allocation decisions, and organiza -
tional learning. The framework further emphasizes that
AI and human judgment must operate in parallel: while
algorithmic systems enhance analytical precision and
scalability, strategic interpretation, ethical oversight, and
accountability remain managerial responsibilities. Mar -
keting analytics is therefore conceptualized as a hybrid
decision architecture that combines computational intel -
ligence with human governance, as illustrated in Fig. 1.

RQ3: How should responsibilities be balanced between

By addressing these questions, the paper clarifies how

Literature review
Prior research has examined AI-enabled marketing ana -
lytics, marketing measurement systems, and data-driven
decision making from multiple perspectives. However,
these research streams remain fragmented in both ana -
lytical methods and theoretical approaches. This section
reviews three key areas of literature: the evolution of AI
in marketing analytics; advances in marketing meas -
urement—including marketing mix modeling (MMM),
multi-touch attribution (MTA), and incrementality
testing; and research examining the relationship between
AI, digital ecosystems, and competitiveness.
While these areas have provided important insights,
they are often studied independently. Research on mar -
keting analytics frequently focuses on the methodology
of predictive modeling and performance measurement,
while studies of marketing measurement emphasize
specific analytical techniques such as MMM, MTA, or
experimentation. As a result, econometric modeling,
behavioral attribution, and causal experimentation are
rarely considered together within a unified analytical
framework capable of generating broader strategic or
economic impact. This fragmentation highlights the
need for integrative architectures capable of coordi -
nating multiple marketing measurement approaches
within coherent decision systems.

Advanced marketing measurement systems
and adaptive performance
As market environments become increasingly volatile
and unpredictable, marketing measurement systems
are evolving from static reporting tools into adaptive
learning infrastructures. Marketing performance meas -
urement frameworks have been conceptualized as
resilience-enabling systems capable of supporting stra -
tegic adjustment under uncertainty [37]. However, many
organizations continue to deploy measurement models
such as marketing mix modeling (MMM), multi-touch
attribution (MTA), and incrementality testing (IT) as
independent analytical tools rather than as integrated
decision systems.
Historically, marketing performance was assessed using
descriptive and retrospective indicators—impressions,
click-through rates, or conversions—that provided lim -
ited strategic insight [7]. Such measures, rooted in single-
touch or last-click attribution, fail to reflect the nonlinear,
multi-device journeys of modern consumers. Predictive
analytics, by contrast, merges econometrics, machine
learning, and big data to forecast outcomes and dynami -
cally optimize marketing investments [4, 36]. Empirical
evidence suggests that organizations adopting predictive
analytics achieve greater ROI accuracy, faster decision
cycles, and improved resource allocation compared with
firms relying primarily on heuristic decision rule [8, 11].
Recent academic journals identify three complemen -
tary methodologies central to modern marketing meas -
urement: marketing mix modeling (MMM), multi-touch
attribution (MTA), and incrementality testing (IT).
Although each of these approaches provides valuable
insights, they operate at different analytical levels. Mar -
keting mix modeling focuses on aggregate econometric
relationships between marketing inputs and sales out -
comes, multi-touch attribution examines microlevel
behavioral interactions across customer journeys, and
incrementality testing isolates causal effects through con-
trolled experimentation.
• MMM employs econometric models to quantify the
impact of marketing inputs—such as advertising
spend, pricing, and promotions—on sales and mar -
ket share [17, 24]. Industry research also highlights
methodological challenges associated with marketing
mix modeling, including data fragmentation, delayed
feedback cycles, and model instability in rapidly
changing digital environments [6].
• MTA provides behavioral granularity by assign -
ing fractional credit across multiple touchpoints
and channels [26]. Multi-touch attribution (MTA)
addresses these limitations by analyzing user-level
behavioral data across multiple touchpoints. Rather
than assigning conversion credit to a single interac -
tion, MTA distributes fractional attribution across
the sequence of exposures that contribute to con -
sumer decisions [26, 31]. Empirical studies indicate
that algorithmic attribution models provide more
accurate representations of consumer behavior than
traditional heuristic approaches such as first-touch or
last-touch attribution [31].However, attribution mod-
els rely primarily on observational data and therefore
face important methodological limitations. Without
experimental validation, attribution models may cap-
ture correlations between marketing exposure and
outcomes without fully isolating causal effects [16].
• Incrementality testing, grounded in causal infer -
ence, isolates the true lift of marketing by comparing
treated and control groups [25]. Incrementality test -
ing has become an increasingly important methodo -
logical complement to attribution modeling. Unlike
observational analytics approaches, incrementality
testing relies on controlled experiments that compare
treated and control groups to measure the causal
impact of marketing interventions [16, 25]. These
experimental designs enable organizations to esti -
mate the true incremental lift generated by advertis -
ing campaigns or marketing activities.
Large-scale experimentation has become increasingly
feasible with the expansion of digital platforms and geo-
based testing methodologies. Geo-based incrementality
testing, for example, allows firms to measure marketing
impact by comparing geographically separated treatment
and control markets while maintaining real-world oper -
ating conditions. Industry implementations demonstrate
that geo-experiments can provide robust causal estimates
while remaining scalable for large marketing budgets
[13].
When integrated, these approaches create a triangu -
lated framework that connects correlation, behavior,
and causation. AI functions as the orchestrating layer,
harmonizing fragmented datasets and uncovering non -
linear interactions between spend allocation, consumer
sentiment, and contextual variables [23]. Maintaining
a balance between algorithmic precision and human
judgment ensures interpretability and ethical govern -
ance—principles essential for sustainable competitive
advantage [21]. Policy-oriented research emphasizes that

AI, digital ecosystems, and competitive dynamics
At a broader systemic level, AI integration has been
associated with the performance of digital ecosystems
and national competitiveness. Research suggests that
AI-enabled infrastructures enhance coordination across
firms, platforms, and markets [28]. Global competitive -
ness assessments similarly identify digital infrastructure
and AI capabilities as critical determinants of long-term
economic resilience [48]. Despite these insights, the role
of marketing analytics as a structural component of such
systems remains largely unexplored.
Recent competitiveness assessments highlight digi -
tal infrastructure and AI integration as central determi -
nants of economic resilience [48]. At the macroeconomic
level, marketing analytics has emerged as a national
productivity driver, linking firm-level intelligence to
innovation, employment, and competitiveness. Recent
macroeconomic modeling further refines the debate
regarding AI-driven growth. Acemoglu (2024) develops
a task-based macroeconomic framework demonstrat -
ing that the aggregate productivity effects of AI depend
critically on the balance between task automation and
the creation of new, complementary tasks. Rather than
guaranteeing exponential growth, AI’s macroeconomic
impact emerges through structural reallocation mecha -
nisms that are gradual and context dependent. This per -
spective cautions against technological determinism and
underscores the importance of institutional and organi -
zational integration mechanisms. In line with Acemo -
glu’s (2024) task-based macroeconomic framework, the
impact of AI-integrated marketing analytics on competi -
tiveness should be understood not as automatic growth
acceleration but as a structural reallocation process. Pro -
ductivity gains arise when AI complements human deci -
sion making and expands high-value tasks rather than
merely automating existing routine.
Economies investing in AI-enabled marketing infra -
structures show higher rates of digital entrepreneurship,
export diversification, and resilience to market volatility
[28, 32]. Examples from South Korea and Finland high -
light how AI-integrated marketing measurement sup -
ports innovation policy and export strategy [47, 48].
These cases illustrate that algorithmic marketing intel -
ligence extends beyond firm performance—it acts as a
systemic enabler of economic stability and innovation
diffusion [37].
Bridging analytical rigor and strategic practice remains
a central challenge in marketing scholarship [30], rein -
forcing the need for integrative architectures such as
AIMx.

The AIMx framework
Conceptual foundation
The AIMx framework (AI-integrated marketing analyt -
ics) is proposed as a conceptual architecture that inte -
grates advanced marketing measurement models within
an AI-driven decision system. Existing marketing ana -
lytics approaches—including marketing mix modeling
(MMM), multi-touch attribution (MTA), and incremen -
tality testing (IT)—provide valuable but partial perspec -
tives on marketing effectiveness. Because these models
are often implemented independently, they can generate
fragmented analytical insights that complicate manage -
rial decision making. The AIMx framework addresses
this limitation by coordinating these methodologies
within a unified feedback architecture linking predictive
measurement, resource allocation, and organizational
learning.
Within this architecture, artificial intelligence func -
tions as the orchestration layer that continuously inte -
grates econometric modeling, behavioral attribution,
and causal experimentation. Through recursive feedback
loops, AIMx enables organizations to translate frag -
mented marketing data into coherent strategic decisions
while maintaining managerial oversight and governance.

predictive models and decision rules are updated through
iterative feedback, firms are able to respond more quickly
to changing market conditions. This adaptive capability
reflects the logic of dynamic capabilities theory, which
emphasizes the role of sensing, seizing, and transforming
mechanisms in enabling firms to adapt to environmen -
tal change [41, 42]. Taken together, these results suggest
that integrated marketing analytics systems may function
as adaptive learning architectures. Rather than serving
solely as retrospective performance measurement tools,
marketing analytics can become mechanisms for contin -

Conclusion
This study introduced the AIMx framework as an inte -
grated analytical architecture designed to unify mar -
keting mix modeling, multi-touch attribution, and
incrementality testing within an AI-driven feedback
system. By coordinating these measurement approaches
through recursive learning loops, the framework dem -
onstrates how marketing analytics can evolve from
fragmented measurement practices into a dynamic
intelligence infrastructure supporting adaptive decision
making.
The simulation-based exploration suggests that inte -
grated analytics architectures may enhance forecasting
accuracy, improve marketing resource allocation, and
strengthen organizational responsiveness to market sig -
nals. These findings indicate that marketing analytics can
function not only as a performance measurement tool
but also as a mechanism for continuous organizational
learning. When predictive modeling, behavioral attribu -
tion, and causal experimentation are integrated within a
unified analytical system, firms are better positioned to
adapt to uncertainty and changing market conditions.
Beyond firm-level decision making, the study also
highlights the broader strategic implications of AI-ena -
bled marketing intelligence. As organizations increas -
ingly adopt integrated analytics architectures, aggregated
insights may contribute to more adaptive digital ecosys -
tems and improved coordination across economic actors.
In this sense, marketing analytics can be understood as
part of a broader knowledge infrastructure that supports
innovation, competitiveness, and economic resilience.
The AIMx framework therefore contributes to the
growing literature on AI-driven decision systems by pro -
viding a conceptual model that links marketing measure -
ment integration with adaptive organizational learning.
By clarifying how different analytical approaches can be
coordinated within a unified decision architecture, the
framework offers a foundation for future research exam -
ining the evolving role of marketing intelligence in digi -
tally transforming economies.
Future studies may extend this research by empirically
testing the AIMx framework using firm-level marketing
analytics datasets, conducting longitudinal analyses of
marketing decision systems, or exploring cross-indus -
try applications of AI-integrated marketing intelligence
architectures. Such research would further advance
understanding of how artificial intelligence, marketing
analytics, and organizational learning interact to shape
firm competitiveness and economic adaptation in the
digital age.
Limitations expanded
Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged.
First, the simulation environment represents a simplified
abstraction of marketing decision systems. Although the
model incorporates realistic parameter ranges derived
from industry and macroeconomic datasets, it remains
a synthetic representation of complex market dynamics.
Consequently, the simulation should be interpreted as an
exploratory analytical exercise rather than a predictive
model of real-world economic outcomes. Future empiri -
cal research using firm-level marketing analytics data and
longitudinal datasets would help validate the behavioral
relationships proposed by the AIMx framework.
Second, the design-science research approach used
to develop the framework relies partly on expert judg -
ment and theoretical synthesis. While this methodology
is appropriate for constructing analytical artifacts, the
assumptions embedded in the model may vary across
industries, institutional environments, and organizational

cultures. Longitudinal field studies and cross-industry
analyses would therefore be valuable for examining how
AIMx operates in different real-world decision contexts.
Third, the results of the simulation are sensitive to
modeling assumptions and parameter choices. Varia -
tions in market volatility, consumer behavior dynamics,
and AI adoption levels could influence the system’s learn-
ing patterns and performance outcomes. Future research
could explore alternative parameter configurations and
scenario analyses to further assess the robustness of the
framework across different market environments.
Finally, the current study focuses primarily on mar -
keting performance and economic competitiveness
indicators. Broader societal dimensions—including sus -
tainability, digital governance, and ethical considera -
tions surrounding AI-driven decision systems—are not
explicitly modeled in the present framework. Expand -
ing AIMx to incorporate social and environmental feed -
back mechanisms would provide a more comprehensive
understanding of how AI-enabled marketing analytics
may contribute to inclusive and sustainable economic
development. As emphasized by Acemoglu (2024), the
broader economic effects of artificial intelligence depend
on institutional adaptation and task complementarities,
suggesting that future work should examine how AIMx
interacts with organizational structures, labor dynamics,
and policy environments.
Future research directions
The AIMx framework provides a conceptual foundation
for understanding how artificial intelligence can integrate
multiple marketing measurement systems within adap -
tive decision architectures. However, several opportuni -
ties remain for extending and empirically validating this
framework.
First, future research could test the AIMx architecture
using firm-level marketing analytics datasets. Longitudi -
nal studies examining how organizations implement inte-
grated analytics systems over time would help validate
the learning dynamics proposed by the simulation model.
Such studies could assess whether the integration of mar-
keting mix modeling, multi-touch attribution, and incre -
mentality testing leads to measurable improvements in
forecasting accuracy, resource allocation efficiency, and
organizational responsiveness.
Second, cross-industry comparative research could
explore how the effectiveness of AI-integrated market -
ing analytics varies across different sectors and mar -
ket structures. Industries characterized by rapid digital
interaction—such as e-commerce, media platforms,
and financial services—may exhibit stronger feedback
effects than more traditional sectors. Examining these
differences would contribute to a deeper understand -
ing of the contextual conditions under which integrated
marketing intelligence systems generate competitive
advantages.
Third, future studies could investigate the organiza -
tional and governance dimensions of AI-enabled mar -
keting analytics. While the AIMx framework highlights
the importance of human oversight, additional research
is needed to understand how firms design governance
structures, ethical guidelines, and decision protocols that
balance algorithmic recommendations with managerial
judgment.
Fourth, the macrolevel implications of integrated
marketing intelligence warrant further investigation.
Researchers could explore how aggregated analytics
capabilities across firms influence innovation diffusion,
digital ecosystem coordination, and economic resilience.
Such work would contribute to the emerging literature on
the role of data-driven infrastructures in shaping national
competitiveness and digital economic development.
Finally, future research may extend the AIMx frame -
work by incorporating broader societal dimensions,
including sustainability, responsible AI governance, and
digital ethics. Integrating environmental and social indi -
cators into marketing analytics architectures could help
organizations align data-driven decision making with the
goals of sustainable and inclusive economic development.
Together, these research directions highlight the poten-
tial for continued theoretical and empirical exploration
of AI-integrated marketing analytics systems and their
role in shaping adaptive organizational and economic
systems.
Final reflection
In increasingly complex and data-intensive markets,
integrated marketing intelligence systems will play an
important role in shaping organizational adaptability
and economic resilience. The AIMx framework provides
a conceptual foundation for understanding how arti -
ficial intelligence can coordinate predictive analytics,
experimentation, and managerial decision making within
adaptive marketing systems. By linking marketing meas -
urement integration with broader processes of learning
and competitiveness, the framework offers a basis for
future empirical research on AI-enabled strategic deci -
sion architectures.
