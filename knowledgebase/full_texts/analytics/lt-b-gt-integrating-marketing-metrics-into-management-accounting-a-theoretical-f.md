Abstract
This study examines the integration of marketing metrics into management accounting systems to enhance strategic
decision-making in a data-driven business environment. Despite the growing importance of marketing analytics, a gap
remains in linking marketing performance indicators with accounting outcomes. The study adopts a quantitative and
conceptual research design to analyze the relationship between key marketing metrics, including return on investment
(ROI), customer acquisition cost (CAC), customer lifetime value (CLV), and conversions, and financial performance
indicators such as contribution margin and net profit. Descriptive statistics, correlation analysis, and regression analysis are
employed to evaluate these relationships. The findings reveal that ROI and CLV have a significant positive impact on
financial performance, highlighting their role as key drivers of profitability. Conversions also contribute positively to net
profit, while CAC demonstrates a relatively weak influence, indicating the need for a balanced approach between
acquisition cost and long-term customer value. The results further show that fixed costs dominate the cost structure, and
budget variances reflect inefficiencies in financial planning. The study supports a three-layer integration framework linking
marketing metrics to accounting outcomes and strategic decisions. The findings contribute to both theory and practice by
providing a structured approach for integrating marketing and accounting functions, enabling organizations to improve
performance evaluation, optimize resource allocation, and achievesustainable competitiveadvantage.

1. Introduction
In the modern competitive and data-driven business
world, it is becoming more common that organisations
are using marketing metrics to measure their
performance and make strategic decisions. The
improvements in digital technologies and analytics have
made companies amass and analyse a large volume of
customer and market data, making marketing
traditionally lensed into quantifiable and responsible
functions (Akter et al., 2016;Erevelles et al., 2016; Wedel
and Kannan, 2016). This has led to the fact that
marketing is no longer considered as a cost centre but as
a major contributor to performance and value creation
inthe firm.
Noted metrics in marketing like the return on
investment (ROI), customer lifetime value (CLV), and
customer engagement haveattracted much attention due
to their capacity to connect marketing with financial
performance. It has been proved that these measures are
paramount in optimising the organisation performance
through a better allocation of resources and alignment
of the strategies (Ali and Shabn, 2024; Hanssens and
Pauwels, 2016; Katsikeas et al., 2016). Specifically, CLV
has become an essential measure of customer
profitability over time, whereas ROI gives the efficient
impression of marketing performance. Moreover,
customer experience and customer engagement are
becoming a key contributor to shareholder value and
competitive edge (Beckers et al., 2018; Bolton etal., 2018;
Homburgetal., 2017).
The high pace of development of digital marketing has
further underlined the significance of incorporating
superior analysis tools and methods into marketing
decision-making. The marketing activities have
broadened due to digital platforms, augmented reality,
and social media, and more complex measurement tools
have to be used to identify their contribution to
performance (Berman, 2018; Du et al., 2022; Järvinen

and Karjaluoto, 2015). Also, marketing communications
and analytics have allowed organisations to create better
strategies, which meet the larger business goals (Batra
and Keller,2016).
Although these have been achieved, there is still a big
gap in the adoption of marketing metrics as part of the
management accounting systems. The conventional
accounting models are centred on financial reporting
and cost management abilities at the expense of the
strategic worth of the marketing operations. This lack of
connexion restricts the capacity of organisations to
utilise marketing knowledge to the maximum in
decision-making and performance analysis. Recent
literature has emphasised the necessity to develop a
more integrated process that will integrate the marketing,
accounting, and finance functions to enhance the
organisational performance (Dimitropoulos et al., 2025;
Battisti et al., 2021).
Internal organisational factors that also determine the
relationships between marketing investments and firm
performance include marketing capabilities and
departmental power. The studies show that strategic
positioning of marketing in organisations has a
substantial influence on its financial performance
contribution (Feng et al., 2015). Equally, investments in
brand and customer-focused approaches have been
identified to add firm value by increasing the connexion
between marketing endeavours and financial results
(Fischerand Himme, 2017).
Furthermore, the growing complexity of customer
journeys and interactions on multiple channels requires
themore holistic approach toperformance measurement.
Customer behaviour and experience in digital, physical,
and social contexts are crucial to the development of
effective marketing strategies and enhancement of the
financial results (Bolton et al., 2018; Homburg et al.,
2017). Incorporation of customer equity and brand
equity views contributes to the development of
marketing measure with financial goals further (Calder
et al., 2025).
Considering such developments, the necessity to include
marketing metrics in management accounting systems so
as to improve strategic decisions is on the increase. This
kind of integration can help organisations to assess the
financial value of marketing activities, allocate resources
in a better way and gain sustainable competitive
advantage. Nevertheless, there is still a lack of empirical
studies in the field with specific reference to establishing
the connexion between marketing performance
measuresand accounting performance.
Thus, the analysis will set out to construct and provide
empirical examples of a theoretical framework to
incorporate the marketing metrics into management
accounting systems. Through the analysis of the
connexionsbetween the strategicmarketing pointers and
the financial results, the research aims at offering an
understanding of how organisations can use the
marketing data to enhance decision-making and
performance. By so doing, the research provides not only
to the literature but also to the practise of managers by
providing a systematic way ofincorporating the measures
of performance used to bridge the gap between the
functions of marketingand accounting.
2. Methods
2.1 Research Design
The research design adopted in this study is quantitative
and conceptual research investigating the use of
marketing measures in management accounting in order
to make strategic decisions. The quantitative
methodology allows the researcher to analyse
quantifiable causation between marketing performance
measures and financial performance whereas the
conceptual dimension aims at creating an analytical
framework that connects the variables to managerial
decision making. Through empirical analysis coupled
with theoretical development, the paper will give a global
picture of how the marketing metrics can be integrated
into the accounting system to enable better and more
informed strategic decisions.
2.2 Data Description
This analysis is founded on an organised system of
variables that measures the performance of the
marketing and accounting results, including the
appropriate control factors. A variety of variables
pertaining to marketing are impressions, clicks, and
conversions, which are indicators of the reach and level
of engagement of the campaign, and derived metrics,
including click-through rate (CTR), cost per click (CPC),
return on investment (ROI), customer acquisition cost
(CAC), and customer lifetime value (CLV), which are
indicators of efficiency and value creation in the long
term.
The accounting variables provide financial performance
through fixed cost, variable cost, total cost, contribution
margin, and net profit that allow evaluating the
profitability and efficiency of operations. Moreover,
budget allocated and budget variance are included as
control variables as they represent planning and
monitoring mechanisms in the management accounting
to be able to evaluate the impact of the deviations in
terms of planned expenditures affecting the overall
performance.
2.3 Analytical Techniques
Various analytical methods are used to analyse the
connexion between marketing and accounting measures.
The data is summarised with the help of descriptive
statistics that will help outline the performance trend in
the sphere of marketing and financial variables. The
analysis is performed in terms of the correlation in order
to determine the strengths and direction of association
between the key marketing indicators and accounting
results with a particular emphasis on the profitability
measuresincluding contributionmargin and net profit.
Besides, regression is used to measure the effects of the
marketing measures on the financial performance. In
particular, the independent variables in the studies were

2.4 Conceptual Framework
The research suggests a three-layered integration model
that is connected to the marketing performance,
accounting performance, and strategic decision-making
in a single model. Measures of marketing like
impressions, clicks and conversions can be regarded as
the initial input which can be used to denote the
performance of the marketing activities. These inputs are
converted to accounting results, such as contribution
margin and net profit, which show the financial
consequences of marketing. The financial insights
obtained are then used to make strategic decisions
regarding budgeting, allocation of resources and
performance reviews. This model identifies management
accounting as an essential linkage that can change
marketing data into practical financial data to increase
the capacity of the organisation to make strategic
decisions based ondata.

4. Discussion
The research results of this paper are essential ideas that
help in understanding the integration of marketing
metrics with management accounting systems and how
they assist in improving the strategic decision-making
process. The findings indicate that marketing
performance metrics including ROI, CLV, and
conversions are strongly related to financial results,
specifically the net profit and the contribution margin.
The findings contribute to the increasing trend of
marketing as a value-generating activity, as opposed to a
costcentre.
One of the main findings of the research is the very
strong positive correlation between the return on
investment (ROI) and net profit that emphasise the
significance of marketing efficiency in the financial
performance. This is in line with the previous studies
that note the importance marketing accountability and
performance measurement to enhance organisational
performance (Ramachandran, 2023). Effective use of
marketing resources can help companies in achieving
greater returns and hence the connexion of marketing
activities and financial performance is enhanced.
Another important determinant of profitability that is
found to be of critical importance is that of customer
lifetime value (CLV) which is positively associated with
net profit and contribution margin. This observation
can be related to the work on customer-centric strategies,
where the emphasis is laid on the fact that long-term
customer value is a factor that contributes to sustainable
competitive advantage (Kumar and Reinartz, 2016).
Through the emphasis on high value customers and
retention, companies would be able to improve on the
revenue creation as well as financial stability.
Furthermore, the value creation depends significantly on
customer engagement since the engaged customers
increase their profitability and market position (Kumar
and Pansari, 2016).
The findings also show that conversions positively affect
net profit in the regression but the correlation between
conversion and profitability is not very strong as in the
correlation analysis. This implies that, although the
higher the conversions, the higher the revenue generated,
it all depends on the cost efficiency and allocation of
resources. This observation underscores the significance
of using accounting measures along with marketing
measures to enable increased activity to translate to real
profitability. Past research has also pointed to the fact
that marketing performance must be measured not only
based on volume measures but also concerning their
financial performance (Petersen and Kumar,2015).
Customer acquisition cost (CAC) on the other hand,
does not demonstrate a strong impact on net profit
which implies that the financial performance cannot be
attributed to acquisition costs only. This implies that the
success of acquisition strategies will be based on the
value that will be created by the customers in the long
run. The interaction between the customer value and
acquisition costs is important to note as it is essential to
have a holistic approach to performance measurement,
where the short- and long-term efficiency, as well as the
long- and short-term value, are taken into account. The
view is supported by the studies of the customer-based
valuation models, indicating the necessity to balance the
cost of the acquisition with the customer lifetime value
(Llobet Genaro,2023).
A cost structure analysis gives additional knowledge
about the financial nature of organisations. The
prevalence of fixed costs in the description statistics and
figures suggests the existence of a large percentage of
expenses that are not directly related to production or
sales volume. This observation has significant
consequences to managerial decisions whereby, high
fixed costs make financial risk and flexibility less.
Marketing strategies are needed in such situations aimed
at adequate revenue to fund these expenses. The
significance of a combination of marketing and financial
strategies has been identified in the previous studies that
underscore the importance of the integrated strategies in
enhancing the firm performance (Pauwels and Neslin,
2015).
Upon these findings on the budget, control mechanisms
in management accounting systems are also emphasised.
The availability of budget variance indicates that there
are variations between planned and actual spending
which implies that there might be inefficiency in the way
the resources are spent. Budget management has hence
become a very important aspect of improving the
financial performance and bringing about alignment
between the strategic objectives and the operations. This
is in line with studies that haveshown the significance of
performance measurement systems in making
managerial decisions and enhancing organisational
performance (Hanssens etal., 2016).
The paper is also adding to the general research on
digital and data-driven marketing. Another indicator of
the growing significance of analytics in decision-making
processes is the inclusion of marketing metrics into
accounting systems, including ROI and CLV. The digital
marketing spaces allow huge amounts of data to be
produced, thus allowing companies to estimate
performance more accurately and make decisions. Past
studies have emphasised the importance of digital

5. Conclusion
This study examined the integration of marketing
metrics intomanagement accounting systems to enhance
strategic decision-making. The findings demonstrate that
marketing performance indicators such as return on
investment (ROI), customer lifetime value (CLV), and
conversions play a significant role in influencing
financial outcomes, particularly net profit and
contribution margin. The results confirm that marketing
activities are not merely operational functions but key
drivers of organizational value. A major insight from the
study is the strong relationship between marketing
efficiency and profitability, highlighting the importance
of aligning marketing investments with financial
objectives. The dominance of fixed costs and the
presence of budget variances further emphasize the need
for effective cost control and performance monitoring
within management accounting systems. Integrating
marketing metrics into these systems enables
organizations to better evaluate performance, allocate
resources efficiently, and improve financial outcomes.
The study also supports the proposed conceptual
framework, demonstrating that marketing metrics can be
systematically translated into accounting outcomes and
used to inform strategic decisions. By linking marketing
performance with financial measures, organizations can
enhance decision quality and achieve sustainable
competitive advantage. Overall, the research contributes
to both theory and practice by providing a structured
approach for integrating marketing and accounting
functions. This integration is essential in today’s data-
driven business environment, where informed decision-
making is critical for long-termsuccess.
