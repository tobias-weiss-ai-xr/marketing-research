ABSTRACT
This conceptual paper examines higher education business curricula, focusing on the Malaysian Qualifications
Agency (MQA) Body of Knowledge (BoK) for Diploma in Business programmes. A business diploma serves a
dual-pathway objective: preparing graduates for immediate entry into the workforce at the frontline supervisory
level or equipping them with the theoretical foundation to pu rsue a Bachelor’s degree. Despite this, current
frameworks relegate customer service to a minor subtopic embedded within marketing modules. Empirical
literature and modern service crises demonstrate that frontline supervision depends heavily on behavioral,
emotional, and operational competencies rather than macro -marketing analytics.  Through a synthesis of
regulatory benchmarks, industry service failures, and generation -specific psychological profiles, this paper
develops an operationalized framework bridgi ng Generation Z attributes, Emotional Intelligence (EI)
development, and professional readiness. We argue that customer service should be repositioned as an
independent, standalone core knowledge domain within business diploma syllabi to enhance graduate
employability, cultivate resilience, and mitigate organizational reputational risk.
Keywords: Curriculum Realignment, Customer Service Education, Emotional Intelligence (EI), Generation Z,
Frontline Supervisory Competencies
INTRODUCTION
According to officia l labor metrics from the Department of Statistics Malaysia, structural underemployment
remains a challenge, with many diploma holders entering semi -skilled roles within sales, retail, and service
operations (Muhammad Hisyam Bin Mohamad, 2022). This reality underscores the dual-pathway imperative of
diploma-level business education: it must serve as an immediate vehicle for career readiness in lower -level
management roles while remaining a rigorous academic stepping stone for transition into a Bachelor’s deg ree
(Muhammad Hisyam Bin Mohamad, 2022).
Despite this dual responsibility, a structural misalignment exists within current national curriculum standards.
The guidelines issued by the Malaysian Qualifications Agencyn (MQA) does not position customer service as a
standalone domain. Instead, it is treated as a peripheral learning component within the broader marketing cluster.
While traditional marketing modules focus on strategic market analytics and macro branding, they systematically
underrepresent the behavioral, emotional, and operational dimensions required to navigate real -time customer
interactions and service recoveries.
This deficit penalizes both pathways. Workforce-bound students graduate underprepared for real-world service
recovery, while degree -bound students transfer with gaps in applied behavioral science. This skills gap is
increasingly visible in public consumer complaints and operational service failures across regional sectors
(Bernama, 2023; The Star, 2022). The problem is further magnified by the distinct psychological profiles of the
current student demographic: Generation Z (Gen Z). Although digitally literate, empirical evidence indicates

LITERATURE REVIEW
Customer Service and Emotional Intelligence
The Theoretical Divide: Strategic Marketing vs. Operational Customer Service
Within traditional literature, customer service is typically conceptualized as a transactional component of the
marketing mix aimed at increasing brand loyalty (Heuett, 2017). However, a distinct operational divide separates
strategic marketing from customer service execution. Marketing focuses on customer acquisition and value
proposition design; customer service addresses real -time value delivery, relationship maintenance, and service
recovery.
Empirical studies demonstrat e that customer service competence comprises an intricate body of structured
knowledge and interpersonal soft skills that can be taught systematically (Pretko, 2018; Versace, 2007). Core
skills like active listening, empathy, and structured problem-solving serve as foundational operational tools for
frontline supervisors to prevent minor service issues from escalating into major corporate crises (Pretko, 2018).
For degree-bound students, these concepts provide an essential theoretical foundation in organizational behavior,
corporate communication, and service management principles.
Generation Z and Emotional Intelligence Profiles
The necessity of formal customer service education is magnified by the developmental profile of Generation Z,
who generally exhibit lower overall EI scores relative to older generations (Fedorova et al., 2023). High reliance
on digital interaction platforms has enhanced Gen Z's visual stimulus processing but simultaneously impaired
face-to-face emotional articulation and linguistic nuance (Gawda et al., 2024).
Literature establishes that EI is a significant predictor of behavioral regulation and mental resilience; lower EI
scores correlate with higher vulnerability to anxiety, stress, and defensive reactions under pressure (Evrensel -
İnanç et al., 2022; Khanna et al., 2025). Structural equation modeling demonstrates that heavy digital
connectivity exerts a negative influence on an individual's capacity to process and regulate real -time
collaborative emotional states (Imjai et al., 2024) . Because modern higher education frameworks traditionally
prioritize cognitive and technical milestones over affective development, dedicated customer service courses
offer a vital, applied pedagogical platform to operationalize EI traits for the professional landscape (Rodionova
et al., 2022).
Structural Mapping of the Higher Education Framework
The critical gap between institutional expectations and actual industry outcomes is rooted in current national
curriculum standards. An evaluation of the MQA Body  of Knowledge guidelines highlights a significant
structural omission in how frontline business competencies are categorized.

Empirical Evidence of Service Redundancy: Interrogating Contemporary Service Failures
To understand the systemic gap in the embedded marketing-centric model, we must bridge theoretical curriculum
design with empirical industry outcomes. Higher education benchmarks frequently proceed on the flawed
pedagogical assumption that strategic marketing analytics naturally yield frontline operational fluidity. However,
analyzing contemporary consumer service failures reveals a severe discrepancy between academic strategic
preparation and operational capability.
Cross-Industry Escalation Dynamics
The risks of neglecting frontline service recovery are clearly illustrated by established global crises. In the Air
India case, operational audits revealed that massive reputational fallout from an in -flight incident was driven
less by the initial disruption and more by delayed, unempathetic communication from frontline supervisors
(Sunil, 2024). Similarly, the Meru Cabs case demonstrates that advanced digital CRM tools can either support
or completely undermine customer trust depending on how effectively supervisors handle data transparency and
human communication during operational breakdowns (Vaithianathan & Bolar, 2013).
When mapped onto the Malaysian baseline, national news agencies and regulatory bodies like the Malaysian
Communications and Multimedia Commission confirm that a vast majority of local consumer grievances stem
directly from service responsiveness and defens ive communication breakdowns by frontline supervisors rather
than technical issues (Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission, 2022). These insights
demonstrate that frontline service failures are ultimately marketing failures that erode brand equ ity. Standard
operating procedures (SOPs) are insufficient if supervisors lack structured training in customer psychology,
emotional triggers, and real-time crisis communication.
Operational Behavioral Breaches
The boundaries of customer service execution demand active emotional regulation and structural de -escalation
strategies—behavioral milestones that traditional marketing analytics cannot cultivate. A profound
manifestation of this gap materialized in the media-reported Tealive data leak and customer harassment incident

Regulatory Compliance, Cyber Risk, and Administrative Friction
Beyond human-to-human interactions, operational customer service encompasses strict information governance,
client reassurance, and administrative consistency. The Bank Rakyat  cybersecurity breach and subsequent
RM1,000,000 regulatory penalty from Bank Negara Malaysia (2026) for non-compliance with the Management
of Cyber Risk (RMIT) frameworks highlights this reality. While fundam entally a data architecture failure, the
ensuing crisis exposed a massive supervisory service deficit: the immediate operational imperative shifted to
managing customer anxiety and restoring systemic trust through focused relationship maintenance, areas fo r
which marketing-trained supervisors remain ill-equipped.
The presence of these systemic deficits is further validated by macro -regulatory interventions, notably the
MCMC Broadcaster Accountability Mandates, which legally bound licensees to close at least 70% of consumer
grievances within three business days due to long -term internal service resolution failures (The Star, 2026).
Furthermore, the Malaysian Media Council (2026) exposed a severe administrative disconnect across national
media networks: a complete lack of integrated, standardized public redress platforms, creating massive friction
for consumers navigating complex grievance procedures.
Sidelining service resolution pipelines delays consumer remediation, triggers strict government crackdowns, and
demonstrates the severe organizational costs of fragmented service operations. When customer service is denied
structural validation as an independent course of study at the diploma level, organizations construct disjointed,
confusing, and frustrating service channels that ultimately compromise public trust.
Proposed Conceptual Framework
To resolve the systemic, operational, and regulatory alignment issues identified across modern service
ecosystems, this paper introduces a comprehensive structural framework. The model replaces the unstructured,
embedded marketing approach with an operationalized path model where Curriculum Orientation serves as the
primary driver of Graduate Operational Readiness, fully mediated by structured Customer Service Knowledge
Domains.

Core Constructs of the Framework
● Independent Variable (IV): Curriculum Orientation – Compares the baseline Embedded Marketing-
Centric Orientation  (where customer service is a brief, secondary component) against the proposed
Dedicated Stand-Alone Customer Service Orientation (established as an independent, compulsory core
course featuring targeted experiential and affective learning).
● Mediating Variables (M): Customer Service Knowledge Domains  – Repositioning the curriculum
requires introducing four targeted knowledge domains to bridge traditional marketing gaps:
1. Interpersonal Service Skills and Affective Regulation:  Operationalizes EI theory through applied
exercises in active listening, empathy, stress control, and tactical de-escalation.
2. Consumer Behavioral Psychology and Injustice Perception: Examines consumer behavior during service
failures, focusing on emotional triggers and the mechanics of service recovery.
3. Omnichannel and Technology -Mediated CRM Competence:  Provides hands-on training in nav igating
CRM systems, maintaining digital communication etiquette, and managing public feedback loops.
4. Regulatory Compliance and Information Governance Architecture:  Evaluates national regulatory
mandates, data security frameworks (e.g., PDPA, MCIPD), and s tructured complaint management
systems.
● Dependent Variable (DV): Diploma Graduate Operational Readiness – Measures the final readiness
of business diploma graduates, functionally splitting to match the dual -pathway objective: Pathway A
(Workforce) evaluates the graduate's capability to act as an effective lower -level supervisor and protect
organizational reputation, while Pathway B (Academic)  evaluates the foundational conceptual literacy
required to transition seamlessly into advanced Bachelor's degree coursework.
Theoretical Hypotheses
● H1: A Dedicated Stand -Alone Customer Service Orientation in Diploma in Business curricula is
positively associated with higher levels of Graduate Operational Readiness across both immediate
workforce entry and advanced academic degree progression pathways.

DISCUSSION & IMPLEMENTATION STEPS
The synthesis of literature and practical evidence underscores that the missing alignment in curriculum design
is an opportunity for continuous improvement to match rapid societal and economic changes. The shift toward
a digital-first service economy presents dual challenges: Gen Z students exhibit developmental vulnerabilities in
real-time emotional processing, while consumers demand rapid, emotionally intelligent, and flawless service
delivery (Imjai et al., 2024). Customer service is not jus t about "being nice"; it is a practical application of
advanced communication, conflict resolution, and emotional intelligence. When regulatory frameworks relegate
customer service to a single lecture within marketing, they ignore the reality of human beha vior in service
encounters. You can automate marketing analytics and digitize promotional campaigns, but emotional
intelligence during a service breakdown cannot be outsourced.

CONCLUSION
Theoretical and Practical Implications
Theoretically, this study advances curriculum development discourse by establishing a clear Marketing-Service-
EI continuum, positioning customer service not as a basic transactional subset of marketing, but as the
operational fulfillment of marketing promi ses. Practically, these findings serve as a highly relevant guide for
higher education decision makers, curriculum review boards, and accrediting bodies like the MQA. Sidelining
customer service education to insert advanced marketing theory weakens the dua l-pathway value of business
diplomas. Re -engineering business curricula to feature customer service as a standalone course provides a
structured, evidence -based pathway to directly elevate graduate employability at the lower -tier supervisory
management level while reinforcing the foundational academic competencies required to successfully transition
into any specialized business Bachelor's degree discipline.
Limitations and Future Research
As a conceptual paper, this study is limited by its reliance on secondary literature and qualitative case synthesis
rather than primary empirical testing. Geographically, its immediate contextual focus centers on the Malaysian
higher education landscape, which may limit generalizability to distinct international frameworks . Future
research should empirically validate the proposed conceptual framework through quantitative or mixed-method
designs. Longitudinal studies tracking diploma graduates into both the immediate workforce and higher
academic tracks could evaluate the direct correlation between standalone customer service training, supervisory
EI levels, and objective performance metrics. Additionally, further studies should explore the dynamics of digital
emotional intelligence (DEI) as human supervisors and advanced sch olars increasingly navigate hybrid, AI -
mediated customer service touchpoints.
