Abstract. The purpose of this article is to explore the role of analytical tools in modern marketing and to assess
their influence on decision-making, competitiveness, and consumer engagement in the context of the data-driven
era. In particular, the study seeks to classify the main categories of analytical methods, determine the principles
of their effective application, and identify the advantages and limitations of using analytics in practice. Special
attention is given to the growing relevance of data-driven marketing strategies in conditions of digital
transformation, the rapid development of big data technologies, and the increasing importance of artificial
intelligence in shaping consumer behavior. Methodology. The research is based on a qualitative analytical
approach that combines a comprehensive review of secondary sources, academic literature, industry reports, and
practical case studies. A comparative analysis of several widely used analytical tools was carried out, including
Google Analytics, Serpstat, KISSmetrics, and SurveyMonkey. These instruments were evaluated in terms of their
functions, usability, advantages, and shortcomings. The study also applied a problem-oriented perspective,
focusing on how analytics contributes to solving concrete business challenges, such as audience segmentation,
campaign optimization, cost reduction, and ROI improvement. The results confirm that marketing analytics has
evolved into a central strategic function rather than a supplementary activity. Its application enables companies to
segment audiences with greater precision, optimize marketing investments, enhance personalization, and forecast
consumer demand at both micro and macro levels. At the same time, the study highlights key risks, including
misuse of statistical data, reliance on vanity metrics that do not reflect business outcomes, and ethical challenges
related to privacy and transparency. The findings further emphasize the future potential of predictive and
prescriptive models, as well as AI-driven tools, which are expected to transform marketing analytics into an even
more powerful driver of competitiveness. Practical implications. The study formulates several recommendations
for enterprises seeking to enhance their marketing performance through analytics. These include: developing
integrated systems that connect CRM, ERP , and advertising platforms for end-to-end evaluation; investing in
automation and advanced digital tools to ensure timely insights; prioritizing staff training to develop both
technical and interpretive competences; applying analytics to consumer-centric strategies, with a strong focus
on personalization; adhering to ethical standards of data use to preserve trust and ensure compliance with legal
frameworks such as GDPR. Collectively, these measures allow companies not only to evaluate past performance
but also to engage in proactive forecasting and innovation. The originality of this article lies in its integrated
perspective that combines theoretical foundations with practical recommendations and comparative analysis of
specific analytical tools. Unlike studies that treat analytics as an auxiliary technique, this research demonstrates
its central role as a strategic instrument of modern marketing management. By linking micro-level insights into
consumer behavior with macro-level market forecasting, the paper offers a holistic framework for understanding
the impact of marketing analytics. The value of the study is reflected in its practical relevance for both small and
large enterprises, providing them with clear guidelines on how to implement analytics effectively and sustainably
in the data-driven era.

1. Introduction
In the era of digital transformation, marketing
is no longer limited to creative communication
or persuasive advertising. T oday, marketing
decisions are largely shaped by data – its
collection, interpretation, and strategic application.
This process is known as marketing analytics ,
which integrates statistical methods, digital
technologies, and business intelligence tools to
transform raw data into actionable insights.
In today's rapidly developing digital technologies,
growing competition and dynamic consumer
demand, marketing is no longer just an art of
intuition and experience. Instead, it increasingly
relies on scientific approaches and evidence-
based data. At the heart of this transformation
are analytical methods – a set of tools that allow
you to transform large volumes of unstructured
information into practical management decisions.
A modern marketer must not only think creatively,
but also have the skills to work with analytics in
order to create effective campaigns, adequately
respond to market changes and predict consumer
behavior. Marketing has become a flexible
strategic management tool that integrates various
approaches to studying the consumer, positioning
goods and services, optimizing pricing policy and
building effective communication with the target
audience. Analytical methods play a significant role
in this process, ensuring the systematic collection,
analysis, generalization and interpretation
of data for making informed marketing
decisions. Analytics in marketing allows you
to respond accurately and timely to changing
market conditions, track trends and consumer
preferences, identify the company's strengths and
weaknesses, and predict future results.
Marketing has evolved into a flexible tool of
strategic management that integrates diverse
approaches to consumer research, product and
service positioning, pricing optimization, and
effective communication with target audiences.
Analytical methods play a crucial role in this
process by ensuring the systematic collection,
processing, synthesis, and interpretation of
data to support informed marketing decisions.
The use of analytics in marketing enables timely
and accurate responses to changing market
conditions, facilitates the monitoring of trends
and consumer preferences, helps identify
organizational strengths and weaknesses, and
supports the forecasting of future outcomes. In the
21st century, marketing has become increasingly
dependent on digital technologies and large
data sets (Big Data), which underscores the
importance of advanced analytical skills and tools
( Johnson, Sihi, Muzellec, 2021).
The aim of this study is to investigate the role of
analytical tools in modern marketing within the
data-driven era, with a particular focus on their
impact on decision-making, competitiveness,
and consumer engagement, as well as on
identifying future development perspectives
for marketing analytics in a rapidly changing
digital environment. T o achieve this aim, the
research addresses several key objectives: to
review the evolution of marketing analytics and
define its conceptual foundations in the context of
the data-driven economy; to classify and analyze
existing analytical tools (descriptive, diagnostic,
predictive, prescriptive) and evaluate their
applications in marketing; to assess how data-
driven tools influence audience segmentation,
personalization, and customer journey
optimization; to examine the contribution
of marketing analytics to ROI improvement,
strategic decision-making, and competitiveness
of companies; to identify potential risks and
ethical challenges of applying analytical methods
in practice; to explore future perspectives
related to artificial intelligence, machine
learning, and big data integration; and finally,
to develop practical recommendations for
businesses and marketing professionals on the
effective implementation of analytical tools in the
data-driven era (Vesterinen, Colleagues, 2025).
2. Key Principles
of Proper Analytics in Marketing
Marketing analytics opens entirely new
horizons – from the microscopic analysis of
individual customer interactions to macro-
level forecasting of demand on national
and global markets. In the context of digital
transformation and the automation of marketing
processes, the role of data and corresponding
 analytical methods is constantly increasing.
This trend underscores the relevance of studying
analytical approaches as an integral element of
modern marketing activity.
Analytical methods in marketing represent
a set of systematized approaches to the collection,
processing, analysis, and interpretation of
marketing information with the purpose of

supporting well-grounded managerial decision-
making. These methods are equally applicable
to strategic planning – such as long-term market
positioning and investment decisions – and to
tactical adjustments of marketing campaigns
in response to immediate changes in consumer
behavior or competitor actions.
The central purpose of these methods is to ensure
that marketing decisions are based on empirical
data rather than intuition or assumptions . By
replacing guesswork with evidence-based insights,
analytics helps organizations not only increase
efficiency but also strengthen adaptability,
enhance competitiveness, and develop more
personalized relationships with customers in an
increasingly dynamic and data-driven marketplace.

analytics are presented in Figure 1.
The primary requirement of effective marketing
analytics is objectivity. This means identifying
and examining the data that directly affect the
problem at hand, while avoiding the influence of
personal biases. Insights should never be derived
from the subjective opinions of an analyst,
a manager, or even external stakeholders who
lack empirical evidence. Flexible analysis also
requires looking at the same dataset from multiple
perspectives, testing different hypotheses, and
allowing the data to challenge prior assumptions.
In this regard, objectivity and flexibility act as
safeguards against distorted conclusions that can
harm decision-making rather than support it.
Analytics should never be conducted
"for the sake of analytics". Companies often
fall into the trap ofinitiating research because
competitors are doing so, because they heard it
recommended at a seminar, or simply because
it appears to be standard practice. However,
true marketing analytics must be problem-
driven: it should aim to address specific business
needs. If a company cannot clearly articulate
where the «pain point» lies or communicate
this issue to the analyst or agency, the analysis
will not provide meaningful solutions. For
example, conducting a complex evaluation of
advertising channels is pointless if there is no
ability to track the customer journey end-to-end.
Without cross-channel, or "end-to-end", analytics,
decision-makers will lack the necessary context to
interpret the performance of individual marketing
activities.
The effectiveness of marketing analytics is
directly proportional to the clarity of the research
question. Vague demands such as "we want all
metrics to increase simultaneously" are bound
to fail because they do not provide analysts with
a measurable target. On the other hand, a task
like "we want to reduce marketing expenses with
minimal negative impact on sales" is much more
actionable. In such cases, an analyst can identify
inefficient channels, outdated audience segments,
and areas where current campaigns can be
optimized. Thus, the formulation of precise and
measurable objectives is essential for generating
insights that are both practical and relevant.
Finally, marketing analytics should not be
treated as an esoteric practice accessible only to
technical specialists. The results of analysis must
be understandable even to individuals without
advanced digital literacy. A properly designed report
should communicate insights clearly, using concise
explanations, visualizations, and straightforward
recommendations. This distinguishes genuine
analytics from the overwhelming reports often
delivered by agencies – hundreds of pages filled
with obscure graphs, charts, and technical jargon
that provide little real value to decision-makers
(Rehman, Colleagues, 2024; Petrescu, Krishen, 2024).

In summary, smart marketing analytics is built
on four pillars: objectivity, relevance, precision,
and simplicity . T ogether, these principles ensure
that analytics is not reduced to a ritualistic
practice but becomes a genuine management tool
that supports growth and innovation. A company
that follows these principles can transform
raw data into strategic knowledge, minimize risks,
and improve competitiveness in both digital and
offline markets.
3. Must-Have T ools for Marketing Analytics
While any analytical tool can be endlessly
customized – integrating complex systems,
adding API connections, and importing vast
amounts of data – this level of sophistication
is usually relevant only for large enterprises
and retailers managing hundreds of thousands
of products. In most other cases, businesses
can achieve significant results with a standard
toolkit that includes essential features. Below are
some of the most widely used and effective tools
for marketing analytics, each providing distinct
advantages for businesses of different scales.
T able 1 presents a comparative analysis of the
main tools for effective marketing research (Nan,
Yuping, Kongjue, 2024; Gupta, Colleagues, 2021).
This comparison demonstrates that each tool
occupies a distinct niche in marketing analytics.
Google Analytics provides a universal baseline for
web performance tracking, Serpstat is essential for
SEO/SEM analysis, KISSmetrics offers deep
behavioral and funnel insights, while SurveyMonkey
delivers primary data directly from consumers.
Their combined use provides businesses with
a comprehensive analytical ecosystem – from
digital traffic evaluation to consumer sentiment
measurement.
After summarizing the information regarding
the key advantages and limitations of marketing
analytics instruments, we consider it appropriate
to provide a detailed description of each of these
elements separately (Nan, Yuping, Kongjue, 2024;
Gupta, Colleagues, 2021).
1. Google Analytics remains one of the
fundamental platforms for tracking and analyzing
digital performance. Its dashboard consolidates
all statistics and KPIs in one place, using widgets
and visualizations that simplify perception and
reduce the need to switch between windows, tabs,
or documents:
– UTM tags make it possible to track not only
advertising channels but also specific groups,
segments, and even individual ads. By appending
tags to URLs, marketers can determine how
many conversions were generated by a particular
campaign, banner, or keyword.
– Automated reports allow users to configure
periodic email updates with chosen metrics,
ensuring that decision-makers receive timely
insights without manually running reports.
– Intelligence Events notify users in real time
about anomalies or issues with campaigns, such
as when a specific ad group stops performing.
This prevents losses that might otherwise be
discovered only weeks later.

2. Serpstat is a powerful SEO and SEM analytics
platform that provides both domain-level and
keyword-level insights:
– Domain analysis generates comprehensive
reports on a website’s performance, competitor
activity, and contextual advertising.
– Semantic analysis helps identify high-
performing keywords used by competitors,
expand the existing semantic core of a website, and
discover new opportunities for ranking.
– Position monitoring delivers timely updates on
search visibility and keyword rankings, enabling
businesses to diagnose traffic drops and monitor
brand-related search queries.
3. KISSmetrics offers a more advanced perspective
on customer behavior and the sales funnel:
– Custom events can be defined to track specific
user actions that may not be easily measurable in
other analytics tools.
– Sales funnel analytics provide detailed data for
each stage of the funnel, allowing marketers to
segment users and implement targeted actions,
such as sending tutorial emails, requesting reviews,
or offering premium upgrades.
– Personal-level data enables businesses to
monitor detailed customer journeys – for example,
identifying when a user first attended a webinar,
how many purchases they have made since
2015, and their most recent interaction with a
promotional email.
4. SurveyMonkey specializes in collecting customer
insights through professional surveys.
– Custom survey design allows the integration of
brand elements such as logos and themes, ensuring
the survey aligns with corporate identity.
– Adaptive scenarios enable dynamic question
flow depending on user responses. For example,
if a respondent indicates they do not read the
corporate blog, follow-up questions can shift
to understanding what type of content would
engage them.
– Advanced filtering helps segment results
by age, location, source, or other criteria, enabling
quick and targeted analysis of audience groups.
T ogether, these tools – Google Analytics,
Serpstat, KISSmetrics, and SurveyMonkey –
form a robust foundation for marketing analytics.
They allow businesses to move beyond intuition
and embrace evidence-based decision-making.
From tracking website traffic and evaluating SEO
performance to analyzing customer journeys
and collecting direct feedback, these instruments
provide both micro- and macro-level insights. For
small and medium-sized businesses, the "standard
toolkit" ensures efficiency without excessive
complexity, while for large enterprises, these
tools can be expanded with APIs and advanced
integrations (Wong, 2023).
4. Conclusions
The results of this study clearly demonstrate that
analytical tools are no longer optional add-ons
but essential components of marketing activity
in contemporary enterprises. They provide the
foundation for understanding consumer behavior,
optimizing resource allocation, and enhancing
the effectiveness of marketing campaigns.
Proper use of analytics allows companies to
move beyond intuition, applying evidence-based
methods to evaluate past performance, detect
inefficiencies, and anticipate future market
trends. In this sense, analytics functions as
a strategic compass that guides decision-makers
toward sustainable growth and competitiveness.
Enterprises that integrate analytical instruments
effectively are able to increase the return on
marketing investments, develop consumer-centric
approaches, and respond more flexibly to market
dynamics.
At the same time, the research highlights that
the effectiveness of marketing analytics depends
on the way it is implemented. T o enhance the
role of analytics, enterprises should prioritize
several key directions. First, they must integrate
systems and data sources into unified analytical
platforms, enabling cross-channel and end-to-
end evaluation of customer journeys. Second, it
is crucial to invest in advanced digital tools and
automation technologies – from web analytics
dashboards to AI-driven predictive models –
which provide deeper insights and faster decision-
making. Third, enterprises need to focus on staff
training and competence development , ensuring
that marketing specialists are not only creative
strategists but also skilled data interpreters.
Fourth, analytics should be applied to
personalization and consumer-centric strategies,
enabling companies to tailor communication,
products, and services to the needs of specific
customer segments. Finally, businesses must
adhere to the principle of ethical data use, ensuring
transparency, compliance with international
standards such as GDPR, and the building of long-
term trust with customers.

Based on these considerations, several
practical recommendations can be formulated
for enterprises seeking to improve their marketing
effectiveness through analytics. First, they
should begin with a clear definition of analytical
objectives, focusing on solving specific business
challenges rather than collecting data for its own
sake. Second, a phased approachis advisable:
companies can start with basic descriptive analytics
and gradually move toward more sophisticated
diagnostic, predictive, and prescriptive models.
Third, analytics should be applied not only for
retrospective evaluation of past activities but also
for proactive forecasting and strategic planning.
Finally, enterprises should foster a data-driven
organizational culture , in which decision-making
is grounded in evidence and analytical reasoning
rather than intuition or managerial preferences.
