 This paper will explore how, as part of Orange Jordan, one of the largest telecommunication firms
in the Jordanian market, the Marketing Analytics Capability (MAC) affects the Quality of
Decision-Making (DMQ). Precisely, it examines the implication of the major dimensions of the
marketing analytics capability such as data assimilation, analytics tools, analytical capabilities, and
data-oriented culture with how organisational analytics usage is a moderating aspect to the quality
of managerial decision-making. A stratified random sample of managerial and operational levels
was chosen on the full -time employees of Orange Jordan which were asked to respond to a
structured questionnaire. Data gathering was done between August and November 2024 and it was
analyzed through partial least squares structural equation modeling (PLS -SEM). The empirical
evidence shows that marketing analytics capability positively and significantly affects the quality
of decision making. Nevertheless, the modifying impact of the analytics use was also identified to
be of no significance. The study is also part of the growing literature on analytics-based marketing
since it presents empirical data on an emergent market setting. Practically, the results are important
to telecommunications m anagers since they highlight the need to intensify marketing analytics
ability to improve decision -making performance. These endeavors aid the creation of more
sustainable marketing approaches by way of better resource allocation based on data, better
organizational learning, and strategizing, therefore, adding to the larger aims of sustainable
development connected to innovation, digital infrastructure, and responsible organizational
approaches.

1. Introduction
Decision-making is one of the most vital factors of organizational performance, especially in a highly competitive and data -
intensive business like telecommunications (Aburub et al., 2024). Modern marketing conditions are witnessing a rise in the
use of marketing analytics capability (MAC) by organizations to improve the quality of decision- making (DMQ) by
converting large amounts of data into actionable information (Hamzeh & Aburub, 2024). MAC helps companies consolidate
various data streams, use sophistic ated analytical databases, and promote a culture of data use that can be applied to
managerial decision-making. Although its strategic significance has increased, a significant paucity of empirical evidence on
the direct effect that marketing analytics capability has on the quality of decisions in the telecommunications industry exists.
Until now, existing research has focused extensively on market orientation, artificial intelligence (AI), or big data analyti cs
alone (Hamzeh et al., 2025), as opposed to framing marketing analytics capability as a system asset that can improve decision
outcomes. Further support for analytics -centered decision -making is found in the growing focus on sustainability and
responsible organizational practices. Analytical capabilities in marketing help in more effective allocation of resources,
transparency, and alignment of resources and strategy, hence leading to sustainable business performance (Al-Nimer, 2022).

 1118
Organizations within the Jordanian context are stimulated to integrate the latest digital and analytical applications within the
framework of increased competitiveness and alignment with country- level digital transformation efforts and global
sustainable development objectives. Based on this, marketing analytics capability is not merely a quality- driving force of
decision-making but also a mechanism that can be used to ensure responsible and evidence -based marketing practices are
encouraged. Recent developm ents in AI -based analytics have transformed the marketing decision -making process with
predictive modeling, behavioral analysis of customers, and automatic generation of insights (Akroush & Al -Mohammad,
2010). Such developments augment the analytical richness of marketing analytics capacity to the extent that it facilitates the
ability of organizations to handle complex data sets and respond better to strategy.  However, the degree to which analytics -
based capabilities can lead to higher quality of decision- making is under -researched, especially in telecommunications
companies within emerging markets. In addition, although digital marketing practices are becoming more grounded in
analytical insights, their success still depends upon how well such insights are  actually applied in managerial decision -
making. The use of analytics is a key aspect that can ensure the gap between analytical capability and the effectiveness of
decision-making outcomes is bridged by converting data -driven insights into strategic moves . Although it is an important
factor, empirical efforts on analytics use as a moderating factor have not been conducted extensively to enhance the
relationship between marketing analytics capability and quality of decision -making. Understanding this role c an maximize
the value of analytics investment and ensure that analytical capabilities are deeply embedded into organizational decision -
making processes.

This paper therefore empirically investigates the influence that marketing analytics capability has on the quality of decision-
making within Orange Jordan, a dominant telecommunications firm. Moreover, it examines the moderating impact of
analytics use on the relationship between marketing analytics capability and decision -making quality. In particular, the
research questions addressed in the study include: (1) To what extent does marketing analytics capability affect the quality
of decision-making in the telecommunications environment? (2) What is the effectiveness of analytical insights in marketing
decisions? (3) Does the use of analytics enhance the relationship between marketing analytics prowess and decision- making
quality? The answers to these questions provide current research with empirical evidence in the context of an emerging
market and a deeper insight into analytics -based decision-making in the telecommunications industry. The rest of the paper
has the following structure. Section 2 reviews lite rature on the issue and formulates hypotheses. Section 3 presents the
research methodology. Section 4 gives the empirical findings, and Section 5 provides a discussion. Section 6 ends the study
with conclusions, limitations, and future studies.

2. Literature Review and Hypotheses Development

Recent studies show that the concept of marketing analytics capability (MAC) is increasingly strategic in improving
organizational performance in terms of sustainability- oriented outcomes (Hanandeh et al., 2021). Marketing technologies
supported by analyti cs, including sophisticated data platforms, AI -enhanced tools, and data -driven digital engagement
initiatives, improve the accuracy of decisions, agility and efficiency of operations, and friendly and sustainable business
practices (Alshawawreh, 2025). Analytics -based decision -making has been linked to Sustainable Development Goals,
especially SDG 9 (Industry, Innovation, and Infrastructure) and SDG 12 (Responsible Consumption and Production), in that
it allows companies to target better, minimize resource wastage, and use evidence- based marketing techniques. Marketing
analytics ability allows organizations to gather, integrate, and analyze large amounts of market and customer data
systematically, thus improving both the quality of decision- making and minimi zing reliance on opinions when making
decisions (Ashour, 2014). This capability is further enhanced by advanced analytics and AI -based tools that provide
predictive insights, real-time tracking, and enhanced resource allocation (Hijazin, 2024). On this bas is, marketing analytics
capability is increasingly considered an enabling factor for sustainable innovation and competitive advantage, especially in
data-intensive sectors like telecommunications.

2.1 Marketing Analytics Capability and Decision-Making Quality

Based on the resource -based perspective, marketing analytics ability is a combined organizational resource of analytical
infrastructure, technological tools, human skills, and decision-support processes (Khrisat & Khaddam, 2023; Ali et al., 2021).
According to previous research, companies that possess analytics power are in a better position to make sense of multifaceted
market information, adapt to the environment, and increase the efficacy of decisions (Abualrejal et al., 2021). Nevertheless,
empirical da ta on the connection between analytics ability and decision outcomes are inconclusive, since performance is
usually based on organizational integration and managerial commitment (Alqudah et al., 2021). This lack of consistency
shows that additional research should be conducted in telecommunication companies operating in emerging markets. Based
on that, the hypothesis in this study is as follows:

H1: Marketing analytics capability is positively related to decision-making quality.

2.2 AI-Enabled Analytics and Decision-Making Quality

Artificial intelligence has greatly improved marketing analytics ability by providing sophisticated predictive modeling,
customer behavior analysis, and automated insights (Abualrejal et al., 2021). AI -powered analytics can enhance better

decisions based on higher forecasting quality and strategic responsiveness (Alqudah et al., 2021). Nevertheless, even with its
benefits, there is little empirical evidence on AI -enabled analytics as a part of marketing analytics capability as opposed to
standalone technologies, especially in emerging markets (Alqudah et al., 2025). Based on this, this study hypothesizes:

H2: AI-enabled analytics capability is positively related to decision-making quality.

2.3 Analytics-Driven Digital Communication and Decision-Making Quality

Digital communication, made possible by analytics, helps organizations customize their approaches, assess campaign
success, and determine how marketing activities and customer demand match each other (Alqudah et al., 2025). There is
earlier evidence that d ata-based personalization increases engagement and leads to more informed marketing decisions
(Alqudah et al., 2025). In the telecommunications industry, where there are several digital touchpoints, the successful
application of analytics in communication decisions is particularly paramount. Thus, the following hypothesis is proposed:

H3: Analytics-driven digital communication is positively related to decision-making quality.

2.4 Analytics-Supported Content Strategies and Decision-Making Quality

Analytics-based content strategies allow companies to measure engagement rates, customer feelings, and conversion rates,
thereby enhancing the effectiveness of decision- making (Alqudah et al., 2025). Analytics -driven content is based on the
principles of consumer engagement theory, brand equity, strategic positioning, and relevancy (Imam, 2023). Considering the
scarcity of empirical data in telecommunications companies, the proposed hypothesis is as follows:

H4: Analytics-supported content strategies are positively related to decision-making quality.

2.5 Moderating Role of Analytics Utilization

The use of analytics indicates the extent to which analytical insights are integrated into managerial decision -making
processes. Although marketing analytics capability provides analytical resources, its success can be determined by the
interpretation and application of insights (Alshrouf et al., 2025). Increased use of analytics minimizes uncertainties, curbs
bias, and improves decision outcomes (Jum'a et al., 2024). In addition, analytics use enhances the effects of AI -enabled
analytics, digital communica tion, and content strategies by ensuring that analytical outputs are put into perspective and
translated into strategic activities (Al Daabseh & Aljawarneh, 2024; Qasim et al., 2024). Based on this, analytics use is
perceived to moderate the connection between marketing analytics aptitude and decision quality. Accordingly, the following
hypotheses are proposed:

H5: The use of analytics moderates the interrelationship between marketing analytics capability and decision quality.
H6: The use of analytics moderates the connection between AI-enabled analytics competence and decision quality.
H7: The use of analytics moderates the association between analytics-driven digital communication and decision quality.
H8: The use of analytics moderates the association between analytics-supported content strategies and decision quality.

between marketing analytics capability and its dimensions as predictors of decision -making quality, with analytics

3. Methodology

3.1 Sample

This research is based on a quantitative research approach. A structured questionnaire was used to collect data between
August and November 2024 from full -time employees of Orange Jordan, one of the largest telecommunications companies
in Jordan. A stratified random sampling technique was used to guarantee representation across organizational units and job
levels. Stratification was done according to organizational branches and functional departments, reflecting different variations
in decision-making practices within the company. Considering the variety of roles, a disproportionate stratified sampling
method was used so that the distribution of strata was sufficient and sampling error was minimized. Four hundred
questionnaires were sent out, with 372 returned. Following completeness and consistency screening, 351 valid responses
were used in the analysis, yielding a response rate of 87%, which is similar to other past empirical research (Hanandeh et al.,
2021; Alshawawreh, 2025; Ashour, 2014; Hijazin, 2024).  The final sample size meets the criteria for partial least squares
structural equation modeling (PLS-SEM) (Jum'a et al., 2024). Moreover, G*Power analysis showed that a sample size of 102
cases was enough to identify medium effect sizes. The obtained sample size thus surpasses the suggested size, providing
sufficient statistical power and strength of findings.

3.2 Measures

Data on the relationship between Marketing Analytics Capability (MAC) and Decision- Making Quality (DMQ) were
collected using a structured questionnaire. Constructs were chosen in accordance with the study objectives and a thorough
analysis of existing lite rature. Measurement items were derived to be content -valid based on prior studies, with slight
contextual changes to adjust for the operational setting at Orange Jordan. All items were measured on a five -point Likert
scale from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree), which is common in structural equation modeling studies and suitable
for capturing the degree of agreement among respondents.  The questionnaire underwent two- stage pretesting for reliability
and content validity before large-scale data collection. During the pre-test phase, the instrument was reviewed by ten scholars
and practitioners in marketing analytics and decision-making to determine clarity, relevance, and suitability of measurement
items. In the second phase, ten respondents from the target population provided feedback on item wording, structure, and
ordering. This feedback led to minor adjustments, including rearrangement of items to enhance clarity and smoothness.
Marketing Analytics Capability (MAC) was assessed with a multidimensional scale based on previous research (Gounaris et
al., 2007), measuring the organization's capacity to gather, combine, examine, and utilize marketing information for
managerial decisions. Four items were used to operationalize each dimension. Decision-Making Quality (DMQ) was assessed
on a four -item scale based on existing decision -making research (Berthon et al., 2001), capturing accuracy, timeliness,
effectiveness, and rationality in strategic and operational situations.

3.3 Common Method Bias

Harman's single-factor test was conducted to measure common method bias as per Podsakoff and Organ (1986). The findings
revealed that the first unrotated factor explained 31% of the total variance, which is less than the recommended 50% threshold.
This implies that common method bias does not have a major impact on this research.

3.4 Data Analysis Technique

Data analysis was performed using partial least squares structural equation modeling (PLS-SEM) with SmartPLS 4, which is
extensively used in exploratory and predictive studies in management research (Jum'a et al., 2024; Al Daabseh & Aljawarneh,
2024; Qasim et al., 2024). Analysis was conducted in two stages: evaluating the reliability and validity of the measurement
model, and evaluating the structural model to test the proposed hypotheses.

4. Results

4.1 Measurement Model

To test reliability, convergent validity, and discriminant validity, the measurement model was assessed. Outer loadings
measured indicator reliability, and all items met the recommended threshold of 0.70 (Hair et al., 2017), indicating good
internal consistency. Convergent validity was evaluated using average variance extracted (AVE), composite reliability (CR),
and Cronbach's alpha. AVE values of all constructs were greater than 0.50, and CR and Cronbach's alpha values were greater
than 0.70, proving suffi cient convergent validity and reliability. Discriminant validity was initially assessed through cross -
loadings, revealing that all indicators had stronger loadings on their own constructs than on others. Additionally, the Fornell-
Larcker criterion was used, where the square root of the AVE of each construct was greater than inter-construct correlations,
providing additional evidence of discriminant validity (Fornell & Larcker, 1981). All findings affirm that the measurement
model exhibits adequate reliability and validity for further structural model analysis  (See Tables 1-3).

4.2 Structural Model

Before hypothesis testing, the standardized root mean square residual (SRMR) was used to measure model fit. The SRMR
of 0.070 is lower than the accepted level of 0.08, indicating good model fit (Henseler et al., 2016). After confirming model

 1122
fit, the structural model was tested with regard to path coefficients, coefficient of determination (R²), effect sizes (f²), and
predictive relevance (Q²) through a bootstrapping procedure.  The coefficient of determination (R²) was used to test the
model's explanatory power. Findings indicate that Marketing Analytics Capability has significant explanatory power for
Decision-Making Quality (R² = 0.835), explaining 83.5% of the variance. The St one-Geisser Q² criterion was also used to
assess predictive relevance, obtaining a Q² of 0.633, which indicates strong predictive relevance. To determine the importance
of structural relationships, a bootstrapping test with 5,000 subsamples was used to derive bias-corrected confidence intervals
(Preacher & Hayes, 2008).  Hypothesis testing results showed a positive and statistically significant relationship between
Marketing Analytics Capability dimensions (Data Quality & Integration, Analytical Tools Sophistication, Insight Utilization)
and Decision-Making Quality, supporting H1, H2, and H4. However, Analytical Skills (H 3) did not show a significant direct
effect. All moderating hypotheses (H5–H8) regarding analytics use were not supported.

5. Discussion

The outcome of the hypothesis testing indicates that there is a significant positive influence of Marketing Analytics Capability
(MAC) on the Quality of Decision- Making (DMQ) in Orange Jordan. The conclusion supports the previous studies that
present the analytics capability as an essential organizational asset to improve the managerial decision-making process in the
data-intensive world. Through a combination of quality of data, powerful analytics software and meaningful use of insight,
organizations can enhance the quality, timeliness, and reasonability of marketing decisions. The data quality and integration
value is massive, which underscores the importance of high- quality and well- integrated data sources in decision -making
based on analytics. This finding corresponds to the previous research that underlines the fact that ineffective data quality may
deteriorate the performance of even advanced analytics systems. Data integration seems crucial in the telecommunications
scenario where the data is generated at various points of contact with customers, and the accuracy of the decision and strategic
direction are needed in this context. In the same vein, the beneficial impact of the sophistication of analytical tools valid ates
the literature on the topic that portrays the role of sophisticated analytics technologies in organizational intelligence.
Predictive and descriptive analytics can help decision -makers to see patterns and anticipate customer behavior and assess
strategic options more efficiently, thus enh ancing evidence-based marketing decisions in competitive markets. The results
also indicate that the use of insights can be a significant factor in improving the quality of decisions, which is why it sho uld
be noted that the quality of analytics is not only in the analysis of data but also in the effective application of analytical results
to actionable decisions. This justifies the perception of marketing analytics capability as a strategic process -based capability
and not a technological operational function. Conversely, the quality of decisions was not remarkably directly influenced by
analytical skills. This might indicate the growing adoption of automated analytics systems and centralized analytics groups
and a decline in the application of analytical a bilities on the individual level. These decision environments which are driven
by systems may restrict individual -level of impact of personal analytical skills on the decision outcomes. On the whole, the
present study adds to the body of literature on marketing analytics since it empirically demonstrates marketing analytics
capability as a higher-order construct that increases the quality of decisions made in the situation of an emerging market. On
the managerial level, the results indicate that telecommunication companies must focus more on data integration and insight
usage and invest in the further development of analytics technologies. Marketing analytics capability also enables sustainable
digital transformation by helping to make marketing decisions more informed and efficient, thus allocating resources better,
minimizing inefficiencies, and building customer-centric strategies.
6. Conclusions

6.1 Theoretical Implications

The research has various theoretical implications on the marketing analytics and decision- making literature. To start with, it
extends existing studies to the conceptualization of Marketing Analytics Capability as a higher -order construct to provide a
more integrated explanation of how analytics resources work together to improve the quality of decisions instead of analyzing
individual tools or systems. Second, the results prove empirically the relevance of Resource -Based View and the Dynamic
Capabilities Theory to explain the analytics-driven decision-making in the telecommunications industry. The findings support
the position that the key to high managerial performance lies not in the isolated technologies, but in the organizational
capabilities. Third, the insignificant role of some of the dimensions of capability is an indication that the impact of analytics
on decision-making depends on the context and maturity of analytics in organizations. This contests the assumptions that
individual analytical skills are adequate and argues that interaction between technological, human and organizational factors
should be investigated in future studies.

6.2 Practical Implications

Regarding the managerial aspects, the results indicate that investments in marketing analytics cannot be reduced to advanced
technologies only, but also to the incorporation of data and efficient use of insights. The telecommunications companies need
to focus on the development of a strong data infrastructure that allows the integration of customer and market data across the
organizational departments. The managers are also advised to include analytics insights in the decision- making process by
creating a data culture and by orienting analytical products on strategic goals. Although technical skills in analytics will not
be insignificant, the findings indicate that the concept of organizational procedures through which analytical findings can be
converted into policy action should be prioritized. When it comes to sustainability, the allocation of resources is more efficient
through analytics-driven decision-making, and unnecessary marketing activities are minimized, as well as customer -centric
strategies are reinforced. Such results lead to the accountable digital transformation and enhanced business performance.

6.3 Social Implications

The growing popularity of marketing analytics prompts valuable social and ethical issues regarding the privacy of data, their
disclosure, and digital responsibility. A company in the telecommunications sector should be able to guarantee that the
decisions adopted using analytics do not violate the rules of data protection and do not interfere with the privacy of the
consumers. Besides, using analytics efficiently will also demand constant upskilling and digital literacy of employees.
Responsible interpretat ion and analytics application may be improved by investing in training programs. The honest and
transparent application of the customer data may also boost the consumer trust and contribute to the social legitimacy of the
analytics-based marketing practices.

6.4 Limitations and Future Research

Although the study has its contributions, there are several limitations to the study. To begin with, the study was in one
telecommunications firm in Jordan, and this might not be generalizable in other telecommunication firms. Future research
might focus on a variety of organizations or cross-country analysis. Second, the cross-sectional type of research design limits
causal inference. The longitudinal studies might give a more thoughtful understanding of the dynamic nature of connection
between marketing analytics capability and quality of decisions. Third, other mediating or moderating variables might be
included in future research including organizational culture, leader support, or regulatory factors. Lastly, the use of self -
reported survey data can lead to bias in the responses; upcoming research can consider using objective performance measures
or qualitative research to augment empirical knowledge.
