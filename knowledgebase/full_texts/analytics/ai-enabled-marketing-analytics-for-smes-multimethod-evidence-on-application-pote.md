Abstract
Artificial Intelligence (AI) is transforming marketing analytics by enabling advanced data processing, predictions, and
decision support. However, small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) often face substantial barriers to AI adoption
due to limited organizational, technological, and human resources. To bridge theoretical and practical perspectives, this
study employs a hybrid approach combining a systematic literature review of 49 studies with an empirical survey of more
than 100 SMEs. The findings identify organizational, technological, and user-related conditions, as well as AI application
potential, that shape AI adoption in SMEs. Based on these insights, a conceptual framework is proposed that differentiates
between opportunistic, operational, strategic readiness, and strategic AI use. The framework translates SMEs’ AI applica -
tion potential and adoption conditions into practice, thus structuring AI-enabled marketing analytics, which provides SMEs
with new opportunities for data-driven operations despite resource constraints.

While AI provides numerous opportunities, organiza -
tions do not realize its potential equally. Although the
number of companies using AI is increasing in all size cat -
egories, the German Federal Statistical Office reports that
in 2024, only 28% of medium-sized companies and 17%
of small companies in Germany used AI, compared to 48%
of large corporations (Statistisches Bundesamt 2024). Since
SMEs constitute 99% of all businesses (European Commis-
sion, 2003) and thus play an essential role in the economy,
it is necessary to further investigate the reasons for SMEs’
lower implementation rate in order to improve acceptance
and adoption. With the challenges of limited resources
(Garengo et al. 2005), insufficient innovativeness (Ćirović
et al. 2026), and the requirement of digital capabilities to
achieve resilience (Conz et al. 2026), SMEs face differ -
ent obstacles regarding AI implementation as opposed to
larger organizations, which requires independent research.
In addition, outlining the potential for SMEs to implement
AI into their marketing analytics provides new opportuni -
ties to enhance their operations, since SMEs often lack a
market research department or specialists and thus depend
on externally conducted market research.
Introduction
Artificial Intelligence (AI) is constantly gaining attention
and becoming increasingly relevant to businesses, which
is reflected in an ongoing rise in AI use documented in a
study by Singla et al. ( 2025). Especially the discipline of
marketing is found to be highly affected and relevant to the
increasing AI implementation (Vlačić et al. 2021), where
e.g., machine learning (ML) finds numerous applications
(Muth et al. 2025a, 2026; Ngai and Wu 2022) and is even
considered a crucial category (V olkmar et al. 2022). Nev-
ertheless, research on AI in marketing analytics remains
scarce, particularly for specific types of companies.

This study applies a state-of-the-art hybrid systematic
literature review (SLR) of 49 studies in combination with
an empirical status quo survey of over 100 SMEs in Ger -
many in a blended format (Block et al. 2025). Notably,
68% of respondents hold top-level executive management
positions, indicating that the data reflect the perspectives of
the actual decision-makers in the surveyed SMEs. The tri -
angulation of the literature findings with empirical insights
serves to bridge the gap between theory and practice, yield-
ing valuable insights. AI in marketing analytics can only
generate value in SMEs if firms are capable of adopting
and integrating these technologies. Thus, insights from AI
acceptance among SMEs and from AI-enabled marketing
analytics are integrated in order to advance the understand -
ing of AI usage in marketing analytics among SMEs. There-
fore, this study provides several contributions. Firstly, the
study bridges the literature on, on the one hand, AI imple -
mentation in SMEs and, on the other hand, AI applications
in marketing analytics. Secondly, the study complements
and compares conceptual insights with empirical evidence
from SME decision-makers and staff. Thirdly, based on the
triangulation of literature and empirical insights, the study
proposes a conceptual framework describing four distinct
types of AI use in SMEs, considering AI application poten-
tial and adoption conditions across different perspectives,
and applies it to the discipline of market research to provide
managerial guidance.
The remainder of the paper is structured as follows: The
methodology section describes the SLR methodology and
the survey used to triangulate this blended format. There -
after, descriptive analyses of the literature samples and
survey sample are provided. The findings section presents
and synthesizes the results more descriptively, while the dis-
cussion section addresses the alignment and mismatches of
literature and practice, thereby deriving the proposed con -
ceptual framework. Finally, the limitations of this study are
acknowledged, and areas for future research are proposed,
followed by the conclusion.
Methodology
In this study, exploratory research is conducted through a
multimethod research design. The combination of an SLR
with real-world data from a survey depicts a state-of-the-art
structure of a hybrid and blended format (Block et al. 2025).
Merging a practitioner-focused with a phenomenon-focused
analysis and synthesizing their triangulation thus offers a
novel and comprehensive examination of these perspec -
tives. In times when AI can also be used to generate SLRs,
this human-conducted SLR is enriched with empirical data
from a survey in order to secure and enhance the value of
the study (Block and Kuckertz 2024).
The two literature streams are selected to capture com -
plementary dimensions of AI-enabled marketing analytics
in SMEs. The first stream addresses AI adoption in SMEs
and thus provides the firm-specific perspective on the con -
ditions that facilitate or constrain the adoption of AI. The
second stream focuses on AI applications in marketing ana-
lytics and provides the application-oriented perspective on
how AI can support analytical activities. Integrating both
perspectives is theoretically relevant because the potential
of AI-enabled marketing analytics depends not only on
available AI applications but also on the conditions under
which SMEs can adopt and utilize them. Neither literature
stream alone therefore captures the intersection of AI adop-
tion, SME-specific conditions, and marketing analytics
addressed by this study.
Following the study selection, the included articles are
compared and synthesized based on recurring themes within
each literature stream. Conceptually related findings are
consolidated into broader categories, resulting in the orga -
nizational, technological, and user-related dimensions of
AI adoption and the three overarching AI application fields
presented in the findings. These categories emerge from the
synthesis of the included literature rather than being pre -
defined prior to the review.
The different study elements serve complementary pur -
poses within the multimethod research design. While the
SLR establishes the conceptual foundation by synthesiz -
ing evidence on AI adoption in SMEs and AI applications
in marketing analytics, the exploratory survey provides
insights into the current manifestation of AI adoption and
use in SME practice. The survey is therefore not intended
to validate the literature findings, but to complement them
with an empirical perspective. Integrating both evidence
streams enables the literature-based AI application potential
and adoption conditions to be considered alongside current
SME practices, thereby informing the subsequent concep -
tual synthesis. Table 1 summarizes the respective objectives,
analytical contributions, and outputs of the study elements
with their contributions to the proposed framework.

Figure 1 depicts the process of conducting the SLR in
late October 2025, following the four phases presented in
the PRISMA statement (Moher et al. 2009). This procedure
is also referred to as data cleaning, in which false positives
are removed – i.e., studies that use the keywords but are,
upon closer examination, unrelated to the specific research
question. Following Linnenluecke et al. ( 2020), studies are
assessed for their suitability and topical relevance rather
than excluded based on perceived methodological qual -
ity, as quality-based exclusion may introduce bias into the
review. To enhance the rigor of the selection process, the
initial screening is conducted by the first researcher and
subsequently reviewed by the second researcher. Any dis -
crepancies regarding study inclusion are discussed between
both researchers until consensus is reached. After applying
the search string addressing AI in SMEs, the search yields
322 results in the identification phase. During the screening
phase, results are filtered by the Web of Science categories
“Business” and “Management” to identify only studies rel -
evant to the research context of this paper. Nevertheless, it
is ensured that studies from the field of computer science
or information technology with a business context are still
included in the results. This filtering leads to 101 remaining
results. Next, the document type is examined. All results are
either articles, early access, or review articles, so no further
filtering is needed. The studies are also all written in Eng -
lish; hence, no language filtering is necessary. Accordingly,
studies are eligible for inclusion if they (1) are assigned to

cles, early-access articles, or review articles, (3) are written
in English, (4) explicitly address AI or a specific AI tech -
nology, (5) substantively consider SMEs, and (6) examine
aspects related to AI adoption, acceptance, implementation,

both literature streams are jointly analyzed and synthesized.

into broader thematic categories, resulting in organiza -
tional, technological, and user-related adoption conditions
as well as three main AI application fields in marketing ana-
lytics: AI-generated synthetic data, AI-based text and con -

are not predefined but emerge from the synthesis of recur -

Empirical status quo survey design
The status quo analysis was conducted in 2024 in order to
assess the current prevalence of AI in SMEs as well as rele-
vant benefits, challenges, and concerns. This serves to better
understand the reasons for and against AI adoption in SMEs.
The research was supported by three chambers of industry
and commerce, which facilitated a target-group-specific
design and distribution of the survey, primarily targeting
Chief Executive Officers (CEOs) and upper management
of SMEs, but also including staff members to capture their
perspective. After the initial development of the question -
naire, a pre-test was conducted through in-depth interviews
with potential members of the target group. This served to
identify comprehension issues, missing elements, and other
weaknesses to support quality assurance of the study.
The questions in the survey are based on various simi -
lar studies conducted by well-known institutes and adapted
accordingly in order to match the specific research context
of this study (see Büchel et al. 2021; Feike et al. 2024;
Kaufmann 2022; Lundborg and Märkel 2019; Streim and
Hecker 2022). Given the exploratory objective of the sta -
tus quo analysis, the questionnaire is designed to capture
current patterns of AI adoption and use in SMEs rather
than to operationalize latent constructs for hypothesis test -
ing. Accordingly, the selected questions capture different
aspects of the current adoption status, including AI use and
application areas as well as perceived benefits, challenges,
and concerns. After an introductory text, the questionnaire
covers the number of employees and the industry of the par-
ticipants’ SMEs. Next, the participants are asked whether
they use AI in their company. Filters are employed in order

status quo objective, the survey data are analyzed descrip -
tively using frequencies and percentages to identify current
patterns of AI adoption and application among SMEs. The
empirical analysis is intended to complement the literature-
based perspective with insights from SME practice rather
than to test hypotheses or empirically validate the subse -

Findings
This section first presents the findings from the literature
search. Hence, the literature findings on AI adoption among
SMEs are addressed, which is followed by the findings on
AI application fields in marketing analytics. Thereafter, the
survey findings are presented, where AI adoption among
SMEs is examined, preceding the analysis of AI application
fields in SMEs.
Literature findings on AI adoption among SMEs
The following sections present the findings regarding AI
implementation among SMEs. Common theories in the lit -
erature regarding AI acceptance are the Technology-Orga -
nization-Environment (TOE) framework (Tornatzky and
Fleischer 1990), the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM)
(Davis et al. 1989) with its subsequent extensions (Ven -
katesh and Bala 2008; Venkatesh and Davis 2000), as well
as the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology
(UTAUT) (Venkatesh et al. 2003) and its extensions, which
is based on the TAM. However, the TOE framework does
not incorporate individual user aspects, whereas the TAM
and the UTAUT primarily focus on the individual user with
limited acknowledgment of external factors. The analysis of
this study segments adoption conditions into organizational,
technological, and user perspectives, which are directly
actionable within SMEs and are consistently addressed in
both the literature and the empirical findings. Environmen -
tal factors, as included in the TOE framework, are regarded
as largely exogenous to SME decision-makers in this study
and are only fragmentarily addressed in the reviewed data.
Their systematic examination is therefore deferred to future
research.
Organizational perspective
Bąk et al. ( 2024) categorize the impacting factors of AI
implementation in SMEs into the categories “strategy and
business model, culture and attitude, resources, support,
entrepreneurship and innovation, competitive position and
environmental conditions”. However, the results of the
literature search exhibit a strong focus on the influence of
leadership and management as well as on economic aspects.
This is also confirmed by Oldemeyer et al. ( 2025), who
demonstrate that knowledge, cost, and management are
among the barriers most commonly stated in the literature
on AI adoption in SMEs.
Paas ( 2023) states that analytical aspirations within an
organization, shaped by leadership, influence the complexity

aspect of AI adoption in SMEs, which includes establish -
ing a learning culture, proper communication, leadership
style, and management commitment (Schwaeke et al. 2025).
Regarding the attitude towards AI among CEOs and manag-
ers, Almashawreh et al. ( 2024) state the positive impact of
employee expertise and training initiatives, reward systems,
proper infrastructure, as well as managerial commitment.
Additionally, Apostoaie et al. (2025) find in their study that
leadership and organizational readiness, i.e., available infra-
structure, knowledge, innovation capabilities, and financial
resources, impact AI adoption among SMEs, whereas lack
of skills and understanding of the advantages appear to be
challenges for AI implementation. Similarly, Ayinaddis
(2025) claims that SMEs face challenges in implementing
AI at the organizational level, including a lack of skills and
competency, resources and financial readiness, as well as
management commitment and support. Metz et al. ( 2025)
state barriers such as limited skills, knowledge, and data
literacy among employees as possible reasons for issues
related to inaccurate or biased data, as well as data security.
Moreover, limited financial resources diminish the avail -
ability of datasets, thus constraining analytical capabilities
(Metz et al. 2025). Baabdullah et al. (2021) demonstrate that
professional expertise and technicality do not significantly
impact AI acceptance as opposed to technology roadmap -
ping, attitude, infrastructure, and awareness. These findings
highlight the importance of staff training to facilitate AI
implementation in SMEs.
Despite limited resources in SMEs, these organizations
often exhibit agility and responsiveness to demand, which
drives innovation and, in turn, AI adoption (Schwaeke
et al. 2025). With a survey of 136 SMEs in the chemical
industry, Hruby ( 2025) shows through structural equation
modeling that an organization’s entrepreneurial orientation
significantly correlates with effective AI integration. Over -
all barriers to AI implementation are stated as “cost, lack of
expertise, or perceived irrelevance” (Hruby 2025). Segarra-
Blasco et al. ( 2025) find in a survey analysis that smaller
organizations, such as start-ups and scale-ups, are more
likely to adopt AI and robots.1 According to the authors, the
innovation type of companies does not significantly affect
AI adoption, but whether the application is broader or more
specialized differs across industries. Moreover, Cimino et
al. (2025) observe that dynamic capabilities positively influ-
ence both the adoption intensity and the acceptance rate of
AI in SMEs.
Another benefit of AI adoption in SMEs is its impact on
revenue growth, which is further enhanced when combined
with other new technologies such as big data analysis or the
Internet of Things, according to Ardito et al. (2025). Exam-
ining the application of AI in new product development and
market analysis in SMEs shows that drivers of AI adoption
are economic value to the business, commitment by senior
management, availability of an executive sponsor, as well
as patience and trust by senior management (Cooper 2025).
Moreover, Ayinaddis (2025) examines that AI adoption in
SMEs can lead to benefits such as improved operational and
economic performance, enhanced competitiveness, or ame-
liorated decision-making.

analysis is the technological perspective. Numerous chal -

As mentioned above, simplicity and user-friendliness

Literature findings on AI application fields in
marketing analytics
The following sections present the findings from the litera -
ture search on the application fields of AI in marketing ana-
lytics. The three main application fields that emerged during
the analysis are portrayed, beginning with AI-generated
synthetic data, followed by AI-based text and content analy-
sis, and lastly AI-driven predictions. The last section cov -
ers additional fields of application and other perspectives
that have emerged in individual studies but should not be
neglected.
AI-generated synthetic data
As mentioned above and upon closer examination of the
literature sample, three main fields of application emerge,
namely, AI-generated synthetic data, AI-based text and
content analysis, and AI-driven predictions. Synthetic data,
also referred to as silicon samples, are generated by LLMs
and seek to imitate human responses in market research –
e.g., survey responses – thereby trying to omit real human
participants (Sarstedt et al. 2024). With a literature review,
Sarstedt et al. ( 2024) analyze the use of silicon samples
for various market research applications, i.e., pre-testing,
pilot studies, and main studies of a quantitative nature,
after examining benchmarking studies comparing human
and synthetic samples. While the authors recommend using
silicon samples generated by LLMs for pre-testing and pilot
studies, their use in main studies still faces some obstacles.
Sarstedt et al. ( 2024) raise ethical concerns, such as bias
or copyright issues, which generally apply to GenAI and
are also relevant to synthetic data. Likewise, Estevez et al.
(2025) find, in a study comparing synthetically generated
data by LLMs with traditional survey data, that LLMs have
limitations in their capabilities, and thus recommend sili -
con samples particularly for exploratory research phases. It
is advised to adopt a hybrid approach, leveraging LLMs to
complement human research. The authors emphasize that
especially SMEs can benefit from implementing LLMs in
this regard, making marketing analytics more accessible and
affordable (Estevez et al. 2025).
In line with these findings, Arora et al. (2025) also advo-
cate a hybrid approach, demonstrating that the enhanced
capabilities enabled by the complementary skills of
humans and LLMs outperform human-only and LLM-only
approaches. Not only do the authors investigate LLMs in
the context of generating synthetic data, but they also show
that LLMs match human competencies in analyzing quali -
tative data (Arora et al. 2025). This broadens the scope for
LLMs to further assist market researchers, e.g., in analyz -
ing data, potentially benefiting SMEs. Similarly, Lasarov
et al. ( 2026) demonstrate an easy adoption of GenAI for
market research by practitioners as well, emphasizing the
non-necessity of “advanced training in research methods or
technical skills”. Hence, SMEs with less expert knowledge
and fewer resources could enhance their marketing analyt -
ics by implementing GenAI. However, the enhanced cost-
effectiveness addressed by other authors, such as Estevez et
al. (2025), is challenged by Lasarov et al. (2026), since they
argue that the saved costs from omitting human subjects
should be allocated towards quality assurance of synthetic
data in order to mitigate legal and ethical concerns, such as
biases, as also noted by Sarstedt et al. (2024).

AI-based text and content analysis
Another field that emerges from the literature sample in this
review is the use of AI in marketing analytics for text analy-
sis, which falls within the domain of natural language pro -
cessing. In this scope, sentiment analysis and topic modeling
appear as common applications in the literature sample.
In market research, these methods are oftentimes applied
to user-generated content, such as social media posts and
reviews, to analyze consumer attitudes (Mustak et al. 2024).
Sentiment analysis refers to the attribution of a rating or
score – e.g., positive, negative, or neutral – to text, which
can follow a lexicon-based or an ML approach. While the
lexicon-based approach uses pre-coded lists and searches
for their appearance in the text data, the ML approach goes
beyond that and is able to identify e.g., context or tone like
sarcasm, with training through human validation (Downer
et al. 2019). However, topic modeling serves to detect topi-
cal patterns within text, i.e., co-occurrences of words and
topics, using ML (Yazıcı and Ozansoy Çadırcı, 2024).
In a comparison study, Hartmann et al. ( 2019) examine
five lexicon-based and five ML algorithms regarding their
performance in sentiment and content classification. They
conclude that all lexicon-based approaches yield inferior
results to ML. Especially, the ML algorithms Random For -
est and Naïve Bayes exhibit the best performances (Hart -
mann et al. 2019). In the study by Garay Gallastegui et al.
(2024), eXtreme Gradient Boosting is the most precise ML
algorithm for sentiment analysis. Extending this line of
investigations, Alaparthi and Mishra ( 2021) advance the
examination of techniques by comparing an unsupervised
lexicon-based model, a traditional supervised ML model,
a supervised deep learning model, and an advanced super -
vised deep learning model. Here, the findings indicate bet -
ter performance by ML and deep learning algorithms than
by the lexicon-based model, with the pre-trained advanced
supervised deep learning algorithm Bidirectional Encoder
Representations from Transformers (BERT) demonstrating
undisputed superiority. Salminen et al. ( 2022) as well as
Yazıcı and Ozansoy Çadırcı (2024) show consistent findings
with refined models of BERT exhibiting the best perfor -
mances. Nevertheless, BERT requires strong computational
resources (Alaparthi and Mishra 2021). Thus, it seems that
more complex algorithms yield better results, but for SMEs,
the required expertise might pose challenges. Still, Mustak
et al. (2024) emphasize the affordability of ML algorithms
for companies of all sizes, given their cost-effectiveness.
As noted above, applying ML entails certain requirements
for expert knowledge, which may constitute a challenge for
SMEs. Cooper et al. (2022) indicate that simpler yet still effi-
cient approaches, such as lexicon-based text analysis, might
suffice for the investigation’s aim while being more suitable
given the available resources. Thus, in an analogous manner,
Downer et al. (2019) purposefully investigate tools that are
easy to implement and do not require modification, rather
than ML, to meet the needs of – in their case – commercial
research agencies. Nevertheless, the tools demonstrate sub-
stantial variation in results for the same survey data, indicat-
ing the need for further research concerning their suitability.
Moreover, Lee et al. (2020) examine tools that integrate ML
and compare them with manual and computer-aided content
analysis without AI, concluding that the AI-enabled approach
achieves high reliability and validity, as well as moderate
efficiency. By enabling the analysis of unstructured data, the
authors state that AI-enabled tools support a transition from
conventional deductive investigations to more inductive and
abductive research. However, the cost and availability of this
technology, as well as the required expertise for correspond-
ing input and guidance, are disadvantages to consider (Lee et
al. 2020). On that matter, Kunathikornkit et al. ( 2025) ana-
lyze the suitability of an LLM for sentiment analysis and find
that it still achieves moderate accuracy, showing promise
due to its cost-effectiveness. Hence, the cost-effectiveness
advantage – also mentioned above for the more complex ML
approach – is achieved by LLMs as well, albeit with dimin-
ished accuracy.
AI-driven predictions
The third major field of AI applications in marketing analyt-
ics that emerges from the literature search lies in the domain
of predictions, e.g., of consumer behavior, including time-
series forecasting. Ma and Sun ( 2020) present predictions,
among other fields, as a crucial application field of ML. A
common industry where ML is applied in market research
is retail, with Fildes et al. ( 2022) confirming the relevance
of forecasting in their literature review, while at the same
time pointing to the mixed evidence regarding the superior-
ity of ML methods over established statistical approaches.
Furthermore, Wang et al. ( 2021) demonstrate significant
predictive capabilities of ML algorithms in this industry.
However, the authors also note potential challenges regard-
ing the implementation, such as resource intensity, ethical
predictions, and customer privacy. Besides the retail indus-
try, Hakim et al. ( 2021) show that applying ML to electro -
encephalography (EEG) data can improve the predictive
accuracy of product choice. However, the complexity of
EEG data also incorporates challenges, such as the necessity
for an appropriate normalization due to large fluctuations in
individual EEG activity (Hakim et al. 2021). Furthermore,
Herold et al. ( 2024) find that while still being able to gen -
erate accurate predictions, ML algorithms lose predictive
capability and reliability due to – e.g., for ethical or legal
reasons – unavailable consumer data. Similarly, Borah and

A. T. Parsegyan et al.
Rutz (2024) argue that their model generates more accurate
forecasts when the full competitive search data is included,
compared to only including a limited subset of the data.
While ML is oftentimes used for binary classification pre -
dictions in marketing, Zhang et al. ( 2025) emphasize the
importance of tailored data preparation to achieve more
effective targeting, conversions, and budget allocation.
On that matter, deep learning, as a subset of AI, stands
out for its ability to produce advanced predictions. Thus,
numerous studies identified in the literature search empha -
size the predictive capabilities of deep learning algorithms.
Wilson and Bettis-Outland (2019) find in their comparative
study that artificial neural networks yield the best predic -
tive performance, but the authors note their disadvantage
when it comes to explaining and understanding the underly-
ing processes due to their black-box nature. Sarkar and de
Bruyn (2021) find in their study that long-short term mem -
ory neural networks outperform most hand-crafted models
in predicting customer behavior, while being able to run
exclusively on raw data. Hence, there is an advantage in not
requiring feature engineering (Sarkar and de Bruyn 2021).
Similarly, Valendin et al. ( 2022) emphasize the capabili -
ties of automatic feature extraction of deep learning mod -
els, omitting manual encoding. The authors also point out
the strong performance without context variables, enabling
forecasts without collecting personal data and thereby
avoiding ethical issues. In line with these studies, Xu et al.
(2024) demonstrate the suitability of deep learning for pro -
ducing accurate predictions from complex consumer data.
However, all the above-mentioned studies acknowl -
edge the disadvantages of low explainability, high resource
requirements, and an overall complex nature, which might
pose challenges for SMEs, since they have fewer available
resources – be they financial, knowledge-based, technologi-
cal, or otherwise. In their study, Muth et al. ( 2025b) con-
duct a comprehensive analysis of 21 prediction methods
and align their managerial implications with the perspective
of SMEs. The most accurate predictions are performed by
an ML method. Hence, the authors recommend that SMEs
choose this method if accuracy is the priority. If, however,
automation is a focus in order to mitigate deficits in e.g.,
expert knowledge or human resources, more automated
methods, such as the second-best-performing AutoGluon,
may be a promising solution for SMEs leveraging AI (Muth
et al. 2025b). The third-best-performing method, Ordinary
Least Squares, can be favored by SMEs when transparency
and explainability are prioritized (Muth et al. 2025b), which
is often criticized in advanced ML and especially deep learn-
ing methods, as discussed above. Hence, various forms of
AI are beneficial for marketing predictions, and SMEs have
numerous options to suit their specific needs and priorities.
Additional application fields and perspectives
During the literature analysis, additional application fields
of AI in marketing analytics and other perspectives emerged
that did not occur multiple times but nevertheless should not
be disregarded, since they may represent key areas for future
research. Huang and Rust (2021) divide AI into mechanical,
thinking, and feeling AI, and discuss their applications in
marketing research (besides marketing strategy and mar -
keting action). In the research stage, the authors determine
that “mechanical AI can be used for data collection, think -
ing AI for market analysis, and feeling AI for customer
understanding”. Nevertheless, limitations are noted, i.e.,
loss of emotional data in automation through mechanical
AI, potential lack of neutrality and transparency in thinking
AI, and technological as well as customer unreadiness for
feeling AI (Huang and Rust 2021). Metz et al. ( 2025) por-
tray segmentation, targeting, and positioning strategies as
application fields of AI and analyze ethical issues consider-
ing the organization’s size. Especially smaller organizations
face challenges in navigating crises and rebuilding customer
trust due to fewer available resources (Metz et al. 2025),
underscoring the relevance of taking ethics into account.
Ma and Sun ( 2020) identify that AI, and especially ML,
is a crucial and transformative field in marketing. Additional
applications of ML in marketing analytics, beyond those
discussed in the current study, include optimizing the mar -
keting mix or the customer journey. The authors state the
advantages of ML, including its ability to process unstruc -
tured as well as large amounts of data, and its high predic -
tive power. However, there are disadvantages, including the
lack of interpretability, thereby reducing insights for mar -
ket researchers (Ma and Sun 2020). Therefore, Goktas and
Dirsehan (2025) employ a model of explainable AI in their
study, demonstrating that this approach yields improved
insights with enhanced interpretability and reliability. More-
over, Chintalapati and Pandey (2022) extend the application
fields of ML in marketing analytics by specifically nam -
ing customer segmentation, consumer behavior, and data
mining. Paas (2023) points out that fewer resources within
organizations lead to simpler forms of ML being employed,
such as univariate or bivariate analyses. The more analytical
aspirations exist in an organization – which might also be
affected by respective leadership as discussed for the orga -
nizational perspective – the more complex the use of ML
becomes and goes on to segmentation or prediction applica-
tions (Paas 2023).
Another form of AI that is relevant to marketing analyt -
ics is LLMs and, consequently, GenAI. Li et al. (2025) find
that LLMs improve clustering for customer segmentation
and that generative chatbots based on LLMs deliver accu -
rate portrayals of consumer choice. The latter aspect falls

Alignment and mismatches between literature and
practical insights
The results in the findings section demonstrate that there are
numerous areas where the literature and the survey insights
are coherent, but there are also mismatches that warrant fur-
ther investigation. While the survey indicates increasing AI
use in marketing-related activities among SMEs, this use
predominantly concerns applications such as text and image
generation and online marketing rather than analytical activ-
ities. In contrast, market research, sales controlling, and cat-
egory management are among the less frequently reported
application areas. The survey therefore provides evidence of
increasing AI adoption in marketing activities but indicates
that the use of more advanced AI-enabled marketing ana -
lytics remains comparatively limited. This contrasts with
the application potential identified in the literature, which
includes more advanced analytical applications such as syn-
thetic data generation, text analysis, and predictions. More-
over, most studies from the literature search on marketing
analytics – which is not restricted to SMEs – focus on ML
and a small number on its subcategory, i.e., deep learning,
with the application context oftentimes being sentiment
analysis or predictions.

The literature search further confirms that there is a lack
of research on AI in market research focusing on SMEs,
as only three studies – i.e., Estevez et al. ( 2025), Metz et
al. (2025), and Muth et al. ( 2025b) – address SMEs in this

analytics, such as synthetic data generation, text analysis,
and predictions, shows promise for SMEs, since AI imple -
mentation can be tailored to each organization’s resources,
as discussed above. In this way, SMEs can enhance their
marketing analytics by benefiting from the advantages AI
has to offer, even if simpler tools are chosen or a specific
focus, e.g., accuracy, automation, or explainability, as out -
lined by Muth et al. ( 2025b), guides the implementation.
Hence, understanding AI adoption in SMEs requires consid-
ering not only the conditions shaping adoption but also the
AI application potential in specific functional domains such
as market research.
From the organizational perspective, the role of execu -
tive management and leadership appears to be an area with
discrepancies. While the literature suggests that the edu -
cational or professional level of the upper management
impacts AI implementation (Paas 2023), the survey does
not reflect such findings. Therefore, a closer examination
of this topic might help to identify additional factors influ -
encing AI acceptance. Furthermore, training of employees
and the overall attitude of the executive management – and
hence their leadership – turn out to be relevant aspects for
AI implementation (Almashawreh et al. 2024; Apostoaie
et al. 2025; Ayinaddis 2025), since the survey also reflects
that the decision-makers recognize this necessity and do not
perceive it as an obstacle. However, an area currently under-
represented in the literature, as revealed by the survey, is
how legal issues and uncertainties may prevent SMEs from
implementing AI. With the introduction of this new technol-
ogy, questions regarding e.g., liability or data security arise,
and companies need to make sure to comply with the law,
such as the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR),
which might leave SMEs overwhelmed, struggling with
limited resources as it is.
Regarding the technological perspective, both the survey
and literature reveal structural issues with the current infra-
structure of SMEs and the effort to integrate AI, as well as
the current data quality (Ayinaddis 2025; Oldemeyer et al.
2025; Schwaeke et al. 2025). Here, it is interesting to inves-
tigate how these issues can be resolved for SMEs. More -
over, when investigating the various applications of AI in
marketing analytics, it becomes clear that different forms of
AI entail distinct issues, e.g., the low explainability of deep
learning models, which should be considered when assess -
ing the potential for AI implementation in SMEs. In addi -
tion, the specific use cases and goals for AI in SMEs affect AI
adoption. The literature demonstrates that SMEs mostly use
AI for simpler tasks (Ayinaddis 2025), which is also stated
in the survey. However, it remains unclear whether this is
the case due to e.g., lack of knowledge or other factors. As
the literature analysis shows, more advanced applications,
such as predictions or text analyses, also provide opportu -
nities that align with SMEs’ resources. Nevertheless, guid -
ance on which type of AI can yield the best results, given
SMEs’ resource limitations and goals, might be necessary.
Lastly, the user perspective reveals that simplicity and
usability appear to be quite relevant aspects for AI accep -
tance in SMEs (Ayinaddis 2025). This accommodates the
restricted resources of SMEs and avoids the need for addi -
tional staff training. Moreover, usability increases the per -
ceived value of AI directly for end users (Schwaeke et al.
2025), which is a promising way to improve AI acceptance.
Furthermore, the literature reveals that management and
peer attitude also impact the acceptance of the individual
user (Noerman et al. 2025). However, the survey results
demonstrate an extremely low rate of AI rejection by man -
agement, whereas rejection by employees is reported to
be substantially higher. This can, of course, be due to self-
reporting bias, since the survey participants are mainly
CEOs and managers. Hence, a closer examination of diverg-
ing management and employee attitudes seems necessary.
Conceptual framework with theoretical and
managerial implications
Interestingly, the theoretical base in the AI acceptance litera-
ture from the SLR appears quite scattered. While numerous
studies do not explicitly draw on an established theoreti -
cal basis, one study uses dynamic capabilities (i.e., Cimino
et al. 2025), one uses the resource-based view with the
complementary asset concept (i.e., Ardito et al. 2025), and
another study addresses entrepreneurial orientation (i.e.,
Hruby 2025). Furthermore, two studies use (variations of)
the TAM and UTAUT (i.e., Moradi and Dass 2022; Noer-
man et al. 2025). Notably, the most frequently used theory
is the TOE framework, with five studies addressing it (i.e.,
Almashawreh et al. 2024; Apostoaie et al. 2025; Ayinad-
dis 2025; Baabdullah et al. 2021; Schwaeke et al. 2025).
However, this framework lacks the individual user’s per -
spective, whereas TAM does not incorporate organizational
factors or most of the technological factors discussed. At
the same time, existing research published in the Journal
of Marketing Analytics has demonstrated the potential of
AI and ML for specific analytical applications and deci -
sion-support tasks (Alaparthi and Mishra 2021; Muth et
al. 2025b; Yazıcı and Ozansoy Çadırcı, 2024; Zhang et al.
2025) but provides less insight into the conditions under
which resource-constrained SMEs can adopt and utilize
this application potential. The present study complements

A. T. Parsegyan et al.
this application-oriented literature by linking AI applica -
tion potential in marketing analytics to the multi-level con -
ditions shaping adoption in SMEs. Moreover, there is no
explicit connection of AI application potential – especially
in specific functional domains such as market research
– with SME-specific requirements and constraints. The
proposed framework addresses this gap by integrating an
application-potential-oriented perspective with the multi-
level conditions of AI adoption in SMEs. In doing so, it
extends existing adoption perspectives by considering not
only whether conditions support AI adoption, but also the
scope of AI application potential that SMEs can leverage in
marketing analytics. The relevance of this configuration is
particularly pronounced in the SME context, where limited
financial, technological, and human resources can constrain
both the adoption conditions and the scope of AI application
potential that can be utilized. Consequently, favorable adop-
tion conditions do not necessarily translate into broad AI
application potential, while specific AI application potential
may be utilized despite unfavorable conditions, for example
through accessible ready-to-use solutions. Although the
underlying dimensions may also be relevant to larger firms,
the proposed configurations reflect the particular need of
SMEs to adapt the scope of AI adoption to their available
resources and application-specific objectives.
Taken together, the findings from the two investiga -
tions reveal both complementary insights and discrepan -
cies between the possibilities of AI-enabled marketing
analytics and its current adoption in SMEs. The system -
atic literature review identified recurring adoption condi -
tions as well as AI application potential, while the survey
provided complementary insights into their manifestation in
SME practice, particularly indicating that the use of more
advanced AI applications remains limited. Considering
these findings jointly provides the basis for conceptualiz -
ing AI use in SMEs along two dimensions: AI application
potential and adoption conditions. Based on this conceptual
synthesis, a preliminary conceptual framework is proposed
(Fig. 12), which combines both perspectives to character -
ize different configurations of AI use in SMEs. The frame -
work is informed by both investigations but represents a
conceptual synthesis rather than an empirically derived or
validated typology.
Fig. 12 depicts the proposed preliminary conceptual
framework, which represents the authors’ conceptual inte -
gration of both evidence streams and serves as a basis for
future empirical validation. It comprises four configurations
of AI use in market research among SMEs. The x-axis rep -
resents AI adoption conditions within SMEs, ranging from
unfavorable to favorable. Adoption conditions refer to how
organizational, technological, and user-related factors sup -
port AI adoption. As described in the section “ Literature
findings on AI adoption among SMEs ”, organizational
conditions include, for example, leadership, management,
employee competence, and available resources. Moreover,
the technological conditions cover, for instance, digital
maturity, data availability and quality, compatibility, and
complexity. Finally, the user aspects comprise social influ -
ence from colleagues and management, usability, hedonic
motivation, and the holistic experience, in addition to the
typical components of TAM models. Hence, favorable
adoption conditions indicate that conditions across these
perspectives predominantly support AI adoption. The
y-axis shows the AI application potential (such as in mar -
ket research) of SMEs from low to high. As the literature
analysis in the section “Literature findings on AI application
fields in marketing analytics” shows, there are many appli -
cation fields for AI in market research for SMEs, although
the implementation in practice appears rather low. The AI
application potential in this conceptual framework indicates
the extent to which AI offers relevant and feasible appli -
cation opportunities, i.e., use cases, for an SME in market
research tasks, such as generating and using synthetic data,
conducting text analyses, carrying out predictions, or other
strategic applications like segmentation.
The four user types proposed in this conceptual frame -
work represent alternative configurations of AI adoption
rather than a normative progression in which broad strategic
AI use is necessarily the optimal outcome for every SME.
Instead, SMEs must determine their optimal degree of AI
adoption depending on their individual goals. The manage -
rial implications provided are informed by the study’s find-
ings and serve as guidelines for possible increases in AI

and implementation. Staff training could be a useful mea -

Operational users are SMEs that exhibit unfavorable
adoption conditions but have high AI application potential
in market research, as shown in the upper-left quadrant of
the matrix. In these organizations, AI is used intensively, but
only in specific fields, e.g., ML is applied to text analysis.
This could be the case because the business model focuses
on a specific task. However, there is low overall governance
and a lack of strategic integration. Here, a broader accep -
tance of the management could be focused on (Almashaw -
reh et al. 2024; Apostoaie et al. 2025; Schwaeke et al. 2025).
Services that offer advice on specific issues preventing a
broader implementation, such as legal or ethical consulting,
might facilitate a more strategic use. In addition, techno -
logical barriers preventing the use of AI in other areas of
market research could be investigated.

SMEs exhibit strategic readiness when adoption condi -

configuration indicates that organizational, technological,
and user-related conditions are generally conducive to AI
adoption, while opportunities for applying AI to market
research remain comparatively limited. In this case, iden -

implementation aligns with the company’s business model
and goals. With the right choice of tools, staff training, and
strong management support in identifying application areas
(Ayinaddis 2025), these SMEs could expand AI use where
this aligns with their specific objectives and resources.
Lastly, SMEs with high AI application potential and
favorable adoption conditions are called strategic users, as
shown in the top-right quadrant of the matrix. These orga -
nizations managed a broad implementation and acceptance
and can thus benefit from efficiency gains, more depth in cus-
tomer insights, and improved decision quality, among other
advantages (Ayinaddis 2025). Management could focus on
establishing and maintaining a competitive advantage with
the strategic use of AI in market research. By maintaining
strong AI use cases and high acceptance among employees
and managers, these SMEs can operate in an agile manner in
order to maximize the benefits of their AI strategy.
Limitations and directions for future research
The findings of this study reveal new perspectives on AI
adoption and acceptance in marketing analytics among
SMEs. However, in order to properly assess the results,
a critical reflection on the limitations is necessary, which
at the same time reveals opportunities and necessities for
future research. The methodology does contain some weak-
nesses that must be addressed. The field of AI is a fast-
moving and continuously developing subject area. Thus, the
2024 status quo survey and the 2025 literature might not
capture the latest developments, and repeated investigations
through survey and literature analyses should be conducted
to detect trends and directions over time. In addition, incor-
porating the two different data collection periods might also
weaken the triangulation of the hybrid SLR format, since
some aspects evaluated in the survey may differ from more
recent findings in the literature due to the evolution of the
research field.
Regarding the survey, it must be noted that the geo -
graphic area of participants is mostly limited to Hesse in
Germany. Hence, a Germany-wide survey, as well as inter-
national comparisons, provide opportunities not only to
identify area-specific issues in AI implementation in mar -
ket research among SMEs but also to enhance the gener -
alizability of the findings. Future research should therefore
examine whether the proposed configurations and underly -
ing adoption conditions are also observed across different
institutional, economic, and cultural contexts. Moreover,
the study considers SMEs as an aggregate category and
does not systematically examine differences between micro,
small, and medium-sized firms. Given their potentially dif -
fering resource endowments and organizational structures,
future research should investigate whether the relevance of
individual adoption conditions and the proposed configura -
tions vary with firm size. In addition, a certain degree of
self-selection bias could have occurred, potentially resulting
in a tendency of participants to be open to and in favor of
AI usage in their companies. In addition, the survey carried
out exploratory research, which led to descriptive findings
on the current state. However, future studies should adopt a

A. T. Parsegyan et al.
deductive approach or aim to examine causal relationships
to improve AI acceptance in marketing analytics among
SMEs.
Considering the methodology of the systematic literature
review, the investigation included two search strings – on
the one hand, the acceptance of AI in SMEs without a spe -
cific focus on marketing analytics, and on the other hand,
the application possibilities of AI in marketing analytics
without a specific focus on SMEs – in order to explore this
majorly under-researched field and combine the findings to
close a research gap. This, however, implies that the syn -
thesis is subject to assumptions that are necessary in order
to apply the findings to the respective context, which was
partly not explicitly examined in the underlying studies.
Furthermore, it must be noted that the generation of search
strings and the selection of relevant literature according to
the PRISMA scheme are prone to at least a slight degree of
subjectivity (Xiao and Watson 2019), even though measures
were taken to ensure scientific standards.
The literature analysis of AI applications in marketing
analytics reveals that acknowledging the resource limi -
tations of SMEs and evaluating AI considering their spe -
cific needs is vastly under-researched. Thus, AI in market
research represents yet another field that requires further
exploration in the context of SMEs, as only a few studies
have done so. Not only should this investigation be carried
out in the three main fields of AI application in marketing
analytics identified above – i.e., synthetic data, text analysis,
and predictions – but also in other market research tasks,
such as quantitative data analysis, visualization, interpre -
tation, and research design. Here, it should be analyzed to
what extent AI can assist in mitigating resource constraints
in expertise or personnel. In addition, the relevance and
feasibility of AI applications should be investigated across
different contexts, such as business-to-business and indus -
try-specific settings, since the relevance of use cases and
therefore AI implementation may differ.
Regarding the different perspectives on AI acceptance in
SMEs, additional opportunities for future research unfold.
From the organizational perspective, the influence of leg -
islation on AI acceptance among SMEs is an area that is
currently under-researched. With laws such as the GDPR,
the AI Act, or other fundamental legislation, such as Euro -
pean competition law, complexity is added to the process
of implementing AI. Uncertainty might arise regarding the
latter legislation, with the possibility of algorithms auton -
omously colluding when employed for pricing (Bostoen
2025). From the technological perspective, resolving infra -
structural issues and their impact on SMEs, as well as creat-
ing guidance for SMEs on which types of AI yield effective
results given their individual constraints and goals, remains
to be investigated, as discussed above. Finally, the user
perspective revealed several research gaps to be addressed.
On the one hand, the usability of AI applications, specifically
for SMEs and in the context of marketing analytics, needs
to be explored in order to identify crucial conditions influ -
encing technology acceptance that could potentially extend
current models and findings. Thus, conducting exploratory
research appears necessary. On the other hand, analyzing
and comparing management and employee attitudes is
required to identify similarities and differences in percep -
tions. In this way, novel aspects and discrepancies can be
detected to enhance the understanding of AI acceptance and
implementation in marketing analytics among SMEs.
Furthermore, how organizational, technological, and
user-related adoption conditions jointly shape AI adoption
in SMEs remains insufficiently understood. The configura -
tions proposed in this study therefore require empirical vali-
dation. Moreover, the assessment of adoption conditions is
yet to be quantitatively operationalized, which is why the
typology serves as a preliminary conceptual framework in
this paper. Future research should operationalize and empir-
ically test the proposed dimensions and configurations to
assess and further refine the framework.
Conclusions
This study applies a multi-method design that combines a
systematic literature review with an empirical status quo
survey on AI implementation in SMEs, with a specific focus
on marketing analytics. The analysis of both investigations
reveals valuable insights for theory and practice in order to
better understand and advance the field of AI in marketing
analytics for SMEs. The survey indicates that marketing is
among the most relevant fields for AI implementation, yet
the discipline of marketing analytics remains comparatively
underexplored in the context of SMEs.
The analysis of the application areas of AI in marketing
analytics demonstrates three main fields – i.e., synthetic
data, text analysis, and predictions – that are currently
addressed in research. Nevertheless, the potential of these
applications for SMEs should be considered and examined
more closely. Additionally, it is proposed that, to better
understand SMEs’ AI usage behavior, AI application poten-
tial should be considered an influencing factor. Further -
more, the literature analysis on AI implementation in SMEs
as well as the survey indicate that influencing conditions
can be distinguished into the organizational, technological,
and user perspectives, which is currently not covered by the
relevant theories to this extent. Moreover, it is noted that
the interplay of these perspectives should not be disregarded
and shows promise for better understanding the adoption
among SMEs. Hence, a matrix is developed that combines

the two dimensions of AI application potential and adoption
conditions, thereby distinguishing four user types in order
to characterize and facilitate AI implementation in SMEs.
This equips managers with actionable insights to identify
critical points of intervention in their organization. In this
way, achieving strategic implementation and use of AI (e.g.,
in market research) can be facilitated if this is the objective.
Nevertheless, this study recognizes its limitations and
identifies avenues for future research regarding AI imple -
mentation in marketing analytics among SMEs. Thus, a
verification of the proposed conceptual framework, as well
as a deeper deductive or even causal analysis of influencing
conditions on the one hand, and more qualitative explora -
tions of the different perspectives, such as the usability of
AI with market research tasks for SMEs on the other hand,
are essential to further advance this field in science and
practice. Overall, this study provides an integrated view of
AI-enabled marketing analytics in SMEs and offers both
researchers a conceptual foundation and practitioners a con-
crete starting point for using AI despite limited resources.
