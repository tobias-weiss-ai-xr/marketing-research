# Market Research and Consumer Insight

Market Research and Consumer Insight explores how market research, consumer insight
and data analytics inform and underpin marketing and communications planning.
Unlike other texts, this book considers how marketing research techniques are applied
by marketing managers in practice to ensure more effective campaigns. It provides a
holistic overview of the key developments within the field of market research, the main
organizations,
development of codes of practice and ethics, core concepts, frameworks
and models. As well as explaining traditional concepts and helping students to under ‑
stand when and how to apply these, digital research methods and analytics and their
application to marketing strategy are explored in detail.
Real‑world examples and case studies are embedded through the text, demonstrating
marketing and brand issues and business problems that use and apply data and research,
both successfully and unsuccessfully. Chapter introductions, summaries and reflective
activities
help to engage students with the theory. Market Research and Consumer Insight
shows students how to turn market intelligence into data‑driven decisions, communica‑
tions propositions and campaign plans.
It is recommended reading for undergraduate, postgraduate and professional students
studying marketing research, digital marketing analytics, marketing and communications
planning, management and strategy. Online resources include PowerPoint slides and a
test bank.

The objective of Market Research and Consumer Insight is to provide a text for academ‑
ics and their undergraduates and postgraduates studying for degrees in business manage‑
ment and marketing, and communications and marketing. It is also a recommended text
for media studies, big data and analytics, strategic and digital marketing planning and
campaign management, as well as those studying for marketing and research professional
qualifications, to give students and graduate trainees and interns a career path to develop
their skills.
This text provides students with frameworks for insight and impact techniques, which
they will apply when they graduate, particularly in roles in the marketing services sector
,
including those in research, insight, digital and communications agencies. The tools and
techniques discussed in the book are also applicable to management in marketing and
communications, roles in major brands and small and medium‑sized enterprises (SMEs).
The text and case studies are useful for graduates in management training programmes
as they progress through continuing professional development (CPD) in marketing, com‑
munications and data analytics. Both students and management will benefit from reading
the text to learn ethics and best practice, the effective use of market research and data
analytics techniques.
Market Research and Consumer Insight aims to help sector professionals, academics
and students understand why such insight is essential for consumer
‑ and customer‑focused
decisions. It will help modify learners’ understanding of market research and applying
insights to help them progress in their studies and careers. The text also shows how mar‑
keting and research tools are used to solve marketing and communications problems. As
the marketing and market research professions have developed, they have created essen‑
tial management frameworks and techniques via practical tools and resources focused on
delivering support for marketing decision making.
Most marketing departments serve as “the face of the company”, coordinating and
producing all materials representing a business and its brand. Insight, big data and infor

Overview
Marketing as a professional practice developed from the mid‑1800s in several phases to
become a key management discipline with a focus on business performance. The effec
‑
tiveness of the marketing process has depends on an organisation’s strategic positioning
and its use of sales, marketing and communications techniques. Some of these processes
depend on manufacturing and production methods using technical product research.
Transactional businesses have also used sales techniques more than marketing tools to
present brands to target customers and secure fast sales.
Global brands have used applied marketing techniques since the 1950s to focus on the
consumer or customer: to understand consumer needs, behaviours, and how sales and
marketing
stimulate purchase and create consumer loyalty. Some brands position their
communications to their customers’ social and ethical practices to provide a more profes‑
sional approach to sales and marketing.
Also, since the 1990s, brands have developed a focus and segmentation on customer
service and relationships between suppliers, product/service buyers and consumers/
potential consumers. Most brands plan and deliver an integrated marketing mix, with
consistent identity and communications messages across multiple channels. Integrated
marketing has increased the opportunity for a business to generate leads, develop strong
brand identity and deliver e‑commerce campaigns. This is a key to success as it brings
together all the “ingredients of the marketing mix“ (CIM, 2015).
The marketing process in the 21st century is about understanding customer behaviour
and monitoring customer engagement. This helps place marketing at the “centre of creating
value
“ (Brown & Thompson, 2023, p. 58). Understanding customer behaviour requires
research and data to provide information on how target markets choose, buy and use
products and services. Research is needed for brands to explore the factors that influence
customers related to personal and cultural preferences as well as behavioural reactions
to marketing and communications strategies and campaigns. Engagement research meas‑
ures a customer’s relationship with a brand, key factors that encourage loyalty and how
campaigns provide consumer satisfaction and the best experience. Customer  ‑engaging
marketing and communications are therefore realised by brands developing cam‑
paigns, focusing on tailoring experiences to satisfy customers and maintain long‑term
relationships.
Marketing depends on the ability of an organisation/business to coordinate its
resources to deliver an excellent customer experience. In doing so, marketing and com
‑
munications techniques help businesses plan, develop and deliver growth. However a

the business and its target audience to create and deliver value. This approach has led to

to encourage their marketers to be creative and innovative. They need to understand:

Marketing and communications are now very important processes as they help brands

The growth of the marketing services sector
The global marketing services sector is expected to earn half a trillion US dollars in
2025/26 (Statista, 2025). It has grown from its origins in social and political marketing
and research, and the development of advertising and media research and communica
‑
tions techniques.
The timeline of the development of marketing, market and social research, and digital
marketing
 on pages 4–5 (Birn & Stone, 2015) shows key milestones in the development
of marketing, research, consumer insight and communications techniques. They were
the foundations for what have become businesses’ most important development tools,
particularly to grow their sales and brand values.
The marketing services sector has experienced continual growth since the Second
W
orld War through the expansion of small and medium‑sized enterprises (SMEs) into
national and global business groups. This fostered support for brands in international
markets as consumers became increasingly connected and more satisfied with a global
society. The birth of the internet and digital marketing provided new channels and appli‑
cations giving consumers direct contact with national and international brands and new
real‑time analytics and research techniques.
As global brands became multinational corporations, advertising agencies and market
research
 agencies and consultancies began to expand their offices and support services
globally. Agencies not only opened offices in different countries but also started to merge
with or acquire other agencies, leading to the creation of large networks. These networks

Marketing services agencies play an important role in driving business growth by

, and competitive land‑

decisions, minimise risks, and capitalise on new opportunities. The expertise and meth‑
odologies employed by these agencies enable companies to stay ahead of market dynam‑

The timeline shows the origins of marketing, market research and communications
and the key dates of significant changes and developments in the use of research and data
.
• Market research originates in political polling in the USA, which established the cred‑
ibility of sample surveys. The birth of TV advertising developed agencies’ skills in
providing accurate feedback on who was seeing the products and services being adver‑
tised. TV measurement surveys were created to provide feedback and viewing and
audience satisfaction. Computerised databases were created to organise and manage
large volumes of customer data, improving supplier and customer relationships. Rela‑
tional databases and database management systems were established to improve meth‑
ods of storing, retrieving and using data. The World Wide Web (WWW) was launched,
allowing users to track consumer behaviour online. The Archive of Market and Social
Research (AMSR) was created as a source of marketing, research, insight and commu‑
nications information demonstrating how the market research industry developed its
surveys and impact reports. It also makes resources from major brands and research
agencies and techniques available for reference and study.
• The development of market research, data analytics and consumer insight increased
projects and services for marketers, product developers and brand managers to under
‑
stand consumer interactions with brands. Marketers used traditional marketing and
research tools so brands could monitor their campaigns on TV, radio and all other
forms of media to understand the success of their campaigns. They now use digital
marketing and research tools to gain feedback on social media, newsletters, content
and affiliate marketing to ensure communications engage with customers.
• Marketers have developed strategies and campaigns to integrate all these tools to
ensure a marketing mix delivers growth and consumer satisfaction.
• The creation of the professional organisations, associations and other bodies in mar ‑
keting, market research and communications has guided ethical projects, new products
and services and brands. These have successfully advanced the profession, supported
the skills and interests of members, fostered networking and developed codes of con
‑
duct to ensure they serve the ‘public good’. This is even more significant for readers
of this text as the services of these organisations focused on developing the reputation
of marketers, including chartered status and qualifications for career and professional
development.
This book provides numerous theories, examples, practical applications and case studies
on how research, data, insight and analytics lead marketing and communications deci
‑
sions; and how they are depended on to monitor and measure strategy and campaign
success.

Chapter outlines
This book provides academics, students and marketing, research and brand manage‑
ment with an understanding of the tools and techniques to develop effective market
‑
ing strategies and campaigns. It shows how to use market and research and consumer
insight in three main sections reflecting the phases of marketing planning and campaign
delivery:
•  Planning marketing and communications
•  Using consumer insight to develop communications propositions
•  Measuring marketing and communications.
This format helps readers work through the marketing process in lectures, seminars and
self‑study, reviewing how market research and consumer insight have been applied to
marketing and communications plans and campaigns. The case studies have been selected
to demonstrate
how research, analysis and interpretation can help brands pursue growth
strategies, some of which are successful and others unsuccessful. Each chapter concludes
with activities for discussion in seminars to apply the techniques to other examples, exer‑
cises and assessments in appropriate courses and degrees covering marketing, research,
insight and communications.

Chapter 1 – Adding value to marketing and communications strategic

This sets the scene for using data, information, research and analytics in marketing and
communications planning. It discusses what it means to use market insight to reveal rel‑

tion. The benefits are highlighted to help marketers meet audience needs and wants to
ensure satisfaction.

This chapter discusses how market research has helped brands reduce risk, and provides

how consumer insight provides a business with the essential knowledge to create value
and deliver more effective marketing and communications campaigns, better products
and tailored/customised services.
The chapter concludes that market research and customer insight deliver value to help
make customer
‑focused marketing decisions. It argues that value is created by having
a deep understanding of consumers and customers, their behaviours, their preferences,
their needs, their engagement and satisfaction with brands.

Introduction 9
Chapter 6 – How global and ‘glocal’ marketing depends on research
and consumer insight
This chapter will explore how market research and consumer insight have contributed to
the maturity of global marketing and the application of glocal (local, tailored, personalised)
communications campaigns. It will discuss the origins of cultural analysis from social work‑
ers and counselling psychologists, and how cultural competence became the norm in health
and healthcare services texts. It also explores how market research helps identify the aware‑
ness of personal values, beliefs and ‘biases of self and others’. The chapter covers intercul‑
tural relations and communications, and how research helps us understand how consumers
experience cultural differences. It discusses the four pillars of intercultural communications – 
language (culture and ‘linguaculture’), intercultural mediation, channels of communication
and subconscious elements in communicative behaviour – and how they depend on research
to ensure they are integrated into the marketing mix. It also explores the development of
intercultural competence and how research can increase an individual’s understanding and
awareness of their cultural world view; developing knowledge of diverse cultural practices
and world views; and how to demonstrate cross‑cultural skills.
The chapter concludes by showing how two other models, if researched in depth,
provide guidance for global marketing and glocal communication. These cover stumbling
blocks on cultural communications and understanding diversity in global businesses to
drive effective and inclusive ethical plans and campaigns.
Chapter 7 – The future: enhancing customer experience through research
and digital data analysis
This chapter will discusses the challenges of developing marketing and communications
plans in the future as technology becomes faster and more sophisticated. It is already
clear that artificial intelligence (AI) will help market researchers spot and predict trends.
AI will therefore become as important as market research, but is unlikely to replace it
entirely. There will be a role for marketing and communications analysts to identify key
data requirements, analyse trends and interpret their impact on a business and brand.
However AI will be particularly effective to identify changes needed when digital analysis
indicates successful or unsuccessful communications. It will produce smarter, more tar‑
geted marketing communications, but media measurement will continue to be used by
marketers to check on the value of their marketing.
AI will virtually change many of the dynamics that define traditional market research
methods, but both will be used to continue to ensure there is high‑quality data analysis
and
consumer insight. The use of algorithms and machine learning is making market
research faster, more cost‑effective and increasing value as it is fostering faster brand
development and implementation.
Market researchers already believe that AI will provide brands with better data accu‑
racy as trends are identified. This in turn is likely to increase the demand for further pri
‑
mary research to identify the drivers of trends. The impact is likely to expand the market
research industry and the marketing services sector. More jobs are being created to run
AI technology and develop qualitative primary research services delving into the back‑
ground to the quantitative information, particularly why AI is successful/unsuccessful in
developing content and material for communications campaigns.
Behavioural economics will also be discussed as this is developing a range of research
tools and econometric techniques to inform the marketer that the world is complex,

It is increasingly important for marketers to know the economic options available for

Chapter 8– Progressive consumer insight in action
The final chapter discusses the changing relationship between brands and consumers as a
result of analysing research and developing consumer insight. It also provides a checklist
of issues to review as consumer relationships continue to change and competitive markets
become more dependent on focusing on customer needs and relationships. Progressive con‑
sumer insight will lead marketing and communications decisions. It will improve the pro‑
fessional approach to marketing management and contribute to more successful marketing
and communications campaigns. Better decisions will produce more effective integrated
marketing, which will help businesses to grow brands, satisfying more loyal customers.
