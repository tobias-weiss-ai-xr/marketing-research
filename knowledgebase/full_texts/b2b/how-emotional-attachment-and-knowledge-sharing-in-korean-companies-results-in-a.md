Abstract
Purpose: Grounded in network theory and human interaction, this paper focuses on how
emotional attachment facilitates the knowledge sharing process so that mutually beneficial
relationships are developed and result in knowledge conversion resulting in a co-owned
‘integrated’ marketing strategy.
Methodological/approach: Four propositions were developed from the literature review and
tested through data collection involving an open-ended questionnaire completed by 15 senior B2B
marketing managers based in 3 international South Korean companies. The data were analyzed
using the open coding, axial coding and selective coding method.
Findings: The findings suggest that there are four different types of ties such as (1) relational ties;
(2) mindset ties; (3) resource ties; and (4) relational resource ties; which are interconnected and
work differently in terms of senior B2B marketing managers building and developing a co-owned
‘integrated’ marketing strategy. Business relational attributes interact with relational resource ties
and influence the knowledge conversion process. Different types of ties work as a catalyst for
activating interaction and reducing mentally induced boundaries, thus enhancing learning that
improves the resource capability of the organization.
Research limitations/implications: Only three international South Korean companies
participated in the study, and it was not possible to obtain data from staff in partner/supplier
organizations.

in relation to sharing knowledge and remaining open minded in terms of learning different views
from different management groups. This will enhance unification of an organizational culture and

Introduction

Affect is an important aspect of knowledge sharing as it determines how identified
instrumental resources (e.g. information, material, advice/knowledge that is specific to accomplish
a specific task) help staff to achieve organizational goals (Quinn and Baker, 2021). Ho et al.,
(2006) recognize that emotional attachment and the development of knowledge through the
utilization of social capital facilitates the development of social ties. Hence, the value of social
capital arises from social ties that are dependent upon the type of network in use rather than the
effect of either brokering structural holes for certain information or establishing a cohesive
network that vitalizes cooperation and trust. Social ties incorporate both bonding ties (eg.,
coordination and the sharing of ideas) and bridging ties (eg., providing access to resources)
(Chang, 2019) and can be considered highly influential in terms of interactive focused trust-
based relationship building.
Understanding how trust affects inter-organizational information sharing helps staff to
develop knowledge that is essential with regards to strengthening inter-organizational networks
that increase the opportunity to access instrumental resources to improve organizational
performance. Hence, the focus of this study is to explain how senior B2B marketing managers
leverage social capital through socialization to build ties that facilitate knowledge sharing so
that resources are accessed and are beneficial to the development of ties found in inter-
organizational arrangements.
According to Fliaster and Schloderer (2010), there are two different types of ties: (i) the
structural form that is based on the pattern/configuration of ties existing in a network, which is
observable; and (ii) the characteristics of dyadic ties (Borgatti and Foster, 2003), which relate
to sensitizing and emphasize the affective/emotional aspect. According to Robins et al., (2023,

relating to networks (Genkin et al., 2022), we contribute to resource utilization by explaining
how the social bonding process facilitates knowledge conversion among staff in an inter-

Background: A co-owned marketing strategy
A co-owned marketing strategy can be defined as “a set of marketing plans that are
integrated into an overarching marketing strategy that is administered by the main business
partner (e.g. the manufacturer holding legal title to the products/brands on offer)” (Trim and
Lee, 2021, p.1943). Developing emotional bonds through socialization imbues commitment and
trust (Morgan and Hunt, 1994) and facilitates knowledge transfer (Arnett and Wittmann, 2014)
that helps strengthen a network alliance (Håkansson and Snehota, 1995). This is especially
important to note in terms of the “co-operate-to-compete” concept because companies need to
maintain their dominant market position (Andersen and Laursen, 2021) in order to be denoted
industry leaders. Knowledge transfer is achieved through  controlling interpersonal relations and
in-house staff work closely with staff in partner organizations to overcome various challenges
(Håkansson and Olsen, 2015). It is with this in mind that emotional attachment can be

Propositions development and conceptual framework
Relationship building, social bonding and information sharing
Relationship building is considered an integral part of network theory and relates to how
actors/groups interact to develop social networks (Borgatti and Halgin, 2011; Holtgrave et al.,
2017). As regards network theory, Borgatti and Halgin (2011) explain the effects of structure,
consequences, and interactions of structure attributes to yield outcomes. Their contribution can
be found in their argument that Strength of Weak Ties (SWT) theory and Structural Holes (SH)
theory have twin notions of characteristics relating to how structure and position play
fundamental roles in a network. For example, SWT is useful as the weak ties tend to bridge a
network cluster; and as regards SH, this shapes the ego network around a person/actor that
grants an advantage(s) to a person. Borgatti and Halgin (2011) also make known that SWT and
SH rely on a social system as a network of paths that act as conduits for information flow (“flow
or pipes model”). The commonalities of SWT and SH are that they have an influence on a
network in terms of how alliances/relationships take shape and how choice, behaviour,
attitudes, beliefs, organizational structural characteristics and rewards are formed. Taking
cognizance of this, managers can encourage in-group members to engage in emotional bonding

Wittmann, 2014) and intra-organizational and inter-organizational networks become
strengthened through additional social bonding (Conway and Swift, 2000) and consequently,
increased commitment results in shared goals (Wolf et al., 2016). Information sharing can be
considered a shared goal because it is done in order to achieve a specific outcome. Through the
process of an actor gaining and utilizing instrumental resources (Quinn and Baker, 2021) from a
number of networks, emotional attachment (Chung et al., 2016) is intensified and the sharing of
information becomes an acceptable practice.

Increasing marketing capability through resource tie utilization
As regards increasing marketing capability, an organization’s ‘market’ based learning
requires that managers utilize both exploratory and exploitative learning when undertaking
marketing projects (Kim and Atuahene-Gima, 2010). Through the process of increasing an
organization’s capability, attention is given to developing the skill level and knowledge base of
employees (Park, 2011; Kraaijenbrink, 2012) and creating the momentum for learning by
utilizing relational energy (Owens et al., 2016). Consequently, staff are encouraged to use
appropriate channels of communication so that an innovative stance is adopted, and innovative
thinking is encouraged (Mu et al., 2018). The resulting knowledge networks can be viewed as
structural couplings involving territorial subsystems in global innovation systems (Tsouri et al.,
2021, p.10).
In relation to increasing marketing capability, Vorhies and Morgan (2003 and 2005)
argue that it is important to divide marketing capabilities into specialized capabilities (referred
to as lower-order and marketing-mix related) and architectural capabilities (higher-order and
marketing strategy planning and implementation related). In addition, identifying a link
between resource needs and resource inputs is important as it helps actors to set priorities and
locate resource ties so that they can meet specific targets. This relates to how relationships
between/among supplier companies are managed regarding knowledge sharing/exchange and
knowledge transfer/utilization. Furthermore, it focuses on how relationships are managed and
become sustainable bearing in mind the need for staff to continually improve marketing
operations. For example, technological knowledge is viewed as primary knowledge and to be
exchanged (Sammarra and Biggiero, 2008) both within and between organizations (eg., informally
and formally), whilst the utilization of knowledge is to be viewed from the stance of social
interaction and placed in the context of trust-based relationships that are reinforced though
interaction (Håkansson and Ford, 2002). Social capital facilitates value creation through social

Social networks are considered valuable in terms of actors gaining intelligence from different

due to cultural and psychological distance among and between actors. Psychological distance,

psychological distance is associated with how people undertake certain tasks and how they view
risk. It can be argued, therefore, that senior marketing managers consider that a lack of resources
is considered high risk because without appropriate resources it is unlikely that the tasks to be
undertaken will be achieved.

Knowledge conversion and marketing strategy
Intelligence gathering is necessary as regards identifying unmet needs and can be used to
identify solutions in relation to unfamiliar challenges (Fletcher et al., 2013). By placing emphasis
on knowledge and its influence (Fonfara et al., 2018), business operations can be streamlined in
conjunction with those of partner organizations. To achieve this, however, senior management
need to be committed to organizational knowledge creation, which “is the process of making
available and amplifying knowledge created by individuals as well as crystallizing and connecting
it to an organization’s knowledge system” (Nonaka and von Krogh, 2009, p.635). Because
knowledge conversion is dependent upon interaction and involves social practices, it is important
for senior management to provide guidance as to how the development of knowledge both within
and between organizations, is used to perfect marketing strategy implementation.

group’ and the fact that actors embrace the same intimacy and idealism. However, to enhance
marketing strategy development while increasing an organization’s capability, it is necessary
to consider how the knowledge sharing process can be encouraged so that weak ties are turned
into strong ties (Jack, 2005). By putting in place support to facilitate interaction between actors
in partner organizations, mutually co-owned ‘integrated’ marketing plans will emerge based on
the use of knowledge sharing and knowledge conversion.

Managerial implications
As regards knowledge sharing between groups, although actors are willing to share
knowledge with their in-group members, they may not be willing to share knowledge with other
groups (Boer et al., 2011; Bizzi, 2017) and this may cause conflict between the groups
(Anderson et al., 1994). Conflict is representative of a weak tie (Jack, 2005) and can be viewed
as counterproductive as indeed is opportunistic behaviour, should it occur. Therefore, it can be
suggested that senior B2B marketing managers need to promote information/knowledge

Conclusion, recommendations for managers and future research
By deepening our understanding of how emotional attachment strengthens relational
ties vis-à-vis the learning and knowledge sharing process that allows mutually beneficial
relationships to develop between actors in organizations, we provide insights that explain how
inter-organizational relations become solidified but remain flexible, supportive and produce a
co-owned ‘integrated’ marketing strategy. This is beneficial because it should reduce the
mentally induced boundaries between actors in partner organizations and support
communication between actors that materializes in a commitment to learning, sharing
information/knowledge and improving business operations.
