Abstract

The development of the Used Cooking Oil (UCO) export market faces challenges related to
supply continuity, quality consistency, traceability requirements, sustainability certification, and
competition for feedstock. These conditions are relevant to the bus iness development of PT
Biofuel Unggul Lestari (PT BUL), which operates a Business -to-Business (B2B) model
involving the collection, quality control, and trading of UCO for export markets. This study aims

determine the priority marketing strategy to support UCO export market development. A case
study approach was employed using data from PT BUL's Business Plan and relevant
supporting information. The analysis consisted of external and internal factor evaluation using
EFE and IFE, followed by SWOT, IE Matrix, and the Quantitative Strategic Planning Matrix

findings indicate that PT BUL's export market development should integrate B2B marketing

transparency, and sustainability compliance to become integral components of the company's
value proposition in international markets.

BACKGROUND
The development of renewable energy and a circular economy has driven increased
attention to the use of waste as an energy feedstock. One such potential raw material is used
cooking oil (UCO), which can be collected and reused as a biodiesel feedstock. The growth of
the biofuel market has also increased the need for a raw material supply chain that can
guarantee the availability, quality, and origin of materials with greater transparency. Recent
literature on bioenergy supply chains indicates that sustainability, traceability, and supply chain
resilience are becoming increasingly important as market demands and international
regulations increase.
In the Indonesian context, UCO offers the potential to be developed as an economically
valuable commodity and as part of waste utilization to support the energy transition. However,
UCO business development is not solely determined by demand. The character istics of raw
materials originating from various sources cause the collection process to face issues of
supplier fragmentation, inconsistent quality, and volume continuity. At the same time, the
international market is increasingly demanding information on  raw material origin, quality,
documentation, and compliance with sustainability standards. These conditions make supply
chain management a crucial part of building UCO business competitiveness. Effective supply
chain management, especially when integrated with environmental innovation, has proven to
be a key factor in enhancing competitive advantage and business performance (Angelia,
Puspitasari, Syah, & Alhabsyi, 2021).
PT Biofuel Unggul Lestari (PT BUL) is a company that develops a Business -to-
Business (B2B) business model through the collection, quality control, storage, and wholesale
of UCO for the export market. This business model positions PT BUL as a liaison between the
domestic UCO supplier network and international buyers. In the company's Business Plan, the

Despite the availability of export market opportunities, PT BUL faces several strategic

This issue is becoming increasingly relevant in B2B marketing. Industrial buyers
consider not only price when selecting suppliers, but also the supplier's ability to provide

Several recent studies and studies have shown that traceability and supply chain
integration are increasingly important in commodity - and bioenergy -based value chains,
particularly when products are directed to markets with higher sustainability and transparency
requirements. Studies on UCO collection networks also demonstrate the importance of
designing efficient collection networks to support UCO utilization within a circular economy
framework. However, studies specifically integrating B2B marketing strat egies, strategic
priorities, supply chain integration, and UCO export market development at the company level
are still relatively limited. This gap is the one this research aims to fill.
This research uses a case study approach at PT BUL by integrating several strategic
analysis tools. The external and internal environments are evaluated using the External Factor
Evaluation (EFE) and Internal Factor Evaluation (IFE), then used in strategy formulation
through the SWOT and Internal-External (IE) Matrix. Strategic alternatives are then prioritized
using the Quantitative Strategic Planning Matrix (QSPM). The formulation results are then
linked to B2B marketing strategies, supply chain integrati on, business implementation,
financial projections, and risk management.
Based on this approach, this study aims to analyze the strategic position of PT Biofuel
Unggul Lestari, formulate alternative marketing strategies, and determine priority strategies
that can support the development of the UCO export market. The study also aims to explain
how these priority strategies can be translated into strengthening the company's B2B
marketing and supply chain.

This study uses a strategic management perspective to analyze the strategic choices
of PT Biofuel Unggul Lestari (PT BUL) in developing the Used Cooking Oil (UCO) export
market. Strategic management views strategy as a process that connects environmental
conditions, company resources, strategic choices, and implementation to achieve competitive
advantage. In the context of companies operating in international markets, environmental
analysis is important because company decisions are influenced by regulatory  changes,
competitive conditions, customer needs, and supply chain dynamics. The strategic
management framework used in PT BUL's Business Plan also places external and internal
analysis as the basis for strategy formulation. 2023, Fred David, Forest David,  Strategic
Management A Competitive Advantage Approach.
In this study, the external environment is analyzed to identify opportunities and threats
that may impact UCO's business. Macroeconomic, regulatory, technological, social, and
environmental factors can shape the market conditions faced by a company. At the  industry
level, competition, supplier bargaining power, buyer bargaining power, the threat of new
entrants, and the threat of substitute products are used to understand competitive pressures.
The results of this identification are then summarized through the External Factor Evaluation
(EFE). Conversely, an internal environmental analysis is used to identify the company's
strengths and weaknesses, which are then summarized through the Internal Factor Evaluation
(IFE). Both instruments are used as a basis fo r determining the fit between the company's
capabilities and environmental conditions.
The strategy formulation in this study uses the SWOT and Internal-External (IE) Matrix.
SWOT is used to match internal strengths and weaknesses with external opportunities and
threats, resulting in alternative strategies. The IE Matrix is then used to assess the company's
strategic position based on a combination of IFE and EFE scores. The next step uses the

METHOD
Research Design and Object
This research uses a case study approach, with PT Biofuel Unggul Lestari (PT BUL)
as the object of study. The case study was selected based on the need to gain a
comprehensive understanding of the company's strategic position in developing its Used
Cooking Oil (UCO) business for the export market.  PT BUL is a company designed to carry
out the procurement and collection of UCO from a network of collectors, followed by quality
control, storage, and trading for export. The company's business model is business -to-
business (B2B), with local collectors as  its primary suppliers. The company is planned to be
located in North Bekasi due to its proximity to the collector network and UCO source areas
such as Jakarta, Tangerang, and Cikarang.
The study focuses on marketing strategies for developing UCO export markets, with
particular attention to the relationship between marketing capabilities and supply chain
integration. This focus includes the ability to source supplies, maintain quality, es tablish
traceability, meet sustainability requirements, and build relationships with international buyers.
The research is not intended to measure the actual success of strategy implementation, as
some of the data used is part of business planning. Therefore, projected financial results and
volume targets are treated as business scenarios, not as actual data.

Data Collection Sources and Techniques
The research data was sourced primarily from PT Biofuel Unggul Lestari's Business
Plan, which includes nine chapters. The document contains information on the company's
condition, external and internal environmental analysis, strategy formulation, marketin g,
operations, human capital, finance, and risk management.  The internal data used includes
information on resources and capabilities, company strengths and weaknesses, marketing
strategies, supplier networks, quality control processes, traceability, and operational planning.
In the internal analysis, for example, PT BUL identified transparent pricing schemes, supply
chain transparency and documentation, export documents, and quality control as strengths;
while payment accuracy, quality stability, traceability, and volume stability were identified as
factors that still needed improvement.
External data is obtained from information used in the Business Plan regarding industry
conditions, regulations, the UCO market, renewable energy developments, competition,
technology, and other factors affecting the company's business environment. Supporting data
comes from scientific literature and relevant institutional sources.

Data Analysis Techniques
The analysis was conducted in stages, following the strategy formulation framework
used in PT BUL's Business Plan. The company document divides the strategy formulation
process into three stages: input, matching, and decision -making. The input stage uses E FE,
IFE, CPM, and Strategic Group analysis; the matching stage uses SWOT and IE Matrix; and
the decision-making stage uses QSPM.
In this article, these stages are condensed into the following analysis flow:
External & Internal Analysis (EFE & IFE) → SWOT & IE Matrix → QSPM → B2B Marketing
Strategy & Supply Chain Integration → Implementation, Business Projection & Risk
Management.
1. EFE and IFE Analysis
External Factor Evaluation (EFE) is used to evaluate opportunities and threats
originating from the external environment. Meanwhile, Internal Factor Evaluation (IFE)
is used to evaluate a company's internal strengths and weaknesses. The weighting and
rating results are used to generate a weighted score for each factor and a total score
for the company.
2. SWOT and IE Matrix
The EFE and IFE results are then used in the matching process. The SWOT
Matrix is used to develop alternative strategies based on a combination of Strengths -
Weaknesses and Opportunities -Threats. Furthermore, the IE Matrix is used to
determine the company's strategic position based on the IFE score on the internal axis
and the EFE score on the external axis. The literature used in the Business Plan
explains that a score of 2.00 -2.99 indicates an average position for both internal and
external dimensions.  2023, Fred David, Forest David, Strategic Management: A
Competitive Advantage Approach.
3. QSPM
Alternative strategies are then prioritized using the Quantitative Strategic
Planning Matrix (QSPM). QSPM compares the relative attractiveness of several
alternative strategies based on strategic factors obtained from the previous stage. This
approach uses the Attractiveness Score (AS) and Total Attractiveness Score (TAS).
2023, Fred David, Forest David, Strategic Management: A Competitive Advantage
Approach. The strategy with the highest TAS value is determined as the priority
strategy. In the case of PT BUL, this process resulted in the Integrative Supply Chain
Strategy as the alternative with the highest TAS value.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
External and Internal Environmental Analysis
External and internal environmental analyses were conducted to obtain an overview of
PT Biofuel Unggul Lestari's (PT BUL) strategic position in developing its Used Cooking Oil
(UCO) business. This stage serves as the basis for the company to determine stra tegic
alternatives and priorities. Within PT BUL's strategic planning framework, external and internal
factors are first summarized through the EFE and IFE matrices before proceeding to the
matching and decision-making stages.
Externally, PT BUL's business environment is influenced by developments in biodiesel
policy, international trade in UCOs, renewable energy developments, technological changes,
and competitive dynamics. External analysis also indicates threats that require attention,
including dependence on biodiesel policy, sustainability certification requirements, competition
for UCOs, and digital infrastructure gaps at the supplier level. The Business Plan uses PEST
analysis and competitive forces analysis to identify th ese factors as the basis for developing
the company's opportunities and threats.
The External Factor Evaluation (EFE) results showed a weighted score of 2.68. This
value indicates that PT BUL has sufficient capacity to respond to the external factors it faces.
Opportunities that can be exploited include the development of UCO trade, su pport for
renewable energy, and opportunities for supply chain digitization. One prominent opportunity
is IoT -based UCO supply chain digitization, which received a weighted score of 0.28.
Furthermore, the company needs to consider its dependence on biodies el policies, ISCC
requirements, and limitations in digital infrastructure.
Internally, PT BUL possesses several capabilities that support its business model. A
transparent pricing scheme for suppliers, transparent supply chain and documentation,
standardized export documents, pre-export quality control, regular purchasing commitm ents,
stable pricing for suppliers, and the ability to provide volume to importers are all strengths the

a priority strategy for PT Biofuel Unggul Lestari (PT BUL). This strategy is then translated into

PT BUL targets two main groups within its business ecosystem. Upstream, the
company collaborates with UCO collectors, primarily regional and large -scale collectors.
Downstream, its target customers include UCO importers and international biodiesel
companies, with a focus on the Asian and European markets. This segmentation reflects the
characteristics of a B2B business that prioritizes transaction volume, long -term contracts,
supply continuity, and sustainability requirements.

CONCLUSION
This study aims to analyze the marketing strategy of PT Biofuel Unggul Lestari (PT
BUL) in developing the Used Cooking Oil (UCO) export market by considering external and
internal environmental conditions, supply chain capabilities, B2B marketing, and busi ness
risks. The results of the analysis show that PT BUL has the opportunity to develop the UCO
export market, but this opportunity is accompanied by a number of challenges, especially
related to continuity of supply, consistency of quality, traceability, sustainabili ty certification,
competition to obtain raw materials. standards, and buyer relationships. The strategic
environmental evaluation yielded an EFE score of 2.68 and an IFE score of 2.74, indicating
that the company possesses adequate internal capabilities and responsiveness to the external
environment, but still  requires strengthening of several key capabilities.  The strategy
formulation results using SWOT and IE Matrix placed PT BUL in the Hold and Maintain
position. The next stage used QSPM to determine the most appropriate strategic alternative.
Based on the latest version of the Business Plan, the Integrative S upply Chain Strategy
obtained a Total Attractiveness Score (TAS) of 7.25, higher than Market Penetration (6.05),
Market Development (6.03), and Product Development (4.70).  This priority strategy
demonstrates that PT BUL's export market development must begin with supply chain
strengthening. This focus includes expanding and strengthening the supplier network,
enhancing quality control, developing a traceability system, improving payment mechanisms,
strengthening documentation, and meeting sustainability certification requirements. These
capabilities then form the basis for the company to build a value proposition for international
buyers. Research also shows that PT BUL's B2B marketing is closely linked to supply chain
capabilities. Buyers require not only UCO but also assurance of quality, volume,
documentation, traceability, and sustainability. Therefore, PT BUL's supply chain functions not
only as an operational activity but also as part of its value proposition and marketing
differentiation. In terms of implementation, the strategy is directed through strengthening
relationships with suppliers and buyers, a NICE-based relationship marketing approach, direct
marketing, a B2B platform, business matching, and strengthening quality and documentation.
This strategy is expected to support an increase in sales volume, which in the Business Plan
is projected from 800 tons in the first year to 1,600 tons in the fifth year. Export revenue is
projected to increase from IDR 13.30 billion to IDR 29.94 billion during the period. This figure
is a business projection and not an actual performance realization.  Risk management is a
supporting element in strategy implementation. Key risks that need to be managed include
supply volume instability, quality failures or export rejections, buyer retention, certification
compliance, price fluctuations, and financial risk. Strengthening supplier diversification, quality
control, traceability, long -term buyer relationships, and risk monitoring are necessary to
maintain the strategy's sustainability.  Thus, the study concludes that PT Biofuel Unggul
Lestari's UCO export market development is best achieved through a B2B marketing strategy
based on supply chain integration. Successful market development is determined not only by
the ability to attract buy ers but also by the company's ability to guarantee quality, supply
continuity, transparency, traceability, and compliance with sustainability standards.
