Abstract

This paper presents an exploratory framework for examining factors
associated with business-to-business (B2B) client acquisition in recruitment
consulting. It focuses on prospecting quality, requirement understanding,
follow-up consistency, and relationship building. The earlier draft described
a quantitative, correlational study using a five -point Likert questionnaire
and 85 responses. However, the dataset was identified during revision as
simulated rather than collected from actual respondents. Therefore, the
numerical values retained in this document are illustrative only and cannot
support empirical conclusions. The paper outlines the conceptual
background, literature, methodology, and a transparent structure for future
analysis using genuine respondent data.
Keywords: B2B client acquisition; recruitment consulting; prospecting
quality; requirement understanding; follow -up consistency; relationship
building; B2B marketing
1. Introduction

Business-to-business (B2B) marketing refers to the marketing of products
and services between organizations. Unlike business -to-consumer
marketing, B2B marketing involves organizational decision -makers and
generally places greater emphasis on business requirements, professional
communication, trust, and long -term relationships. In recruitment
consulting, B2B marketing is important because recruitment agencies need to identify organizations with hiring
requirements and convert them into business clients.
Recruitment consulting involves providing organizations with recruitment and staffing solutions according to their
workforce requirements. The client acquisition process generally includes identifying potential organizations,
understanding their hiring requirements, communicating suitable recruitment solutions, following up with prospects,
and developing professional relationships. Effective client acquisition is therefore important for recruitment consulting
organizations seeking to expand their corporate client base. Several factors may contribute to B2B client acquisition in
recruitment consulting. Prospecting quality helps  identify organizations that are relevant to the recruitment
services being offered. Requirement understanding enables recruitment consultants to identify clients’ specific hiring
needs and provide suitable solutions. Follow -up consistency helps maintain communication and engagement with
potential clients after initial contact. Relationship building focuses on developing trust, responsiveness, and continued
communication with prospective clients.
This study examines the proposed relationship between prospecting quality, requirement understanding, follow -up
consistency, relationship building, and B2B client acquisition. Because the numerical dataset used in the earlier draft
was identified as simulated, the statistics retained in this formatted version are explicitly illustrative rather than
empirical.

1.1 Problem Statement

Despite the growing importance of recruitment consulting services, acquiring and converting B2B clients remains an
important challenge for recruitment organizations. Potential clients may differ in their hiring requirements,
expectations, responsiveness, and willingness to engage with recruitment agencies. Factors such  as prospecting
quality, understanding of client requirements, follow -up consistency,  and relationship building may influence the
process of converting potential organizations into recruitment clients. The relationship between these factors and B2B
client acquisition in recruitment consulting therefore warrants further examination.
1.2 Objectives of the Study

1. To identify key factors associated with B2B client acquisition in recruitment consulting.

2. To examine the relationship between prospecting quality, requirement understanding, follow-up consistency,
and relationship building and B2B client acquisition.
3. To assess the extent to which these factors may contribute to B2B client acquisition in recruitment consulting.
1.3 Research Hypotheses

Null hypothesis (H₀): The identified B2B marketing factors do not have a significant relationship with B2B client
acquisition in recruitment consulting.
Alternative hypothesis (H₁): The identified B2B marketing factors have a significant relationship with B2B client
acquisition in recruitment consulting.
2. Literature Review

Business-to-business client acquisition has been examined through the perspectives of sales prospecting, customer
relationships, and sales performance. Gopalakrishna et al. (2022) examined the drivers of salesperson prospecting and
conversion and highlighted the importance of prospecting and conversion activities  in new customer acquisition. Their
study considered prospecting efficacy and conversion efficacy as important stages of the sales process.
Crosby et al. (1990) examined relationship quality in services selling and highlighted the importance of trust and
satisfaction in developing relationships between salespeople and customers. This is relevant to recruitment consulting,
where client interaction and trust are important elements of B2B service relationships.
Research in the recruitment sector has examined relationships between recruitment service providers and corporate
clients. Peltokorpi’s work on headhunter –client relationships discusses client interactions, changing requirements,
negotiations, and relationship management in recruitment activities. These topics point to the importance of managing
client relationships within recruitment consulting.
Kaushik (2022) examined recruitment process outsourcing and relationships between service providers and clients. This
work provides a relevant basis for considering client –service-provider relationships in recruitment -related business
services. The precise bibliographic details and the fit of each source to the claims made should be checked against the
original publications before submission.
More broadly, B2B sales research continues to examine relationships between sales activities, customer relationship
performance, and sales outcomes. This literature provides a conceptual basis for examining prospecting quality,
requirement understanding, follow-up consistency, and  relationship building together in the recruitment consulting
context.
2.1 Research Gap

The literature provides substantial discussion of sales prospecting, customer relationships, sales conversion, and
recruitment service relationships. However, comparatively less attention has been given to examining these factors
together specifically in the context of B2B client acquisition in recruitment consulting. This paper  therefore brings
together prospecting quality, requirement understanding, follow-up consistency, and relationship building as a
framework for examining B2B client acquisition.

3. Research Methodology

The original draft describes a quantitative, descriptive, and correlational research design using a structured
questionnaire and a five -point Likert scale ranging from “Strongly Disagree” to “Strongly Agree.” The questionnaire
covers respondents’ demographic and professional profiles and measures prospecting quality, requirement
understanding, follow-up consistency, relationship building, and B2B client acquisition.
The original draft states a sample size of 85 and identifies individuals associated with human resource management,
recruitment, hiring, or business decision-making as the intended respondent group. It describes primary data collection
through an online questionnaire and secondary information from research articles and academic publications.
The independent variables are prospecting quality, requirement understanding, follow-up consistency, and relationship
building; B2B client acquisition is the dependent variable. The original analysis plan uses descriptive statistics and
Pearson correlation analysis, with significance assessed at the 5% level and Microsoft Excel identified as an analysis
tool.
Data-integrity statement: The 85-response dataset used in the earlier draft was identified during revision as simulated
rather than collected from actual respondents. Accordingly, the numerical values  and statistical results presented in
this document are retained only as illustrative examples to preserve the original draft’s content. They must not be
reported as empirical findings. To submit  this work as an empirical study, the author must replace the illustrative

5. Discussion

Conceptually, the four selected factors represent distinct but related activities in B2B recruitment consulting.
Prospecting quality concerns the relevance of target organizations; requirement understanding concerns  the ability to
identify hiring needs; follow-up consistency concerns sustained communication; and relationship building concerns
trust and responsiveness. These activities provide a useful framework for future empirical investigation.
The ranking of coefficients in the original draft would, if independently confirmed using genuine data, suggest
examining requirement understanding and follow -up consistency closely. However, no practical conclusion  about the
relative importance of the factors should be drawn from the simulated values retained here.
6. Conclusion and Practical Implications

This paper presents a framework for examining factors associated with B2B client acquisition in recruitment
consulting. It focuses on prospecting quality, requirement understanding, follow -up consistency,  and relationship
building. The numerical results  retained from the earlier draft are  illustrative only and cannot support empirical
conclusions.
As practical areas for future testing, recruitment consulting organizations may examine how they identify relevant
prospects, document hiring requirements, schedule follow -ups, and build professional client relationships.  These are
framework-based implications, not validated recommendations from the simulated data.
7. Limitations and Future Research

The principal limitation of this version is that the 85-response dataset used in the earlier draft was identified as simulated
rather than collected from actual respondents. Consequently, the statistics and associated hypothesis decisions are
illustrative and cannot be generalized to a population. The original age -profile table also omits the 51 –60 age band,
which requires checking against the source data.
Future research should collect genuine responses using a documented sampling procedure, provide the questionnaire
and coding rules, recalculate descriptive statistics and correlations from the raw data, and report appropriate
limitations. A larger sample across multiple recruitment consulting organizations and qualitative interviews could help
examine additional factors such as service quality, pricing, brand reputation, technology adoption, and salesperson
capabilities.
