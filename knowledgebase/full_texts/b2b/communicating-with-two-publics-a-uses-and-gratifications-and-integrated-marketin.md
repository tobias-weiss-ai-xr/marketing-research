ABSTRACT
This study aims to evaluate exhibitor and buyer communication experiences at the 2026
edition of the Indonesia International Furniture Expo (IFEX), Indonesia’s leading B2B
furniture trade show, using Uses and Gratifications Theory (U&G) and Integrated Marketing
Communication (IMC) theory as its framework. Using a mixed methods design, 193
exhibitors (purposive sampling) and 270 buyers/visitors (accidental sampling) completed an
on site, QR code based questionnaire; closed ended items were analyzed descripti vely, and
open ended items were analyzed using reflexive thematic analysis. Results show buyers
mainly attended IFEX to gratify cognitive/informational needs (market and trend updates,
43%) and social/integrative needs (business relations, 24%) rather than  transactional needs
(placing orders, 11%); these needs were largely met (99% found the products sought;
interaction with exhibitors, M = 4.55/5). Organizer exhibitor communication was rated
strongly (M = 3.29/4), while promotion effectiveness (M = 3.09/4)  and visitor
quality/engagement (M = 2.95/4) scored lowest. Sixty two percent of exhibitors reported
quality leads, 70% reported at least one transaction, 51% of buyers had purchased, and 99.6%
intended to return. These findings imply that IFEX gratifies b uyers' informational needs
effectively but needs stronger business matching and buyer acquisition mechanisms to
convert engagement into measurable commercial outcomes.

INTRODUCTION
 Despite this practical importance, peer reviewed evaluations of Indonesian trade shows remain rare, and most
available assessments are internal, organizer commissioned reports rather than academic studies with an explicit
communication framework. A further  gap concerns Integrated Marketing Communication (IMC) research in Indonesia
specifically: a recent review of IMC studies published in Indonesian communication journals found that most still

operationalize the concept narrowly, around brand centered promotional messaging, with little extension to organizational or
event based settings such as B2B trade shows (Estaswara, Yuliastini, & Kurniasari, 2023). This study addresses both gaps by
proposing and applying a two public communication evaluation model combining Uses and Gratifications Theory, Integrated
Marketing Communication theory, and a public relations perspective on stakeholder relationship management to a B2B trade
show in Indonesia, using the 2026 edition of the Indonesia International Furniture Expo (IFEX) as its case.
 The first theoretical lens is Uses and Gratifications Theory (U&G) (Katz, Blumler, & Gurevitch, 1973, 1974), which
conceives the audience as active rather than passive: individuals purposively select and use a communication source to satisfy
specific needs , and the degree of gratification obtained, rather than the source’s persuasive intent alone, shapes their
evaluation of and continued engagement with that source. Although originally developed to explain audiences’ selection
among mass media, U&G has since retained its explanatory power well beyond that original context; Ruggiero (2000) argues
that the theory’s core logic becomes, if anything, more relevant as communication sources multiply and compete for audience
attention, and Gao and Feng (2016) show e mpirically that the specific gratifications audiences seek directly shape how
effectively brand related content is received. Treating IFEX itself as a communication event that buyers “use” makes it
possible to classify their stated reasons for attending in to recognizable gratification categories cognitive (information
seeking), social/integrative (relationship maintenance), personal evaluative (assessing future participation), and
behavioral/transactional (placing orders) and to assess how far each need is met.
 The second lens is Integrated Marketing Communication (IMC). Schultz, Tannenbaum, and Lauterborn (1993) treat
a brand’s communication as effective to the degree that all audience touchpoints website, social media, direct invitation, word
of mouth, and face  to face interaction are experienced as a synchronized message, and hold that communication should
ultimately be judged by the measurable response it elicits, not merely by the exposure it delivers. Duncan and Moriarty (1998)
extend this into a communication based model built on three intersecting elements messages, stakeholders, and interactivity
and Kliatchko (2008) later refines the construct into four pillars stakeholders, content, channels, and results placing particular
weight on results as IMC’s defi ning outcome criterion. Framing IFEX through IMC allows this study to evaluate how
consistently exhibitors and buyers experience the organizer’s communication, how effective the interpersonal (booth level)
channel is once contact is made, and how far communication outcomes translate into an accountable response.
 A third, complementary lens is public relations and event communication, which treats a planned event as a two
way tool an organization uses to build mutually beneficial relationships with multiple stakeholder publics at once in this case
exhibitors, buyers, media, and the wider furniture industry (Broom & Sha, 2013). From this view, the organizer functions
less as a seller of booth space and more as a manager of the relationship between two publics with different informational
needs, and event success is judged partly by how well it serves the perceptions of each public, not only by the sales it generates
for one of them. This becomes especially relevant when, as in this study, the two publics report markedly different needs and
expectations of the same event.
 Indonesia International Furniture Expo (IFEX) has functioned for decades as one of the country’s principal
international showcases for wood, rattan, and bamboo based furniture, and Indonesia’s Deputy Minister of Industry has
publicly described it as the co untry’s largest furniture and handicraft exhibition (VOI, 2025). The 2026 edition was notable
for two reasons. First, it was staged for the first time at a new venue, ICE BSD, representing a structural change to the event’s
physical and experiential touchp oints. Second, it took place amid a business environment marked by soft global demand,
geopolitical uncertainty, and rising logistics costs that continue to pressure Indonesia’s furniture export sector. These
conditions make a structured, theory informed e valuation particularly useful, both to document exhibitor and buyer
experience at the new venue and to identify communication priorities for the organizer’s subsequent planning cycle.
 Specifically, this study aims to: (1) profile the exhibitor and buyer/visitor respondents; (2) map IFEX’s
communication channels and touchpoints and assess their consistency and effectiveness from an IMC perspective; (3)
examine buyer motivation as a pattern of need gratification and assess how far those needs are met; and (4) identify, through
open ended responses, priority areas for strengthening IFEX as a stakeholder communication platform.

METHOD
 This study used a descriptive mixed method design combining quantitative descriptive statistics with reflexive
thematic analysis of open ended responses, consistent with common approaches to event and trade show evaluation
(Gopalakrishna & Williams, 1992).  Data was collected on site during IFEX 2026 through a self administered digital
questionnaire accessed via QR code. Field surveyors assisted respondents in understanding the questionnaire and confirmed
that participation was strictly voluntary; if a prosp ective respondent declined, no further attempt was made to collect data
from that individual.
 Two sampling strategies were used for the two respondent groups. Exhibitors were recruited through purposive
sampling, first from an organizer provided list of mandatory respondents and then from other willing exhibitors until the
minimum sample size requi red for analysis was reached; inclusion required that a respondent (a) represent a formally
registered IFEX 2026 exhibitor, (b) be present at an active booth during the four day survey period, and (c) provide informed
consent. Buyers/visitors were recruite d through accidental sampling, in which field surveyors approached willing visitors
across different areas of the venue; inclusion required that a respondent (a) be physically present at the venue during the
survey period, (b) be attending in a professiona l or business capacity rather than as event staff, and (c) provide informed
consent. The sampling target for buyers was approximately 70% international and 30% domestic. The final dataset comprised
193 valid exhibitor responses and 270 valid buyer/visitor responses.
 A margin of error and confidence level are statistical properties of an estimate obtained through probability (random)
sampling; because purposive and accidental sampling are both non probability methods, no valid margin of error or
confidence interval can be computed for the resulting data. The frequently cited figures of a 95% confidence level and a ±5%
margin of error were therefore used only as a conventional heuristic to set a minimum target sample size before data collection
began, not as a claim about the statistical precision of the final results. Accordingly, the findings reported below describe the
respondents actually surveyed and should not be interpreted as statistical estimates generalizable to the full population of
IFEX exhibitors or buyers.
 Each device’s unique IP address was recorded solely to flag and remove duplicate submissions from the same
respondent, consistent with Indonesia’s Personal Data Protection Law (Law No. 27 of 2022). IP addresses were stored
separately from substantive survey responses, were not linked to any named individual in the analysis dataset, and were not
retained beyond the data cleaning stage; no financial account numbers, national identification numbers, or other sensitive
personal data were collected, and the only  identifying information gathered respondent name, company, and job title was
provided voluntarily for verification purposes only.
 The questionnaire combined single response, multiple response, rating scale, and open ended items. Closed ended
items were analyzed descriptively using frequency distributions, percentages, and weighted averages on 4 or 5 point
satisfaction scales; items describing awareness channels, promotion, and organizer/exhibitor interaction were then mapped
onto the message, stakeholder, and interactivity pillars of IMC (Schultz, Tannenbaum, & Lauterborn, 1993; Duncan &
Moriarty, 1998), while items describing buyers’ reasons for attending were classified into the gratification categories of U&G
(Katz, Blumler, & Gurevitch, 1973, 1974).
 Open ended items were analyzed using reflexive thematic analysis following Braun and Clarke’s (2006, 2019) six
phase procedure: (1) familiarization with the full set of open ended responses; (2) generating initial codes inductively from
the data; (3) searc hing for candidate themes by clustering related codes; (4) reviewing candidate themes against the coded
data and the full dataset for coherence; (5) defining and naming final themes; and (6) producing the narrative account
presented in the Result and Discussion section. Consistent with the reflexive tradition, coding was treated as an interpretive
act shaped by the researchers’ disciplinary background in communication studies rather than as neutral extraction of pre
existing meanings; the two authors coded an initial subset of responses independently and then compared and discussed their
codes to refine the coding frame before one author completed the remaining coding, a form of investigator triangulation
intended to strengthen interpretive credibility rather than to establish inter rater reliability in the positivist sense.
 One methodological limitation should be flagged at the outset. The closed ended items used to classify buyer
motivation into U&G categories, and the items used to describe communication channels through an IMC lens, were adapted
from an existing organizer designed evaluation instrument rather than from a purpose built, validated gratification

RESULT AND DISCUSSION
Respondent Profile
 A total of 193 exhibitor responses were verified, dominated by respondents in commercial functions such as sales,
marketing, and business development (34%), followed by managerial/decision making roles (19%). Exhibitors were
concentrated in living room (77%), dining room (59%), and family room (49%) furniture, with wooden (69%), rattan (48%),
and bamboo (33%) as the dominant materials. Business scale was mostly small to medium (49% below USD 500,000; 42%
between USD 500,000–1,000,000), and participation history showed a mix of loyal returning exhibitors and newcomers (11%
attending for the first time). On the buyer side, 270 valid responses were obtained from a broadly international audience
approximately 31 countries, 63% international and 37% domestic, led by Italy (11%), the United States (8%), Australia (6%),
and Germany (6%) with Interior Design & Architecture (32%), Furniture Manufacturer (14%), and Exporter (13%) as the
largest business type segments. Thirty eight percent of buyers were first time visi tors, while the remainder had attended
previous editions, most commonly 2025 (30%). The findings below are organized first around IFEX’s communication
channels and touchpoint consistency, interpreted through IMC (Schultz, Tannenbaum, & Lauterborn, 1993; Du ncan &
Moriarty, 1998), and then around the needs buyers sought to gratify, interpreted through U&G (Katz, Blumler, & Gurevitch,
1973, 1974).
Communication Channels and Message Consistency (IMC Perspective)
 Exhibitors’ awareness of IFEX 2026 was driven almost entirely by organizer owned channels: email from the
organizer (51%) and the official website (46%) were the dominant sources, followed at a distance by prior participation
experience (17%), invitation f rom a fellow exhibitor (12%), and Instagram (12%). Buyers’ awareness was more evenly
distributed across owned and interpersonal channels: the official website (26%) and Instagram (25%) led, but invitation from
exhibitors (22%) and colleague recommendation (21%) both interpersonal, stakeholder to stakeholder channels were nearly
as influential, together with prior visits (20%). This pattern is consistent with Duncan and Moriarty’s (1998) argument that
brand relationships are built through the interplay of ow ned/mass media and interpersonal communication: buyers’ broader
channel mix suggests they are reached through a genuinely integrated set of touchpoints, whereas exhibitor awareness
remains concentrated in a single organizer controlled channel. At the same time, exhibitors rated event promotion
effectiveness only M = 3.09/4 (83% satisfied or very satisfied) among the lower scoring items in Table 1 and, when asked
which support they needed most, 91% selected marketing and promotion, far ahead of certification  process (40%) and
branding (39%). Interpreted through IMC theory, this indicates a message reach problem rather than a channel absence
problem: the organizer’s channels function and are used, but exhibitors do not yet perceive them as generating a sufficiently
large or relevant audience precisely the accountable, response based criterion Schultz, Tannenbaum, and Lauterborn (1993)
argue IMC should be judged against, rather than exposure alone.
Organizer Exhibitor and Administrative Communication
 The channels through which the organizer communicates directly with exhibitors information and guidance (M =
3.29/4, 95% satisfied), the booking space system (M = 3.20/4, 90% satisfied), booth set up support (M = 4.11/5, ≈75%), and
overall organization and management (M = 4.23/5, ≈80%) were consistently the highest rated items in the entire exhibitor
dataset (Table 1).
 This finding indicates that IFEX’s administrative, organizer facing communication is executed with a high degree
of message clarity and reliability; the interpretive implication is that this reliability gives the organizer a strong foundation of
trust on which to build stronger promotional and audience facing communication, an implication consistent with survey based
evidence that exhibitors’ engagement readiness with an organizer is itself associated with the relationship quality that drives
perceived exhibition performance (Lai & Wong, 2021).

Interpersonal Communication at the Booth (Interactivity)
 Interactivity, the third pillar of Duncan and Moriarty’s (1998) model emerges as one of IFEX 2026’s clearest
strengths. Buyers rated their interaction with exhibitors at M = 4.55/5 (93% rating 4 or 5), the highest scoring item in the
entire buyer dataset (Table 2), indicating that once contact is made, the booth level encounter is experienced very positively.
Exhibitors, however, rated the quality of visitors/prospective buyers as the lowest item overall (M = 2.95/4, 75% satisfied)
and visitors interest and  engagement only slightly better (M = 3.03/4, 81% satisfied). Read together, these two findings
suggest that the interactivity channel itself performs very well once a buyer and an exhibitor meet, but exhibitors aggregate
perception of who they are reaching through it the intended stakeholder remains the weakest link in IFEX’s communication
system; this asymmetry between encounter level and aggregate level evaluation echoes findings from other trade fairs that
relationship quality and perceived performance can diverge across levels of analysis even within the same event (Lai &
Wong, 2021).

Experiential/Physical Touchpoints
 The venue itself functions as a communication touchpoint through which buyers and exhibitors experience IFEX’s
brand promise. Buyers rated venue services and facilities and overall exhibition experience at M = 3.38/4 each (≈97%
satisfied or very satisfied)  and IFEX hospitality services slightly lower at M = 3.34/4, their relatively weakest experience
dimension, though still positive. Open ended responses from both groups converged on specific frictions at this touchpoint
overly cold air conditioning, limite d food and rest areas, and uneven traffic distribution between halls at the new ICE BSD
venue indicating that the first time relocation introduced experiential inconsistencies that partially offset otherwise stron g
administrative and interpersonal communication.

Buyer Motivation as Need Gratification (U&G Perspective)
 Consistent with U&G theory’s premise that audience members actively select a communication source to satisfy
specific needs (Katz, Blumler, & Gurevitch, 1974), buyers’ stated reasons for attending IFEX 2026 map onto distinct
gratification categories (Table  3). The most frequently cited reason, obtaining market and trend updates (43%), reflects a
cognitive/information seeking need; visiting a business partner’s booth (24%) reflects a social/integrative need; evaluating
future participation (21%) reflects a p ersonal evaluative need; and placing a purchase order (11%) reflects a
behavioral/transactional need. This distribution shows that, for most buyers, IFEX is used primarily to gratify informational
and relational needs rather than to complete an immediate transaction. This finding is not unique to IFEX: qualitative research
on B2B trade fairs elsewhere has similarly found that visitors rarely prioritize on the spot buying, and that the most
experienced visitors tend to devalue immediate purchasing the most, treating fairs instead as a relationship building and
market scanning channel (Sarmento, Farhangmehr, & Simões, 2015) a pattern corroborated in this study by the factors buyers
weighed when deciding to attend a furniture exhibition at all, led by keeping u p with furniture trends (36%), the tourism
appeal of the destination country (34%), and product price (33%).

Content Gratification and Outcome
 The specific content buyers said they could find at IFEX but not easily elsewhere unique Indonesian products (64%),
reasonably priced options (52%), and the ability to inspect quality directly (33%) functioned as the core content that IFEX
offers as a comm unication source, and buyers evaluation of that content was strongly positive: 99% reported finding the
product categories they considered important, and product variety, quality, and availability was rated M = 3.44/4 (≈99%
satisfied or very satisfied), the highest scoring item in the buyer dataset besides interaction with exhibitors. Following U&G’s
premise that successful need gratification predicts continued source use (Katz et al., 1974), this near complete gratificatio n
of buyers content needs is consi stent with the very high buyer return intention observed later (99.6%), even though a large
share of buyers had not yet converted that gratification into a purchase during the event itself.
From Gratification to Response
 Schultz, Tannenbaum, and Lauterborn (1993) argue that IMC should ultimately be judged by its ability to elicit an
accountable response, not merely to deliver exposure or satisfaction. Measured against that standard, IFEX 2026’s
communication produced a rea l but incomplete behavioral response. On lead generation, 62% of exhibitors reported
generating quality leads, 23% answered “possibly,” and 15% answered “no”; on transactions, 70% confirmed at least one
transaction occurred while 30% did not. Fifty six per cent of deals reportedly began as a first meeting at IFEX, while 44%
developed only through post event follow up, and 75% of exhibitors reporting transaction counts recorded fewer than 10
transactions. On the buyer side, 51% had already purchased, 19% were planning to purchase, and 30% had not purchased at
the time of the survey, broadly consistent with the U&G finding that only 11% of buyers attended primarily to place an order.
Both sides nonetheless reported very high intent to return (98% of exhibitors,  99.6% of buyers) and rated IFEX above
comparator events (75% and 74%, respectively), indicating that the shortfall lies specifically at the response/conversion stage
of communication rather than in overall message reception or satisfaction.

 Thematic analysis of exhibitors open ended responses converged on five themes, most of which concern specific
communication touchpoints: (1) the need for greater and more relevant buyer traffic and stronger promotion (message reach);
(2) structural concerns about booth layout, visitor flow, and uneven exposure between halls (experiential channel); (3) venue
comfort issues, most frequently overly cold air conditioning and limited food and rest facilities (experiential channel); (4)
requests for more structured business matching and industry knowledge sharing sessions (interactivity); and (5) scheduling
and accessibility concerns, including a preference among some exhibitors for the previous JIExpo venue (channel continuity).
On the buyer side, five themes emerged: (1) product curation was viewed as strong, with only minor gaps in niche categories
(content gratification largely met); (2) trust in suppliers depended heavily on communication quality, responsiveness, and
professionalism (interpersonal channel); (3) global economic conditions and import regulations/trade policy were identified
as the dominant external pressures on buyers businesses (60% and 54%, respectively); (4) buyers wanted Indonesian
manufacturers to strengthen branding, quality consistency, di stinctive design, and export readiness (message and content);
and (5) buyers requested clearer exhibitor information, better wayfinding, and more structured business matching (message
clarity and interactivity).
Synthesis
 Read together, these findings show that IFEX 2026 succeeds strongly at the levels of content gratification (U&G)
and interpersonal interactivity (IMC’s interactivity pillar), but is comparatively weaker at the levels of message reach and
stakeholder response accountability (IMC’s message and stakeholder pillars). Because a large share of buyers use IFEX
primarily to gratify cognitive and relational needs rather than to transact (43% versus 11%), this pattern is consistent with
U&G theory rather than a sign of event failure but it explains why exhibitors, whose participation objectives are
predominantly transactional (boosting sales, 66%; new contacts, 48%), perceive a gap that buyers themselves do not report
experiencing. Read through a public relations lens, this is a case of an organizer successfully serving one public’s relational
and informational needs while a second public’s need for a demonstrable communication return remains only partly addressed
precisely the two public balancing problem event based public relations is meant to manage (Broom & Sha, 2013). This
asymmetry between buyer side gratification and exhibitor side response accountability, a communication to commerce gap
rather than any single weak channel, appears to be the central structural i ssue underlying IFEX 2026’s results, compounded
by the experiential frictions introduced by the first time relocation to ICE BSD.
Limitation
Two limitations should be noted. First, as flagged in the Method section, the questionnaire was originally designed as an
organizer commissioned event evaluation instrument rather than a purpose built communication study; it does not include
validated gratification sought/gratification obtained scales of the kind used in classical U&G research (Palmgreen & Rayburn,
1979), so the categories in Table 3 were derived by mapping existing items onto established typologies rather than measured
directly, and the IMC touchpoint labels in Tables 1 and 2 reflect the same post hoc reclassification. Readers should therefore
treat the communication theoretic interpretation offered in this article as an interpretive reading of business survey data rather
than a first order psychometric test of U&G or IMC. Second, because both sampling techniques were non probability methods,
findings describe the respondents surveyed and should not be generalized statistically to the full population of IFEX
exhibitors or buyers. Future studies of IFEX or comparable trade shows would benefit from a purpose built instrument that
measures gratifications sought and obtained directly, alongside communication specific outcomes such as message recall,
perceived channel consistency, and relationship quality.

CONCLUSION
This study evaluated the 2026 edition of the Indonesia International Furniture Expo through a two public
communication model combining Uses and Gratifications Theory, Integrated Marketing Communication theory, and a public
relations perspective on stakeholder relationship management. The findings show that buyers primarily use IFEX to gratify
cognitive/informational and social/integrative needs rather than to complete an immediate transaction (43% versus 11%), and
that these gratifications are largely met, with strong content satisfaction and very positive interpersonal interaction with
exhibitors. At the level of IMC’s message and stakeholder response pillars, however, the event is comparatively weaker:

exhibitors do not yet perceive the buyer audience reaching them as sufficiently large or relevant, and a meaningful share of
positive engagement does not convert into a documented transaction during the event itself even though both groups report
very high return intentions (98% and 99.6%).
The study’s main contribution is to show that this pattern is best understood not as an event failure but as an
asymmetry between two stakeholder publics with different communication needs, and to offer a replicable framework
gratification sought and obtai ned, message/stakeholder/interactivity/results, and two public relationship management for
evaluating B2B trade shows in Indonesia and comparable emerging market contexts.
Practically, three priorities follow: (1) segmenting promotion by buyers predominant gratification type rather than treating
the audience as undifferentiated; (2) strengthening the interactivity to results pathway through structured business matching,
hosted buyer programs, and systematic post event follow up; and (3) improving the experiential touchpoints venue layout,
wayfinding, and hall exposure equity that open ended responses identified as the most tangible source of friction at ICE BSD.
Future research should test this framework using validated gratification sought/gratification obtained scales and
direct measures of message consistency and relationship quality (see Limitations), and could extend it longitudinally across
future editions o f IFEX to examine whether the communication to commerce gap identified here narrows as the organizer
adopts more targeted, two public communication strategies.
