ABSTRACT
This study examines the role of digital marketing in customer acquisition in the granite industry of Prakasam District, Andhra Pradesh. Online visibility,
messaging platforms, search engines, B2B marketplaces and online reviews have become important channels through which granite manufacturers, dealers and
buyers find one another. The study focuses on the importance of online presence, digital platform use, trust in online information, reviews and comparison,
responsiveness, discovery ofmanufacturersonline,contact afteronline discovery andpurchase after onlinediscovery. Astructuredquestionnaire of27itemswas
analysed for 103 respondent records covering owners, managers, sales staff, dealers, existing customers and potential customers across Chimakurthy, Ongole,
Martur, Markapur, Addanki, Kandukur and other locations. Descriptive findings show that 71 respondents (68.9%) were likely or very likely to discover granite
manufacturersonline and64respondents (62.1%)ratedonline presence asvery orextremely important, whereas only 41respondents(39.8%)oftenor veryoften
contacted a business afterdiscovering it online and 50 respondents (48.5%) were likely orvery likely to purchase after online discovery. Fast response was rated
the most important factor (mean 4.19), while trust in online information recorded one of the lowest means (3.17). Spearman rank correlation results showed that
none of the six proposed associations was statistically significant at the 5% level. The findings point to a gap between online awareness and online action. Note:
the dataset analysedhere isa simulated (mock) dataset andnot real survey data, sothe results areillustrative only. [Delete this note once realsurvey data replace

2.ReviewofLiterature
Kannan and Li (2017) developed a framework for digital marketing research that highlights the touchpoints in the marketing process where digital
technologies have an impact, and set out a research agenda from the perspective of the firm. Chaffey and Ellis-Chadwick (2019) present digital
marketing as an integrated set of online channels, including search, social media, e-mail and websites, that support the acquisition and retention of
customers. Mangold and Faulds (2009)described socialmediaas a hybrid elementof the promotion mix,because customers talk tooneanotherwhile
firmshavelesscontroloverthecontentofthoseconversations.
In business-to-business settings, Michaelidou, Siamagka and Christodoulides (2011) examined the usage, barriers and measurement of social media
marketing among small and medium-sized B2B brands. Järvinen and Karjaluoto (2015) examined the use of web analytics for digital marketing
performance measurement. These studies are relevant because they connect digital channels with the practical questions faced by smaller industrial
firms,namelyadoption,barriersandthemeasurementofresults.
Trust is central to online exchange. Mayer, Davis and Schoorman (1995) proposed an integrative model of organisational trust in which the trustor’s
willingnesstobevulnerabledependsontheperceivedability,benevolenceandintegrityoftheotherparty.Thisisrelevantbecauseabuyerwhofindsa
granitesupplieronlinemustjudgeitsreliabilityfromlimitedonlineinformation,suchasphotographs,listings,reviewsandresponsestoenquiries.
Localand sector context is provided by the Prakasam DistrictAdministration, which describes BlackGalaxy granite as the district’s OneDistrict One
Product, and by the Brief Industrial Profile of Prakasam District prepared by the Government of India’s Office of the Development Commissioner
(MSME),whichincludestheChimakurthygranitepolishingclusteramongthedistrict’sindustrialclusters.
3.ResearchGap
The literature reviewed above indicates a progression from general frameworks of digital marketing towards studies of B2B adoption, performance
measurement and trust. However, the studies reviewed here do not combine online presence, platform use, trust, reviews, responsiveness, online
discovery,contactandpurchasewithinasinglelocalstudyofanatural-stonecluster.
Somestudiesprovidegeneralframeworksofdigitalmarketingwithoutfocusingonaspecificindustrialcluster.
SomestudiesexaminesocialmediamarketingorwebanalyticsinB2Bfirmswithoutdirectlystudyinghowdiscoveryleadstocontactandpurchase.
Sector and district documents describe the granite industry of Prakasam District but do not examine how customers are acquired through digital
channels.
The present study combines the importance of online presence, platform use, trust, reviews, responsiveness and the stages of discovery, contact and
purchaseinthecontextofthegranitesectorinandaroundPrakasamDistrict.

8.ResearchMethodology
Research Design. The study uses a quantitative, descriptive and cross-sectional research design. Information is analysed at one point in time from a
structured questionnaire. The design is suitable for describing digital marketing use and attitudes among granite-sector respondents and for testing
associationsamongmeasuredvariables.
Study Area and Population. The study is centred on respondents associated with the granite sector in and around Prakasam District, Andhra Pradesh,
includingChimakurthy,Ongole,Martur,Markapur,AddankiandKandukur.
SampleandSampling.Thedatasetcontains103respondentrecords(SIM001–SIM103).Ofthese, 94respondentswerefromlocationswithinPrakasam
District and 9 were from outside the district. The Dataset_Status field of every record reads “SIMULATED — NOT REAL SURVEY DATA”; the
records are therefore mock data prepared for demonstration and are not evidence from actual respondents. [Replace with the actual sampling method

Data Collection and Questionnaire. The questionnaire contained 27 questions. Questions 1–5 covered the respondent profile (role, region, industry
experience, primaryinvolvement and business size). Question7 recorded the most-used digital platform as a categorical response. Questions 6 and 8–
27 used five-point rating responses covering extent of use, frequency, usefulness, importance, likelihood, effectiveness, influence, trust, interest and
agreement.Responsessuchas“Notsure”and“Notapplicable”werenotassignedaratingandweretreatedasmissingvalues.
Data Analysis. Frequency and percentage analysis were used for categorical responses. Mean scores were calculated for the rating-scale items using
valid responses only. Spearman rank correlation was used to test the six proposed hypotheses because the main variables were measured on ordered
responsescales.Pairwisedeletionwasusedformissingvalues,andassociationsweretestedatthe5%level(two-tailed).

11.SuggestionsandRecommendations
Granite businesses should prioritise the channels that respondents use most, namely WhatsApp Business, Google Search and Maps, and B2B
marketplaces,andkeepproductinformation,photographsandcontactdetailscompleteandconsistentonthem.
Because fast response was rated the most important factor, businesses should set internal response-time standards for online enquiries and assign
responsibilityforhandlingthem.
Businesses should address the sources ofonlineproblems thatdetercustomers, suchas incompleteinformation ordelayed replies, since three-quarters
ofrespondentsreportedbeingdeterredtoagreatextent.
Given the low trust in online information, businesses should provide verifiable details such as clear specifications, genuine photographs of available
stock,transparenttermsandcustomerreferences.
Online reviews and comparison were rated useful; businesses can encourage satisfied customers to share feedback and can present products in a way
thatsupportscomparison.
Micro and small-scale businesses, which form the largest business-size group in the sample, may benefit from shared training and cluster-level
initiativesbyindustryassociationsindigitalskills.
Businesses that plan to invest more in digital marketing should track leads at each stage (discovery, contact, quotation and purchase) to learn which
channelsconvert.
Futurestudiesshoulduserealsurveydata,largersamplesanddifferentcustomergroupstocompareresultsandimprovegeneralisability.
12.PracticalandTheoreticalImplications
PracticalImplications. The findings are relevant to granitemanufacturers, dealers, industry associations and digital marketers working with the sector.
The most notable pattern in the analysis is the gap between the high perceived importance of online presence and discovery and the lower levels of
contact, purchase likelihood and trust. Marketing efforts should therefore go beyond visibility and focus on responsiveness, credible information and
easeofcontact.
TheoreticalImplications.The studyapplies a digitalmarketing frameworktoa natural-stonecluster andcombines onlinepresence, platform use,trust,
reviews, responsiveness andthe stages of discovery, contact andpurchasewithinone empiricalframework focused on PrakasamDistrict. The absence
of significant correlations in the present sample suggests that stated attitudes and stated intentions should not be assumed to be linked, and that
behaviouraldatawouldaddvalue.
