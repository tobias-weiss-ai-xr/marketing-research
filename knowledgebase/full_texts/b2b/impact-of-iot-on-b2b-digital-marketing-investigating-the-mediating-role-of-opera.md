The rapid incorporation of IoT in business processes
promises transformative changes, particularly in en-
hancing online marketing (Gupta, 2020). As IoT enables
unprecedented data collection from connected devices,
businesses, especially in the business-to-business (B2B)
sector, see potential opportunities for improved market-
ing effectiveness (Mikalef et al., 2019). However, while
the adoption of IoT in B2B, especially in industries like
rentals, is on the rise (Fintorova, 2021; Luttinen, 2022),
its tangible impact on online marketing performance
remains unclear (Baltuttis et al., 2022). Challenges such
as data availability, research heterogeneity, and data col-
lection issues further complicate the scenario (Cortez &
Johnston, 2017; Lilien, 2016). Notably, while IoT’s po-
tential in B2B marketing, including targeted advertising
and personalized communication, has been recognized
(Raghupathi et al., 2022; Silva et al., 2023), there is a dis-
tinct lack of comprehensive studies on its full impact.
Concerns like data privacy, security, standardization,
and integration pose substantial barriers to effective IoT
utilization in B2B marketing (Jurcut et  al., 2020). One
major issue, as identified by Leurent and De Boer (2018),
is the difficulty of IoT integration within organizations.

Abstract. The emergence of the Internet of Things (IoT) has driven a significant shift in business processes, particularly
in online marketing. This study examines the impact of IoT implementation on marketing performance, emphasizing
the mediating role of operational effectiveness in a business-to-business (B2B) context. Using data from a German
company, the research employs regression analyses based on the Baron and Kenny framework to assess direct and
mediated relationships. The findings reveal that IoT has a significant positive effect on marketing performance. While
operational effectiveness alone does not significantly predict IoT’s impact, it has a strong direct influence on marketing
outcomes. The study confirms a partial mediation effect, with a small yet statistically significant indirect pathway. These
results highlight the important role of operational effectiveness in maximizing the benefits of IoT for marketing suc-
cess. This research contributes to the limited literature on IoT’s mediating variables in marketing and offers actionable
insights for optimizing IoT-driven B2B marketing strategies.
Keywords: Internet of Things, operational effectiveness, marketing performance, B2B marketing, mediation analysis.
JEL Classification: M15, M31, O33.
1. Introduction
The rapid advancements in communication technolo-
gies suggest that we are on the cusp of a new industrial
era, with the Internet of Things (IoT) at its core (Chen &
Lien, 2014; Li et al., 2017). IoT, a system of interrelated
devices, is reshaping economic landscapes, impacting
both industries and consumers (Edquist et al., 2021). As
new technologies emerge, industries adapt to maximize
profits (Jensen, 2002). With IoT’s potential to gather ex-
tensive data, a shift towards a data-driven approach in
online marketing is inevitable (Shah & Murthi, 2021).
The evolution of online marketing is tightly bound to
technological progress, with innovations like fifth-gener-
ation technology propelling IoT solutions (Basyazicioglu
& Karamustafa, 2018; Chow et al., 2021; Lei et al., 2021).
As the boundaries of online marketing expand, IoT plays
an increasingly integral role, offering enhanced connec-
tivity and new strategies (Ferrell et  al., 2021; Ziemba
et al., 2018). However, the melding of IoT in e-marketing
brings challenges, both technological and operational,
that businesses must address for successful integration
(Abdel-Basset et al., 2019; Hanafizadeh et al., 2021).

Operational effectiveness is becoming increasingly in -
tertwined with marketing performance, especially in the
B2B realm. Key performance indicators (KPIs) like rev-
enue growth, customer acquisition cost, and customer
lifetime value are pivotal to measuring marketing effec-
tiveness (Chaffey & Ellis-Chadwick, 2019; Ghahremani-
Nahr & Nozari, 2021). However, the introduction of IoT
data offers a paradigm shift. Liu et al. (2020) showcased
that IoT data enhances demand forecasting, leading to
optimal inventory management, elevated customer sat-
isfaction, and augmented sales. Concurrently, Wang
and Wang (2020) emphasized that IoT insights allow
companies to comprehend their clientele more deeply,
leading to tailored marketing strategies and augmented
customer allegiance. While IoT data holds promise in
refining marketing strategies and amplifying operational
effectiveness, comprehensive exploration and methodi-
cal integration are imperative for genuine advancements.
Despite extensive studies in this domain, a distinct gap
remains in understanding the relationship between op-
erational performance, informed by IoT data, and mar -
keting performance.
A significant stride in bridging the research gap sur -
rounding the Internet of Things (IoT) and marketing per-
formance was achieved by Mehralian (2022). This study
elucidated how entrepreneurship orientation serves as a
mediator in the relationship between IoT capabilities – the
ability of organizations to integrate and leverage intercon-
nected devices, sensors, and data analytics to optimize
operations (Jonny & Toshio, 2021) and marketing perfor-
mance. While it effectively sheds light on the intersection
of IoT, marketing performance, and entrepreneurship ori-
entation, a holistic understanding of operational effective-
ness as a mediator remains less explored. Based on these
findings, our study endeavors to analyze the role of op-
erational effectiveness as a pivotal mediator between IoT
and marketing performance. By pivoting from an entre-
preneurial orientation to an operational effectiveness per-
spective, we aim to provide a more comprehensive view of
how IoT impacts and enhances marketing performance in
contemporary business landscapes.
The study employs a quantitative research approach,
utilizing data analysis to investigate the impact of the
Internet of Things (IoT) on marketing performance in
a B2B setting. Archival data from a B2B organization is
drawn upon to perform regression analysis, with the as-
sessment focusing on the direct and indirect effects of
IoT implementation on operational effectiveness and
the consequent marketing outcomes. Mediation analysis
further elucidates the role of operational effectiveness in
this relationship, providing insights into the multifaceted
influence of IoT on marketing performance.
2. Theoretical background
2.1. Internet of Things and marketing
The Internet of Things (IoT) has been increasingly iden-
tified as a leading force reshaping online marketing.
Several scholars, including Sheoran and Vij (2013),
Chung and Liang (2020), and Broo et  al. (2022), have
emphasized the transformative impact of IoT, suggest-
ing that it enhances effectiveness, efficiency, optimizes
data analysis, and influences marketing performance
and strategies. Bajpai and Pillai (2022) and Y ang (2022)
have indicated that IoT is fundamentally changing the
dynamics of how marketing is approached, making it
more data-centric, precise, and customer-oriented.
The IoT is very instrumental in the marketing. It has
tremendously shifted over the past few decades from the
traditional error of blogging, liking, tweeting, writing
online reviews, and sharing videos and photos (Kaplan
& Haenlein, 2010) to new cutting-edge technologies in
several realms, including augmented reality, wearable
technology, chatbots, gamification, transmedia, and so
on that impact on smart marketing (Simões et al., 2019).
IoT-based smart marketing encompasses creating, com-
municating, delivering, or exchanging valuable prod-
uct offerings through storytelling and communication,
receiving customers’ brand perception, experience, or
feedback, understanding customers purchasing behavior,
and real-time persona development (Simões et al., 2019).
A key application of IoT is its enhancement of cus-
tomer relationship management. By leveraging IoT,
marketers can engage in more targeted communication,
making marketing strategies more effective and efficient
(Parra et al., 2019; Ramhormozi et al., 2019; Zare & Ho-
narvar, 2021). Furthermore, IoT allows for a more de-
tailed understanding of customer needs, preferences, and
behavior (Gasimov & Aliyeva, 2020; Rajesh et al., 2022),
leading to improved forecasting and strategic decision-
making (Bajpai & Pillai, 2022; Malik et al., 2022).
IoT’s influence also extends to business-to-business
(B2B) marketing. The integration of IoT data helps in
analyzing customer preferences across all areas of mar -
keting communication (Aras et al., 2022), offering flex-
ibility and resilience in diverse situations. For small
and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), the digital shift
brought about by IoT is critical for competitiveness, ena-
bling them to effectively differentiate their value proposi-
tions in an intensely competitive market (Lu et al., 2022;
Zauskova et al., 2022).
IoT also influences international marketing, enabling
more detailed regional segmentation and customization,
thus enhancing the efficacy of marketing messages and
adapting to regional nuances (Granheim et  al., 2022;
Mondal et al., 2022).
On the consumer side, IoT features are perceived as
essential, providing significant value and satisfaction.
This sentiment is captured by Andarwati et  al. (2020)
and Sinha and Brar (2022), who highlight the consumer’s
readiness to share personal data for tailored experiences.
Moreover, the evolution of marketing through IoT inte-
gration necessitates marketers to adopt non-traditional
methods, prioritizing data-driven approaches over con-
ventional ones, as suggested by Bogoviz et  al. (2021).

2.2. Operational effectiveness and marketing
performance
Operational effectiveness is an important step towards
an organization’s success. The factors such as system, in-
formation, and service quality related to the efficiency of
technological innovations, as well as cost, quality, reli-
ability, flexibility, and speed related to operational effec-
tiveness play a key role (Santa et al., 2014). Fundamen-
tally, it relates to how effectively a company can produce
outputs from its given inputs, maximizing the quality
of products or services while minimizing waste, delays,
and costs (Shaw, 2003). Conversely, marketing effective-
ness measures the success of marketing campaigns in
reaching their objectives, which might range from brand
awareness to sales conversions (Farris et al., 2010).
The interdependence between operational effective -
ness and marketing strategy is evident. Before diving into
complex marketing approaches, it is imperative for an
organization to ascertain that its foundational operations
are optimized and devoid of substantial impediments. As
highlighted by Dibb et al. (2008), even the most prom-
ising marketing strategies might underperform if the
baseline operational processes are suboptimal or com-
promised.
This concept becomes even more pronounced within
B2B settings. Due to the intricate nature of B2B trans-
actions, which often encompass longer sales cycles and
multiple decision-making layers, operational effective-
ness is of overriding importance (Alsaad & Taamneh,
2019). Ordanini (2005) indirectly observed that B2B en-
tities with robust operational frameworks tend to achieve
markedly higher rates of marketing success.
Recent attempts to analyze these interrelated relation-
ships have relied on integrated performance measures
that combine the operational and marketing domains.
However, the comprehensive methodology is still in its
infancy (Ali & Matsuno, 2018; Lamberti & Noci, 2010).
For future research, there is an urgent need to develop
models that capture the nuanced interactions between
operational and marketing effectiveness, especially in the
evolving field of B2B commerce.
Operational effectiveness is an emerging determinant
in the nexus between Internet of Things (IoT) capabili-
ties and marketing performance (Akhtar et  al., 2018).
Established Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) such
as revenue growth, customer acquisition cost, and cus-
tomer lifetime value remain instrumental in evaluating
marketing outcomes (Chaffey & Ellis-Chadwick, 2019;
Ghahremani-Nahr & Nozari, 2021). Recent researches
underscore the transformative potential of IoT data in
this context (Wu et al., 2021).
3. Methodology
While existing studies show that IoT boosts marketing
performance and operational effectiveness, they often
overlook the mediating role of operational effectiveness–
how well a company achieves its strategic goals. Opera-
tional effectiveness is essential for competitive advantage
and profitability, especially in the current complex busi-
ness climate (Akhtar et al., 2018). IoT has the potential
to improve this effectiveness by enhancing productivity,
quality, and cost management. Y et, the mediation effect
of operational effectiveness between IoT and marketing
performance remains underexplored. This study aims to
bridge this gap by investigating how IoT contributes to
marketing performance through the lens of operational
effectiveness. It will address these questions:
a)  How does IoT contribute to operational effective-
ness?
b)  In what ways does operational effectiveness influ-
ence marketing performance?
c)  And how does operational effectiveness mediate
the IoT-marketing performance link?
The hypothesis is that operational effectiveness will
positively mediate the IoT and marketing performance
relationship.
Our study takes inspiration from Mehralian (2022),
who investigated the significant influence of the Internet
of Things (IoT) on marketing performance, while also
recognizing the intermediary role of entrepreneurial ori-
entation. In developing our model (Figure 1), we set the
aim to provide a more comprehensive view of how IoT
impacts and enhances marketing performance. Drawing
on insights from Davis (2017), it is recommended that
the selection of core performance indicators (KPIs) for
assessing marketing and operational performance should
prioritize metrics that directly reflect business outcomes.
In line with these recommendations, we selected KPIs
that capture the essential dimensions of both operational
effectiveness and marketing performance, thereby ensur-
ing a robust and theoretically grounded evaluation of
IoT’s impact. Koob (2021) elucidates that effectiveness
in content marketing is not just about creation but hing-
es significantly on consistent performance measurement
and the strategic use of the derived data. Thus, this pro-
cess of refinement echoes the importance of specializa-
tion both in structure and enabling systems. In addition,
Tariq et  al. (2020) emphasized the transformative role
of IoT, suggesting its capabilities go beyond enhancing
marketing efforts to actively drive objectives of Customer
Relationship Management (CRM) and overall business
growth.
This research utilizes detailed data collected from
the internal Postgres database of a mid-sized B2B com-
pany based in Germany, specializing in the rental of
construction machinery such as excavators and loaders.
The database serves as a centralized data lake aggregating
real-time operational and marketing data generated from
IoT-enabled devices integrated into the company’s fleet.

The analyzed dataset, covering the period from January
2021 to October 2023, includes detailed information
on every successful conversion process, machine usage,
operational efficiency, and customer interaction data,
reflecting the company’s recent strategic shift toward da-
ta-driven decision-making. Our study constructs a con-
ceptual model rooted in key performance indicators that
both gauge marketing effectiveness and are organization-
centric. We specifically focus on online marketing strate-
gies targeting business customers. The Internet of Things
(IoT) plays a vital role in enhancing these strategies, aid-
ing in the creation of targeted advertisements, promot-
ing the company’s unique selling proposition (USP), and
optimizing regional and product-specific campaigns.
Unlike B2C marketing, the B2B arena emphasizes cross-
functional collaboration and frequently requires multiple
touchpoints, often leading to offline final transaction de-
cisions. In such a competitive environment, specialized
quotations and expertise are critical for success.
The validity of the items is based on face validity,
as they are the company’s adopted KPIs. In the study,
operational effectiveness is presumed to mediate the
relationship between IoT and marketing performance.
The analysis employed the renowned Baron and Kenny
(1986) statistical mediation model to test the mediating
role of operational effectiveness. The steps of the Baron
and Kenny model are as follows:
 – demonstrate that the independent variable (X) is
significantly related to the dependent variable (Y)
when tested in a simple regression, indicating the
total effect of X on Y , denoted by path c (Figure 1);
 – establish that the independent variable (X) signifi -
cantly predicts the mediator variable (M) in a sim-
ple regression, representing the effect of X on M,
denoted by path a;
 – confirm that the mediator variable (M) significantly
affects the dependent variable (Y) in a regression
equation that controls for the independent variable
(X), reflecting the direct effect of M on Y , denoted
by path b;
 – verify that the effect of the independent variable (X)
on the dependent variable (Y) diminishes or is no
longer significant when the mediator variable (M)
is included in the equation, suggesting the indirect
effect of X on Y through M, denoted by path c′
There is complete mediation when a mediated ef-
fect is present and path c′  is not statistically signifi-
cant. Conversely, partial mediation occurs when a me-
diated effect is present and path c′  remains significant.
The mediated effect is also quantified as the difference
between the coefficients c and c′  (c – c′ ). Baron and
Kenny (1986) posited that perfect mediation occurs
if c′  becomes nonsignificant after controlling for M,
although they did not provide a significance test for
this condition. The Sobel test and the Preacher and
Hayes (2004) mediation approach build upon Baron
and Kenny’s work by explicitly testing the significance
of the indirect effect.
To investigate the relationship between the Internet
of Things (IoT), operational effectiveness, and mar -
keting performance, we devised a methodology rooted
in current literature. In line with Zhu et al. (2021) and
Khan and Javaid (2022), who highlighted IoT’s role
in e-commerce quality and industrial operations re-
spectively, our approach encompasses data acquisition,
processing, and interpretation. During acquisition, we
employed a Boolean analysis of IoT implementation as
suggested by Mutuku and Muathe (2020) and Shen-
koya (2020), measured the percentage of IoT data uti-
lized as indicated by Tatsumoto et al. (2022), and as-
sessed device response times (Arowoiya et al., 2020) to
gauge operational effectiveness. Anosike et al. (2021)
underpinned our understanding of IoT’s potential
in uplifting operational performance. For marketing
performance, our primary metric was the Customer
Acquisition Cost (CAC) based on findings from Guen-
ther and Guenther (2021). Our interpretation phase
aimed to address the knowledge gaps identified by
Ghosh et  al. (2021) concerning broader IoT usage
considerations. Differing from studies like those by
Sestino et al. (2020) that primarily used surveys, our
emphasis was on real-time data, echoing the necessity
for tangible metrics. Overall, this methodology offers
a structured, yet comprehensive approach to discern-
ing IoT’s tangible impact on business landscapes.

Generally, the items are measured on different scales.
In order to minimize heterogeneity that could bias the
regression results, all attributes except three–Inquiry
Rate, Dedicated IoT Analysis, and Percentage of IoT

was applied to normalize these data values to a range of
0 to 1, as the data consists of non-negative inputs.The

that all items are positively correlated (Table 2). The aver-

both dedicated IoT analysis (–0.035) and the percentage
of IoT data used (–0.016), resulting in a lower correla-
tion of these items with the overall scale (CA = 0.0575).

5. Results and discussion
5.1. Mediation results
The study uncovers that while IoT positively influences
marketing performance within a B2B context, its impact
is nuanced by operational effectiveness, which plays a
partially mediating role. The statistical analysis con-
firms that IoT directly enhances marketing performance
(c = 0.257, p < 0.001) but does not significantly predict
operational effectiveness at a conventional threshold
(a = 0.041, p = 0.056). Operational effectiveness itself sig-
nificantly improves marketing performance (b = 0.772,
p < 0.01), with the mediation effect indicating that a
portion of IoT’s impact on marketing performance can
be attributed to improved operational effectiveness (c’ =
0.226, p < 0.01). The total effect of IoT on marketing
performance was significant, yet the mediation by op-
erational effectiveness was not as strong as hypothesized,
suggesting additional factors may also influence this re-
lationship.
The relatively small mediation effect observed in this
study (0.031, p = 0.068) may result from the complexity
of mechanisms through which IoT influences operation-
al effectiveness. While operational effectiveness strongly
predicts marketing performance, IoT’s direct influence
on operational metrics was weaker than anticipated. It
is possible that additional factors–such as organizational
culture, managerial capabilities, or implementation qual-
ity–play significant mediating or moderating roles be-
yond what was captured by our selected KPIs (Leurent
& De Boer, 2018; Tariq et al., 2020). Future research in-
volving broader datasets and more diverse KPIs might
further clarify these relationships.
5.2. Practical implications
This study offers several valuable insights for business
practitioners in the B2B sector seeking to optimize their
marketing strategies through IoT integration. Firstly,
the demonstrated positive impact of IoT on marketing
performance underscores the importance of strategic
IoT adoption. Managers should focus on leveraging IoT
data for targeted and personalized marketing communi-
cations, which can improve customer engagement and
efficiency in resource allocation.
Secondly, although operational effectiveness played a
modest mediating role, its significant direct impact on
marketing outcomes emphasizes the necessity of con-
tinuously improving operational processes. Practitioners
should invest in enhancing operational capabilities, such
as streamlining service processes, optimizing response
times, and improving data-driven decision-making.
Finally, the nuanced results suggest companies should
not rely solely on IoT-driven operational improvements
for marketing success. Instead, integrating IoT within a
broader strategy–considering managerial competencies,
implementation quality, and organizational culture–is
critical. Businesses are recommended to adopt a holistic
view when implementing IoT solutions, aligning techno-
logical investments with clear marketing and operational
objectives to fully realize potential performance gains.
5.3. Limitations
The study’s limitations arise from its reliance on second-
ary data from a single B2B company, which may limit the
generalizability of the findings across industries or busi-
ness models. Additionally, the use of company-specific
KPIs, while face valid, might not encompass all relevant
aspects of operational effectiveness or marketing perfor -
mance. The low reliability indicated by the Cronbach’s
alpha for operational items also suggests caution when
interpreting these results. Furthermore, the correlational
nature of the data does not allow for firm conclusions
regarding causality.

5.4. Future research directions
Future research could address these limitations by incor-
porating primary data from a broader range of compa -
nies and industries to improve generalizability. Longi-
tudinal studies could help establish causal relationships
more firmly. Exploring additional mediating factors be-
tween IoT and marketing performance could provide a
more holistic understanding of the IoT’s impact on busi-
ness outcomes. Additionally, refining the measurement
instruments to achieve higher reliability and using more
sophisticated statistical methods could further enhance
the robustness of the findings.
