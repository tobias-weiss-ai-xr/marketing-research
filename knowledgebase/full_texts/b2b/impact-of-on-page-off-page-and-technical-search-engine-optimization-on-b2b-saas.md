Abstract
Business buyers of software now do much of their homework before any
salesperson gets involved. They search, skim comparison pages and read
reviews, which makes organic visibility a real growth channel for SaaS
companies. What is missing is practitioner -level evidence on how content,
site health, external authority and analytics fit together in day -to-day work.
This paper is an exploratory, practice-based case study built on a six-month
on-the-job training placement (9 March to 10 September 2026) in the digital
marketing team at Celoxis Technologies Pvt. Ltd., Pune, which sells project
portfolio management software, and on its test management product, Tuskr.
I looked at four linked streams of work as one growth system: on-page SEO
and content, off-page SEO and social visibility, technical SEO with keyword
and competitor research, and reporting in Google Analytics 4 (GA4) and
Google Search Console (GSC). The evidence comes from site crawls,
keyword and ranking tools, observation of search results, analytics platforms
and content  management records, not from a respondent survey. Four
patterns stood out. Search intent guided planning better than keyword
volume. Comparison and industry pages needed more fact -checking and
original material than ordinary blog posts. Audit output only h elped once it
had been checked and ranked by priority. And off -page work lasted longer when it followed relevance rather than
volume. The paper closes with recommendations on topic clusters, scheduled content refreshes, regular technical audits,
joined-up GA4 and GSC reporting, evidence standards for comparison content and relevance-led authority building.
Keywords: B2B SaaS Marketing, Search Engine Optimisation, Content Marketing, Search
Intent, Technical SEO, Web Analytics, Google Search Console, Digital Marketing
1. Introduction
Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) has changed the way organisations buy technology. Long licence negotiations have given
way to subscriptions that teams can assess online: they compare features, read reviews, try free plans and book demos.
In that process the vendor's website, and where it shows up in search, is often the first thing a buyer meets. A  product
page, a helpful article or a comparison page that turns up at the right point in someone's research can shape the shortlist
well before sales gets a call.
That is why SEO and content marketing matter so much for B2B SaaS, and why they are hard. The people searching
are professionals with narrow problems, such as prioritising a portfolio, budgeting a project or managing test cases, and
they expect depth, accuracy and clear writing. The results pages they see are crowded with big software brands, review
sites and established publishers. No single tactic wins here. What counts is how well content quality, technical site
health, outside authority and measurement support one another.

Much of what is written on this topic is either promotional or tied to one tool. Academic accounts of how a B2B SaaS
team actually gets from a keyword to a published page, from a crawl report to a prioritised fix, or from a raw analytics
export to a decision are rare. This paper tries to fill part of that gap with a structured case study of the marketing work
I did for Celoxis, a project portfolio management (PPM) platform, and Tuskr, a test management tool fo r quality
assurance (QA) teams, during a six-month on-the-job training (OJT) placement. It does not test a formal hypothesis. It
describes a real marketing process, examines what the evidence suggests about it, and offers practical recommendations.
2. Review of Literature
Search behaviour and intent.  Broder's (2002) taxonomy sorts  web queries into navigational, informational and
transactional types, and it is still a useful way to think about why people search. The same logic carries over to B2B
SaaS. A query like "what is project portfolio management" points to learning, "best project budgeting software" points
to evaluation, and "pricing" or "demo" points to readiness to act. This fits the staged picture of buying in the consumer
decision journey work of Court et al. (2009), where buyers move through consideration and evaluation l argely by
searching for themselves instead of simply being carried along by advertising.
SEO as a marketing strategy. Berman and Katona (2013) place SEO inside search marketing next to paid placement,
which supports treating organic visibility as a deliberate marketing investment instead of a technical add-on. Ziakis et
al. (2019) bring together the factors linked with higher Google rankings and sort them into content, keyword, metadata
and link groups. That grouping lines up closely with the on-page and off-page split used in practice and in this study.
Content marketing. Pulizzi (2012) and Patrutiu Baltes (2015) describe content marketing as producing useful, relevant
material that draws in and keeps a clearly defined audience. In a specialised B2B category this points towards covering
a set of related topics rather than publishing stand-alone articles, which is the thinking behind the topic-cluster approach
discussed later.
Web analytics and measurement. Jarvinen and Karjaluoto (2015) look at how web analytics supports the measurement
of digital marketing performance, and they note that gathering data is the easy part; turning it into decisions is harder.
That point applies directly to the reporting described here, where the foc us was on explaining change rather than just
logging figures.
Strategy, positioning and services marketing. Porter's (1980) Five Forces framework helps make sense of a software
market with many rivals, well -informed buyers and credible substitutes. Positioning theory (Ries & Trout, 2001)
explains why comparison and industry pages are worth the effort: they let a vendor spell out how it differs from
alternatives in a specific setting. Software behaves much like a service, so the extended services mix of People, Process
and Physical Evidence (Booms & Bitner, 1981) helps explain why expert content, steady processes  and visible proof,
such as reviews and product screenshots, build trust online. Chaffey and Ellis -Chadwick (2019) supply practical
planning frameworks for sequencing digital activity, and Kotler and Keller (2016) provide the segmentation and
targeting ideas applied here.
Research gap. The existing literature establishes, separately, why search intent matters, what SEO consists of, what
content does and why analytics -led measurement is needed. What is harder to find is a practitioner -level account of
how these pieces work together inside one B2B SaaS marketing team that serves two different product domains. This
paper offers a documented, process-level account from such a setting.

4. Research Methodology
4.1 Research Design
I used a descriptive, exploratory and practice -based design. Rather than sending a questionnaire to a sample of
respondents, the study documents and assesses a linked set of marketing activities carried out inside a live business.
That choice suits a placement where the researcher was also a working practitioner, and where the goal is to understand
the process, not to generalise statistically.
4.2 Setting and Duration
The study took place in the marketing function of Celoxis Technologies Pvt. Ltd., Pune, across a six-month OJT period
from 9 March to 10 September 2026. I worked under an industry mentor from the company's marketing team and an
academic OJT mentor from the  School of Business, Indira University. Celoxis sells project portfolio management
software, and its website highlights project planning and tracking, project financials, resource management, intake
management, reporting and AI -supported insights. Tuskr , the second product, is a cloud -based test management
platform launched in 2021 for QA teams. Working on both meant writing for two quite different audiences: PMO leaders
and project managers on one side, QA managers and testers on the other.
4.3 Data Sources
The study draws on secondary and tool -generated data: website pages, observations of search results, keyword and
ranking tools, Screaming Frog crawl reports, GA4, Google Search Console, WordPress records and competitor
websites. Company details come from o fficial public pages. Confidential business metrics, audited financials and
customer-level data were not accessible and are not used.

4.5 Method of Analysis
The work followed a repeating loop: define the requirement, research the topic or issue, make or recommend the change,
check that it went live correctly where possible, watch performance, report what was found, and plan the next task.
Numerical evidence (clicks, impressions, click-through rate, average position, sessions and key events) was compared
period over period using descriptive statistics. Qualitative evidence (observations of search results, competitor page
structure, content quality checks and validation of technical issues) was compared in a structured way and grouped by
theme. I kept the wording of conclusions cautious, using phrases such as "associated with" and "may indicate", because
rankings and traffic respond to many factors beyond any single action.
4.6 Ethical Considerations
No customer data, internal credentials or personal data about employees were used.
Where confidential figures were out of reach, the study does not estimate or invent them.

Three observations came out of this. First, a lower-volume term can be worth more than a high-volume one when it fits
the product, the audience and the buying stage. Second, programmatic pages built from repeatable patterns (software
by industry, role or use case) only earn their place when each page has its own information, examples and terminology.
An aerospace page, a consulting page and a higher -education page cannot be one template with the label swapped.
Third, comparison pages attract buyers who are close to deciding, but they carry reputational risk, so claims about
competing products need checking and a balanced account of where each tool fits and where it falls short.
I wrote meta titles and descriptions to reflect what each page is for, not as generic summaries. The quality checks
differed by page type: whether a blog title matched the query and the content, whether a product page described real
capability without keyword stuffing, and whether programmatic pages avoided repeating one another.
5.2 Off-Page SEO and Social Visibility
Off-page work covered forums and communities, social bookmarking, backlink opportunities, software directories and
LinkedIn. The guiding rule was relevance (Table 4). A directory had to fit the software category, an answer in a
community had to address wha t the person actually asked , and a backlink had to make editorial sense. In forums I
contributed first and mentioned the brand second, and I was open about it whenever the product came up. I avoided
mass submissions because they add little credibility. On LinkedIn I shared educational content adapted to the platform
instead of simply posting links.

Three patterns stand out. First, visibility, traffic and conversions moved together (Figure 1). Clicks, impressions and
landing-page sessions rose by roughly 20 to 26 percent, while key events from organic traffic rose faster, by about 34
percent, which su ggests the extra traffic was relevant rather than incidental. Second, average position improved by
about three places, and several target keywords moved towards or onto the first page (Figure 2). Third, CTR slipped by
0.20 percentage points because impressions grew faster than clicks. That may mean newly visible queries sit at weaker
positions, or that titles and descriptions are not persuasive enough for the new impressions, which is why metadata
optimisation was named as the next action. Consistent with the cautious approach used throughout, I describe these
movements as associated with content publication, metadata work and technical fixes, not as proven results of any
single action, since seasonality, algorithm updates, competitor activity and search demand also affect outcomes.
5.6 Reporting Practice

6. Findings
 SEO for B2B SaaS works best as one connected system of content quality, technical health, authority and
measurement, not as a collection of separate tactics.
 Search intent is a better planning variable than keyword volume; high -intent long-tail terms can be more valuable
strategically than broad, high-volume ones.
 Industry and comparison pages need more fact-checking and more original information than generic blog posts, and
pages built only from a template risk being unhelpful and repetitive.
 A technical crawl is useful only when its issues are validated and prioritised, because not every warning matters
equally to the business.
 GA4 and Google Search Console complement each other. One describes behaviour on the site and the other
describes performance in Google Search, so they are best read together.
 WordPress implementation is part of SEO execution, since the live page decides the user experience and the
crawlable structure.
 Domain knowledge clearly improves marketing quality: understanding project management strengthened the
Celoxis content, and learning QA concepts strengthened the Tuskr content.
 Off-page effort lasts longer when it rests on relevant communities, credible directories and useful contributions than
when it relies on mass link submission.
7. Recommendations
 Build formal topic clusters  for each priority solution and industry, linking core product pages to supporting
educational, industry, use-case and comparison content through internal links.
 Run a scheduled content refresh programme. Review high-value pages every quarter
or half-year for facts, search intent, metadata, internal links, screenshots and competitive depth. A proper refresh
updates far more than the year in a title.
 Make recurring technical audits routine , using a standard crawl checklist, ranking issues by impact, assigning
owners and re-crawling after fixes.
 Integrate GA4 and Search Console reporting  by content group, so that visibility, landing -page traffic,
engagement and conversions are tracked together instead of as separate indicators.
 Set evidence standards for comparison content  with a fact -check sheet that records the source and date for
competitor features, pricing and limitations.
 Focus off-page effort on relevance and authority, putting credible directories, communities, review platforms
and editorial opportunities ahead of bulk submissions.  Keep separate learning libraries  of glossaries, trusted
sources and subject-matter review notes for project management and for QA and testing, to speed up onboarding and
improve accuracy.

8. Limitations and Scope for Future Research
The study covers one company, two product contexts and a single six-month window, whereas SEO results often take
weeks or months to settle. Confidential business metrics, sales data, audited financials and customer -level data were
not available, so the ana lysis cannot link organic performance to revenue. The performance figures in Tables 8 and 9
and Figures 1 and 2 are indicative and still need checking against final exports. Competitive observations rest on search
visibility, not verified market share. Org anic outcomes are also exposed to algorithm updates, seasonality and
competitor activity outside the practitioner's control. Some technical recommendations depend on development
capacity, and off-page results depend on outside publishers and platforms. Finally, my own learning curve during the
placement may confound any comparison between early and later work.
Future research could build a unified dashboard that links content groups to GA4 and Search Console outcomes, and
map organic landing pages to qualified leads where data governance allows. It could compare the long-term contribution
of industry pages, comparison pages and educational blogs, test structured content formats for AI -assisted search and
answer engines while keeping them readable for people, and evaluate a formal digital PR and expert -contribution
programme in project management and software testing communities.
9. Conclusion
This paper has looked at how a B2B SaaS marketing team combines content, technical optimisation, off-page authority
and analytics to build visibility for two software products. The evidence from the placement supports three practical
conclusions. First, results come from the system, not from any single tactic: content attracts relevant searches, technical
health lets search engines reach and understand the site, off -page credibility strengthens discoverability, and analytics
shows what to do next. Second, relevance, whether to search intent, the audience or the platform, matters more than
volume at every stage. Third, careful interpretation, which keeps observation apart from conclusion and checks claims
before they are published, matters as much to B2B SaaS marketing as creativity does. For SaaS firms competing with
larger brands for professional audiences, investing in topical depth, accuracy and measurement is likely to pay off more
