Abstract
This research is directed at examining the effect of the Artificial
Intelligence (AI) based Supply Chain Management (SCM) on the
financial performance of companies in emerging economies.
Specifically, it investigates the role of sustainable development
practices (environmental, social and economic aspects) as a
mediator in the process of transforming the technological
potential of the supply chain into real profit.
A quantitative research design is used, and survey data from a
cross-sectional sample of supply chain managers, sustainability
officers and financial executives in a sample of key emerging
markets were disseminated and subsequently analysed, resulting
in 276 valid responses. Using PLS-SEM technique, the study
aims to test the hypothesized relationships empirically between
AI-SCM adoption, SD initiatives, and firm profitability.
The empirical results showed that AI-SCM has a positive and
significant direct effect on financial performance. More
important, the analysis shows that sustainable development is an
important mediating factor between the two. In turn, the
efficiency of the resources used, waste reduction, and ethical
sourcing (sustainable development) are improved, leading to
cost savings, premium pricing, and an increase in market share
(financial success). While existing literature has been extensive
on the role of AI and sustainability in developed contexts, this
study delves into the distinctive challenges of institutions and
resources faced in emerging economies. It provides a new model
integrated approach that shows that technological advancement
(AI) and ecological/social responsibility (sustainability) do not
necessarily contradict, but are complementary, and is the force
that drives competitive advantage in emerging markets.
Investments in emerging economies that are directly linked to a
specific sustainable development goal (SDG) have the highest
economic payback and should be the priorities for executives.
The study highlights the need for creating policies and
regulations to support the adoption of green AI, fostering
economic growth and environmental sustainability.
Keywords: Artificial Intelligence (AI), Supply Chain
Management, Financial Performance, Sustainable Development,
Emerging Economies, Structural Equation Modeling.

AI optimizes supply chain execution, assisting companies to predict demand with precision, automate
tedious tasks, and optimize resources. By leveraging AI-driven SCM systems, companies can gain real-time

improvements directly contribute to improving the efficiency of the organization and its profitability.
Financial performance is one of the most important measures of the success of an organisation. It is a
measure of a firm's profitability, efficient use of resources, and economic growth potential. Some common
measures of financial performance are profitability, return on investment (ROI), return on assets (ROA), and
revenue growth. Competitive markets make businesses constantly strive to deliver financial results and
resilience in operations. AI's role in supply chain management is one of the most significant factors for
improving financial results, as it lowers operation costs, waste, improves productivity and customer service
quality. (Danach et al., 2024). AI technologies facilitate streamlined decision-making processes and efficient
resource usage, contributing to better financial outcomes and sustainable competitive advantage for
businesses.
Although companies are embracing AI technologies, the connection between AI-powered SCM and
ROA is not straightforward, particularly in emerging economy companies. Emerging economies are
distinguished by their high growth rates of industry, changing market conditions and technological change.
Some of the economic challenges that firms in these economies may encounter include weak institutional
support, lack of technological infrastructure, and regulatory uncertainty (Bag et al., 2021). These are the
factors that affect the impact of AI and its effectiveness in implementing it. Furthermore, AI technologies can
enhance operations, but the sustainability of long-term business success also relies on the incorporation of
sustainability principles into the business operations.
There's a growing focus on sustainable development among organizations around the world. The idea
is about achieving the economic, environmental and social aspects in balance for long-term organizational and
societal well-being (Danach et al., 2024). In today's business environment, sustainability is an increasingly
desired characteristic of business to minimize environmental impact and ensure ethical functioning.
Sustainability is closely related to supply chains as these have a direct impact on the use of resources, waste
generation, energy usage and carbon emissions. Conventional supply chains tend to focus on productivity and
profitability, while ignoring environmental and social impacts. AI-powered technologies, however, have the
potential to revolutionized sustainable supply chain systems that can meet financial and environmental goals.

2. Literature Review
Artificial Intelligence (AI) has revolutionized the world of business operations and supply chain
management (SCM) has undergone a significant change. AI is now being used across various industries to
enhance efficiency, decision making, and financial outcomes throughout their supply chain, from healthcare
to manufacturing. With AI technologies like machine learning, predictive analytics, robotics, and intelligent
automation, companies are able to create more agile, data-driven supply chains. Meanwhile, sustainable
development and responsible business are more and more on the world's agenda, and this has caused
organisations to make their supply chain sustainable. Whereas this is the case of emerging economies where
market uncertainty and lack of infrastructure are problems, and where AI tools in SCM have become a key
enabler in operational excellence and financial sustainability (Pournader et al., 2021). This literature review
investigates the existing studies regarding AI in supply chain management, financial impacts, sustainable
development, and sustainability's moderating impact. The review also pinpoints the key gaps in the literature,
both theoretical and empirical, especially in the context of emerging economy firms.
2.1 AI and Supply Chain Management
Artificial Intelligence is the capability of artificial machines or computer systems that can mimic
human intelligence, such as learning, reasoning, problem-solving and decision making (Russell & Norvig,
2021). AI technologies have increasingly become a part of the supply chain landscape, enabling companies to
process massive amounts of data, uncover patterns, forecast future trends, and automate different areas of the
chain. SCM is the coordination of procurement, production, inventory management, transportation, logistics
and distribution processes to provide products and services efficiently (Christopher, 2016). The traditional
supply chain has a range of inefficiencies, such as forecasting inaccuracies, communication delays, and a lack
of visibility along the chain, among other issues. AI technologies have emerged as solutions to these
constraints, offering intelligent and automated solutions.

2.2 Benefits and challenges of implementing AI in SCM and its impact on financial performance
Financial performance is a key measure of organizational success. It is a measure of the firm's capacity
to make profits, increase productivity, and to achieve long-term growth. Financial performance indicators are
commonly used metrics for measuring the performance of a company's financial operations and include return
on assets (ROA), return on investment (ROI), profitability, revenue growth and operational efficiency. The
Resource Based View (RBV) theory states that organizations are able to secure sustainable competitive
advantage by leveraging valuable, rare and innovative resources, including AI technologies (Dubey et al.
2021). AI-based SCM is seen as a strategic capability of an organization since it enhances operational
efficiency and aids in informed decision making. Previous studies indicate that AI-based SCM can have a
positive impact on financial outcomes. AI technologies can cut down operational expenses, minimize
forecasting mistakes, and optimize resource utilization by automating repetitive tasks. AI-powered systems
also enhance customer satisfaction by delivering faster order fulfilment, customization, and improved product
availability, leading to increased revenue and profitability (Ivanov & Dolgui, 2020).
It is crucial to note that companies that employ AI-powered analytics in their supply chain operation
report improved financial results, thanks to the boost in operational efficiency and strategic planning (Wamba
et al., 2020). Likewise, (Queiroz et al., 2021) have concluded that AI-powered SCM improves organizational
agility and competitiveness in the market, thereby affecting the financial performance. Besides, AI
technologies play a role in reducing costs by optimizing the logistics and transportation systems. Intelligent
route optimization strategies boost fuel use, transportation costs, and delivery delays. AI-powered inventory
management software can also reduce overstock situations and prevent shortages. Yet, while these advantages
may be present, certain researchers question the financial implications of the implementation of AI in different
industries and company environments. For AI to be effective, there is a need for both the employees and
management to be prepared, as well as the technological tools and infrastructure (Kamble et al., 2019).
However, in emerging economies, businesses face financial limitations and limited access to cutting-edge
technologies that can hinder the efficiency of AI-enhanced SCM systems.
2.3 Incorporating sustainable development into Supply Chain Management.
Sustainable development is defined as the development which satisfies the needs of the current
generation without adversely affecting the capacity to meet the needs of future generations. The idea is to
achieve economic, environmental and social sustainability. Sustainability is a vital issue in supply chain
management, as supply chains have a substantial impact on environmental and social outcomes (Oelze et al.,
2016). However, the traditional supply chain processes can be harmful to the environment by using lots of
energy, making a lot of waste, polluting the environment, and emitting lots of carbon. As a result, organizations
are increasingly moving towards implementing sustainable supply chain management (SSCM) initiatives to
minimize environmental footprint and increase their social responsibility.

2.4 AI-Driven SCM and Sustainable Development
Technologies are vital in Sustainable Supply Chain Management. AI-based SCM has the potential to
boost sustainability by ensuring more efficient use of resources, minimizing waste, and providing greater
transparency in operations. AI's role in sustainability is one of its significant contributions: better energy
efficiency. AI systems streamline production schedules and resource management, minimizing energy waste
and unnecessary usage. Smart forecasting systems also enable businesses to reduce overproduction and
unnecessary stock accumulation, reducing the impact on the environment (Qu & Kim, 2024). AI technologies
also contribute to the sustainable logistics and transportation management. Predictive analytics and route
optimization systems optimize fuel usage and lower carbon emissions by determining the most efficient
transportation routes. Furthermore, AI-based monitoring tools can enhance supply chain visibility and
traceability, helping companies promote ethical sourcing practices and adherence to environmental laws (Bag
et al., 2021).
AI-powered supply chains can have a notable impact on enhancing environmental sustainability, for
example, by boosting waste management and resource efficiency as identified by (Benzidia et al., 2021).
Likewise, (Gupta et al. 2022) indicated that AI technologies support sustainable manufacturing and enhance
organization sustainable performance.
AI also plays a role in social sustainability by enhancing worker safety in the workplace and
minimizing or automating dangerous work activities. In addition, AI-powered data analysis can enable
companies to track their suppliers' adherence to labour laws and ethical practices. But there are some concerns
about the sustainability of AI technologies. While large-scale AI systems can benefit the environment in
certain ways, they also have the potential to generate significant energy usage and environmental impacts if
not implemented properly (Vinuesa et al., 2020). Hence, there is a need to ensure responsible and energy-
efficient implementation of AI in organizations.

3. Methodology
The methodology employed to analyse the link between Artificial Intelligence (AI) Supply Chain
Management (SCM), sustainable development and financial performance of emerging economy firms is
explained in this chapter. It explains the research philosophy, research approach, research design, data
collection procedures, research instruments, data analysis techniques and ethics for research. The methodology
is systematic and it aims to guarantee the reliability, validity and accuracy of the research results.
3.1 Research Philosophy
The study is based on positivist research philosophy as this type of research emphasizes on objective
measurement and statistical testing of relations between variables. Positivism is apt for quantitative research
as it emphasizes on the empirical basis, hypothesis testing and scientific analysis (Pearlson et al., 2024). Based
on the positivist philosophy, the researcher can analyse the causal relationship between AI-driven SCM,
sustainable development and financial performance using structured questionnaires and statistical analysis
techniques. The study is aimed at the measurement of organizational phenomena, so positivism is a good
approach.
3.2 Research Approach
In this study deductive research approach is used. A deductive method assumes that findings from
existing theories and literature will lead to the formation of hypotheses, which will then be tested with data
collection and analysis. The Resource Based View (RBV) theory and Triple Bottom Line (TBL) theory are
used to guide this research in explaining the relationship between AI-driven SCM, sustainable development,
and financial performance. The deductive approach is suitable because the study is directed to testing the
hypothesis of whether the use of AI in the SCM has a positive effect on the financial performance of companies
and if the use of sustainable development mediates such an influence.
3.3 Research Design
This is a quantitative cross-sectional survey. Quantitative research allows the researcher to measure
(numerically and statistically) objectively the relationships existing between variables. The cross-sectional
design is one in which data are collected from the respondents at one time. This is an appropriate approach
due to the study's objectives to assess the role of AI-based SCM in financial performance and to explore the
relationship between AI-based SCM and the concept of sustainable development, as well as to investigate the
mediating effect of sustainable development. The cross-sectional survey design also proves to be time
efficient, economical and suitable for gathering data from a large number of respondents.
3.4 Conceptual Framework
The conceptual framework of the study is that AI-driven Supply Chain Management as an independent
variable will mediate the relationship between financial performance and sustainable development as
dependent variables. The framework suggests that AI-based SCM directly impacts the financial performance
and indirectly impacts the financial performance via sustainable development practices.
3.5 Hypotheses Formulation
The result of the literature review and conceptual framework leads to the formulation of four
hypotheses:
H1: The use of AI in SCM has an important positive impact on financial performance.
H2: Sustainable development is positively affected by AI in the context of SCM.
H3: There is a strong positive relationship between sustainable development and financial
performance.
H4: Sustainable development plays a mediating role between AI-driven SCM and financial
performance.

field of AI-supported SCM and SD initiatives. This type of sampling is appropriate since not everyone in the
organization understands AI technologies and supply chain systems. The study will include close to 200-400
respondents. Hair et al. (2019) stated that the number of samples in SEM analysis must be more than 200.
Selected sample size provides reliability, validity, and generalizability of the research findings.
3.8 Data Collection Method
This research uses primary data collection techniques in the form of a structured questionnaire survey.
The questionnaire is sent online via Google Forms, e-mail and professional networks. The use of online
surveys is inexpensive and allows the researcher to gather responses from people from various organizations
and geographical areas quickly.
3.9 Research Instrument (Structured Questionnaire)
The main tool used in the research is the structured questionnaire. This questionnaire is broken down
into four sections:
 Section A (Demographic data):  Includes gender, age/years, education level, work experience,
industry type, job position.
 Section B: AISCM practices and AI adoption of the supply chain activities are measured.
 Section C: Sustainable development practices in environmental, social, and economic sustainability
are measured.
 Section D:  Financial performance indicators like profit, revenue expansion, operational efficiency,
and cost reduction are measured.
Measurements are based on a 5-point Likert scale (1 = “Strongly Disagree” to 5 = “Strongly Agree”).
Variable Measurement
Measurement of variables is based on previous studies:
 AI-powered SCM:  Gauged through predictive analytics metrics, intelligent automation, inventory
optimization, demand forecasting, and real-time tracking metrics.
 Sustainable development:  Measured based on resource efficiency, reduction of waste, ethical
sourcing, compliance with the environment, and sustainable operational practices.
 Financial performance:  Measured using profitability, revenue growth, operational performance,
return on investment, and cost reduction metrics.
3.10 Pilot Study and Reliability Testing
Before the final data collection process, a pilot study is carried out with around 30 respondents. The
pilot test is used to detect ambiguities in the questions, errors in the measurements, and problems with the
reliability of the questions in the questionnaire. Cronbach's alpha is used to evaluate the reliability of the
instruments with an acceptable value of 0.70 as determined.
3.11 Data Analysis Techniques
Data collected in this study were analysed using SPSS and SmartPLS 4 software. Descriptive statistics
were employed to summarize the demographic characteristics of respondents and the distribution of study
variables. Reliability analysis was conducted using Cronbach’s Alpha and Composite Reliability (CR) to
assess the internal consistency of the measurement scales. Validity analysis was also performed through
Average Variance Extracted (AVE), convergent validity, and discriminant validity to ensure the accuracy of
the constructs. In addition, Structural Equation Modelling (SEM), specifically Partial Least Squares SEM
(PLS-SEM), was used to examine direct and indirect relationships as well as the mediating effects among the
study variables. This technique was considered appropriate because the study involved multiple constructs
and mediation analysis.

5. Discussion of Findings
The findings are consistent with the research from previous studies, which have widely investigated
the impact of AI technologies on improving supply chain performance and competitiveness of companies. The
results on the importance of the relationship between the artificial intelligence-based SCM and financial
performance are consistent with the Resource Based View (RBV) theory which emphasizes that technological
capabilities can be regarded as strategic resources for the organization that can create a competitive advantage.
In addition, the positive association between AI-driven SCM and sustainable development was supported by
the previous study in which the environment efficiency and operation sustainability of AI was explored. AI
systems help to optimize the use of energy, minimize waste, and enhance logistics, all of which contribute to
sustainable business practices. Moreover, the strong mediation effect of sustainable development shows that
the positive effect of AI-driven SCM on financial performance is enhanced by sustainability. Embracing
technology innovations and sustainability measures can help companies thrive and withstand shocks.
The findings are particularly relevant to the emerging economy companies as they are increasingly
facing pressures to improve their operational performance and to have sustainable practices in a limited
resource environment. The results and findings of the study were presented in this chapter. The analysis
showed that the use of AI in Supply Chain Management is beneficial for sustainable development and financial
results. Sustainable development, too, had a significant positive effect on financial performance and was a
mediator between the AI-driven SCM and financial outcomes. The findings confirm the importance of the
role of AI technologies and sustainable strategies on the success of organizations in the emerging economy
companies for the long-term.
