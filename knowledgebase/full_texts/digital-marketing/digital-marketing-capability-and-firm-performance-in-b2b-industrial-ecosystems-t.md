Global industrial ecosystems are undergoing digital transformation, yet business -
to-business (B2B) firms often report that technological investments do not
consistently improve financial or operational performance, a phenomenon termed
the "digitalization p aradox." Grounded in Dynamic Capability Theory (DCT), this
study investigates the mediation roles of market agility and customer relationship
quality in the relationship between digital marketing capability and firm
performance. A quantitative explanatory design was employed, with empirical data
collected from 165 managerial respondents in the Jababeka Industrial Estate,
Indonesia. The model was evaluated using Partial Least Squares Structural
Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM). The structural analysis indicates full parallel
mediation. The direct impact of digital marketing capability on firm performance is
non-significant (beta = 0.067, p = 0.304). Digital marketing capability shows
significant positive associations with both ma rket agility (beta = 0.503, p < 0.001)
and customer relationship quality (beta = 0.551, p < 0.001). The indirect effect
through customer relationship quality (beta = 0.286) is larger than the indirect
effect through market agility (beta = 0.155).  The results suggest that in the B2B
industrial context, digital marketing capability acts as an antecedent to higher -
order capabilities rather than a direct driver of firm performance. Limitations
include a cross-sectional design, a single-key-informant approach, and a single-site
context, which suggest that future research should adopt multi-source longitudinal
designs to further validate these associations.

1. INTRODUCTION
The dawn of the Fourth Industrial Revolution has recalibrated the competitive landscape of global
manufacturing and industrial ecosystems. In this era of pervasive connectivity, digital transformation
has become a core imperative for organizational survival. The integration of advanced digital marketing
capabilities encompassing data analytics, integrated platforms, and real-time market sensing promises
to change how firms identify opportunities and engage with business-to-business (B2B) clients (Shams
et al., 2024) . This shift is clearly observable in large -scale integrated industrial hubs like the Jababeka
Industrial Estate in Indonesia, which accommodates numerous multinational and local manufacturing
firms. The deployment of digital infrastructures in such estates underscores the ongoing transition from
traditional industrial operations to digitally integrated B2B ecosystems  (Bamberger et al., 2025;
Tzanidis et al., 2024).

However, as firms aggressively scale their digital investments, a troubling phenomenon known as
the "digitalization paradox" has emerged (Chen et al., 2024). Despite the widespread adoption of digital
marketing tools, many industrial organizations struggle to translate technological capacity into tangible
performance improvements. This inconsistency suggests that the relationship between digital
capabilities and firm performance is highly complex and non -linear. Technological determinism—the
assumption that technology alone drives performance —is increasingly challenged by empirical
evidence indicating that digital assets often remain dormant unless orchestrate d through higher-order
organizational capabilities. In the B2B industrial sector, characterized by complex supply chains and
long-term relational contracts, standard digital tools often fail to capture the nuance of deeply embedded
industrial interdependencies (Royo-Vela & Serrano, 2021; Tzanidis et al., 2024; Vangeli et al., 2023).
To unpack this paradox, this study anchors its conceptual model in the Dynamic Capability Theory
(DCT). DCT posits that superior performance in volatile environments relies on a firm's ability to
integrate, build, and reconfigure internal and external comp etences (Teece, Pisano, & Shuen, 1997;
Eisenhardt & Martin, 2000). These foundational principles suggest that competitive advantage is not a
static asset but a dynamic process of continuous organizational adaptation. In aligning DCT with our
four construct s, we conceptualize Digital Marketing Capability as an essential foundational or
operational capability. However, according to DCT , operational capabilities must be transformed into
dynamic capabilities to sustain competitive advantage. Consequently, we propose two parallel pathways
for this transformation: an adaptive pathway through Market Agility and a relational pathway through
Customer Relationship Quality (Dubey et al., 2024; Ravat et al., 2024).
Market Agility —the firm's capacity to sense and respond to market changes with speed and
flexibility—represents a classic dynamic capability. As digitalization increases the velocity of market
data, agile firms translate these digital signals into swift op erational maneuvers (Koh & Yuen, 2026) .
Simultaneously, Customer Relationship Quality, which encapsulates trust, commitment, and mutual
satisfaction, acts as a crucial relational capability. In complex B2B environments, the human element
remains vital; technology is most effective when deploye d to deepen the trust -based bonds between
business partners rather than simply automating transactions (Aldossary & Agag, 2026).
Despite the theoretical relevance of these constructs, notable gaps persist in the literature. First,
while digital marketing is extensively studied in consumer -facing (B2C) and retail sectors, the heavy
industrial and B2B manufacturing context remains sig nificantly under -represented. Second, most
existing studies examine market agility and relationship quality in isolation, lacking a simultaneous
parallel mediation model to compare their relative impacts. Third, empirical evidence from emerging
markets, specifically within specialized industrial zones, is scarce.
This study addresses these voids by proposing and testing a parallel mediation model that
investigates how digital marketing capability influences firm performance through the dual lenses of
agility and relationship quality. Theoretically, we extend DCT by  positioning digital marketing as a
foundational capability that requires parallel mediation mechanisms to impact firm performance.
Empirically, we provide robust analysis of B2B industrial dynamics in an emerging market, and
managerially, we offer a strategic roadmap emphasizing that industrial performance in the digital age
relies on both rapid adaptability and deep relational integration.
Based on the synthesis of the theory above, the conceptual model of this study is designed to test
the parallel mediation mechanism between digital marketing capabilities and company performance.

2. METHOD
2.1. Research Design and Context
This study adopts a quantitative, explanatory research design to empirically test the proposed
parallel mediation model within the Jababeka Industrial Estate in Indonesia. As one of the largest and
most comprehensive business -to-business (B2B) manufacturin g hubs in Southeast Asia, this context
provides an ideal setting to observe the transition from basic digitalization to advanced capability
orchestration among industrial firms.
2.2. Sampling Strategy and Data Collection
The target population comprises firms operating within the Jababeka Industrial Estate. The unit of
analysis is at the firm level. To ensure data reliability, a purposive sampling technique was employed to
target key informants. The inclusion criteria requi red respondents to hold managerial or executive
positions (e.g., Marketing Managers, Directors, or C -level executives) with a minimum of two years of
tenure, ensuring they possess comprehensive, strategic knowledge of their firm’s digital operations and
performance.
Data collection was conducted over a three -month period from January 2026  to April 2026 .
Questionnaires were distributed via gform to a total of 250 targeted firms. Out of the distributed
questionnaires, 182 were returned. After data cleaning for incomplete responses, a final sample of 165
valid responses was retained, yielding a valid response rate of 66% (165/250) and a completion rate of
90.7% (165/182) among returned questionnaires. This sample size comfortably exceeds the minimum
requirement of 77 respondents established a priori using G*Power software (effect size f2 = 0.15, alpha

Prior to data collection, an ethical protocol was established. All respondents provided informed
consent, participation was strictly voluntary, and participants were assured that their data would remain
anonymous, confidential, and used solely for academic  research purposes. Furthermore, because the
original measurement scales were adopted from English literature, a rigorous translation and back -
translation procedure was conducted. The items were translated into Indonesian by bilingual academic
experts and back-translated into English to guarantee conceptual equivalence and semantic accuracy in
the B2B industrial context.
2.3. Measurement and Instrument Development
The survey instrument was developed using established measurement scales adapted from the
following seminal studies:
1. Digital Marketing Capability (DMC): Adapted from Trainor et al. (2014), focusing on the firm's
utilization of digital platforms and data analytics.
2. Market Agility (MA): Adapted from Roberts and Grover (2012), capturing the firm's speed and
flexibility in responding to market signals.
3. Customer Relationship Quality (CRQ): Adapted from Roberts et al. (2003), evaluating
dimensions of trust, commitment, and mutual satisfaction.
4. Firm Performance (FP): Adapted from Morgan et al. (2009), capturing both financial and non -
financial performance metrics relative to major competitors.
All constructs were evaluated using a 5 -point Likert scale, ranging from 1 ("Strongly Disagree") to
5 ("Strongly Agree"). The full list of items is provided in Appendix A . Prior to the main deployment, a
pilot test was conducted with 15 industrial managers. Based on their feedback, minor modifications
were made to the phrasing of specific items to ensure contextual clarity and relevance to the Indonesian
B2B manufacturing lexicon.
2.4. Data Analysis Strategy
The data were analyzed using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM) via
SmartPLS software. PLS-SEM was selected because of its robustness in estimating complex models with
multiple parallel mediators and its suitability for predictive and explanatory research aiming to extend
existing theories. Following current methodological guidelines, the analysis was executed in two steps:
evaluating the measurement model (validity and reliability) and assessing the structural model using a
5,000-subsample bootstrapping procedure for significance testing of both direct and specific indirect
effects.

3. RESUL TS
The measurement model evaluation serves as a prerequisite to confirm that the survey instrument
accurately and consistently measures the intended latent constructs: Digital Marketing Capability
(DMC), Market Agility (MA), Customer Relationship Quality (CRQ ), and Firm Performance (FP). This
stage involves examining internal consistency reliability, convergent validity, and discriminant validity.
3.1 Internal Consistency Reliability and Convergent Validity
Internal consistency reliability reflects the degree to which items measuring the same construct are
correlated. As presented in Table 1, the reliability was thoroughly assessed using three distinct metrics:
Cronbach's Alpha, rho_A, and Composite Reliabili ty (CR). The results demonstrate exceptional internal
consistency across all constructs. Market Agility exhibited the highest reliability (Cronbach's Alpha =
0.931; CR = 0.944), followed closely by Firm Performance (Cronbach's Alpha = 0.912; CR = 0.930),
Customer Relationship Quality (Cronbach's Alpha = 0.908; CR = 0.929), and Digital Marketing Capability
(Cronbach's Alpha = 0.838; CR = 0.885). All values substantially exceed the recommended threshold of
0.70, confirming that the measurement scales are highly reliable within the B2B context.
Convergent validity, which assesses the extent to which a measure correlates positively with
alternative measures of the same construct, was evaluated through outer loadings and the Average
Variance Extracted (AVE). According to Hair et al. (2022), outer loadings should ideally exceed 0.70, and
the AVE should be 0.50 or higher . Table 1 confirms that all individual item loadings range from 0.713 to
0.891, indicating that each indicator retains sufficient variance extracted from its respective latent
construct. During the initial item purification process, several indicators (DMC1, DMC4, MA1, MA2, MA5,
CRQ6, and FP7) were removed due to low outer loadings (< 0.70) or severe cross-loadings that violated
discriminant validity. The remaining items, as presented in T able 1, demonstrate robust validity and
were retained for the final structural model.. Furthermore, the AVE values for DMC (0.607), MA (0.708),
CRQ (0.686), and FP (0.656) successfully surpass the 0.50 benchmark. This signifies that, on average,
the constructs explain more than 60% of the variance of their corresponding items, thereby establishing
robust convergent validity.

To rigorously establish discriminant validity in accordance with modern PLS-SEM standards (Hair
et al., 2022), the Heterotrait -Monotrait (HTMT) ratio of correlations was evaluated. Following an item

observed between Market Agility and Customer Relationship Quality (0.897), followed by the
relationship between Firm Performance and Customer Relationship Quality (0.873). Because all values
are below 0.90, empirical discriminant validity is adequately supported, though the relationship
between Market Agility and Customer Relationship Quality (0.897) requires cautious interpretation.
This empirical closeness is theoretically expected in heavy B2B ecosystems, where a firm's market agility
is inherently intertwined with its relational ties; rapid market responses are often executed through
close collaboration with key clients. Nonetheless, they remain conceptually distinct: agility captures the
speed of organizational adaptation, whereas relationship quality captures the depth of interpersonal
trust.

statistically non-significant (beta = 0.067, T = 1.030, p = 0.304). This indicates that simply possessing
digital marketing tools and platforms does not inhe rently translate into better financial or operational
performance for B2B industrial firms.

3.5 Mediation Analysis (Specific Indirect Effects)
To address the core objective of the Beyond Digitalization framework, specific indirect effects were
tested to evaluate the parallel mediation roles of Market Agility and Customer Relationship Quality. The
results, detailed in Table 5, reveal significant mediation pathways. Market Agility significantly mediates
the relationship between Digital Marketing Capability and Firm Performance (beta = 0.155, T = 2.845, p
= 0.004), supporting Hypothesis 4. More profoundly, Customer Relationship Quality also acts as a robust
and highly significant mediator (beta = 0.286, T = 4.672, p < 0.001), providing strong support for
Hypothesis 5.
Because both specific indirect effects are significant while the direct effect (H1) is not, the structural
model exhibits a full parallel mediation. This is a pivotal finding: the strategic value of digital marketing
investments is statistically non -significant if not channeled  through agility and relationship building.
Furthermore, the magnitude of the indirect effect via Customer Relationship Quality (0.286) is nearly
double the indirect effect via Market Agility (0.155). This highlights that in heavy industrial settings,
leveraging digital capabilities to build client trust and commitment yields a far greater performance
payoff than merely using digital tools for operational speed.

4. DISCUSSION
The empirical findings of this study offer a meaningful reevaluation of how digital transformation
functions within B2B industrial ecosystems. By integrating dynamic capability theory with relational
perspectives, this research moves beyond the binary logic of technological adoption to uncover the
sophisticated orchestration required for superior firm performance.
4.1 The Digital-Relational Paradox: Why H1 Failed
The rejection of Hypothesis 1  the non -significant direct relationship between Digital Marketing
Capability and Firm Performance  is arguably the most critical insight of this study. In contemporary
literature, particularly studies focused on B2C or e-commerce sectors, digital capabilities are frequently
lauded as direct catalysts for financial success. However, in the context of the Jababeka industrial estate,
where transactions involve complex supply chains, high -volume B2B contracts, and prolonged
negotiation cycles, digital tools alone are commoditized.
When every factory or service provider has access to similar digital platforms, analytics, and CRM
software, the technology ceases to be a unique competitive advantage. It becomes a baseline
requirement. The rejection of H1 effectively challenges the techno -centric assumption , proving that
hardware and software investments do not automatically generate revenue. This finding corroborates
recent critical perspectives in information systems research which argue that digitalization without
strategic alignment often results in the productivity paradox, where heavy IT investments yield stagnant
organizational performance (Jing et al., 2023; Kwarteng-Amaniampong & Tait, 2026; Latifi et al., 2021 ).
4.2 Capability Orchestration: The Full Mediation Mechanism
If digitalization itself does not drive performance, how do industrial firms benefit from it? The
acceptance of H4 and H5 provides the answer, confirming a full mediation model. B2B firms must
orchestrate their digital marketing capabilities to develop hig her-order dynamic and relational
capabilities. First, the validation of H2 and H4 demonstrates that digital marketing capability acts as a
sensory system for the organization. Through data analytics, digital B2B platforms, and real-time market
tracking, heavy industrial firms  which are traditionally rigid  can achieve Market Agility. They can
anticipate supply chain disruptions, pivot their manufacturing focus based on raw material availability,
and respond to sudden client demands faster than their non -digitalized competitors. This agility
subsequently translates into improved performance (Shams et al., 2024).
Second, and most importantly, the validation of H3 and H5 highlights the supremacy of the relational
dimension. The data reveals that the indirect effect of digitalization through Customer Relationship
Quality (0.286) is stronger than its effect through Ma rket Agility (0.155). This exposes the highly
relational nature of B2B industrial marketing : it is a relationship game heavily augmented by data.
Digital marketing capabilities allow firms to provide personalized B2B service, transparent
communication, and integrated problem-solving platforms (Sackey et al., 2026). By utilizing digital tools
to foster trust, commitment, and mutual dependency with client firms, industrial players achieve high
client retention and secure long-term contracts, which are the critical drivers of firm performance in a
B2B setting.

4.4 Managerial Implications
For B2B executives, marketing directors, and policymakers in industrial estates such as Jababeka,
these findings offer actionable strategic directives.  Stop the Hardware -Only Investment Strategy:
Managers must recognize that allocating budgets exclusively to buying the latest digital marketing
software will not yield a return on investment (ROI). Technology must be viewed merely as an enabler,
not a solut ion. Invest in Relational Digitalization: B2B firms should pivot their digital strategies from
broadcasting (e.g., generic social media or mass emailing) to relational integration. This means using
digital platforms to integrate deeply with the client's supply chain, offering transparent data sharing,
and creating dedicated digital communication channels that foster trust and long-term partnership.
Cultivate an Agile Culture: Since Market Agility is a necessary mediator, management must
restructure organizational silos. The data gathered by the digital marketing team must be instantly
accessible to the production and logistics departments to allow th e entire firm to pivot swiftly in
response to market signals.
5. CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS
5.1. Conclusions
This study set out to demystify the digitalization paradox within the B2B industrial ecosystem by
empirically testing a parallel mediation model grounded in Dynamic Capability Theory. The overarching
objective was to understand how digital marketing capabi lities orchestrate firm performance through
the dual mechanisms of market agility and customer relationship quality in a highly competitive
manufacturing hub.  The empirical findings yield a significant conclusion: in complex industrial
environments, technological determinism is insufficient. The study provides empirical support for an
indirect-only (full) parallel mediation mechanism, as demonstrated by the non -significant direct
relationship between digital marketing capability and firm performance. This indicates that the mere
adoption and possession of digital marketing platforms do not inherently guarantee superior financial
or operational outcomes. Instead, digital capability functions strictly as a foundational enabler. Its
strategic value appears to be unlocked primarily when it is transformed into higher-order dynamic and
relational capabilities.
Crucially, while both market agility and customer relationship quality significantly mediate this
relationship, the relational pathway proved to be substantially stronger. This highlights a important
strategic consideration  in B2B marketing: in a landscape where digitalization is increasingly
commoditized, the ultimate differentiator is not merely the speed of operational maneuvers, but the
depth, trust, and commitment forged through tech -enabled human relationships. Ultimat ely, to move

5.3. Limitations and Future Research
While this study offers robust theoretical and practical contributions, it is subject to several
limitations that present exciting avenues for future research. First, the cross-sectional nature of the data
restricts the ability to establish absolute causal inferences over time. Future scholars are encouraged to
employ longitudinal research designs to capture the evolutionary dynamics of digital capability
orchestration. Second, this study relies on a single key informant per firm to provide assessments of
organizational-level constructs, including Digital Marketing Capability, Market Agility, Customer
Relationship Quality, and Firm Performance. While this approach is comm on in strategic B2B
management research to obtain high -level strategic insights, it may introduce a degree of subjectivity.
Consequently, the reliance on a single respondent may limit the breadth of organizational -level
inference; future studies are advise d to employ multi -source data collection, such as surveying both
managers and operational staff, to triangulate perspectives and enhance the robustness of the findings.
Third, the empirical context was limited to the Jababeka Industrial Estate in Indonesia.
