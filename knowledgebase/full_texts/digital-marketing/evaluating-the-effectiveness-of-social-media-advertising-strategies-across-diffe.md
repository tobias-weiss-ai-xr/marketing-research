A B S T R A C T
This paper aims to evaluate the effects of demographic targeting, platform selection, and engagement
metrics on the effectiveness of social media advertising campaigns. In this paper, it is also aimed to
provide a comprehensive understanding of how these factors influence conversion outcomes. We
employed a hybrid analytics method combining ANOVA, correlation, and regression analysis to
examine patterns of user engagement and ad performance. A dataset of 200,235 ad campaign records
conducted in the United States was analyzed to assess variations across gender, age groups, and
platforms. The main findings of the study demonstrate that (i) women consistently exhibited higher
conversion rates compared to men, highlighting the potential of gender-specific content strategies; (ii)
users aged 35 –44 achieved the highest engagement, suggesting that middle -aged audiences may be
more responsive to targeted campaigns; and finally, (iii) among platforms, Instagram led in conversion
performance, while Facebook showed comparable engagement rates, and both significantly
outperformed other platforms in terms of overall impact. Click -through rate was found to be
moderately correlated with conversions , whereas impressions showed weak returns, indicating
diminishing value at higher exposure levels. These results underscore the importance of optimizing
both message and delivery channel to specific audience segments.This study contributes to digital
marketing literature by integrating demographic variables, engagement indicators, and platform
performance into a unified model. The insights derived offer practical implications for businesses and
advertisers aiming to enhance return on investment and optimize campaign strategies in a fast-paced,
digitally driven advertising landscape.

Introduction
Social media has become an indispensable element of modern advertising, enabling brands to engage with targeted audiences in ways
that were unimaginable just a decade ago. As platforms like Facebook, Instagram, and TikTok reshape the marketing landscape,
advertisers now have access to an abundance of tools for micro-targeting and real-time feedback. However, this evolution introduces
complexity: the effectiveness of advertising campaigns on social media hinges not only on the platform and its features but also on
the demographic characteristics of the audience and the campaign’s ability to foster engagement. In a time where billions are  spent
on digital advertising each year, understanding the mechanics behind successful campaigns has never been more urgent.
Despite the widespread use of social media as a marketing channel, scholarly understanding of how demographic variables, such  as
age and gender, interact with platform -specific attributes to influence conversion outcomes remains limited (Dwivedi et al., 20 21;
Choudhary et al., 2024; Hudders & De Jans, 2022). While younger users are often presumed to be more responsive to digital content,
recent studies suggest more nuanced behaviors across generations (Roberts & David, 2019; Zoghi et al., 2024). Similarly, the
relationship between common engagement metrics (e.g., impressions, clicks, likes) and actual ROI or conversions is often overstated,
with some scholars warning against overreliance on superficial indicators (Hollebeek & Macky, 2019; Hirsch, 2014). Furt hermore,
research tends to isolate platform analysis rather than exploring cross -platform performance differences in a unified empirical

Literature Review
The purpose of this literature review is to explore how social media advertising effectiveness varies across platforms and demographic
groups, with a particular focus on engagement metrics and conversion outcomes. As advertising continues to shift toward digital
spaces, understanding platform-specific dynamics and audience behaviors becomes critical for optimizing marketing strategies. This
review synthesizes recent empirical studies and theoretical insights to identify patterns, contradictions, and gaps in current research.
A comparative and integrative approach was adopted, emphasizing cross -platform differences, generational and gender -based
audience responses, and the impact of various advertisement formats and features. By highlighting both established findings and
areas in need of further exploration, such as the effects of newer platforms like TikTok and the tension between personalization and
privacy, this review lays the foundation for a more data-driven and targeted advertising strategy in future research and practice.
Platform differences in social media advertising effectiveness
Studies consistently indicate that the impact of an advertisement is quite different from one social media platform to another. This is
partially explained by the variation in user base, nature/features of platform, and trust of users. The acceptance of ad s seems to be
determined by user trust and reliability with a platform. For example, Nuji et al. (2023) discovered that advertisements on h ighly
credible, professionally focused websites (e.g., LinkedIn) are likely to be viewed as more credible than advert isements on less
moderated sites. Credibility, in turn, has been connected with more engaging consumer experience and greater purchasing inten t.
This implies that a website's credibility and content moderation approach can influence the persuasiveness of i ts advertising.
Marketers can thus anticipate better performance on sites that users inherently trust for content quality. User interaction a ctivities
also differ from site to site, affecting ad performance.
Platform design and advertisement form also contribute to efficacy. For instance, Rosli et al. (2024) found that Instagram's "like" and
"share" features were more strongly associated with purchase intention than those on Facebook, suggesting that visual-first platforms
may foster more action-oriented engagement. Their study underscores the importance of not only measuring engagement volume but
also understanding the type of engagement elicited by specific platform features. Shen (2023) discovered that brand engagement can
be enhanced through the selection of a suitable platform strategy, rather than adopting several content strategies for each p latform,
which corroborates the evidence that amidst the rapid evolution of social media, its sheer variety makes i t hard for brands and
marketers to concentrate on all the platforms (Gawer, 2022). In Pelet and Ettis's (2022) research on Facebook advertisements, it has
been discovered that some attributes of social media advertisements: creativity, entertainment ("liki ng"), and credibility exert a
positive impact on customers' attitudes towards the advertisement and thereby induce purchase intention.
Intrusive or irritating formats, on the other hand, can harm ad effectiveness. Comparative studies across media have been
comparatively rare, but recent research is closing this gap. Yang et al. (2024) provided empirical support for the ambitious
engagement-to-conversion promise of TikTok by illustrating the effect of influencer video features on user engagement. The findings
indicated that entertainment and informativeness were significant predictors of behavioral intention.
Demographic targeting and audience differences
The general consensus through literature is that various demographic segments use social media and advertising differently and thus
need to be addressed through different strategies (Dwivedi et al., 2021). One core factor is age or generation groups. It ha s been
discovered through previous research that younger users (Gen Z and Millenials) are digital natives and use social platforms
excessively, while older groups (such as Gen X and Boomers) access them less and for other reasons. These patterns of use translate
to several different advertising outcomes. Interestingly, there have been studies questioning traditional assumptions about w hat age
groups are most susceptible to social advertisements. Choudhary et al. (2024) conducted a systematic review of gener ational
differences in technology-related behavior, which includes insights into how various age groups engage with social media and digital
advertising. Their findings suggest that while both younger and older users are exposed to social media ads, younge r generations,

particularly digital natives like Gen Z, tend to exhibit higher engagement and purchasing behaviors. This challenges assumptions that
older individuals are more susceptible due to lower digital literacy. In contrast, the review indicates that frequent soci al media

Gendered differences in response to social media advertising are another key sub-theme.
Recent research points to nuanced differences in how males and females interact with and trust ads or influencers online.  Hu dders
and De Jans (2022) described that  females are more drawn to visual -intensive, entertainment-based advertising material (common
on platforms like Instagram) than men, leading to higher interaction with these kinds of advertisements. Particularly, women on
Instagram were most interested in advertisements that are vivid and story -like compared to men (Hudders & De Jans, 2022).
Additionally, Khan (2020) examined social media influencer marketing and found a gender moderator role: the female consumer and
male consumer differed in the conversion of influencer endorsements to purchase intention. Female respondents were more
influenced by social media influencers (specifically authentic micro -influencers) in terms of purchase intent, whereas male
respondents were less so. This aligns with the view that women are more likely to trust peer recommendations and engaging soc ial
content, in line with findings that women value authenticity and two-way dialogue with brands on social media. Men, however, might
be more likely to react to other appeals – e.g., there is evidence that men are more motivated by functional and information -driven
content in advertising, with women being more reactive to experiential and emotional appeals (Peña-García et al., 2018).
Engagement metrics
Social media campaigns are often evaluated on likes, shares, comments, click-through rates, etc., but the relationship between these
metrics for engagement and actual conversion or sales is not fully clear.
Some reports only provide weak or indirect correlation, e.g., Hollebeek and Macky (2019) argue that high online engagement ma y
not necessarily translate into a high growth in sales or loyalty because there are too many intervening variables.
The experimental research of Hirsch (2014) highlighted this gap too: they did not only quantify online behavior, but also off line
actions of behavior (e.g. donations) and discovered some calls to action could cause clicks without contributing to actual meaningful
activities.
Contradictions and research gaps
In terms of gaps, academic research has only begun to keep pace with the rapid evolution of social media platforms.
Firstly, cross-platform comparative studies are scarce. Many studies focus on one platform at a time (often Facebook or Instagram),
leaving a gap in understanding how the same advertisement or strategy performs across different platforms under controlled
conditions. There is a need for studies that examine platform -specific advertising effectiveness, considering demographics and
engagement behavior, as well as the impact of newer formats and algorithms.
One contradiction lies in generational responses: earlier assumptions held that the digitally native Gen Z generation would b e
desensitized and difficult to persuade, yet recent evidence shows Gen Z and Millennials are in fact more likely to trust and act on
social media ads than older adults.
Second, emerging platforms like TikTok (short -form video) or features like Stories and Reels on Facebook introduce new creative
possibilities, but their long-term marketing effectiveness is not yet well documented in scholarly literature (most data on these come
from industry reports).
Another area of mixed findings is personalization and privacy: personalized targeting is attempting to improve ad relevance, but
some studies (e.g. Balaban et al., 2022) note that overly personalized ads can trigger privacy concerns and backfire for certain users.
The balance between effective targeting and consumer comfort remains an open question, especially as regulations and user
awareness of data privacy grow.
Addressing these gaps will be crucial for a holistic understanding of social media advertising effectiveness.  This literatur e further
justifies the relevance of the topic, by stressing the fact that social media advertising strategies should be multifaceted, in order to be
more efficient. This study seeks to close the gaps in terms of cross-platform comparative studies and provide useful recommendations
to advertisers by looking at demographics, platforms and conversion rates, all within one analysis. Thus the results will enhance the
effectiveness of the multi -channel marketing strategy, by improving quantitative characteristics of advertising campaigns aimed at
different audience markets.

Research question / Hypothesis
The research paper was based on the following research questions paired with the hypotheses designed to explore what are the factors
influencing the performance of social media advertising campaigns. They draw on existing literature and have an aim to examine the
relationships between demographic factors, platform characteristics, enga gement metrics, and conversion rates, which will offer
further insights into how these variables have an impact on the success of targeted campaigns.
How do demographic factors (age, gender) influence the performance of social media advertising campaigns in terms of engagement,
conversion rates, and ROI?
This question examines the role that age and gender have in the performance of media advertising campaigns in terms of metrics that
could point to successful campaigns.
How do social media platforms (Facebook, Instagram, X) differ in their ability to drive conversions and engagement for target ed
demographics?
This question explores how effective are different social media platforms in reaching specific demographic groups and driving desired
outcomes.
What is the relationship between engagement metrics (impressions, clicks, engagement score) and conversion rates across different
platforms?
This question investigates the correlation between key engagement metrics and actual conversion outcomes, a focus being put o n
understanding how engagement drives conversions on different platforms.
H1.1: Campaigns targeting younger demographics (18 –24 years) will have higher engagement scores than campaigns targeting
older demographics.
Previous research suggests that younger audiences are more active on social media platforms, engaging more with content, especially
on platforms like Instagram and TikTok, which cater to their preferences for visual and interactive content.
H1.2.: Campaigns targeting female audiences will have higher conversion rates compared to those targeting male audiences.
Gender differences in content preference and response have been documented, with women often engaging more with visually
appealing ads and social media content, leading to higher conversion rates for campaigns targeting them.
H2.1: Engagement rates will be significantly higher on Instagram compared to Facebook.
Instagram’s visual-centric platform is more suited to campaigns that emphasize aesthetics and imagery, leading to higher engagement
rates for campaigns focused on visual content, especially among younger audiences.
H2.2: Conversion rates will be higher on Facebook than on Instagram for campaigns targeting older demographics.
Facebook’s broad and diverse user base, which includes a significant proportion of older adults, may lead to higher conversion rates
for campaigns targeting this demographic because Facebook has existed for a longer time, and has accumulated a large number  of
users that fit in the older demographic, while not drastically changing their platform and functions.
H3.1: Higher click-through rates (CTR) will be positively correlated with higher conversion rates.
Studies suggest that a higher CTR indicates a strong initial interest in the ad, which is often a precursor to conversions. W ith this
said, ads with a higher CTR are expected to have higher conversion rates, as they indicate that the audience is more likel y to act on
the ad’s call-to-action.
H3.2: Campaigns with higher impression counts will lead to diminishing returns on conversion rates beyond a certain threshold.
While increasing impressions can raise awareness and drive engagement, research has shown that beyond a certain point, additional
impressions may lead to oversaturation, reducing their effectiveness in converting users into customers. This reflects the principle of
diminishing returns, where the cost of additional impressions outweighs their benefit in terms of conversion.
Research and Methodology
The methodology of this study is based on the analysis of an authentic, pre-existing data set from social media advertising campaigns
to ensure real-world applicability and validity. The research uses a quantitative approach, using statistical methods to test the proposed
hypotheses and explore the relationships between key variables. It uses a correlational research design that has an aim to explore the
relationships between platform features, demographic factors, engagement factors and conversion rates. The sample consists fr om
data of advertising campaigns from various industries that target different demographic groups.

Even though the data set contained an array of data, only several variables, the ones that were deemed to be useful to provid e
comprehensive research, were used in the analysis. The data used was from the United States of America.
The independent variables are the demographic factors, campaign goals and platform, while the dependent variables are engagement
metrics, conversion data and customer acquisition cost.
To test the hypotheses, an array of statistical methods were used, conducted through the statistical software SPSS:
Descriptive analysis summarized the key features of the data, including averages, standard deviations, and frequency distribu tions,
providing an overview of the sample and the distribution of the key variables.
Pearson Correlation was used to examine the relationships between engagement metrics and conversion rates, helping determine
whether higher engagement correlates with higher conversions across different platforms.
T-tests were used to compare the engagement and conversion rates between gender groups and demographic age groups.
ANOVA was applied to compare engagement and conversion rates across multiple platform categories and campaign goals.
Multiple linear regression analysis was employed to explore the relationship between engagement metrics and conversion rates,
controlling for demographic variables and platform characteristics. This allowed for a deeper understanding of how engagement
metrics predict conversion outcomes across different platforms and demographic segments.

Discussion
This study sought to investigate the influence of demographic characteristics, platform selection, as well as engagement metr ics on
the effectiveness of social media advertising campaigns. This study provided empirical findings that demonstrate that both
demographic and platform -level attributes significantly affect key performance indicators of interest, i.e., engagement score,
conversion rate, and ROI.
Demographics and Engagement/Conversion Outcomes
Our first hypothesis (H1.1) anticipated that younger individuals would have greater engagement scores. Surprisingly, the resu lts
provided only partial support for this expectation. Although engagement scores were consistently higher across younger age groups,
the highest mean engagement was recorded in the 35–44 age group, not the youngest age group (18–24). This challenges a common
assumption in digital marketing that younger users (typically Gen Z) are inherently more engaged with social media advertisin g. A
reason could be content relevance and a difference between purchasing power and usage of the platforms between generations: users
between the age group of 35 –44 might find the campaign content more pertinent to their interests or purchasing power and h ence
interact more, while the Gen Z generation, even though being more technologically skilled, typically use social media platforms for
entertainment purposes. (Patil & Pandey, 2022).
In terms of gender-based performance (H1.2), the findings validated the hypothesis that ad campaigns targeting women were more
likely to enjoy better conversion rates than those targeting men. This aligns with existing literature that suggests women ar e more
positively receptive to personalized advertising, particularly those with emotional or aesthetic appeals. (Yousef, Dietrich, & Rundle-
Thiele, 2021) This result can be particularly useful for marketers when designing gender-based campaigns.
Platform Performance
The second research question tested differences in platform performance. Contrary to hypothesis H2.1, Instagram did not yield
significantly higher engagement rates than Facebook. Rather, the two platforms performed similarly in engaging their audience s,
which might imply that content quality and format are more significant than platform choice. Pinterest performed significantly lower
in engagement, even though the platform having potential to inspire and influence purchase intentions, marketers need to leve rage
features such as design and user-platform fit to successfully run marketing campaigns. (Hazari, Sethna, & O'Meara Brown, 2017)
Even more surprising were the results relating to H2.2. The supposition that Facebook would yield higher conversion rates for older
demographic segments was not fulfilled. Not only did Instagram have higher performing average conversion rates than Facebook ,
but the group means analysis showed that younger demographic segments (18 –24) had the highest conversion rates overall. This
directly challenges the general presumption that older age groups, typically regarded as being more financially stable and thus more
likely to convert, are always the better target. On the contrary, it suggests that younger audiences are more susceptible to highly
optimized online campaigns, possibly due to their familiarity with and acceptance of online purchasing behavior. (Patil & Pandey,
2022).
Engagement Metrics and Conversion
Regarding the third research question, a moderate positive relationship between click-through rates (CTR) and conversion rates was
found (H3.1). This result affirms the assumption that high CTRs are good predictors of ad effectiveness. However, hypothesis H3.2,
proclaiming diminishing returns in conversion rates with bigger impressions, was supported, but weakly. Although the quadrati c
model was statistically significant, the effect size was small and does not appear large enough to be of practical conclusion. Therefore,
although oversaturation can be troubling in theory, (Lambrecht & Tucker, 2013) its actual influence in this data set is minor.
Overall, the findings indicate that while some of the conventional digital marketing assumptions are still holding true, such  as the
importance of gender targeting and CTR, others could need a refreshed approach, since the digital marketing scope is evolvi ng day
by day.
Conclusion
This research set out to explore how demographic characteristics, platform choice, and engagement metrics affect the success of
social media advertising campaigns. By analyzing real-world campaign data, the study aimed to uncover which factors drive higher
engagement and conversion rates, and ultimately improve return on investment (ROI).  It adds to the growing body of evidence that
successful digital marketing depends on more than just choosing a popular platform or targeting a broad audience. Instead, the results

suggest that understanding the nuances of how different audience segments respond to different platforms and which metrics matter
most, is key to designing more effective and efficient advertising strategies. Including a larger cross-platform
As features of certain platforms become more popular, such as short-form video content, platforms are tending to merge their features
to accommodate the demand for such popular forms, which is slowly blurring the differences between social media platforms,  but

Recommendations for further research include examining the role of content format and type, distinguishing between targeted a nd
non-targeted campaigns, incorporating influencer involvement into data analysis, and expanding data collection to include TikTok
alongside other major social media platforms. Given TikTok’s rapid growth and the widespread adoption of its short -form video
features by competing platforms, its inclusion is now essential for a comprehensive understanding of current advertising dyna mics,
especially taking into consideration their advanced algorithm.
Although the study has some limitations, particularly in terms of not accounting for ad content or message framing, as well a s not
accounting for using different features of social media platforms and not including the fastest emerging social media platform TikTok,
it offers useful insights for marketers looking to improve their campaign outcomes in a data-driven way.
The uniqueness of this paper stems from its multi-dimensional comparative approach, examining how different demographic groups
and social media platforms influence advertising outcomes. It provides a deeper understanding of their role in shaping consum er
purchase intent. This dual focus not only contributes to academic discussions on digital marketing effectiveness but also deli vers
practical insights for marketers seeking to optimize campaign strategies across diverse audiences and platforms.
Limitations
One key limitation of this study lies in the absence of data on advertising content format (e.g., text, image, or video), which restricted
the analysis of how different content types may influence engagement and conversion across age groups and platforms. Furthermore,
although gender-based performance was examined, the dataset did not specify whether campaigns were deliberately targeted at men
or women. This introduces a degree of ambiguity when interpreting the observed differences in conversion rates between genders.
Platform-specific variables such as ad placement, algorithm-driven visibility, and user browsing intent, were also not captured in the
dataset. These factors could independently influence engagement metrics, regardless of demographic characteristics. Another notable
limitation is the lack of information regarding influencer involvement, despite their increasing impact on consumer behavior and
campaign outcomes. Moreover, the exclusion of TikTok and other rapidly growing platforms represents a significant gap, given their
expanding influence in the digital advertising space.
While these limitations do not compromise the overall validity of the findings, they underscore important dimensions that should be
addressed in future research to further refine our understanding of effective digital advertising strategies.
Suggestions for Future Research
To address current gaps in the literature, future research should include analysis of different ad formats, such as text, ima ge, and
video, to better understand how content type, platform features, and audience demographics interact to influence engagement and
conversion. Studies should also distinguish between targeted and non -targeted campaigns, especially by age and gender, to more
accurately assess the impact of demographic-specific strategies.
Further research is needed on the role of influencers, particularly micro-influencers, in shaping consumer behavior across platforms.
Additionally, platform-specific factors like ad placement, algorithm reach, and user intent should be considered for a ful ler picture
of campaign performance. As TikTok continues to grow and shape digital marketing through short-form video, it should be included
in future studies to ensure relevance. These recommendations aim to enhance both academic insight and marketing effectiveness.
