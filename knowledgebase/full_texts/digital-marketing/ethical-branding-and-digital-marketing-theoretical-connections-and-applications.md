Ethical Branding and Digital Marketing:

Abstract The´ ethical´ economy´ framework´ values´ customer´ co -production,´
promoting´ an´ inclusive,´ participatory,´ and´ sustainable´ business´ model.´ It´ emphasizes´
transparency,´ authenticity,´ and´ shared´ responsibility,´ engaging´ customers´ as´ partners´
in´ shaping´ ethical´ practices.´ This´ paper´ explores´ the´ role´ of´ User´ Experience´ (UX)´
design,´ servicescapes,´ inﬂuencer´ marketing,´ and´ social´ media´ in´ promoting´ ethical´
branding.´ Using´ Rural´ Radicals,´ a´ cultural´ startup,´ as´ a´ case´ study,´ it´ highlights´
gratitude’s´ role´ in´ cause-related´ marketing´ and´ its´ impact´ on´ consumer´ behavior.´
The´ discussion´ centers´ on´ authenticity,´ ethical´ tourism,´ Community-Based´ Social´
Marketing´ (CBSM)´ in´ digital´ marketing,´ offering´ strategies´ for´ meaningful´ connec-
tions,´ value´ co-creation,´ and´ ethical´ collaboration.´ Practical´ recommendations´ for´
enhancing´ ethical´ branding´ and´ fostering´ emotional´ connections´ with´ audiences´ are´
provided.´

1 Introduction
Ethical´ branding´ comes´ from´ the´ general´ context´ of´ corporate´ social´ responsibility´
(CSR),´ which´ began´ gaining´ prominence´ in´ the´ late´ twentieth´ century.´ CSR´ under-
scored´ the´ need´ for´ businesses´ to´ engage´ themselves´ in´ the´ ways´ and´ manners´ in´
which´ they´ could´ bring´ beneﬁt´ to´ society´ and´ the´ environment.´ The´ principles´ of´
CSR´ morphed´ into´ a´ more´ sophisticated´ practice—ethical´ branding—alongside´ the´
development´ of´ digital´ technologies.´ Ethical´ branding´ is´ not´ only´ about´ meeting´ or´
exceeding´ the´ legal´ and´ regulatory´ provisions´ but´ the´ actual´ value´ coming´ from´ the´
brands´ being´ honest´ and´ integral´ while´ ensuring´ a´ promising´ future´ for´ society.´ In´
contemporary´ times,´ information´ and´ communication´ avenues,´ such´ as´ social´ media´
spaces,´ search´ engines,´ and´ other´ digital´ commercial´ promotions,´ are´ considered´ to´
have´ been´ prominently´ used´ in´ current´ brand´ promotional´ channels.´ Recent´ Statis-
tics´ show´ that´ over´ 50%´ of´ consumers´ now´ discover´ brands´ through´ social´ media´
platforms´ rather´ than´ traditional´ search´ engines´ (https://sproutsocial.com/insights/´
social-media-statistics/).´
This´ paper´ approaches´ as´ a´ case´ study´ a´ new´ cultural´ venture´ that´ commits´ to´
solutions´ in´ rural´ development.´ The´ case´ study´ is´ a´ startup´ initiative,´ Rural´ Radicals´
(ruralradicals.com)´ that´ works´ on´ transforming´ empty´ buildings´ all´ over´ non-urban´
Europe´ into´ alive´ and´ meaningful´ spaces´ that´ contribute´ to´ the´ cultural´ landscape´
and´ enable´ the´ sustainable´ development´ of´ communities.´ It´ proposes´ a´ solution´ to´
the´ depopulation´ problem´ in´ rural´ areas,´ primarily´ due´ to´ the´ migration´ of´ the´ youth´
to´ urban´ centers,´ leaving´ tens´ of´ thousands´ of´ buildings´ empty´ and´ underutilized.´ By´
activating´ such´ spaces,´ it´ is´ possible´ to´ bring´ back´ life´ close´ to´ nature´ and´ foster´ a´
sustainable´ and´ creative´ way´ of´ living´ outside´ urban´ environments.´ The´ emphasis´
on´ ethical´ branding´ weaves´ its´ way´ through´ their´ mission:´ the´ engagement´ of´ the´
community,´ transparency´ in´ the´ operational´ process,´ and´ the´ promotion´ of´ sustainable´
development´ practices.´ Rural´ Radicals´ lies´ at´ the´ intersection´ of´ cultural´ innovation´
and´ rural´ regeneration;´ hence,´ it´ is´ a´ beautiful´ opportunity´ to´ understand´ the´ practical´
application´ of´ principles´ of´ ethical´ branding´ in´ the´ digital´ age.´ Such´ commitment´ to´
ethical´ issues´ is´ inherent´ in´ an´ ability´ to´ transform´ rural´ areas´ by´ creating´ spaces´ that´
will´ be´ incubators´ for´ creative´ and´ social´ enterprises.´ This,´ therefore,´ does´ not´ only´
answer´ some´ of´ the´ severe´ problems´ of´ making´ a´ living´ as´ an´ inhabitant´ of´ a´ rural´
area´ but´ also´ of´ developing´ in´ a´ more´ balanced´ and´ sustainable´ manner.´
2 Ethical Value Co-Creation in the Digital Economy:
Consumer Engagement and Marketing Strategies
In´ today’s´ ethical´ economy,´ consumers´ play´ an´ increasingly´ active´ role´ in´ shaping´
brand´ values´ and´ meanings.´ Rather´ than´ being´ passive´ recipients,´ they´ are´ now´
co-creators´ who´ engage´ with´ brands´ through´ digital´ platforms,´ inﬂuencing´ both

marketing´ strategies´ and´ the´ overall´ value´ creation´ process.´ This´ shift´ has´ rede-
ﬁned´ the´ relationship´ between´ consumers´ and´ businesses,´ blending´ the´ lines´ between´
production´ and´ consumption.´
Arvidsson´ [1]´ highlights´ this´ transformation´ by´ introducing´ the´ notion´ of´ “con-
sumer´ publics”,´ consumer´ groups´ that´ actively´ engage´ and´ collaborate´ with´ compa-
nies´ in´ an´ interactive´ process´ of´ co-creation´ and´ diffusion´ of´ brand´ meanings´ and´
values,´ mainly´ through´ digital´ and´ social´ media.´ This´ challenges´ the´ traditional´
view´ of´ consumption´ as´ passive,´ instead´ highlighting´ its´ role´ as´ a´ productive´ activity´
that´ can´ establish´ new´ ethical´ standards.´ As´ a´ result,´ participation´ of´ consumers´ in´
production´ blurs´ the´ line´ between´ production´ and´ consumption,´ contributing´ to´ value´
creation´ by´ participating´ in´ brand-related´ activities´ and´ communities.´
Within´ this´ ethical´ economy´ framework,´ Arvidsson´ [2]´ also´ discusses´ the´ “cus-
tomer´ co-production”´ where´ consumers’´ active´ involvement´ reshapes´ marketing´ and´
branding´ strategies.´ He´ explains´ that´ charismatic´ leaders´ (or´ brands)´ attract´ affec-
tive´ investments´ from´ their´ community,´ which´ translates´ into´ ethical´ capital.´ This´
capital´ can´ then´ be´ used´ to´ mobilize´ resources´ and´ organize´ productive´ processes.´
On´ the´ contrary,´ the´ increasing´ transparency´ afforded´ by´ digital´ technologies´ means´
that´ brands´ must´ align´ their´ actions´ with´ their´ ethical´ claims.´ Consumers´ can´ easily´
hold´ brands´ accountable´ for´ any´ discrepancies´ between´ their´ stated´ values´ and´ actual´
practices.´ All´ in´ all,´ production´ emphasizes´ the´ importance´ of´ brands´ authentically´
engaging´ with´ their´ consumers´ and´ prioritizing´ ethical´ considerations´ in´ their´ value´
creation´ strategies.´
The´ connection´ between´ ethical´ marketing´ and´ digital´ marketing´ further´ reinforces´
these´ ideas´ [3,´ 4].´ Some´ key´ connections´ between´ the´ two´ begin´ with´ the´ values´
of´ transparency´ and´ authenticity.´ Digital´ marketing´ provides´ brands´ with´ various´
platforms´ to´ communicate´ their´ ethical´ practices´ and´ values´ transparently.´ Digital´
marketing´ channels´ also´ offer´ opportunities´ for´ brands´ to´ educate´ and´ raise´ awareness´
among´ consumers´ about´ social´ and´ environmental´ issues.´ Through´ blogs,´ videos,´
social´ media´ campaigns,´ and´ interactive´ features,´ brands´ can´ involve´ consumers´ in´
ethical´ initiatives´ and´ gather´ feedback´ on´ sustainability´ efforts.´ This´ engagement´
fosters´ a´ sense´ of´ ownership´ and´ strengthens´ the´ consumer-brand´ relationship´ [5].´
It´ is´ not´ uncommon´ for´ digital´ marketing´ practices´ to´ often´ involve´ collaborating´
with´ inﬂuencers´ who´ align´ with´ the´ brand’s´ ethical´ values.´ Inﬂuencers´ can´ help´
amplify´ the´ brand’s´ message´ and´ reach´ a´ targeted´ audience´ that´ is´ receptive´ to´ ethical´
considerations.´ By´ partnering´ with´ inﬂuencers´ who´ genuinely´ support´ the´ brand’s´
ethical´ practices,´ brands´ can´ enhance´ their´ credibility´ and´ connect´ with´ consumers´
who´ value´ ethical´ choices´ [6].´ Table´1´summarizes´ the´ key´ aspects´ of´ value´ co-creation´

3 Case Study: Ethical Branding in Rural Radicals
3.1 Methodology
Rural´ Radicals´ is´ connected´ to´ the´ notions´ of´ space,´ nomadism,´ and´ tourism.´ For´ this´
reason,´ an´ approach´ to´ what´ is´ called´ “Ethical´ Tourism”´ was´ deemed´ necessary´ to´ be´
incorporated´ into´ the´ proposed´ strategy.´ Ethical´ tourism´ is´ simply´ tourism´ practiced´
with´ consideration´ for´ its´ impact´ on´ the´ social,´ cultural,´ environmental,´ and´ economic´
scenes´ of´ travel.´ Being´ responsible´ and´ making´ conscientious´ choices´ while´ touring´
reduces´ all´ negative´ impacts´ and´ maximizes´ positive´ reasons´ for´ visiting´ destinations.´
Ethical´ tourism´ may´ further´ be´ rendered´ through´ support´ for´ local´ businesses,´ respect´
toward´ local´ cultures´ and´ traditions,´ minimal´ environmental´ footprint,´ or´ involvement´
in´ sustainable´ and´ responsible´ activities.´ It´ generally´ tries´ to´ promote´ sustainable´
development,´ maintaining´ and´ upholding´ the´ quality´ of´ life´ of´ local´ communities´
and´ their´ way´ of´ life´ [8].´
Rural´ Radicals’´ target´ audience´ are´ Millenials´ and´ Generation´ Z´ persons´ who´
decide´ not´ to´ leave´ rural´ areas´ or´ to´ move´ to´ the´ periphery´ during´ or´ after´ the´ pandemic.´
These´ generations,´ especially´ Generation´ Z,´ are´ tech-savvy,´ using´ mobile´ devices´ and´
social´ media´ for´ travel´ planning,´ communication,´ and´ sharing..´ Social´ connections´
and´ responsible,´ sustainable´ travel´ practices´ are´ important´ to´ them,´ with´ value-led´
behavior´ inﬂuencing´ their´ travel´ choices,´ such´ as´ supporting´ local´ communities´ and´
seeking´ authentic´ experiences.´
Following´ the´ above,´ customer´ participation´ in´ a´ service´ encounter´ co-creates´
value,´ Customer´ participation´ co-creates´ value´ in´ ethical´ tourism,´ inﬂuencing´ authen-
ticity´ and´ sustainability´ by´ supporting´ businesses,´ respecting´ culture,´ and´ reducing´
environmental´ impact´ [9].´ Moreover,´ empowered´ consumers,´ especially´ Millennials´
and´ Gen´ Z,´ vocal´ advocates´ of´ responsible´ tourism,´ often´ express´ these´ values´ on´
social´ media.´ Rural´ Radicals´ can´ leverage´ this´ by´ engaging´ these´ generations´ as´
co-creators´ of´ authentic,´ socially´ responsible´ tourism´ experiences.´
Behzadinasab´ et´ al.’s´ [10]´ ethical´ social´ media´ marketing´ model´ emphasizes´ tools´
and´ practices´ for´ promoting´ sustainable´ tourism.´ Rural´ Radicals´ should´ adopt´ this´
model´ to´ engage´ its´ tech-savvy´ audience.´ Additionally,´ Community-Based´ Social´
Marketing´ fosters´ local-level´ participation´ in´ sustainable´ tourism,´ and´ a´ decentral-
ized´ narrative´ approach´ [11]´ helps´ communities´ shape´ their´ identity´ as´ ethical´ tourism´
destinations.´ The´ Consumer-V alue´ Brand´ Relationship´ Sustainability´ (CVBRS)´
strategy´ also´ emphasizes´ long-term´ emotional´ connections´ and´ shared´ values´ between´
brands´ and´ consumers,´ essential´ for´ promoting´ authenticity´ and´ trust´ [12].´ Rural´ Radi-
cals´ can´ build´ strong´ relationships´ that´ encourage´ repeat´ visits,´ word-of-mouth,´ and´
social´ media´ advocacy´ through´ cause-related´ marketing´ [13].

4 Applications on the Rural Radicals Online Channels
Branding and Rebranding—Website .´ A´ new´ ethical´ branding´ strategy´ for´ Rural´
Radicals´ can´ focus´ on´ clearly´ presenting´ ethical´ practices´ on´ the´ website´ through´
case´ studies,´ testimonials,´ and´ certiﬁcations´ to´ build´ credibility.´ Interactive´ features´
like´ QR´ codes,´ virtual´ tours,´ or´ maps´ could´ boost´ engagement.´ A´ clear´ Call´ to´ Action´
(CtA)´ encouraging´ users´ to´ share´ stories´ or´ offering´ incentives´ like´ free´ consultations´
would´ foster´ participation.´ An´ impact´ section´ with´ stats´ or´ infographics´ (e.g.,´ jobs´

and´ reﬂecting´ Rural´ Radicals’´ mission´ of´ promoting´ sustainable,´ culturally´ rich´

6 Conclusions
Ethical´ branding´ is´ particularly´ important´ for´ businesses´ due´ to´ the´ direct´ and´ long-
term´ beneﬁts´ it´ offers´ in´ terms´ of´ building´ consumer´ trust´ and´ loyalty,´ enhancing´ brand´
reputation,´ differentiating´ in´ competitive´ markets,´ attracting´ investors´ and´ partners,´
fostering´ employee´ satisfaction,´ mitigating´ legal´ risks,´ creating´ sustainable´ business´
practices,´ and´ driving´ innovation´ and´ growth.´
The´ proposed´ strategies´ for´ Rural´ Radicals´ aim´ to´ enhance´ the´ ethical´ and´ partici-
patory´ dimensions´ of´ the´ brand.´ Central´ to´ these´ efforts´ is´ the´ focus´ on´ transparency,´
authenticity,´ and´ community´ engagement.´ By´ integrating´ ethical´ practices´ such´ as´
case´ studies,´ testimonials,´ and´ certiﬁcations,´ the´ website´ will´ establish´ credibility´ and´
demonstrate´ its´ commitment´ to´ sustainable´ tourism.´ The´ use´ of´ interactive´ features´
like´ virtual´ tours´ and´ QR´ codes´ fosters´ consumer´ engagement´ and´ provides´ a´ plat-
form´ for´ clients´ and´ community´ members´ to´ contribute´ to´ the´ narrative,´ aligning´ with´
co-creation´ theories.´ Visually,´ the´ incorporation´ of´ diverse´ color´ palettes´ and´ logo´
variations´ reﬂects´ the´ brand’s´ commitment´ to´ inclusivity´ and´ diversity,´ key´ elements´
of´ ethical´ branding´ as´ described´ in´ [5].´ The´ integration´ of´ UGC´ and´ behind-the-
scenes´ content,´ reels,´ and´ storytelling´ also´ reinforces´ transparency´ and´ strengthens´
the´ emotional´ connection´ between´ the´ brand´ and´ its´ followers.´ In´ summary,´ Rural
