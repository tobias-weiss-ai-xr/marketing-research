Abstract
Digital marketing analytics has become increasingly important for
understanding consumer behaviour and improving purchase conversion
through data -driven engagement strategies. This study examined the
relationship between digital marketing analytics, consumer engagement, and
purchase decision -making, with particular attention to analytics -enabled
personalisation. A quantitative, cross -sectional, explanatory, and predictive
design was adopted using a secondary Kaggle dataset comprising 1,000
customer observations. Consumer engagement was assessed through click -
through rate, website visits, pages per visit, time on site, social shares, email
opens, and email clicks, while purchase decision-making was represented by
Conversion. Descriptive statistics, chi -square tests, correlation analysis,
binary logistic regression, and ROC -AUC evaluation were employed. The
results showed that 878 customers (87.8%) converted, while 122 (12.2%) did
not. Converted customers demonstrated significantly higher click -through
rates, pages per visit, time on site, email opens, email clicks, previous
purchases, and advertising expenditure. C ampaign Type was significantly
associated with conversion, whereas Campaign Channel was not. The
Consumer Engagement Index showed a strong positive relationship with
conversion, and the predictive model demonstrated reasonable
discriminatory ability. Overa ll, the findings indicate that the intensity and
quality of digital consumer engagement are more informative for purchase
decision-making than channel selection alone. Behavioural analytics can
therefore support customer segmentation, campaign optimisation, and more
relevant personalised marketing strategies.

INTRODUCTION
With the rise of technology and usage of digital platforms
like websites, search engines, social media, emails, paid
advertising, and other forms, consumers are interacting
with brands more than ever, making digital marketing an
integral part of any busine ss strategy. As a result of these
interactions, there is significant behavioural data that can
be used by organisations to assess the effectiveness of
campaigns not only in terms of reach, but also in terms of
consumer behaviour when interacting with marke ting
messages, digital environments and when moving
towards purchase. In recent studies, it has been
determined that social media marketing activities can
affect customers' intentions, particularly by reinforcing
customers' perceived value and interaction with brands
[1]. In addition to the number of exposures, consumer
engagement has also been identified as an important
behavioural outcome of digital marketing activity and
therefore should be investigated not just across single
touchpoints but also across multiple touchpoints [2].
Personalised marketing strategies are one of the most
significant trends in digital marketing. Personalisation is
tailoring messages, recommendations, offers and
customer interactions to consumer preferences,
behavioural patterns and previous interactions.
Personalised product recommendations can help to make
the product more relevant to the users, and help them
make their decisions in digital commerce, which would
lead to more efficient marketing [3]. But personalisation
needs to be tackled sensitively, as some consumers might
react differently in terms of privacy concerns and how
they think their personal data is being used. Research has
demonstrated that privacy-oriented consumers might have
different perspectives on personalised shopping
experiences and thus, relevance and trust must be taken
together [4].
There's a strong link between consumer engagement and
purchase behaviour and digital marketing activity.
Engagement can be measured in terms of click -through
rate, site visits, depth of website visits, time on a website,
social shares, email opens and email clicks. These metrics
show direct content evidence of consumer interactions
with marketing content. In previous studies, it has been
revealed that brands' SMM practices can influence
customer engagement and behavioural reactions, but the
extent of the ef fect differs among various types of
customers and situations [5]. Digital content marketing
can also impact electronic word of mouth and customer
interactions if the digital content is seen as useful and
relevant to the customer [6]. These findings indicat e that
engagement should not be viewed as a one -dimensional
behavioural phenomenon but rather as a multi -
dimensional phenomenon.
The quality and presentation of digital content may also
impact the buyer's decision -making process. When
information is present, pleasing, and fitting, consumers
are more apt to have a positive evaluation of the product.
Data from recent studies shows tha t digital content
marketing that presents product information can impact
purchase intention, due to how enjoyable the content is
and by making them more responsive to the content [7].
In competitive online settings, consumers are likely to
compare alternatives, researching them through more than
one channel online, before planning, which is especially
important. Digital analytics can thus be used to assess
what interactions and content engagement are most
correlated with conversion.
As AI becomes more prevalent, digital marketing
analytics is becoming even more powerful. Customer
behaviour can be understood through AI -driven
marketing by analysing vast amounts of customer data,
identifying patterns, forecasting future consumer needs,
and, of course, providing more personalised marketing
messages [8]. The shift to generative AI has opened even
more opportunities by empowering businesses to generate
dynamic content, make personalised recommendations,
and engage with consumers on a one-to-one basis [9]. The
advancements reflect how marketing analytics plays a
pivotal role in enabling more responsive and personalised
consumer experiences.
Customer-brand relationships and engagement are also
tied in with digital content marketing. Studies reveal that
digital content can impact brand engagement with
consumers and that the type of content created by
influencers can in turn affect the engagemen t with their
brand [10]. Marketing performance must be measured
behaviourally, as it is here. Organisations should know
that engagement can happen in several different ways and
that some engagement signals that might seem superficial
or impression -focused are more indicative of real
customer interest and are better for driving customer
conversions.
Although more research is being done on digital
marketing, personalisation and engagement, there is one
main gap yet to be filled. Numerous studies are about
single platforms, single engagement measures and self -
reported outcomes like purchase intention. T here is a
limited number of studies that combine multiple
behavioural data points such as click -through rate,
website engagement, email interaction, previous
purchases, customer loyalty and characteristics of the
campaign in a single model for an actual conversion. It is
also important to understand whether the purchase
outcome is related to the marketing channel or to the
quality and engagement of consumers in the marketing
channel.
The present study aims to fill the gap by analysing the
digital marketing data on a customer level, from various
perspectives of digital marketing activity, engagement
and purchase behaviour. Analysis includes Campaign
Type, Campaign Channel, Ad Spend, Cli ck-Through
Rate, Website Visits, Pages per Visit, Time on Site, Social
Shares, Email Opens, Email Clicks, Previous Purchases,
Loyalty Points, and Conversion. Available data set does
not include a direct experimental measurement of
personalisation, so perso nalised marketing is defined as
analytics-driven personalisation, in which consumers'
personal behaviour and history data can be used to better
target and communicate with them in a more personalised
way.
Thus, the study is designed to explore the role of digital
marketing analytics and consumer engagement in the
decision-making process of buying and how customer
behaviour information can aid in customer
personalisation in marketing. The study aims to combi ne

2. Methodology
2.1 Research Design
The study employed a quantitative, cross -sectional,
explanatory and predictive research design that attempts
to investigate the relationship between digital marketing
analytics, consumer engagement and purchase decision -
making. As the study used metrics th at can be measured
on a customer basis, e.g. advertising spend, click-through
rate, website usage, email engagement, purchase history,
loyalty activity, conversion, the quantitative approach was
suitable. The explanatory component evaluated the
relations b etween digital marketing and engagement
variables and the conversion of the customers; the
predictive component focused on the most influential
factors in purchase decision-making. Given that the study
was based on secondary data and not experimental data,
analytical-based behaviour targeting was taken as a
personalisation treatment rather than personalised
marketing.
2.2 Data Source
The data set on which the study is based was publicly
available and can be accessed on Kaggle [11].  The data
set included information on the demographic
characteristics of the customers, activities in the
campaign, advertising expenditure, website engagem ent,
interaction with emails, previous purchases, loyalty
behaviour, and conversion outcomes (at the customer
level). The Kaggle dataset was used as a secondary data
source, and a derived dataset of 1000 observations was
used in the present analysis.
2.3 Study Population and Analytical Sample
The analytical sample was 1000 observations of the
customers. These records were individual customer
records and contained data on the customer's
demographics, digital marketing interactions,
engagement, behaviour, and conversion. The sample size
was deeme d sufficient for descriptive analysis, group
comparison, chi -square, correlation analysis, logistic
regression and evaluation of prediction models.
2.4 Sampling Approach
A proportionate stratified sampling was used to obtain the
1000-record analytical sample from the larger Kaggle
database. Stratification was done by Campaign Channel,
Campaign Type and Conversion status. This methodology
enabled the maintenance of the proportionality of relevant
marketing categories and outcome categories within the
input sample. The converted and non -converted
customers were not artificially balanced in the sample so
that the natural distribution of converted and non -
converted customers was preserved.
2.5 Study Variables
Purchase decision -making was termed the dependent
variable which was coded as 0 if the customer was not
converted and 1 if the customer was converted and was
the dependent variable.
The digital marketing variables considered were
Campaign Channel, Campaign Type, Ad Spend, Click -
Through Rate and Conversion Rate. Consumer
engagement metrics included: Website Visits, Pages per
Visit, Time on Site, Social Shares, Email Opens, Email
Clicks, and Click-Through Rate. Previous Purchases and
Loyalty Points were the aspects of customer behaviour
variables considered, and Age, Gender, and Income were
the demographic and control variables considered.
2.6 Operationalisation of Core Constructs
The various elements of Digital Marketing Analytics are
campaign attributes, advertising spend, click -through
behaviour, and conversion tracking. Consumer
Engagement was realised by measurable behaviours
including website activity, website browsing depth, time
spent on the website, social interaction, and email
engagement. The binary variable, Conversion, was used
to measure Purchase Decision-Making.
No direct variable measure of personalisation was found
in the dataset, so personalised marketing was conceptually
operationalised as analytics -enabled personalisation. In
this context, behavioural engagement, campaign
response, previous purchases and loya lty information
were considered analytical inputs which could aid
marketing in making more relevant and targeted
decisions.
2.7 Construction of the Consumer Engagement Index
The Consumer Engagement Index (CEI) was developed
by combining Click-Through Rate, Website Visits, Pages
per Visit, Time on Site, Social Shares, Email Opens and
Email Clicks. All these indicators were on different
numerical scales and were transformed into z-scores:
The standardisation of values was then done, and an
overall engagement score was created. The higher the
CEI, the greater the digital engagement experienced, and
the lower the score, the less the engagement.
2.8 Data Preparation and Screening
The data were initially checked for completeness and
analytical suitability and then used for inferential analysis.
The preparation process involved missing value checks,
where appropriate, duplicate observation checks,
categorical value checks, Confirming  the conversion of
the categorical variable, and checking the ranges of the
continuous variables in the preparation process. Major
factors or variables were evaluated for adequate variation;
those that were not removed from the inferential analysis,
if app licable. The CEI engagement indicators were
standardised, and the categorical predictors for regression
analysis were dummy-coded. Further, correlation between
the explanatory variables was analysed to check for
multicollinearity.
2.9 Statistical Analysis
The sample was initially described using descriptive
statistics. For categorical variables, frequencies and
percentages were presented, and for continuous variables,
means, standard deviations, minimums and maximums
were presented.
Customer data from key marketing and engagement
metrics, such as Ad Spend, CTR, Website Visits, Pages
per Visit, Time on Site, Social Shares, Email Opens, Email
Clicks, Previous Purchases, Loyalty Points, Age and
Income, were compared for both converted an d non -
converted.

variable. A stratified cross -validation approach was
adopted to ensure that the proportion of converted and
non-converted customers was similar across the
validation folds. A significance level of p < 0.05 was used
to determine statistical significance.

3.2 Digital Marketing Characteristics by Conversion Status

converted customers with non-converted customers to identify if there is any difference in their marketing exposure and

The mean Ad Spend was significantly higher for the converted customers at 5,223.11 than for the non -converted

Through Rate. The mean CTR for the converted group was 0.159 while the mean CTR for the non -converted group was
0.111 (p < 0.001).
Converted customers' mean Conversion Rate was also higher than that of Non -Converted Customers, with a difference
of 0.104 vs 0.093, respectively, which was statistically significant at p = 0.041. There was a statistically significant
difference in income between these groups, but not for Age. The results suggest that there were differences in marketing
responsiveness and some customer attributes as a function of purchase outcome. Table 1 compares these digital marketing
characteristics with the customer's characteristics.

3.8 Consumer Engagement Index and Purchase Conversion
The standardised Consumer Engagement Index (CEI) was developed to measure the overall impact of digital engagement,
based on CTR, Website Visits, Pages per Visit, Time on Site, Social Shares, Email Opens and Email Clicks.
Standardisation was done to ensure indicators measured on different scales contributed equally to the composite measure.
The mean CEI for non -converted customers was about −0.291 while the mean CEI for converted customers was about
0.040. This disparity suggests that, in general, the customers that converted had more positive digital engagement.
When the binary logistic regression model was used and the CEI was used as a predictor, there was a statistically
significant positive relationship with purchase conversion. The estimated coefficient was B = 2.671, resulting in an Odds
Ratio of 14.458; a 95% confidence interval of 7.774 – 26.890. There was a high level of significance (p < 0.001).
There is a significant relationship between combined digital engagement and purchase conversion odds, with the higher
the combined score, the higher the odds of conversion. The odds ratio is not a measure of any individual engagement

4. Discussion
The present study explored the association between
digital marketing analytics, consumer engagement and
purchase decision making, focusing on how consumer
behaviour can assist them in personalisation through
analytics. The overall results suggest that the variables
related to engagement were more consistently related to
conversion than the digital channel used and demographic
variables. This highlights the importance of looking at the
customer journey, not just certain performance metrics, in
marketing anal ytics. V ollrath and Villegas highlight the
importance of digital marketing analytics that reflects the
journey that the customers take towards the different
stages of the decision -making process and various
touchpoints [12]. In line with this view, Habib et al. found
a positive relationship between digital marketing and
customer engagement, which ultimately leads to purchase
intention in a digital environment [13].
One of the best indicators in this current analysis was
click-through rate. The multivariable model showed that
CTR was also still significant, and the converted
customers had significantly higher CTR values than the
non-converted ones. This implies that w hen consumers
click on ads, they are more interested and are more likely
to buy the product. Likewise, Onofrei et al. showed that
interactions with digital content, content features and
engagement could have an impact on the behavioural
outcomes in the purchase area [14]. Koay et al. also found
that social media marketing activities can create purchase
intention via the mechanisms of engagement [15]. The
evidence from the meta -analysis conducted by Ao et al.
also corroborates that the more digitally engaged , the
more purchase intention is produced [16].
The results are also very relevant for personalised
marketing. Developing personalised communication
without segmenting consumers does not work the same
way for all consumers, as Segmentation is essential for
developing personalised communication, as demonstrated
by Serrano -Malebrán and Arenas -Gaitán [17]. This is
directly relevant to the current study as consumers can be
segmented based on their behaviour, which can include
website activity, email interaction, previous purchases,
loyalty points and CTR. Additionally, Sakina and Ali [18]
have discovered that personalisation on website
environments can affect purchase intention. However, for
the present analysis, Website Visits was not significant
while Pages per Visit and Time on Si te were significant,
indicating that the amount of browsing and quality of the
browsing is more revealing than just the number of visits.
This discovery backs the relevance and engagement of a
website based on customers' behavioural data.
Data-driven personalisation is also supported by
Davenport, who stated that hyper -personalisation with
artificial intelligence (AI) can enhance customer
engagement with more personalised and relevant
experiences [19]. While this dataset did not include a
direct personalisation variable, there were many of the
behavioural indicators present which can serve to inform
personalisation. Saha et al. also showed that the
characteristics of the consumers affect their click-through
intent for personalised ads [20] a nd lend support to the
notion that CTR is a valid behavioural measure of targeted
digital marketing.
Campaign Type was highly related to the conversion, and
Campaign Channel was not related to the conversion. The
authors' discovery indicates that the goal of a campaign
and its design might be more important to the outcome
than how it is delivered. The use  of social media
marketing activities has been found to be capable of
significantly impacting the intention to purchase online by
the users [21]. In the same way, Susiyanto  et al.
discovered that the link between social media marketing
and purchase intention could be conveyed via customer
engagement [22]. These results are consistent with the
results of the current study, which showed that a strong
CEI was predictive of Conv ersion. This result suggests
that there might be more important elements to the
behavioural response that can be attributed to marketing
than to a specific medium.
The present results also corroborate the overall
importance of personalisation in e-commerce. Choudhury
et al. presented that personalisation could make the digital
commerce environment more engaging for customers
[23]. Soni also concluded that personalise d digital
marketing has the potential to positively impact the
purchasing decisions of consumers in various scenarios
[24]. The current study found that more engagement in
CTR, website interaction and email behaviour led to
increased conversion rates, which is empirical evidence to
support the use of behavioural analytics to aid
personalised targeting.
Additionally, purchases had a significant positive
relationship with Conversion, which was observed in
previous studies. This discovery may indicate that
previous behaviour of the customers could be used to find
people who are more likely to buy again. Kan g & Pan
demonstrated that personalised recommendation services
could impact users' evaluation, satisfaction and purchase
intention throughout the purchase process [25]. The
purchase-history data can thus be used in practice for
recommendation systems, customer segmentation and
customer retention. Similarly, Nabila and Negoro noted
that digital marketing, customer engagement and brand
awareness are all significant factors in purchase intention
[26] and that the three factors are connected.

consideration-stage messaging. The results thus confirm a
segmentation and targeting based on engagement.
Generally, the findings are in line with literature regarding
digital marketing analytics, customer engagement,
personalisation and purchase behaviour in recent years.
The most important finding of the current research is that,
besides the channel choice, behavioural engagement
offers more conversion information. However, as
personalisation had not been directly measured, the results
should only be considered as evidence for the benefits of
behavioural analytics for personalised marketing, but not
proof of a personalisation effect.

5. Conclusion
This study explored the correlation between digital
marketing analytics, consumer engagement and purchase
decision making, based on an existing secondary data
source from Kaggle that contained 1000 customer
observations. The results confirm that behavioura l
engagement is a very important factor in the explanation
of purchase conversion. Customers that converted had
higher engagement on multiple digital metrics, including
click-through rate, pages per visit, time on site, email
opens, email clicks, and prior  purchases. The findings
indicate that deeper and more active forms of engagement
hold greater promise of gaining insight into purchase
behaviour than simple exposure or channel presence.
Campaign Type was also significantly related to
Conversion, with con version campaigns delivering the
best results, and Campaign Channel was not significantly
related to purchase outcomes. This means that the
strategic value and pertinence of marketing action is more
essential than the actual digital medium itself. The
Consumer Engagement Index also had a positive
relationship with conversion, further highlighting the
importance of having multiple behavioural measures to
measure a customer's purchase propensity. Behavioural
analytics can help organisations from a management point
of view to enhance customer segmenta tion, campaign
targeting, prioritising leads, and personalising
communication. Engagement information can be used to
identify customers that are more likely to buy the product
and therefore allow for more efficient spend on marketing.
As personalisation wa s not measured directly, however,
the results are to be interpreted as support for the
usefulness of analytics -based personalisation, not as
evidence of the direct effect of personalisation. In
summary, the research underscores the need for consumer
engagement analytics in enhancing data-driven marketing
and conversion.
