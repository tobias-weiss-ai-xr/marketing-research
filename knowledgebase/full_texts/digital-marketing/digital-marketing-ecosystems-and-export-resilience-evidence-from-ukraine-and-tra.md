ABSTRACT:
The article substantiates an ecosystem -based approach to digital marketing as a resource for
strengthening sustainable exports in Ukraine and other post-socialist transition economies. The study
develops and applies a cross -country analytical framework for  examining the association between
national digital marketing ecosystem conditions and export resilience in Ukraine and selected transition
economies.  Two composite indicators are proposed: the Digital Marketing Ecosystem Index (DMEI),
encompassing digita l infrastructure, institutional support, and innovation capacity, and the Export
Resilience Index (ERI), incorporating export dynamics, partner -country concentration, and
competitive position in global trade. The empirical database was constructed using da ta from The
Global Economy and Trade Map for Ukraine and ten comparable countries. Indicators were
normalized using the min–max method and aggregated using equal weights. The results reveal Poland’s
leadership in both DMEI and ERI. Ukraine achieved a DMEI score of 0.273 and an ERI score of
0.527, ranking tenth and eighth, respectively. Its position is characterized by a combination of existing
digital infrastructure potential, low institutional performance, and negative export dynamics. Based on
the median values of the indices, a sustainable export strategy matrix was developed, distinguishing
sustainable digital export leaders, countries with unrealized digital potential, countries characterized by
traditional export resilience, and countries with vulnerab le export models. The practical value of the
findings lies in providing a rationale for an integrated strategy for Ukraine that combines institutional
improvement, digitalization of international marketing, innovation support for exporters, and
diversification of foreign markets.

Keywords: digital ecosystems; export resilience; sustainable export strategies; digital transformation; quantitative
analysis; transition economies; international marketing; market diversification; digital marketing ecosystem;
institutional environment; Digital Marketing Ecosystem Index; Export Resilience Index

In the contemporary environment of global instability, business digitalization, and
increasing geoeconomic risks, export resilience has become one of the key determinants
of economic security and long -term development in transition economies. For Ukraine,
the issue is especially acute: the full -scale war has damaged production facilities, logistics
networks, and energy systems, narrowed access to overseas customers, redirected trade
flows, and made export results increasingly dependent on firms ’ capacity for rapid
adjustment.
Under these circumstances, traditional approaches to export development based
primarily on production capacity, price advantages, or access to raw materials are no longer
sufficient. Modern export competitiveness increasingly depends on enterprises’ abilit y to
utilize digital communication channels, international B2B platforms, e -commerce
solutions, data analytics, customer relationship management (CRM) systems, digital
branding, marketing automation, and artificial intelligence tools. Consequently, digital
marketing can no longer be viewed merely as a set of promotional instruments. It is
increasingly becoming an ecosystem shaped by digital infrastructure, firms’ digital
preparedness, institutional conditions, innovation resources, international market
intelligence, and the capacity to build durable relationships with customers and pa rtners
abroad.
Particular attention should be paid to the mechanisms through which the
development of a digital marketing ecosystem may strengthen export resilience. In this
study, export resilience refers to the capacity of a country or sector to absorb external
shocks, restore export activity, and reallocate sales across foreign markets when demand,
logistics, institutional conditions, or competitive pressures change . It is reflected in the
continuity or recovery of export flows, broader market coverage, lower dependence on
individual trading partners, responsiveness to demand shifts, and the preservation of
competitive positions in international markets.
The proposed framework identifies several complementary channels through
which the digital marketing ecosystem can contribute to export resilience. First, digital
infrastructure reduces information, communication, and market -access barriers by
enabling ent erprises to use online communication, international B2B platforms, e -
commerce, and data-driven marketing tools. Second, digital institutional support reduces
transaction uncertainty by strengthening regulatory predictability, trust, transparency, and
the s ecurity of digital and cross -border interactions. Third, innovation and knowledge
capacity enhance firms’  ability to use marketing analytics, adapt products and
communications to foreign-market requirements, and reconfigure export strategies under
changing conditions. Finally, international market connectivity provides a direct
transmission mechanism through which digital capabilities can support partner acquisition,
geographic diversification, and a more stable presence in international markets.
These channels are expected to operate jointly rather than independently. Digital
infrastructure represents a necessary foundation for international digital interaction, but
infrastructure alone may generate only limited export -resilience gains when instit utional
quality is weak  or innovation capacity is insufficient. In transition economies, where
institutional transformation and uneven digital modernization frequently coexist,
institutional quality and innovation capacity are therefore expected to play the strongest
enabling ro le, because they determine whether available digital infrastructure can be

Accordingly, the study advances four theoretical hypotheses. H1: a higher level of
digital infrastructure is positively associated with export resilience by reducing barriers to
international market access and communication. H2: stronger digital institutional support
is positively associated with export resilience by red ucing transaction risks and
strengthening trust in cross -border market interactions. H3: greater innovation and
knowledge capacity is positively associated with export resilience through improved
adaptability, competitiveness, and responsiveness to changin g foreign -market demand.
H4: the combined development of digital infrastructure, institutional support, and
innovation capacity is associated with higher export resilience than the development of
any single component in isolation, reflecting the complement ary nature of the digital
marketing ecosystem.
For Ukraine, these mechanisms are especially consequential during the war, as
online channels can partly offset logistics interruptions, reduced access to physical trade
events, disrupted legacy sales networks, and higher transaction expenses. However, similar
relationships are relevant to other transition economies undergoing institutional
transformation, digital modernization, and integration into global value chains. The
comparative analysis therefore provides an opportunity to examine whether differences in
digital marketing ecosystem development are systematically associated with differences in
export resilience and to identify sustainable export strategies relevant to Ukraine and other
transition economies.
A key methodological contribution of the study lies in its ability to integrate two
complementary sources of data. On the one hand, The Global Economy database provides
indicators related to digital infrastructure, the institutional environment, innovation
capacity, business climate, and the overall level of a country’s digital readiness. On the
other hand, Trade Map makes it possible to assess export dynamics, a country’s share in
global exports of specific product groups, import demand dynamics in partner countries,
the geographic structure of exports, market concentration, and the potential of external
demand. Together, the two databases support the construction of composite measures
linking country -level digital and institutional conditions with multidimensional export
resilience.
Existing research commonly examines digital marketing transformation, export
resilience, and sustainable export development through separate analytical perspectives.
Less attention has been devoted to the way country -level digital marketing conditions
interact with the capacity of export systems to withstand shocks, diversify markets, and
sustain long-term performance. This gap is especially relevant for transition economies
and for Ukraine, where wartime disruption and reconstruction needs intensify the
interaction between digital, institutional, and export-related factors.
This study aims to construct and apply a comparative country -level framework
linking measurable characteristics of digital marketing ecosystems with multidimensional
export resilience in Ukraine and selected transition economies.

The research addresses five interrelated tasks: conceptualizing the digital

sustainable exports.
The object of the study is the processes of digital marketing transformation and
the enhancement of export resilience in Ukraine and other transition economies.
The analytical focus covers the theoretical, methodological, and applied
relationships linking digital marketing ecosystem conditions, export resilience, and the
formation of sustainable export strategies.
The study contributes an integrated country -level analytical architecture that
combines digital marketing ecosystem conditions, multidimensional export resilience, and
strategy classification within a single comparative framework.  Unlike approaches that
focus on individual digital tools or general indicators of foreign trade performance, the
proposed model enables the evaluation of relationships among digital infrastructure, digital
business activity, the institutional environment,  innovation capacity, market
diversification, and export stability.
Further justification of the proposed model requires an analysis of scholarly
approaches to digital marketing ecosystems, export resilience, sustainable exports, and the
transformation of international economic activity in transition economies. Such an analysis
will make it possible to identify the theoretical foundations underlying the relationship
between digital marketing transformation, export performance, market diversification, and
long-term export resilience.

2. Literature Review

The issues of digital marketing ecosystems and export resilience emerge at the
intersection of several research streams, including digital marketing transformation, the
ecosystem approach, international marketing capabilities, export diversification,
sustainable export strategies, and the resilience of enterprises and national economies
under conditions of crisis-driven transformation. Despite the substantial body of literature
within each of these fields, insufficient attention has been devoted to understanding how
a country’s digital marketing ecosystem influences the ability of its exports to remain stable,
diversified, adaptive, and oriented toward sustainable development.
Within the academic  literature, digital transformation is increasingly viewed not
merely as the adoption of digital technologies but as a broader transformation of business
models, organizational processes, market interactions, and value -creation mechanisms.
Kannan and Li (2017) proposed one of the foundational frameworks for digital marketing
research, demonstrating how digital technologies affect various stages of the marketing
process, including market analysis, customer engagement, value proposition development,
and commu nication channel management. Verhoef et al. (2021) portray digital
transformation as simultaneous change in operational processes, business-model logic, and
customer-facing interactions. These perspectives are particularly relevant to the present
study because they allow digital marketing to be understood not simply as a collection of
promotional tools but as a component of a broader system of market adaptation.

Nevertheless, a key limitation of this research stream is its predominant focus on the firm
level, while the relationship between a country’s digital marketing environment and export
resilience remains insufficiently operationalized.
Another important body of research examines the impact of artificial intelligence,
data analytics, and automation on marketing activities. Davenport et al. (2020) demonstrate
that artificial intelligence transforms both marketing strategies and consumer behavior by
enhancing demand forecasting, communication personalization, and managerial decision
support. De Bruyn et al. (2020) draw attention to the managerial and ethical challenges
accompanying AI adoption in marketing, particularly those related to data reliability,
transparency, consumer trust, and algorithmic accountability.  These findings are directly
relevant to export -oriented enterprises, as digital analytics can reduce uncertainty in the
selection of foreign markets, the segmentation of international customers, and the
identification of potential business partners. Howev er, most studies on artificial
intelligence and digital marketing focus primarily on communication effectiveness or sales
performance, whereas the contribution of digital tools to export resilience, market
diversification, and adaptation to external shocks remains less thoroughly explored.
The ecosystem approach provides an important theoretical foundation for a
broader understanding of digital marketing. Adner (2017) defines an ecosystem as a
structure of interdependent actors and activities that collectively contribute to value
creation. Similarly, Jacobides, Cennamo, and Gawer  (2018) conceptualize ecosystems as
coordination mechanisms among interdependent organizations that do not necessarily
operate within a single hierarchical structure. In the context of this study, this perspective
makes it possible to view digital marketing not as an autonomous business function but as
an ecosystem that integrates digital infrastructure, platforms, institutions, the business
environment, logistics, data analytics, and international market linkages. Nevertheless, the
foundational literature on ecosystem theory remains largely conceptual and offers limited
quantitative tools for assessing the impact of ecosystems on export resilience.
National digital-readiness frameworks address a related but conceptually broader
problem. For example, the Digital Economy and Society Index (DESI) assesses national
digital performance across dimensions such as human capital, connectivity, integration of
digital technology, and digital public services, whereas the Network Readiness Index (NRI)
assesses national digital preparedness through the dimensions of technology, people,
governance, and impact  (European Commission, 2022; Escalona Reynoso & Lanvin,
2025). These frameworks provide valuable benchmarks of overall digital preparedness, but
they are not specifically designed to explain how national digital conditions are converted
into international marketing capabilities, foreign-market access, market diversification, and
resilience of export flows.
Against this background, the conceptual contribution of the present study lies in
linking national ecosystem conditions to a specific international marketing outcome –
export resilience. Rather than assessing digitalization as a general characteristic of a n
economy, the framework focuses on the marketing mechanisms through which national
conditions support international market intelligence, partner acquisition, digital
communication, trust formation, market adaptation, and diversification. Accordingly, the
proposed Digital Marketing Ecosystem Index is not intended to substitute existing digital-
readiness indices; rather, it operationalizes the measurable core of a broader marketing -

An additional theoretical foundation is provided by the service -dominant logic
perspective. Vargo and Lusch (2016) argue that value is created through interactions
among multiple actors, institutions, and resources within service ecosystems. In the
context of international marketing, this implies that export value is generated not solely by
producers but also through interactions with digital platforms, logistics providers,
certification bodies, intermediaries, buyers, and national and international institutions. This
perspective is particularly relevant for the analysis of sustainable exports because it
highlights the role of interconnected market participants in value creation. However,
service-dominant logic does not provide sufficiently practical indicat ors for evaluating
digital marketing ecosystems, creating a need for further methodological development
through index-based approaches.
A more applied perspective on the issue is provided by studies of enterprise digital
internationalization. Jean and Kim (2020) demonstrate that internet capabilities,
particularly web capabilities and platform capabilities, positively influence export
marketing capabilities and export performance among small and medium -sized
enterprises. Vadana et al. (2020) show that the digitalization of value chains facilitates
faster, more efficient, and less costly internationalization processes. Dethine, Enjolras, and
Monticolo (2020) identify e-commerce, e-marketing, and e-business as key digital drivers
of enhanced export management in SMEs. Collectively, these studies confirm that digital
technologies reduce barriers to entering foreign markets; however, they provide limited
insight into the broader ecosystem conditions that enable such firm -level capabilities to
emerge and translate into resilient export performance.
Within the field of international marketing, the concept of dynamic marketing
capabilities has gained increasing importance. Pfajfar, Mitręga, and Shoham (2024) review
international marketing capabilities through a dynamic-capabilities lens and call for clearer
conceptual definitions and more consistent measurement.  Zahoor and Lew (2023)
demonstrate that strategic flexibility and the adoption of digital technologies strengthen
international marketing capabilities and enhance the export performance of small and
medium-sized enterprises operating under crisis condition s. Drawing on evidence from
Polish exporters, Ciszewska -Mlinarič, Siemieniako , and Wójcik (2024) show that
international dynamic marketing capabilities are positively associated with firms’
international performance during periods of pandemic -related turbulence. These
perspectives are particularly relevant for transition economies,  where enterprises
frequently operate under conditions of institutional instability, resource constraints, and
the need for rapid adaptation to changing external demand. However, this literature
provides limited explanation of how institutional, infrastructural, and innovation
conditions at the national level shape the development and effectiveness of such marketing
capabilities across firms.
The issue of export resilience is closely related to the literature on export
diversification. Cadot, Carrère, and Strauss -Kahn (2011) demonstrate that export

diversification is an important driver of economic development and a mechanism for
reducing vulnerability to external shocks. This proposition is particularly relevant for
transition economies, whose export structures often depend on a limited number of
product groups, markets, or logistics routes. Nevertheless, the traditional export
diversification literature focuses primarily on the product and geographic composition of
exports, while digital marketing factors such as digital platforms, data analytics, o nline
communication channels, CRM systems, and digital branding are rarely considered

Research on global value chains (GVCs) likewise highlights the importance of
resilience and international connectivity. In the World Development Report 2020, the
World Bank argues that participation in global value chains can contribute to productivity
growth, higher incomes, and technological upgrading, while simultaneously exposing
countries to technological, trade-related, and geopolitical shocks (World Bank, 2020). The
Global Value Chain Development Report 2023 further emphasizes the need to build
resilient and sustainable GVCs under conditions of increasing turbulence (WTO et al.,
2023). For Ukraine and other transition economies, this implies that export resilience
depends not only on production capabilities but also on the ability to maintain
international market linkages, adapt logistics networks, communicate effectively with
partners, and utilize digital channels for market outreach. Nevertheless, the role of the
digital marketing ecosystem in supporting such connections remains insufficiently
addressed within the GVC literature.
Another stream of research focuses on sustainable export strategies. Zeriti et al.
(2014) introduced the concept of sustainable export marketing strategy fit and
demonstrated that the alignment of export marketing strategies with external
environmental conditions has a significant impact on export performance. Leonidou et al.
(2015) further showed that environmentally oriented export strategies can enhance both
competitive advantage and exporter performance. These contributions are important
because they broaden the understanding of exports beyond short -term sales growt h
toward long -term competitiveness and compliance with sustainable development
principles. However, these frameworks were developed before the current stage of digital
transformation and therefore do not fully incorporate the role of digital platforms,
marketing analytics, artificial intelligence, e -commerce, and digital trust in shaping
sustainable export strategies.
The measurement of marketing performance represents another important issue
relevant to this study. Katsikeas  et al. (2016) emphasize that marketing performance is a
multidimensional construct and that the diversity of indicators used across studies
complicates meaningful comparisons of research findings. This observation is directly
applicable to export resilien ce, which cannot be assessed solely through export growth
rates. Instead, it should encompass the stability of export dynamics, market diversification,
responsiveness to external demand, a country’s competitive position in international
markets, and its ability to maintain an international presence during periods of disruption.
Accordingly, a composite ERI is introduced to capture export resilience through several
complementary dimensions rather than through export growth alone.

availability of international statistical databases. Trade Map provides indicators related to

The literature review reveals four interrelated research gaps. First, the country -
and cross -country ecosystem dimension of digital marketing remains insufficiently
developed. Second, existing national digital-readiness frameworks do not explicitly capture
the marketing-specific mechanisms linking digital conditions with foreign -market access,
adaptation, diversification, and export resilience. Third, export resilience is rarely examined
in conjunction with digital infrastructure, institutional quality, and  innovation capacity.
Fourth, sustainable export strategies remain insufficiently operationalized through
integrated measures of digital marketing ecosystem development, export dynamics, partner
concentration, and competitive positioning.
Overall, the existing literature provides a solid theoretical foundation for the study
but does not offer a comprehensive marketing -oriented framework that simultaneously
integrates national ecosystem conditions, export resilience, and sustainable export
strategies. The present research seeks to address this gap through the development of the
Digital Marketing Ecosystem Index (DMEI) and the Export Resilience Index (ERI),
followed by a comparative analysis of Ukraine and selected transition economies.

3. Research Methodology and Model Development

The results of the theoretical synthesis provide grounds for conceptualizing the
digital marketing ecosystem as a multi -level system encompassing interactions among
digital infrastructure, the digital business environment, institutional support mechanisms,
innovation capacity, digital communication channels, and international market
connectivity. Unlike the traditional perspective, which views digital marketing primarily as
a collection of promotional tools, the present study considers it an ecosystem -level
resource for fostering export resilience.

Within the proposed framework, the digital marketing ecosystem is defined as the
set of digital, organizational, institutional, innovation -related, and market conditions that
enable enterprises within a country to utilize digital channels for identifying foreign market
opportunities, communicating with international partners, adapting marketing strategies,
diversifying exports, and maintaining a stable presence in global markets.
Export resilience is conceptualized in this study as the ability of a country to
sustain, restore, and diversify export flows under conditions of external shocks, changes
in international demand, logistical constraints, institutional instability, and incre asing
global competition. It is reflected in the capacity to expand or recover exports, reduce
dependence on a limited number of foreign markets, and maintain competitive positions
in international trade.
Drawing on the reviewed literature, the study is based on the assumption that a
higher level of digital marketing ecosystem development is associated with greater export
resilience. The expected association operates through digital access, institutional quality,
innovation capacity, market diversification, and firms’ capacity to recalibrate foreign -
market strategies as external conditions evolve.
To empirically test this assumption, two composite indicators are proposed: the
Digital Marketing Ecosystem Index (DMEI) and the Export Resilience Index (ERI).
The conceptual logic of the study assumes that the digital marketing ecosystem
influences export resilience through several interconnected channels. Digital infrastructure
provides the fundamental conditions that enable enterprises to interact with foreign
markets through online channels. The digital business environment facilitates the use of e-
commerce, international B2B platforms, CRM systems, digital branding, and marketing
analytics. The institutional environment determines the level of trust, regulato ry
predictability, and the security of digital and trade-related transactions. Innovation capacity
affects enterprises’ ability to develop new market offerings and adapt them to the needs
of foreign customers. International connectivity supports the integr ation of enterprises
into global markets, information, and trade networks.

The conceptual model of the digital marketing ecosystem is broader than its
empirical operationalization. Due to the limited availability of comparable international
indicators across all countries included in the sample, the calculation of the Digital
Marketing Ecosystem Index (DMEI) incorporates only three components that can be
quantitatively measured using a common data source: digital infrastructure, digital
institutional support, and innovation capacity. The digital business environment and
international market connectivity remain integral elements of the conceptual framework,
but are not included in the current composite index calculation. This narrower
operationalization may affect cross -country rankings because countries with relatively
advanced e -commerce adoption, digital business practices, B2B platform use, or
international digital and market connectivity may receive lower DMEI scores than would
be expected under the full conceptual model. Conversely, countries performing relatively
strongly on the three measured dimensions may appear more favorable even if their digital
business environment or international connectivity is less develop ed. Therefore, the
DMEI should be interpreted as a parsimonious empirical representation of the measurable
core of the digital marketing ecosystem rather than as an exhaustive measure of its overall
maturity. Country rankings reflect relative performance on the dimensions included in the
index and may change if the omitted components are incorporated in future specifications.
To assess the level of digital marketing ecosystem development, the Digital
Marketing Ecosystem Index (DMEI) is proposed. The index is designed to compare
countries in terms of digital accessibility, the quality of the institutional environment, and
innovation capacity.

Following the structure presented in Table 2, normalized values of the primary
indicators are aggregated into the corresponding subindices. The Digital Infrastructure
Subindex (DI) is calculated as the arithmetic mean  of the normalized indicators
representing the share of internet users and the number of fixed broadband subscriptions.
The Digital Institutional Support Subindex (DIS) is calculated as the average of the
normalized indicators of government effectiveness, regulatory quality, control of
corruption, and the rule of law. The Innovation and Knowledge Capacity Subindex (IKC)
is represented by the normalized value of the Innovation Index.
The indicator of fixed broadband subscriptions is used as a proxy for the scale of
digital infrastructure. However, because it is measured in absolute terms, its value is partly
determined by country population size rather than exclusively by the intensity  or quality
of digital infrastructure development. Under min–max normalization, larger countries may
therefore obtain higher normalized broadband scores simply because they have a greater
number of subscribers, whereas smaller countries may be relatively disadvantaged even
when broadband penetration is com parable or higher. Since the Digital Infrastructure
Subindex combines this indicator with the share of internet users, the population -size
effect is partly moderated but not eliminated. Consequently, the absolute broadband
measure may introduce an upward b ias in the DI and DMEI scores of larger economies
and a downward bias in those of smaller economies, thereby reducing the strict
comparability of country rankings.  The results should therefore be interpreted as
comparative patterns rather than precise measures of overall digital marketing ecosystem
maturity. Future specifications should replace the absolute broadband indicator with
subscriptions per 100 inhabitants or another population -adjusted measure and assess
whether the resulting country rankings remain stable.
A distinctive feature of the proposed index is that it does not measure individual
digital tools used by enterprises. Instead, it evaluates the overall digital and institutional
environment of a country as a prerequisite for international business activity . This
approach makes it possible to compare transition economies not only in terms of internet

The Export Growth Subindex (EG) is calculated as the arithmetic mean of the
normalized indicators of average annual export growth during 2021–2025 and short-term
export growth during 2024–2025. The combination of these two time horizons makes  it
possible to capture both the overall trajectory of export performance and its most recent
dynamics.
The Market Diversification Subindex (MD) is based on the concentration of
partner countries. Since a higher level of concentration indicates greater dependence on a
limited number of markets, this indicator has a negative orientation and is normalized
using a reverse scale. Consequently, lower partner-country concentration results in a higher
value of the market diversification subindex.
The Competitive Position Subindex (CP) is calculated using a country’s share in
global exports and its ranking among world exporters. A country’s share in global exports
has a positive orientation, whereas the ranking position has a negative orientation because
a lower ranking number corresponds to a stronger competitive position.
Indicators such as total exports in 2025, average distance to export markets, and
the average annual growth of partner countries’ imports are used as supplementary
descriptive characteristics but are not directly included in the ERI. Total export value
largely overlaps with information captured by a country’s share in global exports. The
average distance to markets does not have an unambiguous positive or negative
interpretation, as greater distance may indicate either higher logistics costs or broader
geographic market coverage. Likewise, the growth rate of partner -country imports is
excluded from the composite index when it takes the same value for all countries in the
sample and therefore does not contribute to their differentiation.

Following normalization, the primary indicators are aggregated into the
corresponding subindices, and the subindices are subsequently combined into the
composite indices DMEI and ERI. Equal weighting is used as the baseline specification
because, in the ab sence of sufficiently established theoretical or empirical evidence
assigning consistently greater importance to any individual dimension, it provides a
transparent and reproducible benchmark and avoids introducing subjective priorities into
the composite indices. Thus, each of the three DMEI subindices – digital infrastructure
(DI), digital institutional support (DIS), and innovation and knowledge capacity (IKC) –
receives a weight of 1/3. The same approach is applied to the three ERI dimensions –
export growth (EG), market diversification (MD), and competitive position (CP).

analysis was conducted using three alternative weighting schemes. In each scenario, one
dimension was assigned a moderately higher weight of 0.40, while the remaining two
dimensions received weights of 0.30 each. For the DMEI, the alternative specifications
sequentially emphasized digital infrastructure, digital institutional support, and innovation
capacity; for the ERI, the corresponding scenarios emphasized export growth, market
diversification, and competitive position. This approach allows the stability of country
rankings to be assessed without imposing extreme weighting differences.

Countries exhibiting high values of both DMEI and ERI may be regarded as

deepening digital integration, expanding exports with greater value creation, and
strengthening the use of advanced market analytics. In contrast, countries characterized by
a high DMEI but a low ERI face the challenge of converting their digital and institutional
potential into tangible export outcomes through the development of international B2B
platforms, certification systems, digital branding, and exporter support mechanisms.

on production capabilities, resource endowments, or geographic advantages. Countrie s

By combining DMEI and ERI within a common analytical structure, the
proposed approach provides a quantitative basis for cross -country comparison of digital
marketing conditions and export resilience. Export performance is therefore assessed not
through a s ingle trade indicator but through the combined consideration of export
dynamics, market diversification, and competitive positioning. Applying this framework
to Ukraine and selected transition economies reveals differences in their digital and export-
development profiles.
For Ukraine, the proposed approach is particularly relevant because, under
conditions of war and post-war reconstruction, export resilience depends not only on the
physical recovery of production and transport infrastructure but also on the ability of
enterprises to utilize digital channels for partner acquisition, market diversification,
international positioning, and strengthening trust in Ukrainian products.
Thus, the outcome of this stage of the research is the development of a conceptual
and methodological framework that integrates the Digital Marketing Ecosystem Index
(DMEI), the Export Resilience Index (ERI), and the Sustainable Export Strategy Matrix.

Trade Map makes it possible to determine Ukraine’s comparative position among
transition economies and to formulate practical recommendations for the development of
a digitally oriented, sustainable export strategy.

foreign markets. Policy support should prioritize small and medium-sized exporters, which
frequently face resource constraints in finding foreign partners, obtaining product
certification, and building communications for international markets.
The interpretation of the results should take into account  several limitations of
the study. First, the indicator of fixed broadband internet subscriptions is measured in
absolute terms, which may create an advantage for countries with larger populations.
Second, the international indicators included in the DMEI are represented by the most
recent available observations, which may correspond to different reporting years. Third,
the ERI captures available dimensions of export dynamics, market diversification, and
competitive position, but does not include a direct measure of export volatility due to the
absence of a complete annual time series in the compiled dataset. Fourth, the index
framework identifies cross-country associations but cannot determine whether changes in
digital marketing ecosystem conditions cause changes in export resilience.
Thus, the empirical application of the proposed framework revealed substantial
differences among transition economies in terms of both DMEI and ERI. Ukraine
possesses several relatively developed elements of digital infrastructure; however, its
transformation into resilient export outcomes is constrained by unfavorable export

5. Conclusions

Overall, the research establishes a country -level analytical framework that
connects measurable characteristics of digital marketing ecosystems with multidimensional
patterns of export resilience. Unlike approaches that treat digital marketing primarily as a
set of promotional tools, the proposed framework conceptualizes it as an ecosystem in
which digital infrastructure, institutional quality, innovation capacity, international
marketing channels, and market linkages jointly create the conditions necessary  for
sustainable export activity.
To operationalize the framework quantitatively, two composite indices were
developed: the Digital Marketing Ecosystem Index (DMEI) and the Export Resilience
Index (ERI). The DMEI combines the subindices of digital infrastructure, digital
institutional support, and innovation capacity. The ERI incorporates export dynamics,
market diversification, and a country’s competitive position in global trade. The application
of min–max normalization and equal weighting provided a transparent and reproducible
basis for cross-country comparison, while the sensitivity analysis confirmed a high degree
of ranking stability under alternative weighting specifications.
The empirical application of the framework revealed substantial differences
among the countries included in the analysis. Poland ranked first according to both indices,
achieving a DMEI of 0.891 and an ERI of 0.787. Strong performances were also recorded
for Romania, Hungary, Slovakia, and Bulgaria, which combine relatively advanced digital
and institutional environments with resilient export positions.
Serbia ranked second in terms of ERI while recording a DMEI value below the
sample median. This finding suggests that its export resilience is supported primarily by
favorable export dynamics and a diversified structure of trading partners, whereas its digital
marketing ecosystem requires further development. Georgia, by contrast, exhibits a
relatively favorable digital and institutional environment but a lower level of export
resilience, indicating that its digital export potential has not yet been fully realized.
Ukraine achieved a DMEI score of 0.273 and an ERI score of 0.527, ranking tenth
and eighth, respectively, among the eleven countries included in the sample. Its strengths
include a relatively high level of internet usage, a substantial scale of broadband i nternet
infrastructure, and a comparatively low concentration of export partners. At the same time,
low scores for government effectiveness, regulatory quality, control of corruption, and the
rule of law limit the transformation of digital potential into s ustainable international
marketing and export outcomes. Negative export dynamics during 2021 –2025 further
weaken Ukraine’s position in terms of export resilience.
The matrix -based analysis enabled the classification of countries into four
strategic groups. Poland, Romania, Bulgaria, Hungary, and Slovakia were identified as
leaders of sustainable digital exports; Georgia was classified as a country with underutilized
digital export potential; Serbia was categorized as a country characterized by traditional

of eleven transition economies constrains the generalizability of the findings beyond the
analyzed institutional and regional context. Finally, the curr ent comparative index-based

export resilience but does not establish causal effects.
Future research should develop the proposed framework in several directions.
First, annual DMEI and ERI values should be calculated over a longer time horizon to
examine whether improvements in digital marketing ecosystem conditions precede
changes in export resilience and whether these relationships remain stable across different
stages of economic and geopolitical turbulence. Second, the country sample should be
expanded beyond transition economies to include advanced, emerging, conflict -affected,
and pos t-conflict economies, which would allow the external validity and broader
applicability of the framework to be assessed. Third, digital infrastructure indicators should
be recalculated using population-adjusted measures, such as fixed broadband subscriptions
per 100 inhabitants, to reduce the influence of country size on comparative rankings.
Fourth, future studies should apply longitudinal econometric approaches, including panel-
data models with country and time effects, lagged specifications, and, where d ata permit,
dynamic panel or other causal-inference techniques, to assess whether changes in DMEI
and its individual components are associated with subsequent improvements in ERI. Fifth,
the ERI should be extended by incorporating direct measures of export  volatility, speed
of export recovery after shocks, and the stability of market diversification over time. These
extensions would make it possible to test whether improvements in digital marketing
ecosystem development are associated with greater export re silience over time and to
determine whether the proposed framework remains applicable beyond the specific
context of transition economies and wartime Ukraine.
