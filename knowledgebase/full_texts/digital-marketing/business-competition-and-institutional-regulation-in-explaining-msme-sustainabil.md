Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) play a critical role
in economic growth and job creation, yet achieving long -term
sustainability remains challenging in increasingly competitive
business environments. Drawing on the Resource -Based View and
Institutional Theory, this study examines the effects of digital
marketing, circular economy practices, credit facilities, inclusive
partnerships, distribution strategy, pricing strategy, and innovation
strategy on business competition and MSME sustainability,  with
business competition as a mediating variable and institutional
regulation as a moderating variable. A quantitative research design
using a survey was employed to collect data from 250 MSME owners
and managers in East Java, Indonesia. Data were analyz ed using
Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM).
The findings indicate that circular economy practices, distribution
strategy, and pricing strategy significantly affect business
competition. Circular economy practices, inclusive partn erships,
pricing strategy, and institutional regulation have significant
positive effects on MSME sustainability. Business competition has a
significant negative effect on MSME sustainability, suggesting that
intense competitive pressure may weaken long -term business
performance. In contrast, digital marketing, credit facilities, and
innovation strategy have no significant effects on either business
competition or MSME sustainability, while the moderating effect of
institutional regulation on the relationsh ip between business
competition and MSME sustainability is not significant. Business
competition also does not significantly mediate the relationships
between the predictor variables and MSME sustainability.
Practically, the findings suggest that MSME mana gers should
prioritize circular economy practices, inclusive partnerships, and
effective pricing strategies, while policymakers should strengthen

sustainability.

INTRODUCTION
Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) continue to play a central role in
Indonesia’s economic development, particularly in East Java, one of the country’s largest
economic regions with a substantial MSME presence. These enterprises contribute signif icantly
to job creation, income distribution, and regional economic growth. Their importance has become
increasingly evident in sustaining economic resilience during periods of uncertainty. However,
sustaining these contributions has become increasingly ch allenging as MSMEs operate in a
dynamic business environment marked by intense competition, rapid technological advancement,
evolving consumer preferences, and rising sustainability expectations. To remain competitive and
achieve long -term sustainability, MSMEs must continuously strengthen their strategic
capabilities, develop their innovation capacity, and adapt to digital transformation and changing
market conditions (Almendros et al., 2024; OECD, 2021; Verhoef, Broekhuizen, Bart,
Bhattacharya, Qi Dong, et al., 2021).
Sustainability in this context goes beyond financial survival; it reflects a firm’s ability to
adapt continuously, innovate, and remain competitive over the long term. Strategic capabilities
and business innovation have emerged as two key pillars that shap e how firms respond to these
pressures. Firms that strengthen these capabilities and pursue innovation tend to demonstrate
stronger long -term performance and resilience (Clauss et al., 2021a; Egim et al., 2025;
Sabaruddin, 2026) . In particular, innovation, whether in products, processes, or business
models, is consistently associated with sustainable competitive advantage (Dorson, 2018;
Oduro, 2024) . Digital transformation also continues to reshape MSME operations and
competitive strategies. The integration of digital technologies can improve operational efficiency,
broaden market reach, and create new value propositions (Almendros et al., 2024; Verhoef,
Broekhuizen, Bart, Bhattacharya, Dong, et al., 2021) . However, digitalization alone is not
sufficient to generate these benefits without adequate strategic orientation and innovation
capability (Ritter & Pedersen, 2020). Moreover, circular economy practices and eco-innovation
further highlight the importance of aligning business strategies with sustainability goals (Kamal
& Jasni, 2023; Sarango-Lalangui et al., 2023).
Given these dynamics, understanding how Strategic Capabilities, innovation, competition,
and regulatory frameworks interact is essential. This study is motivated by the need to explore

Recent studies have increasingly emphasized the importance of strategic capabilities and
innovation in enhancing firm performance and sustainability. Entrepreneurial orientation, which
reflects a firm’s willingness to innovate, take risks, and act proactiv ely, is positively associated
with firm performance and adaptability (Lia & Fitri, 2023; Taan et al., 2023) . Similarly,
innovation capability has been identified as an important factor in long -term sustainability,
enabling firms to adapt to changing market conditions and customer demands (Khan et al.,
2023a; Latifannisa, Wardi, et al., 2025) . In addition, digital transformation has received
significant attention in recent years. Digital technologies can improve operational efficiency while
enabling new business models and value creation mechanisms (Kraus et al., 2022; Verhoef,
Broekhuizen, Bart, Bhattacharya, Dong, et al., 2021). Empirical evidence suggests that firms
that successfully integrate digital strategies with innovation practices tend to achieve better
performance outcomes.
From a sustainability perspective, eco -innovation and green practices are increasingly
recognized as important factors to firm performance. Studies have shown that environmental
sustainability initiatives are associated with stronger financial and non -financial performance,
particularly when supported by strong strategic capabilities (Sohu et al., 2024; Widyantoro et
al., 2025) . Intellectual capital and inclusive partnerships are also associated with improved
innovation outcomes and sustainability performance, as they strengthen knowledge sharing,
collaboration, and long-term value creation (Dwiputri et al., 2023).
The literature has also widely discussed the role of competition. Competition can stimulate
and constrain innovation. On the one hand, competitive pressure encourages firms to innovate
and improve efficiency; on the other hand, intense competition may redu ce profitability and
hinder long-term sustainability (Distanont & Khongmalai, 2020). Recent studies suggest that
competition may mediate the relationship between strategic actions and firm performance
(Siriram et al., 2023).
From an institutional perspective, the MSME business environment is shaped by both
market and institutional forces. Government policies and regulatory arrangements also influence
how firms operate, compete, and develop over time. In Indonesia, MSMEs are af fected by
regulations on business licensing, access to financing, digitalization programs, and market
governance. These regulations can create opportunities for business growth, but they may also
become obstacles when implementation is inconsistent or administrative procedures are complex.
Many MSMEs often need regulatory support to reduce operational barriers and encourage
business development.
The relevance of institutional regulation becomes more apparent in an increasingly
competitive business environment. Firms operating within supportive regulatory frameworks may
be better positioned to adopt innovations, expand their market reach, and adapt  to changing
customer demands. Regulatory quality can also shape competitive conditions within a market.
Clear and effective regulations help create a more predictable business environment, allowing
firms to focus on improving performance and maintaining b usiness sustainability. Previous
studies have shown that regulatory support is associated with stronger business competitiveness
and long-term performance, particularly among small firms that often face resource constraints
(Rosenbusch, Brinkmann, et al., 2011) . Similar findings have been reported by the (OECD,

Despite the growing body of literature on MSME sustainability, several important issues
remain unresolved. First, prior studies have generally examined strategic factors such as digital
marketing, innovation, pricing, distribution, and sustainability pract ices separately, leaving
fragmented evidence on their relative contributions to sustainability. Second, previous findings
concerning the effects of innovation and competitive dynamics on sustainability remain
inconclusive. While some studies suggest that i nnovation and competition enhance long -term
performance, others report limited or even adverse effects, particularly among resource -
constrained MSMEs operating in emerging economies. Third, although Institutional Theory
highlights the importance of regulat ory environments, empirical evidence on the direct and
contingent roles of institutional regulation in shaping MSME sustainability remains limited.
The empirical context of East Java further strengthens this investigation's relevance. As
one of Indonesia’s largest MSME ecosystems, East Java is characterized by intense market
competition, rapid digital transformation, and increasing sustainability demands. However, many
MSMEs continue to face resource constraints, unequal access to strategic support, and varying
levels of regulatory effectiveness. These conditions provide a relevant setting to examine whether
the relationships among strategic practices, competition, institutional support, and sustainability
differ from those reported in previous studies in other contexts.
This study addresses these gaps by developing and testing an integrated model that
simultaneously examines the effects of digital marketing, circular economy practices, credit
facilities, inclusive partnerships, distribution strategy, pricing strategy, and  innovation strategy
on MSME sustainability. More importantly, this study advances the literature by examining
whether business competition mediates the relationships between these strategic practices and
MSME sustainability and whether institutional regul ation moderates the relationship between
business competition and MSME sustainability. Thus, this study's novelty lies in integrating these
strategic, competitive, and institutional factors within a single framework to provide a more
comprehensive assessment of their relationships with MSME sustainability in the context of an
emerging economy.
Accordingly, this study aims to analyze the effects of strategic and innovation -oriented
practices on MSME sustainability, examine the mediating role of business competition, and
evaluate the moderating role of institutional regulation among MSMEs in East Java, Indonesia.
The findings are expected to contribute to the Resource-Based View and Institutional Theory by
clarifying the relationships between internal capabilities and external institutional conditions in
explaining MSME sustainability.

Theoretical Foundation
This study is primarily grounded in the Resource -Based View (RBV) and Institutional
Theory. RBV explains that firms achieve superior performance when they possess and effectively
utilize valuable resources and capabilities that are difficult for competitor s to imitate (Barney,
1991a). In the context of MSMEs, sustainability depends partly on how firms manage available
resources, develop market -oriented capabilities, and respond to competitive pressures. Digital
marketing, pricing decisions, distribution management, innovation practice s, business

partnerships, and access to financial resources can be viewed as strategic resources that support
long-term firm performance.
Institutional Theory complements this perspective by emphasizing that the external
institutional environment also shapes business outcomes. Organizations do not operate in
isolation; regulatory frameworks, market governance, and institutional support influ ence how
firms operate, compete, and sustain their activities (Scott, 2014a). In emerging economies such
as Indonesia, institutional regulation is particularly relevant because government support,
business development programs, and regulatory certainty can shape the conditions under which
MSMEs operate.
Based on these perspectives, this study examines MSME sustainability in relation to
internal strategic factors and external institutional conditions. In this study, the RBV provides the
theoretical basis for explaining how internal strategic practices contribute to competitiveness and
sustainability. Digital marketing, distribution strategy, pricing strategy, innovation strategy,
inclusive partnerships, circular economy practices, and credit facilities represent different
strategic resources, capabilities, and practices that may strengthen the firm's ability to create value
and maintain competitive advantage. Institutional Theory complements RBV by explaining that
the effectiveness of these strategic practices depends on the regulatory and institutional
environment in which MSMEs operate. Therefore, MSME sustainability is conceptualized as the
outcome of the relationship among internal strategic resources, competitive market dynamics, and
external institutional support.

Digital Marketing, Business Competition, and MSME Sustainability
Digital marketing has become an essential strategic capability for MSMEs in increasingly
competitive markets. According to the Resource-Based View (RBV), firms that effectively utilize
digital technologies can develop valuable and difficult-to-imitate capabilities that enhance market
responsiveness and competitive positioning (Barney, 1991b). Through digital platforms, MSMEs
can improve customer engagement, expand market reach, and access real -time market
information, thereby strengthening their competitive position. Previous studies indicate that
digital marketing adoption is positively associ ated with business competitiveness and market
performance, particularly among small businesses operating in dynamic environments (Chaffey
& Ellis-Chadwick, 2019; Dwivedi et al., 2021).
Beyond competition, digital marketing may also support business sustainability. Attracting
customers, maintaining relationships, and generating revenue through digital channels can
enhance business continuity and long-term performance. Firms that successfully integrate digital
marketing into their strategic activities are generally better positioned to sustain their performance
over time.
From an RBV perspective, digital marketing can be viewed as an intangible capability that
supports value creation, competitive advantage, and long -term sustainability. Based on these
arguments, the following hypotheses are proposed:
 H1: Digital Marketing has a positive effect on business competition.
 H2: Digital Marketing has a positive effect on MSME sustainability.

Circular Economy, Business Competition, and MSME Sustainability
Circular economy practices emphasize resource efficiency, waste reduction, product reuse,
and sustainable production systems. These practices can improve operational efficiency and
reduce production costs, enabling firms to strengthen their competitive position. Businesses that
adopt circular economy principles often differentiate themselves through environmentally
responsible products and processes, enhancing competitiveness in increasingly sustainability -
conscious markets (Geissdoerfer et al., 2017).
 Circular economy practices may also contribute to long -term sustainability by reducing
resource dependency and environmental impact while improving economic efficiency. These
benefits can support business continuity, resilience, and long -term value creatio n. Empirical
evidence indicates a positive association between circular economy adoption and organizational
sustainability (Centobelli et al., 2020).
From a strategic perspective, circular economy practices can support both competitiveness
and long-term sustainability through more efficient and sustainable resource use. Accordingly,
the following hypotheses are proposed:
 H3: Circular Economy practices have a positive effect on business competition.
 H4: Circular Economy practices have a positive effect on MSME sustainability.

Credit Facilities, Business Competition, and MSME Sustainability
Access to credit facilities represents an important financial resource for MSMEs. From an
RBV perspective, financial capital enables firms to acquire productive assets, invest in
technology, improve operational efficiency, and expand market activities. Ade quate financing
allows firms to strengthen their competitive capabilities and respond more effectively to market
pressures.
Credit facilities can also contribute directly to sustainability by supporting business growth,
innovation, and operational continuity. Firms with sufficient financial resources are generally
better equipped to survive economic uncertainty and pursue long -term development objectives.
Previous studies have reported that access to financing positively influences firm growth and
sustainability, particularly among MSMEs constrained by limited resources (Ermawati, 2025).
Within the RBV framework, credit facilities can be viewed as a financial resource that
supports productive investment, strategic development, and long-term business performance.
Therefore, the following hypotheses are proposed:
 H5: Credit facilities have a positive effect on business competition.
 H6: Credit facilities have a positive effect on MSME sustainability

Distribution Strategy, Business Competition, and MSME Sustainability
Distribution strategy determines how effectively products and services reach target
customers. Effective distribution systems improve market accessibility, customer satisfaction,
and market coverage, thereby strengthening firms’ competitive advantage. Firm s with efficient
distribution networks are generally better positioned to respond to market demand and maintain
competitiveness.
Distribution capability may also contribute directly to sustainability by supporting
customer retention, revenue generation, and operational continuity. Efficient distribution systems
help firms maintain their market presence and improve long-term performance outcomes.
From a strategic perspective, an effective distribution strategy can support
competitiveness and long-term sustainability by improving market access and customer reach.
Therefore, the following hypotheses are proposed:
H9: Distribution strategy has a positive effect on business competition.
H10: Distribution strategy has a positive effect on MSME sustainability.

Pricing Strategy, Business Competition, and MSME Sustainability
Pricing strategy is an important factor in market competitiveness because it influences
customer perceptions, purchasing decisions, and firm profitability. Effective pricing strategies
help firms attract customers while maintaining competitive positioning. Previous studies suggest
that pricing decisions are associated with competitive performance in highly competitive markets.
Pricing strategy may also support sustainability by improving revenue stability and long-
term profitability. Firms that adopt appropriate pricing strategies may better balance customer
value with financial performance, thereby supporting long-term sustainability.
From a strategic perspective, pricing strategy can support both competitiveness and
profitability, which are important for long-term business sustainability.
Accordingly, the following hypotheses are proposed:
H11: Pricing strategy has a positive effect on business competition.
H12: Pricing strategy has a positive effect on MSME sustainability.

Business Competition and MSME Sustainability
Business competition is relevant to the Resource -Based View because competitive positions
can reflect how effectively firms transform resources and capabilities into market advantages.
Strategic practices such as digital marketing, circular economy practic es, pricing strategy,
distribution strategy, inclusive partnerships, and innovation strategy may not directly influence
sustainability. Still, they can indirectly affect firms’ competitive position, efficiency,
differentiation, customer value, and market r esponsiveness. Firms that successfully convert
strategic resources into competitive advantages are generally better positioned to achieve long -
term sustainability.
At the same time, competition may generate both positive and negative consequences.
Competitive pressure can stimulate innovation, efficiency, and market responsiveness, thereby
potentially supporting business sustainability. Conversely, intense competitiv e pressure may
reduce profitability and increase operational risks, particularly among resource -constrained
MSMEs. These contrasting perspectives suggest that business competition may mediate the
relationship between strategic practices and MSME sustainabi lity. Therefore, this study
incorporates business competition as a mediating variable to explain how internal strategic
practices influence MSME sustainability.

frameworks that help firms operate more effectively.
A supportive regulatory environment can reduce uncertainty, improve access to

Moderating Role of Institutional Regulation
Institutional Theory suggests that external regulatory environments influence how
effectively firms respond to competitive pressures. Institutional regulation may affect
sustainability while also influencing the relationship between competition and sustain able
business outcomes. Supportive regulations can create fair market conditions, reduce uncertainty,
improve access to resources, and stabilize markets, helping MSMEs manage competitive
pressures more effectively.
Conversely, weak or inconsistent regulatory frameworks may limit the benefits of
competition by increasing uncertainty and reducing the effectiveness of strategic initiatives.
Consequently, institutional regulation may moderate the relationship between bus iness
competition and MSME sustainability. Therefore, this study examines institutional regulation's
moderating role in this relationship.

Research Design
This study employed a quantitative research design using a survey approach to examine
the relationships between strategic capabilities, business innovation, business competition, and
MSMEs' sustainability, with institutional regulation as a moderating vari able. A quantitative
approach was appropriate because the study aimed to test hypotheses and analyze causal
relationships among multiple variables using statistical techniques.
The research design was explanatory, focusing on the relationships among the variables
associated with MSME sustainability. The study used Partial Least Squares Structural Equation
Modeling (PLS-SEM) as the primary analytical method. PLS -SEM was selected b ecause it is
suitable for complex models involving multiple independent, mediating, and moderating
variables, and it does not require normally distributed data(Hair et al., 2022). Additionally, PLS-
SEM is suitable for studies with relatively small to medium sample sizes and focuses on
maximizing the explained variance of endogenous constructs.

Population and Sample
The target population of this study consisted of MSMEs operating across several
regencies and cities in East Java, Indonesia. East Java was selected because it is one of the
country's largest centers of MSME activity, characterized by diverse business sectors, increasing
market competition, and ongoing digital transformation. The study covered MSMEs engaged in
food and beverage, trade and retail, services, manufacturing, and other business sectors. The unit
of analysis was the owner or manager who was directly involved in strategic and operational
decision-making within the business.
The study used purposive sampling to ensure that respondents had sufficient knowledge
of business strategies, competitive conditions, and sustainability practices. Respondents were
included if they met the following criteria: (1) currently owning or managi ng an active MSME,
(2) having managed the business for at least two years, (3) being directly involved in managerial
or strategic decision-making, and (4) being willing to participate voluntarily in the survey. The
analysis excluded incomplete questionnair es and responses from participants who did not meet
the eligibility criteria.
The minimum sample size was initially estimated using the 10-times rule as a preliminary
guideline for PLS -SEM analysis  (Hair et al., 2022) . However, sample adequacy was further
assessed by considering the complexity of the model, including multiple predictors and a
moderation effect. A minimum sample of 200 respondents was considered adequate to achieve
sufficient statistical power for detecting medium effect sizes at a 5% significance level. A total of
270 questionnaires were distributed, of which 250 valid responses were retained for analysis,
yielding a valid -response rate of approximately 92.6%. Therefore, the final sample size was
considered sufficient to ensure the robustness and reliability of the PLS-SEM analysis.

examine collinearity. To minimize common method bias associated with self-reported and cross-
sectional data, respondents were assured of anonymity and confidentiality. The full colli nearity
assessment indicated that common method bias was not a major concern in the study.

Data Analysis Technique
Data analysis used Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM) with
SmartPLS. This technique was selected because it suits complex models involving multiple
constructs, mediating variables, and moderating effects and does not require multi variate
normality (Hair et al., 2022).
The analysis proceeded in several stages. First, the measurement model (outer model)

Discussion
The findings indicate that digital marketing does not significantly affect either business
competition or MSME sustainability. This result suggests that using digital platforms alone is
insufficient to create competitive conditions or sustainability benefi ts without complementary
managerial capabilities and strategic integration. Although previous studies have reported

 pg. 307
positive effects of digital marketing on business performance (Chaffey & Ellis-Chadwick, 2019;
Dwivedi et al., 2021) , the present findings support the Resource -Based View (RBV), which
emphasizes that resources generate greater value when effectively integrated with organizational
capabilities. In the East Java MSME context, traditional customer relationships and direct market
interactions may remain more influential than digital marketing activities in shaping business
outcomes.
Circular economy practices and pricing strategy had significant positive effects on
business competition and MSME sustainability, while distribution strategy had a significant
positive effect on business competition but not on MSME sustainability. These fi ndings suggest
that resource efficiency, waste reduction, and effective pricing decisions provide tangible benefits
for MSMEs by improving competitiveness and supporting long-term business performance. The
results align with prior studies highlighting the importance of sustainable resource use and
market-oriented strategies (Centobelli et al., 2020; Geissdoerfer et al., 2017; Kotler et al., 2022).
From an RBV perspective, these practices and strategies can support value creation by enabling
firms to use resources more effectively and respond to market conditions.
Credit facilities and innovation strategy did not significantly affect either business
competition or MSME sustainability. This finding indicates that access to financing and
innovation activities alone may not automatically improve business performance. F inancial
resources require effective managerial use, while innovation must generate distinctive, valuable
outcomes before it can shape competitive dynamics and long -term sustainability. Thus, the
nonsignificant effects may indicate that the benefits of financial resources and innovation depend
on how effectively they are utilized and integrated into business strategies, which may partly
explain variations in previous empirical findings.
Business competition was found to have a significant negative effect on MSME
sustainability. This result indicated that higher competitive pressure was associated with lower
sustainability outcomes among MSMEs. The finding is consistent with previous evide nce
showing that resource -constrained firms may experience reduced profitability, increased
operational pressure, and limited opportunities for long -term investment under intense
competition (Purnomo et al., 2021; Tambunan, 2021) . The negative coefficient should be
interpreted as a negative linear relationship within the observed sample rather than as evidence of
a nonlinear or threshold effect.
Institutional regulation had a significant positive effect on MSME sustainability but did
not significantly moderate the relationship between business competition and MSME
sustainability. These findings align with Institutional Theory, which emphasizes the  role of
regulatory and institutional conditions in shaping organizational outcomes (Scott, 2014b) .
However, the nonsignificant moderation effect indicated that institutional regulation did not
significantly change the relationship between competitive pressure and sustainability outcomes
within the sampled MSMEs. Similarly, the mediation analysis showe d no significant indirect
effects through business competition, indicating that business competition did not mediate the
relationships between the predictor variables and MSME sustainability.
From a theoretical perspective, this study contributes to the Resource -Based View by
showing that not all strategic resources contribute equally to sustainability outcomes. Circular

MSME sustainability, while digital marketing, credit facilities, and innovation strategy did not.
The study contributes to Institutional Theory by showing t hat institutional regulation had a
significant direct effect on MSME sustainability but did not significantly moderate the
relationship between business competition and MSME sustainability. Practically, MSME owners
should prioritize resource -efficient prac tices, strategic partnerships, and effective pricing
decisions; financial institutions should complement access to financing with capacity -building
support; and policymakers should strengthen regulatory certainty and MSME development
programs to enhance long-term sustainability.

CONCLUSION
This study investigated the effects of digital marketing, circular economy, credit facilities,
inclusive partnerships, distribution strategy, pricing strategy, and innovation strategy on MSME
sustainability, with business competition as a mediating variable and institutional regulation as a
moderating variable. The results indicated that circular economy practices, inclusive partnerships,
pricing strategy, and institutional regulation had significant positive effects on MSME
sustainability. Circular economy  practices, distribution strategy, and pricing strategy also had
significant positive effects on business competition. In contrast, digital marketing, credit facilities,
and innovation strategy did not significantly affect either business competition or MS ME
sustainability. Business competition had a significant negative effect on MSME Sustainability,
indicating that higher competitive pressure was associated with lower sustainability outcomes
among MSMEs. Furthermore, business competition did not mediate the relationships between the
predictor variables and MSME sustainability, and institutional regulation did not significantly
moderate the relationship between business competition and MSME sustainability.
The findings contribute to the Resource -Based View by demonstrating that strategic
resource practices and strategies do not contribute uniformly to sustainability outcomes. The
study also contributes to Institutional Theory by showing that institutional re gulation had a
significant direct effect on MSME sustainability but did not significantly moderate the
relationship between business competition and MSME sustainability. These results highlight the
importance of combining internal strategic capabilities with supportive institutional environments
to achieve sustainable MSME development.
Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the study employed a cross -sectional
design, which captured perceptions and business conditions at a single point in time and limited
the ability to establish causal relationships. Second, the data were collected through self-reported
questionnaires, which may be subject to respondent bias and subjective interpretation. Third,
purposive sampling may limit the generalizability of the findings beyond the selected
respondents. Finally, the study focused exclusively on MSMEs operating in East Java, Indonesia,
where economic structures, competitive conditions, and institutional environments may differ
from those in other regions.
Future studies are encouraged to address these limitations by using longitudinal designs to
examine changes in sustainability over time and probability -based sampling techniques to
improve external validity. Comparative studies across regions or countries may also provide

research may incorporate variables such as digital capability, organizational readiness,
entrepreneurial resilience, or dynamic capabilities to further expla in variations in MSME

practices, inclusive partnerships, and effective pricing strategies. Financial institutions may
consider complementing financing programs with managerial and business development support,
while policymakers should strengthen regulatory certainty and MSME support programs to
support long-term business sustainability.
