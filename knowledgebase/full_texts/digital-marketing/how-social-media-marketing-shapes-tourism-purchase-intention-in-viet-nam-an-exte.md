Abstract
This conceptual and methodological paper advances an expanded framework for explaining how social
media marketing (SMM) may shape consumers' intentions to purchase tourism services in Viet Nam.
Contemporary social platforms combine visual storytelling, electronic word-of-mouth, two-way interaction
and traveller  participation, yet studies in emerging tourism markets often investigate these elements in
isolation or focus on a single psychological mechanism. Drawing primarily on the Stimulus -Organism-
Response (S -O-R) paradigm, with selective support from the Theory  of Planned Behavior and Uses and
Gratifications theory, the proposed model treats content quality, interactivity, source credibility, cultural
relevance and perceived entertainment as external digital stimuli. These cues are theorized to influence
trust, perceived value, emotional attachment, social influence/ eWOM and community engagement, which
in turn contribute to purchase intention. Cultural values, socio-economic status, travel motivation, platform
familiarity and prior travel experience are incorporated as boundary conditions. As a conceptual -
methodological s tudy rather than a report of completed empirical results, the paper outlines a future
survey of Vietnamese social -media users and recommends PLS -SEM as the primary analytical technique.
The resulting framework integrates cognitive, affective, social and cu ltural mechanisms and provides a
foundation for more context-sensitive digital marketing strategies in Vietnamese tourism.

Introduction
Digital transformation has reshaped the way travellers  search for information, evaluate alternatives and
form intentions to purchase tourism-related services. Unlike conventional communication channels, social
media brings visual narratives, direct interaction, peer recommendations and immediate engagement in to
a single environment (Kaplan & Haenlein, 2010; Mangold & Faulds, 2009). This combination is especially
important in tourism because tourism services are largely intangible, experience -based and difficult to
assess before consumption. Prospective travellers therefore rely on photographs, short -form videos,
reviews, comments and influencer -generated content to reduce uncertainty and develop expectations
about experiences they have not yet consumed (Litvin et al., 2008; Xiang & Gretzel, 2010).
Viet Nam offers a particularly relevant context for examining these relationships. As the tourism sector has
recovered after COVID-19, the widespread use of Facebook, TikTok, YouTube, Instagram and online travel
communities has strengthened the role of soc ial media in shaping travel preferences. In this environment,
SMM should be viewed as more than a short-term promotional tool. It can function as a strategic capability
through which tourism organizations communicate destination value, strengthen consumer confidence
and convert online engagement into demand for tourism services.
The proposed framework responds to three main gaps in the literature. First, tourism SMM research often
examines communication elements such as online reviews, influencer credibility or entertaining content
separately, rather than integrating informational , interactive, credibility -related, cultural and hedonic
stimuli in one model. Second, the mechanisms linking exposure to SMM with purchase intention remain
incompletely explained. Trust and perceived value reflect cognitive assessments, emotional attachme nt
represents an affective reaction, while eWOM and community engagement capture social processes;
nevertheless, these mechanisms are frequently studied in isolation. Third, relatively little research has
examined whether the effects of SMM differ according to tourists' cultural orientations, socio -economic
resources, travel motives, familiarity with social platforms and previous travel experience. These issues are
especially relevant in Viet Nam, where travel decisions may also be shaped by family and group
recommendations, cultural identity and rapidly evolving platform practices.
Accordingly, this manuscript is positioned as a conceptual and methodological contribution rather than a
presentation of completed empirical evidence. It has four objectives: (1) to develop an extended S -O-R
framework linking five characteristics of SMM to  tourism purchase intention; (2) to clarify the mediating
roles of trust, perceived value, emotional attachment, social influence/eWOM and community engagement;

Literature Review And Theoretical Framework

Social media marketing in tourism services
Social media marketing refers to the deliberate use of social platforms to create and communicate value,
enable interaction and cultivate continuing relationships with customers (Li et al., 2021; Tuten & Solomon,
2018). Its importance is especially pronoun ced in tourism, where digital media can make intangible
experiences easier to imagine before consumption. Photographs, videos, livestreams, stories, virtual tours
and user -generated content help prospective visitors form preliminary impressions of destinat ions and
tourism services in advance of an actual trip or purchase (Xiang & Gretzel, 2010).
Tourism choices are also strongly information dependent. Prior to booking, travellers  typically evaluate
destination attractiveness, accommodation, itinerary options, safety, reliability and the relationship
between price and anticipated value. Social platforms can reduce search effort by placing firm -generated
messages, influencer narratives and consumer reviews within the same interactive environment. They also
amplify electronic word -of-mouth, which users may perceive as more authentic than communication
created solely by firms (Cheung & Thadani, 2012; Litvin et al., 2008). For this reason, tourism SMM is more
appropriately viewed as a bundle of interconnected digital stimuli than as a single promotional channel.
The model emphasizes five characteristics of SMM. Content quality captures the accuracy, usefulness,
completeness and visual attractiveness of tourism information (Wang & Strong, 1996). Interactivity refers
to reciprocal communication, responsiveness and t he opportunities available for users to participate in
platform-based exchanges (Liu & Shrum, 2002). Source credibility represents perceived expertise,
trustworthiness and attractiveness of firms, influencers or other users who provide tourism information
(Ohanian, 1990). Cultural relevance reflects the extent to which social -media messages correspond with
Vietnamese values, symbols, language and travel practices (Okazaki & Taylor, 2013). Perceived
entertainment describes the enjoyment, interest and emotional relief derived from SMM content (Muntinga
et al., 2011). Together, these dimensions capture the informational, relational, cultural and hedonic
qualities of digital tourism marketing.

The S-O-R paradigm and complementary theories
The S -O-R paradigm provides the main theoretical architecture for this study. Its core logic holds that
environmental stimuli influence an individual ’s internal cognitive and affective states, which then
contribute to behavioural  responses (Mehrabian & Russell, 1974). Within the proposed framework,
characteristics of SMM are treated as the stimuli. Tourists ’ cognitive evaluations, emotions and social
experiences constitute the organismic stage, while purchase intention is specified as the behavioural
response arising from those internal states.
Complementary theories are introduced only where they strengthen the explanation of particular
relationships. The Theory of Planned Behavior supports the use of purchase intention as the focal
behavioural outcome and helps interpret social influence/eWOM i n terms of normative pressure and
subjective norms (Ajzen, 1991). Uses and Gratifications theory explains why consumers actively seek
information, entertainment, social connection and self -expression through social platforms (Muntinga  et
al., 2011). Source credibility theory clarifies how users judge information providers when authenticity is
uncertain or information is abundant. Perspectives on social influence and brand communities further
illuminate the effects of peer recommendatio ns, visible group norms and participation in online
communities. Thus, S -O-R supplies the overarching structure, while the complementary perspectives
provide more specific theoretical justification for selected constructs and paths.

Clarifying social influence/eWOM and community engagement
Social influence/eWOM and community engagement are conceptually related, but they represent distinct
social mechanisms in this framework. Social influence/eWOM  captures the informational and normative
effects of reviews, recommendations, visible popularity and approval from important others. Its emphasis
is on the way other people's opinions and observable behaviour shape an individual's evaluation or
intention. Community engagement, by contrast, reflects a more sustained form of psychological and
behavioural involvement in an online tourism community, including participation, belonging, identification
and repeated contribution. In short, social influence/eWOM  concerns the persuasive effect of others'
communication, whereas community engagement reflects the degree to which a tourist becomes
relationally embedded in a shared digital community.

CONCEPTUAL MODEL AND HYPOTHESES DEVELOPMENT
The conceptual model treats five SMM characteristics as external digital stimuli and connects them with
five organismic states: trust, perceived value, emotional attachment, social influence/eWOM  and
community engagement. These internal states are expected to carry the effects of SMM forward to purchase
intention. Because tourists differ in their personal characteristics and surrounding context, the framework
also introduces cultural values, socio -economic status, travel motivation, platform familiarity and prior
travel experience as moderators of selected relationships.

SMM stimuli and organismic states
Content quality is expected to enhance both trust and perceived value. In tourism, information that is
accurate, up to date, sufficiently detailed and visually convincing can reduce uncertainty and make it easier
for consumers to assess a service before booking (Filieri et al., 2015; Wang & Strong, 1996). Useful planning
information may also signal provider competence and transparency. At the same time, higher -quality
information can increase perceived value by making expected benefits clearer while reducing the time and
effort needed to search among alternatives (Zeithaml, 1988).
Interactivity is expected to strengthen emotional attachment, social influence and community engagement.
When destination pages or tourism brands respond quickly, personalize communication and encourage
participation, travellers may feel recognized rather than simply exposed to promotional messages (Liu &
Shrum, 2002). Reciprocal exchanges of this kind can deepen emotional closeness to a destination or service
brand (Hudson et al., 2016). Interactive settings also increase the visibility of comments, reviews and peer
conversations, thereby making social influence more salient. With repeated participation over time, these
exchanges may also foster belonging and involvement, which are central elements of community
engagement (Brodie et al., 2013).
Source credibility is similarly expected to reinforce trust and perceived value. Social-media users encounter
large volumes of information whose authenticity cannot always be verified. Under such conditions,
perceived expertise and trustworthiness become i mportant signals when deciding whether tourism
information deserves attention (Ohanian, 1990). Credible sources can reduce perceived functional,
financial and psychological risk, while knowledgeable communicators may help consumers understand
service benefits more clearly and regard them as more meaningful (Lou & Yuan, 2019).
Cultural relevance is proposed to influence emotional attachment as well as social influence. Tourism
messages that incorporate familiar language, symbols, cultural references and travel practices may be more
readily recognized by Vietnamese audiences (De Mooij, 2019; Okazaki & Taylor, 2013). Such cultural
congruence can encourage empathy, cultural pride and affective closeness toward the promoted
destination or brand. In social settings where collective preferences and group travel carry weight,
culturally aligned content may also make shared expectations more visible and consequently increase peer
influence.
Perceived entertainment is expected to support both emotional attachment and community engagement.
Enjoyable videos, stories, challenges and livestreams can generate positive affect and motivate users to
spend more time interacting with tourism content (Ki m & Ko, 2012; Muntinga  et al., 2011). Entertaining
material may also lower the barrier to participation because users are generally more inclined to comment,
share or join activities when the content is engaging. Consequently, the entertainment value of SMM may
contribute to both emotional bonding and sustained participation in online communities.

Organismic states, purchase intention and boundary conditions
Trust is particularly important in tourism because consumers often reserve or purchase a service before
directly experiencing it. When a tourism provider is perceived as competent, honest and attentive to
customer interests, perceived risk can decline and booking intention can increase (Gefen et al., 2003).
Perceived value is also expected to shape intention because it represents the consumer's overall judgment
of the benefits received relative to the sacrifices required, including money, time, effort and r isk (Sweeney
& Soutar, 2001; Zeithaml, 1988).
Emotional attachment captures a relatively strong affective bond with a tourism brand or destination
(Thomson et al., 2005). Once such a bond develops, purchase intention may reflect not only a rational
service evaluation but also a desire to realize or su stain an anticipated relationship. Social influence and
eWOM can affect intention through both informational and normative mechanisms (Ajzen, 1991; Cheung
& Thadani, 2012). Positive reviews, recommendations and visible engagement may serve as social proof
and reduce uncertainty. Community engagement may further rei nforce intention by creating a sense of
belonging and repeatedly exposing members to shared experiences, advice and behavioural cues
(Algesheimer et al., 2005).
Moderation is specified for particular relationships rather than introduced as a broad contextual layer.
Cultural values are expected to strengthen the effects of cultural relevance on emotional attachment and
social influence/eWOM  because people who attach greater importance to cultural orientation may react
more strongly to culturally congruent communication. Socio-economic status may change the relationship
between perceived value and purchase intention because income, education and occupation can affect price
sensitivity and risk assessment. Travel motivation may condition the effects of content quality and
entertainment: tourists motivated by cultural exploration may place greater emphasis on informative and
culturally rich content, whereas leisure -oriented travellers may respond more strongly to entertainment.
Platform familiarity may reinforce the interactivity -community engagement relationship because
experienced users can make more effective use of interactive functions. Prior travel experience may
likewise alter the trust -intention and perceived value -intention relationships, as experienced travellers
may apply stricter evaluation criteria and rely less on surface -level social cues.

Research Methodology / Research Design

Research design and sample
The proposed empirical phase adopts a quantitative cross -sectional survey design. The target population
consists of Vietnamese consumers aged 18 years or older who use platforms such as Facebook, TikTok,
Instagram or YouTube to search for tourism information, compare services or prepare for travel. Screening
questions should verify that respondents have used social media for tourism -related information during
the preceding 12 months and have at least considered purchasing, booking or further investigating a
tourism service after exposure to relevant social-media content.
Potential respondents may be recruited through tourism-oriented Facebook groups, TikTok and Instagram
travel communities, university alumni networks, tourism -business pages and online travel forums.
Eligibility requires: (1) being at least 18 years old; (2 ) residing in Viet Nam when data are collected; (3)

Because there is no complete sampling frame for Vietnamese consumers who use social media as a tourism-

initial empirical stage. The proposed model incl udes approximately 66 observed indicators together with
multiple mediation and moderation relationships. A sample of about 500 -650 valid responses is therefore
recommended. This range is consistent with conventional observations-per-indicator guidance and should
offer sufficient statistical power for PLS-SEM, mediation analysis and multi-group comparisons.

Measurement instrument
The constructs should be measured with multi-item scales adapted from established international research
and carefully contextualized for Vietnamese tourism. For latent psychological variables, a five -point Likert
format from 1 = strongly disagree to 5 = s trongly agree is proposed. Content -quality measures may draw
on information-quality research; interactivity can build on Liu and Shrum; source credibility on Ohanian;
cultural relevance on cross -cultural advertising research; and perceived entertainment on  Uses and
Gratifications-oriented social -media studies. Trust, perceived value, emotional attachment, social
influence/eWOM, community engagement and purchase intention should likewise be operationalized with
measures that have been established in the respective literatures on online trust, consumer value, brand
attachment, eWOM, brand communities and purchase intention.
A careful adaptation procedure is necessary to achieve linguistic equivalence and content validity. The
questionnaire should first be translated into Vietnamese, independently back -translated into English and
then reviewed by experts. Specialists in tourism, marketing and research methodology can assess whether
the items are clear, culturally appropriate and conceptually aligned with the constructs they are intended
to measure. Before the main data collection, a pilot study with approximately 150 -200 respon dents is
recommended to detect wording problems and provide preliminary evidence of reliability and construct
validity.

Data analysis strategy
Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM) with SmartPLS 4 is proposed for evaluating
the model. Compared with covariance-based SEM, PLS-SEM is suitable in this study because the framework
is prediction oriented, contains many constructs and indicators, includes several mediation and
moderation effects, and can be applied when survey data do not fully meet strict distributional assumptions
(Hair et al., 2022). CB-SEM is typically more appropriate when the primary purpose is confirmatory theory
testing with emphasis on global model fit, whereas the present study focuses on developing and predicting
a relatively complex framework in an emerging-market context.
Evaluation of the measurement model should begin with indicator reliability, with outer loadings above
0.708 generally preferred. Internal consistency can be assessed using Cronbach's alpha, rho_A and

composite reliability, for which values above 0.70 and below 0.95 are commonly considered acceptable.
Convergent validity should be examined through average variance extracted (AVE), using 0.50 as the usual
minimum criterion. Discriminant validity can then be evaluated with both the Fornell-Larcker criterion and
the heterotrait-monotrait ratio (HTMT).
Common-method bias should be considered both at the questionnaire -design stage and during statistical
assessment. Procedural safeguards can include voluntary and anonymous participation, neutral item
wording, separation of predictor and outcome constructs,  variation in item ordering and an explicit
statement that there are no objectively correct answers. Statistical checks may include full-collinearity VIFs
and Harman's single -factor test as preliminary diagnostics, supplemented where feasible by a marker -
variable or common -latent-factor procedure. These steps are intended to reduce the risk that estimated
relationships are primarily artifacts of the data -collection method rather than substantive associations
among the constructs.
The structural model should be assessed through path coefficients, explained variance (R²), effect sizes (f²),
predictive relevance (Q²) and out -of-sample prediction. Bootstrapping with at least 5,000 resamples is
recommended for examining direct, indirect  and total effects. Mediation should be evaluated using the
statistical significance and confidence intervals of indirect effects. For moderation, continuous moderators
can be estimated through product-indicator or two-stage interaction procedures, whereas socio-economic
status and prior travel experience may be tested through multi -group analysis when theoretically
meaningful categories can be established.

Results And Discussion / Research Findings
Because the manuscript is conceptual and methodological, empirical findings are not yet reported. After
data collection, the analysis should follow a sequence that separates data quality, measurement quality and
structural relationships. The first stage sh ould profile the sample and document screening procedures,
missing-data handling and descriptive statistics. The second stage should evaluate the measurement model
by examining indicator loadings, reliability, convergent validity and discriminant validity.  The third stage
should test structural paths, explained variance and predictive relevance, followed by interpretation of
direct, indirect and moderated effects in relation to the S-O-R framework and its supporting theories.

If subsequent empirical evidence supports the model, the findings would suggest that SMM influences
tourism purchase intention through several concurrent mechanisms rather than through a single pathway.
Content quality and source credibility are expected t o operate mainly through trust and value -oriented
evaluations, while interactivity and entertainment may exert stronger effects through relational and
emotional processes. Cultural relevance is expected to be especially important for emotional and normative
responses in the Vietnamese setting. Conversely, weak or non -significant relationships would also be
theoretically informative because they could indicate platform -specific, segment -specific or context -
dependent boundaries to the effectiveness of SMM.
The eventual discussion should interpret each major result in relation to prior research on tourism SMM,
online trust, eWOM  and consumer engagement rather than simply restating statistical significance.
Particular attention should be paid to whether cultural relevance and peer influence are especially
prominent in Viet Nam and whether entertainment has a limited direct effect once mediating mechanisms
are considered. Such comparisons can help distinguish relationships that appear transferable across
markets from those that depend more strongly on the Vietnamese context.

Implications / Solutions / Strategic Directions
Theoretical contributions
The proposed framework makes three primary theoretical contributions. First, it brings informational,
interactive, credibility-based, cultural and hedonic SMM stimuli together within one S -O-R structure. This
broader representation addresses the tendency o f earlier research to treat SMM primarily as a narrow
promotional activity rather than as a multidimensional digital environment.
Second, the framework specifies the organismic stage in greater detail by considering trust, perceived
value, emotional attachment, social influence/eWOM and community engagement simultaneously. This
perspective recognizes that tourism purchase intention can develop through rational evaluation, emotional
connection and social processes rather than through a single type of internal response.
Third, the model incorporates cultural and individual boundary conditions, thereby adding a stronger
contextual dimension. It assumes that the effectiveness of SMM may vary across tourists according to their
values, economic circumstances, travel motivatio ns, familiarity with digital platforms and previous travel
experience.

Managerial implications
Because this manuscript proposes a conceptual-methodological model, its managerial implications should
be interpreted as propositions that require empirical verification. For tourism businesses and destination
marketing organizations in Viet Nam, the framework implies that effective SMM involves more than posting
frequently or purchasing visibility. Content should be accurate, visually persuasive and practically useful
for travel planning. Clear details concerning prices, itineraries, cancellation policies, safety and evidence of
service quality can reduce uncertainty and make the offered value easier for consumers to evaluate.
Interaction should likewise be managed as part of relationship building rather than treated only as an
operational response activity. Prompt, personalized and transparent communication may support both
trust and emotional attachment. Polls, livestream ques tion-and-answer sessions, travel storytelling and
hashtag-based campaigns can stimulate participation and help develop communities around tourism
brands or destinations.
Credibility also requires active management. Partnerships with influencers should prioritize expertise,
authenticity and audience fit rather than follower numbers alone, while user -generated content should be
curated in ways that preserve perceived authent icity without sacrificing accuracy. Cultural relevance
deserves similar attention: messages that respect local values, family or group travel patterns, cultural
pride and regional identity may create stronger emotional resonance and encourage sharing among
Vietnamese audiences.
Segmentation is therefore an important managerial consideration. Travellers motivated by cultural
discovery may respond more favourably to heritage -focused, information -rich storytelling, whereas
leisure-oriented groups may prefer immersive and entertainin g formats. More experienced travellers are
likely to expect detailed and credible information, while less experienced travellers  may depend more
heavily on social proof and community recommendations. Platform familiarity should also guide channel
execution because short videos, livestreams and interactive functions are used differently across Facebook,
TikTok, YouTube and Instagram.

Conclusion
This paper develops an extended framework for explaining how social media marketing may shape
consumers' intentions to purchase tourism services in Viet Nam. It treats content quality, interactivity,
source credibility, cultural relevance and perceived ent ertainment as SMM stimuli, and identifies trust,
perceived value, emotional attachment, social influence/eWOM and community engagement as the
principal organismic mechanisms connecting those stimuli with behavioural intention. The model further

Several limitations are inherent in the proposed design. The framework remains conceptual and
methodological until the hypothesized relationships are evaluated with empirical data. A cross -sectional
survey can identify associations but cannot fully demonst rate causality or reveal how responses evolve
over time. Self -reported purchase intention may also differ from actual booking behaviour . In addition,
purposive and snowball sampling may constrain generalizability if the final sample overrepresents highly
active social-media users or people living in urban areas.
Future research should test the framework across a wider range of tourist segments, including domestic
and international travellers, Gen Z tourists, family travellers  and older consumers. Comparisons across
TikTok, Facebook, YouTube and Instagram may reveal whether specific mechanisms depend on the
affordances of particular platforms. Later studies could also combine survey responses with behavioural
indicators such as clicks, comments, shares, saved posts, enquiries and completed bookings. Longitudinal
and experimental designs would be particularly valuable for assessing causal relationships and
determining whether SMM-induced changes in purchase intention persist over time.
