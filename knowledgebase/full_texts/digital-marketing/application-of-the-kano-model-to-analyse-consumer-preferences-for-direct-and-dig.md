context. Directand digital marketing is now an imperative toolin a business's arsenal for communicating with customers and the
promotion of products and services. This study is an analysis of consumer preference for direct and digital marketing by the

preference from one channel to another can be achieved through appropriate statistical techniques. The study aims to explore the
effects of personalisation, convenience, trust and communication quality on consumer responses. The results can be useful for
enhancing the effectiveness ofmarketing and creating strategies that market products to the customer. Theoverall findings of the

1.2StatementoftheProblem
Despite the increasing adoption of both direct and digital
marketing channels, organisations continue to face substantial
difficultiesindeterminingwhichmarketingattributesgenuinely
influence consumer satisfaction and which attributes merely
prevent dissatisfaction. This problem is intensified by the fact
that consumer expectations differ considerably across
demographic segments, channel types, and cultural contexts,
making it difficult for businesses to allocate limited marketing
resourcesinaninformedandeffectivemanner.
The present study is motivated by several specific problems.
First, many organisations invest heavily in marketing channels
without a systematic understanding of which attributes
consumers regard as essential versus which attributes create
delight. Marketing decisions are frequently based on tradition,
intuition, or competitor imitation rather than on structured
analysis of consumer expectations. Second, without a
framework such as the Kano Model, businesses struggle to
distinguish between must-be attributes that prevent
dissatisfaction and attractive attributes that create positive
satisfaction, leading to misaligned investment priorities. Third,
there is insufficient empirical evidence comparing consumer
preferencesfordirectmarketingversusdigitalmarketingwithin
a unified analytical framework, making it difficult to design
integrated marketing strategies. Fourth, marketing budgets are
often allocated disproportionately, with excessive resources
directed toward attributes that consumers regard as indifferent
while essential attributes remain under-resourced. Finally,
while the Kano Model has been applied extensively to product
development and service quality contexts, its application to the
comparative analysis of direct and digital marketing attributes
remains underexplored, particularly in emerging market
contexts.
This study addresses these problems by applying the Kano
Model to systematically classify marketing attributes, compare
consumer preferences across marketing channels, and provide
evidence-based recommendations for marketing strategy
development.

Richard L. Oliver, in his article Whence Consumer Loyalty?

Noriaki Kano, Nobuhiko Seraku, Fumio Takahashi, and
Shinichi Tsuji introduced the Kano Model in their influential
article Attractive Quality and Must-Be Quality (1984). The
model explains that different product and service attributes
have different effects on customer satisfaction. It classifies
customer requirements into five major categories: must-be,
one-dimensional or performance, attractive or delighter,
indifferent, and reverse attributes. Must-be attributes represent
basic expectations whose absence creates dissatisfaction.
Performance attributes increase satisfaction when their
performance improves. Attractive attributes can generate
unexpected satisfaction, while indifferent attributes have little
effect on satisfaction. Reverse attributes may reduce
satisfaction when they are present for certain consumers.
Appliedtodirectanddigitalmarketing,reliablecommunication
and appropriate privacy protection may represent basic
expectations; message relevance and response speed may
function as performance attributes; and useful personalised
offers may act as attractive attributes. However, these
classifications must be established through consumer responses
rather than assumed in advance. The Kano Model therefore
provides the central analytical framework for identifying and
prioritisingmarketingattributesinthepresentstudy.
2.2CriticalAnalysisofPreviousStudies
While previous studies have contributed significantly to
understandingconsumerbehaviourandmarketingeffectiveness,
several limitations remain. First, most existing studies have
examined direct marketing and digital marketing separately
rather than through a unified comparative framework. This
separation limits understanding of how consumers evaluate
attributes across channel types and how resources should be
allocated between direct and digital approaches. Second, many
studies have relied on conventional satisfaction measures that
assume a linear relationship between attribute performance and
satisfaction, without distinguishing between attributes that
prevent dissatisfaction and those that create positive
satisfaction. Third, the application of the Kano Model to
marketing communication contexts remains limited, with most
applications focusing on product development, service quality,
and manufacturing contexts. Fourth, existing research has
predominantly been conducted in Western contexts, with
limited application to emerging markets and diverse consumer
populations. Fifth, many studies have relied on small samples
or convenience sampling methods, limiting the generalisability
oftheirfindings.
2.3ResearchGap
The above authors provide valuable theoretical foundations for
understanding marketing communication, digital engagement,
customer satisfaction, and quality attributes. However, their
works address these areas from different perspectives. There is
scope for a focused empirical study that compares direct and
digital marketing within a common Kano Model framework.
The present research seeks to identify which marketing
attributes consumers consider essential, which improve
satisfaction in proportion to their performance, and which
create additional value. By collecting consumer responses and
classifying the attributes, the study can help businesses
prioritise marketing investments and develop more effective,
customer-centredcommunicationstrategies.
3.THEORETICALFRAMEWORK:THEKANOMODEL
3.1ConceptandOriginoftheKanoModel
The Kano Model was created in 1984 by Professor Noriaki
Kano and his research team to describethe connection between
product or service features and customer satisfaction. It is a
concept that can be applied to explain the relationship between
various features and customer expectations, satisfaction and
dissatisfaction. The Kano Model is different from conventional
models that assume the more customers are satisfied with a
product or service, the more the satisfaction is improved. The
Kano Modelisbasedontheconceptthattheimpacts ofvarious
feature levels on customers are not equal in all cases. This
modelis of special value in marketing research for determining
all the key expectations and features that customers value and
thatmake theproductorservice even morevaluable.TheKano

3.2PrinciplesandFeaturesoftheKanoModel
The key message behind the Kano Model is that customers'
needs do not all equally contribute to satisfaction. Some
attributes represent expectations, some enhance satisfaction
based on how they perform, but some offer added value in
waysthatwerenotanticipated.Themodelingeneralemploysa
structured questionnaire with functional and dysfunctional
questions regarding each of the attributes. Functional questions
are ones asking consumers how they feel when the feature is
there, while dysfunctional questions are ones asking how
consumers feel when the feature is not there. Responses are
evaluated in combination with each other by the use of the
Kano evaluation table, and each attribute is classified. This can
help marketers focus on customer needs, enhance marketing
efforts,andmakeefficientuseofresources.
3.3CategoriesofConsumerRequirements
The Kano Model categorises consumers' needs into five
categories. Essential features are the basic features that
consumers expect; if they aren't there, they will be unhappy.
These can encompass the right information, communication
and handling of personal data in marketing. One-dimensional
or performance attributes affect satisfaction as a function of
their performance. These could be related to the importance of
promotional messages, speed of response, and usefulness of
product information. Attractive or delighter attributes offer the
unexpected, enhancing satisfaction, whether it is valuable,
personalized offers or interactive digital experiences.
Indifferent attributes are attributes that do not have a great
impact on consumer satisfaction or dissatisfaction. Some
consumersmightnotappreciatereverseattributesliketoomany
notifications or too many promotional calls. These
classifications are based on real consumer feedback and may
differdependingonconsumergroup.
3.4 Application of the Kano Model to Direct and Digital
Marketing
The Kano Model can be used to differentiate consumer
preferences between direct marketing channels (SMS, email,
telemarketing and direct mail) and digital marketing channels
(social media, websites, mobile apps and online ads). Both can
provide clear information and a respectful tone to consumers,
and convenience, personalisation, and engagement can have
varying impacts on satisfaction across channels. For instance,
what consumers expect is accurate product details, while what
they like is personalised recommendations. Repeated unwanted
messages,ontheotherhand,canbeanattributethatisreversed.
Understanding these distinctions will help businesses tailor
their promotional strategies and ensure effective
communicationwiththeirmarketsegments.

attribute by the Kano evaluation matrix. The classifications

the customer. The satisfaction and dissatisfaction coefficients

6.FINDINGSANDDISCUSSION
6.1SummaryofMajorFindings
The study aimed at analysing consumers' preference for direct
and digital marketing using the Kano Model. The key findings
show whether consumers have different expectations,
satisfactionandpreferenceindifferentmarketingchannels.The
basic attributes like accurate product information, reliable
communication, and privacy protection were considered as
must-be attributes, while response time, relevance of the
message,andconveniencefunctionedasperformanceattributes.
Personalised promotional offers, interactive content, and
valuable recommendations going beyond consumer
expectations were classified as attractive features. Trusted
brands, the feeling of being personalised, communication,
availability and the appropriateness of the promotional
messagesallaffectconsumersatisfaction.
The demographic findings show a relatively young respondent
base, with 35% of respondents below 25 years and 30%
between 25 and 35 years. Female respondents constituted 52%
of the sample, and postgraduates represented 40% of the
educational group. Consumer awareness of marketing channels
was generally high, with 80% reporting moderate to high
awareness. Digital marketing channels were preferred by 70%
of respondents, with social media advertisements being the
most preferred option at 40%. Digital advertisements were
noticedveryoftenoroftenby55%ofrespondents.
The Kano analysis showed that accurate and reliable product
information, protection of personal information, and freedom
from excessive promotional messages were classified as must-
be attributes. Quick responses to enquiries, convenient access
to digital advertisements, and relevant product
recommendations wereclassifiedasone-dimensionalattributes.
Personalised promotional offers, interactive digital
advertisements, and choice of communication channel were
classified as attractive attributes. Regular promotional updates
wereclassifiedasanindifferentattribute.
The hypothesis testing revealed that digital advertising
exposure and trust are significantly related to purchase
intention,whileagegroup was notsignificantly associated with
preferred marketing channel. Purchase intention did not differ
significantlyacrossagegroups.
6.2DiscussionofFindings
The findings of this study provide important insights into
consumer preferences for direct and digital marketing. The
classification of accurate product information and protection of
personal information as must-be attributes indicates that
consumers consider these basic requirements whose absence
would cause significant dissatisfaction. This finding is
consistent with the work of Kotler and Keller (2016), who
emphasised the importance of delivering value and building
trust in customer relationships. Organisations must ensure
consistent delivery of these basic requirements to avoid
customerdissatisfaction.
The classification of personalised promotional offers and
interactive digital advertisements as attractive attributes
suggests that these features create unexpected delight and
additional satisfaction. This finding aligns with the work of
Chaffey and Ellis-Chadwick (2019), who discussed the
potential of digital channels to provide personalised messages
and interactive experiences.These attributes offer opportunities
for competitive differentiation and can be used to exceed
customerexpectations.
The classification of quick responses to enquiries and relevant
product recommendations as one-dimensional attributes
indicates that consumer satisfaction increases proportionally
with improved performance in these areas. This finding
supports the Kano Model's proposition that performance
attributes have a linear relationship with satisfaction.
Organisations should invest in improving these attributes to
enhancecustomersatisfaction.
The finding that age group is not significantly associated with
marketing channel preference suggests that consumer
preferences may be driven more by individual characteristics
such as convenience, trust, and prior experience than by
demographic factors. This finding is consistent with Oliver's
(1999)discussion ofloyalty as aprocess influenced bypositive
experiences and commitment rather than demographic
characteristics.
The significant relationships between digital advertising
exposure,trust,and purchaseintentionhighlight theimportance
of trust in consumer decision-making. This finding aligns with
the broader marketing literature on the roleof trust in customer
relationshipsandsupportstheworkofMorganandHunt(1994)
onthecommitment-trusttheoryofrelationshipmarketing.
6.3ComparisonwithPreviousStudies
The findings of this study are broadly consistent with previous
research on consumer behaviour and the Kano Model. The
classification of basic requirements as must-be attributes is
consistent with the original Kano et al. (1984) framework and
subsequent applications of the model in service quality and
product development contexts. The finding that personalised
offers and interactive features create satisfaction is consistent
with studies on digital marketing effectiveness that have
emphasisedtheimportanceofpersonalisationandengagement.
However, unlike some previous studies that have reported
significant demographic differences in marketing preferences,

6.4ImplicationsofFindings
The results can assist companies prioritise vital marketing
needs, enhance customer interaction, limit unwanted promotion
messages,andcreatecombinedmarketingstrategies.Ingeneral,
the application of the Kano Model can be used to aid the
decision-making process of consumers, making marketing
moreeffective,andincreasingcustomersatisfactionandloyalty.
For practitioners, the findings suggest that organisations should
ensure consistent delivery of must-be attributes such as
accurate information and privacy protection to prevent
dissatisfaction. They should also invest in performance
attributes such as response speed and recommendation
relevance to enhance satisfaction. Additionally, they can use
attractive attributes such as personalised offers and interactive
features to create delight and differentiate from competitors.
Excessive promotional updates, classified as indifferent, do not
significantly affect satisfaction and should not be
overemphasisedinmarketingstrategies.
7.SUGGESTIONSANDMANAGERIAL
IMPLICATIONS
The key in direct marketing is to enhance personalisation by
delivering the right message, offer and product information to
consumers based on their preferences. A digital marketing
approach should focus on the customers, be interactive and
user-friendly, and allow promotional material to be useful to
customersandactivelyengagethem.
RecommendationsBasedonKanoClassifications:
1. Must-Be Attributes: Organisations must ensure that
all must-be attributes, including accurate and reliable
product information, protection of personal
information, and freedom from excessive promotional
messages, are consistently provided. Failure to do so
will result in customer dissatisfaction. Privacy
protection, in particular, has become increasingly
important in the digital age and should be treated as a
fundamentalrequirement.
2. Performance Attributes: Investment in performance
attributes such as quick responses to enquiries,
convenient access to digital advertisements, and
relevant product recommendations should be
proportional to their impact on satisfaction. These
attributes offer opportunities for competitive
differentiationandshouldbecontinuouslyimproved.
3. Attractive Attributes: Organisations should consider
investing in attractive attributes such as personalised
promotional offers, interactive digital advertisements,
andchoiceofcommunicationchannel tocreatedelight
and exceed customer expectations. These attributes
provide opportunities to stand out in competitive
markets.
4. Indifferent Attributes: Resources allocated to
indifferent attributes such as regular promotional
updates should be reviewed, as they do not
significantly affect satisfaction. Organisations should
avoid excessive emphasis on the frequency of
promotionalmessages.
To build consumer confidence, organisations need to be
transparent, ensure that consumers' data is protected, seek
consent and limit promotional messages. Combining direct and
digital marketing channels can enhance customer experience,
ensure consistent communication and help businesses reach a
varietyofconsumers.
When it comes to marketing investments, the Kano Model can
beusedtodeterminemust-befeaturesthatcausedissatisfaction,
performance features that enhance the satisfaction level and
attractive features that add value. Customer feedback should be
gathered and analyzed on a regular basis to better understand
consumer preferences and enhance marketing efforts. In
addition, companies can measure the success of their
campaigns by customer satisfaction, engagement, response
rates, and retention metrics. The tips provided can assist
marketing professionals make wise resource allocation
decisions, enhance customer relationships, build customer
loyalty, and create sustainable marketing strategies in a highly
competitivebusinesslandscape.
8.LIMITATIONSANDFUTURERESEARCH
8.1LimitationsoftheStudy
There are some drawbacks to the study that should be
acknowledged. The results could be affected by the size of the
sample, the sampling method, the geographical scope, the
demographic profile and the marketing experience of the
sample population. When convenience sampling is employed,
the results obtained can pose problems in terms of being
representative of the entire consumer population. The study is
limited to consumers with experience of direct and digital
marketing in the selected study area, and the findings may not
begeneralisabletootherpopulationsorcontexts.
Additionally, consumer preferences can shift over time due to
advancements in technology, shifts in market conditions, and
changing expectations. The cross-sectional nature of the study
providesasnapshotofconsumerpreferencesatasinglepointin
time and may not capture changes over time. The study relies
on self-reported data, which may be subject to social
desirability bias or recall bias. The Kano classifications are
based on consumer perceptions at the time of the survey and

may change as consumers gain experience with different
marketingchannels.
8.2DirectionsforFutureResearch
Future studies could encompass the use of larger and more
varied samples, comparisons of the results across geographic
regions and industries, and the study of the effects of age,
income,educationanddigitalliteracyonmarketingpreferences.
Longitudinal studies could track changes in consumer
preferences over time and identify trends in the adoption of
directanddigitalmarketingchannels.
The Kano Modelcan also beused alongside statistical methods
orothercustomersatisfactionmodelsforanalysistounderstand
consumer behavior. Future research could examine the
integration of Kano classifications with other analytical
approaches such as importance-performance analysis or the
SERVQUAL model. Comparative studies across different
industries and marketing contexts would provide insights into
whether Kano classifications are consistent across contexts.
Experimental designs could test the causal effects of specific
marketing attributes on consumer satisfaction and purchase
intention.
