DIGITAL MARKETING STRATEGIES AS A TOOL TO IMPROVE MANAGEMENT
EFFECTIVENESS OF FDI-BACKED MANUFACTURING ENTERPRISES

Yuriy Vorobyov, PhD student, Department of Economics and Management , Ukrainian State
University of Science and Technologies, Lazaryana, 2, Dnipro, 49010, Ukraine ,
https://orcid.org/0009-0001-5875-5268, e-mail: yurka48@yahoo.com

Abstract. Foreign direct investment (FDI)- backed manufacturing enterprises operate under
higher transparency, reporting and performance requirements, while their B2B customers
increasingly buy through s elf-directed, data -driven and omnichannel journeys. This chapter
substantiates digital marketing strategies as a managerial tool that improves margin, sales- cycle
velocity, customer retention and commercial -process transparency. A reproducible mixed -method
research design is proposed, combining secondary data analysis on FDI and digitalisation,
structured benchmarking of international B2B practices, and multiple -case analysis. The study
develops an integration model linking strategy, processes, data governa nce, people/competencies
and technology stack; a KPI matrix with explicit calculation rules (ROMI, CAC, LTV, MQL→SQL
conversion, pipeline velocity); a five -level maturity model; and a change -management roadmap
aligned with global approaches (Balanced Scorecard, ABM, Revenue Operations, ISO 9001 process
approach, Kotter and ADKAR). Practical scenarios and examples for metallurgy, machinery and
chemical production are provided to support implementation.
Keywords: digital marketing; manufacturing; foreign direct investment; B2B; KPIs; ABM;
Revenue Operations; change management.

Introduction. FDI-backed manufacturers compete simultaneously for customers and capital:
investors expect measurable returns and governance discipline, while customers expect a seamless
digital experience comparable to B2C. Global FDI flows remained volatile: UNCTAD reports a 2%
decline to USD 1.3 trillion in 2023, followed by an 11% fall to USD 1.5 trillion in 2024 (headline
figure affected by conduit economies). [1- 2] At the same time, McKinsey’s B2B Pulse Survey
indicates that buyers use around ten interaction channels during the purchasing journey- roughly
double the 2016 level -making omnichannel capability a core growth factor. [4] For manufacturers
with complex products, long sales cycles and after -sales revenue streams, digital marketing strategy
is therefore not a communication add- on but part of the management system that shapes demand
generation, partner enablement, service profitability and data quality. The purpose of this chapter is
to develop scientific and practical provisions for integ rating digital marketing strategies into the
management system of FDI -backed manufacturing enterprises through an integration model, a KPI
framework, and a change -management mechanism, benchmarked against international approaches
and illustrated with implementation scenarios.
Theoretical foundations and research gap . In the industrial B2B context, a digital
marketing strategy is defined here as a set of decisions regarding target markets, value proposition,
digital interaction channels, data architecture an d platform stack that jointly enable the enterprise to
achieve management objectives (growth, profitability, resilience, compliance). Unlike a tactical
“channel plan”, a strategic approach requires: alignment with corporate strategy and product-market
portfolio; primacy of first -party data and governance (quality, consent, security, cross -border
flows); integration of marketing and sales into a single revenue engine (Revenue Operations). [5 -7]
However, many manufacturing firms -especially those with FDI- still manage marketing as a cost
centre, which limits evidence -based investment decisions and weakens accountability. The gap
addressed in this chapter is methodological: enterprises need an operationally reproducible way to
link digital marketing activities with management outcomes using transparent KPIs and governance
rules that are acceptable for corporate headquarters and investors.
Research methodology . A mixed -method design is applied to ensure both empirical
grounding and practical reproducibility. The m ethodology consists of four stages:  Stage 1.
Secondary data analysis. We analyse international datasets and analytical reports on FDI dynamics
and digital business transformation to set the macro- context and to derive constraints typical for
FDI-backed fir ms (reporting discipline, compliance, cross -border governance). [1- 3] Stage 2.
Structured practice review. We conduct a structured review of international B2B digital marketing
practices (omnichannel, ABM, marketing automation, RevOps, genAI enablement) and extract
repeatable process patterns and capability components. [4- 6] Stage 3. Constructive modelling.
Based on Stages 1 -2, we build artefacts that can be implemented in companies: an integration
model, KPI matrix, maturity model and change roadmap. KPI f ormulas are specified to enable
auditability and comparability. Stage 4. Multiple-case validation. We validate the artefacts through
multiple-case logic (three scenario classes) and triangulation: comparing KPI behaviour, process
maps and governance rules across scenarios; checking coherence with established management
frameworks (BSC, ISO 9001 process approach) and change models (Kotter, ADKAR). [7- 10]

Limitations.The proposed framework is designed for managerial reproducibility; however,
several limitations should be acknowledged:
• Causality: observed KPI changes may be influenced by parallel initiatives (pricing, product
updates, salesforce changes). To reduce  bias, pilots should freeze non- marketing variables where
possible or document changes explicitly.
• Data availability: enterprises with low CRM discipline or fragmented ERP structures may
require a preliminary data-cleaning stage before KPI computations become reliable.
• Attribution uncertainty: multi- touch attribution in long industrial cycles remains
approximate; therefore, the framework recommends reporting both ‘created’ pipeline and
‘influenced’ pipeline using stable windows.
• External validity: thresholds and expected effects vary by segment, geography and business
model (direct sales vs distributor); calibration is required prior to scaling.
Operational research design: instruments, measurement plan, and reproducibility
To satisfy the criterion of a “detailed methodology”, this section specifies research
instruments, measurement procedures, and reproducibility rules that can be applied by researchers
and practitioners. The chapter operationalises the mixed- method design through three instrument
families:  documentary instrument (structured extraction forms for secondary sources), process
instrument (standardised process maps and checklists), and measurement instrument (KPI
dictionary, data quality tests, and attribution rules). [3, 5, 6]
Instrument I: Secondary -source extraction form. For each eligible report or article, the
following fields are extracted: context (industry, geography, firm size); digital capability addressed
(channels, automation, analytics, RevOps); outcome variables (r evenue, margin, cycle time,
retention, governance); measurement approach (survey, experiment, case study, panel data);
limitations and threats to validity. The extraction form includes a “replicability flag” (0/1)
indicating whether the source provides suf ficient methodological detail for reproduction. [12, 18,
19]
Instrument II: Process mapping and interface checklist. Each scenario class (A -C) is
documented using a unified BPMN -lite template comprising: customer journey stages, internal

checklist is applied to verify that handoffs between marketing, sales, service and finance are explicit
and measured: MQL/SQL definitions, response SLAs, quote standards, mar gin visibility, and
service-to-upsell triggers. [9-10, 14-15]

controlled document. For every KPI the dictionary defines: KPI purpose (management decision
supported), f ormula, measurement frequency, owner, system of record, inclusion/exclusion rules,

recommended: v1.0, v1.1…) so that any metric definition changes are vi sible to auditors and to
headquarters. [5-7, 10]
Measurement plan (cadence). Weekly cadence is recommended for operational KPIs
(response SLA, lead follow -up, pipeline hygiene), monthly cadence for commercial performance

segment, partner contribution, maturity level). All reports should include both absolute values and
deltas vs baseline, with explanation of major changes and an “attribution integrity note”. [19]

stakeholders participate, and several channels influence the decision. Therefore, the chapter

requirements.
Created vs influenced pipeline. Two parallel indicators should be reported:  Marketing-created
pipeline: opportuniti es whose first recorded touchpoint is a marketing- driven conversion (e.g.,

Economic evaluation and budgeting logic for digital marketing transformation . For FDI-

based on incremental gross profit and risk- adjusted value. The ec onomic evaluation can be
structured into three layers:

wins (created pipeline) and incremental service gross profit from lifecycle programmes. This layer
supports ROMI calculations and quarterly budget reviews. [5-6]

Implication 2: Management control should include governance KPIs, not only growth KPIs. A

Implication 3: Organisational design matters as much as technology. Empirical research on
digital transformation suggests that technology adoption without organisational routines leads to
limited performance effects. [18] In manufacturing, sales and service routines are often entrenched;
thus, the transformation should begin with interface clarity (what marketing hands to sales; what
sales returns; what serv ice triggers). The SLA/RACI templates and pilot checklists are designed to
structure these routines and to provide evidence for learning across business units. [9-12]
Implication 4: Performance effects are mediated by sales -cycle structure. In long- cycle capex
markets, marketing effects may appear first in upstream indicators (buying -group engagement,
meeting rates) and only later in revenue. Therefore, governance requires a staged KPI logic: leading
indicators (engagement, coverage) support early diagnosis , while outcome indicators (velocity,
ROMI) are evaluated with longer windows. In short -cycle service/parts markets, ROMI and margin
effects can be observed earlier. This boundary condition should be stated in pilot reports to avoid
premature rejection of long-cycle initiatives. [19, 24]
Practical sensitivity analysis for ROMI. ROMI is sensitive to attribution assumptions and
gross profit estimation. To reduce disagreement with finance and headquarters, the chapter
recommends a three-scenario ROMI computation:
- Conservative (created-only): count only deals where marketing created the opportunity.
- Moderate (created + partial influence): apply a fixed influence weight (e.g., 20%) to
influenced deals.
- Upper bound (influenced): report influenced pipeline separately, not as ROMI, or compute
an "influence ROMI" clearly labelled.
This approach provides a transparent range rather than a single contested number. [5, 19]

personal data (business emails, job titles) and potentially sensitive technical information. Ethics and
compliance require: privacy -by-design, minimal necessary data collection, clear opt -out
mechanisms, and secure storage. For genAI, ethics includes avoiding  disclosure of confidential
information and ensuring that generated content does not misrepresent technical safety properties.
These requirements can be embedded into approval workflows and governance checklists. [20-23]

Comparative analysis with global developments . Comparative analysis with similar global
developments in industrial digital marketing and management systems.
International practice and research converge on several developments that frame this chapter's
propositions. First, the shift toward omnichannel B2B purchasing has increased the importance of
self-service, digital touchpoints, and remote decision making. Surveys and managerial analyses
report that B2B buyers increasingly combine digital and human interactions, and expect
transparency comparable to B2C experiences. [4 -5] For manufacturing subsidiaries, this
development implies that marketing strategy is inseparable from commercial process design: digital
assets must reduce information asymmetry, accelerate qualification, and support proposal
development.
Second, account -centric governance has become more prominent. ABM and buying- group
thinking recognise that in complex industrial purchases, the "unit of analysis" is not an individual
lead but an account and a set of stakeholders. This shifts measurement from lead volume to account
coverage, engagement, and progression. The chapter aligns with this development by introducing
buying-group coverage metrics and by embedding ABM  plays into RevOps interfaces rather than
treating ABM as a standalone marketing tactic. [8-9]
Third, the revenue -operations movement provides an organisational answer to fragmented
responsibilities. Instead of separate marketing and sales optimisation, RevOps emphasises
integrated processes, shared metrics, and data governance. While the concept is widely discussed in
practice, manufacturing contexts introduce distinctive constraints: long quotation cycles, complex
configuration, margin sensitivity, and a strong service component. The chapter adapts RevOps to
these constraints by formalising interfaces across lead -to-order, order -to-cash, and issue- to-
resolution, and by extending the KPI system to include quote-to-order conversion and service attach
rate. [9-10, 14-15]
Fourth, quality -management and information- security standards are increasingly used to
formalise commercial and data processes. ISO 9001 provides a process approach suitable for audit
evidence, while ISO/IEC 27001 strengthens information secur ity governance. This chapter's
governance checklists and evidence requirements are aligned with these standards, which is
particularly relevant for FDI-backed subsidiaries where headquarters and investors expect auditable
controls. [10-11]
Finally, emerging AI adoption creates both opportunities and governance requirements. While
AI can improve productivity and decision support, international guidance stresses risk management
and accountability. The chapter therefore proposes governance -by-design for genAI in industrial
marketing, consistent with risk-management frameworks and privacy requirements. [23]
Overall, the comparative analysis indicates that the chapter's artefacts are consistent with
global developments but contribute additional operational specificity for FDI-backed manufacturing
enterprises: explicit KPI formulas and thresholds, controlled attribution, governance KPIs, and
scenario playbooks tailored to metallurgy, machinery and chemical production.

Managerial recommendations . Managerial recommen dations (synthesised).
Recommendation 1: Start with governance, not with channels. For subsidiaries under FDI, the first
milestone should be a "measurement -ready" commercial system: CRM discipline, consent records,
and an approved KPI dictionary. This prevents later disputes and enables consistent reporting across
business units. [10, 20-22]
Recommendation 2: Choose one scenario for the first 4-8 months and execute it deeply. Overly
broad transformations dilute evidence and create change fatigue. Scenario C (service lifecycle) often
yields faster measurable returns; Scenario A (capex ABM) requires longer windows but can produce
strategic wins. Scenario choice should be justified by baseline diagnostics and business priorities. [4-
6]
Recommendation 3: Use a steering cadence with clear artefacts. Weekly operational reviews
should focus on SLA compliance, data quality, and early leading indicators. Monthly reviews
should focus on pipeline hygiene and velocity. Quarterly reviews should focus on ROMI and
strategic KPIs, with dictionary version control and documented changes. This cadence embeds
digital marketing into management routines. [7, 9-10]
Recommendation 4: Integrate finance early. ROMI and margin -linked KPIs require finance
agreement on gross profit computation, cost coding, and attribution rules. Establish a joint finance -
RevOps governance mechanism to avoid retrospective disagreements and to ensure that KPI outputs
are investor-grade. [5-6]
Recommendation 5: Treat content as an engineering asset. In industrial markets, content is not
decoration; it is an operational asset that reduces risk and speeds decisions (specifications,
compliance documentation, ROI cases). Content production should follow quality controls and be
embedded into sales enablement and service knowledge bases. [14-15, 18]
Recommendation 6: Adopt AI with controlled boundaries. AI use cases should be catalogued
by risk, with enterprise -grade controls and human approval for customer -facing outputs. This
preserves confidentiality and compliance while enabling productivity improvements. [23]
Data analysis procedures. Data analysis procedures and reporting format. Secondary data
analysis procedures. Quantitative secondary indicators (FDI trends, digitalisation indices, market
growth proxies) should be analysed using descriptive statistics and trend comparison across years.
Because the chapter aims at managerial applicability rather than econometric identification, the
analysis focuses on establishing context and constraints for subsidiaries : volatility of investment,
need for transparency, and adoption of digital interactions. When multiple sources provide similar
indicators, triangulation is applied: values are compared, and discrepancies are recorded in a data
log. [1-3]
Case validation an alysis procedures. For each scenario case, analysis follows a common
structure:
1) Context description: product, market, sales model, baseline maturity.
2) Intervention description: process change, assets, technology, governance controls.
3) Measurement extraction: KPI exports from systems of record with timestamps.
4) Computation: KPI formulas applied without manual corrections except documented data
cleaning.
5) Interpretation: compare deltas vs baseline and, where feasible, vs control group; evaluate
governance KPIs in parallel.
6) Explanation: qualitative feedback from sales/service teams used to interpret mechanisms
(e.g., cycle-time reduction due to better qualification).
7) Documentation: pilot evidence package created, signed off by steering committee.

Future research agenda . Future empirical agenda and opportunities for further refinement.
To deepen scientific contribution, further empirical work can address three directions. First,
measurement models can be validated on larger samples of subsidiaries across countries, allowing
statistical testing of relationships between maturity levels and performance outcomes (velocity,
margin, retention) while controlling for industry and market conditions. [12, 18] Second, attribution
models specific to industrial purchasing can be improved by co mbining CRM outcomes with
buying-group interaction patterns and contract data, and by validating touchpoint weights through
expert panels and post -decision interviews. [19] Third, the governance dimension can be linked to
risk outcomes by tracking incident s (privacy, security, brand compliance) and estimating expected-
loss reduction due to implemented controls. [20-23]
In addition, the integration of sustainability and ESG information into digital value
propositions deserves attention, particularly for meta llurgy and chemicals where compliance and
traceability influence partner selection and customer trust. Finally, as AI- enabled tools evolve,
research should compare productivity and quality outcomes across different governance
configurations (human-in-the-loop vs automated), ensuring that efficiency gains do not compromise
compliance or technical accuracy. [23]
Summary of contribution.
The chapter contributes by integrating three lines of knowledge that are often treated
separately: B2B digital marketing and  buying-group engagement, management control systems and
KPI accountability, and governance requirements typical for FDI -backed subsidiaries (audit
evidence, privacy, security). The integration is operationalised through reproducible artefacts: a
maturity model, scenario playbooks, SLA/RACI templates, governance checklists, a KPI dictionary
with attribution rules, and pilot evidence packages. This combination enables both practical
implementation and scientific replication, because it clarifies what exactly  is measured, how it is
computed from systems of record, and how organisational routines ensure data integrity. In
addition, the chapter provides boundary conditions for interpreting results in long- cycle and short -
cycle industrial markets, preventing simplistic transfer of B2C measurement assumptions into
manufacturing. These contributions can serve as a methodological baseline for subsequent empirical
studies on subsidiary performance and digital commercial transformation.
Practical note on documentation discipline. In subsidiaries with multilingual environments,
documentation should be standardised in one corporate language for auditability, while customer -
facing materials may be localised. All controlled documents (KPI dictionary, playbooks, consent
policy, SLAs) should have owners and version histories. Such discipline is not bureaucracy; it is the
mechanism that allows learning and scaling across business units and protects the organisation
when staff rotate or when headquarters require evidence. [10]

Note on transferability. Although the playbooks are tailored to manufacturing, the

Conclusions
1) Digital marketing strategies for FDI- backed manufacturing enterprises should be treated as
a management subsystem because they affect margin, sales- cycle velocity, customer retention and
commercial-process transparency.
2) A reproducible methodology is proposed that combines secondary data analysis,
international benchmarking and constructive modelling, producing implementable artefacts: KPI
matrix (with formulas), maturity model and change roadmap.
3) Benchmarking demonstrates compatibility with established international frameworks
(BSC, ABM, RevOps, ISO 9001, Kotter/ADKAR) while adding FDI -specific dimensions: cross-
border data governance, compliance evidence, and investor-grade KPI discipline.
4) Three implementation scenarios provide concrete process steps and KPI targets that can be
adapted to metallurgy, machinery and chemical production, enabling gradual capability building
without compromising governance.
