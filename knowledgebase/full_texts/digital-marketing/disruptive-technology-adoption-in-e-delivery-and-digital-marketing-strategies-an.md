Abstract
This study examines the relationship between the adoption of selected disruptive digital technologies
operationalised as e-delivery adoption and digital marketing strategies and the performance of small and
medium-sizedenterprises(SMEs)inPortHarcourtMetropolis, RiversState,Nigeria.Thestudyfocusesonthese
two dimensions because they represent customer-facing applications that directly connect market access, order
fulfilment, customer engagement, and business-process outcomes. Other technologies, including cloud
computing, artificial intelligence, and digital payment systems, are recognised as important enablers of SME
digital transformation but are treated as contextual technologies rather than separate predictor variables in this
study. A cross-sectional survey research design was employed, targeting a population of 1,200 registered SMEs
in Port Harcourt. Stratified random sampling was used to select a sample of 250 respondents. Of the 250
questionnaires distributed, 215 were returned and used for analysis. Spearman’s rank-order correlation
coefficient was used for data analysis through SPSS version 22. The findings revealed significant positive
relationships between e-delivery adoption and business growth, and between digital marketing strategies and
operational efficiency. The study concludes that the selected customer-facing dimensions of disruptive
technologyadoptionareassociatedwithimprovedSMEperformanceinPortHarcourt.

shaped by digital commerce, online platforms, social commerce, artificial intelligence, cloud
services, digital payments, and data-driven decision-making. Digital transformation can help
SMEs overcome size-related limitations by widening market access, improving coordination,
reducing transaction costs, and strengthening customer relationships. However, SMEs do not
adopt all technologies at the same rate or for the same purpose. The OECD (2021) notes that

2.0 Review of Related Literature
Recent scholarship presents SME digital transformation as a multidimensional process rather
than the simple acquisition of isolated technologies. It includes changes in business models,
customer relationships, internal processes, data use, skills, and organisational capabilities.
The OECD (2021) identifies online platforms, artificial intelligence, blockchain ecosystems,
digital security, and cloud-based services as important elements of the changing SME
environment. Thereport also emphasisesthat SMEsfacebarriers includinglimited resources,
inadequate digital skills, security concerns, and unequal access to infrastructure. These
constraints are particularly important in developing-country contexts, where the potential
benefits of digitalisation coexist with unreliable connectivity, high costs, and uneven
institutionalsupport.
Digital commerce has expanded the opportunities available to SMEs by enabling them to
display products, receive orders, communicate with customers, and coordinate delivery
through online channels. Platform economies are important in this process because platforms
connect SMEs to customers and complementary service providers without requiring each
enterprise to develop a complete digital infrastructure independently. However, platform
participation may also create dependence on platform rules, commissions, algorithms, data
practices, and changing visibility conditions. Consequently, SME performance depends not
only on adoption but also on the ability to use platforms strategically and manage the
associatedrisks.
Social commerce represents another recent development. It combines social interaction,
product discovery, promotion, customer reviews, and transaction-related activities through
social-media platforms. For SMEs, social commerce can lower promotional barriers and
facilitate direct engagement with niche customer groups. It also creates a closer relationship
between digital marketing and e-delivery because customers may discover a product,
communicate with the seller, place an order, and arrange fulfilment within an integrated
digital journey. Recent research describes social media, mobile applications, artificial
intelligence, and related technologies as significant components of contemporary digital
transformation(Almeidaetal.,2024).

included 1,200 registered SMEs in Port Harcourt; which Agwu and Emeti (2014) report that

used to select a sample size of 250. Data was collected through structured surveys, and
secondary data was obtained from trade association and government reports. Of the 250
respondents, questionnaires distributed and 215 were returned, resulting in an 86% response
rate and used for analysis. The data was analyzed using Spearman’s Rank Order Correlation
