ism encompasses more than simply experiencing agricultural products and farm life and

Abstract
The aim of the study is to investigate the implementation of digital marketing tools
for the promotion of farmhouses in rural areas. Based on a case study of a small, rural
and remote Italian region, the study assesses the quality of websites and social media
and the level of digitalization maturity of farmhouses, and performs a segmentation
analysis of their digital marketing approach. The results of the web content analysis
suggest that the websites appear unprofessional in terms of both content and visual
aspects and require improvement in several areas. In terms of digitalization maturity,
online tools of farmhouses are at an early stage of development and require significant
effort to progress to next stages. Regarding the utilization of social media, it emerges
a limited engagement with this medium, thereby missing opportunities to interact
with and engage customers, and consequently influence their booking decisions.
The segmentation analysis reveals four distinct profiles of digital marketing approach
of farmhouses, of which two are exclusively based on social media, while the remain-
ing two employ a comprehensive array of digital tools. These profiles show remarkable
differences in performance levels. Finally, the utilization of digital tools appears to be
more farm-centered than territory-centered, in that websites and social media pay little
attention to the rural territory and its natural, traditional and cultural resources. Based
on results from the case study, strong interventions and support policies are needed
at the level of individual organizations and the entire system of rural actors to promote
the development of farmhouses in rural areas and the vitality of these areas based
on the agritourism offer.

The agritourism market is expected to experience substantial growth worldwide in the
coming years (Grand View Research 2024). Europe has a dominant position in the global
agritourism market (Fortune Business Insights 2024). In the Mediterranean region, agri -
tourism is considered a promising strategy for transforming agrifood systems into more
resilient, inclusive and sustainable models from economic, social and environmental
perspectives (Partalidou and De Matteis 2024). Italy has a well-established and success -
ful agritourism sector, as evidenced by the substantial growth of farmhouses over the
past few decades (ISTAT 2024).
To achieve success along this trajectory, agritourism is required to undergo transfor -
mations that offer opportunities but also pose challenges. Among these transformations,
the digital transformation, defined as the integration of technology into business oper -
ations, has become increasingly important for firms to embrace new opportunities by
leveraging the digital environment. Digitalization serves as the path toward digital trans-
formation and refers to the use of digital tools and technologies to improve the efficiency
and accessibility of goods and services (Kannan 2017).
In the context of marketing communication strategies, websites and social media are
currently employed by a significant number of organizations. These tools fulfill several
functions: a communication function, which enables the reaching of both domestic and
international customers; an information function, which presents the facilities and ser -
vices offered; furthermore, they can be used as image strategy tools and offer a direct
sales channel. Digital communication tools have become pervasive in the tourism sector
and its diverse offerings (Cicha and Rutecka 2024). Moreover, according to Kumar and
Shekhar (2020), technological advancements have transformed the way rural tourism is
conducted.
The purpose of this study is to investigate the implementation of digital marketing
tools among farmhouses located in a rural region. In particular, the study assesses the
quality of websites and social media and the level of maturity of the digitalization of
farmhouses located in a rural and remote context and identifies the digital profiles of
farmhouses based on the strategies they put into place on the digital market.
To fulfill study aims, a regional case study approach was adopted, and a quality assess -
ment of the digital marketing tool implementation among farmhouses located in an Ital-
ian region was implemented by combining different evaluation models and adopting a
farm perspective. The research uses the “case study” as a method which is applied in eco-
nomics to understand complex phenomena when the structure of a given sector or the
economy of a city or region is being investigated (Yin 2018); in this study, it is intended
more as an instrumental case study to facilitate the understanding of the research issue
(Ridder 2017).
A strength point of the study is the integrated approach to the digitalization of farm -
houses and their online marketing strategy based on the joint assessment of the quality
of both websites and social media in terms of their content, level of maturity and techni -
cal performances.
Based on the case study, some general strength/weakness points and suggestions to
further develop digital communication strategy among farmhouses are drawn to support

Literature background
Digital marketing tools in the agritourism sector
In the context of the agricultural sector, a substantial body of literature has emerged
investigating the role of websites and social media in relation to the wine industry
(Canovi and Pucciarelli 2019; Finotto and Mauracher 2020; Fontes et  al. 2024; Galati
et  al. 2019). Other studies addressed different topics, such as the online presence of
SMEs in the agrifood sector (Checchinato et al. 2024), the direct marketing of agricul -
tural products (Dreist et al. 2025), and the digital strategies of agrifood cooperatives and
collective organizations (Ciliberti et al. 2024). A rich body of literature has also emerged
in relation to wine, olive oil and cheese tourism (Fusté-Forné and Filimon 2025; Salvado
and Monteiro 2024; Vîlcea et al. 2024).
Digital communication tools play a key role in promoting facilities and attracting visi -
tors, especially for less known regional contexts that are on the fringe of the tourism
flow in a country. This role is even more important for geographically dispersed firms
located in rural peripheral areas or in lagging regions marginalized from the main tour -
ist flows that could take more advantages of digital visibility and enter in a digital rela -
tionship with their potential customers and guests.
As regards agritourism, the valorization of farmhouses through websites represents a
central topic in the relevant literature and is considered one of the most effective ways
to promote such firms. The utilization of a website for a farmhouse not only enhances its
visibility but also facilitates the differentiation of its offering, improves communication
with customers and enables direct management of bookings. A well-structured website
has the potential to enhance accessibility, communication and customer trust, thereby
influencing booking decisions and increasing the likelihood of success for a farmhouse.
Social media marketing allows to showcase agritourism experiences and builds an online
community around the brand. Online booking systems are also used to make reserva -
tions, and e-commerce platforms are used to sell local products.
Digital tools may contribute enhancing the region’s economy and preserving tradi -
tional knowledge. Virtual tours of the region’s natural and cultural heritage may be used
to attract visitors who are interested in exploring the territory (Mutipforo et  al. 2024;
Muwani et al. 2024).
As regards Italy, to which this study refers, Ammirato (2010) conducted a survey of
farmhouses in the Calabria region to assess the functionality of their websites for pro -
motion, farm product selling and territory-related agritourism services. Platania (2014)
evaluated the websites of a sample of farmhouses in the Sicily region and analyzed the
strategic choices made by farmers. Streifeneder et  al. (2023) reviewed the websites of
seventeen high-quality farmhouses located in South Tyrol/Italy and interviewed experts
to investigate the commercialization of agricultural and touristic features of their offer
and the content of their websites. Palmisano (2020) conducted a comparative analysis of
the websites’strategies adopted by farmhouses in Italy, Germany and Austria and exam -
ined textual and visual contents from a cultural perspective.

Besides websites, social media platforms are regarded as pivotal instruments in the
formulation of a digital marketing strategy (Cristobal-Fransi et  al. 2023b). The use of
social media has become a central topic as it offers a cost-effective instrument for the
promotion of farmhouses, particularly within a competitive tourism context. Social
media are recognized as important tools for effectively pursuing several functions rel -
evant for farms, such as the construction of a brand image, the impact on decisions
to visit rural areas and stimulate emotions, the provision of personalized and targeted
information (Bowman et al. 2020). In addition, social media allows to create an image
of authenticity and sustainability, characteristics that could be highly appreciated in
agritourism facilities. Social media are fundamental tools for increasing the visibility of
farms and reaching a wide audience and potential customers who might not be reached
through traditional marketing channels, most of all in remote or inner areas (Kaplan and
Haenlein 2010). By engaging with customers on social media platforms and facilitat -
ing two-way communication, farmhouses can establish direct and continuous relation -
ships, which can result in customer loyalty and increased brand awareness (Bowman
et al. 2020). Interactions, including comments, messages and shares, have been shown
to engender a sense of community around the brand, thereby enhancing customer per -
ceptions and frequently exerting an influence on booking decisions (Xiang et al. 2022).
As Holland et al. (2022) discovered, effective social media marketing is one of the fac -
tors influencing farmers’decisions regarding the introduction or addition of agritourism-
related activities.
A fundamental aspect of online valorization of firms is the use of visual content, such
as images, videos and virtual tours, for evoking emotions and attracting tourists. The
websites and social media of farmhouses should use content that shows not only the
structure and services provided but also the surrounding natural environment and the
landscape, the experiences offered (such as excursions, tastings or agricultural activities)
and the local products, and, in general, the local history (Chan et al. 2023). In a study on
rural tourism facilities in Poland, Król and Zdonek (2020) distinguished between tra -
ditional rural tourism facilities, whose logos were emblematic of rural areas, and com -
mercialized rural tourism facilities, which were regarded as modern. Joyner et al. (2018)
explored emotional response to images used in farmhouses in West Virginia, tying the
content and subject of the photo to negative or positive feelings.
The efficacy of an online marketing strategy is contingent upon the integration of a
variety of digital tools. A website is enhanced by the support it receives on social media
(Król et al. 2019). Farmhouses may integrate their websites with social media to expand
their audience and create a digital marketing ecosystem (Leung 2019).
Despite the numerous advantages, using websites and social media also presents chal -
lenges that can prevent their adoption or limit their effectiveness. The changes brought
forth by digital transformation may create a difficult situation for farmers who do not
have access to or expertise with digital technologies, most of all when they are aged, and
for small and family farms where time is a limited resource to share with activities on
the field (Arcuri et al. 2023). Managing an online presence requires time, resources and
expertise (Katsande et al. 2024) and smaller firms may not have the staff or resources to
effectively manage social media with the daily operations of a farm, highlighting the need
for a well-planned strategy (Setiawan et al. 2025). Some authors underlined the limited

knowledge of how to use digital marketing tools and social media among agritourism
operators (Miller et al. 2021), their communications training needs (Miller et al. 2012)
and the need to give operators a support to access to resources and knowledge they

To leverage on the above advantages, face challenges and improve digital marketing
strategies, a preliminary step is to assess the use of digital tools by farms in the sector
and the quality of their websites and social media.
Methodologies for the assessment of digital marketing tools
From a methodological perspective, there is no universally accepted methodology in the
literature for carrying out the quality assessment of digital marketing tools, as has been
noted by Cristobal-Fransi et al. (2018) and Sánchez-Rivero et al. (2023).
A methodology widely applied in international literature is the web content analysis
(WCA). WCA is an evolution of the content analysis (CA) research method defined as
a"technique for the objective, systematic, and quantitative description of the manifest
content of communication"(Berelson 1952). CA has been adapted to the digital domain
as WCA for analyzing online content (Herring 2004; Liauw 2022; Neuendorf 2002). A
rich body of literature has addressed the topic under several perspectives, such as the
opportunities and challenges the application of CA to the web and social media may
have (Weare and Lin 2000; Zhang 2005); the types of CA and their several purposes
(Ghosh 2018; McMillan 2000; Tayade and Inzalkar 2024); and the differences among
WCA techniques (Wadawadagi and Pagi 2019).
Web content analysis has been employed in studies addressing different tourism
sectors. Illustrative examples include the study by Sánchez-Rivero et  al. (2023) which
measured the websites of tourism accommodation in Extremadura; the study by Cris -
tobal-Fransi et  al. (2018) and by Ramón-Cardona et  al. (2022) which applied WCA to
assess the websites of ski resorts; and the study by Cristobal-Fransi et al. (2023b) which
applied a WCA approach to health resorts.
The extended Model of Internet Commerce Adoption (eMICA) is a framework
designed to evaluate the level of maturity of websites belonging to organizations. It is
widely applied in tourism studies (Chung and Vega 2023; Ramón-Cardona et al. 2022;
Sánchez-Rivero et  al. 2023), in the accommodation sector (Ahmed and Shaker 2021)
and in health tourism (Cristobal-Fransi et al. 2023a). The eMICA model has been also
employed in studies that evaluated the quality of websites of high-quality restaurants
(Daries et  al. 2018) and of wine and olive tourism organizations (Salvado & Monteiro
2024). The adaptation of the eMICA model to the specific context of each analysis is a
common feature of these studies.
In the field of agritourism, the extant literature proposes a range of methodologies
for the evaluation of digital tools. However, these methodologies have not yet achieved
widespread utilization. Havlícek et al. (2013) assessed the quality of the websites of 60

Page 6 of 25Forleo et al. Agricultural and Food Economics           (2025) 13:62
Czech farmhouses in rural areas comparing the content, structure, design and accessi -
bility. Zopounidis et al. (2014) studied the use of websites among farmhouses in Greece
and ranked them adopting 13 different criteria (such as information, online reservation
and payment). Król (2019) used ten diagnostic variables to assess the website quality of
farmhouses in Poland. Król et al. (2019) analyzed a sample of Polish farmhouses with a
set of 35 diagnostic variables for measuring three dimensions of the web quality, includ -
ing economic and marketing indicators, functionality and website development tech -
nique. Nyahunzvi and Mutenheri (2024) assessed the effectiveness of websites of eight
farmhouses in Zimbabwe by using fifteen variables (accessibility, functionality, usability,
navigability, interactivity, quality of information, online payment capability, design and
esthetic attractiveness, value addition links and translation capabilities). Platania (2014)
evaluated the quality of websites of farmhouses in the Sicily region by using the eMICA
methodological approach.
The present study was inspired by the methodological framework—web content analy-
sis and the extended Model of Internet Commerce Adoption—employed by Ramón-Car-
dona et al. (2022) and Cristobal-Fransi et al. (2018, 2023a, 2023b) to the tourism industry
that was adapted to the investigated sector with the purpose of describing communica -
tion contents and assessing website characteristics (Wimmer and Dominick 2013). In
addition to the implementation of the aforementioned models, a technical assessment
of the websites was carried out in the present study. Furthermore, the study investigated
the quality of social media of farmhouses by considering their content, technical perfor -
mance and engagement capability.
Data and methods
The regional case study: Molise region (IT)
The Molise region is located in the central part of Italy, between the Adriatic Sea to the
east and the Apennine Mountains to the west. It is the second smallest Italian region.
The region has a population of just over 300,000 people, which makes it one of the least
populated areas in Italy. The region is characterized by its picturesque landscapes, abun -
dant natural resources, charming hilltop villages and rich cultural heritage that dates to
ancient times, with evidence of Roman and Samnite civilizations throughout the area.
Three reasons motivated the choice of Molise as a case study.
Agriculture is a significant productive sector, contributing 5.8% of the regional econ -
omy in 2022 (2.0% in Italy). Contrary to the trend of decreased added value reported
in Italy (− 4.1%), the regional agricultural sector demonstrated a substantial expansion
(25.7%) during the period 2002–2022. This development was accompanied, as in the rest
of the country, by a deep structural transformation (Banca d’Italia 2024). As in other
regions, the agricultural sector in Molise has undergone a profound evolution over the
last decades, while maintaining levels of innovation and digitalization below the national
average. Finally, due to demographic dynamics—reduced birth rate, aging and exodus—
the incidence of young entrepreneurs in agriculture is low, as well as the level of educa -
tion of farmers in the sector.
The diversification of agricultural activities in the region was comparatively lower than
that observed in Italy. In 2020, the proportion of farms engaging in related activities was
3.2% of the total (5.7% in Italy); specifically, the tourist accommodation activities were

in Molise is assuming an important role, also thanks to the region’s landscape richness

sustainable agriculture and cultural activities, leading farmhouses to evolve into multi -

The levels of innovation and digitalization in the regional sector were found to be

In this regional context, it becomes crucial for farmhouses to embrace new oppor -

fundamental role in promoting such farms and attracting visitors, both nationally and
internationally, also considering the remoteness of the region far from the main Italian
tourism routes.

Nevertheless, the Register is not immediately usable for analyzing the regional agri -

Firstly, the Register serves to officially certify the activity, reporting exclusively a gen -

Therefore, a preliminary activity has been devoted to checking the validity of the num -

online search differ from the data reported in the Register. A total of 114 units were
found, of which only 36.3% had an active website, 70.0% had a social media page, and
32.4% used both digital tools. Finally, 27.2% of units had no website or social media,
which is a percentage lower than that reported in the official Register.
The subsequent analysis was thus based on data obtained from the web search.
The website assessment models
The WCA model is used to evaluate the quality and comprehensiveness of a website
content, with a focus on aspects such as completeness, accuracy, updating and acces -
sibility of the information provided. The web content analysis applied to the websites
of farmhouses in Molise encompasses four dimensions (see Table  A.1), each of which
has its own characteristics to be evaluated. For each dimension, assessment criteria have
been identified, and items adapted to the agritourism context have been defined for col -
lection. (The list of items is reported in Table A.2.)
The assessment of the technical performance of the websites was added in this study to
get a wider picture. A technical analysis is essential to assess structural and performance
aspects that affect the usability, user experience and online visibility of a website. This
analysis was structured in four sections reporting the accessibility of information, the
speed performance of websites, their structure and design, and the use of search engine
optimization tools. (The description of the sections and the list of items are reported in
Tables A.3 and A.4.)
The extended Model of Internet Commerce Adoption, initially proposed by Burgess
and Cooper (2000), is a reference model used to analyze the maturity level of business
websites. According to this model, the digital development of a firm passes through suc -
cessive phases, from a very simple initial stage up to more and more complex stages. The

The description of the two WCA and eMICA models highlights their complementa -

The social media assessment model
The social media analysis was aimed at investigating how farmhouses in Molise region
use social media, such as Facebook, Instagram and YouTube, for advertising purposes,
for letting know their offer and for interacting with potential customers. Aspects such as
easy navigation, mobile optimization, clear information and loading speed are all factors
that contribute to greater customer satisfaction.
The analysis focuses on items organized in three specific areas reported in a survey
sheet:
• Content: the main thematic areas that are covered by the firm’s posts published on
different social networks (e.g., promotion of services and activities, special events,
offers);
• Frequency and constancy with which farmhouses publish on their social pages: this
can highlight the degree of farmhouse involvement;
• Engagement: the interaction of users to posts, that is, if they interact through likes,
comments or shares.
These areas offer a complete picture that allows to evaluate the content, the level of use
and the effectiveness of social media among investigated farmhouses (Table B.1).
Methods
According to Cristobal-Fransi et al. (2018), a counting method is one of the most widely
used methods in evaluating websites, determining the content richness and evaluat -
ing their performance. The implementation of this method requires the utilization of a
checklist to verify the presence of specific characteristics on a website, in conjunction
with the appointment of a group of experts to undertake the actual counting process
within a laboratory setting. This method was followed in the present study for the web -
sites and the social media analyses.
The initial phase of the analyses required the preparation of the dataset, which was
based on the two WCA and eMICA models for the website quality assessment. This was
achieved by developing a survey sheet that incorporated all items for each active farm -
house located in the Molise region that had a website. Initially, the dichotomous and rat-
ing scale responses to each item were categorized. Two macro-indicators for the website
assessment (WEB WCA score and WEB Accessibility score) were obtained by summing
the score for each item.

Page 10 of 25Forleo et al. Agricultural and Food Economics           (2025) 13:62
A similar approach was adopted for the analysis of the social media (SM). A compara -
tive analysis was carried out to highlight differences among the platforms under con -
sideration (Facebook, Instagram and YouTube). One macro-indicator (SM score) was
calculated to summarize the single scores.
A global macro-indicator (SM + WEB) was used to summarize the absolute scores
reported on the three assessment sections.
The quality of websites and social media is then deemed to be greater the closer the
indicators are to n (the maximum achievable score on all items) and lesser the closer
they are to 0.
The absolute scores reported for the macro-indicators were then converted into rela -
tive terms based on the highest achievable score per assessment section. This was done
in order to assess and compare the quality of the digital tools used by farmhouses and
across digital tools.
Descriptive statistics were utilized to provide a comprehensive overview of the char -
acteristics of the digital tools, encompassing the various WCA dimensions, the eMICA
stages and the SM analysis.
In a second stage, focusing on the subpopulation of farmhouses having a website or
using social media, a cluster analysis was implemented for segmenting the population in
homogeneous groups with well-defined characteristics. The segmentation analysis was
inspired by several studies that measured the quality of the websites and social media in
different sectors, as reported in the literature section (Checchinato et al. 2024; Cristobal-
Fransi et al. 2018, 2023a; Platania 2014; Sánchez-Rivero et al. 2023).
The application of a hierarchical clustering with the Ward algorithm was based on the
value of the three macro-indicators (WEB WCA score, WEB Accessibility score and SM
score) for each farmhouse. The identification of the number of groups was based on two
stopping rules suitable for hierarchical clustering: the Calinski and Harabasz pseudo-F
index and the Duda–Hart Je(2)/Je(1) index. For both rules, larger values indicate more
distinct clustering; the Duda–Hart Je(2)/Je(1) rule also gives pseudo-T-squared values,
where smaller values indicate more distinct clustering.
The website and social media analyses were performed in the period September 1,
2024–November 30, 2024.
All statistical analyses were carried out with the Stata SE statistical package.

The communication dimension of the WCA model appears to be underdeveloped

description of the services and products offered, and of the experiences and activities
that guests can have during their stay is not always adequate. This makes it somewhat
difficult to appreciate the targets to which farmhouses intend to address. This result
about the communication dimension show similarity with the findings of the studies by
Palmisano (2020) about farmhouses in Sicily and by Król et al. (2019) that explored the
digital strategies of Polish farmhouses. In this case study, websites are mainly used for
the promotion of traditional agricultural farms and their products, while the communi -
cation to potential guests of the offer of tourist services is not given a high consideration.
The presence of several interactive functionalities is quite low on the websites, but it is
frequent the presence of linkages to the social media.
Another aspect addressed in the analysis of websites is the images that are used and
how they are placed on the page. In general, of the total organizations with a website,
6% do not have images suitable to highlight the identity of the farmhouse and describe
the structure and services offered. Overall, images across the different sections of the
websites are not always descriptive and appealing, nor it is possible to fully appreciate
the structure of the farm or what it offers. From a visual perspective, the analyzed web -
sites appear closer to a"traditional"typology characteristic of small-scale agricultural
farms that base their activity on regional traditions and rural practices, rather than to a
modern typology, called"commercialized"by Król and Zdonek (2020), that concentrates
activities in the field of tourism rather than agriculture, is typical of bigger farms and
offers a wide range of tourist services.
In 70% of cases, Italian is the only language available on the websites. Of the fifteen
websites that do allow for other languages, English is present in all cases, while other
languages are available in a limited number of cases (French, four sites; German, three
sites; Spanish or others, one site). Finally, two languages are used by three farms, and
three or more languages are used by other three farms. The lack of attention given to the
presentation of web pages in languages other than Italian is critical and in line with the
study by Król (2019) on farmhouses in Visegrad countries that affirmed that, despite the
global potential, the websites had a purely local impact.
In relation to the provision of dynamic information to guests, a criterion that allows
progression to the second phase of the eMICA scheme is the presence of maps or road
directions. None of the websites provides users with access to interactive and navigable
maps.
About the remaining two dimensions of the WCA model, the percentages demon -
strate that several items are absent in the websites.

In relation to the e-commerce dimension of the WCA model and to Phase 3 regarding

or through external platforms, such as Tripadvisor or Booking.com. Only seven web -

provide an online booking and payment system and a communication form, indicates

Zopounidis et al. (2014), in a now dated study, reported for the Greek agritourism sector.
Regarding the additional information dimension included in the WCA framework, the
linkages with the territory, its cultural and natural resources, are inadequately empha -
sized, except for a generic presentation of the territorial context and its attractions.
Furthermore, the online communication of farmhouses activities in the environmental
sphere is an area of potential development: only 8% of websites mention sustainable ini -
tiatives, such as the implementation of biodynamic agriculture or the adoption of recy -
cling systems according to a circular approach within the organization. Indeed, regarding
its cultural heritage, the Molise region boasts a history that spans the Neolithic period,
the Samnite and Roman eras, and the Middle Ages, as evidenced by the presence of
numerous museums and archeological sites. This rich cultural heritage is further com -
plemented by a plethora of historical and artistic assets. As regards the environmental
heritage, the list of excellence in the Region is quite long despite its small dimension: In
the region, there are two national parks, three state and four natural regional reserves,
one UNESCO Biosphere site included in the Man and Biosphere program, one cultural
and one natural site included in the UNESCO WHL; finally, the area of land included in
the EU Natura 2000 protection network is about 28% of the regional territory.
This result deserves further investigation to assess whether it depends on a lack of
awareness among farmhouses about the significance of the territorial heritage as a visi -
tor attraction or on other reasons. In a remote region characterized by a limited pres -
ence of farmhouses, the activities offered in individual farms may not be sufficiently
appealing to guests seeking a comprehensive holiday experience within the region. The
valorization of the regional heritage in the digital offer of agritourisms could help build -
ing an identity and positioning in the national agritourism context, which is much more
competitive, even only among the regions of central–southern Italy.
The above thorough analysis of the content of websites in the case study led to iden -
tify three main categories: The most common is an informative category of websites
that merely presents information about the farm and its location and provides general
info about the main services offered; the interactive category, although with a low level
of intensity and mainly on a one-way direction (from farmhouses to potential custom -
ers), that offers the opportunity for a deeper communication with guests; and finally, the
transactional websites, the least common category in the case study, that allow also for
online payments.

Findings of the study offer insights and recommendations, also based on suggestions
drawn from some relevant literature. To improve their quality, websites in the case study
should consider the following features: the presence of an online communication form,
the updating of information about the products and services provided, information
about the area of interest and on various topics related to the rural context, a social share

Regarding the accessibility of websites measured by their technical performance
(Table A.3), satisfactory values are obtained, as the loading times of the information fall
within the optimal ranges.
The websites of regional farmhouses have satisfactory loading times, thereby enhanc -
ing the perception that users have of the website. However, it is hypothesized that this
performance is likely attributable to the very simplicity of websites.
In many cases, the choice of readable fonts and appropriate colors was conducive to an
enhanced visual experience and usability. However, improvements may be necessary to
enhance the visibility of the URL, the menu and its accessibility even during the scrolling
of the page, and to manage empty spaces.
Website security is another area that needs improvement: 76% of websites, not all
sites, have an SSL certificate that allows establishing a secure and encrypted connection
between the user’s browser and the website server. When a site has an SSL certificate, it
means that data exchanged between the parties (such as password and credit card num -
bers) are encrypted and thus protected against possible cyber-attacks, such as the inter -
ception or theft of information. On the other hand, this result was expected because,
as pointed out above, websites do not widely offer the possibility of making online
transactions.
The comprehensive evaluation of the quality of websites indicates that they appear to
lack professionalism, in terms of both content and visual design. Overall, the security,
the quality of visual content and user interactivity, several other dimensions of the WCA
approach are areas of improvement of the websites of farmhouses in the case study,
especially from a perspective of tourist attractiveness and digital competitiveness.

Social media assessment
Among farmhouses in the case study, the most widely used social media platform
is Facebook (67%, 76 units over 114), followed by Instagram (47%, 54 units) and, to a
lesser extent, YouTube (7%, 8 units). As the number of units on YouTube is minimal, data
collected for this platform are not reported. On Facebook, the most prevalent content
shared by farms pertains to four themes that are observed in almost 80% of the social
media: local products and traditions, landscape and environment, daily life in the farm -
house, and special or seasonal events. The promotion of agritourism services is the pre -
vailing content, with 47% of farms offering discounts and promotions, 33% providing
cultural activities and 15% focusing on sustainable topics. There is a preference of Molise
farms for the Tripadvisor platform as the main promotion channel, compared to other
Italian portals in the sector such as Agriturismo.it or Agriturismi.it.
Thanks to the ease and speed of updating social media, information on farms could be
constantly updated. However, the case study exhibits a low propensity to update social
media pages with a high degree of frequency (Table B.1). This is consistent with the find-
ings of Król and Zdonek (2020) that assessed the quality of the websites of thirty Polish
farmhouses and entitled a “zero-category” farmhouses for the usability of their websites
due to outdated information, a simple construction, small number of graphic files and
lack of functionality. Indeed (as Table B.1 reports), the percentages of absent items, a
sort of zero-category farmhouse and the percentages calculated at the lowest levels of
the response scales are very high on several aspects.
A limited attention is devoted to the use of hashtags and interactive tools, on both
Facebook and Instagram. In two-thirds of platforms, a rare or no response to guest’s
comments was observed. To complete the above picture, two other aspects deserve to be
described: First, almost all social pages do not allow an online direct booking; second, it
is not rare the case of social pages (38% on Facebook, 52% on Instagram) where the link
to the farmers’ website is not reported.
Posts on Facebook do not extensively show customers’experiences (9%), news related
to the local community (8%) or institutions (4%). Regarding the content reported on Ins-
tagram, no significant differences were observed in comparison with the information
provided through Facebook, apart from a lower attention put to special events (66%)
and, most notably, to offers and promotions (17%).
Social media is an essential channel to show the public the link with the territory and
local initiatives, aspects that often do not emerge with the same immediacy on the inves-
tigated websites. As regards the attention that social media of farmhouses in the case
study pay to the rural context, a differentiated picture emerges. Local food products and
traditions are frequently introduced in posts through the formulation of menus and gas -
tronomic dishes. However, there is a paucity of attention devoted to providing descrip -
tions of their characteristics or showing the preparation of traditional recipes. This is
also the case for the presentation of the specific characteristics of agrifood products pro-
duced within the same farms (offered for sale or used as ingredients in recipes) or origi -
nating from the regional context.
The rural environment and its natural resources are given some consideration on
social media pages; however, their valorization occurs in terms of the landscape of
the territory in which farmhouses are located. The consideration of the regional

Page 16 of 25Forleo et al. Agricultural and Food Economics           (2025) 13:62
context is not adequately developed, and this aspect should be given greater value
because landscape is important in shaping the probability that an Italian farm runs
agritourism activities (Lupi et  al. 2017). Study findings confirm the results of the
study by Palmisano (2020) that found peculiarities related to the use of words such
as “nature” in the Italian websites, where nature is referred to the external contexts
of the farmhouse, compared to the German and Austrian websites, where it enters as
leisure activity. In addition, almost all social media in the case study do not involve
influencers or regional partners. Finally, the network of relations with regional and
local institutions, as well as collaborations with regional partners and actors, if any, is
almost neglected in the social media of farmhouses. As in the analysis of websites, the
regional system of people, institutions and events is not given much consideration in
the social media of farmhouses.
The case study demonstrated that the investigated farmhouses utilized social media
with limited intensity, thereby missing opportunities to interact with customers and
eventually influence their booking decisions.
Results of the study would be deserving further investigation in order to assess the
challenges that farms in the case study face in effectively using their social media plat -
forms. The literature offers many insights on the topic, such as a lack of technical
expertise (Meyers et al. 2015; Mutipforo et al. 2024), limited infrastructure and poor
internet connectivity, difficulty in keeping pace with the pace of innovation as tech -
nology evolves (Katsande et al. 2024), agricultural mentality and the time-consuming
nature of social media (Canovi and Pucciarelli 2019). Furthermore, future research
should investigate whether agritourism operators in the case study had received for -
mal training in business marketing and whether they possessed knowledge of how to
use social media (Miller et al. 2021).

Implications of study findings
Findings of the study provide implications and recommendations for farmhouses and
policymakers to harness the potential of digital communication and social media mar -
keting tools for the development of the sector.
To this end, it is a priority to have a full comprehension of the “state of the art” in
the sector, of the strength and the weaknesses, the opportunities and the obstacles for
implementing/improving the digital profiles of farmhouses. A digital marketing strategy
cycle, from analysis and research, planning and goal setting, implementation and execu -
tion, monitoring and optimization, is recommended in that it allows adaptions in the
fast changing digital world.
An active role of farmers is needed. To adequately equip farmers for the digital age, it
is a priority that farmhouses have all the required resources and are encouraged to intro-
duce digital marketing tools and to implement them in a professional and continuous
way.
Policy makers should strengthen measures devoted to the digitalization of the
marketing strategies among regional farmers, such as the measures supported by
European policies. In addition to interventions aimed at supporting individual organ -
izations, measures aimed at farmers’ networks and for supporting long-term digital

Page 20 of 25Forleo et al. Agricultural and Food Economics           (2025) 13:62
communication strategies could be promoted to share resources and experiences and
to present a wide agritourism offer, eventually under a single regional umbrella.
Indeed, in a broad sense the digitalization of the agricultural and multifunctional
sector is one of the topics addressed by the Common Agricultural Policy, mainly
through the measures set in the national and regional Rural Development Plans
(RDPs).
As for the programming period 2014–2022, although the digitalization topic entered
indirectly or horizontally in several measures—as in the case of the first pillar sectoral
interventions payments; and in some measures of the RDP , such as those concerning
the competitiveness of the sector and the LEADER program—the measures more tied to
the topic and to the agritourism sector were set in the second Pillar, namely M1 Knowl -
edge and information transfer; M2 Advisory services; and M16 Cooperation. M1 and
M16 were activated in all Italian regions; M2 was not activated in the provinces of Bozen
and Trento, and in Friuli Venezia Giulia and Valle d’Aosta regions. Anyway, above meas-
ures in Pillar II were among those with the lowest progress in spending (Ministero delle
Politiche Agricole, Alimentari e Forestali 2024). The public funding of M1 through the
European Agricultural Fund for Rural Development assigned the worst performance to
Molise region (3%) compared to excellent performances in Tuscany, Umbria and Abru -
zzo (about the 100% of the expenditure); for M2 percentages went from the minimum in
Sicily (8%) and Molise (17%), to the maximum in Abruzzo and Emilia Romagna; and for
M16 from the minimum around 30% in Sardinia, Calabria, Lazio and Molise, to about
100% in Liguria, Tuscany, Emilia Romagna and Veneto regions.
Within the new CAP 2023–2027, digitalization is a crosscutting objective together
with knowledge and innovation, but the “support for the adoption of digital technologies
by farmers under the CAP shows rather low ambition” affirmed the European Commis -
sion Report mapping and analyzing CAP strategic plans (European Commission 2023,
page 974). According to the National Strategic Plan in its latest amendment, measures
for the digitalization of the Italian sector were reinforced and related to the interven -
tion of the Agricultural Knowledge and Innovation Systems (AKIS), to the Knowledge
Exchange and Innovation (SRH) and the Cooperation (SRG) measures, and to actions
set for the competitiveness and the diversification of firms (SRD1 and SRD3). These
measures allow for interventions aimed at increasing the level of digitalization of mar -
keting strategies in the agritourism sector. As regards Molise region, measures under
SRG and SRD1 count for 5% each of the total expenditure in the programming period
2023–2027, measures under SRH the 4.3% and under SRD3 the 1.3%.
However, the last Performance Report of the Italian CAP affirms that for several
measures under SRG, SRH and SRD1 the milestone for 2024 was not reached—also
due to the overlapping with the period 2014–2022—but there is confidence about
reaching the value of the milestone planned since 2025 (Rete Rurale Nazionale 2024).
Within above rural policy framework and despite the low performances of meas -
ures addressed to the topic of digitalization in the sector, there could be space for
study suggestions to be taken into account in the case study and in other regions. The
regional rural policy framework set measures to support the setting a global digital
marketing strategy, allowing for an active involvement of farmers, their associations,
rural development agencies and other actors.

Conclusions
Study findings indicated that the potential of digital marketing media to enhance the
visibility and promotion of the agritourism offer in the investigated rural region has
not been fully explored. Only a small number of farms have adopted a digital com -
munication strategy that utilizes a range of diversified tools that reaches high levels of
quality.
Digital media are primarily used as a tool for visibility rather than for commercial pur -
poses, and the full potential of digital tools remains largely unexplored. The implementa-
tion of digital tools appears more farm-centered than territory-centered in that websites
and social media put almost totally attention to the product and services offered, and to
the activities and experiences guests can have in the farmhouse rather than to the rural
territory with its natural, traditional and cultural resources.
Based on these findings, improvements in the digital communication strate -
gies encompass several aspects that apply in the case study but that can have a wider
application.
Firstly, in the digital environment the connections between farmers, their associations
and the local community should be reinforced for transmitting to potential customers a
sense of authenticity and the identity of the regional agritourism offer. This aspect is a
prerequisite for supporting the development of the regional agricultural sector and its
multifunctionality.
Secondly, study results demonstrated the need for websites and social media to be
implemented more professionally. Both tools should be geared toward full and improved
implementation in terms of content and visibility, including online booking and payment
services.
Thirdly, it is important that websites and social media applications and content remain
consistently updated and engaging. The regular posting of content on various online
social media platforms, as well as the creation of linkages among them and the websites,
may maximize reach and engagement.
A limitation of the present study is that it refers to a specific rural region, although the
analysis included the universe of farmhouses located in the territory and not merely a
sample of them. A comparative study of the digital communication strategies employed
by farmhouses in different geographical contexts, including rural and non-rural areas,
could offer valuable insights for understanding the use of digital marketing tools in
relation to different territorial, social and economic contexts. Furthermore, although
the analysis has been applied in a specific case study, it is believed that the models of
the analysis can be usefully replicated and validated in other agritourism and territo -
rial contexts. The present analysis could be enlarged by encompassing additional digital
platforms. Finally, the time frame of data collection represents a potential shortcoming
when compared to a longer time frame. Broadening the time horizon and repeating the
analyses over time can help mitigate the impact of seasonality on agritourism and its
active promotion through digital media.
