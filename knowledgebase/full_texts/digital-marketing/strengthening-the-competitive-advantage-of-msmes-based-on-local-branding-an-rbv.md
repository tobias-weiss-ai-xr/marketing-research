ABSTRACT
Increasingly fierce business competition demands that MSMEs
have a sustainable competitive advantage, one of which is
through strengthening local branding that represents identity,
cultural values, differentiation, and perceptions of product
quality. However, the effectiveness of local branding in
creating a competitive advantage is inseparable from the
ability of MSMEs to adopt digital marketing as a supporting
capability for marketing strategies. This study analyzes the
influence of local branding on the competitive advantage of
MSMEs and examines the role of digital marketing adoption as
a moderating variable in this relati onship. The study used a
quantitative approach with a survey method on MSME actors,
and the data were analyzed using Structural Equation
Modeling–Partial Least Squares (SEM -PLS). The results showed
that local branding had a positive and significant effect on the
competitive advantage of MSMEs, while digital marketing
adoption did not have a direct effect on competitive
advantage. However, the results of the moderation test proved
that digital marketing adoption was able to strengthen the
influence of local branding on competitive advantage,
indicating that the use of digital marketing plays an important
role in increasing the effectiveness of local identity -based
branding strategies. These findings confirm that the
integration between strengthening local branding and optimal
digital marketing adoption can be a relevant strategy for
MSMEs in building stronger and more sustainable business
competitiveness.

INTRODUCTION
The Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) sector is the backbone of the
Indonesian economy because it absorbs a large workforce and drives productivity activities in
various regions. Amidst tight global competition, MSMEs are required to have strategies that are
able to build sustainable competitive advantages (Deby Laras Wati et al., 2024) One area that has
great potential in the MSME sector is Kesambi Village, Porong District, Sidoarjo Regency, which is
known as a center for stainless steel and aluminum kitchen equipment craftsmen. Products such
as sablukan steamers, meatball steamers, milk pans, single food containers, tangkring ovens,
and langseng have long been the hallmarks and identities of the production of the Kesambi
Village community. In fact, most of the residents in this village depend on their livelihoods as
craftsmen or workers in the household industry sector.
In line with Prasetyo's (2022) findings, household manufacturing MSMEs still face various
obstacles when trying to promote local brands to achieve long -term strategic value (Prasetyo et
al., 2022)This situation demonstrates that the potential of local wisdom -based resources has not
been fully matched by managerial capacity, particularly in managing regional identity to create a
competitive advantage.
However, in recent years, the development of kitchen equipment MSMEs in Kesambi
Village has shown signs of stagnation, particularly in marketing. The community still relies on
conventional sales methods such as word -of-mouth or traditional market sales, without
leveraging the potential of digitalization and strong brandi ng strategies (Cn and Helmita, 2023) .
The use of digital marketing can actually support business performance through the use of social
media, marketplaces, and digital promotional strategies, although its effectiveness is highly
dependent on the ability of business actors to manage and integrate digital channels
appropriately (Sulistiyowati and Rahmawati, 2024) . As a result, local residents find it difficult to
compete in an increasingly open market, especially with the influx of cheaper and more
widespread imported products from China. This phenomenon has led to a price war among local
MSMEs, which has actually reduced profitability and weakened long -term competitiveness
(Saputera, Rahmiati and Adhipurna, 2024)
Research by Astuti & Wibowo (2023) confirms that MSMEs  that fail to build a strong local
brand image will be more vulnerable to price -based competition (Astuti, Wibowo and Juwono,
2023)On the other hand, Lestari's (2021) study shows that adopting a branding strategy that
utilizes elements of local wisdom has been proven to increase consumer emotional appeal and
strengthen product positioning in the domestic market (Lestari, 2021)  However, to date, there
has been little research linking local branding directly to the Resource-Based View (RBV) model of
MSME competitive advantage, particularly in the traditional household appliances sector.
Viewed from the RBV perspective, Kesambi Village possesses highly valuable intangible
resources, such as hereditary production skills, distinctive kitchenware designs, and local
wisdom inherent in every MSME product. RBV emphasizes that unique, valuable, and difficult -to-
imitate internal resources, including local branding, can be the primary foundation for building
sustainable competitive advantage (Barney, 1991) However, in practice, the potential for local
branding in Kesambi Village has not been managed strategically. The local identity that should
function as a key differentiator in the market has not provided an optimal contribution to the
competitiveness of MSMEs (Agustin et al., 2024)
This finding aligns with research by Rahmawati et al. (2023) which states that the
competitive advantage of MSMEs is determined more by intangible ass ets, such as brand image
and local knowledge, than by physical resources alone (Novi Ria Rahmawati et al., 2023)However,
most RBV studies still focus on large companies or the service sector, so research examining the
application of RBV in the context of locality -based MSMEs, particularly in the home industry
sector, is still relatively limited. This limitation is the conceptual ga p that this study aims to
bridge.

On the other hand, in the context of increasingly complex MSME competition, the
success of a local branding strategy is determined not only by the strength of the brand identity
itself, but also by the market response to the value offered by the brand. Several previous
studies have shown that market satisfaction and acceptance are often used as indicators of
marketing success, as they reflect the extent to which consumers accept and appreciate a
brand's value.
Study Alhamda ny et al. (2023) found that brand equity significantly influences
satisfaction and positive responses, while Sucma and Sugiat (2023) emphasized that customer
satisfaction plays a crucial role in shaping brand loyalty. These findings demonstrate that
branding has important psychological implications in building relationships between products
and consumers.
However, most of these studies emphasize consumer psychological processes and place
customer satisfaction and loyalty as the primary mechanisms explaining  marketing
performance. In the context of local wisdom -based MSMEs such as those in Kesambi Village, this
approach fails to fully explain why local branding, already recognized and accepted by
consumers, has not consistently translated into sustainable competitive advantage. Therefore,
this study does not position customer satisfaction and loyalty as analytical variables or
intermediary mechanisms, but rather as empirical contexts that illustrate market responses to
MSME branding strategies. The primary foc us of this research is directed at how local branding,
as a strategic resource, can be converted into competitive advantage through the support of
digital marketing capabilities.
With this background, this study aims to analyze the influence of local branding on
competitive advantage in Kesambi Village by considering the adoption of digital marketing as a
factor that strengthens this relationship. Previous research has shown that the use of digital
marketing is an important strategy for MSMEs to improve bus iness performance and expand
market reach amidst increasingly competitive competition  (Setini et al., 2025) . The focus of this
study is directed at how utilizing local identity as a strategic resource can produce sustainable
competitive advantage, especiall y in facing increasingly competitive and digitalized market
dynamics. This approach provides a more focused understanding of the internal factors that
influence MSME stagnation, while also offering strategic solutions based on local potential.
This study uses the Resource -Based View (RBV) framework as the main theoretical basis,
because RBV emphasizes that the competitive advantage of a business is primarily determined
by the utilization of unique, valuable, and difficult -to-imitate internal resources, not solely by
external factors such as price or market conditions (Eravia and Samsir, 2025)In this context, local
branding is positioned as an intangible resource that represents the unique identity of Kesambi
Village, Porong District, Sidoarjo Regency, and has strategic potential to create competitive
advantages for MSMEs.
To deepen our understanding of the relationships between variables , this study
developed a research model that positions Digital Marketing Adoption as a moderating variable.
This variable is viewed as an organizational capability that determines the extent to which local
branding can optimally impact the competitive advantage of MSMEs. This research does not
focus on consumer psychology as an intermediary mechanism, but rather on the role of digital
marketing capabilities in strengthening the effectiveness of MSMEs' internal resource utilization
in the digital era.
The selection of variables in this study is based on the understanding that the problems
faced by MSMEs in Kesambi Village are not only related to weak local brand identities, but also to
the limited ability of business actors to maximize the strategic value of their resources. Local
branding, as a distinctive regional identity, has great potential to become a source of competitive
advantage, but this potential has not been fully converted into sustainable competitiveness. This
condition indicates that the main problem lies not solely in the branding aspect, but rather in the
capability of MSMEs to manage and convey brand value to the market effectively.

In this context, the adoption of digital marketing is a crucial factor in determining the
success of local branding in creating a competitive advantage. Without adequate digital
marketing support, local branding tends to remain merely a symbolic identity capable of
reaching a broad market, building ongoing interactions with customers, or highlighting the
uniqueness of local products amidst increasingly fierce competition. Therefore, digital marketing
is positioned as a strategic capability that can strengthen or weaken the influence of local
branding on the competitive advantage of MSMEs.
Thus, this study successfully identified the root causes of the weak competitive
advantage of MSMEs in Kesambi Village through the RBV perspective, emphasizing the role of
internal resources and organizational capabilities. This study contributes novelty by presenting a
conceptual m odel that positions digital marketing as a moderating variable in the relationship
between local branding and competitive advantage, which is still relatively limited in the context

literature but also provide applicable strategic recommendations for strengthening the
competitiveness of local MSMEs amidst the challenges of globalization and economic

LITERATURE REVIEW
Resource-Based View (RBV)
Resource-Based View (RBV) Theory emphasizes that sustainable competitive advantage is
obtained through the management of internal resources that are valuable , rare , inimitable , and
non -substitutable, or known as the VRIN criteria (Barney, 1991). In the context of MSMEs, these
resources are n ot only physical assets, but also intangible resources such as reputation, skills,
customer relationships, and local wisdom values (Ilham et al., 2024)
RBV places local branding as a form of strategic resource that is capable of creating long -
term competitive advantage because it represents the uniqueness of local identity and values
that are difficult for competitors to replicate (Barney, 1991)Meanwhile, according to Wernerfelt
(1984), companies that are able to identify and exploit their unique internal resources will more
easily build a strong competitive position in the market (Wernerfelt, 1984) However, the
effectiveness of utilizing  local branding as a strategic resource is highly dependent on the
organization's capability in managing and communicating this value to the market, especially
through the use of digital marketing technology. In this study, RBV is the main framework to
explain that local branding is a strategic resource that comes from the internal potential of
MSMEs, such as the skills of craftsmen and local cultural values of Kesambi Village, which if
managed well will produce sustainable competitive advantages (Barney, 1991)

Local Branding
Local branding is a marketing strategy that emphasizes the uniqueness, cultural identity,
and distinctive values of a region in the image of a product or service. Local branding functions
to build an emotional attachment between customers and the product due to the authentic
value and pride in the product's region of origin (Shava, 2024)
According to Winnie & Keni (2020), a strong brand can create a perception of quality and
consumer trust, which ultimately strengthens the positive consumer response to the brand
(Winnie and Keni, 2020) In the context of MSMEs, local branding is a strong differentiator from
mass products, especially amidst increasing consumer preference for products that have cultural
value and authenticity (Nugroho et al., 2025) Pranadetha & Hanoum (2025) showed that
strengthening local brand identity can increase perceived value and customer engagement with
traditional products. This finding suggests that local branding not only builds brand image but
also plays a role in strengthening competitive advantage through added value and clearer
differentiation in the eyes of consumers (Pranadetha, Hanoum and Noer, 2025)

Competitive Advantage
Competitive advantage is the ability of a company to create greater value for customers
than competitors (Porter, 1998) According to Barney (1991) in the RBV framework, competitive
advantage comes from the effective use of unique internal resources (Barney, 1991)
According to David (2021), competitive advantage can be cost -based or differentiation -
based (David and David, 2021)  In MSMEs, differentiation often stem s from product uniqueness,
personalized service, and local identity. Meanwhile, research by Fathonah & Wati (2024) shows
that strong branding increases MSME competitiveness by enhancing perceived quality and
customer loyalty (Fathonah, Wati and Winarno, 2024)
Thus, In the context of this research, competitive advantage is understood as the result
of utilizing local branding as a strategic resource for MSMEs supported by digital marketing
capabilities in creating business differentiation and competitiveness.

Digital Marketing Adoption
Digital Marketing Adoption refers to the extent to which MSMEs integrating digital
technology into marketing act ivities, including social media, e -commerce, websites, and other
digital platforms (Andika et al., 2021) Research by Mulyanto & Budi (2025) proves that the
adoption of digital marketing strengthens the influence of branding on business performance,
because it allows two -way interaction between producers and customers (Mulyanto and Budi,
2025). In addition, a study by Amalia et al. (2023) shows that the use of digital media can expand
the market reach of local products and increase consumer trust in brands (Amalia et al., 2023) .
Research by Indriyani & Shan (2024) shows that the results of adopting digital marketing in
MSMEs are seen as an approach that can support marketing activities, with varying levels of
effectiveness depending on the business context and internal conditions of each MSME actor
(Indriyani and Shan, 2024).
In this study, Digital Marketing Adoption is positioned as a moderating variable (Z) that
serves to strengthen the relationship between local branding and competitive advantage. As the
level of digital marketing utilization increases, the influence of local branding on competitiveness
also becomes stronger (Muhammad Robbiansa Pramadhika et al., 2025) Therefore, digital
marketing not only acts as a promotional medium, but also becomes a tool that helps MSMEs
affirm their local brand identity in the digital space, thus being more responsive to changes in
current consumer behavior (Adi Prayuda et al., 2025)

Relationship between variables
1. Local Branding (X) is positioned as an intangible resource source that originates from the
distinctive identity, cultural values, and uniqueness of regional products, which from an RBV
perspective has strategic potential to create competitive advantages for MSMEs.
2. Digital Marketing Adoption (Z) acts as an organizational capability that moderates the
relationship between local branding and competitive advantage. The level of digital
marketing adoption determines the extent to which local branding can be effectively
communicated and utilized to create business differentiation and competitiveness.
3. Competitive Advantage (Y) is the result of utilizing local branding as a strategic resource
supported by digital marketing capabilities, thus enabling MSMEs to achieve sustainable
competitiveness.

The Relationship between Local Branding and Competitive Advantage
From the Resource -Based View (RBV) perspective, sustainable competitive advantage
stems from the utilization of valuable, rare, difficult to imitate, and non -substitutable (VRIN)
internal resources (Barney, 1991)Local branding can be positioned as an intangible resource that
meets these criteria because it represents the identity, cultural values, and unique characteristics
of a region that competitors do not possess. When local branding is managed strategically, it can
create clear differentiation and provide added value to MSME products.

resources will more easily build a superior competitive position in the market (Barney, 1991) In
the context of MSMEs, local branding serves  as a key differentiator that not only highlights the
functional aspects of the product, but also the symbolic and emotional value attached to the
product's origins. This uniqueness allows MSMEs to escape price -based competition and build
more sustainable competitiveness.
Empirical research by Astuti and Wibowo (2023) found that MSMEs with a strong local

(Astuti, Wibowo and Juwono, 2023)  Similar results were also presented by Lestari (2021), who
showed that branding based on local wisdom can increase product competitiveness in the
domestic market through more meaningful differentiation (Lestari, 2021) Thus, effectively
managed local branding has a positive and significant impact on the competitive advantage of
MSMEs.

The Relationship between Digital Marketing Adoption and Competitive Advantage
Digital Marketing Adoption is seen as an organizational capability that enables MSMEs to
utilize digital  technology to improve marketing effectiveness and business competitiveness.
Within the RBV framework, this capability acts as a mechanism that helps organizations optimize
the use of internal resources, including local branding, to generate greater economic value
(Barney, 1991)
The use of social media, marketplaces, and digital platforms opens up opportunities for
MSMEs to reach a wider market and increase communication intensity with consumers. However,
the effectiveness of digital marketing in creating competitive advantage is not always universal
and is heavily influenced by organizational readiness, market characteristics, and the marketing

The Role of Digital Marketing Adoption as a Moderating Variable in the Relationship
between Local Branding and Competitive Advantage
In theoretical studies, Digital Marketing Adoption can be positioned as a moderating
variable in the relationship between Local Branding and Competitive Advantage. RBV explains
that local branding as an intangible resource has the pot ential to create competitive advantage,
but its effectiveness depends on the organization's capability in managing and communicating
that value (Barney, 1991)
Digital marketing provides a platform for MSMEs to showcase their local identity, cultural
values, and product stories to a wider market. In certain contexts, this capability has the
potential to strengthen local branding by increasin g reach and engagement with consumers.
However, in other contexts, adopting digital marketing that isn't integrated with local branding
characteristics can potentially blur differentiation and diminish the strength of brand identity.
Previous research has shown mixed findings regarding the moderating role of digital
marketing. Most studies found that digital marketing can strengthen the influence of brand -
based resources on MSME performance, while other studies indicated that its effectiveness

relationship between Local Branding and Competitive Advantage needs to be tested  empirically
in the context of MSMEs in Kesambi Village.

Grand Theory
This research is based on the Resource -Based View (RBV) as the main theory, which is a
strategic approach that focuses on the organization's internal resources as the main basis for
building and maintaining a sustainable competitive advantage. RBV was introduced by Barney
(1991) who explained that an organization can gain a competitive advantage if it has valuable,
rare, inimitable, and non -substitutable resources known as the VRIN fram ework criteria.
(Barney, 1991)
In the context of kitchen equipment MSMEs in Kesambi Village, Sidoarjo, the RBV theory
is relevant because this sector has a wealth of internal resources in the form of inherited skills,
distinctive designs, and local wisdom values inherent in each product. These resources are
intangible assets, which often serve as differentiators and are not easily imitated by competitors,
including imported products from China that rely more on cost efficiency and mass production.
This is in line with the views of Wernerfelt (1984) and Barney (1991) that competitive advantage is
more sustainable if it is based on the internal uniqueness of the organization, not only on
external factors such as price or distribution (Wernerfelt, 1984) (Barney, 1991)
According to Grant (1991), RBV theory assumes that organizations capable of identifying,
developing, and exploiting their unique resources will have a greater chance of surviving in a
competitive market (Grant, 1991) intangible resources such as reputation, trust, and customer
perception, all of which are key factors in creating added value (valuable creation).

Empirical studies support the relevance of RBV to branding and the competitiveness of
MSMEs. Research by Agustin et al. (2024) shows that developing local identity -based branding
can strengthen the competitiveness of MSMEs by increasing the perception of value and
consumer attachment to local products (Agustin et al., 2024)  In line with this, Rahmawati et al.
(2023) found that the right branding strategy significantly increases the competitiveness of
MSMEs by improving brand image and expanding market reach (Novi Ria Rahmawati et al., 2023)
In addition, a study by Nugroho et al. (2025) emphasized that MSME product branding strategies
by utilizing local identity and digital media play an important role in creating added value and
increasing the competitivenes s of business actors in an increasingly competitive market [24].
These findings strengthen the argument that intangible assets such as local brand image,
reputation, and product uniqueness are important components in building RBV -based
competitive advantage.
Furthermore, in the modern RBV perspective, competitive advantage is not only based
on the ownership of internal resources, but also on the organization's ability to convert these
resources into dynamic capabilities to respond to changes in technology and the business
environment (Teece, 2018)  This is in line with the Digital Marketing Adoption variable , which
functions as a form of actualization of the dynamic capabilities of MSMEs in strengthening the
impact of local branding on competitive advantage th rough the use of digital technology as an
adaptive strategy.
Considering its overall theoretical foundation, the Resource -Based View (RBV) provides a
strong conceptual foundation for this research. The RBV perspective helps explain how local
branding, as a unique and difficult -to-imitate internal resource, can be converted into a
competitive advantage for MSMEs when supported by appropriate organizational capabilities,
particularly through the adoption of digital marketing as a form of dynamic capability. T he RBV
framework provides analytical space to understand how the internal potential of MSMEs in
Kesambi Village can be optimally utilized in responding to the increasingly competitive dynamics
of the digital era.

METHODS
Types and Approaches of Research
This study uses a quantitative approach with an explanatory research type that aims to
test the causal relationship between local branding and the competitive advantage of MSMEs, by
considering the adoption of digital marketing as a moderating variable. Thi s approach was
chosen because it is able to provide a measurable and objective picture of the influence between
variables through statistical analysis, as well as explaining the extent to which digital marketing
strengthens the effectiveness of local branding in creating business competitiveness. According
to Sofyani (2023), quantitative research is used to test hypotheses formulated based on theory
through the collection of numerically measurable data (Sofyani, 2023) This approach is based on
the Resource -Based View (RBV) perspective, which views local branding as an intangible asset
(intangible resource) of strategic value, solely the adoption of digital marketing is positioned as
an organizational capability that determines the optimization of internal resource utilization in
building the competitive advantage of MSMEs.

Research Location
This research took place in Kesambi Village, Porong District, Sidoarjo Regency. The area is
known as a hub for MSMEs producing household appliances made from stainless steel and
aluminum, such as steamers, pots, and food containers. This location was chosen because its
business characteristics aligned with the research focus, making it a relevant object of study.

Population and Sample
The population of this study is all household appliance MSMEs in Kesambi Village who
are actively op erating in 2025. Based on data from the local village government, there are 102
active MSME units in the area. Because the population is relatively small and still within the limits
that allow for comprehensive research, this study uses a saturated sample technique (census
sampling). Saturated sampling is a sampling technique when all members of the population are
used as respondents (Sofyani, 2023)

Data Types and Sources
The types of data used in this study are primary and secondary data. Primary data were
obtained directly from questionnaires distributed to owners and managers of household
appliance MSMEs in Kesambi Village. Secondary data were obtained from various written
sources, including documents, reports, journals, scientific articles, and relevant statistical data
regarding MSMEs, local branding, digital marke ting adoption, and competitive advantage (Ollivia
et al., 2025) (Novi Ria Rahmawati et al., 2023)

Data Collection Technique
Data for this study were collected through questionnaires and limited interviews. The
main instrument, a questionnai re, was structured based on indicators for each variable (Local
Branding, Digital Marketing Adoption, and Competitive Advantage) using a Likert scale of 1 -5 to
obtain measurable quantitative data. To enhance validity, limited interviews were conducted
with several MSMEs to verify respondents' answers and gain contextual understanding of local
branding strategies.
The questionnaire in this study was distributed directly (offline) and indirectly (online) via
the WhatsApp application to owners and managers of household appliance MSMEs in Kesambi
Village through field visits. This technique was used because most MSME actors have varying
levels of digital literacy, so online distribution is less than optimal (Prasetyo et al., 2022)
Respondents who were eligible to fill ou t the questionnaire were business owners, business
managers, or family members involved in the production or marketing process of the product, in
accordance with the characteristics of the local MSME population (Agustin et al., 2024)  In
addition to direct distribution, researchers also provided assistance in filling out the
questionnaire to ensure that respondents understood each question item, thereby reducing the
potential for bias and misperception. Limited interviews were also conducted to confirm some of
the respondents' answers and enrich the data c ontext as recommended by Sofyani (2023) in
primary data collection in quantitative research (Sofyani, 2023)

Data Analysis Techniques
Data analysis in this study was conducted using the Structural Equation Modeling Partial
Least Squares (SEM -PLS) method with the help of SmartPLS 4 software. This technique was
chosen to simultaneously test the relationships between latent constructs. The analysis stages
include:
1. Structural Equation Modeling Partial Least Squares (SEM-PLS)
The SEM-PLS approach is a method for structural equation modeling that is characterized by
variance and is generally used in research with complex model structures, limited sample
sizes, and data that does not meet the assumption of normality. This technique was chosen
because it c an analyze relationships between latent constructs simultaneously. The analysis
stages used include:
a. Measurement Model (Outer Model)
The measurement model aims to assess the extent to which indicators represent the
construct being measured. Three main aspects are analyzed:
1. Convergent Validity
Convergent validity is seen from the outer loading value of each indicator on its construct,
with a standard of > 0.70. In addition, the Average Variance value Extracted (AVE) must reach >
0.50 so that the indicator is considered capable of explaining more than 50% of the variation
in the construct.
2. Construct Reliability Test
Construct reliability is determined through Composite Reliability and Cronbach's Alpha values,
each of which must have a minimum value of 0.70. This indicates that the indicators within a
construct have adequate internal consistency.
b. Structural Model (Inner Model)
The structural model describes the causal relationships between latent constructs in the
model. The inner model includes several important dimensions as follows:
1. R-Square Value (R2)
R2 measures the proportion of variance in the dependent variable that can be explained by
the independent variable. Interpretation of R2 values is classified as high (>0.75), medium (0.50 -
0.74), and low (< 0.25).

Ekombis Review: Jurnal Ilmiah Ekonomi dan Bisnis,  Vol. 14 No. 4 October 2026 page: 5665 – 5686 | 5675
2. Predictive Relevance (Q 2)
Q2 is used to assess the model's ability to predict endogenous variables. If the Q2 value is above
0, the model can be considered to have adequate predictive relevance.
3. Effect Size (f 2)
f value 2 shows how much an independent construct contributes to explaining the dependent
construct, the effects are categorized as large ( >0.35), medium (0.15 -0.34) and small (0.02 -
0.14).
c. Significance Test of Direct and Moderation Relationships
Significance testing is conducted to examine  causal relationships between latent constructs
in the research model. This testing includes direct and moderating influences between
research variables.
1. Direct Effect Test
The direct influence between variables was tested using the path coefficient (β) v alue and
tested using the method generated from the bootstrapping process in SmartPLS. The
relationship between variables was considered significant if the T -statistic value was > 1.96
and the p-value was < 0.05 at the 5% significance level.
2. Moderation Test (Moderating Effect)
A moderation test was conducted to determine the role of Digital Marketing Adoption (Z) in
strengthening or weakening the influence of Local Branding (X) on Competitive Advantage
(Y). The moderation test was conducted by forming an in teractive construct (X × M) in a
structural model as recommended in the SEM -PLS approach. The moderation effect is
declared significant if the interaction construct has a T -statistic value > 1.96 and a p -value <
0.05.
This moderation analysis is important to explain how digital marketing capabilities act as a
reinforcing factor in optimizing the use of local branding as a strategic resource for MSMEs,
in line with the Resource-Based View (RBV) perspective which emphasizes the importance of
organizational c apabilities in converting internal resources into sustainable competitive
advantages.

DISCUSSION
The Relationship between Local Branding and Competitive Advantage
The results of the hypothesis testing indicate that local branding has a positive and
significant effect on the competitive advantage of kitchen utensil -making MSMEs in Kesambi
Village. This finding indicates that the stronger the implementation of local branding that
emphasizes the identity, cultural values, and uniqueness of regional products, the higher the
competitive advantage of MSMEs, both in the form of product differentiation, increased
competitiveness, added value, and business sustainability.
This finding is in line with the Resource -Based View (RBV) perspective which emphasizes
that sustainable competitive advantage stems from the utilization of valuable, rare, difficult  to
imitate, and non -substitutable internal resources (Barney, 1991). In the context of this research,
local branding is positioned as an intangible resource that represents the unique identity of
Kesambi Village, hereditary production skills, and the value of local wisdom inherent in MSME
products. These characteristics are difficult for competitors to replicate, especially mass -
produced a nd imported products that emphasize cost efficiency rather than identity -based
differentiation.
Empirically, the results of this study are in line with research by Astuti and Wibowo (2023)
who found that MSMEs with a strong local brand image have better competitive resilience and
are less likely to be trapped in price -based competition (Astuti, Wibowo and Juwono, 2023) .
Similar findings were also put forward by Lestari (2021) who stated that branding based on local
wisdom can increase the competitiveness of MSME p roducts by creating meaningful
differentiation in the domestic market (Lestari, 2021) . In addition, Agustin et al. (2024)
emphasized that strengthening local identity as part of a branding strategy contributes to
increasing the perceived value and competitive advantage of MSMEs (Agustin et al., 2024).
The results of this study also reinforce the view of Rahmawati et al. (2023) who stated that
intangible assets such as brand image and local identity have a more dominant strategic role
than physical resources in building the co mpetitiveness of MSMEs (Novi Ria Rahmawati et al.,
2023). Thus, local branding functions not only as a symbolic element, but also as a strategic
instrument capable of creating added value and sustainable differentiation. However, it does not
always show consistent results. Research by Prasetyo et al. (2022) actually found that branding,
including local branding, does not have a sig nificant effect on the competitive advantage of
MSMEs, especially in the small -scale manufacturing sector, where competitiveness is more
determined by operational factors such as cost efficiency, production capacity, and pricing
strategy (Prasetyo et al., 2022) . This difference in findings can be explained by differences in the
context and characteristics of the research objects, where in MSMEs that have not yet managed
their local identity strategically and are still oriented towards price competition, branding tends
not to be able to be converted into a source of competitive advantage. In line with these findings,
other research also shows that the adoption of digital marketing in MSMEs does not necessarily

In the context of MSMEs in Kesambi Village, local branding is a key differentiating factor

utilitarian function, but also from the emotional and authentic value felt by consumers.
Practically, these findings demonstrate that MSMEs need to consistently manage local
branding, for example by showcasing regional identity on packaging, product labels, and
marketing narratives. This local identity -based differentiation allows MSMEs to emerge from
price-based competition and build a better perception of quality in the eyes of consumers,
ultimately enhancing business competitiveness and sustainability.

The Relationship between Digital Marketing Adoption and Competitive Advantage
The results of the hypothesis testing indicate that digital marketing adoption does not
significantly impact the competitive advantage of MSMEs. This finding indicates that the level of
digital marketing adoption by MSMEs has not directly increased business competitiveness,
expanded competitive position, or created sustainable advantages. In other words, the use of
digital media by MSMEs has not yet fully converted into a tangible source of competitive
advantage.
From a Resource-Based View (RBV) perspective, digital marketing adoption is viewed as an
organizational capability, specifically the ability of MSMEs to integrate digital technology into
marketing activities. However, the RBV emphasizes that the mere existence of capabilities is
insufficient to create a competitive advantage if those capabilities are not optimally managed,
are not unique, and are easily imitated by other business actors. This indicates that the adoption
of digital marketing that is still basic and operational has not been able to meet the criteria of a
valuable, rare, difficult to imitate, and irreplaceable (VRIN) strategic resource, so its impact on
competitive advantage is insignificant.  In the context of business development, the use of digital
technology is seen as being able to influence the effectiveness of the business planning process
for business actors, including MSMEs (Rusnanti et al., 2025).
The findings of this study align with several previous studies that stated that digital
marketing adoption does not always have a significant impact on the competitiveness of MSMEs.
Research by Andika et al. (2021) found that although MSMEs have utilized digital marketing, its
impact on competitive advantage is still limited due to low digital literacy and weak strategic
marketing content management capabilities (Andika et al., 2021) . Similar results were also
presented by Prasetyo et al. (2022) who stated that in small -scale manufacturing MSMEs, the
focus of business actors is still predominantly on production and cost efficiency aspects, causing
digital marketing adoption to be unable to significantly increase competitive advantage (Prasetyo
et al., 2022) . In addition, Cn and Helmita (2023) showed that MSME marketing digitalization is
often partial and not yet integrated, so that digital media functions more as a means of
conveying information than as a strategic tool for building business differentiation and

strategies, internal resource readiness, and the integration of digital marketing with brand
strengthening and product differentiation.
In the context of MSMEs in Kesambi Village, the results of this study indicate that the use
of digital media such as social med ia and marketplaces is still in its early stages, limited to
expanding the reach of product information and facilitating communication with customers.
However, the use of digital marketing has not been strategically integrated with strengthening
brand identity, product differentiation, and the creation of sustainable unique value. This
condition has resulted in Digital Marketing Adoption not being able to play a decisive role in
building MSMEs' competitive advantage, especially amidst competition with impor ted products
and large periodic manufacturers.
Thus, the results of this study confirm that digital marketing adoption has not yet become
a significant factor influencing the competitive advantage of MSMEs, but rather still functions as
a tool to support marketing operations. For digital marketing to contribute strategically to
competitiveness, MSMEs need to improve the quality of their digital capabilities by strengthening
human resource competencies, integrating digital marketing strategies with local bra nding, and
managing digital platforms consistently and oriented towards long-term value creation.
When linked to the Digital Marketing Adoption indicator, the insignificant direct impact on
competitive advantage indicates that digital media utilization by MSMEs is operational and not
yet strategic. Indicators of digital media utilization and intensity indicate that most optimally
integrate it with product differentiation strategies. Furthermore, uploaded marketing content
does not fully emphasize local iden tity and product uniqueness, thus digital marketing has not
been able to create sustainable added value.
Practically, these findings indicate that digital marketing alone is not enough to build a
competitive advantage. MSMEs need to improve the quality of their digital content management,
strengthen customer interactions, and align digital marketing activities with local branding
strategies so that digital media serves not only as a promotional tool but also as a tool for
creating business competitiveness.

CONCLUSION
Based on the data analysis and discussion, several key conclusions can be drawn from this
study. This study aims to analyze the influence of local branding on the competitive advantage of
MSMEs, the role of digital marketing adoption on competitive advantage, and the function of
digital marketing adoption as a moderating variable in the relationship between local branding
and competitive advantage.

First, the research results show that local branding has a positive and significant impact on
the competitive advantage of MSMEs. This finding confirms that local branding is a strategic
intangible resource in building business competitiveness. Local identity, cultural values, product
differentiation, and the perception of quality inherent in a product can create a uniqueness that
is difficult for competitors to imitate. Thus, local branding serves not only as a symbolic identity
but also as a strategic asset that drives the sustainability and competitive advantage of MSMEs.
Second, digital marketing ad option does not have a significant direct impact on the
competitive advantage of MSMEs. This finding indicates that MSMEs' use of digital media is still
operational and has not been fully utilized as a strategy to create competitive advantage. The
use of digital media that is not yet integrated with differentiation and branding strategies means
that digital marketing is unable to independently increase business profitability.
Third, the research findings demonstrate that digital marketing adoption acts as a
moderating variable, strengthening the influence of local branding on competitive advantage.
This demonstrates that digital marketing serves as a supporting capability that optimizes the use
of local branding. When the identity, cultural value, and uniqueness of local products are
effectively communicated through digital media, the impact of local branding on competitive
advantage becomes stronger and more widespread.
Overall, the results of this study confirm that the competitive advantage of MSMEs is not
only determined by the adoption of digital technology, but primarily by the ability of MSMEs to
strategically manage and combine local resources with digital capabilities. Local branding is the
primary foundation of competitive advantage, while digital marketing serves as a tool that
strengthens and extends the value of that branding. This finding aligns with the Resource -Based
View (RBV) perspective, which emphasizes the importance of managing unique and difficult -to-
imitate resources in creating sustainable competitive advantage.

SUGGESTIONS
1. Developing Research Variables
Further research is recommended to add other variables that have the potential to influence
MSME competitive advantage, such as entrepreneurial orientation, product innovation,
human resource quality, digital literacy, or information technology capabilities. Discussion of
these variables is expected to provide a more comprehensi ve picture of the factors
influencing MSME competitiveness.
2. Deepening the Role of Digital Marketing
Given that the research results indicate that digital marketing adoption does not always have
a direct effect on competitive advantage, future research can examine the role of digital
marketing as a mediating or moderating variable with a more specific approach, for
example, by separating the dimensions of use of more specific approaches, such as social
media, marketplaces, and digital content.
3. Expanding the Research Object and Area
This research is limited to kitchen equipment MSMEs in Kesambi Village. Therefore, future
researchers are recommended to expand the research object to other types of MSMEs or
different regions to provide broader generalizability.
4. Longitudinal Approach
Further research can also use a longitudinal research design to observe changes in digital
marketing adoption behavior and their impact on MSMEs' competitive advantage over a
specific period of time, thereby more accurately capturing t he dynamics of business
development.
