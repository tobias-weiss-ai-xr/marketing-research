Abstract. This study is based on case analysis and has three dimensions: policy changes,
market competition and digital marketing which represent Tesla's multidimensional
development. The study indicates that in differentiated policy regimes such as China, Europe
and the United States, Tesla has proven to be globally adaptable, while simultaneously being
impacted by geopolitical and supply chain localization pressures. On the competitive side,
the pattern now is that Tesla has three tracks of technology where it is clashing with Build
Your Dreams and Toyota, while its brand and intelligence edge is being undermined by its
slow pace of product iteration. The direct sales and social media model, which are
dependent on a zero-advertising approach, are in dire need to advance in terms of user
operations, and set examples in terms of efficiency, for digital marketing. Tesla should
further strengthen the regional localization cooperation at the policy level to avoid the risks
of geopolitics, increase the frequency of product iteration in the market competition and
develop into lower-tier markets. Meanwhile, it must investigate the process of moving away
from an advertising-free model to targeted user interactions and a community fission mode,
to create a more sustainable growth model. The results of this study indicate that the major
keys to Tesla's future development are the introduction of new models, commercialization of
Full Self-Driving and a new brand-cost balance.
Keywords: Tesla, policy, market competition, digital marketing
1. Introduction
Under the macro background of the deep change of the global automotive industry and the rapid
restructuring of the energy system, new energy vehicles (NEVs) have already gone through the
technology verification stage and entered a new stage of large-scale popularization and multi-route
competition. The policy direction in recent years for NEVs in major economies has undergone
significant changes, with China's purchase tax on NEVs being reduced from full exemption to 50%
since 2026 (with the ratio of "double-credit" rising from 38% to 48%) [1], the European Union's
(EU) carbon border adjustment mechanism being gradually implemented and the United States'
(US) Inflation Reduction Act's requirements for domestic manufacturing being continuously raised.
The falling policy dividends and rising entry thresholds have forced automakers to change from
relying on an external incentive to endogenous capacity building. At the same time, empirical

evidence has proven the fuel economy benefits of battery electric vehicles (BEVs) over conventional
internal combustion engine vehicles: BEVs save over 44% on fuel costs, 34% when charging from

Therefore, it is necessary to systematically assess the strategic adaptability of the leading NEV
enterprises, which has research significance. As a global first mover in the pure electric vehicle
industry, Tesla has been enjoying the advantage of early policy support and first-mover
technological advantages; however, it is facing a number of challenges including market saturation,
growing competition and management bottlenecks. By analyzing the actions and performance of
Tesla in three aspects: policy, competition, and marketing, it is possible not only to understand the
logic of the evolution of the NEV industry, but also to serve as a reference for similar businesses in
the strategic adjustment process.
The analysis of the case of Tesla Motors is carried out from three perspectives: policy, market
competition and digital marketing. On the policy side, the half sale of the purchase tax in China has
speeded dividends' downward trend, which has pushed Tesla to enhance cost control, while the "dual
credit" ratio has been raised to 48%. Market competition wise, the brand "Build Your Dreams"
(BYD) became the global pure-electric sales champion in 2025, taking advantage of the scale
advantage, whilst Toyota sped up its pure-electric transformation on a hybrid base. The three
companies created a strategic competition pattern in which there is a "first mover," a "scale player"
and a "transformer." From a digital marketing standpoint, Tesla has created a unique operating
system from selling hardware to continuously generating sustainable service revenue via Direct-to-
Consumer (DTC), continuous iteration, and Full Self-Driving (FSD) software subscription changes.
Collectively these three dimensions define Tesla's strategic decisions and sustainability outlook in a
context of retightening policies and ramping up competition.
The method of research used in this paper is the comparative analysis and case study from
multiple dimensions. From the policy perspective, the two most dominant policy developments in
China, the European Union and the United States are policy decline and policy tightening in the last
two years (e.g. modification of purchase tax, "double credit" ratio requirements and carbon tariff
provisions). The market competition dimension highlights how, from publicly available data on sales
figures, financial reports, and product matrix from the three companies from 2025 to 2026, Tesla and
BYD swap positions for the top spot in global pure electric vehicle sales and how Toyota's pace of
transition to electric vehicles differs from that of online competitor Tesla. Digital marketing and
corporate governance are key areas, featuring practices like Tesla's DTC direct sales model and its
Over-The-Air (OTA) continuous update capability, as well as a changing business model to software
subscriptions and an owner loyalty ecosystem. They are based on the comparison with the dealer
network of BYD Company and the Toyota Company's global production management system [2].
This study was carried out through systematic analysis of the three dimensions, and it shows the
strategic choices and sustainability of Tesla in the face of the triple pressures of declining policy
dividends, heightened competition, and management change. It draws out experiences for the new
energy vehicle industry in terms of technical paths, market positioning and use by users, and gives a
fundamental analytical framework for further research on the competitiveness modeling of electric
vehicle companies.

2. Research purpose and the background of Tesla's business
2.1. Tesla motors: Case background overview and development history of Tesla motors
Tesla is an international leader in electric vehicles and clean energy, seeking to accelerate the world's
transition to sustainable energy. Currently, the automotive sector is the largest, and its share of
revenues is 79% of the total, which is 97.7 billion US dollars in 2024, but its growth rate is being
slowed down [3]. The company is actively developing its energy storage, autonomous driving and
robotics businesses, which come with a price tag of about $35000 per vehicle, as well as facing
challenges of reducing margins due to price competition, and a growing competitive market, while
retaining technological barriers and brand value by vertically integrating, software subscription
(FSD Gross Profit >80%) and an ecosystem of more than 50000 charging stations [3].
2.2. Research purpose and significance
Summarize and expound the essential facts and trends of Tesla in three aspects: domestic and
international policies on new energy vehicles, the structure of competition and digital marketing
management. The three-dimensional nature of the reality (policy responses, evolution of the
company's competitive situation, the zero-advertising direct sales model) is clearly discussed, which
allows the multiple factors driving Tesla's strategic behavior to be understood. Policy-wise, Tesla is
flexing to the various policies of China, the US and Europe (dual-credit system in China, Inflation
Reduction Act in the US and carbon tariffs in EU) and is trying to localize. In the dimension of
market competition, to cope with price wars and share struggles, maintain advantages through cost
control and new businesses, such as energy storage and autonomous driving. In the digital world,
zero advertising, zero direct sales DTC, and data-driven full cycle operations create an efficient
management paradigm.
3. Three-dimensional system analysis on policy, market competition and digital marketing
3.1. Home and abroad new energy vehicle and policy environment
In China, the transformation from inclusive subsidy to "competition-oriented" policy system is
occurring under the guidance of policy measures, such as the new energy vehicle purchase tax
reduction and double credit, which encourage the survival of the fittest in the industry. From 2026,
the purchase tax will no longer be exempt but reduced to half (5%); The car company credit ratio
requirement for new energy vehicles will be raised from 30000 yuan to 15000 yuan in 2026 and
2027, putting higher demands on the compliance of companies. Meanwhile, China is continuing to
enhance its technical standards and data security management. Tesla's supervised FSD was granted
to enter China in May of 2026, subject to data storage requirements in the country [4].
The China Electric Vehicle Hundred People's Association and the industry associations have an
open and guiding attitude towards this with the focus on compliance and standard setting. With the
coming of FSD, the industry will have to speed up its transition to intelligence, while competition
will be further sparked in the field of advanced intelligent driving. The policy transformation, the
redoubled price wars, as well as the delayed introduction of FSD into China and the slower iteration
speed of intelligent driving systems than local brands are all steeply challenging Tesla's product
strength and price advantages in China. The EU is developing a "carbon compliance + localization"
access system to counteract the macro context of contracting policies and increasing barriers to new

3.2. Analysis of competitive economy and competitive landscape
From an innovation ecosystem perspective, the characteristics of the market economy and
competition of Tesla Motors include Tesla builds a disrupting innovation ecosystem by recruiting
Silicon Valley and Internet talents; Toyota's symbiotic ecosystem is based on complementary
resources in the world, BYD's open platform ecosystem is developed in the ecosystem of the
industrial cluster in Shenzhen. Market positioning: Tesla model is to pursue "high-end leadership"
policy, first to enter the "high-end" car market, and then to open up the "mid-level" market, and the
initial car market segment is the "high-end" car market, "environmental protection pioneer." This
differs from the public transport and low end of the market that BYD has been pursuing and Toyota's
"progressive penetration" strategy which takes a full range of hybrid vehicles.
As it comes to technological innovation paths, Tesla is a fast disruptor with its own original
technologies such as lithium-ion batteries, dual motor all-wheel drive and aluminum bodies, while
Toyota is a gradualist – and is using hybrid tech as a buffer – and BYD is a base foundation, with
battery technology. Business model: Tesla is an asset light company and uses experiential marketing
– fixed assets reduced with IPO, strategic financing and outsourcing production and a brand
premium is achieved by direct contact with consumers.
In contrast, Toyota is using the lean manufacturing and global production and sales network,
BYD is expanding its size by using the public transportation sector and financial lease models [6].
Tesla is facing challenges due to a lack of charging infrastructure, policy barriers, etc. in the
competitive landscape, but its technology open source strategy and high-end brand have formed a
moat; Toyota has advantages in scale with 7 million hybrid sales, and a global layout; BYD has
moats in the public sector by providing low-cost integrated innovation and core battery technology
[6].
The three firms have three battlegrounds: "high-end disruption," "global symbiosis," and "low-
end integration." For Tesla to leverage its first-mover advantage and maintain competitiveness in the
market, it must overcome the scale hurdle while still being ahead in the technology curve. The
competition structure of the Chinese market for new energy vehicles has been reshaped by Tesla's
launch, based on the sales data in 2020–2021 in six cities of China [7]. Consumers would like EVs
that are less expensive, consume less energy per 100 km, have medium range, and large, heavy
body. Even with the strong competition of local auto makers, Tesla maintains large market share as a
luxury brand, whereas the new forces – the emerging Chinese brands – are showing good
competitive ability by using the differentiation strategy [7]. Counterfactual analysis reveals that
introduction of Tesla has increased the variety of EVs and has positive effects on consumers'

3.3. Digital marketing and enterprise operation management
3.3.1. Digital marketing
Tesla's digital marketing strategy in the Chinese market is primarily manifested in the following
aspects: First, direct connection with consumers via its official website and online platforms to build
a "Service Plus" direct sales model, establish sales centers and online connections, and create a
unified brand experience; second, continuously provide the updated software of the product through
OTA update and maintain the technological leadership; third, shape the brand image with social
media and online channels, and combine the personal influence of Elon Musk to do digital
communication; fourth, carry out the precision marketing with data, and adapt the application of
digital function (such as rear seat entertainment system) according to the needs of the Chinese
market [8]. Beyond that, Tesla's strategy to sell its cars directly online, eliminating the need for
traditional dealerships, guarantees transparency of pricing and satisfaction for buyers, while its user-
friendly customer experience is further bolstered by a network of digital charging stations.
3.3.2. Enterprise operation management
Tesla's supply chain management is very collaborative, and they develop their strategies by forming
a variety of partnerships. It has included supplier cooperation with Lotus Automobile to share
factories and platforms to reduce manufacturing costs, to conduct research and development with
Panasonic and jointly develop battery technology, and to establish strategic partnerships with
automotive manufacturers like Toyota and Daimler for capital injection, engineering technology and
production experience, while also cooperating with the US government for loan support.
The four steps of enterprise management are: finding the driving force (such as obtaining vehicle
design and manufacturing technology); finding the facilitating factor (complementary corporate
style); finding the component factors (setting up joint working groups, drawing up stage plans, etc.)
and finding the expected results (technological upgrading, self-built factory goals, etc.). Tesla has
been able to customize the different formal or long-term partnerships flexibly, and in the end,
become an independent car company and own its production after resource integration, cost
reduction, technological upgrade and customer satisfaction.
4. Suggestions
4.1. The enterprise development according to policy strategies for the local state plan needs to
be adjusted
Take advantage of the price difference on trading in old and new cars in China, high-end models
above 167, 000 yuan, package FSD monthly fees to obtain data compliance dividends, avoid EU
carbon tariffs, rapidly establish local supply chains and fully comply with regulations, accelerate
cost reduction and efficiency improvement in the United States due to the end of subsidies, and use
carbon credits to offset transformation software profits.

4.2. Market driven strategy
Market driven strategy recommendations based on the market economy and the environment of
competition: Enterprises should build a culture of market orientation, focus on customers,
continuously innovate and develop their own unique capabilities that cannot be simply imitated.
They should also pay attention to the value the customers appreciate and use value perception
pricing rather than cost pricing. They should also monitor the external opportunities and threats as
well as the internal strengths and weaknesses and ensure sustainable competitive advantage through
strategic circular evolution [9].
4.3. Management strategies
Proposals for Tesla's management strategy are to take the progressive innovation mode,
continuously improve the battery performance and cost according to existing technology, and
balance the technology's iterative development with the market penetration; carry out the open
innovation, establish the industry standard based on patent open source, integrate the internal and
external knowledge resources, form the cross-disciplinary team to promote the technological
integration; implement the vertical integration and direct sales; adopt the strategy of offline
experience and online customization to alleviate the pressure of inventory, feel the pulse of the
dynamic adjustment of the competitive environment, apply for the policy of financial aid, promote
the development of green technology, and maintain the leading position in the fierce new energy car
market [10].
5. Conclusion
Tesla has shown varied flexibility when it comes to the policy adjustments of the three key markets:
China, Europe and the United States. Now the United States is trying to get the subsidy benefits
from the Inflation Reduction Act, but at the same time is under pressure from labor unions and
localization of the supply chain; The European market is impacted by carbon tariffs and China's EV
import policies, and must balance the Berlin factory with exports to Shanghai; The competition in
the Chinese market is tough, and Tesla is using a variety of methods, including tweaking prices and
upgrading configurations, to deal with changes to policy; and China's foreign investment access
policy relaxation is favorable to Tesla. In conclusion, Tesla's global footprint is quite robust;
however, it is very exposed to one market (Shanghai factory), and geopolitical and tariff risks are the
largest risks.
As a part of the competition, there are three different paths and logics in technology: Tesla, BYD
and Toyota. In China, BYD is ahead of Tesla in sales and product iteration speed, with vertical
integration and hybrid + pure electric dual-line car development strategy; Toyota maintains a
diversified strategy of hybrid, hydrogen BEV car development. Although it is slower on the uptake
of electrification, it still has the edge when it comes to the fact that it has a huge global production
and sales network, plus cost control. Tesla has a number of advantages, including brand premium,
being the first name that comes to mind for autonomous driving vehicles, and its direct-selling
model. However, the slow adoption of new models and the old product portfolio have directly
affected its mainstream price range, which includes models like BYD Seal/Han. The race among the
three has evolved from "electrification" to a new round of "intelligence + cost control."
Tesla has set the example of direct (zero advertising) sales + online booking in the digital
marketing and enterprise operation management. The user fission and the topicality of a brand have
