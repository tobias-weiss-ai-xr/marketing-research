Abstract
The rapid proliferation of social media platforms has fundamentally transformed brand-consumer
dynamics, positioning social media engagement as a vital predictor of consumer behaviour. This
conceptual paper investigates the relationship between social medi a engagement and consumer
purchase intention, with a specific focus on the moderating role of Hofstede’s cultural dimension
of masculinity versus femininity. While existing literature often assumes a uniform impact of digital
interaction, this study argues  that cultural values surrounding achievement, assertiveness, and
nurturing, significantly alter how consumers interpret digital marketing stimuli. The study adopts
a systematic content analysis approach, synthesizing theoretical insights and empirical evidence
from reputable scholarly databases published between 2017 and 2025. This methodology enables
the identification of patterns, theoretical gaps, and the synthesis of a robust conceptual framework
grounded in Uses and Gratifications Theory (UGT) and Cul tural Dimensions Theory. Findings
from the synthesis indicate that while social media engagement; comprising frequency of
interaction, time spent, and brand -related content interactions p ositively drives purchase
intention, the efficacy of specific messaging is culturally contingent. The study concludes that a
"one-size-fits-all" digital strategy is inadequate in a globalized market. It recommends that
marketers adopt culturally tailored e ngagement strategies and invest in training for marketing
teams regarding cultural psychology to align brand narratives with the deep -seated values of
diverse audience segments.

Keywords: Social media engagement, consumer purchase intention, masculinity vs. femininity,
digital marketing, cultural orientation

1. Introduction
Social media has fundamentally transformed the marketing landscape, redefining how brands
communicate and engage with consumers on a global scale. Platforms such as Facebook,
Instagram, and TikTok are n o longer mere promotional tools,  they have become dynamic arenas
of interaction where consumers co-create brand narratives (Appel, et al, 2020). This rise of social
media engagement manifested in actions such as following brand pages, liking, sharing,
commenting, and interacting with con tent has been widely acknowledged as a critical driver of

increased social media engagement fosters brand trust, loyalty, and ultimately purchase intention

of marketing messages; they are empowered participants who shape brand identity and influence
the purchasing behavior of others (Majeed, et al, 2021).
However, it would be misguided to assume that the effects of social media engagement are uniform
or universally effective. As Hu and Zhu (2022) rightly argue, the mere usage of social media does

highlight a critical blind spot in the literature: that the mechanisms through which social media

that consumer engagement is neither homogenous nor culturally neutral; individu al and cultural
differences significantly determine how consumers interpret and respond to digital marketing
stimuli.
While a wealth of studies (Dincer & Dincer, 2023); Sharkasi & Rezakhah, (2023); Nguyen, Nhu
& Duong (2020); Rais, Khan & Zaheer (2023); Tirpude, (2022); Liang, 2017) affirm a robust link
between social media engagement and consumer purchase intentions, they often overlook a critical
dimension: culture. More specifically, there is a conspicuous gap in the literature regarding how
cultural values, particularly those surrounding gender roles, moderate this relationship. By
neglecting these dimensions, researchers risk presenting a one -size-fits-all model of consumer
engagement that fails to capture the complexities of global consumer behavior.
In a world where brands must navigate increasingly pluralistic and culturally diverse markets,
ignoring cultural moderators is no longer tenable (Zimu, 2023). The masculinity versus femininity
dimension of Hofstede’s cultural framework offers a compelling lens to examine these variations.
Masculine cultures, which prioritize assertiveness, achievement, and material success, may
interpret social media content through a lens of status and competition. In contrast, feminine
cultures, which value emotional conn ection, cooperation, and quality of life, may favor brand
messages that are relational, empathetic, and inclusive.
This conceptual paper argues that the masculinity –femininity cultural dimension plays a pivotal
moderating role in the relationship between social media engagement and consumer purchase
intention. Despite the growing acknowledgment that culture influences persuasion and decision-
making processes, this cultural variable remains underexplored in the context of digital marketing.
By integrating theoretical insights from cross-cultural psychology and marketing communications,
this study aims to critically examine whether, and how, gendered cultural values alter the efficacy
of social media strategies across markets. In doing so, this paper not only fills a notable gap in
current literature but also provides practical guidance for marketers seeking to craft cultu rally
nuanced social media campaigns that resonate with distinct consumer segments worldwide.

1.1 Research Objectives
i. To examine the relationship between social media engagement (frequency, time spent, and
interaction) and consumer purchase intention.
ii. To investigate how the cultural dimension of Masculinity vs. Femininity moderates the
impact of social media engagement on purchase decisions.
iii. To explore the underlying gratifications that drive user engagement in different cultural
contexts.

2. Literature Review
2.1 Concept of Social Media Engagement
Social media engagement refers to the degree of active participation, interaction, and involvement
that users exhibit with content, brands, or communities on social media platforms. It encompasses
a wide range of behaviors, including liking, sharing, commenting on posts, following brand pages,
participating in discussions, clicking advertisements, and responding to polls or contests (Dessart,
et al, 2020). This engagement is not limited to passive content consumption but involves dynamic,
reciprocal exchanges between the brand and its audience, fostering deeper relationships and loyalty
(Hollebeek, et al, 2020).
The significance of social media engagement in marketing has grown in recent years as brands
increasingly recognize its influence on consumer perceptions, trust, and ultimately, purchase
intentions. High engagement levels are often viewed as indicators of consumer interest and brand
resonance, providing marketers with valuable insights for tailoring digital content and strategies
(Appel et al., 2020). Engagement can be measured both quantitatively (e.g., frequency of likes,
shares, comments) and qualitatively (e.g., sentiment analysis of user-generated content), allowing
for comprehensive assessment of how consumers interact with brands online (Pradhan et al.,
2023).
Furthermore, social media engagement is shaped by various factors, including individual
preferences, cultural values, and the relevance of content to the audience. (Hu & Zhu, 2022). For
brands, fostering meaningful engagement requires not only the dissemination of promotional
messages but also the creation of interactive, value -driven experiences that encourage users to
participate, share, and advocate for the brand (Gentina & Singelis, 2022).

2.2 Concept of Consumer Purchase Intentions
Consumer purchase intentions refer to an individual’s expressed likelihood or willingness to buy
a particular product or service in the near future. This concept is widely used in marketing and
consumer behaviour research as a predictor of actual purchasin g behaviour, providing valuable
insights into how marketing strategies and external influences influence consumer decision -
making processes (Pradhan et al., 2023). In the context of social media, purchase intentions are
shaped by various factors, including  exposure to brand -related content, engagement with
advertisements, peer influence, and the perceived value or trustworthiness of the brand (Okeke  et
al, 2022). When consumers interact with brands on digital platforms through activities such as
liking, sharing, commenting, or participating in promotions their attitudes and readiness to make
a purchase can be influenced either positively or negatively. Purchase intention is often measured
using self-reported survey items that assess the consumer’s agreement with statements such as “I
intend to buy this product after seeing it on social media” or “I am likely to purchase products
promoted by brands I follow online.” This variable serves as a crucial outcome in evaluating the

2.3 Concept of Cultural Orientation
As explained by Shi (2023) , culture is a powerful force that influences the everyday lives of
consumers, therefore, it is a considerable factor when shaping consumer behaviour around
different parts of the world. Juršė. Makackaitė, Jakutytė & Kievišienė  (2019),  defines cultural
orientation as an intricate web of shared beliefs, customs, values, traditions, religion, and symbols
that are used to identify a group or society. The most thoroughly explored cultural dimensions in
research are those identified by the researcher Geert Hof stede (1984). These dimensions are,
namely, Power Distance, Individualism / Collectivism, Masculinity / Femininity, Uncertainty
Avoidance, and, lastly, Long/Short term orientation. (Juršė et al., 2019) . According to Hofstede
(1984), every culture is assigned a score on each cultural dimension, which helps researchers to
understand how people belonging to a specific culture function and express themselves. Asamoah
and Chovancová (2016), stated the reason why these dimensions are broadly explored in marketing
and advertising research is that they are believed to influence how people are persuaded and
influenced by advertising messages.  In fact, de Mooij, (2017)  states that not taking into
consideration the influence of culture in the marketing area has led several companies to lose profit,
because they lack deep knowledge of the local culture.

2.4  Theoretical Perspective on the Moderating Role of Masculinity vs. Femininity
The assertion that cultural orientation significantly shapes consumer behavior is not merely
theoretical, it is both practical and undeniable. Among the most critical cultural dimensions
influencing digital consumer interactions is masculinity versus femininity, as posited in Hofstede’s
(2011) framework. According to This dimension focuses on the extent to which a society stresses
achievement or nurture. Masculinity is seen to be the trait which emphasizes ambition, acquisition
of wealth, and differentiated gender roles. Femininity is seen to be the trait which stress caring,
and nurturing behaviors, sexuality equality, environmental awareness and more fluid gender roles.
In Hofstede’s definition, “masculinity stands for a society in which social gender roles are clearly
distinct: Men are supposed to be assertive, tough, and focused on material success; women are
supposed to be more modest, tender, and concerned with the qu ality of life.” “Femininity stands
for a society in which social gender roles overlap: Both men and women are supposed to be modest,
tender and concerned with the quality of life.” (Hofstede, 2001).
It is arguable that masculine societies, which valorize competition, assertiveness, and status
achievement, are more inclined to interpret social media content through a lens of ambition and
personal advancement. In such contexts, marketing strategies that  highlight dominance, success,
and exclusivity are not only effective they are essential. Consumers in these societies are culturally
conditioned to aspire for symbols of strength and prestige, and social media campaigns that fail to
communicate these values are likely to fall flat.
Conversely, dismissing the role of feminine cultural values, which elevate empathy, community,
and quality of life, can be equally detrimental to brand messaging. As Farina (2021) contends,
consumers in feminine-oriented societies gravitate towards marketing communications that reflect
emotional intelligence, inclusivity, and social cohesion. This is supported by Gentina and Singelis
(2022), who argue that emotional resonance and ethical appeal often carry more persuasive weight
in these contexts than competitive advantage or technical superiority. Thus, the cultural dichotomy
between masculinity and femininity must be acknowledged as a crucial moderating forc e in

2.5  Theoretical Framework
This study explores the relationship between Social Media Engagement and Consumer Purchase
Intention, with cultural orientation serving as a moderating variable. The study is underpinned by
the Uses and Gratification Theory (UGT) and Hoefstede’s Cultural Dimension Theory. The
integration of these theories is particularly suitable because it allows the study to capture both the
motivations behind consumer engagement (UGT) and the way cultural context alters the impact
of that engagement (Hofstede).

2.5.1 Uses and Gratifications Theory (UGT)
Developed by Katz, Blumler, and Gurevitch (1973), UGT posits that individuals are active agents
who seek out specific media to satisfy psychological and social needs, such as information -
seeking, entertainment, personal identity, and social interaction . Unlike traditional theories that
view the audience as passive, UGT emphasizes that social media engagement is user -driven and
intentional. Within this framework, the fulfillment of these gratifications is hypothesized to
influence the consumer's purchase intention, measured by their willingness and likelihood to buy.

2.5.2 Hofstede’s Cultural Dimensions Theory (Masculinity vs. Femininity)
While UGT explains the mechanisms of engagement, Hofstede’s (1984)  theory provides the
moderating context by examining how cultural value systems shape consumer responses.

2.5.3 Synthesis of the Conceptual Model
The core of this framework lies in the synthesis bridge  between these two theories . While social
media engagement serves as the independent variable and purchase intention as the dependent
variable, the Masculinity vs. Femininity  dimension acts as the "switch" that determines which
types of gratifications and messages are most effective . This layered approach recognizes that
engagement does not affect all consumers uniformly; its strength and direction are steered by the
underlying cultural value systems of the target audience.

3.1  Methodology
This study adopts a qualitative content analysis approach, employing a systematic review of
existing literature to examine the relationship between social media engagement and consumer
purchase intention, as moderated by the masculinity-femininity cultural dimension. As noted by
Snyder (2019), a systematic review is appropriate for synthesizing evidence from diverse empirical
studies and theoretical discussions, providing a structured evaluation of patterns and moderating
influences across various cultural contexts. This method allows for the identification of key
measurement proxies and the recognition of common gaps in existing research regarding cultural
moderators in digital marketing.
The study relies entirely on secondary data obtained from reputable and scholarly sources,
including peer-reviewed journal articles, published conference papers, and industry-based research
reports. To ensure the currency and relevance of the data, the review focuses primarily on literature
published between 2017 and 2025. Relevant literature was gathered from high-impact academic
databases, specifically: ScienceDirect and SpringerLink for marketing and psychological theories,
Taylor & Francis and Google Scholar for cross-cultural consumer behavior studies, and
ResearchGate for contemporary industry insights and conference proceedings.
The data analysis process involved three distinct stages. First, the identification stage used search
strings such as "social media engagement," "purchase intention," and "Hofstede’s masculinity vs.
femininity". Second, the screening stage evaluated sources based on their methodological quality
and focus on cultural moderation. Finally, the synthesis stage compared findings across various
studies to understand how digital engagement translates into purchase behavior under different
cultural conditions. This systematic method provides a solid foundation for future research and
offers practical applications for digital marketing strategies in globalized markets.

4.1 Conclusion
This study concludes that social media engagement plays a significant and measurable role in
shaping consumer purchase intention. The three key components of engagement: frequency of
interaction, time spent, and brand -related content interactions are stron g indicators of consumer
responsiveness to digital marketing efforts. When consumers engage actively with branded
content, they are more likely to exhibit positive attitudes toward a product or service, ultimately
increasing their likelihood and willingness to make a purchase.
The cultural dimension of masculinity vs. femininity moderates this relationship by influencing
how individuals interpret and respond to social media content. Consumers from masculine cultures
are more persuaded by assertive, goal -oriented, and competitive  messaging, while those from
feminine cultures respond better to relational, emotional, and quality-of-life–oriented content. This
suggests that marketers and content creators must not only focus on generating high engagement
but also ensure cultural alignment and emotional relevance in their messaging strategies.
 In essence, for businesses and marketers to successfully drive purchase behavior through social
media, they must consider both the quantitative aspects of engagement and the qualitative, cultural
nuances that shape consumer perceptions. Tailoring strategies to fit diverse audience profiles can
enhance the impact of digital marketing and foster long-term customer loyalty.

5.1 Proposition
Building on the limitations of this study, several suggestions are proposed to guide future research:
i. Future studies should collect primary data through surveys, interviews, or experiments to
empirically test a conceptual framework for the study. This will allow for stronger validation of
the causal relationships between social media engagement, cultural orientation, and consumer
purchase intention.
ii. While this study focused on the masculinity vs. femininity dimension of culture, future
studies can expand the scope by incorporating other cultural variables from Hofstede’s framework,
such as individualism vs. collectivism or uncertainty avoidance, to gain a broader understanding
of cultural influences on consumer behavior.
