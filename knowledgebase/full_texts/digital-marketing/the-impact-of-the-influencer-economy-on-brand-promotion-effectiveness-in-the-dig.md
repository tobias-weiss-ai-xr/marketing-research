Abstract. With the quick growth of social media, influencer-owned DTC brands have
emerged as a new business form, which differs from the traditional influencer who endorses
third-party brands. Taking MrBeast's Feastables Chocolate as a single case, based on Source
Credibility Theory and Parasocial Interaction Theory, and relying on multiple public
secondary data sources, including the official Feastables website, open‑access YouTube
videos and social‑media public metrics, industry analysis reports, and mainstream
news‑media coverage, to explore the brand promotion mechanism, performance, benefit,
and potential risks of influencer-owned DTC brands. The results show that influencers'
credibility and parasocial emotional role can help brands achieve remarkable cold‑start
brand awareness in beginner. However, this IP transfer also faces a lot of risks, such as
influencer reputation, which means some negative events involving an influencer will
directly affect the brand's reputation, over‑reliance on personal IP, and so on, and thus lead
to the brand being unable to transform or extend.
Keywords: Digital Marketing, Social media, Influencer Economy, MrBeast, Feastables
chocolate
1. Introduction
1.1. Research background
In the digital era, social media platforms grow faster and faster; the effect of conventional
advertising decreases. Enterprises are willing to put more budget into influencer marketing, which
means using online celebrities through platforms such as social media to promote and sell goods or
services.
Moreover, many of the online celebrities no longer cooperate with other enterprises; they will use
their own traffic and a strong fan base to build their own brand, leveraging the trust of their fans and
parasocial interactions to transform their online fans into real consumers of physical consumer
goods.
However, online celebrities will not always maintain their fans and traffic; they might quickly
lose their fans and traffic due to matters such as tax evasion, which poses a huge challenge to their

1.2. Research significance
1.2.1. Theoretical significance
Most existing studies have focused on the effectiveness of online celebrities who has take
cooperation with third-party brands, but have insufficiently explored the promotion of online
celebrities' own consumer brands. This study takes Feastables as a case to analyze the IP
transformation path of online celebrities into their own brands, and also explores the evidence of
how the online celebrity economy drives brand promotion.
1.2.2. Practical significance
Summarizing the logic of top-stream influencers who establish their own brands, this provides
practical references and risk warnings for influencers who want to commercialize their IPs, and also
provides a way to optimize traditional brands' marketing strategies. Moreover, it also encourages
start-ups to utilize influencer resources to promote their own brands and build their brand
reputations.
1.3. Research content and methods
This paper will examine the theories of influencer marketing and brand promotion effectiveness
within the context of digital marketing. Using case analysis to explore three dimensions- Influencer
Marketing and Brand Communication, Influencer Credibility and Consumer Trust, and Influencer
Marketing and Brand Promotion- to analyze the role of the influencer economy in Feastables' brand
promotion. Additionally, a literature review is used to summarize the theoretical perspectives on the
influencer economy, digital marketing, and brand promotion to further analyze Feastables' brand
promotion. This paper is structured as follows. Chapter 2 reviews related literature and theories.
Chapter 3 introduces the methodology. Chapter 4 conducts case analysis of Feastables. Chapter 5
discusses findings. Chapter 6 summarizes conclusions, limitations, and future directions.
2. Literature review and theoretical foundation
2.1. Key concepts
2.1.1. Digital marketing
The core definition is a new marketing model for brand promotion that uses digital technology and
algorithms as its platform, achieving precise reach, personalized content, and user interaction.
Social media, search engines, and brand promotion are the three main points of digital marketing;
among them, social media is the core area for communication between brands and users, and the
influencer economy is currently the core area of growth.
The influencer economy is a derivative idea of digital marketing that has developed significantly.
The influencer economy mainly focuses on the role of influencers in promoting products within the
digital marketing environment.

2.1.2. Influencer marketing
The core definition is a marketing model that uses social media creators as the platform, utilizes
their fan base, content creation ability, and trust relationships to promote products to the audience,
and is an important part of digital marketing.
Currently, there are two mainstream models: Third-party brand endorsement: Influencers
collaborate with companies to promote their products and services, which is the most common
model at present; Influencer-owned DTC (Direct-to-Consumer) brand: Influencers use their personal
IP to create their own brands and directly achieve traffic monetization, which is an emerging
upgraded model in recent years. Existing research mostly focuses on the first model, while this study
focuses on the second one - the promotion effect and mechanism of top influencer-owned brands.
2.1.3. Brand promotion effect
The core definition is the comprehensive outcomes achieved by a brand through marketing activities
in the market, which is not a single sales aspect, but a multifaceted, comprehensive manifestation.
Three core aspects:
• Brand awareness: Market recognition, exposure breadth;
• User engagement: Community interaction, content activity level;
• Brand loyalty and brand image: Repurchase intention, brand reputation.
This study starts from the above three aspects and systematically assesses the brand promotion
effect of MrBeast's influencer economy on Feastables chocolate.
2.2. Literature review
2.2.1. Influencer marketing and brand communication
Wu Tongtong proposed a three-dimensional framework of "Technology - Content - People": AI
algorithm-based precise push, UGC (User-Generated Content) for user participation enhancement,
and the quasi-social relationship between influencers and fans for strengthening trust. These three
elements jointly drive brand communication innovation [1].
In the context of short-video platforms, the effect of content placement on social media is
significantly better than that of traditional advertising; the mimic reality assessment of live-
streaming combined with timely discounts can further reduce users' resistance to advertisements and
stimulate them to become real consumers [2].
Most of the existing research focuses on influencers acting as brand endorsers for third-party
platforms. There is a lack of systematic case studies on the creation of their own consumer brands by
influencers themselves, as well as the transformation of their personal IP into brand assets.
Particularly, there is a scarcity of deep research on top creators establishing their own brands.
2.2.2. Influencer credibility and consumer trust
Source credibility is the core foundation for the effectiveness of influencer marketing; the
attractiveness, credibility, and interactivity of influencers are the three core elements that affect the
acceptance of the audience [1].
Trust plays a significant role in the characteristics of influencers and consumer purchases;
influencers usually establish consumer trust first, and then this trust is transformed into a purchase
intention. This mechanism has been verified in various categories such as cosmetics [3].

Trust dimensions: professional ability, sincerity and integrity, and emotional intimacy. These

Most of the existing research is based on the "celebrity endorsing third-party products"; for the
DTC (Direct to Consumer) brand of celebrities themselves, there is insufficient deep analysis of how
"fans' personal trust in the celebrity" can be transformed into "organizational trust in the brand".
2.2.3. Influencer marketing and brand promotion effect
Digital marketing has a significant positive impact on brand awareness, sales conversion, and user
engagement. Influencer marketing is one of the channels with the highest ROI [4]. The selection of
suitable influencers can quickly enhance brand awareness and consumer trust, but the effectiveness
depends on the compatibility between the type of influencer and the brand positioning [5].
Although influencer marketing can quickly boost sales in the short term, the long-term brand
value ultimately depends on the product itself; popularity and heat do not equal long-term brand
assets.
Most existing studies focus on the short-term sales conversion aspect but lack research on long-
term promotion effects such as consumer loyalty.
2.3. Theoretical foundation
2.3.1. Source credibility theory
Proposed by Hovland and his colleagues, Source Credibility Theory explains that the persuasive
effect of information depends on the credibility of the information source, mainly including three
core aspects: attractiveness, credibility, and expertise. If audiences regard the information source as
trustworthy and appealing, they are more willing to accept the viewpoints and trust them [6].
In the field of influencer marketing, social media influencers act as important information sources
for product recommendations. Consumers will judge whether to accept promotional content
according to influencers' perceived reliability, personal charm, and professional performance. High-
credibility influencers can reduce audiences' sense of resistance.
The high personal credibility accumulated by MrBeast through long-term public benefit
orientation is transferred from the influencer himself to his self-owned DTC brand Feastables. It lays
a trust foundation for consumers to accept the brand's products and make it successful.
2.3.2. Parasocial interaction theory
Put forward by Horton and Wohl, Parasocial Interaction Theory describes the one‑sided emotional
bond formed between media audiences and media figures. Although there is no real‑time social
communication, audiences will generate intimacy, a sense of familiarity with media personalities
after long‑term watching of their content, just like interpersonal relationships in real life [1].
In influencer‑marketing practice, continuous content output, comment replies, and interactive
activities build parasocial interactions between influencers and their fans. Such emotional
connection can expand onto products and brands recommended, enhancing emotional awareness
toward brands.
In the case of Feastables, MrBeast establishes close parasocial relationships with massive
YouTube followers via entertaining, challenge‑style videos. Therefore, it promoted the promotion of
Feastables.

3. Research methodology
3.1. Case study method
This study employed the single case study method to deeply explore "how the Influencer economy
affects the promotional effect of the brands owned by influencers". Compared to quantitative
research, which focuses on the relationships between variables and outcomes, the case study method
is better suited to understand the mechanisms and logic behind a single phenomenon, and to better
understand the process of the transfer of the individual IP of influencers to physical brands.
MrBeast is the top YouTube creator globally, and Feastables is the DTC consumer goods brand
created by him. It is a typical example of internet celebrities moving from "third-party endorsement"
to "owned brands", with strong representativeness.
3.2. Secondary data collection and analysis
This study relies entirely on publicly‑available secondary data without questionnaires, interviews, or
other primary‑data collection. The data sources consist of the official Feastables website,
open‑access YouTube videos and social‑media public metrics, industry analysis reports, and
mainstream news‑media coverage.
To improve the reliability of qualitative case analysis. Drawing on Source Credibility Theory and
Parasocial Interaction Theory as the analytical lens. Collected materials around four core aspects to
analyze: trust‑transfer effect, entertainment value, IP transfer, and native promotion. Since internal
confidential sales data is inaccessible, this research focuses on public data rather than precise
internal operational figures.
4. Case analysis and results （ MrBeast's Feastables chocolate ）
4.1. Overview of the case
4.1.1. Information about top youtube influencer mrbeast
Jimmy Donaldson (known online as MrBeast) is a leading global YouTube creator. In June 2026, he
became the first individual creator to reach over 50 million subscribers all around the world. His
YouTube channel has over 510 million subscribers, and his total followers exceed 900 million across
all platforms. There are a large number of repeat users on different platforms; the actual independent
audience is lower than this figure. His core content includes high-cost challenge videos, large-scale
charity, and real experiments. Through these videos, a "generous, genuine, and highly invested"
image was created, accumulating strong credibility and shaping popularity and personal charm
worldwide.
Corresponding to the research conclusion of KO JUNSIK (2024): Credibility and entertainment
value are the two strongest characteristics influencing consumers' purchase intentions. MrBeast
happens to possess both of these traits [7].
By launching its own brand "Feastables" through Beast Industries, the company successfully
transferred its IP and became a typical representative of the DTC model in the influencer economy.

4.1.2. The background of the Feastables chocolate brand
In 2022, the DTC chocolate brand "Feastables" was launched by Beast Industries. MrBeast has been
deeply involved in the product formula, packaging design, and brand operation, rather than just
being an endorsement.
It hardly invests in traditional offline advertisements. The core initial traffic comes totally from
MrBeast's own social media, and it is a typical influencer DTC brand.
4.2. The effect of influencer on brand promotion
4.2.1. Trust transfer effect
Based on the Source Credibility Theory, MrBeast's personal trust accumulated through years of
charity and real challenge videos was directly transferred to the Feastables brand. Compared to
third-party endorsements, fans would directly assume, "This is his own brand, and he himself must
approve of the quality", reducing consumers' doubts about Feastables.
This phenomenon also verifies and confirms the KAPINGA (2024) path of "credibility → trust
→ purchase intention" [3].
4.2.2. Entertainment value
Based on the Parasocial Interaction Theory. Fans and influencers form quasi-social emotional
connections through long-term content consumption; this reduces consumers' psychological
resistance to advertisements and enables brand information to be naturally accepted. Feastables
rarely conducts independent traditional advertisements; instead, they are all integrated into
MrBeast's regular video challenges and giveaways, and naturally appear as plot props.
Highly entertaining videos are watched in their entirety, with users engaging in interactive
comments or sharing the videos with friends, which completes the natural secondary dissemination.
This precisely corresponds to the conclusion of KO JUNSIK (2024) - entertainment value is the
second-largest factor influencing purchase intentions, second only to credibility [7].
4.3. Performance of Feastables' brand promotion effect
4.3.1. Brand awareness (exact data ）
Due to the lack of precise data for non-listed enterprises, this section conducts an objective
assessment of Feastables based on publicly available data from the platform and third-party industry
reports. As of the public observation point, the cumulative views of content related to Feastables on
the YouTube platform have exceeded 4.3 billion, and the views of Feastables-themed videos have
reached several billion. In terms of social media accounts, the official TikTok account of Feastables
has attracted over 4.5 million followers. According to publicly reported industry media, the sales of
Feastables' chocolate products in the first week of its launch exceeded 10 million US dollars; this
data is from publicly available industry reports. In summary, the publicly observable data shows that
Feastables, relying on MrBeast, completed the transformation from 0 to high popularity within a
short period of time. However, it should be noted that the high popularity of Feastables is highly tied
to the individual IP, and its long-term existence is uncertain.

4.3.2. Comparison with different brands
Compared with traditional chocolate candy brands, Feastables achieved faster customer acquisition
by relying on the influencer's own traffic. While traditional brands build consumers' brand
awareness through TV advertisements, Feastables achieved a 90% brand recognition rate among
American Gen Z in a short period of time and reached $10 million in sales in the first week. This
indicates that social media traffic can significantly shorten the time for consumers to establish brand
awareness. However, traditional brands have a mature supply chain and nationwide offline retail
coverage, which are exactly what Feastables currently lacks. Moreover, the early brand value of
Feastables was highly dependent on MrBeast's personal IP rather than the brand itself, which is
consistent with the research findings: the compatibility between high-impact figures and the brand
can accelerate early recognition, but it may also bring risks of brand personality binding [8].
However, compared with traditional DTC food brands, Feastables had significantly lower
customer acquisition costs and higher user engagement.
4.3.3. Stable formation of brand loyalty
Initially, the users who came from MrBeast's fans were attracted by MrBeast's parasocial emotional
ties and were willing to try and support. However, brand loyalty still relied on the product itself.
Fans could only support the first purchase, while brand loyalty always depended on the product's
taste, cost-effectiveness, etc. If the product quality wasn't good enough, it could not support user to
repeat purchases.
This corresponds to the viewpoint that "brand loyalty is jointly determined by brand reputation
and product quality" [9].
4.4. Potential risks
The brand is deeply linked to the founder. Negative events of MrBeast's will directly affect the
Feastables brand itself, causing a negative impact on its brand's reputation. This corresponds to the
Source Credibility Theory - when the source credibility collapses, trust in the brand will also
collapse at the same time.
Furthermore, the dissemination of the brand relies on MrBeast's personal content output; if the
creator reduces updates or exits, the brand's traffic will directly decrease.
The promotion of Feastables relies on social media platforms such as YouTube; without stable
channels independent of third-party platforms like offline retail, traffic control is not in the hands of
the brand but in the platform algorithms.
5. Discussion
5.1. Existing research conclusions
In this case, the source credibility theory and the Parasocial Interaction Theory are reflected. Fans
establish trust and Parasocial emotional relationships with Mr Beast based on their long-term
viewing of his videos, and then transfer this trust to the brand. However, most of these theories focus
on online celebrities endorsing third-party brands, while in the self-owned DTC model, the path for
trust transfer is shorter.

5.2. Extended findings
In the conventional model of influencer endorsement, fans can distinguish between the "influencer
themselves" and the "third-party company being endorsed", and there is a certain distance in terms
of trust; but under the DTC model of influencers, the influencer becomes the founder, and personal
trust and traffic can be more directly transferred to the brand. However, at the same time, risks are
also magnified. The DTC brand model causes the brand to be highly bound to the creator, making it
difficult for the brand to extend beyond the influencer image. Previous literature has focused more
on the benefits brought by influencers to the brand and has discussed less about the "long-term risks
of the binding" [10].
5.3. Practical effect for brand marketing
For online content creators who plan to establishthemselves in DTC and traditional companies that
want to collaborate with online influencers, the cold start stage can fully use source credibility and
the parasocial relationship with fans to reduce costs and accumulate customers. However, the short-
term traffic advantages do not equal long-term accumulation. Product quality is always the
foundation.
6. Conclusion
6.1. Main research conclusions
This study takes MrBeast's Feastables chocolate as an example, based on the source credibility
theory and the quasi-social interaction theory, to explore the brand promotion mechanism, effect,
and risks of the DTC brand of online celebrities. First, the credibility of the online celebrity and the
quasi-social emotional relationship established with the fans enable the self-owned brand of the
online celebrity to develop rapidly in the cold start stage. Second, compared with the celebrity
endorsing a third-party brand, the DTC model of the celebrity enables the transfer of fan trust to
occur faster and more credibly. At the same time, the high binding of the IP also brings potential
risks. Third, short-term traffic and high popularity cannot achieve long-term brand loyalty; product
quality is the foundation of a long-term brand. Fourth, the DTC model of online celebrities faces
risks such as celebrity reputation risks and excessive reliance on personal IP, becoming risks for
brand development.
6.2. Research limitations
This article only selects the DTC brand of MrBeast, a globally renowned YouTube influencer, as the
case study. The sample of this case is limited, and the traffic and fan base of the top influencers are
something that most creators do not possess. Moreover, all the research is based on public secondary
data and platform public information. A non-listed company has no way to obtain internal
information. Also, Feastables has a relatively short establishment time and lacks long-term tracking.
6.3. Future research directions
Based on the limitations of this study, there are some future research directions. First, more case
studies can help better understand the different of promotion mechanisms of the DTC model of
online influencers at different fan levels, different types, and different regions. Second, research can
