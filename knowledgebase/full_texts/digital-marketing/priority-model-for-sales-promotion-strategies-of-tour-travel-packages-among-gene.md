Abstract
Advances in digital technology have transformed consumer behavior in obtaining information and selecting travel
products, so tour and travel companies need promotional strategies that align with consumer characteristics. This
study aims to analyze the factors that Generation Y considers when purchasing travel packages, determine priorities
for promotional media, and  identify the most suitable promotional strategies for PT Cheria Trip Bahagia. The
research employs a descriptive qualitative approach with a case study design. Data were collected through in-depth
interviews, observation, and documentation, and strategies were prioritized using the Analytical Hierarchy Process
(AHP) method. Eight experts were selected through purposive sampling. The AHP hierarchy comprised the
objective of selling travel packages, four criteria (destinations, facilities, costs, and insurance), and six alternatives
(influencers, digital marketing, social media, direct referrals, cashback, and conventional marketing). Cost had the
highest priority (45.62%), followed by facilities (23.23%), insurance (22.27%), and destinations (8.89%). Social
media ranked first under every criterion: 42.47% for destinations, 39.83% for facilities, 40.14% for cost, and 38.46%
for insurance. Social media (particularly Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, and YouTube) plays a crucial role in
reaching Generation Y through visual information, demographic and interest -based targeting, and customer
engagement. Combining social media and digital marketing with conventional marketing can help PT Cheria Trip
Bahagia expand its market reach and boost travel package sales.
Keywords: Analytical Hierarchy Process (AHP), Generation Y, Promotional Strategies, Social Media, Travel Packages.

1. Introduction
Tourism is a vital sector that not only meets the need for recreation and travel
experiences, but also contributes to the economy through job creation, increased community
income, and foreign exchange earnings (Ashoer et al., 2025; Muharto et al., 2022) .
Developments in digital technology have subse quently transformed consumer behavior in
terms of searching for information, making choices, and purchasing tourism products. Social
media has become a promising marketing channel due to its large user base and the ability it
affords companies to reach con sumers without geographical boundaries  (Charviandi et al.,
2023).
Changes in digital consumer behavior have made social media increasingly important
for the tour and travel industry. Consumers use social media to obtain information about
destinations, view reviews and testimonials, and gain travel inspiration (Darmawansyah &
Sismiati, 2021). For companies, social media can be used to build brand awareness, showcase
tourism products, provide customer service, and communicate promotions quickly. The use

of digital marketing also enables companies to reach consumer segm ents in a more targeted
and interactive manner (Wali et al., 2026).
This situation is particularly relevant for PT Cheria Trip Bahagia, which needs to
determine the most effective promotional medium for marketing its travel packages,
particularly to Generation Y. The multitude of social media platforms presents a challenge, as
each platform has different user characteristics, content formats and levels of interaction.
Therefore, the selection of promotional media must be based on criteria that align with
consumer characteristics, rather than solely on the popularity of the platform.
This issue highlights the importance of a more targeted promotional strategy in
supporting the competitiveness of the tourism sector. In this context, the Analytical Hierarchy
Process (AHP) can be used to systematically prioritize media and promotional strategies based
on various criteria. Previous research has applied AHP to promotional strategies in the
property and ecotourism sectors (Bianco & Marcianò, 2019; Hendro et al., 2020) . However,
research specifically applying AHP to prioritize promotional media for travel packages
targeting Generation Y at tour and travel companies remains limited. This indicates a
contextual gap, which forms the basis of this study at PT Cheria Trip Bahagia.
Previous research indicates that the AHP method has been widely used to prioritize
strategies in tourism destination development, with criteria including facilities, attractions,
accessibility, promotion, and community empowerment (Fazrin & Gunarto, 2025; Perangin-
Angin & Hardianto, 2023). Studies on digital marketing have also been conducted, but these
tend to focus on the use of social media and c ontent development as means of expanding
promotional reach (Ariyanto et al., 2024; Pratama et al., 2026) . However, studies that
integrate various aspects of digital marketing and determine strategic priorities based on the
specific conditions of a destination remain limited. Consequently, there is a research gap
regarding the more systematic integration and prioritization of digital marketing strategies,
which can be addressed through the AHP approach.
This study aims to analyze the factors considered by Generation Y in the promotion of
holiday packages, determine the priorities for promotional media using the AHP method, and
identify the promotional strategies that should be prioritized by PT Cheria Trip Bahagia. The
research findings are expected to provide a systematic basis for decision-making, enabling the
company to determine effective digital promotional strategies that are aligned with the
characteristics of Generation Y.

2. Literature Review
2.1. Promotional Strategy
2.1.1. The Concept of Promotional Strategy
A strategy is a long -term plan devised to achieve organizational objectives through the
process of identifying problems, determining alternative solutions, and systematically
implementing decisions (Steiner, 2010) . In the context of marketing, a strategy is used to
determine how a company creates and delivers value to its target market. Marketing strategy
encompasses the identification of target markets, positioning, and an integrated marketing
mix (Kotler & Keller, 2016).
Promotion is a component of marketing strategy that serves to convey information,
persuade, and remind consumers about a product or bran d in order to drive purchasing
decisions and loyalty (Tjiptono & Diana, 2022) . Thus, a promotional  strategy can be
understood as a series of planning and commu nication actions used by a company to
effectively convey the value of a product to the target market.

2.1.2. Analysis of Promotional Media
Promotional media analysis is the process of selecting and evaluating communication
media based on their suitability for the  target market and marketing objectives. The
effectiveness of a medium can be assessed by its ability to generate brand awareness, consumer
engagement, and increased sales (Celestin et al., 2024) . Promotional media may take the form
of digital media, traditional media, direct marketing, as well as events and sponsorship (Juska,
2017).
2.1.3. Digital Marketing
Digital marketing refers to marketing activities that utilize technology, media and digital
platforms to reach, interact with and build relationships with consumers in a measurable way
(Chaffey & Ellis -Chadwick, 2019) . Digital marketing enables companies to reach a wider
audience, personalize communications, measure campaign performance in real time, and
adjust strategies based on market response (Kotler & Keller, 2016).
Digital marketing can be carried out through organic channels, such as Search Engine
Optimization (SEO), content marketing, organic social media marketing, and digital public
relations, as well as paid channels such as Search Engine Marketing (SEM), Pay -Per-Click
(PPC), and paid social media marketing (Bala & Verma, 2018).
2.1.4. Social Media
Social media is an internet -based platform that enables users to communicate, share
content, build networks, and interact in a two-way manner. These characteristics make social
media one of the key channels in digital marketing, as it enables companies to establish direct
communication with consumers and promote products through both organic content and paid
advertising. Platforms such as Instagram, TikTok, Facebook and LinkedIn can be utilised to
increase brand awareness, engagement and market reach.
2.2. Tourism
2.2.1. The Concept of Tourism
Tourism is the temporary travel of a person away from their place of residence to gain
experiences, for recreation, or for other purposes, without the intention of earning an income
at the destination (Muharto  et al., 2022). Tourism activities involve various parties, namely
tourists, the tourism industry, the government, local communities, support service providers,
and relevant institutions.
2.2.2. Destinations and Components of Tourism
A tourist destination is an area that possesses attractions and is supported by
accessibility, amenities and institutional frameworks to meet the needs of tourists (Ilhamalimy
et al., 2025) . The components of tourism include attractions, accommodation , facilities and
services, transport, infrastructure and institutional frameworks. The presence of these
components forms the basis for determining a destination’s appeal and competitiveness.
2.2.3. Tourism Facilities and Travel Costs
Tourism facilities are the infrastructure and amenities that support tourists’ needs whilst
at a destination, whether in the form of primary, supporting, or ancillary facilities (Lee, 2016).
In addition to facilities, travel costs are als o a consideration for tourists, covering  transport,
accommodation, meals, entrance fees, parking, and the cost of tourist activities.

2.2.4. Travel Insurance
Insurance is a form of protection against financial risks arising from unforeseen events

2.3. Generation Y
Generation Y, or the millennial generation, comprises those born between
approximately 1981 and 1994. This generation grew up alongside the development of
information technology and the internet and is therefore relatively accustomed to using digital
technology to obtain information and communicate (Ware, 2013). These characteristics make
Generation Y a relevant consumer group for the implementation of digital-based promotional
strategies.
2.4. Analytical Hierarchy Process (AHP)
2.4.1. The Concept of AHP
The Analytical Hierarchy Process (AHP) is a decision -making method developed by
Thomas L. Saaty to resolve problems involving var ious criteria and alternatives. AHP
simplifies complex problems through a hierarchical structure comprising objectives, criteria,
sub-criteria and alternatives (Saaty, 2008) . The decision -making process is carried out
through pairwise comparisons to determine the priority weights of each element.
2.4.2. Principles and Hierarchical Structure of AHP
AHP is based on three main principles: hierarchy con struction, priority setting, and
logical consistency (Saaty, 2008). The hierarchical structure comprises the objective at the top
level, criteria or sub-criteria at the next level, and alternatives at the lowest level. This structure
enables complex problems to be analyzed systematically.
2.4.3. Stages of AHP Analysis
The stages of AHP include: (1) defining the decision objective, (2) constructing the
hierarchy, (3) conducting pairwise comparisons using a 1-9 scale, (4) normalizing the matrix,
(5) calculating priority weights, (6) testing consistency using the Consistency Index (CI) and
Consistency Ratio (CR), and (7) determining the final ranking of alternatives. The matrix is
considered consistent if the CR value is < 0.10 (Saaty, 1993).
2.4.4. Application of AHP in the Selection of Promotional Strategies
In this study, AHP was used to prioritize promotional strategies based on a number of
criteria and promotional media alternatives. The use of AHP allows for the systematic
comparison of various quantitative and qualitat ive considerations, thereby identifying the
strategy alternatives with the highest priority. Consequently, AHP is a relevant decision -
making method for determining the promotional media or strategies best suited to the
characteristics of the target tourists.

3. Methods
3.1.1. Research Paradigm
This study employs an interpretative paradigm with a qualitative approach to
understand the informants ’ experiences, perceptions and interpretations of promotional
strategies for tourist travel packages. The researcher acts as the primary instrument,
interacting directly with the informants through interviews, observation and documentation.
This approach is used to understand the process of designing promotional strategies, selecting
promotional media, adapting strategies to consumer characteristics, as well as the constraints
and successes of the promotions carried out by the company.
3.1.2. Research Design
This study employs a descriptive qualitative approach using a case study design at PT
Cheria Trip Bahagia. The qualitative appr oach is used to understand phenomena in their
natural context based on the informants ’ perspectives without manipulating the research
subjects. Research data consists of interview transcripts, observations, field notes, documents,
photographs, recordings, and other relevant information (LeCompte & Schensul, 1999) .
Informants were selected purposively based on their knowledge and experience relevant to the
promotion and marketing strategies for travel packages. The primary data sources  in
qualitative research consist of the informants’ words and actions, whilst documents were used
as supporting data (Moleong, 2017).
3.1.3. Research Site
The research was conducted at PT Cheria Trip Bahagia between May and July 2026.
Research informants included the CEO and marketing team of PT Cheria Trip Bahagia,
representatives of the tourism business association, stakeholders involved in tourism
promotion strategies, and consumers with experience in selecting and purchasing tourist
travel packages.
3.1.4. Data Sources
The research data sources comprised primary  and secondary data. Primary data was
obtained through interviews with informants possessing knowledge and experience regarding
PT Cheria Trip Bahagia’s promotional strategies. Secondary data was obtained from company
documents, promotional materials, social media, archives, photographs, recordings, and other
relevant documents. The research subjects included the CEO and marketing team of PT Cheria
Trip Bahagia, representatives of the tourism business association, parties involved in tourism
promotion strategies, and consumers. The research focus is on promotional strategies utilising
digital marketing, social media and conventional media, as well as their role in the process of
selecting and purchasing travel packages at PT Cheria Trip Bahagia.
3.1.5. Data Collection Techniques
Data collection was carried out through in -depth interviews, observation and
documentation. Semi-structured interviews were conducted with informants selected through
purposive sampling to obtain information regarding promotional strategies, the utilisation of
digital marketing and social media, the selection of promotional media, consumer responses,
as well as the challenges and effectiveness of promotional activities (Sugiyono, 2017) .
Observations were made of the promotional and marketing activities of PT Cheria Trip
Bahagia, particularly the use of digital media, social media, and other promotional media.
Documentation was used to supplement the data, comprising promotional materials, social
media posts, photographs of activities, company documents, marketing archives, and other
documents relevant to the promotional strategy. A combination of these three techniques was

3.1.6. Data Analysis Methods
This study used the AHP model to prioritize alternative promotional strategies for selling
travel packages (Miftahun & Yuliarty, 2017). The hierarchy implemented to collect and process
the expert judgments has three levels, as follows:
A. Level I: The objective of the decision is placed at the top of the hierarchy. In this case,
the objective is “Sales of Travel Packages.”
B. Level II: Evaluation criteria are proposed that influence consumers ’ decisions to
purchase travel packages. These criteria consist of tourist destinations, facilities, costs,
and insurance.
C. Level III: Alternatives are proposed in the form of promotional strategies. These
alternatives consist of influencers, digital marketing, social media, direct referrals,
cashback, and conventional marketing.

Generation Y is not modeled as a separate level in the hierarchy. It serves as the
contextual focus of the study, and the priority weights are calculated for the overall objective
of selling travel packages rather than segmented by generation.

4.2.1. Implementation of the Analytical Hierarchy Process (AHP)

research objectives, which comprises four main elements: objectives/targets, criteria, factors
and alternatives. This research aims to determine promotional strategies for marketing
holiday packages at PT Ch eria Trip Bahagia. In the decision support system designed, the
criteria driving tourism that were analyzed are tourist destinations, facilities, costs and
insurance, as these are aspects that influence consumers ’ decisions when purchasing holiday
packages and in implementing promotional strategies at PT Cheria Trip Bahagia.  The AHP

B. Pairwise Comparison
The stage following the identification of alternatives, factors and criteria that form a key
part of achieving the company’s decision-making objectives in selecting a promotional strategy
for the sale of travel packages is to carry out pairwise comparisons. This process compares
each criterion, factor and alternative with the others using a scale or elements determined in
accordance with the Analytical Hierarchy Process (AHP) method.
This pairwise comparison stage will be st ructured in the form of a questionnaire
following the AHP method, which covers the alternatives and related criteria. The
questionnaire is then completed by experts, who select the alternative or criterion deemed
superior in each pairwise comparison, assigning a rating on a scale or using elements ranging
from 1 to 9. Each scale value or element has the following weightings:
1) Element 1: both criteria (on the left and right) have the same level of importance
2) Element 3: one of the criteria (on the left or righ t) is slightly more important
3) Element 5: one of the criteria (on the left or right) has a higher level of importance
4) Element 7: one of the criteria (on the left or right) is significantly more important
5) Element 9: one of the criteria (on the left or right)  is entirely more important.

Elements 2, 4, 6 and 8 may be selected as intermediate values. This means that this scale
is used when there is a compromise between two options. The weighting of each scale or item
assigned by the expert when completing the questionnaire will determine whether the
responses provided are valid or not. If the results of the pairwise comparison with the scales
or items processed in Microsoft Excel show a value greater than 10 per cent or 0.1, the
questionnaire is deemed invalid. In this situation, the researcher has two options: to ask the
expert to complete the questionnaire again or to find a new expert.

recommend exhibitions and active social media marketing together, because relying on walk-

4.2.2. Novelty
This study contributes by applying AHP to prioritize promotional alternatives for travel
packages at a tour and travel company, using purchase criteria (tourist destinations, facilities,
costs, and insurance) judged by eight experts. As noted in the Introduction, research applying
AHP to prioritize promotional media for travel packages remains limited. The study therefore
offers an empirical weighting of six alternatives (influencers, digital marketing, social media,
direct referrals, cashback, and conventional marketing) for PT Cheria Trip Bahagia, with
Generation Y as the contextual focus. The weights are not segmented by generation, and the
model does not compare individual social media platforms. Future studies could add platform-

5. Conclusion
The use of social media platforms such as Instagram, Facebook, TikTok and YouTube,
as well as digital marketing, has proven effective in promoting travel packages as it can expand
market reach, inc rease brand awareness, engagement and leads at a relatively low cost.
However, conventional strategies remain necessary to build consumer trust. The results of the
analysis using the AHP method indicate that a combined strategy of digital and conventional
approaches is the most ideal option, as it can reach a wider audience through social media,
influencers, tourism exhibitions and brochures.
Implementing this strategy requires coordination, budget management, analysis of
consumer behavior, and regular evaluation of campaigns to ensure they remain aligned with
market dynamics. This research contributes to the fields of marketing and tourism by
demonstrating that AHP can assist companies in determining marketing strategies based on
relevant criteria, such as destination, facilities, costs and insurance.
For tour and travel companies, a combination of digital and conventional marketing can
serve as a strategy to increase market reach and sales. However, this study has limitations as
it employs a qualitative app roach and focuses primarily on social media. Further research is
recommended to explore additional marketing variables and methods, as well as to adopt a
more diverse range of approaches in order to obtain more comprehensive results.
