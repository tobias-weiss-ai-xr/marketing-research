Abstract: This study examines the relationships among Digital Marketing Strategies, Brand
Equity, and Customer Loyalty among online consumers in DKI Jakarta and West Java, Indonesia.
A quantitative cross -sectional survey was conducted with 400 respondents selected t hrough
purposive and quota sampling, with equal representation from the two regions. Respondents
evaluated one brand they had purchased online, and the constructs were measured using a five-
point Likert scale. Data were screened in Microsoft Excel and SPSS  and analyzed using Partial
Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling in SmartPLS 4. The reported results indicate that
Digital Marketing Strategies were not statistically associated with Customer Loyalty at the 5%
significance level ( β = 0.218, p = 0.063), whereas Digital Marketing Strategies were positively
associated with Brand Equity (β = 0.701, p < 0.001), and Brand Equity was positively associated
with Customer Loyalty (β = 0.400, p < 0.001). The reported indirect effect through Brand Equity
also did not meet the 5% significance criterion (β = 0.152, p = 0.067). These findings suggest that,
within this sample, digital marketing perceptions were more closely related to consumer -based
brand value than to loyalty directly. The study contributes by examining a multi-touchpoint view
of Digital Marketing Strategies and by distinguishing brand -building outcomes from loyalty
formation. The cross -sectional design, self -reported data, and geographically bounded sample
limit causal and broader population inferences.

INTRODUCTION
Digital consumer behavior is increasingly organized around connected encounters rather
than a single advertising exposure or purchase channel. Consumers may discover a brand through
social media, compare it in a marketplace, evaluate reviews, interact with  the brand through
messaging features, and complete or reconsider a purchase through a mobile application. This
shift makes digital marketing a strategic configuration of content, interaction, data use, and
platform experience rather than a narrow promotio nal activity (Li et al., 2021). Research on
digital and social media marketing similarly emphasizes that contemporary consumer journeys
combine firm -generated communication, user -generated content, and technology -mediated

The multi -touchpoint character of online consumption makes this issue especially
relevant to brand evaluation. Omnichannel research shows that consumers assess experiences
across several dimensions, including communication, personalization, service consist ency,
information safety, delivery, and loyalty-related benefits (Rahman et al., 2022). Service integration
across channels can shape customer experience and loyalty because the consumer evaluates
whether information and processes remain coherent from one digital contact point to another
(Quach et al., 2022). Related evidence indicates that integration, individualization, and interaction
can contribute differently to brand experience and customer retention, depending on how
consumers purchase across channel s (Yin et al., 2022). Personalization also does not operate
uniformly: personalized price promotions can reduce perceived effort and support loyalty in some
online settings, whereas recommendation systems do not always generate the same relational
response (Hallikainen et al., 2022). These findings imply that the quality of a digital journey may
matter more than the sheer intensity of digital promotion.
Electronic word -of-mouth further complicates the relationship between marketing
exposure and loyalty because consumers evaluate brands through information produced by other
consumers. Online reviews influence purchase decisions through dimensions such as s ource
credibility, volume, comprehensibility, and relevance (Fernandes et al., 2022). Textual review
content can also reveal brand image and positioning because consumers express functional and
emotional associations in their own language (Alzate et al., 2022). Recent work shows that e-WOM
can contribute to brand equity when consumers regard reviews as trustworthy and when review
content supports brand awareness and image formation (Le et al., 2024). In social -commerce
settings, reviews and brand equity can  jointly shape consumer identification and behavioral
intention, although their roles may differ by context and level of analysis (Huang et al., 2022).
Consequently, online consumers may respond to a brand's digital strategy through both firm -
controlled signals and socially generated evidence.
Customer loyalty is more demanding than a favorable reaction to an advertisement,
promotion, or platform feature. Online customer experience can be associated with loyalty
through engagement, indicating that relational mechanisms may be necessary between a n
experience and a durable behavioral intention (Ahmad et al., 2022). In live-streaming commerce,
trust in technological and social elements is related to continuance intention, which illustrates
why interactivity alone may not be sufficient unless consume rs develop confidence in the
transaction environment (Zhang et al., 2022). Platform stickiness research likewise links real -
time interaction, perceived value, satisfaction, and flow to continued use rather than treating
exposure as equivalent to retention (Bao & Zhu, 2023). Brand relationships can also become more
stable when trust and relationship quality convert positive consumer advocacy into loyalty
(Quaye et al., 2022). This distinction is important for the present study because Customer Loyalty
includes repurchase intention, recommendation intention, brand preference, and resistance to
switching, not merely repeated purchase.

brand-related outcomes, but the dominant evidence does not support one simple pathway. In

Among portable technology consumers, brand a wareness and brand image help connect social

Evidence from handicraft marketing similarly shows that social media marketing activities can
strengthen brand awareness, brand image, and brand equity (Guha et al., 2021). Taken together ,
these studies indicate that brand value is a plausible intermediate outcome of digital marketing,
even when the final loyalty response varies across settings.
The inconsistency of loyalty outcomes is itself theoretically informative. A meta -analysis
found a positive overall relationship between social media marketing activities and brand loyalty,
while also showing that the size of that relationship varies across contextual and methodological
conditions (Ibrahim, 2022). Studies of luxury and e -commerce settings further identify brand
experience, satisfaction, relationship quality, engagement, brand love, and trust as mechanisms
that can explain why similar marketing activities produce different loyalty outcomes (Fetais et al.,
2023; Song & Kim, 2022; Banerji & Singh, 2024; Kumar & Hsieh, 2024). In online travel and digital-
platform contexts, brand trust and brand loyalty have also been modeled as intervening
mechanisms between social media marketing and later consumer responses (Azhar et al., 2023),
while evidence from B2B platforms suggests that digital adoption may even weaken loyalty when
it reduces the value of e-WOM (Belhadi et al., 2023). More recent studies of influencer marketing
and consumer experience likewise show that e -WOM and e-brand experience can be associated
with e-brand loyalty, with emotional attachment modifying some relationships (Valmohammadi
et al., 2025), and restaurant research identifies s atisfaction and brand love as relevant
antecedents of loyalty and e-WOM (Aragone s-Jerico   et al., 2024). Despite this growing evidence,
prior studies have predominantly examined specific forms of social media marketing or isolated
relational mechanisms such as trust, engagement, satisfaction, and brand experience. Less
attention has been given to an integrate d multi-touchpoint view of Digital Marketing Strategies
that simultaneously distinguishes Brand Equity from Customer Loyalty and tests Brand Equity as
a mediating mechanism. This limitation provides the specific research gap addressed in the
present study.
Against this background, the present study examines Digital Marketing Strategies, Brand
Equity, and Customer Loyalty among online consumers in DKI Jakarta and West Java. Digital
Marketing Strategies are treated as a multi-touchpoint construct covering content quality, social
media interactivity, personalization, digital promotion, electronic word -of-mouth, and platform
experience, while Brand Equity captures awareness, image, perceived quality, and brand
association. The study tests whether Digital Marketing Strategies are related to Customer Loyalty
directly, whether they are related to Brand Equity, whether Brand Equity is related to Customer
Loyalty, and whether Brand Equity mediates the relationship between Digital Marketing
Strategies and Customer Loyal ty. The theoretical contribution lies in distinguishing digital
marketing as a brand-building process from loyalty as a stronger relational outcome rather than
assuming that campaign exposure automatically produces retention. The practical contribution is
to help firms interpret digital marketing performance beyond reach, clicks, promotions, and other
short-term indicators by examining whether consumers develop favorable and differentiated

Digital Marketing Strategies as a Multi-Touchpoint Consumer Interface
Digital Marketing Strategies refer in this study to coordinated consumer-facing activities
that operate across digital content, social interaction, personalized offers, promotions, e -WOM,
and platform experience. This conceptualization follows the broader view that social media
marketing strategy involves deliberate patterns of digital engagement rather than isolated
communication tactics (Li et al., 2021). It also reflects the reality that consumer journeys combine
owned, paid, and socially generated touch points whose effects depend on how consumers
interpret and connect them (Dwivedi et al., 2021). The six dimensions used in the instrument
represent different forms of evidence available to consumers: content informs and attracts
attention, interactivity si gnals responsiveness, personalization increases relevance, promotion
supplies economic incentives, e-WOM supplies peer evaluation, and platform experience shapes
transaction convenience. Research on omnichannel customer experience supports treating such
encounters as interconnected evaluations rather than independent channel effects (Rahman et
al., 2022).
The dimensions should nevertheless be interpreted as consumer perceptions of visible
digital activity, not as direct measures of a firm's internal marketing capability. Personalized
recommendations and promotions, for example, can reduce cognitive effort b ut can generate
different loyalty responses depending on how consumers evaluate their relevance (Hallikainen et
al., 2022). Online reviews also influence decisions through credibility, relevance, and
comprehensibility rather than through their presence alo ne (Fernandes et al., 2022). These
findings support a perception-based measurement approach in which respondents evaluate how
one focal brand is experienced across digital contact points. In the present model, Digital
Marketing Strategies therefore represe nt the consumer's integrated assessment of those
touchpoints.
Digital Marketing Strategies and Customer Loyalty
Digital marketing may be associated with Customer Loyalty when repeated digital
encounters reduce effort, improve relevance, support interaction, and create a favorable
experience. Meta-analytic evidence indicates that social media marketing activities are generally
related to brand loyalty, but the relationship varies across contexts and study designs (Ibrahim,
2022). In e -commerce, perceived social media marketing activities can contribute to loyalty
through relationship quality, showing that trust, commitment, and satisfaction may be necessary
between communication and retention (Banerji & Singh, 2024). Brand experience has also been
identified as a mediator between social media marketing activities and brand loyalty (Kumar &
Hsieh, 2024). These studies imply that a direct relationship is plausible but should not be assumed
to be uniform across online consumer settings.
Customer Loyalty in this study combines repurchase intention, recommendation
intention, brand preference, and resistance to switching. This definition sets a higher threshold
than simple repeat purchase because it requires both future behavioral intention and a
comparatively stable preference. Research on online customer experience indicates that
engagement can connect experience with loyalty (Ahmad et al., 2022), while live -commerce
studies show that platform value, satisfaction, trust, and flow are relevant to continued use (Zhang
et al., 2022; Bao & Zhu, 2023). Digital activities can therefore support loyalty, but consumers may
still switch when competing offers are more attractive or when relational mechanisms remain

H1: Digital Marketing Strategies positively influence Customer Loyalty.
Digital Marketing Strategies and Brand Equity
Brand Equity represents the value consumers attach to a brand through recognition,
image, perceived quality, and distinctive associations. Digital touchpoints can contribute to that
value because they repeatedly expose consumers to information and social e vidence that shape
what the brand means and how credible it appears. Banking studies show that social media
marketing activities can be related to brand equity through brand trust, brand love, brand
experience, and perceived uniqueness (Hafez, 2021, 2022).  Studies in technology markets
similarly associate social media marketing dimensions with brand awareness and brand image,
both of which can support later repurchase responses (Yang et al., 2022; Malarvizhi et al., 2022).
The brand-building role of digital marketing is therefore expected to be stronger when different
touchpoints present coherent and credible signals.
This relationship is particularly relevant when consumers cannot inspect a product or
service physically before purchase. Content quality can clarify brand meaning; interaction can
signal responsiveness; personalization can increase perceived relevance; e -WOM can provide
social confirmation; and a reliable platform experience can support judgments of quality.
Research on handicraft promotion found that social media marketing activities were related to
brand awareness, brand image, and brand equity (Guha et al., 2021), while social networking
activity has also been linked with customer -equity outcomes that include brand -related value
(Wang et al., 2021). These studies support the expectation that consumers who evaluate a brand's
digital marketing more favorably will also report stronger Brand Equity.
H2: Digital Marketing Strategies positively influence Brand Equity.
Brand Equity and Customer Loyalty
Brand Equity may support Customer Loyalty because recognized, positively evaluated,
and differentiated brands reduce uncertainty and provide reasons to remain with the same
choice. When a consumer can recall a brand easily, regards its image positively, pe rceives its
quality as favorable, and associates it with a distinctive character, competing offers must
overcome more than price or convenience. Research on brand relationships shows that trust and
relationship quality can connect positive consumer advocac y with loyalty (Quaye et al., 2022).
Evidence from digital retail similarly links e -brand experience and e -WOM with e-brand loyalty
(Valmohammadi et al., 2025). Restaurant research also demonstrates that satisfaction and brand
love are relevant antecedents  of loyalty and e -WOM, suggesting that accumulated brand
evaluation can stabilize future preference (Aragone s-Jerico   et al., 2024).
In the present study, Brand Equity does not include brand loyalty as one of its dimensions
because Customer Loyalty is modeled as a separate endogenous construct. This separation
reduces conceptual overlap and allows the analysis to test whether awareness, image, perceived
quality, and association are related to repurchase intention, recommendation, preference, and
resistance to switching. Recent e -WOM research further shows that trust in review information
can strengthen brand awareness and image and contr ibute to brand equity formation (Le et al.,
2024). The proposed relationship therefore treats Brand Equity as an evaluative basis from which
loyalty-related responses may emerge, while recognizing that the cross -sectional design cannot
establish the temporal order of that process.

H3: Brand Equity positively influences Customer Loyalty.
Brand Equity as a Mediating Mechanism
The mediation hypothesis tests whether the statistical relationship between Digital
Marketing Strategies and Customer Loyalty operates through Brand Equity. The mechanism is
conceptually plausible because digital encounters can first shape consumer knowled ge and
evaluation of a brand, which may then be associated with a stronger preference for repurchase
and recommendation. In online travel, social media marketing has been linked to purchase
behavior through brand trust and brand loyalty, illustrating the importance of intervening brand-
level states (Azhar et al., 2023). Hafez (2022) likewise shows that brand experience can help
explain how social media marketing activities relate to brand equity. These studies do not
establish that Brand Equity must mediate  in every market, but they support testing an indirect
pathway rather than assuming only a direct promotional effect.
The proposed mediation is also consistent with evidence that online customer experience
can be associated with loyalty through engagement (Ahmad et al., 2022) and that social -media-
driven brand experience can connect marketing activity with continued usage and loyalty (Kumar
& Hsieh, 2024). If the indirect effect is supported, the model would indicate that consumer
evaluations of digital marketing are related to loyalty partly through accumulated brand value. If
it is not supported, the result would suggest  that Brand Equity and loyalty are related but that
additional relational or experiential variables may be required to explain how digital activity is
connected to loyalty. The mediation test is therefore treated as an empirical question rather than
as a necessary consequence of the direct paths.
H4: Brand Equity mediates the relationship between Digital Marketing Strategies and
Customer Loyalty.
Conceptual Framework
The conceptual framework positions Digital Marketing Strategies as the exogenous
construct, Brand Equity as the mediator, and Customer Loyalty as the final endogenous construct.
The model contains one direct path from Digital Marketing Strategies to Custom er Loyalty and
one indirect pathway through Brand Equity. Online Consumer Behavior is the empirical setting
rather than a separately measured latent variable. Figure 1 summarizes the four hypotheses

METHOD
Research Design
The study used a quantitative explanatory survey with a cross-sectional design. Data were
collected once from eligible online consumers and analyzed as statistical relationships among the
latent constructs Digital Marketing Strategies, Brand Equity, and Cu stomer Loyalty. The design
was suitable for estimating a measurement model, direct structural paths, and an indirect
mediation path within one model. Because observations were collected at one point in time, the
reported path estimates are interpreted as a ssociations that are consistent or inconsistent with
the hypotheses rather than as evidence of temporal causation. PLS-SEM was selected because the
study estimated latent constructs from multiple indicators and examined prediction -oriented
structural relationships and mediation (Dash & Paul, 2021; Memon et al., 2021).
Research Setting, Population, and Sample
The research was conducted among online consumers domiciled in DKI Jakarta and West
Java who had purchased products through digital channels. The target context included
marketplaces, social media stores, brand websites, mobile applications, and social -commerce
platforms. The final sample contained 400 respondents, with an equal quota of 200 respondents
from DKI Jakarta and 200 from West Java. Purposive sampling was used because participation
required specific online-purchase and digital-brand-interaction experience, while quota sampling
maintained equal regional representation. Respondents were eligible when they were at least 17
years old, lived in one of the two study regions, had purchased products online at least twice
during the preceding six months, had interacted with brand content through digital channels, and
could identify one brand they had purchased online and evaluate that brand. The source
manuscript did not specify the calendar period of data collection, so no collection dates are added
in this revision.
Instrument and Measurement
Data were collected with a structured online questionnaire using a five -point Likert
response format ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). Digital Marketing
Strategies were measured with eight items representing content quality, social media
interactivity, personalization and digital offers, digital promotion, e -WOM, and platform
experience. Brand Equity was measured with six items covering brand awareness, brand image
and trustworthiness, perceived quality, and distinctive brand association. Customer Loyalty was
measured with five items covering repurchase intention, consideration for the next purchase,
recommendation intention, brand preference, and resistance to competitor promotions. Brand
loyalty was not included as an indicator of Brand Equity because Customer Loyalty was modeled
separately as the endogenous outcome. Respondents answered all construct items with reference
to one specific brand that they had previously purchased online, which reduced ambiguity about
the object being evaluated.

Data Collection and Screening Procedure
The questionnaire was distributed online through social media, online consumer
communities, student and professional networks, e -commerce user groups, and other relevant
digital channels. Screening questions were placed before the main instrument to assess  age,
domicile, online purchase frequency, digital brand interaction, and the respondent's ability to
identify a focal brand. Responses that failed the eligibility criteria were excluded. Before structural
modeling, the dataset was screened for missing res ponses, duplicate submissions, response
duration, straight-lining, consistency with domicile and purchase criteria, and the presence of a
named focal brand. Microsoft Excel was used for data organization and duplicate checking, while
SPSS was used for descriptive inspection and response-pattern screening. Only the 400 valid cases
reported in the source manuscript were retained for the analysis presented here.
Measurement Model and Structural Analysis
The measurement model was examined before the structural model. Indicator reliability
was assessed through outer loadings, with values above 0.70 treated as satisfactory for the
reflective indicators, while discriminant validity was examined with the Heter otrait-Monotrait
ratio using the reported threshold below 0.85. The original method also stated that Cronbach's
alpha, composite reliability, and Average Variance Extracted would be evaluated, but their
numerical results were not available in the source ma nuscript; those statistics are therefore not
reconstructed or inferred in this revision. The structural model was assessed using the reported
R-square and adjusted R-square values, path coefficients, t -statistics, p-values, and the reported
indirect effect. Bootstrapping in SmartPLS 4 was used for significance testing, although the source
manuscript did not unambiguously state the final number of bootstrap resamples. The original
method also mentioned VIF, f-square, and Q-square, but corresponding numerical outputs were
unavailable; interpretation is therefore restricted to the results that can be documented from the
supplied manuscript.
Mediation Analysis and Decision Rule
Mediation was assessed for the indirect pathway Digital Marketing Strategies → Brand
Equity → Customer Loyalty. The source manuscript used a 5% significance level and treated an
indirect effect as supported when the bootstrapped result was statistically si gnificant and its
confidence interval excluded zero. Direct and indirect paths were interpreted jointly to determine
whether the proposed mediation was supported. The statistical values reported in the Results
section are reproduced from the supplied manuscript without recalculation or alteration. Because
the reported indirect coefficient and confidence-interval information require verification against
the original SmartPLS output, the mediation conclusion should be checked by the author before
final submission.

Discussion
The reported results separate brand building from loyalty formation in a way that is
consistent with the multi -touchpoint logic developed in the literature review. Digital Marketing
Strategies were strongly and positively associated with Brand Equity, whereas the direct path to
Customer Loyalty did not meet the 5% significance criterion. This pattern suggests that
respondents who evaluated a focal brand's content, interaction, offers, promotions, reviews, and
platform experience more favorably also tended to report stronger awareness, image, perceived
quality, and distinctive association, but those evaluations were not sufficient to explain a
comparably strong direct loyalty response. The result is compatible with research showing that
social media marketing activities can strengthen brand-equity dimensions and other customer -
equity outcomes (Hafez, 2021; Wang et al., 2021; Yang et al., 2022; Malarvizhi et al., 2022). Within
this sample, the more defensible interpretation is therefore that digital marketing p erceptions
were closely associated with consumer-based brand value, not that digital activity automatically
created loyal behavior.
The non-significant H1 result is particularly relevant because a considerable part of the
literature reports positive relationships between social media marketing activities and loyalty.
Ibrahim's (2022) meta -analysis identifies a positive overall associat ion but also shows that
contextual and methodological conditions affect the magnitude of that relationship. More recent
e-commerce research similarly indicates that relationship quality can mediate the link between
social media marketing activities and cus tomer loyalty (Banerji & Singh, 2024), while brand
experience can connect such activities with continued use and brand loyalty (Kumar & Hsieh,
2024). The present result does not contradict the possibility that digital marketing contributes to
loyalty; inst ead, it indicates that the direct route was not statistically supported under the
reported model and decision rule. One possible interpretation is that favorable evaluations of
digital touchpoints may coexist with continued comparison of competing alternatives. Because
switching costs, competitor attractiveness, and actual switching behavior were not directly
measured, this explanation should be treated as a theoretical interpretation rather than an
empirical finding of the present study.  That explanation remains theoretical because switching
conditions were not measured directly in this study.
The supported H2 path reinforces the interpretation of Digital Marketing Strategies as a
brand-building interface. Prior research in banking has shown that social media marketing
activities can be associated with brand equity through brand trust, brand love, brand experience,
and perceived uniqueness (Hafez, 2021, 2022). Guha et al. (2021) also found that social media
marketing activities were related to brand awareness, brand image, and brand equity, while
recent work on e-WOM shows that trustworthy review content can support brand awareness and
image formation (Le et al., 2024). These studies help explain why a composite assessment of
content, interaction, personalization, promotion, reviews, and platform experience can align
strongly with consumer-based brand value. The finding is also coherent with the respondent task
in this study, because participants evaluated one specific brand rather than digital shopping in

general. Their digital evaluations and brand-equity judgments therefore referred to the same focal
object, which gives the H2 association a clear interpretive basis.
The positive H3 relationship indicates that stronger Brand Equity was associated with
stronger Customer Loyalty. This result is consistent with the idea that loyalty becomes more likely
when consumers possess a favorable and differentiated representation of the brand rather than
merely recognizing a promotional offer. Quaye et al. (2022) show that trust and brand relationship
quality are important mechanisms in brand loyalty, and Valmohammadi et al. (2025) link e-brand
experience and e-WOM with e-brand loyalty in online retail. Aragone s-Jerico   et al. (2024) likewise
show that satisfaction and brand love are relevant antecedents of loyalty and e-WOM. The present
model does not measure those relational states, but the significant Brand Equity –Customer
Loyalty path is consistent with the broader view that accumulated brand evaluation provides a
more stable basis for repurchase, recommendation, preference, and resistance to switching than
promotional exposure alone. Because the study is cross-sectional, this relationship should still be
read as statistical association rather than proof that Brand Equity temporally caused loyalty.
The reported mediation result did not meet the 5% significance criterion. However,
because the reported indirect coefficient and confidence -interval information still require
verification against the original SmartPLS output, the non-mediation finding should be regarded
as provisional. Accordingly, interpretation of H4 should remain limited until the underlying
bootstrapping results are confirmed. This is theoretically informative because the direct H2 and
H3 paths were significant, yet the reported indirect test was not. Research in related settings
frequently identifies additional mechanisms between digital activity and consumer outcomes,
including engagement (Ahmad et al., 2022), trust (Azhar et al., 2023), satisfaction and flow (Bao
& Zhu, 2023), and brand experience (Kumar & Hsieh, 2024). Belhadi et al. (2023) further show
that digitalization can have counterintuitive loyalty implications when it changes the value
consumers assign to e-WOM. These studies suggest that a single mediator may not fully represent
the process through which digital marketing perceptions become loyalty. However, the numerical
inconsistency in the reported indirect output must be resolved before the non-mediation result is
treated as a definitive statistical contribution.
The Jakarta and West Java setting adds context to these relationships without justifying
broader regional generalization. The sample was deliberately balanced across the two regions,
but the main model did not test regional differences, so the results represent the combined sample
rather than evidence that the two markets behave identically. The respondent profile also shows
that the sample was concentrated among younger adults, diploma/bachelor graduates,
employees, students, and marketplace users. Those characteristics are relevant because platform-
based consumers routinely encounter reviews, promotions, recommendations, and competing
offers in the same decision environment. Omnichannel studies show that customer experience
depends on integration, individu alization, and cross -channel consistency (Rahman et al., 2022;
Quach et al., 2022; Yin et al., 2022), which supports interpreting the Digital Marketing Strategies
construct as a bundle of connected consumer perceptions. Future work could test whether the
observed paths differ by region, platform type, product category, or shopping intensity rather
than assuming homogeneous online consumer behavior.
The study's main contribution is therefore conceptual rather than causal: it distinguishes
the ability of digital marketing touchpoints to correlate with brand value from their ability to
correlate directly with durable loyalty. For managers, this distinct ion implies that high
engagement, attractive promotions, or convenient digital interfaces should not be treated as
substitutes for evidence that consumers recognize, value, prefer, and trust the brand over

alternatives. Digital campaigns can be assessed not only through immediate response metrics but
also through whether they strengthen brand awareness, image, perceived quality, and distinctive
associations. At the same time, the non-significant direct and reported indirect paths indicate that
loyalty may require complementary relational mechanisms that were not measured here. The
findings are therefore best used to motivate more complete models incorporating trust,
satisfaction, perceived value, engagement, or service quality rather than to conclude that Brand
Equity is unimportant. Longitudinal or repeated-measure designs would be especially useful for
testing whether changes in digital experience precede changes in brand equity and loyalty over
time.
CONCLUSIONS
This study examined the relationships among Digital Marketing Strategies, Brand Equity,
and Customer Loyalty in a cross-sectional sample of 400 online consumers from DKI Jakarta and
West Java. Based on the statistics reported in the supplied manuscript, Di gital Marketing
Strategies were not significantly related to Customer Loyalty at the 5% level, while Digital
Marketing Strategies were positively and significantly related to Brand Equity and Brand Equity
was positively and significantly related to Customer Loyalty. The reported indirect effect through
Brand Equity also did not meet the 5% significance criterion. These results support a cautious
interpretation in which favorable evaluations of digital marketing touchpoints are closely
associated with consum er-based brand value, whereas loyalty appears to require a broader
relational basis than digital exposure or promotion alone. The study contributes by treating
Digital Marketing Strategies as a multi -touchpoint consumer interface and by separating Brand
Equity from Customer Loyalty rather than embedding loyalty inside the equity construct.
The conclusions are bounded by the study design and the available analysis output. The
data are self -reported, cross-sectional, purposively sampled, and limited to respondents in two
Indonesian regions, so causal and population-wide claims are not warranted. The model does not
compare regions or product categories and does not include potentially relevant mechanisms
such as brand trust, satisfaction, perceived value, customer engagement, or e -service quality. In
addition, several PLS-SEM statistics described in the original method, including Cronbach's alpha,
composite reliability, AVE, VIF, f-square, Q-square, and the exact bootstrap resample count, were
not available in the supplied manuscript and should be verified before submission. The H1 t -
statistic/p-value combination and the H4 indirect -effect/confidence-interval reporting also
require direct checking against the original SmartPLS output. Future research can strengthen the
model by using longitudinal designs, broader and probability -based samples wher e feasible,
product-category comparisons, and verified serial or parallel mediation models.
