Abstract
This study examines the boundary-spanning process undertaken by C-Klik Media to integrate corpor ate brand value into bookstagra m
review content, along with the resulting impact of this collaboration. Employing a descriptive qualitative approach, data were collected
through in-depth interviews, non-participatory observation, and documentation involving two internal informants and three bookstagram
partners. The findings reveal five interconnected stages: strategic selection of bookstagrammers through multi-criteria researc h;
integration of brand value through flexible and authentic cont ent briefs; negotiation between brand control and creator autonom y;
performance-based evaluation as a boundary-control mechanism; and the strategic use of Instagram as an arena for brand reinforcement.
A key finding is that none of the bookstagram partners underwent a draft review before publication, contradicting the company's  stated
policy and revealing an asymmetrical power relation in barter-based collaborations. Unlike prior studies that position content creators
as the primary boundary spanners, this research offers a novel perspective by positioning the publisher as the central boundary-spanning
actor, with practical implications for developing lightweight, self-assessment-based quality-assurance mechanisms that preserve creators'
creative freedom while maintaining brand consistency. Theoretically, this study extends boundary spanning theory beyond Institutional
public relations contexts into digital marketing collaboration between book publishers and social media content creators, demonstrating
that brand value can be effectively communicated through negotiate d authenticity rather than strict message control. The collab oration
ultimately strengthens branding through organic soft selling and builds a trust ecosystem linking publishers, creators, and rea ding
communities.
Keywords : Boundary spanning, Brand value, Bookstagram, Digital marketing, Content creator
Introduction
The publishing industry in the 21st century has changed significantly in relation to economic, social, and cultural
developments. Computerisation, digitalisation, and the emergence of new media and tools have compelled the publishing
industry to adapt. The development of the internet has brought both threats and opportunities to this industry, whereby on
one hand it is regarded as a substitute for conventional distribution, and on the other hand it has become a new channel for
developing marketing innovations. The places where readers purchase and access books have also undergone
transformation, with numerous substitutes emerging and introducing new innovations (Tselenti, 2022). Changes in the
book industry have similarly occurred in Indonesia, which has experienced evolution in line with technological
developments and the COVID-19 pandemic.
The rapid advancement of technology today has led to an explosion of information. The digital world is increasingly
characterised by the emergence of various social media platforms that enable people to connect and obtain information
with ease, whether from friends or from individuals in different parts of the world. Facebook, LinkedIn, and Twitter are
platforms for building personal and professional relationships, whereas Instagram, YouTube, and TikTok are platforms for
sharing photos, videos, and documents (Ratih et al., 2025). This has made internet use unavoidable.
Social media is one of the primary products of technological development, widely used by society today. Social
media is an online media application that enables users to interact, share content, and connect with others virtually. The
presence of social media has bridged the gap between smartphones and the wider world, transforming human
communication behaviour (Andika & Sazali, 2024). Among the many social media platforms available, Instagram has
become one of the most popular and influential, primarily because it enables aesthetic, interactive visual content. Instagram
has the potential to serve as a marketing communication medium, as it enables users to upload photos and videos through
the feed and reels features, which are beneficial tools for companies to enhance brand awareness, foster consumer
engagement, and promote products (Ratih et al., 2025).
In the realm of book literacy, Instagram has attracted the attention of reading communities known as bookstagram.
The bookstagram phenomenon emerged in the early 2010s alongside the development of social media, which made books

more appealing through visual presentation rather than words alone (Tselenti, 2022). In 2011, consumers introduced the

content about reading materials, packaged in attractive, aesthetic photos or videos, thereby facilitating book enthusiasts in
exchanging information with one another (Rinanda & Rohmaniyah, 2024). The content on social media attracts new

Bookstagram offers book publishers an opportunity to engage audiences authentically through content produced from
a reader-to-reader perspective, an approach audiences find far more trusted than conventional advertising. This
collaborative activity is not solely concerned with direct promotion but also with how book narratives and the formation
of reader identity can unfold organically. This potential can be maximised when the company's brand value is consistently
integrated into bookstagrammers' review content. Elysa & Dr istiana (2024) found that bookstagram functions as a space
for the exchange of recommendations, the shaping of reading styles, and a promotional medium for publishers, in which
aesthetics serves as the primary communicative currency.
Nevertheless, collaboration between publishers and bookstagrammers does not always proceed linearly. On one hand,
publishers have an interest in conveying and maintaining the consistency of their brand value through content produced by
external parties. On the other hand, bookstagrammers possess distinctive creative autonomy and communication styles
that, if excessively constrained, would reduce the authenticity of the content and erode audience trust. This difference in
orientation can lead to message inconsistencies if not managed through adaptive communication mechanisms (Andika &
Sazali, 2024). The integration of brand value into bookstagram content can therefore be understood not merely as a
promotional activity, but as a communication process involving value negotiation, message adjustment, and the
management of a relationship between two parties with differing interests.
In this context, the concept of boundary spanning becomes relevant. Boundary-spanning positions the organisation
as an active intermediary between internal and external environments, with primary functions of environmental monitoring,
information filtering, and two-way communication between the company and its publics (Kriyantono, 2017). This theory
affirms that organisations cannot rely solely on closed-system internal interaction processes but must actively bridge
internal interests with the dynamics of the external environment. Stephens et al., (2024) define boundary spanning as a
bridging concept that highlights the key role of translating between the different priorities, logics, or languages of various
organisations, while Burbach et al., (2023) conceptualise it as the skill of building connections that transcend institutional,
professional, and organisational boundaries.
C-Klik Media, a book publisher based in Sleman, Yogyakarta, serves as a concrete example of how an organisation
confronts such challenges. Since 2022, C-Klik Media has actively engaged in collaborations with bookstagram accounts
through its official Instagram account @cklikmedia as part of its marketing communication strategy. This collaboration
reflects a broader organisational challenge concerning how a company can effectively integrate its brand value into
externally produced content without sacrificing the authenticity that makes such content trusted by audiences.
Previous studies on bookstagram have generally focused on the role of influencers in increasing reading interest or
the effectiveness of book promotion on social media (Fahzaria et al., 2025; Elysa & Dristiana, 2024; Rinanda &
Rohmaniyah, 2024). Meanwhile, studies on boundary spanning have more frequently examined institutional public
relations practitioners as key actors (Rahmadanty et al., 2019; Mukminah & Rachmawati, 2025; Sridharan, 2023). This
study addresses that gap by positioning C-Klik Media as a publishing organisation rather than a bookstagram account, as
the primary boundary spanning actor, and by analysing how the process of brand value integration unfolds from an
organisational perspective. On the basis of this background, this study aims to analyse the boundary spanning process
carried out by C-Klik Media in integrating the company's brand value into bookstagram review content, as well as the
impact generated by the collaboration.
To understand how C-Klik Media manages its relationships with these bookstagram accounts, this study draws upon
boundary spanning theory as its primary analytical framework. Boundary spanning theory is a concept rooted in the public
relations tradition that positions the organisation as an open system that cannot rely solely on internal interaction processes
(Kriyantono, 2017). Through the public relations boundary spanning function, an organisation interacts with its
environment to monitor, select, and gather information, which is then communicated to its external stakeholders.
Individuals who carry out this role are referred to as boundary spanners, that is, those who not only cross contexts on both
sides of the boundary but are also capable of adapting according to the context as interpreted by the other party (Koyama
& Kasper, 2020). In this study, C-Klik Media is positioned as the organisation that performs the boundary spanning
function, with bookstagram accounts as the external party that serves simultaneously as a partner and as the target of its
communication.
According to Kriyantono (2017), boundary spanning activities encompass three primary functions. First, explaining
information regarding the organisation to the public by interpreting the company's philosophy, policies, and programmes.
Second, monitoring the environment surrounding the organisation by identifying and resolving problems that may arise.
Third, creating a two-way communication system with the public as a facilitator that enables both parties to mutually adapt
to their environment. In the context of bookstagram collaboration, these three functions operate simultaneously when C-
Klik Media selects partners, conveys product knowledge, negotiates content briefs, and evaluates the results of the

collaboration. Previous studies on boundary spanning have generally focused on institutional public relations practitioners
as key actors (Rahmadanty et al., 2019; Mukminah & Rachmawati, 2025; Sridharan, 2023). This study presents a different
perspective by positioning the book publisher as the boundary-spanning actor within the digital marketing collaboration
ecosystem with social media content creators.
The boundary spanning process carried out by C-Klik Media is fundamentally directed towards a single primary
objective, namely, integrating the company's brand value into content produced by external parties. Brand value constitutes
the most valuable asset for a business, determined by consumer perceptions, brand reputation, customer loyalty, and
perceived quality, and is increasingly shaped through digital experiences (Endarwati, 2025). Gupta et al., (2020) affirm
that brand value capabilities can determine brand differentiation and drive marketing objectives. In this study, brand value
is not conceptualised in financial terms, but rather as a communicative construct comprising the values that C-Klik Media
seeks to build and communicate through bookstagram collaboration. These values are reflected in the messages, narratives,
and visual representations in book review content on Instagram, encompassing the image of publisher professionalism,
credibility regarding book quality, and the brand identity intended to be perceived by audiences. Pratama & Daulay (2025)
identify that brand value encompasses utilitarian and hedonic dimensions that equally determine the intensity of consumer
engagement with a brand, an understanding that is relevant for reading how bookstagram audiences perceive the C-Klik
Media brand through independent content creators.
The integration of brand value does not take place in a vacuum, but within an ecosystem of digital marketing that has
now become the primary arena of brand competition. Digital marketing is a strategy that employs digital platforms to
extend brand reach, build closer relationships with consumers, and increase brand awareness through interactive content
(Widagdo et al., 2025). Rimadias et al., (2021) add that companies need to utilise all brand communication channels,
including social media, to create and sustain brand value. Nardo et al., (2024) affirm that integrating brand value in digital
communication demands a strategic approach that combines functional and emotional value within digital content. Within
this framework, bookstagram collaboration is positioned not merely as a promotional channel but as a digital
communication practice that enables C-Klik Media to organically integrate its brand value through the voices of creators
already trusted by the reading community. Andika & Sazali (2024) reinforce this argument by demonstrating that every
interaction and conversation occurring on social media constitutes an element in the construction of brand perception in
the minds of audiences, such that content produced by bookstagram accounts concretely contributes to the formation of C-
Klik Media's brand identity in the digital space.
The role of bookstagram as a digital marketing partner of C-Klik Media must be understood within the broader
context of influencer marketin g. Influencer mark eting is a marketing te chnique that utilises publ ic figures or content
creators on social media to influence audiences, and collaboration between brands and influencers serves not only as a
promotional tool but also as a means of integrating brand value into content in a creative manner. Roni Aunul Kholik &
Budianto (2023) found that digital marketing strategies through influencer collaboration can increase brand awareness,
strengthen brand image, and build consumer trust towards the promoted products. Andi S et al., (2024) further affirm that
the use of influencers in digital marketing has proven effective in building consumer engagement and increasing brand
loyalty. More specifically, within the bookstagram ecosystem, Fahzaria et al., (2025) found that nano-micro bookstagram
influencers are capable of maximising engagement through visual storytelling techniques, captioning, and the use of
hashtags that are personal and authentic. Rinanda & Rohmaniyah (2024) demonstrate that bookstagram content exerts an
influence on the reading interest of its followers. These findings cumulatively affirm that bookstagram accounts are not
merely content distribution channels, but agents of reading taste formation that possess organic credibility in the eyes of
their audiences. It is this potential that makes bookstagram a strategic partner for book publishers in implementing the
boundary-spanning mechanism to authentically integrate brand value into the digital space of the reading community.
Methodology
This study employs a qualitative, descriptive research method. This approach was selected because the study aims to gain
an in-depth understanding of the boundary-spanning process undertaken by C-Klik Media to integrate the company's brand
value into bookstagram review content, a dynamic, contextual communication phenomenon that involves the negotiation
of meaning among different actors. Qualitative research emphasizes understanding the social context in which phenomena
occur and the social interactions that take place within it. Through this methodology, data are collected using various
methods, including interviews, observations, and document analysis, thereby yielding rich, varied data that provide more
comprehensive insights into the phenomenon under investigation (Rifa'i, 2023).
The research subjects consist of two groups selected for their direct involvement in the collaboration process.
Research subjects are individuals or groups that become the focus of the study, selected for their relevance to the
phenomenon under investigation. In the problem being studied, the phenomenon from which data are obtained constitutes
the research subject (Wijaya et al., 2025). In total, five informants took part in this study across two categories. The first
group comprises the internal parties of C-Klik Media, namely the Marketing Division and the Social Media Specialist
Division. C-Klik Media was selected because it represents the primary actor serving as a boundary spanner, that is, the
organisational unit that performs the activities of conveying organisational values and interests to external parties,
monitoring the external environment, conducting two-way communication and negotiation, filtering and adjusting

4120
messages, as well as conducting evaluation and feedback. The second group consists of three bookstagram accounts that
have previously collaborated with C-Klik Media, namely @Ranbookshelf, @Astrndrn, and @Fatimuin. These
bookstagram accounts were selected because they serve as external parties directly involved in C-Klik Media's boundary-
spanning relationship and function as intermediaries in conveying brand messages and values to audiences. The three
accounts were chosen purposively, as each had collaborated directly with C-Klik Media and represented distinct
engagement profiles and content styles, ranging from narrative-driven reviews to visually aesthetic packaging. This
variation supports richer cross-comparison and, combined with the inclusion of internal informants, enables the source
triangulation described below.
The data sources in this study are divided into two categories. Primary sources are obtained directly through in-depth
interviews with research informants, while secondary sources are obtained indirectly through content observation and
documentation (Ma'ruf et al., 2025). In-depth interviewing is one of the primary strategies in the qualitative approach,
namely, gathering information for research purposes through question-and-answer sessions conducted directly to obtain
primary data regarding C-Klik Media's strategies, considerations, and experiences in bridging corporate interests with the
characteristics of bookstagram accounts. Interviews were conducted in a semi-structured manner using an interview guide
to remain flexible and allow for in-depth data exploration. Interviews took place from February to March 2026, with each
informant interviewed individually, either in person at the C-Klik Media office or remotely through Google Meet. Through
these interviews, the researcher explored how each actor involved perceived, negotiated, and integrated the company's
brand value into review content. Non-participatory observation positions the researcher as an observer from a certain
distance without involvement in the interactions or activities of the subjects (Romdona et al., 2025), focused on the
narrative, visual, and communicative style of the content as a concrete form of the implementation of boundary spanning
and brand value in collaborative practice on social media. This observation was conducted for one month in March 2026,
during which the researcher reviewed the collaborative content of the three partner accounts on Instagram without engaging
directly with the subjects or their audiences. Documentation was carried out through the collection of communication traces
in the form of content uploads, captions, and collaboration materials on Instagram, which were analysed to identify message
consistency and patterns of brand value integration as transmitted by the company in its capacity as a boundary spanner to
its audiences.
Data collection was conducted through three techniques. First, semi-structured in-depth interviews using an interview
guide based on boundary spanning functions to explore the processes of communication, negotiation, and brand value
integration in the collaboration. Second, non-participatory observation of collaborative content on Instagram from the three
partner bookstagram accounts, encompassing the collection of quantitative data (number of likes, comments, and views)
and qualitative analysis of the presence of brand-value elements in the content. Third, documentation in the form of feed
content, captions, and reels from the @cklikmedia account and partner bookstagram accounts. The study was conducted
from February to March 2026 at the C-Klik Media office in Sleman, Yogyakarta, and on Instagram.
Prior to data collection, each informant received prelim inary information about the study's purpose and procedures
and agreed to participate voluntarily. Verbal consent was obtained in person from the internal informants at the C-Klik
Media office, while written consent was obtained from the bookstagram informants through WhatsApp. The confidentiality
of sensitive information disclosed during the interviews was maintained throughout the study.
Data validity in qualitative research is a critical concept for ensuring the credibility and reliability of research findings
(Luthfiyani & Murhayati, 2024). This study explicitly applies source triangulation as its validity technique. Source
triangulation in this study was conducted by comparing and verifying data from internal and external informants who hold
different positions and interests in the boundary spanning process. Through this technique, the researcher sought to compare
data from each source to uncover the truth of the information (Alfansyur & Mariyani, 2020). The comparison of these
sources aimed to achieve a more objective and comprehensive understanding of how the boundary-spanning process of
integrating brand value is carried out, negotiated, and perceived by each communication actor involved. By simultaneously
contrasting data from internal C-Klik Media parties and external bookstagram parties, source triangulation enabled the
researcher to identify both gaps and convergences between claims and practices occurring in the field, most notably the
discrepancy between C-Klik Media's claim of a mandatory draft review and the bookstagram informants' unanimous report
that no such review ever occurred, thereby ensuring that the validity of interpretations can be methodologically accounted
for.
Data analysis explicitly follows the thematic analysis procedure of the Miles and Huberman model, which
encompasses three stages, namely first, data reduction with reference to the boundary spanning functions and the brand
value framework; second, data presentation in the form of narrative text that groups findings according to themes; and
third, conclusion drawing by comparing empirical findings against the theoretical framework employed. Coding within the
reduction stage proceeded through open coding to identify keywords and recurring statements in the transcripts,
classification of these codes into broader categories such as partner selection, communication patterns, and performance
evaluation, and finally selective coding to determine the five themes presented in the findings.
In greater detail, the data reduction stage was carried out by sharpening, grouping, and selecting data relevant to the
research focus (Haryono, 2020). Interview data were reduced with reference to the five boundary spanning functions,
namely the conveyance of organisational values and interests to external parties, monitoring of the external environment,
two-way communication and negotiation, filtering and adjustment of messages, and evaluation and feedback; while

4121
observational data were reduced with reference to the dimensions of brand value, encompassing the meaning of message
and content narrative, visual representation and brand image, consistency of values conveyed, and the level of content
authenticity. The data presentation stage was presented as narrative text, grouping findings according to themes generated
through the qualitative thematisation process, thereby facilitating the researcher's identification of patterns and
interconnections between communication practices in the field and the theoretical framework employed. Subsequently, the
conclusion-drawing stage was carried out by interpreting the research findings based on the boundary spanning and brand
value theoretical frameworks, in order to assess the extent to which C-Klik Media performs the boundary spanning
functions and the extent to which the company's brand value is consistently integrated into the bookstagram review content
produced by independent creators.
Data saturation was assessed against the research objectives and problem formulation. Saturation was considered
achieved once interviews with all five informants yielded recurring, consistent patterns across the five themes identified in
this study, with no substantially new themes emerging after the fifth interview, particularly given the consistent absence of
a formal draft review process reported by all three bookstagram informants.
Result and Discussion
Result
This study found that the boundary spanning process carried out by C-Klik Media in integrating brand value into
bookstagram review content unfolds through five interconnected and adaptive-cyclical stages. These five stages encompass
multi-criteria bookstagram selection, brand value integration through product knowledge and flexible briefs, the balancing
of brand control and creator autonomy, performance-based evaluation as a boundary control instrument, and the utilisation
of Instagram as a boundary spanning arena for long-term branding reinforcement. These stages do not proceed in a linear
fashion, but rather mutually inform one another and serve as material for the refinement of subsequent collaboration cycles.
The collaboration process begins with a structured selection of prospective bookstagram partners based on the quality
of visual content, number of viewers, level of engagement, and relevance of book genres typically reviewed. This selection
is followed by proactive and adaptive communication, ranging from the initial offer through to the maintenance of
relationships that continue even after the collaboration concludes. In each collaboration, C-Klik Media sends a product
knowledge document containing the book's selling points as a guide for the bookstagram account, while simultaneously
granting the creator full freedom over the concept and format of the content. A notable finding in this respect is that all
bookstagram accounts serving as research informants stated that they had never undergone a draft review process prior to
publication, a condition that contradicts the claims of C-Klik Media's internal parties and indicates a gap between policy
and practice in the field, particularly in barter-based collaborations.
Despite this gap, non-participatory observation of the collaborative content of the three partner bookstagram accounts
demonstrates that elements of C-Klik Media's brand value remain present consistently in each upload. Post-collaboration
evaluation is conducted routinely using combined quantitative and qualitative indicators, with the results of the evaluation
serving as the basis for strategic decisions regarding the continuation of the partnership. All content performance data and
representations of brand value elements obtained from non-pa rticipatory observation are summarised in Tables 1 and 2

Discussion
The structured and multi-criteria bookstagram selection process carried out by C-Klik Media constitutes a concrete
manifestation of the environmental monitoring function in boundary spanning as theorised by Kriyantono (2017). The
action of the C-Klik Media team in analysing content quality, engagement, viewers, and book genre relevance prior to
deciding on a collaboration represents a practice of strategic environmental intelligence gathering. Through this process,
the organisation actively filters and selects the external actors most compatible with the company's internal interests, which,
in boundary spanning terminology, functions as a gatekeeping mechanism towards the external partner ecosystem.
This selection approach aligns with Stephens et al. (2024), who conceptualise boundary spanning as a process of
translation between the differing logics and languages of various organisations. The selection of appropriate bookstagram
accounts is fundamentally an initial act of translation, namely ensuring that the selected external actors possess
communication language and values aligned with C-Klik Media's brand message. By selecting bookstagram accounts that
are genre-relevant and have strong engagement, C-Klik Media is not merely seeking content distribution channels but
partners capable of conveying its brand value in a voice trusted by the reading community.
The adaptive and proactive communication pattern carried out by C-Klik Media throughout the collaboration cycle
confirms the findings of Mukminah & Rachmawati (2025) regarding the digital public relations boundary spanning
activities of Komunitas Narasi TV, which demonstrate that the effectiveness of boundary spanning is largely determined
by the organisation's capacity to build sustained two-way communication. In the case of C-Klik Media, consistent
communication constitutes a strategic advantage that builds relational capital with bookstagram accounts. This relational
capital ultimately increases partner loyalty and facilitates future collaborations, as bookstagram accounts develop a deeper
sense of attachment to the company.
From a critical standpoint, a selection approach that prioritises quantitative metrics such as viewers and engagement
rate carries potential weaknesses that warrant attention. Bookstagram accounts with smaller but more segmented and loyal
audiences risk being excluded from the partner ecosystem, whereas in the context of niche marketing, such as book
publishing, more segmented communities often possess higher conversion rates. Fahzaria et al., (2025) demonstrate that
nano-micro bookstagram influencers are capable of maximi sing engagement through more personal and authentic visual
storytelling techniques and captioning. The implication is that C-Klik Media is advised to balance quantitative
considerations with a deeper qualitative assessment of the depth of community and the quality of the creator's relationship
with their audience.
Moving from the selection dimension to the substantive dimension of communication, brand value integration
through product knowledge and flexible briefs demonstrates how C-Klik Media operationalises its brand value within the
practice of content collaboration. The mechanism of delivering product knowledge to each bookstagram account prior to
collaboration constitutes a manifestation of the first boundary spanning function according to Kriyantono (2017), namely
explaining information regarding the organisation to the public. The cross-divisional preparation of selling points internally
ensures that the information reaching bookstagram accounts is not only technically accurate but also reflects the company's
values comprehensively.
What differentiates C-Klik Media's approach from conventional marketing practice is the strategic decision to
explicitly encourage honest reviews as part of the brand communication strategy. As Endarwati (2025) affirm that brand
value is shaped by consumer perceptions, brand reputation, customer loyalty, and perceived quality, the opening of space
for creators to convey assessments that are not always positive constitutes a deliberate act of trust delegation. This strategy
positions authenticity as an instrument of brand reputation building rather than as a threat to the company's image, and
implicitly expresses the company's confidence in the quality of its own products.
Empirical confirmation of the effectiveness of this strategy is found in non-participatory observation data, in which
all primary elements of C-Klik Media's brand value appear consistently in the content of @Ranbookshelf, @Astrndrn, and
@Fatimuin. The tagging of the publisher's account, the inclusion of selling points in captions, clear visual displays of the
books, and content styles consistent with each creator's indi vidual character are uniformly present without a strict draft
review mechanism. This confirms that the provision of adequate product knowledge has proven effective as an implicit
guide for bookstagram accounts in representing the brand, while simultaneously substantiating the view of Nardo et al.,
(2024) that integrating brand value in digital communication demands an approach that combines functional and emotional
dimensions within content. An alternative reading of this consistency should nonetheless be considered. The uniform

4123
presence of publisher tagging and clear book visuals across all three accounts may reflect an established genre convention
of bookstagram content itself rather than the specific influence of C-Klik Media's product knowledge, since such elements
are commonly found in book collaboration posts regardless of the publisher involved. If this alternative explanation holds,
the causal link between C-Klik Media's communication strategy and the observed consistency would be considerably
weaker than the findings initially suggest.
Nevertheless, there is one nuance that warrants critical attention. Although creators stated their readiness to honestly
convey the shortcomings of books, none of the content from the three observed bookstagram accounts explicitly featured
points of criticism, as recorded in Table 2. This condition does not necessarily indicate inconsistency, as it is possible that
the books reviewed did not present significant shortcomings according to the creators' own assessment. That said, this
finding raises the question of the extent to which relational considerations between creators and publishers shape a tendency
towards positive content, and whether the value of authenticity promoted by C-Klik Media is genuinely and consistently
realised in content practice in the field. A plausible alternative interpretation is that the absence of criticism reflects a
relational courtesy bias, whereby creators are reluctant to voice unfavourable assessments openly because doing so could
jeopardise an ongoing and mutually beneficial partnership. Under this reading, the uniformly positive tone of the observed
content would stem less from the genuine absence of shortcomings than from the creators' awareness of the relational stakes
involved in sustaining future collaboration with C-Klik Media.
Proceeding further to the relational dimension of the collaboration process, the balancing of brand control and creator
autonomy presents the most dynamic and complex dimension of the entire boundary spanning process carried out by C-
Klik Media. Burbach et al., (2023) define boundary spanning as the skill of building connections that transcend institutional,
professional, and organisational boundaries, and the findings of this study extend that understanding by demonstrating that
meaningful cross-boundary connections can only be formed through active negotiation that respects the interests of both
parties.
C-Klik Media consistently prioritises flexibility as an operational value in collaboration. Bookstagram accounts are
granted full freedom over their choice of content format, openness to brief negotiation, and receive creative solutions from
the company when facing technical limitations. This approach aligns with Koyama and Kasper's (2020) characterisation of
boundary spanners as parties capable of adapting according to context as interpreted by the other party. The transfer of
selling points to caption text when they cannot be included in the video constitutes a concrete example of such contextual
adaptation, demonstrating that C-Klik Media prioritises the substance of the brand message over insisting on any particular
format of delivery.
A crucial finding in this dimension is the absence of a draft review process, as consistently stated by all bookstagram
accounts, a condition that differs from the claims of the company's internal parties. This divergence in perceptions indicates
that the brand control mechanism in barter-based collaborations is very loose, reflecting an unbalanced power relationship.
In barter-based collaborations, bookstagram accounts occupy a structurally more vulnerable position because the absence
of financial compensation means their bargaining power is lower, while C-Klik Media retains full control over the
continuation of the collaboration through performance assessment. This condition creates an imbalance that needs to be
acknowledged ethically in the management of the partnership.
Paradoxically, the observational data prove that the absence of a formal curation mechanism does not result in a
failure of brand value integration. All observed collaborativ e content consistently contains brand elements, and the soft-
selling approach is internalised by creators without explicit instruction in the brief. This finding affirms the effectiveness
of the trust-based collaboration model built by C-Klik Media, while simultaneously confirming that the initial
communication process through product knowledge functions as a sufficiently strong implicit guide. Nevertheless, this
condition also means that the representation of brand value is heavily dependent on the good faith of each individual
creator, which has the potential to produce inconsistencies in the future if not supported by a lightweight but structured
quality assurance mechanism.
This apparent effectiveness, however, should not be read as evidence that a trust-based collaboration model is without
limitation. The model's success in this study rests on a relatively small and self-selected group of three bookstagram
accounts, each already vetted through C-Klik Media's multi-criteria selection process, and its consistency cannot be
assumed to hold once the number of partner accounts expands. As the pool of collaborators grows, the organisation's
capacity to informally monitor whether brand value is being represented appropriately is likely to diminish, increasing the
probability that some creators will diverge from the brand's intended message without the company's immediate awareness.
The model is also structurally fragile in the sense that it depends entirely on the individual creator's discretion rather than
on any binding organisational mechanism, which means a single instance of brand misrepresentation could occur and reach
the audience before C-Klik Media has any opportunity to intervene. Furthermore, because the trust extended to creators is
not accompanied by formal compensation in barter-based arrangements, what appears in this study as voluntary alignment
with brand value may in part be a function of the creators' dependent position within an unequal partnership rather than a
purely trust-based relationship in the conventional sense. Taken together, these considerations suggest that the trust-based
model observed in this study should be understood as effective under the specific and relatively favourable conditions
present in this case, rather than as a universally reliable mechanism for brand value integration.
Having examined the dynamics within the collaboration process, attention turns to how C-Klik Media measures and
manages the effectiveness of the collaboration through a performance-based evaluation mechanism. This mechanism

4124
represents the cyclical dimension of the boundary spanning process that is frequently overlooked in previous studies, yet
is empirically proven to be an important boundary control instrument in the long term. Kriyantono (2017) notes that
boundary spanners serve as management partners in identifying and resolving problems, and performance-based evaluation
constitutes a concrete manifestation of that role.
The use of combined indicators comprising views, likes, sales figures, visual quality, and content narrative as
evaluation benchmarks reflects a pragmatic and results-oriented orientation. Gupta et al., (2020) state that brand value
capabilities drive marketing objectives through evidence-based brand strategy, and in this context, the performance
evaluation of bookstagram collaborations represents an effort to measure the extent to which the collaboration contributes
to the reinforcement of brand value in the market. This evaluation mechanism simultaneously performs two functions
within C-Klik Media's boundary spanning system, namely a selective function and a feedback function, which together
render the boundary spanning process dynamic and continuously updated on the basis of actual performance data.
Observational data empirically verify that the variation in performance across bookstagram accounts is both real and
significant. The Reels content of @Astrdrn recorded the highest view count at 14,200, far surpassing @Fatimuin with 895
views and @Ranbookshelf with 791 views, as shown in Table 1. This variation confirms the relevance of the performance-
based evaluation mechanism implemented by C-Klik Media and distinguishes its approach from the qualitative editorial
standard-based evaluation identified by Sridharan (2023) in a journalistic context, reflecting the data-driven paradigm
characteristic of the digital marketing ecosystem.
Nevertheless, the case of @Astrdrn, who concealed likes through the hide likes feature, reveals an inherent weakness
in an evaluation system that relies on a single metric. The creator's decision to hide likes constitutes a form of resistance to
the metrics culture prevalent within the bookstagram community and does not correlate with poor overall content
performance, as evidenced by the high view count nonetheless recorded by that account. This finding reinforces the
recommendation that C-Klik Media develop more comprehensive evaluation dimensions, for instance, through audience
comment sentiment analysis and assessment of the consistency of brand value representation in content, so that the
measurement of collaboration effectiveness does not rest solely on figures that can easily be concealed or manipulated.
At a more macro level, the entire boundary spanning process carried out by C-Klik Media requires an arena in which
it is operationalised, and Instagram occupies that central position. The choice of Instagram as the primary platform is not
merely a tactical decision, but reflects a deep strategic understanding of the behaviour of the reading community in
Indonesia. Widagdo et al. (2025) state that digital marketing is a strategy that employs digital platforms to extend brand
reach and build closer relationships with consumers, and the characteristics of Instagram that support high-quality visual
content and rich interaction features make it the most appropriate platform for the book community ecosystem.
Elysa and Dristiana (2024) found that bookstagram functions as a space for the exchange of recommendations, the
formation of reading styles, and a promotional medium for publ ishers, in which aesthetics serve as the primary capital in
communication. This finding further affirms the strategic relevance of Instagram as the collaboration arena selected by C-
Klik Media. Moreover, the use of Instagram is also closely related to the concept of social proof, whereby reviews from
independent third parties are perceived as more credible by audiences than messages delivered directly by the brand itself.
This understanding is reflected in C-Klik Media's strategic awareness that the voices of bookstagram accounts carry greater
persuasive power than company statements.
The convergence of a soft-selling orientation across all observed collaborative content, without any brief instruction
explicitly requiring it, proves that the cultural norms of the bookstagram community on Instagram and C-Klik Media's
brand value are organically aligned. This condition creates an arena in which corporate interests and creator interests can
meet and mutually reinforce one another without requiring explicit control from the company. Rimadias et al. (2021) state
that companies need to utilise all brand communication channels, including social media, to create and sustain brand value,
and this finding demonstrates that C-Klik Media has succeeded in doing so through a collaboration model that relies on
trust and alignment of values. It remains equally plausible, however, that this convergence reflects a broader genre norm
within the bookstagram community rather than a brand-specific outcome. Since soft-selling language is widely
characteristic of book review content in general, regardless of publisher, the consistency observed here may indicate that
creators are following established community conventions rather than internalising C-Klik Media's brand value
specifically, a possibility that tempers the strength of the causal claim being made.
As argued by Andika and Sazali (2024), every interaction and conversation occurring on social media constitutes an
element in the construction of brand perception in the minds of audiences. In this context, the interaction between
bookstagram accounts and their audiences on Instagram represents a process of C-Klik Media brand image formation that
takes place organically and continuously. C-Klik Media's strategic maturity in understanding this dynamic is reflected in
its capacity to allow bookstagram accounts to review books in their own voices and styles, which produces content that is
more organic, more readily accepted by audiences, and more sustainable in shaping long-term brand value. Instagram is
thus not merely a content distribution channel, but an arena in which the brand identity of C-Klik Media is collectively
constructed together with its bookstagram partners through a continuously ongoing boundary-spanning process.
Taken as a whole, the findings discussed above should be read with appropriate caution. While the trust-based
collaboration model adopted by C-Klik Media appears effective within the scope of this study, its apparent success is bound
to the particular conditions of a small, purposively selected partner group and may not extend automatically to larger or
less curated collaboration networks. Several of the patterns identified, including the consistent presence of brand value

4125
elements and the uniform soft-selling tone, are also open to explanations rooted in bookstagram genre convention and
relational courtesy rather than in the specific effectiveness of C-Klik Media's communication strategy alone.
Acknowledging these limitations and alternative readings does not diminish the contribution of this study but rather situates
its conclusions more precisely within the boundaries of the data collected.
Conclusion
This study demonstrates that C-Klik Media's boundary-spanning process in integrating brand value into bookstagram
review content unfolds through five adaptive and interconnected stages, from strategic partner selection to performance-
based evaluation, with Instagram serving as the central arena in which this process is enacted. Rather than relying on formal
draft control, the collaboration functions primarily through a trust-based model, in which flexible product knowledge and
sustained two-way communication guide creators towards consistent brand representation without the need for strict
message control. This finding refines rather than contradicts the boundary spanning framework, showing that brand control
can operate effectively through implicit rather than explicit mechanisms, although this effectiveness remains conditional
on the scale and composition of the partner network rather than something that can be assumed permanent by design.
Theoretically, this study extends the application of boundary spanning beyond institutional public relations into the
domain of digital marketing collaboration between book publishers and social media content creators, showing that brand
value can be communicated through negotiated authenticity rather than through direct organisational control. Practically,
the findings suggest that C-Klik Media would benefit from a lightweight quality assurance mechanism, such as a brief
creator self-assessment checklist, to safeguard brand consistency as its network of collaborators expands, without
undermining the creative autonomy that currently underpins the effectiveness of its collaboration model. Future research
is recommended to incorporate audience perspectives in order to assess whether end consumers genuinely perceive the
authenticity claimed by creators and publishers, and to extend this inquiry to other platforms such as TikTok and YouTube
so as to produce more generalisable findings.
