 Intense competition among urban pharmacies requires independent
businesses to adopt strategies that integrate commercial sustainability with
professional pharmaceutical services. Sinar Berkat  Farma Pharmacy in
Bandung faces strong competitive pressures, limited digital promotion,
and underdeveloped delivery services. This study aimed to formulate
strategic management priorities for maintaining and strengthening the
pharmacy’s competitiveness. A qualitative descriptive case study was
conducted from October to December 2025. Data were collected through
observations, interviews, and document reviews and were subsequently
analyzed using the Internal Factor Evaluation (IFE) Matrix, External
Factor Evaluation (EFE) Matrix, Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities,
and Threats (SWOT) analysis, and the Quantitative Strategic Planning
Matrix (QSPM). The findings identified responsive staff,  pharmacist
availability, specialist physician services, a strategic location, and product
availability as key strengths. Strengths scored 2.52, weaknesses 0.71,
opportunities 1.13, and threats 2.48, positioning the pharmacy in SWOT
Quadrant II. This posit ion supported strategies that leverage internal
strengths to address external threats. Service diversification achieved a
Total Attractiveness Score (TAS) of 6.85, exceeding the score of 6.41 for
product diversification. These results suggest prioritizing pharmacist-led
education, differentiated services, and healthcare partnerships, supported
by digital marketing and the gradual development of delivery services.
The study concludes that service diversification offers the preferred
strategic direction; howe ver, its effects on customer retention,
profitability, and competitiveness require subsequent empirical evaluation
following implementation over a sustained observation period.

INTRODUCTION
Sinar Berkat Farma Pharmacy is a pharmacy established in March 2022 at Jalan
Margacinta No. 99, Mustika Hegar Regency, Margasari, Buah  Batu, Bandung City, a densely
populated area that is also strategically located near numerous other pharmacies. As a relatively
new entrant in the pharmaceutical retail industry, the pharmacy faces significant challenges in
maintaining its competitive position. Health is a fundamental human need and a major priority
for the community; therefore, the availability of easily accessible healthcare facilities is
essential (Akbar et al., 2022; Arif et al., 2021) . As pharmaceutical service facilities located
close to the community, pharmacies often serve as an initial point of access for obtaining
medicines and receiving basic health consultations. Regulation of the Minister of Health of the
Republic of Indonesia Number 9 of 2017 defines a pharmacy as a pharmaceutical service
facility where pharmacy practice is performed by pharmacists (Basya et al., 2020; David et al.,
2023). A pharmacy is required to have a pharmacist responsible for pharmaceutical services
and for ensuring compliance with applicable professional standards. The orientation of the

pharmacy business has also undergone a paradigm shift from being predominantly profit -
oriented to becoming increasingly patient -oriented, requiring patient safety and professional
ethics to be integrated with business sustainability (Nurfaizah & Hidayat, 2024; Pasaribu et al.,
2022; Ramli & Hamzah, 2021; Susanto et al., 2025).
Increasing public awareness of the importance of health has also contributed to the
substantial growth of the pharmacy business in Indonesia. This growth has been driven by
increasing demand for medicines, lifestyle changes, evolving health trends, greater  digital
access to health information, the increasing complexity of diseases, and the growing number
of pharmacy graduates each year. The implementation of the National Health Insurance
(Jaminan Kesehatan Nasional or JKN) program by the government has also expanded public
access to prescription and generic medicines, contributing to the proliferation of pharmacies
around public and private hospitals. Furthermore, the presence of telemedicine platforms such
as Halodoc, Alodokter, SehatQ, and GoApotik , along with the expansion of major
pharmaceutical retail networks into e -healthcare, has introduced a new dimension of
competition in the pharmacy industry. Consequently, conventional pharmaceutical services
must increasingly be integrated with the conven ience of online purchasing, personalized
consultations, and home delivery services.
These developments have directly contributed to increasingly intense competition
among pharmacies. Based on data from the Ministry of Health of the Republic of Indonesia,
more than 32,110 pharmacies operate throughout Indonesia, with West Java having the largest
number at 5,114 units, followed by East Java with 4,531 pharmacies and Central Java with
4,013 pharmacies. Five major pharmacy retail networks—Apotek Kimia Farma, Apotek K-24,
AS Watsons Group, Guardian, and Century Healthcare —collectively account for more than
10% of the total revenue of the national pharmacy market. Statistical data from the West Java
Provincial Health Office, published through the West Java Open Data portal, also indicate that
the number of pharmacies in West Java, particularly in Bandung City, has continued to increase
since the end of the COVID -19 pandemic. This phenomenon requires pharmacy managers to
adapt to market changes, pursue innovation, and e stablish clear business directions and
strategies to maintain their operations and competitiveness.
The intensity of this competition is clearly reflected in the high concentration of
pharmacies surrounding Sinar Berkat Farma Pharmacy. Within a radius of approximately 2
km, there are at least 17 competing pharmacies, with the nearest competitor, Mutiara Farma
Pharmacy, located only approximately 100 meters away; several other pharmacies are located
within 500 meters. Such geographical concentration increases competition for the same local
consumer market, requiring Sinar Berkat Farma Pharmacy to establish clear competitive
advantages in terms of service quality, product availability, pricing, and customer convenience
to maintain customer loyalty amid the availability of numerous competing pharmacies in the
surrounding area.
These challenges are also reflected in Sinar Berkat  Farma Pharmacy's sales
performance over the past three years. In 2023, the pharmacy was in its initial expansion phase,
during which the newly established business had not yet gained broad public recognition;
consequently, its sales volume remained relati vely low. The introduction of physician
consultation services at the pharmacy substantially increased customer visits, contributing to a

significant increase in sales from 2023 to 2024  (Rasyid, 2022; Rianto et al., 2025; Sugiyono.,
2022; Zulfi P, 2021) . Consistent growth throughout 2024 indicated that the business was
beginning to mature after its initial expansion phase. However, in 2025, sales began to

more than IDR 100,000,000 per month. This trend indicates that the organic growth achieved
during the first two years may not necessarily be sustainable without a planned, measurable,
and systematically implemented competitive strategy.
The high concentration of competitors surrounding the business location and
fluctuations in sales performance indicate that the growth achieved by Sinar Berkat  Farma
Pharmacy can no longer depend primarily on locational advantages or market momentum.
Instead, sustainable growth requires systematic management through the application of
strategic management principles. Strategic management is essential for establi shing a clear
organizational direction, identifying market opportunities, mapping the organization's internal
strengths and weaknesses, and anticipating opportunities and threats arising from the external
environment. Through the processes of strategy form ulation, implementation, evaluation, and
control, including the application of Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, and Threats
(SWOT) analysis as a strategic environmental assessment tool, Sinar Berkat Farma Pharmacy
is expected to develop appropriate competitive strategies to maintain and enhance its
competitiveness amid intense competition in the pharmacy industry in Bandung City.
Based on this background, this study aims to analyze the application of strategic
management at Sinar Berkat Farma Pharmacy, encompassing the formulation, implementation,
evaluation, and control of existing and proposed strategies, while identifying internal and
external factors that support or constrain the pharmacy's performance. The central research
problem concerns the management strategy most appropriate for maintaining and enhancing
the competitiveness of Sinar Berkat Farma Pharmacy in Bandung City  (Kiton & Wau, 2023;
Mulyana & Muttaqin, 2024; Murhadi, 2024) . This problem is further examined through the
formulation of competitive strategies, their implementation, and the evaluation and control
mechanisms employed by the pharmacy to sustain its competitiveness. Accordingly, this study
seeks to formulate competitive management strategies through systematic strategy formulation,
implementation, and evaluation based on SWOT analysis. Theoretically, this research is
expected to enrich the literature on strategic management within the pharmaceutical retail
industry and provide a reference for future research in the healthcare sector. Practically, the
findings are expected to provide recommendations for Sinar Berkat  Farma Pharmacy to
enhance its performance, address internal weaknesses, capitalize on market opportunities, and
strengthen its competitive position. In addition, the study may provide practical insights for
other pharmacies seeking to develop more effective strategic management practices and serve
as a reference for future researchers examining pharmacy management strategies and
competitiveness.

METHOD
Place and Time of Research
The study was conducted at Sinar Berkat Farma Pharmacy in Buahbatu  District,
Bandung City, West Java, from October to December 2025. The research site was selected

because the pharmacy operated in a highly competitive environment with numerous competing
pharmacies in the surrounding area. As a relatively new pharmacy, it faced challenges in
maintaining its market position and sustaining its business performance, maki ng it a relevant
setting for examining strategic management and competitiveness.
Research Design
This research uses a qualitative approach, which according to Sugiyono (2023) is used
to study objects in natural conditions with the researcher as the main instrument, data collection
through triangulation, and inductive analysis that emphasizes the meani ng of phenomena.
Creswell (2023) added that this approach allows for an in -depth understanding of a problem
through a systematic and reflective process of data collection and analysis.
This approach was chosen because the research aims to understand the survival strategy
of Apotek Sinar Berkat Farma in facing similar business competition, which is contextual and
dynamic so that it requires the extraction of information directly from rela ted parties. This
research is in the form of a descriptive study on one object, namely Apotek Sinar Berkat Farma.
The internal and external conditions of the pharmacy are studied from informant data and
documents, then analyzed through the identification of internal-external factors, the preparation
of IFAS and EFAS matrices, SWOT analysis, and the determination of strategy priorities with
QSPM.
Data Collection
Data were collected through triangulation techniques, namely:
1. Observation, on operational activities, pharmaceutical services, business environment
conditions, and competition situation around pharmacies.
2. Interviews, flexibly with owners, pharmacists, and staff to dig up in -depth information
regarding pharmacy management, operations, and survival strategies (Sugiyono, 2023).
3. Document Review, to obtain supporting data regarding the operational, sales, and financial
conditions of the pharmacy.
Data Analysis
Data analysis is carried out interactively until the data reaches saturation, including data
reduction, data presentation, and conclusion drawn. Primary data is obtained from interviews
and observations of owners, pharmacists, and consumers, while secondar y data is obtained
from sales data, pharmacy profiles, and literature related to strategic management and
pharmacy competitiveness (Sugiyono, 2023).
1. Data Reduction selects and groups data on internal factors (organizational structure, human
resources, finance, facilities, products and services) and external factors (geographical,
demographic, socioeconomic, epidemiological, technological, customer, sup plier,
competitor) relevant to the focus of the research.
2. Presentation Data The data that has been reduced is presented as Critical Success Factors
which are grouped into Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, and Threats, as the basis for
the preparation of the IFAS and EFAS Matrix.
3. Conclusion The SWOT factors are entered into the IFAS and EFAS Matrix, weighted and
ranked to determine the strategic position of the pharmacy on the SWOT chart, which is
the basis for determining alternative strategies. The final conclusion is obtained af ter the
results of the analysis are verified with field data.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
The mapping of competitive strategies in this study follows the SWOT analysis
approach, which is the systematic identification of internal and external factors to formulate
strategies that maximize strengths and opportunities, while minimizing weaknesses and threats.
Internal factors are arranged into an IFAS (Internal Factors Analysis Summary) matrix and
external factors into an EFAS (External Factors Analysis Summary) matrix; each factor is
given weights and ratings so that a weighted score is obtained as the basis for determining the
position and direction of the pharmacy strategy.

need for health consultation and the need for medicine. This condition can open up

Strategic locations obtained a score of 0.51. The position of pharmacies in areas with a

media activities that are still lacking, the lack of direct delivery services, and promotional

The weakness with the largest score is the lack of activity on social media, which is
0.24. This condition shows that the use of digital media as a means of communication and
marketing still needs to be strengthened. In the midst of changing people's habit s that are
increasingly easy to obtain information through the internet, low social media activity can
cause information about products, services, and the advantages of pharmacies to not reach
potential consumers optimally.
Operating hours that have not reached 24 hours get a score of 0.18. This condition can
be a limitation when consumers need drugs or health products outside of service hours.
Although extending service hours is not always the main choice because it is relat ed to costs
and resources, managers need to adjust operational hours to the pattern of community needs.
Promotions that have not been maximized have a score of 0.15. This shows that
activities to introduce products and services can still be developed. Promotions do not always
have to be in the form of discounts, but can be done through providing health infor mation,
service introductions, educational content, or certain programs that are able to increase
consumer attachment.
The status as an independent pharmacy obtained a score of 0.08. As a stand -alone
business, pharmacies have limitations compared to network companies in terms of procurement
scale, brand recognition, and marketing resources. However, these conditions can also provide
space for managers to make more flexible decisions according to local market conditions.
The unavailability of direct delivery services gets a score of 0.06. This condition can be
an obstacle when consumers want ease in obtaining products without having to come to the
pharmacy.

1. S-O Strategy
An S-O strategy is a strategy that leverages internal strengths to benefit from external
opportunities.
Based on the results of the research, the S-O strategies that can be applied are:
a. Maximizing strategic locations and product completeness to reach a wide target market.

b. Using friendly and communicative employee services as a support for the development of

c. Collaborating the existence of specialist doctors with service personnel to organize health

d. Utilizing the existence of specialist doctors as a bargaining chip in building cooperation

The strategy shows that pharmacies actually have a certain amount of internal capital
that can be developed. An easy -to-reach location can be strengthened through more active

customers.
2. W-O Strategy
The W-O strategy is geared towards reducing internal weaknesses by taking advantage
of available opportunities.
Alternative strategies obtained include:
a. Digitizing marketing to expand market reach.
b. Developing new services according to the needs of the community.
c. Increase service hours by considering consumer needs and operational capabilities.
d. Building institutional cooperation to overcome limitations as an independent pharmacy.
Of these alternatives, marketing digitalization is one of the most important steps. Social
media activities that were previously low can be improved through regular content
development. The information provided does not have to focus on sales, but can be in the form
of information about pharmacy services, drug use education, information on operating hours,
and product introductions.
3. S-T Strategy
The S-T strategy obtained the highest score so that it became an alternative that needed
to receive the main attention.
Strategies that can be carried out include:
a. Utilizing the presence of specialist doctors and communicative employee services to form
routine health service packages.
b. Optimize the role of pharmacists to provide direct education to patients about drug use and
abuse.
c. Taking advantage of strategic locations and product availability to provide health packages
at competitive prices while still paying attention to applicable regulations.
d. Strengthen cooperation with specialist doctors to build referral networks with health
facilities around pharmacies.
The S-T strategy shows that the strength of pharmacies can be used as a differentiator
when facing competition. Pharmacies do not have to deal with competitors only through price.
Service approaches, relationships with health workers, education, and ease o f access can be

QSPM Analysis Results
For the purpose of preparing the Quantitative Strategic Planning Matrix (QSPM),
several alternative strategies were selected that were representative of each strategy group
based on the results of the SWOT analysis. The selection of strategies was carried out by
considering the realistic level of implementation and its contribution to the development and
increase of the competitiveness of Pharmacy Sinar Berkat  Farma. In this study, the strategy
that became the focus of the assessment was the diversification strategy, which included
diversification of services and product diversification.
The preparation of the QSPM begins by integrating internal and external strategic
factors that have been obtained through the IFAS (Internal Factor Analysis Summary) and
EFAS (External Factor Analysis Summary) matrices. Each strategic factor that has a weight is
then assessed based on its level of attractiveness (Attractiveness Score / AS) to each alternative
strategy. The assessment shows how much a factor affects the success of the implementation
of the strategy under consideration.
The attractiveness score (AS) is given using a scale of 1 -4, with the following
conditions:
1. Value 1 = unattractive, i.e. factors that have no relevance or meaningful influence on the
strategy
2. Value 2 = rather interesting, i.e. factors have a relatively low influence on strategy
3. Value 3 = attractive, i.e. factors have a strong enough influence on the strategy
4. The value of 4 = is very interesting, i.e. the factor has a very strong influence and supports
the implementation of the strategy.
Next, the weight of each factor is multiplied by the attractiveness value (AS) to obtain
the Total Attractiveness Score (TAS). The TAS value of all factors is then added up to obtain
the total value of each alternative strategy. Strategies with higher TAS values indicate a greater
level of relative attractiveness, making them a more prioritized alternative to implement.
The QSPM table in this study is compiled based on internal and external strategic
factors that are considered most relevant to the development of Pharmacy Sinar Berkat Farma.
The assessment is focused on diversification strategies as an alternative to business
development, which consists of service diversification and product diversification. Service
diversification is directed at the development or addition of types of services that can increase
added value for customers, while product diversification is oriented towards expanding the
types and choices of products offered. The comparison of the TAS values of the two
alternatives is then used to determine the most attrac tive diversification strategy and in
accordance with the strategic conditions of Pharmacy Sinar Berkat Farma.

CONCLUSION
This study concludes that the strategic priorities of Sinar Berkat Farma Pharmacy should
focus on leveraging its internal strengths to address substantial external threats. The SWOT
analysis positioned the pharmacy in Quadrant II, supporting a Strength –Threat (ST) strategy
that capitalized on responsive and communicative  staff, pharmacist availability, specialist
physician services, a strategic location, and product availability. The Quantitative Strategic
Planning Matrix (QSPM) prioritized service diversification, with a Total Attractiveness Score

pharmacist-led education, stronger collaboration with healthcare providers, and differentiated
pharmaceutical services emerged as promising strategic directions for mai ntaining
competitiveness. Digital marketing and carefully planned delivery services could further
support these priorities by addressing existing operational weaknesses. Overall, these findings
provide a systematic basis for strategic decision -making; however, they do not establish that
implementing the proposed strategies will necessarily improve business performance.
Future research should evaluate the implementation and outcomes of the recommended
strategies through longitudinal studies assessing customer satisfaction, customer retention,
sales growth, profitability, and service quality. Comparative studies involving multiple
independent pharmacies could determine whether the strategic priorities identified in this case
are applicable across different locations and competitive environments. Future studies should
also incorporate broader stakeholder perspectives, includ ing those of customers, pharmacists,
pharmacy owners, and healthcare partners, to strengthen the assessment of internal and external
strategic factors. Sensitivity analyses of factor weights and attractiveness scores could further

decisions while ensuring compliance with pharmaceutical service standards and maintaining
