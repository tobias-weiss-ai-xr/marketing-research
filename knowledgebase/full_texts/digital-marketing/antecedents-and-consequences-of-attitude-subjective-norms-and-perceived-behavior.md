ABSTRACT: As ecotourism continues to evolve, understanding the psychological and behavioral factors underlying tourists’
decisions to revisit destinations has become increasingly relevant to sustainable destination management. This study examines the
antecedents of attitude, subjective norms, and perceived behavioral control, as well as their relationships with tourists’ revisit
intention. The study extends the Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) by incorporating ecotourism knowledge, pro-environmental
behavior, eudaimonia, curiosity, and digital marketing. Using a quantitative survey design, data were collected from 300 tourists
who had previously visited ecotourism destinations in North Minahasa Regency, Indonesia. Respondents were selected through
purposive sampling, and the proposed relationships were examined using Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) with AMOS. The
findings indicate that eudaimonia was positively associated with attitude, subjective norms, and perceived behavioral control.
Ecotourism knowledge positively influences subjective norms and perceived behavioral control, while pro-environmental behavior
positively influences subjective norms. Curiosity positively influences subjective norms and perceived behavioral control, whereas
digital marketing positively influences perceived behavioral control. Furthermore, attitude, subjective norm s, and perceived
behavioral control positively influence revisit intention, with perceived behavioral control demonstrating the strongest direct effect.
The findings suggest that revisit intention is shaped by meaningful travel experiences, environmental knowledge, social influence,
and perceived behavioral control. Theoretically, the study extends TPB in ecotourism by integrating cognitive, psychological,
environmental, and digital antecedents. Practically, destination managers and policymakers should foster meaningful experiences,
strengthen environmental education and visitor information, and optimize digital marketing strategies to enhance revisit intention
and support sustainable destination management.

I. INTRODUCTION
The tourism sector constitutes a strategic pillar of Indonesia’s national economy, leveraging the country’s cultural diversity,
creative industries, and abundant natural resources (Ministry of Tourism, 2025). In 2024, the sector accounted for approximately
4.04% of Indonesia’s Gross Domestic Product (GDP) and supported nearly 25.01 million jobs across the tourism and creative
economy sectors (Ministry of Tourism, 2025). Alongside its economic contribution, Indonesia’s tourism development agenda has
increasingly embraced wellness, nature-based tourism, ecotourism, and adventure tourism, with greater emphasis on ecological
balance, environmental stewardship, and long-term sustainability (Ministry of Tourism and Creative Economy, 2023). Within this
policy orientation, ecotourism has emerged as a strategic approach for harnessing Indonesia’s natural and cultural resources while
promoting responsible tourism development and local community participation.  Related research has also highlighted the
importance of green image, green trust, and green satisfaction in strengthening consumers’ longer -term relationships with
environmentally oriented offerings (K & Chrisjatmiko, 2018).
The resurgence of international tourism following the COVID-19 pandemic further underscores the sector’s expanding economic
significance. International tourist arrivals to Indonesia increased substantially, reaching 13.88 million in 2024 compared with 1.55
million in 2021 (BPS-Statistics Indonesia, 2024). Tourism-related foreign exchange earnings likewise increased from USD 0.52
billion in 2021 to USD 16.71 billion in 2024 (Ministry of Tourism, 2025). These developments demonstrate the increasingly
prominent role of tourism in national economic recovery, employment generation, and foreign exchange earnings. Nevertheless,
aggregate growth at the national level does not necessarily correspond to an equitable distribution of tourist demand across
destinations. Destinations endowed with substantial natural and cultural resources may continue to experience difficulties in
attracting visitors and, more importantly, encouraging them to return.

This challenge is particularly salient in North Sulawesi, where tourism development has been reinforced through the
establishment of the Likupang Special Economic Zone (KEK Likupang) in North Minahasa Regency. Likupang was designated as
one of Indonesia’s five national Super Priority Destinations, with the broader objective of enhancing destination competitiveness
and encouraging longer tourist stays (Rahafuna et al., 2023). In accordance with national tourism priorities, destination development
in the region has increasingly incorporated green tourism and community-based ecotourism (CBET) principles, emphasizing
environmentally responsible tourism experiences and community participation (Shasha et al., 2020). However, policy recognition,
ecological resources, and destination development initiatives do not necessarily guarantee sustained tourist demand or repeat
visitation.
The uneven distribution of visitors across destinations in North Minahasa provides a clear illustration of this challenge. In 2024,
North Minahasa Regency recorded 40,773 domestic and 20,216 international visitors. Yet, substantial differences were evident
across individual destinations. Lihaga Island attracted 4,200 domestic and 3,451 international tourists, while Bangka Island received
948 domestic and 3,038 international tourists. By comparison, Bahoi Eco-Village recorded only 181 domestic and 378 international
visitors during the same period (Minahasa Utara Regency Tourism Office, 2024). This disparity is particularly noteworthy given
that Bahoi represents a community-based ecotourism destination characterized by marine ecosystems, mangrove forests, and
community-led conservation initiatives. Its relatively low visitation, despite considerable ecological assets and its proximity to a
nationally prioritized tourism area, illustrates a broader managerial challenge in translating destination resources into sustained
visitation and repeat travel behavior.
Tourists’ revisit intention therefore warrants closer examination as a determinant of the long -term viability of ecotourism
destinations. Repeat visitors can contribute to more stable tourism revenues, strengthen destination loyalty, and facilitate positive
word-of-mouth recommendations (Manyangara et al., 2023). Revisit intention reflects tourists’ willingness to return to a destination
they have previously experienced (Zheng & Khong-khai, 2025). Within tourism and behavioral research, the Theory of Planned
Behavior (TPB) has been extensively employed to explain behavioral intentions through three central determinants: attitude,
subjective norms, and perceived behavioral control (Ajzen, 1991). Previous tourism research has also applied behavioral intention
frameworks to examine tourists’ intentions to revisit ecotourism destinations (Jin et al., 2020). However, the conventional TPB
framework may not fully accommodate the multidimensional influences that characterize decision-making in ecotourism settings.
Several considerations underscore the need to broaden the conventional TPB framework. First, ecotourism knowledge may shape
how tourists evaluate environmentally oriented destinations and formulate behavioral responses. Although existing research
indicates that ecotourism knowledge can foster favorable attitudes toward ecotourism (Huo et al., 2024), empirical evidence remains
inconsistent across destination contexts (Jayasekara & Rajapaksa, 2024). Second, pro-environmental behavior represents another
relevant dimension because tourists’ environmental orientations may influence their preferences for sustainable destinations and
tourism practices (Damijani et al., 2023). Nevertheless, the persistence of an intention–behavior gap suggests that environmental
concern does not invariably translate into corresponding behavioral outcomes, including repeat visitation (Snow et al., 2021). These
findings indicate that cognitive and environmental dimensions may complement the core TPB constructs in explaining tourists’
revisit intention within ecotourism contexts.
Beyond cognitive and environmental considerations, tourists’ decisions may also be shaped by intrinsic psychological
motivations and increasingly influential digital environments. Eudaimonia reflects a form of well-being associated with personal
growth, self-development, reflection, and the pursuit of meaning, dimensions that may resonate strongly with tourism experiences
centered on nature, authenticity, and personal enrichment (Medeiros et al., 2020). Related research also indicates that positive
experiences can strengthen perceived quality, trust, satisfaction, and longer-term loyalty toward an entity (Rahmat & Kurniawati,
2022). Curiosity may similarly encourage tourists to seek novelty, explore unfamiliar environments, and pursue experiences that
satisfy their desire for discovery  (Berlyne, 1960; Davari & Jang, 2024). At the same time, digital marketing has become an
increasingly influential component of tourism decision -making by facilitating access to destination information, shaping
expectations, and reducing information asymmetry prior to travel (Rodrigues et al., 2023). Although these dimensions have received
attention within tourism and behavioral research, their interconnected roles within an extended TPB framework remain insufficiently
examined, particularly in ecotourism destinations.
Against this backdrop, the present study extends the Theory of Planned Behavior by incorporating ecotourism knowledge, pro-
environmental behavior, eudaimonia, curiosity, and digital marketing into a comprehensive framework of tourists’ revisit intention.
Specifically, the study investigates how these cognitive, environmental, psychological, and digital factors influence attitud e,
subjective norms, and perceived behavioral control, and how these three TPB constructs subsequently shape revisit intention. By
examining these relationships among tourists who have previously visited ecotourism destinations in North Minahasa Regency,
Indonesia, this study seeks to broaden the application of TPB within tourism marketing by integrating multidimensional antecedents
of revisit behavior. The study also generates empirical insights for destination management organizations and policymakers in
designing strategies that strengthen visitor engagement, encourage repeat visitation, and support the sustainable development of
ecotourism destinations.

II. RESEARCH  METHOD
This study employed a quantitative, cross-sectional research design to examine the hypothesized relationships. The study
examined the structural relationships among Ecotourism Knowledge, Pro-Environmental Behavior, Eudaimonia, Curiosity, Digital
Marketing, Attitude, Subjective Norms, Perceived Behavioral Control, and Revisit Intention in ecotourism destinations in North
Minahasa Regency, Indonesia. Bahoi Eco-Village was considered a representative ecotourism destination within the study context.
A cross-sectional design was adopted, with primary data collected at a single point in time through an online questionnaire
distributed via Google Forms through digital media and communication networks. Primary data were used to capture respondents’
experiences, perceptions, and evaluations of ecotourism destinations based on their previous visits.
The target population comprised tourists who had previously visited ecotourism destinations in North Minahasa Regency,
Indonesia. As the exact population size was unknown, a non -probability sampling approach using purposive sampling was
employed. Screening questions were placed at the beginning of the questionnaire to ensure that respondents met the eligibility
criteria. Respondents were required to have previously visited an ecotourism destination in North Minahasa Regency; those who
had not visited an eligible destination were excluded from further participation. The unit of analysis was the individual tourist.
The sample size was determined based on Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) guidelines, which suggest a minimum of five to
ten respondents for each indicator (Hair et al., 2019). With 34 indicators in the research instrument, the recommended sample size
ranged from 170 to 340 respondents. The study obtained 300 valid responses, exceeding the minimum recommended sample size
for the analysis. The structural model was analyzed using Structural Equation Modeling with AMOS (SEM-AMOS).
The measurement items were adapted from relevant scientific literature and contextualized to the ecotourism destination context
in North Minahasa Regency. The study measured nine constructs: Ecotourism Knowledge, Pro -Environmental Behavior,
Eudaimonia, Curiosity, Digital Marketing, Attitude, Subjective Norms, Perceived Behavioral Control, and Revisit Intention. All
measurement items were assessed using a five-point Likert scale ranging from 1 = Strongly Disagree to 5 = Strongly Agree.

IV. DISCUSSION
1. Ecotourism Knowledge on Attitude
The results indicate that ecotourism knowledge does not have a significant effect on attitude (β = 0.050; p = 0.497). This finding
suggests that possessing factual knowledge about ecotourism is not necessarily sufficient to form favorable evaluations of an
ecotourism destination. Tourists may understand environmental principles and conservation practices without necessarily translating
such knowledge into positive destination evaluations. This result differs from findings reported by Wang et al. (2024) and Huo et
al. (2024), which indicate a positive relationship between environmental or ecotourism knowledge and favorable attitudes. The
present finding may reflect a knowledge–attitude gap, whereby cognitive understanding alone does not necessarily correspond to
tourists' affective evaluations of a destination. Other experiential or psychological factors may therefore be required to strengthen
the formation of favorable attitudes.

2. Ecotourism Knowledge on Subjective Norms
Ecotourism knowledge has a positive and significant effect on subjective norms (β = 0.150; p = 0.021). This finding suggests that
tourists with greater knowledge of ecotourism may be more attentive to social expectations concerning environmentally responsible
tourism. The result is consistent with Wang et al. (2024) and Fu & Zhao (2024), who emphasize the relationship between
environmental awareness, social expectations, and normative considerations. Ecotourism knowledge may therefore provide a
cognitive basis through which tourists recognize and respond to socially expected environmentally responsible behavior.
3. Ecotourism Knowledge on Perceived Behavioral Control
Ecotourism knowledge has a positive and significant effect on perceived behavioral control (β = 0.259; p = 0.001). This result
indicates that greater knowledge of ecotourism is associated with higher perceived confidence in tourists’ ability to engage with
ecotourism destinations. The finding supports Lee & Jan (2017) and Huo et al. (2024), who highlight the role of environmental
knowledge in strengthening individuals’ perceived capacity to undertake environmentally related actions. In the present context,
knowledge may help tourists understand destination characteristics, conservation practices, and available activities, thereby reducing
uncertainty and enhancing their perception of behavioral control.
4. Pro-Environmental Behavior on Attitude
Pro-environmental behavior does not have a significant effect on attitude (β = 0.014; p = 0.361). This finding indicates that
tourists’ existing environmentally responsible practices do not necessarily correspond to more favorable evaluations of ecotourism
destinations. Individuals may engage in environmentally responsible activities in their daily lives without these behaviors directly
determining how they evaluate a particular tourism destination. The result differs from Kinyua et al. (2024) and Cayolla et al. (2023),
who reported relationships between pro-environmental orientations and tourism-related attitudes. The finding may indicate that
destination-specific evaluations are influenced by factors beyond tourists’ general environmental behavior.
5. Pro-Environmental Behavior on Subjective Norms
Pro-environmental behavior has a positive and significant effect on subjective norms (β = 0.162; p = 0.006). This result suggests
that tourists who demonstrate environmentally responsible behavior may be more receptive to social expectations concerning
sustainable tourism practices. The finding is consistent with Batool et al. (2023) and Shah & Yang (2023), who highlight the
relationship between environmentally responsible behavior and normative influences. Tourists with stronger pro-environmental
behavioral orientations may be more attentive to the expectations of others and to socially accepted standards of environmental
responsibility.
 6. Pro-Environmental Behavior on Perceived Behavioral Control
Pro-environmental behavior does not have a significant effect on perceived behavioral control (β = 0.043; p = 0.268). This finding
suggests that engaging in environmentally responsible practices in everyday life does not necessarily increase tourists’ perceptions
of their ability to undertake a particular ecotourism visit. This result differs from Batool et al. (2023) and Liu et al. (2024). One
possible explanation is that perceived behavioral control in an ecotourism context may depend more strongly on destination-specific
conditions, such as accessibility, facilities, transportation, cost, and available infrastructure, rather than on touris ts’ previous
environmental practices.
 7.  Eudaimonia on Attitude
Eudaimonia has a positive and significant effect on attitude (β = 0.975; p < 0.001). Among the antecedents examined, this
relationship represents the strongest association with attitude. The finding suggests that tourists’ pursuit of personal grow th,
meaning, self-development, and psychological well-being is strongly associated with favorable evaluations of ecotourism
destinations. This result is consistent with Nelson-Coffey & Schmitt (2023) and Linden (2024), who emphasize the relevance of
eudaimonia to meaningful experiences and personal development. Related evidence from Kurniawati and Galingging (2024) also
highlights the role of ethical behavior, trust, and brand experience in shaping consumers’ responses to an entity. Although their
study was conducted in the sports brand context, it reinforces the broader importance of credible and meaningful interactions in
shaping consumer experiences. In the context of ecotourism, destinations that provide opportunities for reflection, connection with
nature, and personally meaningful experiences may therefore be particularly appealing to tourists who value eudaimonia.
8. Eudaimonia on Subjective Norms
Eudaimonia has a positive and significant effect on subjective norms (β = 0.181; p = 0.003). This finding indicates that tourists
with stronger eudaimonia orientations may also be more responsive to social expectations and shared values associated with
responsible tourism. The result is consistent with Linden (2024) and Margolis et al. (2022), suggesting that the pursuit of meaningful
and fulfilling lives may be connected with broader social and environmental values. Thus, personal well-being may coexist with
greater sensitivity to socially valued forms of environmentally responsible tourism.
9. Eudaimonia on Perceived Behavioral Control
Eudaimonia has a positive and significant effect on perceived behavioral control (β = 0.215; p = 0.001). The result suggests that
tourists who place greater value on personal meaning and psychological development may perceive themselves as more capable of
undertaking ecotourism-related activities. This finding is consistent with Nelson-Coffey & Schmitt (2023) and Linden (2024),  who

associated with greater confidence in tourists’ capacity to engage in ecotourism experiences.
10. Curiosity on Attitude
Curiosity does not have a significant effect on attitude (β = 0.076; p = 0.059). Although curiosity may encourage tourists to
explore unfamiliar destinations and seek novel experiences, such motivation does not necessarily result in a favorable evaluation of
an ecotourism destination. The finding differs from Murray & Nadelhoffer (2024) and Åström et al. (2025), which emphasize the
role of curiosity in exploration and experiential engagement. The present result may suggest that curiosity initiates information
seeking or exploration, while a favorable attitude depends on whether the destination experience subsequently satisfies tourists’
expectations.
11. Curiosity on Subjective Norms
Curiosity has a positive and significant effect on subjective norms (β = 0.318; p < 0.001). This is the strongest antecedent of
subjective norms in the proposed model. The finding suggests that more curious tourists may be more attentive to the opinions,
recommendations, and expectations of people around them when considering ecotourism experiences. The result is consistent with
Sinae Lee (2023) and Anim et al. (2023), who highlight the relationship between information seeking, social influence, and
behavioral decision-making. One possible explanation is that curious individuals tend to explore diverse sources of information and
consequently become more exposed to social opinions and normative expectations. In the context of ecotourism, this heightened
attention to social information may strengthen tourists’ perceptions of what important others expect them to do.
12. Curiosity on Perceived Behavioral Control
Curiosity has a positive and significant effect on perceived behavioral control (β = 0.201; p = 0.008). This finding indicates that
curiosity is associated with greater perceived capacity to manage unfamiliar tourism experiences. Curious tourists may be more
inclined to seek information about destinations, activities, facilities, and potential challenges, which may help them feel m ore
prepared to undertake an ecotourism visit. The finding is consistent with Prasad & Hommel (2024) and Arneklev (2023), which
emphasize information seeking and exploratory tendencies as mechanisms related to perceived competence and control.
13. Digital Marketing on Attitude
Digital marketing does not have a significant effect on attitude (β = −0.018; p = 0.340). The finding indicates that exposure to
digital tourism information or promotional content does not necessarily translate into favorable  attitudes toward ecotourism
destinations. Promotional content may be perceived primarily as commercial communication and may therefore have limited
influence on destination evaluation in the absence of authentic or personally relevant experiences. This result differs from
Kurniawati and Siregar (2019), who found that social media marketing activities positively influenced self -expressive brand
dimensions and brand love, suggesting that digital marketing can contribute to consumers’ psychological connections with an entity.
In the present study, however, such effects may not automatically extend to tourists’ destination attitudes, particularly when digital
exposure is not accompanied by personally meaningful experiences. This result differs from  Rüterbories et al., (2024) and El
Gharbaoui (2023), which report positive roles of digital marketing and online communication in shaping tourism-related evaluations.
14. Digital Marketing on Subjective Norms
Digital marketing does not have a significant effect on subjective norms (β = 0.089; p = 0.090). This result suggests that digital
promotional communication alone may be insufficient to generate the social pressure or normative influence represented by
subjective norms. Although digital platforms provide access to destination information, normative influence may depend more
strongly on interpersonal recommendations, peer opinions, and social interactions. The finding differs from Hammad et al. (2022)
and Antre & Sanjay (2024), who identify relationships between digital communication and social influence in tourism-related
decision-making.
15. Digital Marketing on Perceived Behavioral Control
Digital marketing has a positive and significant effect on perceived behavioral control (β = 0.133; p = 0.034). This finding
suggests that accessible and useful digital information may enhance tourists’ perceptions of their ability to plan and undertake an
ecotourism visit. Information regarding itineraries, prices, facilities, trans portation, and destination conditions may reduce
uncertainty and make the visit appear more manageable. The result is consistent with Purnama et al. (2024) and Miah (2024),
supporting the role of digital information in facilitating tourists’ perceived ability to organize tourism activities.
16. Attitude on Revisit Intention
Attitude has a positive and significant effect on revisit intention (β = 0.221; p < 0.001). This finding supports the central
proposition of the TPB that favorable evaluations of a behavior are associated with stronger behavioral intentions (Ajzen, 1991). In
the present context, tourists who evaluate their ecotourism experience more favorably are more likely to express an intention to
revisit. This result is also consistent with Kurniawati and Sari (2020), who found that attitude positively influences behavioral
intention in the context of mobile banking adoption. Although their study examined technology adoption rather than tourism, the
finding provides broader empirical support for the role of attitude as a determinant of behavioral intention. In the ecotourism context,
a favorable evaluation of a previously experienced destination may therefore contribute to tourists’ willingness to return. The finding

17. Subjective Norms on Revisit Intention
Subjective norms have a positive and significant effect on revisit intention (β = 0.293; p < 0.001). This result indicates that
tourists’ perceptions of social approval and expectations are associated with their intention to revisit ecotourism destinations. The
finding is consistent with Liang & Nguyen (2024) and Liao et al. (2023), suggesting that social validation and recommendations
can contribute to repeat visitation intentions. In the ecotourism context, tourists may therefore be more inclined to revisit when
returning is perceived as socially supported or positively regarded by relevant others.
18. Perceived Behavioral Control on Revisit Intention
Perceived behavioral control has a positive and significant effect on revisit intention (β = 0.339; p < 0.001). Among the three
core TPB constructs, perceived behavioral control represents the strongest direct predictor of revisit intention in this study. The
finding is consistent with C. Ko (2023) and Hossain et al. (2024), indicating that tourists who perceive a repeat visit as feasible,
accessible, and within their control are more likely to express an intention to return. This result reinforces the relevance of perceived
behavioral control in explaining repeat visitation and suggests that practical conditions surrounding the destination may play an
important role in shaping tourists’ revisit intentions.

V. CONCLUSIONS
This study examined an extended Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) model by integrating Ecotourism Knowledge, Pro-
Environmental Behavior, Eudaimonia, Curiosity, and Digital Marketing to explain tourists’ Revisit Intention in ecotourism
destinations. The findings confirm that Attitude, Subjective Norms, and Perceived Behavioral Control significantly influence Revisit
Intention, with Perceived Behavioral Control demonstrating the strongest effect. Among the extended variables, Eudaimonia
emerged as the strongest predictor of Attitude, while Curiosity had the strongest influence on Subjective Norms and Ecotourism
Knowledge had the strongest influence on Perceived Behavioral Control. Ecotourism Knowledge also positively influenced
Subjective Norms, Pro-Environmental Behavior positively influenced Subjective Norms, Curiosity positively influenced Perceived
Behavioral Control, and Digital Marketing positively influenced Perceived Behavioral Control. These findings indicate that tourists’
revisit intentions are shaped by a combination of psychological, cognitive, environmental, social, and digital factors operat ing
through the core TPB constructs. Overall, the model demonstrated substantial explanatory power, explaining 75.8% of the variance
in Revisit Intention.
The theoretical contribution of this study lies in extending the TPB framework by incorporating positive psychological, cognitive,
environmental, and digital dimensions within the ecotourism context. The findings indicate that revisit intention is not shaped solely
by conventional behavioral determinants, but is also associated with meaningful psychological experiences, ecotourism knowledge,
environmental behavior, curiosity, and digital information environments through their relationships with Attitude, Subjective
Norms, and Perceived Behavioral Control. This extension provides a broader perspective for understanding repeat visitation in
ecotourism destinations.
From a managerial perspective, destination managers should strengthen strategies that enhance visitors’ perceived behavioral
control by providing accessible destination information, clear travel guidance, and practical digital content that facilitate s trip
planning. Destination managers should also develop meaningful and self-enriching ecotourism experiences to strengthen favorable
attitudes, while incorporating environmental education and conservation-oriented activities to reinforce visitors’ environmental
understanding and social expectations. Digital marketing should be developed not merely as a promotional medium but as an
informative and functional channel that helps tourists obtain relevant destination information and facilitates their decision to revisit.
Policymakers can support these efforts by improving destination accessibility, visitor information systems, and enviro nmental
management practices to create conditions that encourage sustainable repeat visitation.
