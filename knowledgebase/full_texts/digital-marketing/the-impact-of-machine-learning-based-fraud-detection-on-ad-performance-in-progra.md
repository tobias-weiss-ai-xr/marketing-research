Abstract
Owing to the rapid development of programmatic advertising, the number of fraudulent activities has increased, thereby
reducing the performance of digital marketing campaigns. Machine learning (ML)-based fraud detection systems have
been identified as a solution to such fraud and to enhance advertising performance. This study examines the effects of ML-
based fraud detection on the most important ad performance parameters, real-time bidding (RTB) efficiency, and consumer
trust. This study utilizes information obtained from 780 marketing professionals to investigate the direct and indirect
impacts of fraud detection systems on ad performance. The findings indicate that ML-based fraud detection positively
impacts ad performance, such as click-through rates (CTR) and return on ad spend (ROAS), by improving RTB efficiency
and decreasing fraud-induced distortions. Nonetheless, consumer trust, although positively influenced by fraud detection,
has a less positive correlation with advertisement performance than anticipated. This study also analyzes how data privacy
regulations moderate the relationship and increase the efficiency of RTB and consumer trust. The results can be useful to
practitioners in the digital advertising field, as they show that it is crucial to combine fraud detection systems and follow
the rules of data privacy to make ads more effective. This research adds to the theoretical background of the role of fraud
detection in programmatic advertising and provides viable suggestions on how digital advertising results can be enhanced.

nent of advertising, advertisers are experiencing new chal -

2 Literature review
2.1 Theoretical underpinning
The Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) created by Davis
(1989) can be adequate to describe the relationship between
fraud detection and real-time bidding efficiency, consumer
trust, and ad performance measurements. The TAM believes
that the major factors that influence technology adoption are
perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use. Applied to
the online advertisement industry, machine learning-based
fraud detection schemes are deemed beneficial by advertis -
ers to improve ad performance and reduce fraud. The utility
of these systems, which involves higher efficiency in real-
time bidding and accurate performance indicators, directly
relates to the adoption of such technologies in advertising
platforms (Venkatesh & Bala, 2008).
To advertisers, when machine learning algorithms are
applied in fraud detection systems, it helps the real-time bid-
ding (RTB) to be more efficient because only legitimate bids
are met, leading to better and more efficient ad targeting.
According to TAM, the greater the persuasion that machine
learning systems are useful and effective, the greater the
likelihood that advertisers will adopt them, which improves
the overall effectiveness of their RTB processes and boosts
the performance metrics of advertisements, including the
click-through rate (CTR), conversion rates and the return on
ad spend (ROAS) (Venkatesh & Bala, 2008).

2.2 Machine learning-based fraud detection and Ad
performance metrics
Recently, machine learning-driven fraud detection has
proven to be a vital element in guaranteeing the effective -
ness and integrity of online advertising, especially in pro -
grammatic advertising contexts (Akash et al., 2026). Fraud
detection systems with incorporated machine learning algo-
rithms are used to detect fraudulent operations, including
ad stacking, click fraud, and impression fraud, which may
have a devastating impact on ad performance metrics. These
fraudulent practices lead to misleading performance indica-
tors, thus hindering advertisers’ ability to accurately assess
the effectiveness of their campaigns (Verma et al., 2025).
Through machine learning models, marketers can obtain
improved results in fraud detection, which further has the
potential to increase important ad performance metrics such
as click-through rates (CTR), conversion rates, and return
on ad spend (ROAS) (Korbut, 2025). Anomaly detection
and predictive models represent machine learning algo -
rithms capable of processing large amounts of data in real
time and detecting odd patterns that might be used to com -
mit fraud (Choudhary et al., 2025). This will enable a better
measurement of campaign effectiveness and will also make
ensure that the metrics show the actual user interaction with
advertisements, as opposed to fabricated data due to fraudu-
lent practices.
H1: Ad performance measures are positively affected by
the implementation of machine learning-based fraud detec-
tion systems because of the reduction in the effects of fraud-
ulent practices in programmatic advertising.
2.3 Machine learning-based fraud detection, real-
time bidding efficiency, and consumer trust
Fraud detection systems powered by machine learning are
essential for improving the effectiveness of real-time bid -
ding (RTB) in programmatic advertising. RTB is the process
in which ads are purchased and sold in real time in real-time
auctions, which are automated, and the performance of such
systems is usually undermined by the presence of fraudulent
activities. Fraud cases, such as click or impression fraud,
cause poor bidding decisions, decreasing the overall effec -
tiveness and efficiency of RTB (Alzahrani et al., 2025).
Machine learning models can address these issues by rec -
ognizing fraud and preventing it as it happens, allowing
them to process only legitimate bids. As machine learning
algorithms are included in the process of fraud detection,
RTB systems will identify suspicious activity trends, such
as abnormalities in bidding behavior, quickly and eliminate
fraudsters in the auction process (Sağlam & Kirçova, 2025).
H2: The combination of machine-learning-based fraud-
detection systems enhances the effectiveness of real-time
bidding (RTB) by reducing fraudulent activity , leading to
more precise bidding.
Machine learning models can fix these issues because
they can recognize fraud and prevent it as it happens, to pro-
cess only legitimate bids. As machine learning algorithms
are included in the process of fraud detection, RTB systems
will identify suspicious activity trends, such as abnormali -
ties in bidding behavior, quickly and eliminate fraudsters
in the auction process (Sağlam & Kirçova, 2025). With the
use of machine learning in fraud detection, the process of
advertising becomes transparent. Machine learning models
(e.g., anomaly detection and predictive analytics) can iden -
tify fraud in real time and provide marketers with correct
data that reflects the real consumer behavior (Verma et al.,
2025). When consumers believe that the advertising space
is free of fraudulent activities, they will trust the platform
more, and more favorable consumer attitudes and behaviors
towards advertisements will emerge.
H3: The effectiveness of machine learning-based fraud
detection has a significant positive relationship with con -
sumer trust in digital advertising platforms.

ing ad performance. Efficient RTB processes allow adver -
tisers to target the right audience in real time, optimizing
the chances of engagement and conversion (Choudhary et
al., 2025). Through advanced algorithms and real-time data,
RTB systems can bid more efficiently on ad impressions,

2.5 Mediation role of real-time bidding efficiency
and consumer trust
The mediating variable between machine learning-based
fraud detection and ad performance measures is the real-
time bidding (RTB) efficiency. As frauds are detected more
efficiently with the help of more developed systems, RTB
systems will have an opportunity to distribute resources
more efficiently and target the right audience (Ismaliyanto
et al., 2026). This results in a more efficient bidding pro -
cess, which directly affects important ad performance mea -
sures, such as click-through rates (CTR) and return on ad
spend (ROAS). A successful RTB can be attained by having
a successful fraud detection to make sure that the budgets
are spent on valuable impressions and therefore enhancing
the overall performance of the campaign (Choudhary et al.,
2025). The impact of machine learning-based fraud detec -
tion can be boosted by RTB efficiency, which converts non-
fraudulent data into quantifiable ad performance (Verma et
al., 2025).
H6: The efficiency of real-time bidding is a mediator
between machine-learning-based fraud detection and ad
performance measures, which improves the effectiveness of
fraud detection in campaign success.
Consumer trust can also be used as an intermediary for
machine learning-based fraud detection and ad performance
measures by amplifying consumer engagement. When con-
sumers are confident that a platform is performing well
in fraud detection, they are more inclined to engage with
ads, leading to an increase in CTR and conversion rates.
Trust minimizes the risks perceived due to the possibility
of fraud, and consumers make ads more relevant and inter -
esting (Sağlam & Kirçova, 2025). This credibility impacts
key performance indicators as it initiates positive behavioral
outcomes such as clicks and purchases that are significant
signals of ad success (Sadeh, 2026).
H7: Consumer trust mediates the relationship between
machine learning-based fraud detection and advertisement
performance measures, increasing engagement and conver-
sion rates.
2.6 Moderation effects of data privacy regulations
The relationship between machine learning-based fraud
detection and real-time bidding (RTB) efficiency also has
a moderating effect on data privacy regulations. More strin-
gent data privacy laws may necessitate greater transparency
and data protection, which can influence the performance of
fraud detection systems (Lami et al., 2026). These regula -
tions enforce stricter rules and, therefore, make fraud detec-
tion systems operate within more established limits, thereby
guaranteeing greater alignment with RTB processes. Con -
sequently, RTB systems operate more accurately because
fraud detection algorithms must be based on clean, well-
managed data (Shafa & Sultana, 2024). These regulations
are used to ensure that the data processing of advertisers is
in line with privacy requirements, resulting in more favor -
able conditions in bidding. This enables it to focus on more
and reduce the risks of utilizing fraudulent data in RTB,
which ultimately results in an improved metric of the ad
performances (Dinev & Hart, 2006).
H8: Data privacy laws widen the association between
machine learning-based fraud detection and real-time bid
efficiency, enhancing the favorable effects of fraud detec -
tion on bidding results.
Data privacy regulations also moderate the relationship
between machine learning-based fraud detection and con -
sumer trust. Regulations that impose sound data protec -
tion can boost consumer confidence in systems that rely
on machine learning to detect fraud (Wen & Xin, 2026).
Customers are more willing to interact with advertisements
when they believe that their personal information is used
responsibly and safely (Park, 2025). These laws serve as
security measures that enhance customer trust in the effec -
tiveness of fraud detection systems. This makes consumers
more willing to trust a service that applies machine learn -
ing to identify fraud and guarantee data privacy, resulting
in increased engagement and better advertisement perfor -
mance metrics (Aljunaid et al., 2025).
H9: Data privacy regulations mediate the relation -

3 Methodology
3.1 Research design and approach
This research design is cross-sectional in nature and will
enable the researcher to obtain data at one point in time from
a group of diverse respondents. Marketing research often
utilizes cross-sectional studies to provide a picture of the
present trends, behaviors, and attitudes of various popula -
tion segments (Bryman & Bell, 2015). The method is espe-
cially effective in comprehending the effects of machine
learning-powered fraud detection on ad performance met -
rics in programmatic advertising because such an approach
allows researchers to collect data from a very diverse range
of respondents in real time, which captures the current situ-
ation in the digital advertising community.
A total of 1500 questionnaires were distributed to mar -
keting professionals. Among these 950 were returned, giv -
ing a response rate of 64%. Screening of data showed that
there were 780 responses that could be used to analyze. This
is larger than the minimum size needed to run a structural
equation modeling (SEM) hence providing strong statisti -
cal power and valid findings (Rasoolimanesh & Ali, 2018).
The researchers used 780 respondents, who were chosen
both online and face-to-face. This study had a wide sample
that was representative of the two approaches. The online
approach has broader geographic coverage because it can
capture the answers of people who may be inaccessible
face-to-face. The face-to-face approach ensures that indi -
viduals who have limited access to the Internet or prefer
a more face-to-face approach are not left out and that the
demographic is more representative (Fowler, 2013).
ADANCO and SPSS software were used for the analy -
sis. ADANCO is an effective structural equation modeling
(SEM) tool for evaluating complex relationships among
variables (Henseler et al., 2015). The hypotheses of the
study can be tested with these tools, and the relationships
between machine learning-based fraud detection and real-
time bidding efficiency, consumer trust, and ad performance
metrics can be examined.
3.2 Demographics
780 respondents had a wide range of demographics. Age-
wise, the largest proportion of respondents fell within the
age groups 36–44 (22.6%), 46–55 (20.4%), and 18–25
(19.9%), while groups 25–34 (19.4%) and 35–45 (17.3%)
also represented significant portions of the sample. In terms
of gender, the distribution was quite balanced, with 33.8%
identifying as male, 33.1% as female, and 33.0% as other.
As far as education is concerned, the majority of the respon-
dents are educated, with most of them having secondary or
higher education, with 26.4% having secondary education,
23.9% having some college education, and 23.4% having a
bachelor’s degree. The remaining 16.3% have other forms
of education, including postgraduate education. In terms of
professional experience, respondents had an average experi-
ence of 10.4 years, with the majority (nearly 60%), experi -
encing between 6 and 15 years of experience, and a small
percentage (around 10%) having less experience (under 6
years of experience). The diverse demographic composi -
tion of respondents is a strength of the study, as it enables a
subtle analysis of the different groups.
3.3 Measures
The items included in the scale of this study were modified
based on different sources to gauge important constructs
in machine learning-based fraud detection in program -
matic advertising. In the case of the Machine Learning-
Based Fraud Detection (ML-FD) construct, the items were
modified to fit the scale, as developed by Davis ( 1989) and
Venkatesh and Bala (2008) to assess Perceived Usefulness
(PU) and Perceived Ease of Use (PEU). The measure of the
Real-Time Bidding Efficiency (RTB) construct was based
on items modified based on Alzahrani et al. (2025). The Ad
Performance Metrics (APM) construct was modified based
on Choudhary et al. ( 2025). In the case of the Consumer
Trust (CT) construct, scale items were modified by Gefen
et al. (2003) and McKnight et al. (2002). Data Privacy Reg-
ulations (DPR) construct was operationalized by items of
Dinev and Hart (2006) and Malhotra et al. (2004). All items
were selected attentively to be relevant to the constructs
being studied and to represent the effects of machine learn -
ing on fraud detection, as well as ad performance metrics, in
the framework of programmatic advertising.
All constructs were rated on a 5-point Likert scale
(1 = Strongly Disagree to 5 = Strongly Agree). A pilot test
was also conducted on a small sample of 30 respondents to
determine clarity, understanding, and internal consistency
to determine the validity and reliability of the research. The
adapted items were tested to ensure reliability (Alpha > 7),
and the data collection phase was conducted.

The findings indicate good discriminant validity, with no

very collinear and can be analyzed further without worrying
that the standard errors will be inflated by multicollinearity.

4 Findings and discussion
The results of the analysis indicate important information
about the connection between machine learning-based fraud
detection, real-time bidding (RTB), consumer trust, and ad
performance metrics. The findings provide empirical evi -
dence that justifies the incorporation of machine learning
in improving RTB effectiveness, advert performance, and
consumer confidence in programmatic advertising contexts
(Tables 4 and 5). The findings will lead to the enhancement
of research on using advanced fraud detection technologies
to streamline digital advertising campaigns and provide
practical information on what can be done by global and
domestic advertisers.
The application of machine learning-based fraud detec -
tion (ML-FD) has a positive impact on ad performance met-
rics (APM) with a beta of 0.298 and a p-value of 0.004, which
supports H1. This is consistent with the current literature,
which indicates that ML-based fraud detection systems can
enhance key performance indicators (KPIs), such as click-
through rates (CTR) and conversion rates, by minimizing
fraudulent interactions that skew these metrics (Verma et
al., 2025). Likewise, for H2, ML-FD has a positive impact
on RTB efficiency (beta = 0.453, p-value = 0.001), which
proves that machine learning increases the accuracy of the
real-time auction by filtering out bids with fraudulent inten-
tions. This finding supports Alzahrani et al. ( 2025) work,
which highlighted the importance of fraud prevention in
optimizing the efficiency of RTB processes.
Interestingly, the correlation between ML-FD and con -
sumer trust is the opposite (beta = − 0.185, p-value = 0.016),
which indicates that fraud detection is not always associ -
ated with an increase in trust, although it increases metrics.
This is contrary to other studies that claim fraud detection
enhances consumer confidence (Choudhary et al., 2025),
but it might be due to market-specific or sample-based
exceptions. Moreover, the fact that RTB has a positive influ-
ence on APM (beta = 0.348, p-value = 0.007) supports the
idea that enhancing RTB performance has a direct positive
impact on ad performance (H4). This finding is consistent
with Choudhary et al. (2025), who explained the importance
of optimizing RTB in improving ad targeting and resulting
in improved engagement and performance.
Finally, consumer trust (CT) and APM negatively impact
each other (beta = − 0.250, p-value = 0.008), indicating that
higher levels of consumer trust do not necessarily result in
better ad performance. This could be due to factors such as
ad fatigue or overexposure to ads, an aspect less emphasized
in previous research (Sadeh, 2026). Overall, most hypoth -
eses are consistent with the current literature. Some, such as
the negative influence of consumer trust on advertisement
performance, offer more detailed feedback that needs to be
examined in more detail in future research.

The results of this research fill a major knowledge gap
in the existing body of literature by providing empirical
evidence of the effectiveness of machine learning-based
fraud detection tools in programmatic advertising on ad
performance indicators. Although past studies have already
recognized the role of fraud detection in enhancing key
performance indicators such as click-through rates (CTR)
and return on ad spend (ROAS), this research is the first
to focus on the dual channels, both direct and indirect, in
which fraud detection impacts ad performance, specifically
through increased efficiency in real-time bidding (RTB) and
consumer trust.
The novelty of this research is that it addresses the par -
tial mediation effects of RTB and consumer trust that have
not been thoroughly investigated in the context of machine
learning fraud detection systems in the field of digital adver-
tising. Practitioners will find the study especially helpful,
as it also displays practical information about how machine
learning can be combined to achieve better ad targeting,
lessen the distortions caused by fraud, and, finally, lead to
improved campaign results. In addition, this study provides
useful insights to advertisers who seek to adopt fraud detec-
tion mechanisms as they struggle to balance consumer trust
and privacy laws.
Interestingly, the study also reveals some inconsisten -
cies with previous research, especially the negative impacts
of Hypotheses H3 and H5, where the correlation between
fraud detection and consumer trust (CT) was established to
be negative and weak. This is the opposite of the prior stud-
ies, which stressed that there is a closer connection between
consumer trust and enhanced ad performance (Sağlam &
Kirçova, 2025). In addition, the opposite impact of con -
sumer trust on ad performance in H5 offsets the literature
on trust per se positively affecting engagement and conver -
sion rates, and suggests other sources could also have a role,
including ad fatigue or overexposure, which were not effec-
tively studied before. These contradictions provide some
novel insights and research opportunities that can further
explore the complexities of trust and performance in online
advertising.
5 Conclusion and implications
The current study has gone a long way in advancing the
body of knowledge about the effects of machine learning-
derived fraud detection systems on ad performance met -
rics in the area of programmatic advertising. The findings
not only validate the efficacy of fraud detection systems
in increasing advertising performance but also illustrate
the interdependency of relationships among fraud detec -
tion, real-time bidding (RTB) performance, consumer trust,
and ad performance metrics, such as click-through rates
(CTR), conversion rates, and returns on ad spend (ROAS).
Offering a literature gap on the direct and indirect effects
of fraud detection systems, this research offers a novel

and comprehensive framework to comprehend how digital
advertising can be optimized with the assistance of machine
learning, particularly in domestic markets.
The investigation of the dual effects of machine learn -
ing-based fraud detection on advertisement performance is
one of the key contributions of this study. The study shows
that fraud detection not only directly improves ad perfor -
mance measures but also indirectly through increasing the
RTB efficiency. By filtering out fraudulent participants in
real time through machine learning, the overall accuracy of
the ad targeting and bidding processes is increased, which,
in turn, results in more successful campaigns. This twofold
effect of fraud detection on RTB and ad performance is a
new addition to the literature because earlier research was
mainly concerned with one or the other aspect, but not both.
Further, the study has a more detailed insight into the ability
of fraud detection systems to recover consumer confidence
by introducing consumer trust as a mediator, a vital factor
in generating a greater level of engagement and enhancing
ad performance.
However, this study also has some surprising results that
contradict the literature. In particular, the negative corre -
lations found between fraud detection and consumer trust
(H3) and between consumer trust and ad performance (H5)
are opposite to previous research, which highlights the posi-
tive impact of trust on ad performance. Although earlier
studies have indicated that fraud detection systems result
in increased trust and subsequently improved campaign
results, the current study concludes that the connection
between fraud detection and consumer trust is not simple.
This negative effect could be attributed to some external
reasons, such as ad fatigue or overexposure, as this could
influence consumer behavior with online ads. These contra-
dictions offer a new path to be followed by future research
to reveal more about the complexity of consumer trust and
its role in digital advertisement.
The other importance of the research is that it is sensitive
to the data privacy laws and the mediating influence on the
association between machine learning-based fraud identifi -
cation, RTB efficiency, and customer trust. The results indi-
cate that tighter data privacy rules have a positive impact
on RTB efficiency and consumer trust, which eventually
leads to a better ad performance indicator. These findings
underscore the need to comply with the laws of data pro -
tection in online advertising; through transparency and safe
management of consumer information, advertisers can cre -
ate a trustworthy atmosphere, which not only increases the
efficiency of the bidding process but also the performance
of the advertising campaigns. This highlights the need to
adhere to regulations in the establishment of consumer
trust, especially in a time when data privacy is increasingly
becoming a hot topic.
5.1 Theoretical contribution
This study expands the Technology Acceptance Model
(TAM) to include machine learning-based fraud detection
systems as programmatic advertising. It presents empirical
data that the perceived usefulness and ease of use of fraud
detection systems affect ad performance metrics, real-time
bidding (RTB) efficiency, and consumer trust.
This study dispels the common belief that the better the
consumer trust, the better the ad performance. The negative
relationship between consumer trust and ad performance in
Hypothesis H5 came unexpectedly, implying that consumer
trust is not a one-dimensional factor but is affected by other
factors such as ad fatigue and overexposure.
This research will provide a new theoretical input by
showing the two-fold routes through which fraud detection
systems affect ad performance, either directly or indirectly.
This twofold influence in terms of RTB efficiency and con-
sumer trust implies that subsequent ad performance models
must consider both direct and indirect elements.
This research adds to the body of knowledge on privacy
and advertisement by exploring the moderating effect of
data privacy regulations. This demonstrates that with tight -
ened privacy policies, RTB effectiveness and consumer
confidence increase, which in turn boosts ad performance
indicators.
This study adds to the theoretical understanding of the
fraud detection system in programmatic advertising, linking
to the key results of advertising, including RTB efficiency
and consumer trust. It emphasizes the need to have a more
advanced conceptualization of fraud detection.
5.2 Practical implications
To practitioners in the digital advertising sector, this
research study has brought to light the fact that including
machine learning-based fraud detectors can significantly
enhance ad performance metrics (measures of CTR, ROAS,
and conversion rates). Advertisers can use these systems to
obtain a more accurate performance measure, since they
are able to filter out fraudulent activities that can distort the
performance.
This study shows that the effectiveness of RTB can be
improved by incorporation of fraud detection systems which
can eliminate the fraudsters during bidding. The insights
can inform practitioners to optimize their RTB processes
and make sure that the ad budget is spent on valid and useful
impressions. This helps adverts to make sure they maximize
their investments and do not jeopardize their investments in
fraudulent bids.
Although the study revealed that consumer trust has
a negative relationship with ad performance in certain

instances, it highlights the relevance of fraud detection in
enhancing consumer trust. To enhance transparency, adver-

inform the consumers that their personal data is safe and
that they are interacting with authentic adverts.
This study emphasizes the importance of data privacy
regulations in improving the effectiveness of fraud detection
systems and enhancing RTB efficiency. To assure consumer

The results of this study can enable advertisers to develop

minimize ad fraud, and enhance the relevance of their ads.
6 Limitations and future research directions
This study has several limitations. First, the cross-sectional
design restricts the capacity to make causal inferences
between machine learning-based performance metrics and
machine learning-based fraud detection. Moreover, the
research is limited to a set of constructs, which are sig -
nificant; there can be other untested variables- advertiser-
specific variables or market conditions- that determine the
effectiveness of programmatic advertising fraud detection
systems. Another limitation of the sample is that market -
ing professionals from one geographical area are domi -
nant, which can restrict the extrapolation of the results to
other geographical areas or industries. Moreover, the self-
reported nature of the data used in the study can add biases
because respondents can have subjective interpretations of
ad performance or the effectiveness of fraud detection.
Further studies in this direction should consider longi -
tudinal designs to establish the causal links between fraud
detection systems and long-term ad performance in the
long run. Further, the study could be extended to a more
varied sample of various geographical areas and industries
to provide a wider scope on the applicability of machine
learning-based fraud detection systems in digital advertising
globally. More potential moderators or mediators (e.g., the
nature of platforms (social media or search engines) could
be researched by scientists to understand the effects of these
variables on the efficiency of fraud detection mechanisms
better. Furthermore, it would be interesting to delve into the
potential long-term effects of fraud detection on consumer
attitudes and brand loyalty and the way it would be applied
to new technologies, like blockchain advertising.
