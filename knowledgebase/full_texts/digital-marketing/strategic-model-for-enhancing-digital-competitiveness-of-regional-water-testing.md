Abstract:
Background: Access to safe drinking water continues to face global and national challenges,
requiring reliable laboratory systems to ensure compliance with quality standards. In
Indonesia, population growth, stricter regulatory demands, and competition among accredited
laboratories highlight the need for strategic strengthening. Beyond technical competence,
laboratories must improve service efficiency and digital access. In this context, digital
transformation and service innovation have become critical for sustaining competitiveness,
particularly for regional laboratories operating alongside private competitors.
Purpose: This study aims to identify strategic internal and external factors influencing PT
XYZ Laboratory’s performance, formulate development strategies using SWOT analysis,
and determine priority strategies to enhance digital competitiveness and market positioning.
Design/methodology/approach: A mixed-methods case-study approach was applied over
four months, integrating qualitative interviews and observations with quantitative analyses
using SWOT, IE Matrix, AHP, and QSPM. Data were obtained from internal experts,
customer records, digital-platform analytics, competitor benchmarking, and regulatory
reviews.
Findings/Result: The IE Matrix positioned PT XYZ Laboratory in the Grow & Build
quadrant, supported by strong accreditation, competitive pricing, and competent personnel.
However, the analysis identified weaknesses in digital visibility, branding, and service
responsiveness. QSPM results indicated that the highest strategic priority is the integration
of digital marketing, website development, and a Laboratory Information Management
System (LIMS) to enhance competitiveness and customer trust.
Conclusion: PT XYZ Laboratory possesses strong internal capabilities but must accelerate
digital transformation to remain competitive within an increasingly crowded market.
Strengthening branding, online platforms, and technology-based monitoring systems can
significantly improve service quality and strategic positioning.
Originality/value (State of the art): This study provides a comprehensive strategic-
management model specifically tailored for regional testing laboratories by integrating
SWOT-AHP-QSPM with digital competitiveness assessment, an approach rarely applied
in prior laboratory-sector research. The findings extend theoretical insights on Laboratory
4.0 readiness while offering practical guidance for policy and managerial decision-making.
Keywords: strategic management, laboratory operations, SWOT-AHP-QSPM analysis,
digital transformation, water-quality testing

INTRODUCTION
Access to safe and clean water remains one of the
most critical global challenges of the 21st century,
as it directly affects public health, environmental
sustainability, and socio-economic development.
According to the WHO/UNICEF Joint Monitoring
Programme (2023), approximately 2.2 billion people
still lack access to safely managed drinking-water
services, while 3.5 billion people remain without safely
managed sanitation as of 2022. These findings align
with the World Bank (2024), which emphasizes that
disparities in access to clean water persist and continue
to hinder global well-being.
In Indonesia, this urgency is becoming increasingly
evident as the population is projected to grow from 281.6
million in 2024 to 284.4 million in 2025 (BPS, 2025).
The rising demand for safe and potable water places
significant pressure on water-quality testing laboratories
to ensure compliance with national regulations.
Regulations such as Ministry of Health Regulation No.
492/MENKES/PER/IV/2010 and Ministry of Health
Regulation No. 32 of 2017. Laboratory testing plays
a strategic role in detecting physical, chemical, and
microbiological contaminants that pose risks to public
health and the environment (Pontororing et al. 2019;
Suruata et al. 2021). Strengthening laboratory systems
is therefore essential for maintaining national resilience
and safeguarding public health security.
Along with the increasing demand for clean-water
services, the laboratory industry is also undergoing
a significant transformation driven by technological
advancements. Globally, the laboratory industry is
undergoing a significant digital transformation driven
by the Fourth Industrial Revolution. The Laboratory
4.0 concept integrating the Internet of Things (IoT),
automation, and real-time data analytics has been
proven to enhance accuracy, transparency, and testing
efficiency (Azam & Mustafa, 2024; Narolita, 2020).
The adoption of end-to-end workflows and electronic
reporting further reinforces public trust in laboratory
results (Jovičić & Vitkus, 2023). As a result, laboratories
are increasingly required not only to provide accurate
testing results but also to deliver services efficiently
through digital platforms.
Competition in Indonesia’s laboratory-testing industry
has intensified due to the increasing number of service
providers offering similar testing services. Data from the
National Accreditation Committee (KAN, 2025) indicate
that 94 medical laboratories, 1,573 testing laboratories,
and 382 calibration laboratories are accredited. Major
laboratories such as Sucofindo, Envilab, and Genau have
integrated digital marketing initiatives and branding
ecosystems as part of their strategies to expand market
share. However, many regional laboratories still struggle
with limited resources, weak branding, and low digital
visibility. This gap illustrates a digital divide that limits
competitiveness even among laboratories that are
technically competent and accredited.
This situation is evident in PT XYZ Laboratory.
Although the laboratory holds KAN accreditation (LP-
552-IDN) and ISO/IEC 17025:2017 certification and
offers competitive testing fees, it continues to face
challenges in expanding its market reach. Limited use
of social media, the absence of a dedicated website,
and insufficient customer testimonials as social proof
weaken its competitive positioning (Ardella, 2024).
Meanwhile, external factors such as regulatory changes
and evolving KAN data-recording requirements
necessitate more responsive digital systems, including
IoT-based temperature and humidity monitoring, which
has been shown to improve recording accuracy by up
to 98 percent (Susantok et al. 2025). Additionally,
the integration of web-based Laboratory Information
Management Systems (LIMS) enhances accuracy,
scheduling, and operational accountability (Dalimunthe
et al. 2023).
Beyond external pressures, laboratory competitiveness is
also shaped by internal factors such as customer relations,
service quality, and personnel competence. Service
quality, speed, and testing accuracy play central roles
in improving customer satisfaction and loyalty (Harun
Al Rasyid, 2017). The use of information technology
has been proven to strengthen customer relationships
and enhance service competitiveness (Harun Al Rasyid,
2017). Theoretically, this study contributes to the
literature on digital competitiveness within the public-
service sector. Practically, it offers guidance for regional
laboratories seeking to develop sustainable competitive
advantages in the era of Industry 4.0.
The increasing demand for clean-water services, together
with intensified competition and accelerated digital
transformation, makes strategic planning both timely
and necessary for laboratory organizations. Previous
studies have employed strategic tools such as SWOT,
IFAS-EFAS, and AHP to formulate organizational

Data collection was carried out using both primary and
secondary data sources. Primary data were obtained
through direct observation, in-depth interviews with
internal stakeholders, and expert questionnaires.
Secondary data included financial reports, customer
databases, Google Reviews, social media insights,
and customer-satisfaction surveys involving 279
respondents. All data sources were triangulated to
ensure validity and to enable comparisons with practices
applied by private laboratories. A non-probability
expert-sampling technique was employed, involving
five internal experts aged 32-47 years, each with
2-13 years of experience in the water-quality testing
industry. Expert selection was based on competence,
strategic understanding, and professional experience
(Tuan & Canh, 2022). The expert-sampling technique
was selected due to the strategic nature of the analysis,
which requires informed managerial judgment rather
than statistical generalization.
A mixed-methods approach employing a case-study
design was used, integrating qualitative analyses
(interviews and observations) with quantitative
analyses (SWOT, IE Matrix, AHP, and QSPM). This
combination provided a comprehensive understanding
of the laboratory’s strategic position across internal
performance, market conditions, and strategic priorities
(Mulyadi et al. 2020). The integration of SWOT, IE
Matrix, AHP, and QSPM enables a structured transition
from qualitative strategic diagnosis to quantitative
decision-making, thereby reducing subjectivity
in strategy formulation. Similar methodological
integrations have proven effective in prior studies (Diep
et al. 2025; Li & Zhang, 2025). The SWOT analysis
was used to identify internal and external strategic
factors, the IE Matrix to determine the laboratory’s
strategic position, AHP to assign priority weights based
on expert judgment, and QSPM to evaluate and rank
strategic alternatives.
The framework of thought in this study is presented
in the form of a data-analysis flow that illustrates the
sequential stages of strategic formulation, from problem
identification to strategic-solution development. The
framework provides a structured guide for integrating
qualitative and quantitative analyses in developing
business strategies for laboratory testing services. The
sequential stages of data analysis are illustrated in

2024). However, most of these studies remain focused on
operational efficiency and service quality, with limited
attention to digital marketing, branding, and strategic
positioning as key dimensions of competitiveness. This
limitation highlights the need for a more comprehensive
and integrative problem-solving approach in the digital
era.
To address these challenges, this study adopts an
integrated strategic analysis approach by combining
SWOT analysis, IE Matrix, the Analytic Hierarchy
Process (AHP), and the Quantitative Strategic Planning
Matrix (QSPM). SWOT analysis is used to identify
internal strengths and weaknesses as well as external
opportunities and threats. Subsequently, AHP is applied
to assign relative weights to each strategic factor
through pairwise comparisons, ensuring consistency
and objectivity in decision-making. Finally, QSPM
is employed to evaluate and prioritize alternative
strategies based on their overall attractiveness scores.
This sequential approach enables the identification of
strategic priorities for enhancing digital competitiveness
and strengthening market positioning through an in-depth
case-study analysis. The research design integrating
SWOT, IE Matrix, AHP, and QSPM.
Based on the background, research gap, and problem-
solving approach described above, this study aims to
achieve the following objectives: Identify internal and
external factors that influence the performance of PT
XYZ Laboratory; Formulate alternative development
strategies using SWOT analysis; Determine priority
strategies to enhance digital competitiveness and
strengthen the market position of regional testing
laboratories.
METHODS
This study was conducted at PT XYZ Laboratory
in Surabaya over a four-month period from June
to October 2025. Research activities included data
collection, processing, analysis, and report preparation.
The laboratory was selected due to its strategic role
in ensuring drinking-water quality and its increasing
competition with private laboratories. This study utilized
both primary and secondary data. Primary and secondary
data were collected to support a comprehensive strategic
assessment.

Stage 1: Preparation and Exploration of Company
Profile
The initial phase involved identifying research
problems, reviewing relevant literature, and
constructing the research framework. The company’s
vision, mission, objectives, and market position were
analyzed as the foundation for strategic assessment
(Aisyah et al. 2024; Mulyadi et al. 2020).
Stage 2: Exploration of Internal Conditions
Internal analysis was conducted by identifying
strengths and weaknesses. These findings were then
used to formulate internal factors within the SWOT
framework (Vitasari, 2021).
Stage 3: Exploration of External Conditions
External analysis was carried out by identifying
opportunities and threats through competitor
benchmarking, customer feedback, regulatory
assessment, and reviews of macroeconomic conditions.
This stage provided a comprehensive overview of the
external environment influencing the organization’s
strategic direction.
Stage 4: IE Matrix Analysis (Internal-External)
The Internal-External (IE) Matrix was constructed by
combining the weighted scores of internal (IFE) and
external (EFE) factors. Based on the total IFE scores
(x-axis) and EFE scores (y-axis), the organization’s
position was mapped into one of nine strategic cells,
classified into three main strategy groups: growth/build,
stability/hold & maintain, or retrenchment/harvest.
The placement within a specific quadrant served as the
basis for determining the most appropriate strategic
alternatives for the company (Ben-Abdallah et al.
2022; Sibarani & Djunita Pasaribu, 2024).
Stage 5: Weighting Using the Analytic Hierarchy
Process (AHP)
The Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) was applied
to determine the relative importance of each SWOT
factor using a pairwise comparison matrix. This
method generates quantitative weights for each factor,
providing an objective foundation for establishing
strategic priorities (Jati et al. 2023). To ensure
consistency in factor weighting, the Consistency Ratio
(CR) was calculated. An assessment is considered
consistent when CR ≤ 10 percent. (Diep et al. 2025).
Stage 6: Determination of Strategic Priorities Using
the Quantitative Strategic Planning Matrix (QSPM)
The Quantitative Strategic Planning Matrix (QSPM)
method was used to evaluate and rank strategic
alternatives (SO, WO, ST, WT) objectively based
on their level of attractiveness. Each internal and
external factor listed in the IFE and EFE matrices was
assigned an Attractiveness Score (AS) ranging from
1 to 4 by expert evaluators, where 1 (not attractive),

Stage 2 and 3: Internal and External Environmental

Internal environmental factors were examined using

The external environmental analysis was conducted
by examining competitor dynamics, government
regulations, market trends, and customer feedback.
Competition with private laboratories has intensified,
particularly with the presence of well-established
testing service providers that are widely recognized
by the public. Government regulations related to
water quality standards and laboratory accreditation

developments. On the other hand, substantial

regarding the importance of water quality increases,
accompanied by growing demand for testing services

on this analysis, five key opportunities were identified,

external factors were subsequently weighted using
the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP). The resulting
weights were then incorporated into the IFAS and
EFAS matrix.
Stage 4: Internal-External (IE) Matrix Analysis
The internal and external factors identified were
evaluated using the IFAS and EFAS matrices. The
assessment process involved assigning weights using
AHP and ratings to each strategic factor. The weights
indicate the level of importance of each factor to the
laboratory, while the ratings reflect the laboratory’s
ability to respond to them. The rating scale ranged from
1 to 4, where 1 indicates a major weakness, 2 a minor
weakness, 3 a minor strength, and 4 a major strength
(Mulyadi et al. 2020).

(highly attractive). These scores were multiplied by
the corresponding factor weights to obtain the Total
Attractiveness Score (TAS) (Prananto et al. 2025).

Stage 7: Solution Analysis
The final stage integrates all research findings into
implementable strategic solutions. The results provide
practical recommendations for strengthening laboratory
competitiveness, contributing to the strategic-
management literature through the application of the
integrated SWOT-AHP-QSPM model.
RESULTS
Stage 1: Preparation and Exploration of Company
Profile
PT XYZ’s laboratory operates as a service unit
specializing in water quality testing in accordance
with national and international regulatory standards.
It fulfills a dual function: supporting the company’s
primary water service operations and generating
alternative revenue through analytical testing services
offered to the public and industrial sectors.
The analysis began with an examination of PT XYZ’s
vision and mission. The company envisions itself
as a modern drinking water provider, supported
by a mission emphasizing financial transparency
for community welfare, fostering water-conscious
behavior, delivering efficient and sustainable services,
and cultivating a work environment grounded in
integrity and achievement. Although these statements
align with community needs and local development
priorities, they remain broad and oriented toward PT
XYZ as a whole. Therefore, the testing laboratory must
translate the parent company’s vision and mission into
more specific operational objectives.
The laboratory’s operational goal is to provide water
quality testing services that prioritize speed, precision,
and accuracy, executed by certified professionals in
accordance with ISO/IEC 17025:2017. This objective
aligns with current sectoral demands and demonstrates
the company’s commitment to strengthening its
competitiveness in the testing services industry.

Stage 6: Strategy Prioritization Using the
Quantitative Strategic Planning Matrix (QSPM)
To determine strategic priorities, the Quantitative
Strategic Planning Matrix (QSPM) was employed. This
method integrates SWOT factors as vertical indicators
and alternative strategies as horizontal indicators. Each
factor is evaluated based on its Attractiveness Score
(AS), and the Total Attractiveness Score (TAS) is
calculated to determine implementation priority.
The calculated IFAS score of 3.0077 and EFAS score of
2.6813 place PT XYZ in Cell IV of the Internal-External
(IE) Matrix. Cell IV corresponds to the Grow & Build
category, indicating a strategic position characterized
by strong internal capabilities and moderate external
attractiveness. This position suggests that PT XYZ
has the capacity to leverage its technical advantages

Stage 7: Solution Analysis
Based on the weighting results derived from the SWOT-
AHP model for PT XYZ Laboratory, the strategy
integrating internal strengths and external opportunities
(SO) emerges as the most optimal approach for
sustainable laboratory development. This strategy
leverages existing opportunities while maximizing
the laboratory’s internal advantages, including official
accreditation, competitive service fees, professional
laboratory personnel, and accurate, reliable test results.
In practice, the SO strategy emphasizes strengthening
digital-based promotion to expand market penetration
by capitalizing on ongoing digitalization trends and the
increasing industrial demand for routine water quality
testing.
These findings support the proposed hypothesis that
the implementation of digital transformation strategies
positively contributes to strengthening the digital
competitiveness and market positioning of regional water
testing laboratories. The findings are also consistent with
the Laboratory 4.0 perspective proposed by Jovičić and
Vitkus (2023) and Azam and Mustafa (2024), which
emphasize that digital integration, automation, and real-
time information systems support laboratory efficiency,
transparency, responsiveness, and service competitiveness.
In addition, the prioritization of digital promotion, online
registration systems, and digital branding aligns with
previous strategic-management studies by Mulyadi et al.
(2020) and Diep et al. (2025), which demonstrated that
the integration of SWOT, AHP, and QSPM provides an
effective framework for identifying strategic priorities
and strengthening organizational competitiveness.
However, this study extends prior research by specifically
incorporating digital competitiveness dimensions
such as online visibility, digital branding, customer
responsiveness, and Laboratory Information Management
Systems (LIMS) within the context of regional water
testing laboratories. Therefore, the findings not only
confirm previous strategic-management theories but
also contribute to the growing literature on digital
transformation and Laboratory 4.0 readiness in public-
service laboratory sectors.
