The present research aimed to assess the impact of digital marketing trends on the decision -making
process and spending behavior of tourists in Pakistan. The tourism sector in District Faisalabad is
currently experiencing exponential growth, aided by incre asing internet penetration and the
popularity of social media networks. The article discusses the phenomenon of digital marketing and
uses quantitative cross -sectional data collected from 160 respondents to explore the relationship
between the examined asp ect and tourist behavior. PLS -SEM and binary logistic regression analysis
were conducted to evaluate the impact of digital marketing on the tourism sector and identify
mediation and moderation effects. The results suggest that the effect of digital marketi ng on both
decision-making and spending behavior of tourists is positive and significant. Moreover, digital
marketing tends to increase the likelihood of tourist decision -making reliance by 5.35 times and the
probability of high spending by six times. The results also indicate that satisfaction and interaction
mediate the relationship between digital marketing and the dependent variables, explaining 36.1%
and 24.8% of the observed effect, respectively. At the same time, gender and location were found to
moderate the impact of digital marketing trends on tourist spending behavior, reducing the likelihood
of high spending for both female tourists and residents in rural areas. The study also found education
and income to be two critical factors enabling effective digital marketing, while cultural and economic
barriers reduced its effectiveness for the studied population. The research confirmed the applicability
of several theoretical models, including Technology Acceptance Model, Theory of Planned Behavior,
Expectation Confirmation Theory, and Self - Determination Theory, to the tourism sector in Pakistan.
The present findings contribute to understanding the role of digital marketing in tourism and offer
policymakers and marketers guidelines on implementing equal opportunities marketing in the sector.

Introduction:
Consider exploring another realm and getting immersed in it. According to UNWorld  Tourism
Organization, tourism is ‘a travel and stay of an individual away from their regular environment for
business or pleasurable reasons’ (UNWTO, 2024). However, the activity requires a night of stay
outside of the traveler’s regular residence for no more than consecutive 12 months to be identified
as tourism (Aftab and Khan, 2019). On a personal level, this escape breaks the routine, creating
vibrant "flashbulb memories" that deeply enrich a person’s quality of life and expand their
understanding of the world (Kim, 2016).
Zooming out, this simple act of traveling fuels a multifaceted global industry. It acts as an economic
engine, generating essential foreign exchange, building infrastructure, and creating jobs ranging
from professional roles to part -time opportunities for students and pensioners (Goeldner and
Ritchie, 2012; Jucan and Jucan, 2013). Its economic power is staggering driving nearly 40% of the
Maldives' Gross Domestic Product, for instance. Yet, the impact is a double-edged sword, as tourism
secures new roads, the preservation of traditional cultural resources (Ramkissoon, 2022; Meyer and
Meyer, 2015), and is intrinsically linked to ecological conservation (Uysal et al., 2016), but at the
same time, it brings the threat of pollution and degr adation (Ren et al., 2019). Nonetheless, the
presence of tourists is a powerful ally in the fight to offset the damage to the ancient cultural
heritage sites and the local wildlife (Ursache, 2015).
The way people explore the world has changed, as tradition al travel agencies and printed
brochures are no longer the primary source of information about the destination. Today, the
intangible nature of travel where you cannot truly assess a destination until you arrive (Grubor et
al., 2019) has met the democratiz ing power of Web 2.0. A new, "information -empowered" traveler
has emerged. They are highly skeptical of traditional advertising (Ferreira et al., 2019) and
increasingly reliant on the sprawling digital ecosystem to dictate their travel plans (Imane et al.,
2025; GOK and Baltaci, 2024). This digital marketing landscape forces the entire industry to
adapt its philosophies, intervening in the non -linear decision-making process from sparking the
initial desire to making the final booking (Khan and Rehman, 2024; Singh et al., 2025; Hudson and
Thal, 2013; Fotis et al., 2012).
The main actors in the described context are users. In particular, user-generated content (UGC) and
electronic word of mouth (eWOM) have become critically important information coming from
these sources is perceived by consumers as more authentic, trustworthy, and less risky than
traditional forms of advertising (Albastroiu and Felea, 2014; Tsiakali, 2018; Elsaid and Sayed,
2022). This source of knowledge influences the formation of alternativ e ideas (Kennell and
Rushton, 2015), including cognitive, normative, and affective aspects (Pourfakhimi et al., 2020) and
significantly affects the final choice of the tourist (Bilos et al., 2022; Sparks and Browning, 2011).
At the same time, social media are actively shaping the perception of travel destinations through
such mechanisms as attention (Afren, 2024), inspiration (Fatanti and Suyadnya, 2015; Sobrino,
2022), influencing the cognitive (Riera et al., 2015), emotional (Sakshi et al., 2018), and beh avioral
aspects (Fatanti and Suyadnya, 2015). In particular, Instagram and TikTok have become popular

makes it possible to form a cognitive image of the destinat ion and the desire to visit it (Bilos et al.,

Why can one single d igital post exert such a powerful influence over our holiday decisions?
According to the theory of planned behavior (TPB), the reason lies in the interplay between
attitudes toward the online content, the degree of social network pressures, and confidence level
when navigating these resources (Ajzen, 1991). Empirical data has since demonstrated the TPB’s
predictive capacity regarding individuals’ final travel decisions (Javed et al., 2020; Majeed et al.,
2020).
Meanwhile, once in their selected destinations  and having found out whether their expectations
match reality or not, customers can use the Expectation Confirmation Theory (ECT) to justify their
expenditures. Indeed, according to Oliver (1977), meeting or exceeding expectations leads to
making tourists  return to this destination again, increasing sales. Indeed, the self -determination
theory (SDT) shows that engaging with online travel content satisfies their psychological needs for
autonomy in vacation planning, competence when making online payments, and connection while
“travelling” virtually with friends (Deci and Ryan, 2000; Rasoolimanesh et al., 2021; Ramkissoon,
2022). Notably, the conclusions of this study contribute to research exploring various aspects of the
SDT, including the application of th e same to the younger generations in Portugal (Magano and
Cunha, 2020), interactive online marketing in Turkey (Armutcu et al., 2023), destination image in
China and Pakistan (Majeed et al., 2020; Khan et al., 2022).
The present research aims to investigat e the relationship between digital marketing and tourism
using Pakistan as the subject of analysis. As compared to other regional countries, Pakistan has
tremendous potential for attracting both local and foreign tourists fascinated by adventure tourism
and a rich history of the country. Moreover, it has been revealed that before covid -19 restrictions,
the number of tourists was limited due to specific challenges that hindered the development of the
tourism sector. Digital marketing can offer an innovative solution by attracting a broader audience
of inquisitive travelers to Pakistan’s tourism sector. With a rapidly growing number of smartphone
users (Yuan et al., 2022) and increased access to the internet among the youth, traditional
marketing strategies ar e becoming increasingly irrelevant (Raji et al., 2024). Thus, the current
study will focus on district Faisalabad in Punjab province to determine how digital marketing is
changing contemporary tourists’ decision-making process and expenditures.
Literature Review:
The history of contemporary tourism starts with the transformation of the way the destinations are
found and experienced by the travelers. Being no longer forced to rely on the limited possibilities
of printed guides and the recommendations of trad itional travel agencies, the modern tourism

market underwent a significant explosion under the umbrella of digitization. Chu et al. (2020) was
among the first researchers to note that as the industry began to embrace social media advertising,
it lacked the appropriate set of theoretical frameworks to understand the phenomenon. Hence, as
marketing practices evolved and diversified (Magano and Cunha, 2020), it became apparent that
the tourism industry had already entered the new reality. Firstly, it was identified that the younger
generations were already embracing social and mobile technologies as part of their travel planning
(Dewi, 2020). For example, the tourism business in Bali managed to understand how to leverage
marketing 4.0 to target customers in the  digital space and promote their services via Instagram,
apps, and online marketplaces. Secondly, according to Valentaza and Metaxas (2023), the
businesses in Greece that participated in promoting and advertising their products and services
digitally (most ly through social media) improved their image and became more influential in
2023.Thus, the paradigm of digital marketing as the foundation of contemporary tourism was set.
As the online space became the new marketplace, the researchers began observing the changes in
travelers’ perceptions and attitudes. It has been revealed that the user -generated content and
electronic word-of-mouth (eWOM) were paramount in the decision-making process. According to
Pourfakhimi et al. (2020), the eWOM had a significant inf luence on the tourists’ cognitive,
normative, and affective evaluation of the destinations. Interestingly, This, in turn, resulted in
stronger buying intentions. Nevertheless, the credibility of eWOM was noted to have its
limitations; according to Zelenka et al. (2021), the users had to rely on verified reviews in order to
avoid potentially dangerous fake reviews and ensure their safety. Additionally, Furner et al.
(2022) suggested that there were specific accommodations where the trust was more difficult to be
established, in particular, when it came to sharing -economy platforms versus traditional hoteliers.
Lastly, Zhou et al. (2020) used Dramaturgical Theory to illustrate how Chinese tourists differed in
terms of the platforms they selected to share their  eWOM. In turn, Kupi and Bakó (2023) stated
that, in the context of digital tourists in Hungary, the quantity of information available online
became the key factor that influenced their buying behavior.
Since the tourists were found to place their trust in  online reviews and user -generated content, it
was only a matter of time before the social media platforms transformed from the means of
communication into the source of inspiration. As Javed et al. (2020) illustrated with the example of
Czech Millennials, the tourists relied exclusively on the information found on social media when
planning their travel. In a similar vein, Nzeku et al. (2021) argue that the social media platforms
successfully facilitated the cognitive and affective responses of the visitor s to the tourism
destinations in South Africa, thereby serving as a major marketing tool for promoting the local
attractions. Indeed, Chen et al. (2024) explored the role of social media in the context of the
Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) and showed th at the tool’s convenience, interactivity, and
social norms were some of the factors that impacted the intentions of the potential travelers in
China. Thus, the social media platforms have become the central figure in the development of
tourists’ interest in a particular destination.
However, when it comes to inspiring the potential visitors, social media relies on such tools as
influencers. Namely, as Pop et al. (2022) suggest, the tourists who subscribed to the social media

accounts of influencers received  credible information about their preferred travel options, which
affected them at all stages of their travel decision-making process. Upananda and Bandara (2022)
agree with the statement and note that, in the case of Sri Lanka, the success of influencer marketing
depended on the perceived trust and content quality. At the same time, the researchers also remark
that the influencers’ impact was different depending on the social connection mechanism used.
Huang (2023) proves that it was the connection tool that facilitated the influencers’ ability to reach

(envious) affective responses, thus influencing their decision to visit certain places.
It is common knowledge that the good story should have a happy one to mark the beginning of a
better future. However, in the case of the  tourists and their travel experiences, it was the Covid -19
pandemic that brought a significant change to their behaviors. In particular, Toubes et al. (2021)
state that the pandemic changed the travelers’ perception of their tourism experiences, making
them favor contactless and online services. Nevertheless, the social media marketing ensured that
the businesses would still get enough attention during the pandemic. Namely, Tsai and Hsin (2023)
remark that, when it came to post-pandemic recovery, the influencers who posted relevant content
for the specific destination were successful in convincing the public in terms of their intentions to
visit the place. With the pandemic restrictions being lifted, the tourism sector moved to the next
level with the help of technology. Sakas et al. (2022) analyzed data from webometrics in Greece
and remarked that, unlike the logistics sector, the tourism websites were highly dependent on the
engagement rate and personalization of experience, which was driven by the volume of traffic.
Moreover, Ghesh et al. (2024) conducted a comprehensive study on the concept of AI -enabled
Customer Experience (AICX) and concluded that the artificial intelligence -driven marketing
tools re-positioned the attention of modern tourists, thus acting as an efficient facilitator as well as
raising ethical concerns.
As the digital marketing tools positioned the tourists at the chosen destination, it was only a matter
of time before they began impacting their expenditures. In particular, Pulido -Fernandez et al.
(2020) conducted a regression model analysis of the emergent regional destinations in Spain in
order to identify the ways the government could affect the tourists’ behavior. The analysis showed
that it was indeed possible to predict and influenc e the visitor spending patterns on the local
accommodations. In a similar vein, Sahoo et al. (2022) conducted another regression analysis in
India and found that the tourism expenditures were conditional upon the demographics of the
individuals making them. For instance, the travel experiences were reported to be a luxury for the
lower-class citizens while middle - and higher -class individuals purchased tourism services as a
matter of necessity.
It is also important to remark that the integration of technolo gy into the tourism experiences has
been shown to have positive economic and social outcomes. Namely, Wang et al. (2025) conducted

between the use of digita l technology, women’s status, and the tourism expenditure per capita in
China. The results of the analysis indicated that there was a link between the active use of

At this point, it becomes evident that the tourism market is highly reliant on the digital resources
and marketing tools. Hence, it is no surprise that as the literature reviews start focusing on the
contemporary issues of tourism, such matters as sustainability and the responsible use of resources
become the major points of interest. In particular, Aman and Papp -Vary (2022) argue that the
industry can significantly benefit from the sustainable tourism marketing, prov ided that it is used
correctly. Furthermore, Reyes-Menendez et al. (2020) suggest that the online marketing strategies
and eWOM have the ability to shape the consumers’ perception of sustainable business practices,
thus increasing their likelihood to choose such options.
Indeed, as Sanjaya et al. (2024) report, the digital marketing strategies in Bali successfully
promoted the environmental friendliness of the tourism options in Indonesia, which in turn
influenced the travelers’ pro -environmental behaviors and their intentions to return to the
destination. Moreover, Oka and Subadra (2024) remark that the Bali tourism villages used digital
literacy and the opportunities provided by the online space to both survive and thrive
economically. Similarly, Piras and  Pedes (2025) find that the Italian rural areas acknowledge the
necessity of incorporating technology into their agricultural tourism activities in order to ensure
their longevity. As such, it is possible to suggest that the digital marketing has become a valuable
tool for the modern tourism sector.
Methodology:
This study adopted a quantitative research design that was cross -sectional to assess the impact of
digital marketing on tourism. District Faisalabad in Punjab province was selected as the study area
because it is the third-largest city in Pakistan in terms of population size. In addition, Faisalabad is
an industrial city that is famously called the Manchester of Pakistan. It is also one of the most
urbanized cities in the country and has a growing middle class that has disposable income at a very
high rate. Faisalabad has an internet penetration rate of about 65%.
Conceptual Framework
Drawing on the theoretical base and the empirical literature, this study suggested an empirical
framework that portray s the relationship between the study variables. The framework is thus
displayed three factors; digital marketing as the independent variable, tourism decision making
and tourism spending behavior as the dependent variables, and as a mediating and moderatin g
variable the factor satisfaction and digital marketing interaction. Additionally socioeconomic
factors are included as control variables

According to the conceptual framework, digital marketing has direct and indirect impacts on

 Sampling and Data Collection:
The convenience sampling technique was used, which resulted in obtaining responses from 160
participants. This sample size is adequate for the current study because it surpasses the
recommended minimum sample size used in multivariate analysis and structural  equation
modeling. The questionnaires were administered to respondents using face-to-face interviews and
online surveys. In addition to increasing the response rate, the respondents were selected from a
wide range of locations in the district, including Universities, shopping malls, Parks, and Residents’
houses.
Variable Measurement:
The survey questions were divided into various sections that measured different variables, which
include the independent variable, dependent variable, and mediating variables.
Independent Variable:  Digital marketing (Social media marketing, Online reviews, Online
promotional content).
Dependent Variables: Tourism decision making and Spending behavior.

Econometric Models and Data Analysis:
The paper used two statistical models, including Partial least squares structural equation modeling
and Binary logistic regression to analyze the data. The first model was used to estimate the direct
effect of digital marketing on the dependent variables as well as the indirect effect through t he
mediation of tourist satisfaction and digital marketing interaction. In testing for mediation, the
Baron and Kenny approach was used together with the Sobel test to establish whether tourism
satisfaction and digital marketing interaction significantly h ad a mediating effect on the
relationship between independent variables and the dependent variable. On the other hand,
binary logistic regression analysis was applied to examine the likelihood of each independent
variable on the odds ratio of each of the dependent variables.
Binary logistic regression analysis was used because the dependent variables were dichotomous
variables. To deal with the problem of non-normality of error distribution in ordinary least squares
(OLS) regression, the dependent variables  were made into dummy variables by using the median
as a cut -off point. As a result, there was a generation of two categories, which are low and high
decision-making reliance and spending behavior. The regression analysis helped establish the
variables that had the most significant effect on the odds ratio of high decision-making relying and
high spending behavior.

The empirical investigation utilized a robust quantitative framework combining descriptive
statistics, correlation matri ces, ordinary least squares (OLS) regression, binary logistic regression,
and mediation analysis to dissect the influence of digital marketing on tourists’ decision -making

Conclusion:
This study presents empirical evidence that digital marketing is a cr itical factor influencing the
decision-making and spending behavior of tourists in Pakistan, which was conditional to a lesser
extent on socio-demographic characteristics. The results indicate that the use of digital marketing
tools, such as social media, content, and online reviews, improves the likelihood that tourists will use
digital resources to plan trips and encourages spending on tourism. Tourist satisfaction and
interactivity are important variables for which digital marketing has a mediating effec t. This is
particularly evident for the former, as satisfaction accounts for up to 36% of the influence on
tourists’ decision -making processes. Notably, the results also highlight key differences between
various socio-demographic groups, as the impact of digital marketing on tourism was uneven, with
females, residents of rural areas, and families being particularly affected in terms of economic
participation.
While overall, the use of digital marketing tools appears to have a positive effect across the board,
its influence is significantly stronger among several subgroups that can be seen as critical for the
tourism industry in Pakistan. At the same time, the study’s results support the theoretical
framework and the alternative theories used, notably the Tec hnology Acceptance Model, Theory
of Planned Behavior, Expectation Confirmation Theory, and Self-Determination Theory. However,
they also highlight the importance of further research into context-specific conditions and nuances
that may affect the relations hip between digital marketing and tourism in different cultural
settings. The results should encourage various stakeholders to consider the findings’ practical
implications. In particular, companies should invest more effort and resources into providing high-
quality content to engage customers and encourage spending on tourism -related activities.
Moreover, policymakers need to address the issue of unequal opportunity in tourism in rural areas
and provide women with greater access to economic participation. Finally, this study contributes to
the existing body of knowledge on the complex relationship between digital marketing and
tourism, which is critical to both academics and businesses. The results highlight Pakistan as a
promising market for the developmen t of tourism-related business ventures, including innovative
digital experiences to engage the clientele in novel ways.
Limitations:
Though the current research has numerous strengths, such as a solid methodological research
design and valuable results, it  also has several weaknesses and limitations. Firstly, the research
design is a cross-sectional study that only reflects the situation at one moment. Therefore, the study
cannot demonstrate how tourists’ perception of the impact of digital marketing change s during
their journey from inspiration to return. Secondly, the data was collected using self -reported
questionnaires, which might lead to response bias or even to providing false results. Further, the
current case uses the convenience sampling method, which cannot guarantee the representatively
of the sample. Further, the research was conducted only in District Faisalabad, which raises
questions about the generalizability of results to the level of the whole country. Finally, though
using median values fo r dichotomous categorization is an effective method for binary logistic
regression, the process is not perfect since it involves a loss of information.
