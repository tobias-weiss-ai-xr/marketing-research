Abstract

Agribusiness competitiveness requires adaptation to market, technological, and
environmental changes, yet empirical studies integrating business innovation,
digital marketing, and environmental sustainability within a single model remain
limited, particula rly in Bireuen Regency, Indonesia. This study examined the
associations of these three factors with agribusiness competitiveness. A
quantitative cross -sectional survey conducted in July 2026 involved 80
agribusiness actors selected through purposive sampli ng. Data were collected
using a structured five -point Likert -scale questionnaire and analyzed through
partial least squares structural equation modeling (PLS-SEM) in SmartPLS 4. All
three factors were positively and significantly associated with agribusine ss
competitiveness. Environmental sustainability had the largest estimated path
coefficient (β = 0.339, p < .001), followed by business innovation (β = 0.292, p
= .003) and digital marketing (β = 0.251, p = .009). The model explained 40.6%
of the variance in competitiveness (R² = .406). These findings highlight the
relevance of business capabilities and sustainable practices to agribusiness
competitiveness in this setting. The study contributes an integrated
competitiveness model and identifies innovation, digital marketing, and
environmental sustainability as relevant priorities for agribusiness development
strategies in Bireuen.

INTRODUCTION
The agribusiness sector plays a strategic role in the economic development of Bireuen
Regency because agricultural activities are not limited to production but also encompass
processing, distribution, marketing, and value creation. In 2025, the agricultura l sector
contributed 34.63% to the Gross Regional Domestic Product (GRDP) of Bireuen Regency
and involved 73,589 agricultural households. This potential is also reflected in rice
production, which reached 158,432 tons, supported by more than 85,000 units of agricultural
machinery and equipment. Despite this strong production base, increased production needs
to be accompanied by modernization, downstream processing, product quality
improvement, marketing development, broader market access, and stronger suppl y chains
so that local agricultural products can generate higher added value and competitiveness
(Badan Pusat Statistik, 2026).
Changes in the business environment indicate that agribusiness competitiveness can
no longer rely solely on production capacity (Worku et al., 2024) . Business actors are
increasingly required to develop and combine resources, adapt products to consumer needs,
utilize technology, and respond rapidly to market changes. Digital transformation has been
shown to strengthen agribusiness innovation capabilit y and quality by enhancing
technological capacity, reducing financing constraints, and improving access to innovation
resources, including technology, labor, and capital (Xue et al., 2024) . These conditions
indicate that improving competitiveness requires more adaptive business capabilities and
cannot be separated from innovation, digitalization, and changes in the business
environment.
Business innovation is an important capability in building competitiveness because it
enables agribusiness actors to renew products, production processes, services, marketing
methods, and business management practices. Such capabilities help firms create
differentiated value while adapting to market dynamics. In agriculture -related enterprises,
digitalization has also been shown to promote green transformation through more efficient
resource allocation and stronger innovation capabilities. Thus, innovation not only supports
productivity and business differentiation but can also serve as a mechanism for aligning
business processes with increasingly strong environmental demands (Yuan et al., 2024).
In addition to business innovation, digital marketing has become an increasingly
important capability for strengthening the market position of agribusiness firms (Kanellos et

al., 2024) . The use of social media, e -commerce, digital branding, and content -based
marketing enables business actors to expand their consumer reach, increase product visibility,
obtain market information, and communicate with customers more quickly. Studies on
agricultural product marketing show that digital strategies can expand market reach and
increase product value. However, their implementation still faces several constraints,
including low digital literacy, limited infrastructure, and insufficient technical a ssistance,
particularly in rural areas (Sukmawati et al., 2025) . These findings indicate that access to
digital technology does not automatically create competitive advantage unless it is
accompanied by the strategic ability to utilize that technology effectively (Saruchera &
Mpunzi, 2023).
Digital marketing should also not be viewed merely as a promotional instrument.
Among micro, small, and medium-sized enterprises in Indonesia, internet marketing, market
orientation, and entrepreneurial orientation have been associated with business sustainability,
marketing performance, and competitiveness. These findings suggest that digital channels
can become part of a firm’s strategic capabilities for maintaining customer relationships,
expanding markets, and strengthening competitive position (Munandar et al., 2025) .
Therefore, in this study, digital marketing is positioned as a business capability to use
marketing technology to create and deliver value to the market more effectively.
Alongside innovation and marketing orientation, agribusiness competitiveness also
needs to consider environmental sustainability. The dependence of agribusiness on natural
resources makes input efficiency, waste reduction, resource management, and the adop tion
of environmentally friendly technologies important components of business strategy.
Empirical evidence from agricultural enterprises shows that technology utilization can
improve environmental performance through green product and process innovation. This
suggests that economic and environmental performance are not necessarily conflicting
objectives; rather, they can reinforce each other when environmental practices are integrated
into innovation processes and corporate strategy (Lin et al., 2024).
Sustainability orientation is also increasingly relevant at the supply -chain level. A
systematic review of 67 recent scientific publications in the agrifood industry shows that
innovations such as precision farming, green technology, blockchain, the Internet of Things,
and artificial intelligence can improve operational efficiency, traceability, resource
management, food safety, and environmentally responsible practices. However, the success

of such transformation continues to depend on infrastructure readiness, human resource
capacity, and collaboration among supply -chain actors (Astrima et al., 2025) . Therefore,
environmental sustainability should not be understood solely as ecological responsibility but
also as a potential source of efficiency, differentiation, and business resilience.
Although previous literature has demonstrated the importance of innovation,
digitalization, and sustainability, earlier studies have tended to examine these dimensions
separately or in limited combinations. Annosi et al. (2024), for example, show that digital
transformation has created a new competitive environment in agribusiness that requires
strategic change, competency development, and the integration of economic, social, and
environmental concerns. At the same time, their s tudy highlights the need for a more
integrated research agenda linking digital transformation and agribusiness sustainability.
Research in Indonesia has also examined the effects of digital marketing and product
innovation on sustainable business performan ce in agribusiness enterprises, but its primary
focus remains on sustainable business performance and has not directly explained how
business innovation, digital marketing, and environmental sustainability jointly shape
agribusiness competitiveness (Fitri & Halik, 2023).
Based on this synthesis, three research gaps can be identified. First, previous studies
have more frequently examined innovation, digital marketing, and environmental
sustainability separately or in limited combinations. Second, relatively few studies have
simultaneously positioned these three constructs as determinants of agribusiness
competitiveness rather than merely business performance or business sustainability. Third,
empirical evidence concerning the relationship among these three factors in the con text of
agribusiness in Bireuen Regency remains limited. This gap is important because a strong
agricultural production base does not automatically guarantee the ability of business actors
to create added value, expand markets, and sustain their businesses  over time. Therefore, a
model is needed to explain the internal and strategic factors that can transform production
potential into business competitiveness.
The novelty of this study lies in the development of an agribusiness competitiveness
model that simultaneously integrates business innovation, digital marketing, and
environmental sustainability within the context of Bireuen Regency. The model does not
treat technology merely as a tool but positions innovation, digital marketing capability, and
environmental orientation as complementary capabilities that need to be developed in an

integrated manner. Theoretically, this study is grounded in the Resource-Based View (RBV)
and dynamic capabilities perspectives. These perspectives are relevant because competitive
advantage is determined not only by the ownership of resources but also by an organization’s
ability to recognize change, seize opportunities, and reconfigure resources in response to
changes in the business environment. Recent studies in the agrifood sector also show that
dynamic capabilities, particularly digital and analytical  capabilities, help firms respond to
digital transformation and exploit new opportunities more effectively (Ciasullo et al., 2026).
Based on these considerations, this study aims to analyze the effects of business
innovation, digital marketing, and environmental sustainability on agribusiness
competitiveness and to develop an empirical model of agribusiness competitiveness in
Bireuen R egency. This study is expected to extend the literature on agribusiness
competitiveness by integrating innovation capability, digital marketing capability, and
sustainability orientation within a single analytical framework. Practically, the findings are
expected to provide an empirical basis for agribusiness actors and local stakeholders in
designing business development strategies that are more adaptive to market changes, better
able to utilize digital technology, and more attentive to environmental sustainability.

METHODS
This study employed a quantitative approach with a cross-sectional survey design to
examine the effects of business innovation, digital marketing, and environmental
sustainability on agribusiness competitiveness. Data were collected at a single point in ti me
and analyzed using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM). This
method was selected because it is appropriate for examining relationships among latent
constructs while simultaneously evaluating the explanatory and predictive capabilities of the
endogenous construct (Hair & Alamer, 2022).
The study was conducted in Bireuen Regency, Aceh Province, Indonesia, in July
2026. The population consisted of agribusiness actors engaged in the production, processing,
trading, or marketing of agricultural products. A total of 80 respondents were selected using
purposive sampling. The respondent criteria were: (1) owners or managers of agribusiness
enterprises operating in Bireuen Regency; (2) directly involved in business management or
decision-making; (3) having operated the business for at least one y ear; and (4) willing to

participate in the study. These criteria were applied to ensure that the information was
obtained from business actors with direct experience in the agribusiness activities being
investigated.
The sample size of 80 respondents was determined by considering the structure of
the model, which consisted of three predictor constructs and one endogenous construct, as
well as statistical power requirements. Sample size determination in PLS -SEM should n ot
rely solely on simple rules such as the 10 -times rule but should also consider model
complexity, data characteristics, predictive objectives, and statistical adequacy. Guenther et
al. (2023) emphasized the importance of systematically justifying sample size and model
evaluation in business research using PLS -SEM. With three predictors, a 5% significance
level, 80% statistical power, and an assumed medium effect size (f² = 0.15), 80 respondent s
were retained as the final sample size.
Primary data were collected using a structured questionnaire developed based on the
conceptual and operational definitions of each construct. The questionnaire consisted of two
sections. The first section contained respondent and business characteristics, such as type of
agribusiness activity and length of business operation. The second section contained
statements measuring four research constructs: business innovation, digital marketing,
environmental sustainability, and agribusiness competitiveness. All items were measured
using a five-point Likert scale, ranging from 1 = strongly disagree, 2 = disagree, 3 = neutral,
4 = agree, to 5 = strongly agree.
The business innovation construct measured the ability of agribusiness actors to
introduce improvements in products, processes, services, and business management
methods. The digital marketing construct measured the ability to use social media, e -
commerce, digital promotion, and online communication to reach and retain customers.
Environmental sustainability covered resource -use efficiency, waste reduction,
environmentally friendly practices, and the management of business -related environmental
impacts. Agribusiness competitiveness measured the ability of firms to create value, maintain
their market position, respond to competition, expand market access, and sustain business
continuity.
The quality of the measurement model was evaluated through the outer model.
Internal consistency reliability was assessed using Cronbach’s alpha and composite reliability,
while convergent validity was evaluated using outer loadings and Average Variance Extracted

(AVE). Reliability values of ≥ 0.70  and AVE values of ≥ 0.50 were used as acceptance
criteria. This evaluation approach is consistent with confirmatory composite analysis
practices for assessing measurement quality in PLS-SEM (Hair et al., 2020).
Discriminant validity was assessed using the Heterotrait -Monotrait Ratio (HTMT).
An HTMT value below 0.85 was used as a conservative criterion to indicate adequate
discriminant validity among the constructs. The use of HTMT was based on its ability to
detect discriminant validity problems that may not always be identified through traditional
criteria such as the Fornell-Larcker criterion and cross-loadings (Henseler et al., 2015).
Data analysis was conducted using SmartPLS 4 in two stages. The first stage involved
evaluating the measurement model through construct reliability and validity. The second
stage involved evaluating the structural model through the assessment of the Varian ce
Inflation Factor (VIF), path coefficients, coefficient of determination (R²), effect size (f²),
and the model’s predictive relevance. The structural model was evaluated comprehensively
so that interpretation was not based solely on statistical significance but also on explanatory
quality and predictive capability, as recommended in PLS -SEM applications in business
research (Gudergan et al., 2025).
Hypothesis testing was conducted using a bootstrapping procedure to obtain path
coefficients, t-statistics, p-values, and confidence intervals. Relationships between constructs
were considered statistically significant at the 5% level (p < 0.05). In additi on to statistical
significance, the direction and magnitude of the path coefficients, R², and f² values were used
to assess the relative contribution of each predictor to agribusiness competitiveness. The
hypotheses tested included the effect of business i nnovation on agribusiness
competitiveness, the effect of digital marketing on agribusiness competitiveness, and the
effect of environmental sustainability on agribusiness competitiveness.
Before analysis, the data underwent editing, coding, screening, and cleaning
procedures to ensure completeness and response consistency. Incomplete responses or
responses that did not meet the research criteria were excluded from the analysis.
Participation was voluntary after respondents received information about the purpose of the
study. Respondent identities were kept confidential, and the research findings were reported
in aggregate form for academic purposes.

DISCUSSION
The results show that business innovation, digital marketing, and environmental
sustainability have positive and significant effects on agribusiness competitiveness in Bireuen
Regency. Together, the three constructs explain 40.6% of the variance in agribus iness
competitiveness. Among them, environmental sustainability has the largest path coefficient,
followed by business innovation and digital marketing. These findings indicate that
agribusiness competitiveness is determined not only by the ability to prod uce and market
products but also by the capacity of business actors to renew business practices, utilize
technology, and manage resources more sustainably. This result is consistent with Vahdanjoo
et al. (2025), who show that digital transformation and sustainability pressures have reshaped

the competitive environment in the agri-food sector and require more adaptive competencies
and strategies.
The positive effect of business innovation on agribusiness competitiveness indicates
that the ability to improve products, processes, services, and business management methods
can help agribusiness actors respond more effectively to market changes. Innovation enables
firms to create added value, improve efficiency, and differentiate their products from
competitors. In the context of Bireuen, this capability is particularly important because
increased production does not automatically lead to a stronger mark et position without
improvements in the way products are developed and marketed. This finding is consistent
with Sulemana et al. (2026), who found that entrepreneurial innovation and absorptive
capacity can strengthen business competitiveness. The result also supports the view that
competitive advantage develops when firms are able to use and renew their resources in
response to changes in the business environment.
Digital marketing also has a positive and significant effect on agribusiness
competitiveness, although its path coefficient is smaller than those of the other two
predictors. The use of social media, e -commerce, digital promotion, and online
communication helps business actors expand market reach, increase product visibility, and
build more direct relationships with consumers. However, its relatively smaller contribution
may be associated with the uneven ability of business actors to utilize digital technol ogy
optimally. Yelfiarita et al. (2025) show that digital marketing can expand markets and increase
the value of agricultural products, although its effectiveness remains influenced by digital
literacy, infrastructure, and technical assistance. Therefore, strengthening competitiveness
through digital marketing requires strategic capabilities in managing platforms, content, and
customer relationships rather than merely establishing a presence in digital media (Wulandari
et al., 2026).
The finding that environmental sustainability has the largest path coefficient indicates
that resource efficiency, waste reduction, and environmentally friendly business practices are
closely associated with agribusiness competitiveness. Sustainability pra ctices can help firms
reduce resource waste, improve efficiency, and create greater product value (Myshko et al.,
2024). This finding is in line with Fülöp (2025), who shows that sustainability-related factors
are associated with competitiveness and marketing performance among SMEs. In the
broader agri-food context, Paredes -Rodríguez et al. (2024) also show that innovation and

technology can support operational efficiency, traceability, and more sustainable supply -
chain management. Thus, environmental sustainability can be understood not only as an
ecological responsibility but also as part of a strategy to strengthen business e fficiency and
resilience (Cao & Tang, 2025).
Theoretically, the findings support the idea that agribusiness competitiveness is
shaped by a combination of complementary capabilities. Business innovation enables the
renewal of business activities, digital marketing strengthens the ability to reach and respond
to markets, while environmental sustainability supports long -term efficiency and business
resilience. This pattern is consistent with the Resource-Based View and dynamic capabilities
perspectives, which emphasize the importance of firms’ ability to  develop and reconfigure
resources when the business environment changes. Wang and Zhang (2025) show that digital
transformation can strengthen innovation capabilities and business performance, while
Wijayanto et al. (2024) found that digital marketing and related digital capabilities are
associated with market reach and competitiveness among Indonesian MSMEs. The present
study extends these findings by demonstrating that business innovation, digital marketing,
and environme ntal sustainability can be positioned simultaneously as determinants of
agribusiness competitiveness in a regional context.
From a practical perspective, strengthening agribusiness competitiveness in Bireuen
Regency should be directed toward the integrated development of innovation capacity, digital
marketing skills, and sustainable business practices. Agribusiness actors shoul d be
encouraged to differentiate their products, improve the use of digital platforms, and adopt
more efficient resource -management practices. Local governments and business -support
institutions can contribute through innovation training, digital literacy programs, branding
development, improved access to technology, and assistance in implementing sustainable
agribusiness practices.
This study has several limitations. It involved only 80 agribusiness actors in Bireuen
Regency and employed a cross-sectional design. In addition, the R² value of 0.406 indicates
that other factors outside the model may also explain agribusiness competitiv eness. Future
studies may therefore expand the geographical coverage and sample size, apply a longitudinal
design, and consider additional variables such as market orientation, access to finance, human
resource capability, institutional support, and digital capability.

CONCLUSION
This study demonstrates that business innovation, digital marketing, and
environmental sustainability have positive and significant effects on agribusiness
competitiveness in Bireuen Regency. Together, the three constructs explain 40.6% of the
variance in agribusiness competitiveness, with environmental sustainability showing the
strongest relationship. These findings indicate that improving agribusiness competitiveness
depends not only on production and marketing capabilities but also on the ability of business
actors to innovate, utilize digital technologies, and implement sustainable business practices.
The contribution of this study lies in the development of an agribusiness
competitiveness model that integrates business innovation, digital marketing, and
environmental sustainability within a single empirical framework. From a practical
perspective, the findings can provide a basis for agribusiness actors and local stakeholders to
strengthen innovation, improve digital marketing capabilities, and promote more efficient
and environmentally responsible agribusiness practices.
Future research is recommended to expand the geographical coverage and sample
size, employ longitudinal research designs, and consider additional variables such as market
orientation, access to finance, digital capability, human resource quality, and insti tutional
support.
