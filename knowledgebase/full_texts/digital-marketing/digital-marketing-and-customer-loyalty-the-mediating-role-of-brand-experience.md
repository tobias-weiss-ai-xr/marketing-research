Abstract
This study investigates the effect of digital marketing on customer loyalty, with emphasis on the
mediating role of brand experience in South-East Nigeria. Despite the growing adoption of digital
marketing, its ability to generate sustained customer loyalt y remains uncertain. Grounded in the
Stimulus–Organism–Response (S -O-R) framework, the study proposes that brand experience
serves as a key mechanism linking digital marketing efforts to customer loyalty. A quantitative
cross-sectional survey was conducted  using 385 respondents who actively engage with digital
platforms. Data were analyzed using Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) to examine direct and
indirect relationships among variables. The findings reveal that digital marketing significantly
enhances brand experience, while brand experience strongly predicts customer loyalty. Although
digital marketing also has a direct effect on loyalty, the effect is relatively weak compared to the
indirect effect through brand experience. Mediation analysis confirms that brand experience
partially mediates the relationship. The study concludes that customer loyalty is largely driven by
experiential and emotional engagement rather than mere exposure to digital marketing activities.

1. Introduction
In the contemporary digital economy, marketing has undergone significant transformation due to
advancements in information and communication technologies. Firms are increasingly shifting
from traditional marketing approaches to digital strategies that leve rage social media, mobile
technologies, big data analytics, and artificial intelligence to engage customers in real time. This
shift has redefined brand –consumer interactions, moving from one -way communication to
interactive, personalized, and experience -driven engagement (Dwivedi et al., 2023). As a result,
digital marketing has become a critical tool for building customer loyalty, which remains essential
for long-term business success and competitive advantage. However, emerging evidence suggests
that the relationship between digital marketing and customer loyalty is not always direct but is
often mediated by experiential and psychological factors such as brand experience and trust
(Gargun, 2025).  Globally, the proliferation of digital channels has intensi fied competition,
exposing consumers to numerous alternatives and increasing their expectations. Customers now
seek holistic brand experiences rather than mere product satisfaction, emphasizing emotional,
cognitive, and sensory interactions (Lemon & Verhoe f, 2016). Consequently, firms are adopting
experience-driven strategies such as personalization, storytelling, and interactive engagement to
build meaningful relationships.
In Africa, digital marketing adoption has grown rapidly due to increased internet penetration and
mobile usage. Despite these opportunities, its effectiveness in fostering customer loyalty is
influenced by contextual factors such as infrastructural challenges, cultural diversity, and varying
levels of digital literacy. African consumers tend to value trust, relationship quality, and social
influence, highlighting the importance of experiential and relational strategies in digital marketing.
In Nigeria, digital marketing has gained prominence with the rise of social media platforms such
as Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, and WhatsApp. While these platforms enhance brand visibility
and customer engagement, challenges such as inconsistent internet infrastructure, trust concerns,
and intense competition persist. Empirical evidence suggests that digital marketing alone is
insufficient to drive customer loyalty; instead, experiential factors such as emotional connection
and perceived value play a crucial role.
In South-East Nigeria, particularly in commercial hubs like Onitsha, businesses increasingly adopt
digital marketing tools to remain competitive. However, customer loyalty in this region is still
strongly influenced by traditional relationship -based practices, including trust and interpersonal
interactions. This creates a hybrid environment where digital marketing must be integrated with
experiential and relational approaches to be effective.
Given these dynamics, there is a need to examine how digital marketing influences customer
loyalty through the mediating role of brand experience. This study contributes to existing literature
by providing empirical evidence from an emerging market context and offers practical insights for
businesses seeking to enhance customer retention in a rapidly evolving digital landscape.

1.1 Statement of the Problem
Despite the widespread adoption of digital marketing globally, organizations continue to face
challenges in translating digital marketing efforts into sustained customer loyalty. While firms
invest significantly in social media marketing, content creation, and personalized communication,
these strategies often generate awareness and engagement without fostering the deeper emotional
connections required for long-term loyalty (Gargun, 2025; Dwivedi et al., 2023).
This challenge is more pronounced in developing economies, particularly in Africa, where factors
such as infrastructural limitations, varying levels of digital literacy, and trust concerns affect the
effectiveness of digital marketing. Although digital pla tforms provide opportunities for broader
market reach, their ability to drive customer loyalty remains inconsistent. African consumers are
generally relationship -oriented, placing high value on trust, perceived value, and experiential
satisfaction. However, many firms fail to incorporate these experiential elements into their digital
marketing strategies.
In Nigeria, despite the rapid growth in internet usage and social media engagement, businesses
often struggle to convert digital interactions into loyal customer relationships. Many organizations
focus on short -term promotional activities, neglecting the r ole of brand experience in fostering
emotional attachment and long-term commitment. As a result, customers may interact with brands
online but remain indifferent or easily switch to competitors.
This issue is particularly evident in commercial hubs such as Onitsha, where competition is intense
and customer expectations are evolving. While businesses increasingly adopt platforms like
WhatsApp, Facebook, and Instagram for marketing, customer loyalty  continues to be driven
largely by traditional factors such as trust and interpersonal relationships. This creates a disconnect
between digital marketing practices and loyalty outcomes.
Therefore, the core problem of this study lies in the weak and inconsistent relationship between
digital marketing and customer loyalty, largely due to the limited integration of experiential
factors. This study seeks to examine whether brand experience me diates this relationship,
particularly within the context of South-East Nigeria.

2.  Literature Review
2.1 Conceptual Review
2.1.1 Digital Marketing
Digital marketing has become a dominant paradigm in contemporary marketing, driven by the
digital transformation of economies and societies. It involves the strategic use of digital
technologies and platforms to create, communicate, and deliver value while  managing customer
relationships. Unlike traditional marketing, digital marketing is interactive, data -driven, and
customer-centric, enabling real -time engagement and personalized offerings (Dwivedi et al.,
2023). Digital marketing encompasses various tool s such as social media, search engine
optimization (SEO), email marketing, influencer marketing, and mobile marketing, which support
multichannel and omnichannel strategies. A key feature is its ability to leverage big data and
analytics, allowing firms to  understand consumer behavior and deliver personalized experiences
that enhance engagement and satisfaction. Additionally, digital platforms facilitate two -way
communication, enabling customers to actively participate in value co-creation through feedback,
reviews, and interactions.
Despite these advantages, the direct relationship between digital marketing and customer loyalty
remains limited. While digital marketing effectively drives awareness and engagement, loyalty is
largely influenced by deeper psychological and emotional facto rs such as trust and experience
(Gargun, 2025). Furthermore, information overload in digital environments makes it difficult for
brands to sustain customer attention.
From a theoretical perspective, digital marketing functions as a stimulus within the Stimulus–
Organism–Response (S -O-R) Theory , influencing consumer responses through internal
experiences. Thus, its effectiveness in driving loyalty depends on its ability to create meaningful,
personalized, and engaging brand experiences.

2.1.2 Brand Experience
Brand experience is a key construct in modern marketing, reflecting the shift from product -
centered to experience-driven approaches. It refers to consumers’ internal and subjective responses
to brand-related stimuli, encompassing sensory, affective, cognit ive, and behavioral dimensions
(Brakus et al., 2009). These experiences arise from interactions with various touchpoints such as
advertisements, websites, and social media platforms.
In digital environments, brand experience is strongly influenced by platform design, usability,
interactivity, and content quality. Features such as personalized recommendations and interactive
interfaces enhance customer engagement and shape perceptions o f the brand. Digital marketing
plays a crucial role in creating these experiences by enabling immersive, personalized, and
emotionally engaging interactions (Dwivedi et al., 2023).
Brand experience also serves as a key differentiator in competitive markets, where customers
increasingly prefer brands that offer memorable and meaningful interactions. Importantly, it is

2.1.3 Customer Loyalty
Customer loyalty is a central concept in marketing, reflecting a customer’s commitment to a brand
and willingness to maintain a long-term relationship through repeat purchase and recommendation.
It comprises two key dimensions: behavioral loyalty (repeat p atronage) and attitudinal loyalty
(emotional attachment and psychological commitment). While behavioral loyalty may be
influenced by situational factors such as price or convenience, attitudinal loyalty represents a
deeper and more enduring form of commitment.
In the digital era, customer loyalty has evolved beyond functional benefits to include experiential
and relational factors. The growth of digital platforms has made consumers more informed and
selective, increasing the importance of positive brand experien ces, authenticity, and value
alignment in driving loyalty (Gargun, 2025). Additionally, digital environments have expanded
loyalty to include engagement and advocacy, where customers actively promote brands through
reviews and social media interactions.
Brand experience plays a critical role in fostering loyalty by enhancing satisfaction, trust, and
emotional connection. Positive experiences strengthen customer relationships, while negative ones
may lead to switching behavior. Within the Stimulus–Organism–Response (S -O-R) Theory ,
customer loyalty represents the behavioral response resulting from the interaction between
marketing stimuli and internal psychological states.
Overall, customer loyalty is a multidimensional construct increasingly driven by experiential and
relational factors, highlighting the importance of brand experience in shaping long-term customer
behavior.

2.2 Theoretical Review
2.2.1 Stimulus–Organism–Response (S-O-R) Theory
The Stimulus–Organism–Response (S-O-R) Theory, developed by Albert Mehrabian and James
A. Russell  (1974), provides a psychological framework for understanding how environmental
stimuli influence behavior. The theory posits that external stimuli (S) affect an individual’s internal
state (O), which in turn leads to a behavioral response (R). In marketing, this framework explains
how marketing activities shape consumer emotions, perceptions, and actions.
In this study, digital marketing activities (e.g., social media engagement and personalized content)
represent the stimulus, brand experience serves as the organism, and customer loyalty constitutes
the response. This mapping highlights that consumer behavior is not a direct reaction to marketing
stimuli but is mediated by internal cognitive and emotional processes.
A key strength of the S -O-R theory is its ability to explain mediation, suggesting that digital
marketing influences loyalty indirectly through experiential and psychological mechanisms. This
is particularly relevant in digital environments, where interact ive platforms enhance engagement
and intensify consumer experiences.

2.2.2 Relationship Marketing Theory
The Relationship Marketing Theory, developed by Robert M. Morgan and Shelby D. Hunt (1994),
represents a shift from transactional to relational marketing by emphasizing long -term customer
relationships as a source of organizational success. The theory is anchored on two key constructs—
trust and commitment—which are essential for sustaining relationships. Trust reduces uncertainty
and perceived risk, while commitment fosters emotional attachment and long -term orientation,
ultimately driving customer loyalty (Morgan & Hunt, 1994).
In the digital marketing context, the theory has gained renewed relevance due to the interactive
nature of digital platforms. Social media, online communities, and personalized communication
enable continuous engagement, facilitating stronger relational bonds between firms and customers.
Within this framework, brand experience plays a critical role, as positive and consistent
experiences enhance trust and strengthen commitment, thereby increasing the likelihood of
customer loyalty.
Furthermore, the theory highlights the importance of customer retention in highly competitive
digital environments where switching costs are low. Firms that leverage digital marketing to create
personalized and value -driven interactions are better position ed to build enduring relationships.
Although the theory has been criticized for its limited consideration of technological
advancements, its core emphasis on trust, commitment, and relational exchange remains
fundamental in explaining customer loyalty in digital markets (Morgan & Hunt, 1994; Dwivedi et
al., 2023).

2.2.3 Customer Experience Theory
Customer Experience Theory provides a holistic perspective on how customers perceive and
evaluate their interactions with a brand. It emphasizes that customer experience is not confined to
a single transaction but encompasses the entire customer journey, i ncluding pre -purchase,
purchase, and post-purchase stages.
According to Lemon and Verhoef (2016), customer experience is a multidimensional construct
that integrates cognitive, emotional, social, and sensory responses. This perspective is particularly
relevant in the digital age, where customers interact with bran ds across multiple touchpoints,
including websites, mobile applications, social media platforms, and physical stores.
In digital marketing, customer experience is shaped by several key factors, including:  Usability
and interface design , Content relevance and quality , Interactivity and responsiveness ,
Personalization and customization
These factors collectively influence how customers perceive the brand and determine their level
of satisfaction and engagement. Positive experiences enhance customer satisfaction, build trust,
and foster emotional attachment, all of which are critical for developing loyalty.
An important contribution of Customer Experience Theory is its emphasis on experience as a
competitive differentiator. In markets where products and services are easily imitated, the
experience associated with a brand becomes a key source of differentiation. Firms that are able to
deliver superior experiences are more likely to attract and retain customers.

Within this study, brand experience is conceptualized as a subset of customer experience, focusing
specifically on brand -related interactions. While customer experience encompasses the entire
journey, brand experience captures the immediate sensory, emotio nal, cognitive, and behavioral
responses to brand stimuli. This distinction is important, as it allows for a more focused analysis
of how digital marketing influences consumer perceptions and behaviors.
However, one limitation of Customer Experience Theory is that it may be difficult to measure and
manage due to its subjective and dynamic nature. Customer experiences vary across individuals
and contexts, making it challenging for firms to design standardi zed strategies. Despite this, the
theory remains highly relevant for understanding the role of experience in shaping customer
loyalty.

2.2.4 Integration and Synthesis of Theories
The integration of S -O-R Theory, Relationship Marketing Theory, and Customer Experience
Theory provides a comprehensive and multidimensional framework for analyzing the relationship
between digital marketing, brand experience, and customer loyalty.The S-O-R theory explains the
psychological process through which digital marketing influences behavior. The Relationship
Marketing Theory emphasizes the importance of trust, commitment, and long-term relationships.
The Customer Experience Theory highlights the role of experiential interactions across the
customer journey.
Together, these theories suggest that digital marketing influences customer loyalty through a
complex interplay of psychological, experiential, and relational mechanisms. Digital marketing
acts as a stimulus that shapes brand experience, which in turn enha nces trust and emotional
attachment, ultimately leading to customer loyalty.
This integrated perspective addresses the limitations of individual theories by combining their
strengths and providing a more holistic understanding of consumer behavior in digital
environments.

2.2.5 Theoretical Gap and Justification for the Study
Despite the extensive application of these theories in marketing research, several gaps remain.
First, most studies have been conducted in developed economies, where digital infrastructure,
consumer behavior, and market dynamics differ significantly from t hose in developing regions
such as Nigeria. This limits the generalizability of existing findings.
Second, while previous research has examined mediating variables such as trust and engagement,
limited attention has been given to brand experience as a central mediating construct, particularly
in emerging markets and SME contexts. This represents a signi ficant gap, as brand experience
integrates multiple dimensions of consumer response and provides a more comprehensive
explanation of how digital marketing influences loyalty.
Third, there is a need to examine how these theoretical frameworks interact within specific local
contexts, such as South-East Nigeria, where traditional relationship-based commerce coexists with
emerging digital practices.
Therefore, this study seeks to address these gaps by integrating the three theoretical perspectives
and empirically investigating the mediating role of brand experience in the relationship between
digital marketing and customer loyalty. This contributes to  both theoretical advancement and
practical understanding of digital marketing effectiveness in emerging markets.

2.3 Empirical Review
Gargun (2025) conducted a comprehensive study on small businesses and found that digital
marketing significantly enhances customer interaction and engagement. However, the study
revealed that the direct effect of digital marketing on customer loyalty is of ten weak or
insignificant, suggesting that other variables play a mediating role. These finding challenges earlier
assumptions that digital marketing alone is sufficient to drive loyalty.
Similarly, Dwivedi et al. (2023) emphasized that digital marketing strategies such as social media
marketing, content marketing, and personalized communication improve customer engagement
but do not necessarily translate directly into loyalty. Their findin gs indicate that engagement and
experience act as intervening variables that strengthen the relationship.
In another empirical study, Zhang and Mun (2025) examined the coffee shop industry and found
that digital marketing strategies significantly influence customer loyalty, but the relationship
becomes stronger when mediated by customer engagement. This suggests that digital marketing is
more effective when it fosters meaningful interactions rather than merely promoting products.
Collectively, these studies indicate that while digital marketing is an important driver of customer
engagement and awareness, its direct impact on loyalty is limited. This underscores the need to
explore mediating variables such as brand experience.
Dwivedi et al. (2023) found that digital marketing significantly enhances brand experience through
personalization, interactivity, and content quality. Their study showed that consumers who engage
with personalized and interactive digital content report higher levels of satisfaction and emotional
connection with the brand.
Similarly, recent research indicates that social media marketing activities—such as user-generated
content, influencer marketing, and interactive campaigns  play a crucial role in enhancing brand
experience. These activities create opportunities for consumers to actively participate in the brand
experience, thereby strengthening their emotional attachment.
Another study focusing on online communities found that digital interactions within brand
communities significantly influence consumers’ perceptions of the brand, leading to enhanced
brand experience. This highlights the role of community -based engagement in shaping consumer
experiences.
Wijaya and Simamora (2023) found that customer experience significantly influences brand trust
and customer loyalty. Their study revealed that positive experiences enhance trust and emotional
attachment, which in turn lead to loyalty. This highlights the r ole of experience as a driver of
relational outcomes.

2.3.5 Research Gap
Despite the extensive empirical evidence, several gaps remain:
Geographical Gap:  Most studies have been conducted in developed countries, with limited
research in African contexts, particularly Nigeria.
Contextual Gap: There is limited research focusing on SMEs and informal business sectors, which
are dominant in regions like South-East Nigeria.
Conceptual Gap: While many studies examine engagement and trust as mediators, fewer studies
focus specifically on brand experience as a central mediating variable.
Integrated Framework Gap:  There is a lack of studies that integrate multiple theoretical
perspectives (S-O-R, relationship marketing, and experience theory) in a single model.

2.4 Conceptual Framework
The conceptual framework of this study illustrates the relationship between digital marketing,
brand experience, and customer loyalty, with brand experience serving as a mediating variable.
The framework is grounded in the integration of Stimulus –Organism–Response (S-O-R) Theory,
Relationship Marketing Theory, and Customer Experience Theory, which collectively explain how
external marketing activities influence internal psychological states and behavioral outcomes.

The conceptual framework illustrates both the direct and indirect relationships among digital
marketing, brand experience, and customer loyalty. The direct path represents the influence of
digital marketing on customer loyalty, indicating that digital mark eting activities can
independently drive repeat patronage and customer commitment. However, this relationship is not
sufficient on its own to fully explain customer loyalty in the digital environment.
The framework also presents an indirect (mediated) relationship in which digital marketing
influences customer loyalty through brand experience. This pathway demonstrates that digital
marketing enhances customer loyalty by creating meaningful and engaging experiences for
customers. In this context, brand experience serves as a critical intermediary that translates
marketing efforts into behavioral outcomes.
Furthermore, brand experience functions as the internal psychological mechanism through which
customers interpret and respond to digital marketing stimuli. It encompasses emotional, cognitive,
and behavioral responses that arise from interactions with digi tal platforms. These experiential
responses shape customers’ perceptions of the brand and ultimately determine their level of
loyalty. Thus, the framework emphasizes that while digital marketing acts as the stimulus,
customer loyalty is largely driven by the quality of brand experience formed during the interaction
process.

marketing, brand experience, and customer loyalty, with particular emphasis on the mediating role
of brand experience. A cross -sectional survey design was employe d to collect data from
respondents at a single point in time, which is appropriate for analyzing consumer perceptions and
behavioral intentions.
The target population consists of customers who actively engage with businesses through digital
platforms such as social media, websites, and mobile applications in South-East Nigeria. A sample
size of 385 respondents was selected based on recommended guid elines for Structural Equation

4.7 Discussion of Findings
The findings reveal that digital marketing significantly influences brand experience, indicating that
interactive and personalized marketing strategies enhance customer perceptions and engagement.
This supports previous studies , which highlight the importance of digital marketing in shaping
customer experience (Dwivedi et al., 2023).
The strong relationship between brand experience and customer loyalty suggests that experiential
factors play a critical role in driving customer behavior. Customers who have positive emotional
and cognitive experiences with a brand are more likely to deve lop loyalty, as reflected in repeat
purchases and recommendations (Gargun, 2025).
The relatively weaker direct effect of digital marketing on customer loyalty further confirms that
loyalty is not solely driven by marketing exposure but by the quality of experience associated with
the brand. This finding aligns with the Stimulus –Organism–Response (S -O-R) theory, which
emphasizes that behavioral responses are mediated by internal psychological states.
The mediation analysis confirms that brand experience is a key mechanism through which digital
marketing influences customer loyalty. This highlights the importance of integrating experiential
elements into digital marketing strategies.
