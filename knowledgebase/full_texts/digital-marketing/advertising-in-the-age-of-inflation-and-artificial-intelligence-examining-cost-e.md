The global advertising system is increasingly shaped by two concurrent
pressures: persistent inflation and the rapid integration of generative artificial
intelligence (GenAI) into advertising production and media management. This
study examines the trade -offs between advertising cost efficiency, consumer
perceptions, and downstream communication effectiveness using Transaction
Cost Economics, the Persuasion Knowledge Model, Signaling Theory, and Mental
Accounting Theory. Study 1 applies panel econometric mo deling to data from
184 advertising agencies and marketing enterprises to assess production and
media-placement efficiency across different Consumer Price Index (CPI)
regimes. Study 2 employs a 2 × 2 between -subjects experiment (N = 512)
comparing Human-Created and Disclosed GenAI advertisements under neutral
and high -inflation salience conditions. Study 1 shows that GenAI adoption
reduces production costs by 44.6%, although higher inflation weakens the
acquisition-efficiency benefits of predictive advertis ing. Study 2 shows that AI
disclosure increases perceived manipulative intent and reduces perceived
authenticity, particularly when inflation salience is high, with adverse effects on
brand trust and purchase intention. Overall, the findings provide an AI -enabled
managerial decision -support perspective for balancing production efficiency,
acquisition performance, and consumer trust when making advertising
resource-allocation and human–AI deployment decisions.

Macroeconomic uncertainty and technology change are dominating the contemporary marketing
environment. The world has been facing inflation for a while and that trend has had a significant
impact on consumer spending, cutting into profits for companies and reducing consumer
discretionary spending worldwide. This in turn puts unprecedented pressure on chief marketing
officers (CMOs) to optimize their marketing ROI (ROAS) and reduce CPA (cost-per-acquisition). At the
same time, democratizing automated algorithmic media management has transformed commercial
asset production with generative artificial intelligence (AI). Algorithmic systems can now generate
hyper-personalised copy, close-to-real visuals and synthetic brand avatars at the cost of almost a
penny, revolutionising traditional advertising production processes.

However, there is an unanswered behavioral and economic paradox with this technological paradigm
shift. On the supply side, companies see GenAI as a deflationary tool to counteract inflationary
pressures on their operations by automating tasks. On the dema nd side, price -sensitive consumers
become more vigilant about corporate persuasion and value. Advertisements generated by AI systems
or algorithmic prompts may lack the perceived authenticity and emotional authenticity of the original
commercial messages, as well as any sense of brand authenticity.
In the past, an expensive signaling theory argued that a high production value advertisement served as
an intangible "red flag" that the company was strong, product quality was good and long -term brand
value was high. If the psychological ‘signal' is low c ost in the sense of near zero creative production,
does it lose psychological credibility? Moreover, with the emergence of regulatory standards (e.g., the
European Union AI Act and the Federal Trade Commission requirements in the U.S.) requiring that
synthetic commercial media carry labels with algorithmic information, how are consumers going to
interpret algorithmic origin labels within limited family budgets?
Although industry discussion is plentiful, there are no consolidated academic studies. The existing
literature focuses on studying the organizational and algorithmic optimization potential of AI in digital
media buying or the cognitive reactions to AI -generated creative assets under stable macroeconomic
conditions, respectively. The study of the relationship between macroeconomic conditions (such as
financial vulnerability resulting from inflation) and technological origin (human -made vs. GenAI
commercial content) has been largely under-researched.
This study aims to fill this gap by posing three major research questions:
RQ1: What are the effects of the use of GenAI creative pipelines and predictive algorithmic media
buying on the cost efficiency of advertising campaigns at the firm level in different macroeconomic
inflationary periods?
RQ2: Does the deliberate provision of generative AI creative provenance affect consumers' ideas of
authenticity and manipulative intent and value?
RQ3: Does financial salience due to inflation moderate the psychological mechanisms between AI
disclosures and consumer brand trust and end-of-pipe intentions?
This paper proposes an integrated theoretical framework and tests it using a multi-method design that
combines an archival -econometric analysis of agency operational data (Study 1) with a randomized
2 × 2 factorial behavioral experiment (Study 2). From a decision -making perspective, the study treats
GenAI adoption as an organizational resource -allocation problem in which firms must balance
production efficiency against downstream consumer -response risks. B y linking firm -level cost and
acquisition outcomes with experimentally observed consumer reactions, the framework supports
managerial decisions concerning the intensity, timing, and communication of AI use in advertising
under different inflationary conditions.
2. Theoretical Background and Hypotheses Formulation
2.1 Theoretical Foundations
Transaction Cost Economics (TCE)
Under the premise of Transaction Cost Economics, governance mechanisms are organised within the
firm to reduce search costs, contracting costs, coordination costs and asset specificity costs. There is a
lot of friction and high transaction costs involved in agency contract ing, physical photography, artistic
revision and human copywriting in conventional creative production. Generative AI fundamentally
changes this operational model and renders this creative work an asset specific operation that
becomes internalised and on-demand automated output. In times of inflation, when production factors
go up, GenAI becomes a structural transaction cost mitigation.
The Persuasion Knowledge Model (PKM)
Persuasion Knowledge Model (PKM) by Friestad and Wright suggests that consumers store common
knowledge about the agents of persuasion, their company's agendas and the manner used to
communicate persuasive messages. Persuasion knowledge is activated when ta rgets interpret a
received message as an intentional effort to persuade them to take some sort of action, thus triggering

defense cognitive coping mechanisms. When AI-generated ads are explicit, consumers draw inferences
that the advertiser is using a strategy that requires a calculation, a synthetic approach, or a cost-saving
strategy, but is not actually making a real human effort or investment in the relationship.
Signaling Theory
The signaling theory suggests that in the information -asymmetry environment, consumers use the
information they cannot observe to judge the quality of the product and reliability of the company.
Over the years, high expenditure advertising campaigns have b een a "money-burning" indicator: If a
brand will spend millions on high -end creative talent and medium, it meant that it had confidence in
itself. GenAI democratizes high fidelity visual and textual sophistication, making it more accessible to
all. Thus, when it is discovered that an ad is machine-made, the communication signal in the ad changes
from a quality and stability indicator to a less clear signal of margin preservation and cost reduction.
The theory of mental accounting and scarcity theory.
Thaler's Mental Accounting Theory and Mullainathan and Shafir's Scarcity Theory give insight into the
scarcity effect on cognitive bandwidth. In times of high inflation, consumers put more emphasis on
cognitive limits when budgeting, causing increased pain  of paying. In such high -strain environments,
consumers grow more vigilant and suspicious of company pricing honesty, profit margins and
exploitation.

2.3 Hypotheses Development
The challenges of Production Efficiency and Macroeconomic Volatility.
The operational cycles, asset revision iterations and physical staging expenses are significantly
shortened by GenAI creative automation. Algorithmic predictive bidding platforms not only automate
real-time programmatic ad placements, they  also do this in real -time. But in inflationary times,
aggregate consumer spending power decreases, leading to lower CTRs and conversion rates. While AI
helps reduce asset creation costs, it has a macroeconomic headwind when it comes to increasing
acquisition efficiency of media. Thus:

• H1: The adoption of GenAI in creative workflows has a significant and positive effect on firm
production cost efficiencies, but the production cost efficiencies of consumer acquisition costs
(CPA) are negatively moderated by macroeconomic inflation rates.

Provenance Disclosure, Authenticity, and Persuasion Knowledge
Commercial authenticity is a form of human intentionality, true artistic engagement and cultural
resonance to the consumer. Consumer awareness of the AI -generated content effects the
"craftsmanship" and intent behind it, removing the human element from the  artifact. Under the
influence of the PKM, consumers think that the use of synthetic assets is a business equation to reduce
operating costs.
• H2: If you disclose that content is commercial and is generated by AI, then it is less likely to be
perceived as authentic (H2a) and more likely to be perceived as manipulative (H2b).

The role of inflationary salience as a moderator of negative behavior
In a salient inflation environment, consumers feel vulnerable which leaves them vulnerable to
corporate pricing practices and honesty. When a consumer in a severe financial situation sees a
corporation using artificial, automated advertising, attribution t heory implies he will think the same
way as the one who conducted the advertising, which, in this instance, is to cut costs and boost
margins, not deliver value to customers. However, if the overall economic climate is neutral, consumers
can be fascinated or be something fresh with AI-driven advertising.
• H3: There is a moderating effect between AI disclosure and consumer cognitive responses: The
negative effect of AI disclosure on perceived authenticity ( H3a) and the positive effect of AI
disclosure on perceived manipulative intent (H3b) are stronger when the salience of inflation is
high.

Downstream Behavioral Consequences (Moderated Mediation)
It is found in advertising literature that brand perceived authenticity positively influences brand trust,
perceived value and purchase intent, while perceived manipulative intent generates cognitive
resistance and avoidance. These effects combine to create a conditional indirect effect:
• H4: The indirect negative effects of disclosure of AI on brand trust (H4a) and purchase intention
(H4b) are conditionally mediated by perceived authenticity and perceived manipulative intent,
both through parallel paths.
Collectively, these hypotheses operationalize the managerial decision problem as a trade-off between
AI-enabled cost efficiency and consumer-response risk under different macroeconomic conditions.

3. Study 1: Supply-Side AI Adoption and Operational Decision Efficiency
3.1 Sample and Data Collection
Study 1 explores the cost structure of organizations, creative asset workflows and campaign results
before and after the adoption of generative AI tools. The multi -wave panel data was gathered from
𝑁 = 184 independent and holding -company digital advertising agencies and corporate marketing
departments (in -house) in North America, Europe and the Asia -Pacific region. The data covered 16
quarters (from Q1 2021 to Q2 2024), reflecting pre-GenAI baseline conditions, early adoption of GenAI
as well as macroeconomic inflationary shocks.
Financial data was pulled using standardised and anonymised enterprise enterprise resource planning
(ERP) exports, as well as campaign performance dashboards (Google Ads, Meta Ads Manager,
programmatic DSPs). The sample was spread across a variety of sectors: Fast-Moving Consumer Goods
(32.6%), Consumer Technology (24.5%), Retail & E-Commerce (27.2%) and Financial Services (15.7%).

4. Study 2: Demand-Side Behavioral Decision Responses to AI Disclosure
Study 2 aims to understand the micro-level psychological mechanisms behind the demand contraction
found in Study 1 by exploring how consumers cognitively and emotionally process synthetic
commercial content with different macroeconomic conditions.
4.1 Experimental Design and Method
Participants and Power Analysis
Thus, a statistical power analysis using 𝐺 ∗ Power 3.1 for a 2 × 2 factorial ANOVA  ( 𝑓 = 0.16, 𝛼 =
.05,1 − 𝛽 = .95, 𝑑𝑓 = 1,number of groups = 4) revealed that a minimum of 𝑁 = 478 was required.
𝑁 = 540 adult consumers from the U.S. were recruited from Prolific Academic for adequate sample

Experimental Design and Manipulations
2(Advertising Production Origin:Human Baseline vs. Disclosed GenAI) ×
2(Macroeconomic Inflationary Salience:Neutral Baseline vs. High Inflation Salience) between-
subjects factorial design was used in the experiment.
1. Macroeconomic Context Priming: The participants were randomly assigned to read a fake
newspaper article from a well-known business newspaper.
• High Inflation Salience Condition: The piece covered the rising Consumer Price Index (CPI) rates,
which have reduced the purchasing power of households, the increasing prices of daily
necessities and the diminishing of real wages.
• Neutral Baseline Condition: The  article contented general neutral consumer lifestyle and
packaging sustainability trends; no mention of price, inflation or economic pressure.
2. Advertising Stimuli and Disclosure Manipulation:  Next, participants were shown an advertisement
for a made-up consumer electronics company ("AuraSound ANC Headphones") in the form of a digital
display ad with advertising stimuli and disclosure manipulation. All visual material and copy were kept
completely the same between the two conditions and were pre -tested to establish high baseline
aesthetic similarity (𝑀 = 5.62 on a 7-point scale).
• Disclosed GenAI Condition: There is a standardised regulatory disclosure label prominently
displayed in the upper right quadrant: "Notice: Visual imagery and advertising copy synthesized
using Generative Artificial Intelligence.
• Human Baseline Condition: The same layout shows a standard production agency logo
“Designed and photographed by human creative directors at Horizon Creative.”

5. General Discussion
5.1 Synthesis of Findings
The multi -method research provides two complementary insights. Study 1 results showed that
generative AI is an excellent operational deflationary tool for generating creative assets, cutting
production costs by an average of 44.6%, but its media acquisitio n efficiency (CPA) is limited by the
macroeconomic factors. In the inflationary context, enterprise algorithmic optimization is fighting
against the minimum decreases in consumer spending.
In Study 2, researchers found that when consumers feel economically strained as a result of  inflation,
it triggers a defense against the persuasive content that is AI-generated. In the era of high inflation, AI
disclosures are not seen as objective technological indicators. Rather, they view them as signals of the
company's cost -cutting, thereby  increasing their perceived manipulative intent and decreasing
perceived authenticity. This psychological response negatively affects the confidence level of the brand
and purchase intentions, but only as a condition. Taken together, the two studies show t hat the AI -
adoption decision is conditional rather than purely efficiency driven: firms may realize production
savings while simultaneously facing weaker acquisition efficiency and more adverse consumer
responses when inflationary pressure is salient. This  creates a managerial decision problem requiring
simultaneous consideration of operational efficiency and consumer-facing effectiveness.
5.2 Theoretical Contributions
The study contributes to AI -enabled managerial decision -making, marketing, and communication
research in four ways:
1. Bridging Macroeconomics and Marketing Technology: Prior AI advertising  scholarship has been
mostly macroeconomic in isolation of broader economics and algorithms. This paper combines the
theories of Transaction Cost Economics and Scarcity Theory and sets the stage that macroeconomic
inflation is an important boundary conditi on that moderates consumer acceptance of synthetic
commercial communication.
2. Extension of the Persuasion Knowledge Model (PKM): Extending the PKM to the generative
computing era. This study shows that the disclosure of the source of a product in an algorithmic context
elicits more deep -rooted suspicions of operational shortcut -taking and opportunistic manipulation
than that of a human influencer context, which previous research has shown leads to persuasion
knowledge. Provenance transparency in algorithmic generation leads to more deep-rooted suspicions
of operational shortcut-taking and manipulative opportunism than human influencer contexts, which
previous studies have shown cause persuasion knowledge.
3. Reframing Costly Signaling Theory: In classical advertising theory , the amount of creative
sophistication and production value were reliable indicators of brand commitment and product quality.
Our results show that consumers' awareness of AI -generated content is sufficient to break away the

4. AI Adoption as an Efficiency –Effectiveness Decision Trade -off: The findings show that AI adoption

by adverse consumer perceptions and weaker downstream e ffectiveness, particularly when
inflationary pressure is salient. This reframes GenAI adoption as a multi -criteria managerial decision

5.3 Managerial Decision-Support and Regulatory Implications
The combined evidence from Studies 1 and 2 can be translated into practical decision-support logic for
advertising managers, as illustrated in Figure 2. Under inflationary pressure, the relevant managerial
question is not simply whether AI should be adopte d, but how intensively it should be deployed and
which advertising functions should remain human -led. The  findings therefore provide a basis for
balancing AI -enabled efficiency with acquisition performance, authenticity, trust, and consumer
response when selecting advertising-production strategies.

1. During economic downturns, avoid the "Efficiency Trap": When times are tough, enterprise leaders
need to refrain from cutting human creative teams, instead. Although production savings may be easy
to come by, it could damage the overall campaign ROI and conversions, particularly in inflationary
times, if consumers perceive that the intent of manipulation is there.
2. Hybrid Co-Creation Models: Hybrid models of co-creation are needed for brands that are mandated
to disclose synthetic assets under new regulatory requirements, such as the EU AI Act or the FTC. It
helps to defuse authenticity erosion to emphasize that assets were " Designed by Human Directors in

3. Utility -Oriented Advertising in Difficult Economies: when conditions  are tough, brand messaging
based on fictionalized personas should shift focus to more utilitarian ad messaging. Rather, brands
should leverage AI to deliver real-world value to the consumer, including personalized pricing, visibility
into the supply chain and localized promotions.
5.4 Limitations and Future Research Directions
There are several restrictions that allow for further research. First, Study 2 assessed an FMCG /
Consumer Electronics scenario, while future studies could examine other luxury branding or
professional services / healthcare areas where the consequences of using AI-generated content may
be greater. Second, we employed static display advertising in our experimental design and future
research should focus on conversational AI, synthetic video avatars and real -time voice synthesis.
Lastly, longitudinal research  is required to determine if the skepticism of consumers in regards to AI
disclosures will fade as AI generative technology becomes a common part of everyday digital existence.

6. Conclusion
This study examined how GenAI adoption interacts with inflationary conditions to shape both the
operational efficiency and downstream effectiveness of advertising. Study 1 showed substantial
production-cost savings associated with GenAI adoption while indicating that inflation can reduce the
acquisition-efficiency benefits of AI -enabled advertising. Study 2 showed that disclosed AI -generated
advertising can reduce perceived authenticity and increase perceived manipulative intent, with these
responses becomi ng stronger when inflation is salient and subsequently affecting brand trust and
purchase intention. Taken together, the findings position GenAI adoption as a conditional managerial
decision rather than a purely technological or cost -saving choice. The study contributes an integrated
AI-enabled decision-support perspective linking macroeconomic context, operational efficiency, and
consumer response. For managers, the findings support evaluating human, AI, and hybrid advertising
configurations against both e fficiency and market -response considerations. Future research should
test this decision logic across sectors, advertising formats, and longitudinal settings.
