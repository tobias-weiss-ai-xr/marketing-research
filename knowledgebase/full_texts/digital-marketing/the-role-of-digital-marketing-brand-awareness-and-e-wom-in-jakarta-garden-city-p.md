 Background: The rapid growth of the Indonesian property industry has
increased competitive pressure among developers, requiring effective
digital strategies to strengthen consumer purchase intention. However,
digital marketing exposure alone is insufficient to drive p roperty
purchase decisions because consumer trust, brand awareness, and
engagement influence the decision-making process.
Objective: This study aims to examine the effects of Digital Marketing,
Brand Awareness, and Electronic Word of Mouth (E-WOM) on Purchase
Intention at Jakarta Garden City, with Consumer Engagement as a
mediating variable and Consumer Trust as a moderating variable.
Methods: This study employed a quantitative cross -sectional survey
design involving 375 respondents selected through purposive sampling
from Jakarta Garden City’s digital consumer database. Data were
collected using a structured questionnaire and analyzed using Structural
Equation Modeling–Partial Least Squares (SEM-PLS) with SmartPLS 4.
Results: The findings indicate that Digital Marketing and Brand
Awareness significantly and positively affect Purchase Intention, with
Brand Awareness showing the strongest direct influence. E -WOM has a
significant negative effect, indicating consumer skepticism  toward
online reviews in high-value property decisions. Consumer Engagement
mediates the relationship between Digital Marketing and Purchase
Intention, while Consumer Trust significantly strengthens the effect of
Digital Marketing on Consumer Engagement.
Conclusion: This study highlights the importance of integrating digital
marketing strategies with brand strengthening, consumer trust
development, and meaningful engagement to increase property
purchase intention. The findings are limited to a single property
developer context, which may affect generalizability.

INTRODUCTION
Indonesia's property industry is experiencing massive growth, especially in the Greater
Jakarta area, in line with the increasing demand for housing from young professional groups and
young families  (Abidoye et al., 2021) . This condition not only presents great opportunities for
property developers, but  also creates increasingly complex and fierce competition. Based on
Statista data (2023), Indonesia's property industry is dominated by several large developers with
very significant total assets, such as PT Bumi Serpong Damai Tbk (66.8 trillion rupiah), P T Lippo
Karawaci Tbk (49.5 trillion rupiah), and PT Ciputra Development Tbk (44.1 trillion rupiah).
Meanwhile, PT. Modernland Realty Tbk. as the developer of Jakarta Garden City has not been

included in the list of the 10 largest developers in Indonesia, so the pressure to optimize marketing
strategies is becoming more urgent. The competitive environment requires property developers
to understand not only promotional effectiveness but also the  psychological mechanisms that
transform digital exposure into actual purchase intention  (Prasad, 2026). Therefore, examining
the interaction between digital marketing capability, brand -related factors, electronic word of
mouth, trust, and engagement becomes strategically important in understanding contemporary
property consumer behavior.
In response to these competitive dynamics, Jakarta Garden City implements an integrated
marketing strategy that includes offline (walk-in, call-in, exhibition, open booth, canvassing) and
online activities (Instagram, TikTok, website, and personal social m edia sales). The company's
internal data in 2025 shows that out of a total of 5,957 consumer databases, digital sources will
be the largest contributor, far surpassing other sources such as exhibitions (2,717 data), weekend
open house activities (409), per sonal social media sales (372), and referrals (156). These initial
findings provide a strong empirical indication that the digital marketing strategy implemented
plays a crucial role in attracting potential customers for Jakarta Garden City.
However, the comparative data on the percentage of qualified chats between Jakarta
Garden City and similar business units owned by PT. Modernland Realty Tbk ., namely Kota
Modern, shows that the quality of Jakarta Garden City's digital marketing is consistently lower
throughout 2025. The percentage of qualified chats in Jakarta Garden City ranges from 35%-67%,
while Modern City reaches 45% -73%. This difference  indicates a gap in content suitability, ad
target accuracy, and integration across digital platforms. This condition is an important strategic
evaluation for the management of PT. Modernland Realty Tbk., especially the Jakarta Garden City
division. This difference may influence purchase intention because digital marketing effectiveness
is not merely determined by the quantity of consumer interactions, but also by the ability of firms
to create relevant, credible, and persuasive information that supports consumers throughout the
property decision-making process.
In terms of digital consumer engagement, Instagram Jakarta Garden City has an
engagement rate of 0.78% with 12,559 followers, while TikTok reaches 4.75% with 11,200
followers. Although both are above the industry average according to Social Insider (2026),  the
pattern of passive engagement identified where consumers read more content without actively
interacting is a challenge in converting engagement into purchase intent. In addition, the
condition of the Electronic Word of Mouth (E -WOM) Jakarta Garden Cit y deserves special
attention. Google Review data shows an overall rating of 4.5 out of 5 stars, but in the past year
there has been an increase in 1 -star reviews with negative sentiment related to the quality of
infrastructure, buildings, and services that  have the potential to progressively lower the
company's digital reputation. These conditions demonstrate that digital engagement and
electronic word of mouth represent interconnected dimensions of consumer perception, where
online interaction quality and public evaluations can jointly shape consumer confidence before
purchasing high-involvement products such as property.
On the other hand, Jakarta Garden City received various national awards in the period
2021-2024, including Best Township Development from the PropertyGuru Indonesia Property
Awards (2024), Best Mid End Housing Development, and Best Regional Scale Development Region
Jakarta from the Golden Property Awards (2023). These awards represent brand reputation and
consumer trust assets that should be used as competitive differentiators in digital
communications, but so far have not been optimally optimized.
From an academic perspective, there has been no research that comprehensively
integrates the variables of digital marketing, brand awareness, E-WOM, consumer trust, consumer
engagement, and purchase intention in one integrated model in the context of the I ndonesian
property industry. Research b y Sari (2025) discusses E -WOM and media marketing efforts in
apartment purchase decisions without including brand awareness and consumer engagement.
Zwakala & Church (2025) examines digital marketing strategies in the Cape Town property
market from the perspective of agents, not consumers.  Windi (2025) tested social media
marketing on trust and intention to buy housing in Bekasi, but without a consumer engagement
component as a mediator. These three studies leave a research gap that is the main justification
for this research. The novelty of this study lies in integrating six constructs within a single
empirical model and examining the property purchasing context from the consumer perspective,

while previous studies have generally examined only partial relationships among these variables.
Yunishafira et al. (2025) found that social media marketing dimensions significantly
influence brand trust and brand loyalty through consumer engagement mechanisms. Their
findings indicate that consistent, authentic, and responsive digital communication strengthens
consumer relatio nships with brands. This evidence supports the relevance of examining
engagement and trust as important mechanisms in the digital purchase decision process.
Research by Romadhoni et al. (2023) states that eWOM  and SMM do not directly affect
Purchase Intent. However, both significantly affect Purchase Intent indirectly through Customer
Satisfaction. In contrast, ICT Effectiveness and Support do not significantly moderate the
relationship between Customer Satisfaction and Purchase Intent. This study concludes that digital
marketing strategies are only effective in influencing Purchase Intent when mediated by
Customer Satisfaction. This highlights the important role of satisfaction in the digital buying
journey, es pecially on high -engagement products such as fitness equipment. Meanwhile,
(Ratnawia et al., 2022) found that the influence of social media, marketing, and brand awareness
on purchase decisions had a determination coefficient of 0.771 or 77.1%. While the remaining
22.9% (100-77.1) was caused by variables outside the study.
Research conducted by  Faronsyah & Taufik Hidayah (2025) shows that social media
marketing has a positive effect on purchase intention. In addition, brand trust and brand
engagement play a role as mediators that strengthen the relationship, while brand awareness has
not been proven to have a mediating effect. T hese results indicate that marketing strategies
through social media are more effective in increasing purchase intent if they can build consumer
trust and engagement with brands.
Based on the above background, this study is designed to test the influence of Digital
Marketing, Brand Awareness, and E -WOM on Purchase Intention in Jakarta Garden City with
Consumer Engagement as the mediator and Consumer Trust as the moderator. Five hyp otheses
are proposed in this study: (H1) Digital Marketing has a significant positive effect on Purchase
Intention; (H2) Brand Awareness has a significant positive effect on Purchase Intention; (H3) E -
WOM has a significant effect on Purchase Intention; (H4 ) Consumer Engagement mediates the
relationship between Digital Marketing and Purchase Intent; and (H5) Consumer Trust moderates
the relationship between Digital Marketing and Consumer Engagement. This research is expected
to make a theoretical contributio n in the form of developing a six -variable integration model in
the context of the property industry, as well as producing practical recommendations for the
management of Jakarta Garden City in optimizing its digital marketing strategy.

METHOD
This study employed a quantitative approach using a survey method with descriptive -
causal objectives. The research design was cross -sectional, in which data were collected at a
specific point in time to provide an overview of the actual relationships betwe en the variables.
The unit of analysis was the individual, specifically potential buyers or consumers who had been
exposed to Jakarta Garden City’s digital marketing strategy. The research paradigm was positivist,
with minimal (non-interventionist) researcher involvement, ensuring that respondents were not
subjected to manipulation or special treatment.

The research population comprised all consumers in the Jakarta Garden City digital
database, which consisted of 5,957 individuals based on the company’s internal data from 2025.
The sample size was determined using the Slovin formula with an error rate (e)  of 5%, resulting
in a minimum sample size of 375 respondents. The sampling technique used was non-probability
sampling with a purposive sampling method, in which respondents were selected based on
specific criteria to ensure their relevance to the research topic.
Ethical considerations were maintained throughout the research process. Respondents
were informed of the research objectives, the confidentiality of their responses, and the voluntary
nature of their participation before completing the questionnaire. Personal information obtained
from the company database was used solely for research purposes and was processed
confidentially.
The inclusion criteria for respondents were as follows: (1) male or female; (2) aged 25 –
55 years; (3) domiciled in Greater Jakarta or outside Greater Jakarta; (4) employed; (5) had seen
or been exposed to content from at least one Jakarta Garden City digit al platform (Instagram,
TikTok, or the company website); and (6) had provided personal information in the form of their
name and WhatsApp number to be contacted by the Jakarta Garden City sales team. These criteria
were established to ensure that all respondents had direct experience with Jakarta Garden City’s
digital marketing activities, allowing their assessments of the research variables to be based on
actual experience rather than hypothetical perceptions.
This study used two types of data sources. Primary data were collected through a
structured questionnaire distributed online via Google Forms to respondents who met the
specified criteria. Secondary data were obtained from internal company documents, digit al
platform reports, and relevant online sources. The questionnaire employed a 5-point Likert scale
(1 = Strongly Disagree to 5 = Strongly Agree) to measure six latent variables comprising a total of
38 indicators: Digital Marketing (DM1 –DM10, 10 indicator s), Brand Awareness (BA1 –BA4, 4
indicators), E -WOM (EWOM1 –EWOM4, 4 indicators), Consumer Engagement (CE1 –CE6, 6
indicators), Consumer Trust (CT1 –CT7, 7 indicators), and Purchase Intention (PI1 –PI7, 7
indicators).
Before the questionnaire was distributed to the 375 main respondents, the research
instrument was first tested through a pilot study involving 30 respondents who met the same
inclusion criteria. The results of the pilot study were used to identify unclear or ambiguous
indicators and to ensure that the loading factor values of all indicators exceeded 0.7. Following
the refinement process, the final questionnaire was distributed via WhatsApp by the Jakarta
Garden City sales team to consumers in the digital da tabase over a period of two months. All 375
questionnaires collected were deemed valid and were used in the subsequent analysis.
Data analysis was conducted in two main stages. First, descriptive analysis was performed
to describe the respondents’ profiles and their perceptions of each variable. Each questionnaire
item was converted into a cumulative score percentage and classified using a continuum line with
the following categories: Very Bad (20%–36%), Not Good (36%–52%), Fairly Good (52%–68%),
Good (68%–84%), and Very Good (84%–100%). The highest possible cumulative score was 375
× 5 = 1,875, while the lowest was 375 × 1 = 375. These scores corresponded to a highest
percentage of 100% and a lowest percentage of 20%, resulting in a range of 16 percentage points
for each category.
Second, Structural Equation Modeling –Partial Least Squares (SEM -PLS) analysis was
conducted using SmartPLS  4. The analysis included: (a) evaluation of the outer model through
convergent validity, assessed using loading factor values (>0.7) and Average Variance Extracted
(AVE) values (>0.5), as well as reliability testing using Cronbach’s Alpha and Composite Reliability
(CR), with values exceeding 0.7; and (b) evaluation of the inner model through R -Square (R²) to
assess predictive ability, Predictive Relevance (Q²) with a value greater than 0 to assess predictive
relevance, Effect Size (f²) with thresholds of 0 .02 for small, 0.15 for medium, and 0.35 for large
effects, and path coefficients tested using a bootstrapping procedure. Hypotheses were
considered supported when the t -statistic exceeded 1.96 and the p -value was below 0.05. SEM -
PLS was selected as a soft  modeling approach because it allows predictive relationship testing
without requiring strict assumptions regarding data distribution (Haryono, 2016).

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Results
Respondent Characteristics
Of the 375 respondents who were successfully collected, the majority were aged 25 -35
years old (52.3%), female (54.1%), domiciled in East Jakarta and its surroundings (67.5%), and
worked in the private sector (58.4%). All respondents have been exposed to a t least one digital
platform in Jakarta Garden City, with Instagram as the most used platform (61.1%), followed by
TikTok (24.3%) and the official website (14.6%). This profile is consistent with the main target
market of Jakarta Garden City, which is a group of young professionals in the Greater Jakarta area
who actively use social media.

Descriptive Analysis Results
Digital Marketing
The results of the descriptive analysis of Digital Marketing variables from 375
respondents showed that 10 indicators (DM1 -DM10) obtained a score percentage between
70.31% to 75.47%, all of which were in the Good  category. DM8, which measures consumer
attention when watching Jakarta Garden City ads on mobile phones without interruption,
recorded the highest score (75.47%), while DM3, which measures the extent to which social
media content helps understand the product, ranked lowest (70.31%). This pattern indicates that
Jakarta Garden City's digital marketing activities have succeeded in attracting the attention of the
audience (eye -catching), but  are not fully effective in conveying product messages in an
informative and educational manner.

Brand Awareness
Different from Digital Marketing, all Brand Awareness indicators (BA1 -BA4) fall into the
Not Good category with a score range of 47.41% -50.61%. BA3 measures the memory of the
Jakarta Garden City brand when consumers are actively looking for a home to get the highest score
(50.61%), while BA2 measures the ability to recognize the unique characteristics of Jakarta
Garden City is in the lowest position (47.41%). This pattern indicates that Jakarta Garden City's
brand awareness is reactive: consumers are more likely to remember the brand when they are in
active property search mode, but do not yet have strong brand awareness in the consumer
segment who have not thought about buying a property.

E-WOM
The four E -WOM indicators (EWOM1 -EWOM4) are in the Good category with a score
percentage of 73.28%-75.62%. EWOM4 measures trust in online reviews as a means of voluntary
promotion by fellow consumers with the highest score (75.62%), while EWOM2 measures th e
extent to which online reviews can increase product understanding and encourage buying
interest to be at the lowest (73.28%). This difference reveals an interesting phenomenon:
respondents acknowledge the existence and social value of E-WOM, but doubt its ability to have a
real impact on property purchase decisions.

Consumer Engagement
All Consumer Engagement indicators (CE1 -CE6) are in the Good category with a score
percentage of 68.16% -69.38%. CE2 measures the frequency of responding to or responding to
Jakarta Garden City content on social media recorded the highest score (69.38%), wh ile CE6
measures the activeness of sharing Jakarta Garden City content with others occupies the lowest
position (68.16%). Although all indicators are in the Good  category, patterns of passive
engagement are consistently identified: consumers are more likely to be viewers of content rather
than actively engaged participants.

Consumer Trust
The Consumer Trust variable shows an alarming condition, where all seven indicators
(CT1-CT7) fall into the Not Good category with a score range of 52.32% -55.20%. CT5 measures
consumer confidence that Jakarta Garden City always strives to keep its commitme nt to obtain
the highest score (55.20%), while CT2 measures confidence in the honesty of the information

Discussion
The Influence of Digital Marketing on Purchase Intention (H1)
The results of the H1 test confirmed that Digital Marketing had a positive and significant
effect on Purchase Intention (coefficient = 0.197; t = 6.863; p = 0.000). Although significant, the
relatively small effect size value (f2 = 0.097) indicates that Di gital Marketing is not the dominant
factor in shaping the purchase intention of property consumers. These findings are consistent
with Nurjaya et al. (2025) who prove that digital marketing activities through easy access to
information, relevant content, and interactive experiences can increase buying interest. Otopah et
al. (2024) in the context of Ghanaian banking also confirmed a similar positive influence.
Descriptive analysis provides an important nuance: DM3 (content helps to understand the
product) has the lowest score among all Digital Marketing indicators (70.31%), indicating a gap
between the visual appeal of the content and its ability to communicate the value of the product
substantively. This explains why although Digital Marketing has a positive effect, its contribution
to purchase intent is relatively small because existing content prioritizes aesthetics and eye -
catching rather than the depth of in formation that encourages serious purchase considerations.
Habib et al. (2022) also found that in the context of high-engagement products, digital marketing
requires the support of other factors such as trust and quality engagement to produce maximum
impact on purchase intent.

The Influence of Brand Awareness on Purchase Intention (H2)
H2 was received with the highest path coefficient among all direct relationships
(coefficient = 0.227; t = 4.469; p = 0.000), proving that Brand Awareness is the most powerful
direct driver of purchase intent. These findings are particularly relevant to th e characteristics of
the property industry: large transaction values, high decision risk, and long consideration periods
make consumers rely heavily on strong brand recognition as a signal of developer quality and
reliability. Brands that are already known  and trusted provide a sense of psychological security
for consumers in making big financial decisions.
These findings are in line with Nurjaya et al. (2025) who prove that Brand Awareness is a
major differentiating factor in a highly competitive market, and Ali Mussalam et al. (2025) who
confirm the significant positive influence of brand awareness on purchase intention in small and
medium-sized businesses in Jordan. Zeqiri et al. (2025) added a cross -border perspective that
brand awareness built through social media consistently has a positive effect on purchase
intention in developing economies. The most critical of these findings is that the contrast between
the power of influence (the highest path coefficient) and the actual conditions (the Not Good
category in the descriptive analysis) creates the potential for a huge increase in purchase
intention if brand awareness investment is systematically and consistently increased.

The Effect of E-WOM on Purchase Intention (H3)
H3 produced findings that contrasted with the initial hypothesis: E-WOM had a significant
but negative effect on Purchase Intention (coefficient = -0.203; t = 2.038; p = 0.042). This means
that the more intense E -WOM that consumers encounter about Jakarta Garden City, the lower
their purchase intention. This phenomenon can be explained from two dimensions. First,
contextually: the increase in negative reviews on Google Reviews in the past year related to the
quality of infrastructure, buildings, and service s results in actual E -WOM that does not support
purchase intent. The 1-star reviews that are starting to dominate give a strong negative signal to
potential consumers who do online research before deciding to visit a sales gallery.
Second, theoretically: in high -value and high -risk products such as property, consumers
are more sensitive to negative E -WOM than positive (negativity bias).  Khwaja et al. (2020)
identified that consumer trust inclination moderates the effectiveness of E -WOM when the
baseline confidence level is low as shown by the Consumer Trust score of the Bad category,
diverse E -WOM increases doubt and decreases purchase intention. These findings differ from
Nurjaya et al. (2025) who found E-WOM to be positive in the context of MSMEs, a difference that
can be explained by differences in product value, decision risk, and the actual condition of E-WOM
in circulation.

The Role of Consumer Engagement Mediation (H4)
H4 yielded the most controversial finding: the DM -CE-PI mediated pathway was
statistically significant (t = 5.044; p = 0.000) but with a negative coefficient ( -0.140), contrary to
the initial assumption. This means that Consumer Engagement formed from Jakarta Garden City's
Digital Marketing exposure reduces Purchase Intention indirectly , a phenomenon that at first
seems paradoxical but can be explained coherently through descriptive analysis.
The pattern of passive engagement identified in which consumers read more content
without active interaction, read posts more often (highest CE1) but share content with others less
often (lowest CE6) creates conditions where existing "engagement" is more o f an exposure than
a meaningful engagement. In this condition, the more consumers are exposed to Jakarta Garden
City content without getting enough information to build purchase confidence, the greater the
ambiguity they feel. These findings differ from  Tran (2025) and Alrwashdeh (2025) who all
reported positive mediation, likely because all three studies were conducted in contexts where
the engagement formed was more active and interactive.

The Role of Consumer Trust Moderation (H5)
H5 was received in a positive and significant direction (coefficient = 0.129; t = 3.319; p =
0.001), proving that Consumer Trust strengthens the relationship between Digital Marketing and
Consumer Engagement. The higher the consumer trust in Jakarta Garden  City, the more effective
digital marketing activities are in encouraging more meaningful and quality engagement. These

The significance of these findings is further reinforced by the large effect size of Consumer

CONCLUSION
The study demonstrates that purchase intention at Jakarta Garden City is shaped by the
combined influence of digital marketing, brand awareness, E -WOM, consumer engagement, and
consumer trust. The findings confirm that digital strategies alone are insuffic ient without
strengthening brand recognition and consumer confidence, particularly in high -involvement
property purchases. Theoretically, this study contributes an integrated model explaining the
mechanism through which digital communication affects purcha se intention. Practically, the
findings encourage property developers to prioritize trustworthy digital experiences, strengthen
brand positioning, and actively manage online consumer perceptions. Nevertheless, this study is
limited to a single property dev eloper context; future research should validate the model across
different property companies and geographical markets.
First, Brand Awareness proved to be the strongest direct driver of Purchase Intention
(coefficient = 0.227), confirming that brand strength is the most determinant strategic asset in
shaping property purchase intention. The condition of Brand Awareness whi ch is still in the Bad
category represents a huge growth opportunity for Jakarta Garden City.
Second, Digital Marketing has a significant positive effect on Purchase Intention
(coefficient = 0.197), but with a relatively small contribution (f2 = 0.097). The effectiveness of
digital marketing needs to be improved not only in terms of visual appeal, but also in terms of the
depth of product information conveyed to potential consumers to encourage more serious
purchase considerations.
Third, E -WOM has a significant negative effect on Purchase Intention (coefficient = -
0.203), reflecting the real condition of online reviews which are dominated by negative sentiment
and consumer doubts about the authenticity of high -value property reviews . Online review
reputation management is a strategic priority that cannot be ignored.
Fourth, Consumer Engagement as a mediator between Digital Marketing and Purchase
Intention shows a negative direction (coefficient = -0.140), indicating that the passive engagement
that is currently formed is not strong enough to convert digital engagement  into real purchase
intent. The transformation of content strategy from simply attracting attention to building deep
understanding is inevitable.
Fifth, Consumer Trust proved to be a significant positive moderator as well as the
dominant contributor to the model (f2 = 1.587 for Consumer Engagement). This proves that
consumer trust is the asset that most determines the quality of the entire Jakarta G arden City
digital ecosystem. Based on all of these findings, the study recommends four priority strategies:
(1) investment in long-term brand awareness campaigns that target the cold audience consumer
segment, not just the warm audience; (2) build a proac tive and responsive online reputation
management system; (3) transforming digital content strategies from an attention -based
approach to one based on education and trust; and (4) prioritizing improving the security and
transparency of digital platforms as a foundation of sustainable consumer trust.
The limitations of this study include focusing on a single property developer object, so
generalizations need to be done with caution, as well as cross-sectional designs that are unable to
capture changes in consumer behavior dynamics over time. Future research suggests extending
the object to multiple property developers for comparative analysis and using longitudinal design
to more comprehensively capture the dynamics of changing consumer attitudes.
