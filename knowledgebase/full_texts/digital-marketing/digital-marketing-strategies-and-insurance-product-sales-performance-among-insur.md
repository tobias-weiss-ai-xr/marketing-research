Abstract
The Nigerian insurance industry, despite its vast potential, grapples with persistently low
penetration rates, a challenge often attributed to archaic marketing practices and a disconnect
from the digitally native consumer. This study investigates the nexus between digital marketing
strategies and insurance product sales performance within the context of Lagos State, Nigeria.
Adopting a positivist paradigm and a quantitative cross -sectional survey design, the study
examines the influence of three key digital marketing dimensions, Search Engine Optimization
(SEO), Social Media Marketing (SMM), and Content Marketing (CM)  on sales performance.
Data were collected from a stratified random sample of 436 top, middle, and lower -level
managers drawn from a population of 2,062 employees across 11 selected insurance companies
in Lagos. An adapted and validated questionnaire served as the primary instrument,
demonstrating strong reliability (Cronbach’ s α > 0.70) and validity (Content Validity Index >
0.5). Results from the multiple regression analysis reveal that all three digital marketing
strategies are significant positive predictors of insurance product sales performance, with
Content Marketing exhibiting the most substantial impact ( β = 0.577, p < 0.001). The model
accounted for a substantial 78.4% of the variance in sales performance. The study concludes
that the strategic deployment of digital marketing is not merely ancillary but a critical driver
of commercial success in the Nigerian insurance market. We recommend a paradigm shift from
traditional, relationship-centric models to a hybrid approach that integrates digital agility with
human-centric advisory services.

Introduction
Today, the worldwide insurance industry is in the midst of a massive digital transformation that
is changing the traditional business model and the way insurance products are acquired and
retained. This digital imperative is even stronger in emerging markets, where mobile adoption
and internet penetration have opened up new opportunities for connecting to previously
untouched segments of the population (Oxford Business Group, 2024). This is what Nigeria,
the largest economy in Africa exemplifies. Although the population is growing at a rapid rate,
exceeding 200 million, and the risk pool is considerable, the insurance penetration rate in the
country is found to be very low, which is estimated to be less than 1% of GDP (Leadway
Assurance Company Limited, 2022; Okorie & Chilekezi, 2025). The inability to perform well
is often attributed to low level of consumer awareness, cultural skepticism and importantly
outdated marketing and distribution channels which do not reach the tech-savvy young people
(Olayinka & Salau, 2022). The level of insurance penetration in Nigeria is among the lowest
in Africa with around 0.5% –0.6% of the population being covered (Leadway Assurance
Company Limited, 2022). Although it is one of the most widely used insurance products, health
insurance coverage is still very low, with only approximately 3% of Nigerians being currently

Sales Performance
Sales performance is the degree to which sales activities meet the organisation's prescribed
goals, objectives and targets including revenue, market expansion, customer acquisition and
profitability (Igor, 2023). Sales performance refers to the quantifiable results that a salesperson,
sales unit or organisation produces in respect of a stated target or benchmark (Pan et al., 2023).

developments, or even the fact that an organization has invested in training or a digital
marketing program (Igor, 2023). Finally, it is situation dependent; its meaning differs from
industry to industry and environment to environment; for instance, in the insurance sector, sales
performance is heavily influenced by the level of trust, complexity of product, regulatory
context, and consumer awareness (Wahua & Kenea, 2025). In this study, sales performance is
defined as the degree of insurance p roduct uptake, premium generation and customer
acquisition efficiency of the insurance companies in Lagos State within a particular time frame
and to a given target.

Digital Marketing Strategies
Digital marketing strategies is defined as a coordinated sequence of action, technique and tool
used by organisations to promote their products or services using digital media to meet
marketing goals like customer acquisition, brand awareness, engagement and sales conversion
(Ologunebi & Taiwo, 2023). According to Purnomo (2023), digital marketing strategies is the
combination of internet -based platforms and technologies, such as social media marketing,
search engine optimization (SEO), email marketing, content marketing, mobile marketing, and
online advertising, to connect with and persuade target audiences through a more interactive,
measurable, and cost -efficient way. In the era of technology and intense competition, digital
marketing efforts are more clo sely connected to one another, and they are used to create an
organization's communication of value propositions, customer relationship management, and
positioning in the market (Rowi et al., 2024; Salam et al., 2025). In the service sector, including
insurance, banking and telecommunications, these approaches are becoming increasingly
acknowledged as key enablers of market visibility, consumer trust-building and improved sales
results (Mohammad, 2022).
The characteristic of digital marketing strategies is the fact that it is interactive, as they facilitate
two-way communication between organisations and consumers, enabling them to engage and
interact with one another, while also providing the opportunity to seek feedback and build
relationships in real time (Parveen et al., 2024). They a re data -driven and use analytics,
customer behaviour monitoring and performance metrics to guide decisions and further refine
their strategy (Mandung, 2024). Digital marketing strategies are also evolving in nature, as they
continuously evolve to adapt to technological advancements, changes in the platform
algorithm, and consumer preferences (Baranikumar et al., 2024). Moreover, they are
specifically targeted, and companies can segment audiences according to their demographics,
interests and behaviours and tailor content accordingly (Ocak, 2023). Finally, they are
measurable, with the digital platforms offering real -time metrics like impressions, click -

Theoretical Framework
Technology Acceptance Model (TAM)
The Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) was developed in 1989 by Fred Davis as an
extension to the previous theories of psychology and technology usage, especially the Theory
of Reasoned Action (TROA) (Schorr, 2023). It was born out of an attempt to understa nd the
reasons behind the adoption and rejection of information systems in organizational settings,
particularly as computer-based information systems increased in prominence in the operation
of businesses in the late 20th century (Musa et al., 2024). According to the model, the level of
user acceptance of technology is predominantly influenced by two beliefs: perceived usefulness
and perceived ease of use. Perceived usefulness is defined as the extent to which a person
believes that using a specific techno logy will improve his or her job performance, and
perceived ease of use is defined as the extent to which using the specific technology is free of
effort (Latif et al., 2025; Schorr, 2023). TAM has since been broadly adopted and developed in
research on information systems, digital tools and marketing environments that are enabled by
technology (Yurder, 2025).
Major assumptions of TAM include the fact that behavioral intention is the strongest predictor
of actual technology use, and that general attitudes towards the ease of use and usefulness of a
technology are the most important cognitive determinants of the intention to use that
technology (Badda, 2025). It makes rational user decision-making assumptions, and it does not

The relevance of TAM to the present study on digital marketing strategies and sales
performance of insurance companies in Lagos State, Nigeria, is quite relevant when explaining
why insurance companies use digital marketing tools like social media, customer engagement
systems and online advertsing channels. Insurance marketers are more likely to adopt these
technologies when they see potential in them to enhance policy sales and customer outreach,
and to lower marketing expenses. Likewise, user -friendliness can be a factor that determines
whether or not the employees of insurance companies can incorporate digital tools into their
daily marketing activities. Thus, TAM offers a solid conceptual foundation for understanding
how internal decision-making processes underlie the adoption of digital marketing and how it
affects sales performance in the competitive insurance market in Lagos State.

Diffusion of Innovations (DOI) Theory
Everett Rogers developed the Diffusion of Innovations Theory in 1962 and further revised it in
later editions, such as 1995 (García -Avilés, 2020, Lund, 2025). The theory was derived from
sociological research which sought to understand the dissemination of agricultural innovations
within farmers' communities in rural areas in the United States (Savari et al., 2025; Singh &
Strzelecki, 2026). It developed into a widely used framework for comprehending the adoption
of new ideas, technologies, and practices in social systems and systems (Guo & Huang, 2024).
According to DOE, diffusion is a process of communicating an innovation over time among
people in a social system, and it points out that the process of diffusion is affected by
communication channels, time,  and social structure in terms of the diffusion process, which
determines the behavior of the diffusion process (Fitriani et al., 2026; Reis & Pinheiro, 2025).
The core assumptions of DOI theory include the following: the process of innovation adoption
has a predictable pattern among adopters who are classified as innovators, early adopters, early
majority, late majority, and laggards (Reis & Pinheiro, 2025): It further assumes that five
perceived attributes of innovation, namely relative advantage, compatibility, complexity,
trialability and observability (Overbye et al., 2025) affect the rate of adoption. The theory
highlights that the adoption of innovations does not occur at the same speed for individuals and
organizations, and that internal and external communication networks have an impact (Reis &
Pinheiro, 2025). As per the scholars' words, DOI has been credited with explaining the diffusion
of technology in other sphere of knowledge such as marketing (M panza 2025), education and
information systems (Mpanza 2025). But it has also faced criticisms for its deterministic nature,
narrow focus on power dynamics and the universalization of patterns of adoption across
cultural and institutional settings (Maharati & Entezarian, 2025).
Digital marketing strategies and insurance companies' sales performance in Lagos State are
especially relevant in the light of DOI theory, as it helps to understand the speed and trajectory
of digital transformation in the insurance sector. Insurers implem ent digital marketing
innovations that they believe will yield benefits like better customer targeting, better brand
visibility, and more policy sales. The theory also helps to understand variation in adoption rates
between the companies, with some insurance companies being innovators or early adopters of
digital platforms while others do not as a result of some organizational resistance or lack of
ICT capacity. Moreover, the positive outcomes of the observed practices, such as higher sales
conversion rates and better customer engagement, further encourage the adoption and diffusion

Methodology
This study adopted a quantitative research approach, employing the deductive reasoning aspect
of the method. A cross-sectional survey research design was used, involving the collection of
primary data through adapted questionnaires. The study was anchored on a positivist research
philosophy, which emphasizes objectivity, measurement, and empirical validation. Lagos State
was selected as the study area. The target population comprised management staff of selected
insurance companies operating within Lagos State, Nigeria. The sample size of 436
respondents was determined using the Taro Yamane (1967) sample size calculator. The initial
computation yielded 335 respondents; however, to accommodate potential non -responses, an
additional 30% was added, resulting in a final sample size of 436.
A pilot study was conducted to evaluate the clarity, relevance, and comprehensibility of the
research instrument prior to its administration in the main study area. The pilot exercise was
carried out in an insurance company located in Lagos State, which was excluded from the main
study to prevent contamination of results. A total of forty-four (44) copies of the questionnaire
were distributed to employees of the company , with a response rate of 91% . The validity and
reliability of the instrument were assessed using the returned questionnaires, and the data were
analyzed with Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS), version 25.

Interpretation
Table 4 shows the multiple regression analysis results for the effect of digital marketing
strategies on the sales performance of insurance companies in Lagos State, Nigeria. The results
showed that Search Engine Optimization ( β = 0.255, t = 8.015, p < 0.0 5), Social Media

0.05) have positive and significant effects on sales performance. This implies that all the digital
marketing dimensions (Search Engine Optimizat ion, Social Media Marketing, and Content
Marketing) are important factors which in turn yield an increase in sales performance in the
insurance companies in Lagos State, Nigeria.
The R-value of 0.886 supports this result and indicates that digital marketing strategies have a
strong positive relationship with sales performance in the insurance companies in Lagos State.
The coefficient of multiple determination Adj. R² = 0.784 indica tes that about 78.4% of the
variation that occurs in sales performance can be accounted for by the digital marketing
strategies, while the remaining 21.6% of changes that occur in sales performance are accounted
for by other variables not captured in the m odel. The predictive and prescriptive multiple

The intercept (0.252) in the regression predictive model represents the mean value of sales
performance when all of the predictor variables (digital marketing dimensions) in the model
are equal to zero. Therefore, holding digital marketing dimensions to a constant zero, sales
performance would be 0.252 which is positive. In the predictive model, it is seen that all the
variables had positive and significant effects and are therefore retained in the prescriptive
model. The results of the multiple regression analysis as seen in the prescriptive model indicate
that when all dimensions of digital marketing (Search Engine Optimization, Social Media
Marketing, and Content Marketing) are improved by one -unit, sales performance would also
increase by 0.221, 0.200, and 0.516 units respectively and vice-versa.
The results further revealed that Content Marketing (β = 0.577) has a stronger significant effect
on sales performance in the insurance companies. Hence, more attention should be paid to this
dimension of digital marketing. In addition, the F-statistics (df = 3, 416) = 507.558 at p = 0.000
(p < 0.05) indicated that the overall model was significant in predicting the effect of digital
marketing strategies on sales performance. Therefore, the null hypothesis which states that
digital marketing strategies have  no significant effect on sales performance in the insurance
companies in Lagos State, Nigeria is rejected.

The results of the present research confirm the theoretical conclusions of the Technology
Acceptance Model (TAM). The Technology Acceptance Model can be used as a theoretical
base for comprehending how acceptance and utilization of digital marketing technologies affect
sales activities in insurance organizations. The theory suggests that perceived usefulness and
perceived ease of use are two factors that greatly effect a person's acceptance and use of
technological innovations. For insurance companies, the digital integration of digital marketing
platforms and tools is more likely to happen when they believe the technologies will support
their marketing efforts, boost customer engagement, and positively affect sales results.
Moreover, if the technology is easy to understand, use, and integrate, users are more likely to
accept the technology, which makes it more likely to be successfully implemented, TAM
suggests. Employees and customers that find digital marketing strategies useful and
convenient, like social  media marketing, email marketing, mobile marketing, content
marketing and search engine optimization, are more likely to make them effective. The theory
also has a focus on positive attitudes regarding the adoption of technology, which in turn
increase behavioural intention and actual use of the technology, which then leads to better
organisational performance. As a result, digital marketing technologies can help insurance
providers connect with more customers, engage customers better, build brand awarenes s, and
boost sales. Hence, the results support the idea of TAM which states that technology acceptance
is a key enabling process in organizational success and better business performance by using
digital marketing strategies.
