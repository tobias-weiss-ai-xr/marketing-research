1. Introduction

Celebrity endorsement remains a common advertising tactic used to build brand awareness and shape consumer
preferences. Athletes are increasingly used as endorsers because their fan bases, public narratives, and perceived authenticity
can strengthen emotional and social connections and, in turn, stimulate purchasing (Kim et al., 2020; Lee & Koo, 2015).
Advertising exposure influences purchase intention, message evaluation, and judgments of source credibility. Athletes
often signal achievement,  discipline, and public admiration, which can enhance consumer confidence, improve brand
evaluations, and support loyalty formation (Pornpitakpan, 2004; Tzoumaka et al., 2016). Endorsement effects are typically
stronger when the endorser matches the produc t, the message elicits affect, and the choice is perceived as socially endorsed
(Singh Rai et al., 2021). In the present study, athlete endorsement is treated as a broad advertising format encountered by
consumers across general promotional media. The stud y, therefore, focuses on how consumers evaluate athlete -endorsed
product advertising in general, rather than on platform -level differences in sponsorship disclosure, creator interaction, or
algorithmic exposure.
This study examines how endorser credibility , endorser–product fit, emotional appeal, and social influence relate to
purchase intention in athlete endorsement contexts. Endorser credibility captures perceived knowledge, trustworthiness, and
honesty (Leong et al., 2015; Pikas et al., 2012). Endorser–product fit reflects the alignment between an athlete’s public persona
and the endorsed product, shaping perceived legitimacy and believability (Harish & Ikhwan, 2024; Lee & Koo, 2015). Emotional

Abstract Athlete endorsements are widely used in marketing, yet the mechanisms through which endorsement cues
influence consumer buying decisions remain insufficiently integrated. This study examines the effects of Emotional Appeal,
Endorser Credibility, Endorser–Product Fit, and Social Influence on Purchase Intention, while testing the mediating role of
Brand Trust. Data were collected through a structured survey administered via Google Forms to 610 respondents in Goa
with prior exposure to athlete-endorsed advertising. The data were analysed using partial least squares structural equation
modelling (PLS-SEM) with bootstrapping, multigroup analysis (MGA), importance –performance map analysis (IPMA), and
PLSpredict. The results show that Emotional Appeal, Endorser Credibility, and Endorser–Product Fit have significant positive
direct effects on Purchase Intention. These three variables also have significant indirect effects through Brand Trust,
confirming its mediating role. Social Influence has a weak but significant direct effect on Purchase Intention, but its indirect
effect through Brand Trust is not significant. PLSpredict results show that the Q²_predict values for all four Purchase
Intention indicators are greater than zero and that the MAE values are lower than or equal to those of the linear benchmark
for three of the four indicators, indicating limited but acceptable out -of-sample predictive validity. The multigroup results
further show that the effects of Emotional Appeal, Endorser Credibility, and Social Infl uence on Purchase Intention differ
significantly between male and female respondents. The importance –performance analysis identifies Brand Trust as the
most influential predictor of Purchase Intention, although its performance remains moderate. Overall, the findings suggest
that athlete endorsement operates mainly through Emotional Appeal, perceived authenticity, and trust formation rather
than through social pressure alone. The study contributes to endorsement research by proposing an integrated model of
athlete-based persuasion and offers practical guidance for marketers designing credible, emotionally engaging, and
strategically aligned campaigns.

Clarifying these mechanisms is important for brand managers because athlete endorsements are costly and strategically

Recent studies have typically examined credibility, congruence, emotional response, and social validation as isolated
predictors or in limited combinations. As a result, there is still limited evidence on how endorser credibility, endorser–product
fit, emotional appeal, and social influence jointly influence purchase intention within a single athlete endorsement framework,
and whether brand trust mediates these relatio nships. The present study addresses this gap by integrating these four
antecedents and brand trust in one model. Goa provides a useful empirical setting because it is a globally visible tourism
destination in which consumers are exposed to diverse branded communication, domestic and international market influences,
and heterogeneous consumption environments. This makes it a relevant context for examining whether athlete endorsement
mechanisms identified in broader literature also operate in a regional Indian market. In this sense, consumer responses in Goa
may resemble broader markets shaped by frequent exposure to brand -led persuasion, while still reflecting local influences
linked to tourism intensity, regional culture, and mixed consumer profiles.
The study makes three theoretical contributions to the athlete endorsement literature. First, it develops an integrated
explanation of athlete endorsement effectiveness by examining endorser credibility, endorser –product fit, emotional appeal,
and social influenc e within a single framework. Second, it positions brand trust as the central relational mechanism through
which these endorsement cues are translated into purchase intention. Third, it focuses specifically on athlete endorsers rather
than celebrity endorse ment in general, thereby recognising that athletes may generate forms of admiration, symbolic
attachment, and evaluative response that differ from those associated with generic celebrity promotion, even though
parasocial relationships are not modelled as a separate construct.
More importantly, the study advances athlete endorsement theory by moving beyond an attribute-based view in which
endorsement effectiveness is explained mainly through isolated source characteristics. Instead, it conceptualises athlete
endorsement as a multi -route persuasion process in which cognitive evaluation, affective response, and social validation
operate together to shape purchase intention. In this framework, brand trust functions as the key mechanism that converts
endorsement evaluation into consumer buying intention. This provides a more explicit theoretical explanation of how athlete
endorsement works, rather than only showing that particular variables are statistically significant.
Conceptually, each predictor represents a d istinct route through which athlete endorsements may affect consumer
behaviour. Credibility can support persuasion by strengthening trust and favourable brand evaluations when athletes are
perceived as honest, intelligent, and proficient ( Pikas et al., 201 2; Pornpitakpan, 2004; Sun et al., 2015 ), and credible athlete
endorsers have been linked to stronger consumer responses and higher buying propensity (Lee & Koo, 2015). Endorser –
product fit, which is consistent with the match -up hypothesis, may increase en dorsement legitimacy; when athletes endorse
products aligned with their lifestyle or career, the message appears more authentic and can strengthen trust and purchase
intention (Harish & Ikhwan, 2024; Lee & Koo, 2015; Singh Rai et al., 2021; Terason, 2014).  Emotional appeal may strengthen
endorsement outcomes because the pride, inspiration, and resonance associated with sport can transfer to the brand,
supporting engagement and increasing purchase likelihood ( Damayanti & Wahyudi, 2022; Putri & Roostika, 2022 ; Tzoumaka
et al., 2016). Social influence operates through social norms and peer validation; consumers may adopt products perceived as
approved or used within their networks, and such validation may strengthen the perceived persuasiveness and credibility of
athlete-endorsed messages (Jia et al., 2023; Muralidharan, 2012; Nichols & Shapiro, 2023; Shuart, 2007). Overall, these cues
are expected to influence purchase intention through partly distinct yet potentially connected pathways, with brand trust
serving as the main mechanism through which endorsement evaluation translates into buying intention.
Recent research suggests that athlete endorsement on social media should not be treated as a simple extension of
traditional celebrity endorsement. In contemporary digital environments, endorsement outcomes are shaped not only by
source credibility but also by platform -specific content cues, authenticity, parasocial interaction, and the relational dynamics
of influencer communication. Evidence from recen t influencer studies further shows that trust and purchase intention are
influenced by content value, homophily, and credibility on platforms such as TikTok, while athlete -specific research indicates
that sports -themed posts can generate stronger credibili ty and behavioural responses than lifestyle -oriented posts.
Accordingly, the present study positions athlete endorsement within a contemporary social -media environment rather than
within a purely traditional mass -media setting (Alcántara et al., 2024; Liu & Zheng, 2024; Pan et al., 2025; Schartel Dunn &
Nisbett, 2025).
This study proposes the following hypotheses on the basis of the theoretical framework and identifies research gaps to
examine the direct, mediating, and moderating effects of athlete endorsement attributes on Purchase Intention.

1.1. Emotional appeal

Emotional appeal is a powerful driver of consumer behavio ur, particularly in enhancing purchase intentions. Positive
emotions, such as inspiration, pride, and admiration, can deepen the consumer–brand connection, fostering stronger purchase
intent. Abd Al -Aziz and Metawie (2022) reported that emotional responses, along with social factors, significantly influence
online purchase intention. Mistry et al. (2024) reported that emotional bran ding content on social platforms increases
consumer engagement and purchasing behavio ur. Kostić (2024) and Zhang and Luo (2018) echoed similar findings, affirming
that emotionally resonant content enhances marketing outcomes. Recent digital research indica tes that emotional cues
continue to matter in social -media endorsement. Meta -analytic evidence shows that post characteristics, especially hedonic
value, have a relatively strong effect on purchase intention in influencer marketing. Social -media users resp ond not only to
information but also to emotional and relational cues, and these responses are strengthened when content is perceived as
authentic and socially engaging. In athlete endorsement, emotionally engaging content can therefore make products appea r
more appealing, meaningful, and personally relevant to followers (Liu & Zheng, 2024; Pan et al., 2025). Ge (2025) explored
emotional interaction and reported that intimacy and familiarity positively influence purchase intention through affective an d
cognitive trust. Kang and Jin (2015) emphasised the cross -cultural validity of emotional appeals, demonstrating that emotions
driven by products and the store atmosphere increase purchase intention in both China and India. According to Abbasi et al.
(2014), humorous emotional appeals are particularly effective among younger consumers in Pakistan. Zhao (2024) reported
that emotional gratification in online settings drives impulsive purchases. Wang (2022) extended the scope by showing that
emotional appeals also promote sustainable or green consumption by enhancing perceived environmental responsibility.
Vrtana and Krizanova (2023) cautioned, however, that emotional advertising may backfire if perceived as insincere. Together,
these studies illustrate that emotional appeal is central to effective marketing, as it builds affective trust and fosters long-term
engagement.

H1: Emotional Appeal has a significant positive effect on purchase intention.

1.2. Endorser credibility

Endorser credibility is a key determinant  of consumer behaviour, particularly purchase intention. Credibility —often
operationalised through trustworthiness, expertise, and attractiveness —shapes consumer attitudes and purchase propensity.
Harish and Ikhwan (2024) report that credibility, driven ma inly by attractiveness and expertise, enhances advertising
effectiveness and brand equity, increasing consumers’ likelihood of purchasing. In digital settings, Giri and Alfaruqi (2023)
reported that credible celebrity endorsers on platforms such as Travelo ka strengthen brand attitude and brand credibility.
Recent digital research supports this argument. In social-media environments, source credibility is interpreted not only through
expertise, trustworthiness, and attractiveness, but also through authentici ty, informative value, and the quality of the
relationship that followers perceive with the endorser. Recent studies show that these factors strengthen brand credibility,
trust, and purchase intention in influencer settings. Athlete -specific evidence also suggests that credibility remains central in
social-media endorsement, although its effectiveness depends on how followers interpret the athlete’s content and public
persona across platforms (Alcántara et al., 2024; Liu & Zheng, 2024; Pan et al., 2025; Schartel Dunn & Nisbett, 2025). Credibility
also operates through affective and attitudinal pathways. Trissetianto and Wibowo (2024) report positive effects on purchase
intention and brand love, whereas Setiana and Ariyanti (2023) identify brand attitude as a mediator in the relationship between
credibility and purchase intention. Complementary mechanisms are reported via brand image and brand credibility. Jamaludin
and Sugiyanto (2024) show that service quality and credibility jointly enhance brand image, and Damayanti and Wahyudi (2022)
identify brand credibility as a mediator in the endorsement –Purchase Intention link. At the attribute level, trustworthiness,
expertise, and attractiveness significantly predict purchase decisions, particularly among female st udents (Kharisma &
Kurniawati, 2021; Riskhi et al., 2018). Finally, professional image exerts a stronger influence on purchase intention than
reliability does (Chiu et al., 2019), and high -credibility endorsers generate greater purchase intention in the ai rline sector
(Febrina & Sjabadhyni, 2018).

H2: Endorser credibility has a significant positive effect on purchase intention.

1.3. Endorser–product fit

Endorser–product fit, which is typically framed by the match -up hypothesis, is a key determinant of celebrity
endorsement effectiveness. Hwang (2011) shows that observable endorser –product congruence improves advertising
effectiveness and consumer attitudes, particularly in print media. Majeed (2021) similarly proposes a model in which str onger
match enhances customer perceptions and purchase likelihood. This relationship remains important in digital markets. Recent
studies show that congruency continues to shape the effectiveness of endorsements in platform -based settings. Song et al.
(2024) found that perceived congruency affects purchase intention, with functional congruency playing a particularly important
role. More recent evidence also suggests that fit should be understood in relation to platform context and post type, because
the effectiveness of athlete endorsement depends on whether the endorsed product aligns with the athlete’s identity, content

1.4. Social influence

Social influence increasingly shapes consumer behaviour in digital and peer -driven contexts. It reflects the effects of
social norms, peer approval, and social presence on purchasing decisions. Abd Al-Aziz and Metawie (2022) found that subjective
norms and peer i nfluence significantly increase online purchase intentions, indicating that consumers often rely on others’
views when deciding to buy. Recent research suggests that social influence continues to matter in digital markets, mainly
through social proof, conformity, and visible network approval rather than through credibility itself. Consumers may be more
willing to consider athlete -endorsed products when they observe broader social approval or positive community signals,
especially in fast -moving social -media environments. Therefore, social influence is expected to have a positive effect on
purchase intention, although this effect is likely to be weaker and more context -dependent than the effects of emotional
appeal, credibility, and endorser–product fit (Liu & Zheng, 2024; Pan et al., 2025). Evidence in endorsement settings also points
to a social-proof mechanism. By signalling social approval, Jia et al. (2023) and Muralidharan (2012) report that peer validation
strengthens the effectiveness of celebrity endo rsements. Similarly, Nichols and Shapiro (2023) showed that perceived
collective acceptance increases the credibility of athlete endorsements and strengthens purchase intention. Shuart (2007)
further argues that peer approval and social endorsement amplify marketing messages.

H4: Social Influence has a significant positive effect on purchase intention.

1.5. Mediation of emotional appeal and purchase intention through brand trust

In digital athlete endorsement, Brand Trust is not built merely through visibility or popularity. It develops when endorsed
content is perceived as authentic, credible, transparent, and consistent with the athlete’s image and the brand message. Recent
influencer research also suggests that trust formation in social -media environme nts is shaped by parasocial relationships,
homophily, and perceived authenticity, whereas excessive commercialisation, over -endorsement, misinformation, and
reputational controversy can weaken credibility and reduce trust. Accordingly, Brand Trust in athle te endorsement should be
understood as a credibility-based and authenticity-based evaluation formed under conditions of greater consumer skepticism
and higher reputational volatility in digital media (Cheah et al., 2024; Liao & Chen, 2024; Liu & Zheng, 202 4).
Brand trust has been shown to mediate the relationship between emotional appeal and purchase intention. Recent
studies support a trust -based view of emotional and relational cues. Emotional appeal may influence purchase intention not
only directly, but  also by increasing confidence in the endorsed brand. Therefore, brand trust is expected to mediate the
relationship between emotional appeal and purchase intention (Liu & Zheng, 2024; Pan et al., 2025). When emotional content
is perceived as authentic, it  can build affective connections that strengthen Brand Trust and, in turn, Purchase Intention
(Mahbob et al., 2024). Emotional resonance may also enhance perceived expertise and congruence, which are central to trust
formation (Randers et al., 2023). Altho ugh emotional appeal is often embedded in celebrity endorsements, the indirect
pathway via Brand Trust remains underexamined in some models (Damayanti & Wahyudi, 2022), underscoring the value of
testing this mediation.

H5: Brand Trust mediates the relationship between Emotional Appeal and Purchase Intention.

1.6. Mediation of endorser credibility and purchase intention through brand trust

Credibility—comprising trustworthiness, expertise, and attractiveness —can strengthen consumer trust, which in turn
increases purchase intent (Iqbal et al., 2024; Setiana & Ariyanti, 2023). Digital studies in the recent past have supported this
mechanism. Credibility helps build trust, and trust then  shapes purchase intention. In athlete -specific social media
endorsement, credibility also remains closely linked to purchase outcomes. Thus, brand trust is a plausible mediator of the
credibility–purchase intention relationship (Alcántara et al., 2024; Liu & Zheng, 2024; El-Shihy, 2024). This trust-based pathway

1.7. Mediation of endorser–product fit and purchase intention through brand trust

Endorser–product congruence can enhance perceived authenticity and, in turn, strengthen trust (Randers et al., 2023).
Past research has shown that the product fit remains important in digital endorsement. When the endorser and product appear
well matched, the endorsement seems more genuine and consistent. This can strengthen trust in the brand and, in turn,
increase purchase intention. Therefore, brand trust is expected to mediate this relatio nship (Pan et al., 2025; Saternus et al.,
2024; Song et al., 2024). McKinney and Benson (2013) similarly argue that congruency is central to trust formation in the
relationship era, particularly when the endorser aligns with the brand image.

H7: Brand Trust mediates the relationship between Endorser–Product Fit and Purchase Intention.

1.8. Mediation of social influence and purchase intention through brand trust

The relationship between Social Influence and  Brand Trust requires a more cautious theoretical explanation than the
other mediation paths. Trust in a brand is not usually formed through peer pressure alone. In social -media environments,
however, visible support from a valued community, influencer, or  follower network can function as social proof, particularly
when consumers are uncertain about the endorsed brand or the motives behind the endorsement. Recent research suggests
that opinion leadership and parasocial interaction can help build follower br and trust, while authenticity, homophily, and
content value strengthen these relational mechanisms. At the same time, this pathway should not be overstated. Trust is
shaped more directly by credibility, authenticity, and message quality, and it can weaken when consumers perceive excessive
commercialisation or over -endorsement. Therefore, Brand Trust may mediate the relationship between Social Influence and
Purchase Intention in athlete endorsement, but this pathway is likely to be weaker and more contingent  than the mediation
paths associated with Emotional Appeal, Endorser Credibility, and Endorser –Product Fit (Cheah et al., 2024; Kim et al., 2024;
Liu & Zheng, 2024; Pan et al., 2025).

H8: Brand Trust mediates the relationship between Social Influence and Purchase Intention.

2. Materials and Methods

2.1. Sampling and data collection

Data were collected through a structured questionnaire administered to respondents in Goa with prior exposure to
athlete-endorsed product advertisements. A non -probability purposive sampling approach was used because the study
required participants who wer e familiar with athlete endorsements and able to assess their influence on purchase -related
outcomes (Patton, 2002; Willig, 2001).
Respondents were recruited through face -to-face contact and online channels. Eligibility was screened by asking
whether they had previously seen advertisements in which athletes endorsed products or brands. Only respondents reporting
such prior exposure were included, and only their responses were retained for analysis. The questionnaire was administered
through Google Forms. Incomplete responses were removed from the final dataset. In total, 610 valid responses were retained
for analysis.

2.2. Sample size and respondents’ profile

The final sample consisted of 610 valid respondents. Of these, 284 were male, representing 46.56 percent of the sample,
and 326 were female, representing 53.44 percent. Most respondents were aged 18 to 32 years, accounting for 65.57 percent
of the sample, followed by those aged 33 to 47 years at 33.44 percent. Respondents aged 48 to  62 years represented 0.98
percent. The demographic profile of the respondents is presented in Table 1.

2.3. Common method bias assessment

Since the independent and dependent variables were collected from the same respondents through a single
questionnaire at one point in time, common method bias was assessed. A full collinearity assessment was conducted in the
PLS-SEM model. All construct -level VIF values were below the threshold of 3.3 (Kock & Lynn, 2012), suggesting that common
method bias is unlikely to present a serious threat to the results. The VIF values are reported in Table 2.

2.5. Data analysis techniques

Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM) was used to analyse the data in SmartPLS version 3 (Ringle
et al., 2022). PLS-SEM was selected because it accommodates complex models with multiple constructs and mediation effects
and focuses on maximising explained variance in endogenous variables such as Brand Trust and Purchase Intention. SmartPLS
also supports the modelling  of latent constructs —Endorser C redibility, Endorser –Product Fit, Emotional Appeal, and Social
Influence—using their observed indicators (Hair et al., 2021). Consistent with Hair et al. (2019), PLS -SEM is well suited to
exploratory and prediction-oriented research and can be preferable t o Covariance-Based SEM (CB-SEM) when the objective is
to explain and predict consumer outcomes. PLS-SEM is also relatively robust with smaller samples and is tolerant of nonnormal
data typical of Likert-type survey measures, which aligns with this study’s analytical requirements.
The study was carried out in four stages. The first stage involved assessing the measurement model for reliability and
validity by evaluating indicator loadings, internal consistency reliability using Cronbach’s alpha and composite  reliability,
convergent validity through average variance extracted, and discriminant validity.
Second, the structural model was evaluated to test the proposed relationships among  Endorser Credibility, Endorser –
Product Fit, Emotional Appeal, Social Influence, Brand Trust, and Purchase Intention using path coefficients, p -values, and R².
Third, mediation and moderation were examined. Bootstrapping was used to test the mediating role of Brand Trust
between the four predictors and Purchase Intention, and Mult i-Group Analysis (MGA) was conducted to assess moderation
across demographic segments such as gender.
Fourth, Importance –Performance Map Analysis (IPMA) was used to complement the PLS‒SEM results by identifying
construct importance and performance in predicting Purchase Intention and deriving strategic priorities.

2.6. Scale items and sources

The study instrument was designed to measure six core constructs, namely Purchase Intention, Brand Trust, Emotional
Appeal, Endorser Credibility, Endorser–Product Fit, and Social Influence. The measurement items were drawn from established
studies and adapted where necessary to suit the context of sportsperson endorsed products. Multiple items were used for
each construct to improve content coverage and supp ort the assessment of reliability and validity. The measurement items
and their sources are presented in Table 3.

3.1. Evaluation of the measurement framework

To ensure the reliability and validity of the study’s model,  convergent validity and discriminant validity  were assessed

The positive role of emotional appeal suggests that athlete endorsements are persuasive because they activate feelings
beyond product information. Athletes often symbolise aspiration, discipline, pride, and achievement. These meanings may
make the endorsed brand more attractive and personally relevant. This interpretation is consistent with prior studies showing
that emotional messaging strengthens attention, brand  awareness, and purchase -related outcomes ( Henni & Sondari, 2024;
Pol et al., 2024; Subasinghe & Weerasiri, 2024). The mediating role of brand trust further suggests that emotional appeal does
not only attract attention. Emotional resonance may also make t he brand seem more sincere, relatable, and psychologically
closer, which in turn supports purchase intention (Fianto et al., 2022; Laulita & Clorina, 2023).
Endorser credibility and endorser–product fit also emerged as important drivers of purchase intention. This is consistent
with source credibility theory and the match -up hypothesis, which suggest that persuasion is stronger when the endorser is
perceived as trustworthy, expert, and well aligned with the product (Hovland & Weiss, 1951; Ohanian, 1990; Til l & Busler,
2000). Prior research likewise reports positive effects of credibility and fit on purchase intention and related outcomes (Gi ri &
Alfaruqi, 2023; Kharisma & Kurniawati, 2021; Santoso & Immanuel, 2022; Siwantara et al., 2023). The present result s further
suggest that these cues also operate through brand trust. When the athlete appears believable and naturally matched with
the product, consumers are more likely to view the endorsement as authentic rather than commercially forced, which may
facilitate trust formation (Azizah & Faisol, 2024; Dewi & Widagda, 2024; Nuraida et al., 2022; Tanjung & Keni, 2023 ).
The most theoretically important result concerns social influence. Although social influence showed a modest direct
relationship with purchase i ntention, its indirect effect through brand trust was not significant. This suggests that social cues
may increase visibility, trend awareness, or social approval, but may not make the brand appear trustworthy. In consumer
psychology terms, social influence is often an external cue, whereas trust is a deeper internal judgment. Consumers may notice
that peers admire an athlete or that an endorsement is socially popular, yet such visibility alone may not build confidence i n
the brand. This interpretation is b roadly consistent with studies suggesting that social influence is more consequential when
combined with trust or affect (Venkatesh et al., 2003), while other work also reports limited trust -mediated effects of social
influence on purchase intention ( Herawati & Imronudin, 2024; Inzaghi & Sukmaningsih, 2022 ). The findings therefore suggest
that athlete endorsement may operate more as an individual, affective, and self -expressive form of consumption than as one
driven mainly by social norms.
The IPMA results support this pattern. Brand trust emerged as the most important driver of purchase intention,
indicating that it is the central conversion mechanism in the model. Emotional appeal also showed high strategic relevance and
strong performance, suggesting that  emotionally resonant communication is already effective. Endorser –product fit showed
meaningful importance but the weakest performance, making it the clearest area for managerial improvement. Endorser
credibility and social influence showed lower strategi c leverage, suggesting smaller gains from these factors. This pattern
suggests that brands may benefit more from strengthening trust-bearing elements such as authenticity, emotional resonance,
and endorser –brand congruence than from relying heavily on popu larity-based or trend -driven tactics ( Ahmad et al., 2014;
Sulistyandari & Ramadhan, 2021; Ting et al., 2020).
The multigroup analysis adds a gender -based dimension to these results. Significant gender differences were found in
the direct effects of emotional appeal, endorser credibility, and social influence on purchase intention, whereas no significant
gender differences were found in the paths involving brand trust. This suggests that men and women do not differ substantially
in the basic role of brand trust, but they do differ in the endorsement cues that most strongly translate into purchase intention.
Emotional Appeal and Endorser Credibility were stronger among female respondents, whereas Social Influence was stronger
among male respondents.

One plausible explanation is that female consumers in this context may place greater weight on affective and trust -
related cues when evaluating athlete endorsements. Emotional appeal may be more persuasive when the athlete’s image
evokes inspiration, admiration, or personal connection, while credibility may be interpreted not only as expertise but also as
sincerity, authenticity, and trustworthiness. This interpretation is broadly consistent with prior research on gender differences
in responses to emotional advertising and norm-linked purchase pathways (Fisher & Dubé, 2005; Gundala et al., 2022). In this
context, women appear more responsive when the endorsement feels emotionally meaningful and when the athlete seems
believable and genuine.
By contrast, the stronger effect of social influence among male respondents suggests that socially visible approval may
play a more active role in shaping purchase intention for men in this setting. This should not be read as a rigid gender
stereotype. A more careful interpretation  is that the two groups may assign different weights to endorsement cues when
translating evaluation into intention. Female respondents appear to rely more on internally processed emotional and credibility
cues, whereas male respondents appear somewhat mor e responsive to socially observable signals linked to peer recognition,
trend salience, or public approval. Because the brand trust paths did not differ significantly across gender groups, the difference
appears to lie less in trust formation itself and mo re in the route through which endorsement cues influence purchase
intention.
These results contribute to endorsement research in two ways. First, they show that brand trust is not a generic
mediator that automatically carries every endorsement cue into pur chase intention. Instead, trust mainly transmits cues that
consumers perceive as personally diagnostic, such as emotional appeal, credibility, and fit. Second, the results suggest that
athlete endorsement should not be understood only as a social influence  process. In this setting, it is better explained as a
combined affective and cognitive process in which consumers respond to emotion, authenticity, and congruence, while social
cues play a more limited and context -specific role. In this sense, the study e xtends source credibility theory, the match -up
hypothesis, and expectancy –disconfirmation-based trust reasoning by showing that different endorsement cues do not
operate through the same psychological route.
The managerial implications follow directly from  this pattern. Brands using athlete endorsers should focus first on
strengthening brand trust through credible, authentic, and emotionally resonant campaigns. Storytelling that highlights the
athlete’s journey, values, discipline, and connection with the b rand may be more effective than campaigns based mainly on
popularity or trend signals. Since endorser –product fit showed meaningful importance but relatively weak performance,
managers should give greater attention to selecting athletes whose public image,  lifestyle, and symbolic meaning align closely
with the brand. The gender results also suggest that campaign calibration may be useful. For female audiences, emotionally
rich and credibility -centred messages may be more persuasive, whereas for male audienc es, socially visible and community -
oriented cues may deserve greater emphasis. Even so, brands should avoid overreliance on social influence alone, because
popularity by itself appears insufficient to generate trust-based purchase motivation.
Finally, the study has several limitations. The cross -sectional design limits strong causal interpretation, and the Goa -
based sampling frame may restrict wider generalisation. Consumer responses to athlete endorsements may also vary across
product categories, age groups, and cultural settings. Future research could test whether the weak trust-mediated role of social
influence persists in other endorsement contexts, especially for products with stronger public visibility or group -signalling
value. Longitudinal and mixed-method designs may also help explain how consumers move from admiration of the athlete to
trust in the brand and, eventually, to purchase behaviour.

5. Final Considerations

This study examines the mechanisms through which endorsement -based advertising shapes Purchase Intention. The
results show that Emotional Appeal, Endorser Credibility, and Endorser–Product Fit significantly affect Purchase Intention both
directly and indirectly through Brand Trust. These findings are consistent with credibility theory, the match-up hypothesis, and
expectancy–disconfirmation theory. In contrast, Social Influence has only a weak effect on Purchase Intention, suggesting that
social cues alone may be insufficient to build trust or prompt consumer action.
The empirical results support H1, H2, H3, H5, H6, and H7. Emotional and cognitive endorsement cues affect Purchase
Intention through both direct paths and indirect paths via Brand Trust. H4 is s upported, although the effect is weak. H8 is not
supported, indicating that Social Influence does not significantly affect Purchase Intention through Brand Trust. Multigroup
analysis (MGA) was conducted to examine whether the structural relationships diffe red across groups. Although most
hypothesised relationships were supported , future research may further investigate whether endorsement responses vary
across demographic and psychographic segments.
The study also contributes to the literature by positioning Brand Trust as a central mediating mechanism in endorsement
models. Trust links endorsement-related attributes with behavioural intention. The IPMA results suggest that marketers should
prioritise endorsement content that builds emotional resonance and s trengthens the fit between the endorser and the
product. Emotional Appeal appears both statistically significant and practically important. Endorser –Product Fit is also
important, but its lower performance indicates scope for managerial improvement. By contrast, Social Influence shows limited
importance, suggesting that trust-based and emotion-based endorsement strategies may be more effective.

This study has several limitations. First, the use of cross-sectional data limits causal inference because the relationships
were observed at a single point in time and temporal ordering could not be examined directly. Second, the sampling frame was

cultural, social, and sporting environments.
Future research should adopt longitudinal designs to examine how endorsement effec ts, Brand Trust, and Purchase
Intention evolve over time. This would provide stronger evidence on causal direction. Future studies should also compare
consumers across cultural settings and sports contexts, including niche and mass sports, to assess whether these relationships
remain stable across settings. In addition, future research may extend the model by incorporating psychographic variables such
