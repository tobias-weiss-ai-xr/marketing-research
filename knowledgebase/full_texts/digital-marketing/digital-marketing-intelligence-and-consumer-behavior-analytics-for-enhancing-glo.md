Abstract
Companies are increasingly seeing consumers signal their preferences with constant clicks, likes,
shares, and purchases across search, social, mobile, and commerce channels  an environment in
which global brands are competing for the consumer voice. Even with this transition to digital,
numerous companies still rely on regular market research and disjointed decision -making
practices for their brands. This research aims to investigate the interaction between digital
marketing intelligence capability and consumer behavior analytics to increase the effectiven ess
of global brand management strategies and, as a result, total brand performance. Based on the
dynamic capabilities and brand equity theory, we propose and test a sequential model that
includes digital marketing intelligence capability as an antecedent of the adoption of consumer
behavior analytics, which in turn has as its consequences an increase in the effectiveness of global
brand management and brand performance. A survey was conducted with 300 senior brand and
marketing managers from companies from five regions of the world and analysed using partial
least squares structural equation modelling (PLS -SEM). Empirical results show that digital
marketing intelligence capability has a strong impact on digital marketing analytics use (β = 0.62),
that digital marketing analytics use has a moderate impact on global brand management (indirect
β = 0.29, explaining around 59% of the indirect relationship), and that the relationship between
digital marketing analytics use and global brand management effectiveness is partially mediated
by global brand management. The model as a whole accounts  for 41% of the variance in brand
performance. This study offers an integrated and empirically supported understanding of the
link between data-informed insights and the strategic brand benefits, and offers a roadmap for
managers to allocate their resource s among capability development, analytics integration, and
global brand governance.
Keywords: Digital Marketing Intelligence; Consumer Behavior Analytics; Global Brand Management;
Brand Performance; Dynamic Capabilities; PLS-SEM.

I. INTRODUCTION
Brands are undoubtedly part of the most valuable intangible assets of multinational
companies, and the challenge has always been to balance local consumer sensitivity with a unified
global brand image when managing it in multiple international markets  (Fuster et al., 2019 ;
Cornelli et al., 2023) . But the operating context of this balance has changed completely. Brands
today are influenced as much by the online "buzz" and actions of consumers, occurring through
constant search, comparison, sharing, and buying, as they are by formal brand communication.
Modern brands are impacted by the online "buzz" and activiti es of consumers, as they are
constantly searching, comparing, sharing, and buying. For global brand managers, the strategic
issue isn't just whether they have digital data, but whether that data can be turned into timely,
credible insights that can lift th e decision -making process simultaneously at multiple
geographical markets.   The growth of digital channels and the data they provide has been well
reported, and the impact of multichannel journeys on customer experience and of big data
analytics on consumer understanding has been explored in different research areas, but each of
these has largely developed as a separate stream. In particular, research on digital marketing
often emphasizes channel and campaign effectiveness, with analytics studies more focu sed on
technical approaches and data processing, while inquiries on brand management are more
focused on brand positioning and equity. Therefore, the organizational mechanisms between
digital market intelligence and consumer behavior analytics, and between  consumer behavior
analytics and the quality of global brand governance decisions, have not been studied empirically
to any great extent.
 This separation of disciplines is a peculiar theoretical and managerial challenge in three ways.
For one, firms invest significantly in data platforms and analytics expertise, yet returns to the
organization have been as varied as the resources themselves, meaning that while investments in
resources can be an effective means to improve the brand, they are not alone. Secondly, the
empirical evidence is largely from the single -country or single -sector context, which is not
representative of the intricate cross -border coordination issues that multinational brands may
encounter. Third, the literature seldom explores the direct impacts of advanced analytics on
brand performance or its indirect role in improving the operations of brand management, which
is crucial for strategic resource allocation.   This study aims to explore and analyze the combined
effects of digital marketing intelligence capability and consumer behavior analytics on improving
global brand management strategies and overall brand performance in order to address these
gaps.
The research has three main goals: (1) conceptualization and measurement of digital
marketing intelligence capability and consumer behavior analytics use in a global branding
context; (2) investigation of the structural relationships between these two orga nizational
resources and global brand management effectiveness; and (3) testing the influence of global
brand management effectiveness as the key mechanism in translating the intelligence and
insights gained through the use of consumer behavior analytics into concrete brand performance.
This study has four main contributions. First, it brings together three distinct streams of literature
that were previously isolated and establishes a sequence. Second, it offers strong empirical data,
based on 300 senior managers who work across five world regions, which goes beyond single -

II. LITERATURE REVIEW AND HYPOTHESIS DEVELOPMENT
2.1 Global Brand Management and Brand Equity
The theory of brand equity sets out the basic processes by which brands create organizational
value. In subsequent years, researchers conceptualized brand equity as a multi -dimensional
matrix that includes brand awareness, perceived quality, brand associat ions, and brand loyalty,
all of which increase or decrease the value of the product or service to consumers  (Fuster et al.,
2019). In later years, Keller grounded this construct within consumers' memory structures,
claiming that brand knowledge, a construct made up of awareness and image, creates the
differential consumer response that makes up customer-based brand equity (Cornelli et al., 2023).
In the case of multinational brands, this knowledge structure is developed in different countries,
cultures, languages, and markets, and involves a constant process of balancing the adaptation to
the local market and the standardization of the brand.
The hyper -connected digital context has put key tenets of these traditional models under
strain, contemporary scholarship claims. Brands are increasingly co -created with active
consumers, and symbolic meanings spread quickly across borders, while the boundaries between
formal corporate communication and consumer-generated discourse have broken down (Berg et
al., 2020). Social media platforms offer fundamental components of social interaction (identity,
conversation, sharing, presence, relationships, and reputation) that companies need to
understand to be able to engage in online communities in a way that is credible  (Vučinić, 2020).
In this study, we estimate global brand management effectiveness as the company's ability to
keep a consistent brand positioning in different countries, to adjust the positioning to the local
consumer context, to coordinate marketing actions among region al subsidiaries, and to interact
with consumers in a complex digital scenario.
2.2 Digital Marketing Intelligence as a Dynamic Capability
Digital marketing has significantly increased the number of touchpoints companies have for
consumers, as well as the amount of data constantly being created by them. Researchers regarded
digital marketing as an interactive ecosystem with technology, consum er behavior, and firm
strategy constantly intermingling, with a focus on the importance of data-driven insights in such

an ecosystem  (Cheng & Qu, 2020) . Likewise, research highlighted that digital connectivity
permeates the world and generates new data sources at the same time as complex organizational
requirements emerge that are not available in many traditional enterprises (Buchak et al., 2018).
The dynamic capabilities perspective offers a suitable theoretical framework to answer these
organizational requirements. The researchers have identified dynamic capabilities as the firm's
capacity to detect opportunities and threats, act upon them, and ad just organizational routines
(Mehrabi et al., 2025). Expanding this view, digital marketing intelligence capability is the
systematic ability of the firm to sense, collect, and integrate market signals from digital sources
(search queries, social media conversations, e -commerce data, customer relationship
management data, etc.) and make them available for brand decisions across different markets.
This is a capability that is the enabling sensing component of an organizational capability
architecture.
2.3 Consumer Behavior Analytics
Data-driven marketing analytics combines empirical data, sophisticated quantitative analysis,
and organized organizational practices to inform managerial decision -making in environments
that have a large volume of data. Analytical value was seen as being realized only when products
are used in practice and not just created in isolation (Pierri & Timmer, 2020). Additionally, they
said that big-data consumer analytics is heavily dependent on internal organizational resources
and people, changing the way organizations understand and react to the needs of consumers
(Zheng et al., 2023). In the customer experience domain, understanding behaviors at each stage
of the customer journey, from pre-purchase, purchase, and post-purchase, has been identified as
an essential foundation for shaping experiences that help develop strong brands (Phan et al.,
2020). Consumer behavior analytics use is measured by how much a business applies analytics
to understand consumer behavior and make strategic brand decisions – including through
customer segmentation, journey mapping, sentiment analysis, and predictive modeling.
2.4 Hypotheses Development
While capabilities focused on sensing digital market signals are the raw materials for advanced
analysis, they don't produce strategic insight on their own. Organizations with more
comprehensive and intelligent intelligence resources also have more comprehensive, timely, and
granular data resources, which significantly reduce the marginal cost of using analytical tools and
techniques, and improve the chances that analytics are used in everyday decision-making (Pierri
& Timmer, 2020 ; Zheng et al., 2023) . Therefore, digital marketing intelligence capability is
expected to have a positive influence on consumer behavior analytics use:
H1: Digital marketing intelligence capability has a positive effect on consumer behavior
analytics use.
Advanced analytics provides the tools that enable the brand manager to discover different
customer segments, trace how the brand meaning changes over time, and predict consumer
reactions to brand interventions in various geographic markets. This empirical expertise helps to
address the key responsibilities of global brand management, namely defining a global
positioning, adapting the brand in the local context, and managing cross -regional operations
(Cornelli et al., 2023 ; Berg et al., 2020) . Hence, the increased application of consumer behavior

analytics should increase the effectiveness of global brand management:
H2: Consumer behavior analytics use has a positive effect on global brand management

Digital marketing intelligence capability can be directly used to improve brand management
effectiveness, in addition to the analytical applications. By monitoring digital discourse along
with market signals in real time, brand teams can identify consumer sentiment shifts and
competitive activity before it is completely analyzed (Vučinić, 2020; Khan et al., 2023). This direct
sensing mechanism allows for rapid tactical adjustments and responsive governance:
H3: Digital marketing intelligence capability has a direct positive effect on global brand

Brand equity is built through positioning, communication, and customer experiences, with
strong brand knowledge directly leading to positive customer response and company results
(Fuster et al., 2019 ; Cornelli et al., 2023) . Companies that manage their brands better on global
markets stand to be able to deliver better brand performance, in the form of stronger brands, more
loyal customers, increased market share and premium pricing compared to their competitors:
H4: Global brand management effectiveness has a positive effect on brand performance.
Advanced analytics can also help deliver performance directly: by optimizing the targeting of
audiences, by delivering precise personalization, and by streamlining customer journeys across
touchpoints, without any formal modifications to the global brand governance structures (Pierri
& Timmer, 2020; Phan et al., 2020).
H5: Consumer behavior analytics use has a direct positive effect on brand performance.
Combining all of these theoretical claims suggests a linear series of mediations. Grounded in
the dynamic capabilities logic of sensing, seizing, and transforming, intelligence capability is
expected to influence brand management largely because its outputs are converted into analytical
insights [10]. In the same way, analytics is anticipated to help in brand performance as it will
enable efficient brand management processes:
H6: Consumer behavior analytics use mediates the relationship between digital marketing
intelligence capability and global brand management effectiveness.
H7: Global brand management effectiveness mediates the relationship between consumer
behavior analytics use and brand performance.
III. CONCEPTUAL FRAMEWORK AND METHODOLOGY
3.1 Conceptual Framework
There are four basic constructs that are sequential within the conceptual framework. Digital
marketing intelligence capability is the antecedent resource at the foundation. Consumer
behavior analytics use acts as the first mediator, capturing how this resource is operationalized
into analytical practice. Global brand management effectiveness is the second mediator, which is
the process that translates these insights into synchronized brand decisions in various countries
around the world. Lastly, brand performance is the goal of a brand. Direct paths between

3.2 Research Design, Sample, and Data Collection
A cross-sectional survey design was used to validate the theoretical model using senior brand,
marketing, and digital managers as the respondents. The target population was enterprises that
sell a branded product/service in at least three countries. Participants were recruited via
professional networks and commercial contact databases, with an essential screening process for
those with first-hand experience in strategic brand and/or marketing decision-making.
A total of 1240 qualified managers were invited to complete the survey over a 10-week period,
with 25.2% (300) completing it. As potential non-response bias is evaluated, comparisons of early
and late respondents were made on each primary construct and on each of the firm demographic
variables; no significant differences were found, suggesting that non -response bias is not likely
to seriously affect the results. The final sample is broadly geographically and sectorally
representative, with five world regions and five industry sectors with different firm sizes, as
described in Table 1.

3.3 Measurement Scales
All constructs were multi -item reflective scales that were measured with seven -point Likert
scales ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 7 (strongly agree). The items used for measuring each

construct were modified from existing scales used in the marketing and brand management
literature and rephrased to be appropriate for the global branding context. The questionnaire was

Digital marketing intelligence capability was assessed in terms of six constructs that reflect

The use of consumer behavior analytics was evaluated using five items related to using

Six items were used to assess the effectiveness of global brand management, focused on

Four measures were used to assess brand performance, measuring brand strength, customer
loyalty, market share growth, and price premium over major brands.
3.4 Data Analysis Procedures
Partial least squares structural equation modeling (PLS -SEM) was used for estimating the
structural equation model, which is appropriate for predictive models in which mediating
variables are numerous and the sample size is moderate. The analytical procedu re was a two -
stage protocol. The measurement model was assessed on the reliability of the indicators, internal
consistency reliability, convergent validity, and discriminant validity. Second, the structure
model was evaluated for collinearity, path coefficient significance, explained variance (R2), effect
size (f2) and predictive relevance (Q2). Hypothesis testing and indirect effect evaluations were
conducted using a non-parametric bootstrapping procedure that sampled 5,000 times. We used
Harman's single factor test (with the single largest factor explaining 34.8 %of the variance) and a
full collinearity check (with all variance inflation factors under 3.3) to intensively check for
potential common method bias.
IV. RESULTS
4.1 Measurement Model Evaluation
Psychometric evaluation of the measurement model showed good levels of measurement
quality for all of the study constructs. All outer loadings for the indicators were above the cut off
value of 0.70. The internal consistency reliability was found to be strong with Cronbach's α values
and composite reliability above the recommended level of 0.70. Average variance extracted (AVE)
for each construct was greater than .50, which met the convergent validity criterion. Fornell -
Larcker criterion was employed to validate discriminant validity by ensuring that the AVE square
root for each construct was greater than the highest inter -construct correlation, and heterotrait-
monotrait (HTMT) ratio was used to validate discriminant validity by keeping the ratio of each
construct less than 0.85, which is the conservative threshold for the ratio.

4.2 Structural Model Assessment
All the inner variance inflation factor (VIF) values were below safety limits in collinearity
diagnostics, showing no multicollinearity problems. The structural model showed high
explanatory power, as a significant part of the variance of the endogenous va riables was
explained by the model, and blindfolding procedures were used to confirm the good predictive
relevance (Q2>0). A standardized root mean square residual (SRMR) of 0.052 further validates
the model fit, and falls below the maximum of 0.08 recommended. There were no statistically
significant differences found for the control variables on any of the endogenous constructs.
A combination of the results from the structural path estimations and the hypothesis testing is
presented in Table 3 and Figure 1. The empirical support for H1 is strong as digital marketing
intelligence (DMI) had a significant positive impact on the use of consumer behaviour analytics.
In turn, analytics have highly improved global brand management effectiveness (H2). The
capability of intelligence was also directly positively related to the effectiveness of brand
management (H3). In addition, the effectiveness of global bran d management was a strong
predictor of brand performance (H4), while use of analytics had a direct positive association with
brand performance (H5).
Both sequential pathways (H6 and H7) were confirmed by mediation analyses. The total effect
was mediated by an intermediate variable —namely, consumer behaviour analytics —which
accounted for most of the effect. Likewise, the effectiveness of global brand ma nagement was a
partial mediator between the use of analytics and brand performance. The total indirect sequence
relating intelligence capability to brand performance was highly significant when combined.

Good structural stability was confirmed in multi-group invariance testing across the primary

intelligence capability to the use of analytics were found across regions, while all other path
coefficients were very similar, and the conceptual model was found to be robust across regions.
V. DISCUSSION
The empirical results lend strong support to the proposed sequential relationship between
digital marketing intelligence capability, digital marketing consumer behaviour analytics use,
global brand management effectiveness and brand performance. The highest structural
correlation was found between intelligence capability and the use of analytics. This result
reinforces the view that the ability to conduct analysis at a high level is rooted in the capacity of
the organisation to provide and combine data, and not just a technical tool. Companies that have
developed systematic digital sensing capabilities are much more likely to have consumer
analytics as part of the normal way of doing business, while following closely the "se nsing,
seizing, and transforming" logic of dynamic capabilities theory.  Secondly, analytics use is the
most important way in which intelligence capability improves the effectiveness of global brand
management. The indirect path dominated the direct path, and amounted to the bulk of the
overall effect. This finding contradicts the notion that the deployment of digital monitoring
infrastructure and platforms alone is enough. Although the direct path is a major tactical
advantage, most of the organizational advantage comes with a more thorough behavioral
analysis of the raw digital signals.

The second important mediation finding indicates that the effectiveness of global brand
management partially mediates the effect of analytics on brand performance. Therefore, analytics
can help to improve firm results by improving tactical efficiency (personalized targeting,
optimized customer journey across touchpoints, etc.) and strategic brand governance (consistent
positioning across countries but local adaptation, etc.) This latter strategic path connects the
empirical results with the brand equity theory, thereby establishing the direct link between the
way a brand is governed and the way it is experienced and thus its impact on the consumer's
knowledge structures and market performance.
The findings also provide insights into how brands play in hyper-connected markets. Today's
brands are constantly formed through the interplay of global consumer conversation and
interactions in online communities, so brand management demands a constant st ream of live,
real-time behavioral data. This is reflected in the high -level correlation between intelligence
capability and brand management, which is seen in functional approaches to digital and social
media monitoring. Similarly, the structural relation ships are supported by multi -group
robustness checking, which further indicates that the relationships are present in various regional
contexts. The slightly higher first stage effect of the Asia-Pacific region is potentially indicative of
a higher prevalence of mobile and platform-based consumer engagement in these markets, but is
something which needs to be studied further in its own right.   In general, this study enhances
the integrated knowledge on digital marketing by proving that there is a sequentia l
organizational structure between digital technology, digital data and digital consumer behavior.
The real value is in the identification of the global brand management as the key organizational
nexus in which analytical knowledge is effectively transformed into enduring brand success.
 5.1 Theoretical Implications
This study brings to the international branding discourse the notion of dynamic capabilities,
showing how the brand's sensing capabilities first impact brand performance through analytic
routines and then through brand governance. Further, it adds to the literature on brand equity
by defining data-driven organizational routines as an important empirical factor for good brand
governance. Last but not least, it helps to explain how to handle the marketing analytics literature:
it se parates out the direct impact on tactical performance from indirect influence on strategic
governance paths.
5.2 Managerial Implications
Four practical recommendations are drawn from this. First, managers have to think of
intelligence operations and analytics as an interdependent chain, not individual investments, by
coordinating the collection of intelligence with the processing of intelligence. Second, companies
should proactively integrate consumer behaviour  analytics into their routine brand decision -
making processes, and require evidence -based data when reviewing positioning and making
adaptation decisions. Third, brand teams from multinational companies need to create joint data
governance processes to mak e sure that insights developed locally feed into cross -market
coordination. Both tactical efficiency and strategic governance returns should be assessed by the
executive leadership as the fourth step in assessing brand performance.

VI. CONCLUSION
The study analyzed the interaction between digital marketing intelligence capability and the
consumer behaviour analysis to improve the strategies of managing the global brands and overall
brand performance. The findings highlight that the capability to use digital marketing
intelligence significantly explains the use of consumer behaviour  analytics, analytics use acts as
the main driver of brand management's lift on the global level, and global brand management
effectiveness partially mediates the relationship between analytics and brand performance, using
empirical data from 300 senior brand and marketing managers across five world regions.
The research provides academic researchers with an integrated and empirically supported
research model that is able to connect three largely separate streams of research—those in digital
marketing, marketing analytics, and global brand management—under the theoretical umbrella
of dynamic capabilities and brand equity theory. The results show that for practitioners, data
collection is not enough to gain competitive advantage. Rather, value is created in a structured
organizational process: carefully listen to digital market signals, understand customer behavior,
and integrate the understanding directly into the global brand management processes.
Despite these contributions, there are a number of possibilities for further research. The cross-
sectional design limits clear causal inferences and suggests the need to follow this group
longitudinally to see how these capabilities change over time. Moreover, the use of self-reported
key informants data indicates that future studies might include some market or financial
performance indicators. Lastly, future research may focus on contextual factors like cultural and
linguistic differences across borders, data privacy laws, and how artificial intelligence and
generative technologies are changing brand ecosystems across the globe.
