Abstract. This community service activity aimed to strengthen digital marketing and
customer engagement at KATAMA Coffee & Space in Bandar Lampung through a
Community-Based Research (CBR) approach. The activity was motivated by the increasing
competition among coffee shops and the need for KATAMA to optimize digital
communication while strengthening customer engagement through social media and
community-oriented strategies. The CBR approach involved direct observation, structured
interviews, identification of custome r needs, and collaborative formulation of practical
marketing strategies based on the experiences of customers and the business context.
Data were collected from ten KATAMA customers consisting of eight students and two
private-sector employees, with observations conducted during active business hours. The
findings indicate that customers perceive KATAMA as a comfortable, affordable, and
productive space for studying, working, socializing, and spending time. Friends and social
media platforms, particularly Instagram and TikTok, were identified as important
channels through which customers discovered and developed interest in KATAMA. The
community-based analysis further identified opportunities to strengthen digital content
emphasizing KATAMA's 24 -hour operation, comfortable atmosphere, facilities, menu
variety, and customer experiences. The activity also highlighted several areas requiring
attention, including menu innovation, facility cleanliness, and the development of
community-oriented programs. Based on these findings, a digital marketing and customer
engagement strategy was formulated around the positioning of KATAMA as a “Student &
Productivity Hub,” supported by interactive social media content, student -focused
programs, community events, and customer -generated content. The CBR approach
demonstrates the value of integrating community knowledge and customer experiences
into practical digital marketing development for local businesses.
Keywords:  Community-Based Research; Digital Marketing; Customer Engagement; Social
Media; Coffee Shop; Community Service.

already possesses valuable community and customer assets that can be developed
through a more systematic digital marketing and customer engagement strategy.
 The relevance of social media for customer engagement has also been established in
the broader literature.  Algharabat et al. (2020) , through a systematic review of customer
engagement and social media research, demonstrate that customer engagement has
evolved beyond transactional interactions and increasingly encompasses customers'
behavioral, emotional, and relational interactions with brands through digital platforms. A
systematic review by Chen et al. (2025) similarly identifies likes, comments, and shares as
commonly examined manifestations of customer engagement, while emphasizing the need
for broader approaches to understanding interaction between brands and customers. In
the context of SMEs, the systematic literature review by Yasiukovich & Haddara (2021)
shows that social media can be integrated with customer relationship management to
support customer acquisition, interaction, and relationship development. Meanwhile,
research on digital marketing in community -based enterprises by  Tran et al. (2024)
indicates that social media and websites are among the primary digital marketing tools
used by community -based enterprises, with potential effects on customer satisfaction,
entrepreneurial skills, and business performance.

iterative relationship between research and action. Applying these principles to KATAMA
creates a methodological novelty: digital marketing is not introduced as a generic
promotional package, but is developed from evidence generated through direct
interaction with customers and the business context and subsequently translated into
practical customer-engagement strategies.

coffee-shop context. Rather than treating KATAMA merely as a business case for

findings that customers use KATAMA for studying, working, socializing, and other
productive activities, while its 24 -hour operation, Wi -Fi, power outlets, free printing
service, menu variety, and comfortable spaces constitute important perceived -value
assets. In this sense, the novelty is not simply the use of social media, but the participatory
process of converting community and customer knowledge into context -specific digital
marketing and engagement strategies.

methodological. Practically, strengthening KATAMA's digital marketing capacity can help

2. Methods
 This community service activity employed a Community -Based Research (CBR)
approach to strengthen digital marketing and customer engagement at KATAMA Coffee &
Space, Bandar Lampung. CBR was selected because it enables the community or partner to
participate actively in identifying problems, providing contextual knowledge, interpreting
findings, and formulating practical solutions  (Arriola-Pacheco et al., 2025) . The activity
was conducted at KATAMA Coffee & Space, located on Jalan Letjen Alamsyah Ratu
Prawiranegara No. 86, Way Halim Permai, Bandar Lampung. The approach positioned
KATAMA not merely as an object of observation but as a community -business partner
involved in the identification of digital marketing needs and the development of customer-
engagement strategies. This orientation was adapted from the principles of community -
based research, particularly collaboration, mutual learning, contextual problem
identification, and action-oriented outcomes.
 The first stage was community and partner engagement, which focused on
establishing communication with KATAMA Coffee & Space and understanding the
business context, customer characteristics, existing promotional practices, and perceived
challenges in digital marketing. Direct observation was conducted during the active
operating period between 10:00 a.m. and 1:00 p.m. in May 2026. The observation
examined customer density, customer activities, service flow, seating patterns, duration of
visits, interactions between customers and staff, and the utilization of facilities. The
activity also considered KATAMA's existing business assets, including its comf ortable

 The second stage involved needs assessment and community -based data collection.
Structured interviews were conducted with ten customers who were selected purposively

willingness to revisit. Particular attention was given to the role of Instagram, TikTok,
friends, and other forms of digital or interpersonal communication in shaping customers'

organized into themes related to customer motivation, perceived value, social influence,
digital media exposure, satisfaction, and customer suggestions.
 The third stage was participatory analysis and strategy formulation. Findings from
observations and interviews were discussed in relation to KATAMA's existing business
assets and digital marketing needs. The analysis identified several opportunities for
strengthening customer engagement, particularly through Instagram and TikTok content
that highlights the coffee shop's atmosphere, 24 -hour operation, facilities, menu variety,
and customer experiences. The findings also indicated the need to strengthen KATAM A's
positioning as a space for studying, working, socializing, and productive activities. Based
on the community -based analysis, the proposed strategy emphasized the development of
interactive digital content, student -oriented programs, community events, customer -
generated content, and communication that reinforces KATAMA's identity as a Student &
Productivity Hub. The strategy was formulated by connecting customer perspectives with
the practical characteristics and resources available at the partner location.
 The final stage was action -oriented development and evaluation, in which the
identified findings and strategic recommendations were translated into practical
directions for improving KATAMA's digital marketing and customer engagement. The
evaluation focused on the relevance of the proposed strategies to customer needs, the
utilization of KATAMA's existing assets, and the feasibility of implementing the strategies
through available digital platforms and community activities. The activity did not measure
changes in sales turnover, follower growth, engagement rate, or other quantitative
business-performance indicators because such measurements were not included in the
original data collection. Instead, the outcomes were assessed descriptively through the
identification of customer needs, digital communication opportunities, strategic
recommendations, and the alignment between customer experiences and the proposed
marketing initiatives. This approach ensured that the community -service program
remained grounded in the evidence obtained from the partner and its customers.

3.2. Digital Media Exposure and Customer Engagement Opportunities
 KATAMA Coffee & Space is discovered by customers through a combination of
interpersonal communication and digital media exposure. Based on the interviews,
several respondents stated that they first learned about KATAMA from friends, while
others encountered information about the coffee shop through Instagram and TikTok.
This finding demonstrates that the process of customer awareness does not necessarily
begin when customers physically encounter the coffee shop. Instead, awareness may be
constructed beforeh and through conversations within social networks and exposure to
digital content. In the context of contemporary consumer behavior, friends function as an
interpersonal information channel, while Instagram and TikTok operate as mediated
channels that extend the visibility of KATAMA beyond its physical location. For example, a
student who hears from a friend that KATAMA is suitable for studying may subsequently
search for its Instagram account, examine photographs of the interior, look at the
available menu,  and watch TikTok videos showing the atmosphere before deciding
whether to visit. Thus, the initial relationship between customers and KATAMA may
actually begin in the communication environment rather than at the coffee shop itself.
 The interviews further indicate that visual information plays an important role in
shaping customers’ initial perceptions of KATAMA. Respondents referred particularly to
information concerning the menu, atmosphere, facilities, and activities. This is significant
because coffee -shop consumption is not exclusively determined by the physical product
being sold. Customers may also evaluate the atmosphere, comfort, facilities, and activities
that accompany the consumption experience. A photograph showing a comfo rtable corner
for studying, for instance, communicates something different from a photograph that
merely displays a cup of coffee. Similarly, a short TikTok video showing customers
working on laptops, interacting with friends, attending an event, or spending time in the
coffee shop can communicate the social character of the venue more effectively than a
conventional promotional poster. Therefore, digital media can function as a pre -visit
experience, allowing prospective customers to construct an initial mental image of
KATAMA before they physically arrive. In this sense, Instagram and TikTok are not merely
promotional channels but also spaces where the identity and experiential value of the
coffee shop are communicated.
 Another important finding is the role of reference groups, particularly friends, in
creating awareness and encouraging customer engagement. Recommendations from
friends represent interpersonal communication that may carry a different level of
credibility from commercial advertising because the information comes through an
existing social relationship. For example, when a student tells a classmate, “KATAMA is
comfortable for working on assignments because it is open 24 hours,” the message

contains not only information about the business but also a personal recommendation
based on experience. This interpersonal recommendation may then interact with digital
communication: the prospective customer can search for KATAMA on Instagram or
TikTok to verify what the friend has described. Consequently, interpersonal
communication and digital communication should not be treated as two completely
separate pathways. Instead, they can reinforce one another, with word -of-mouth
generating curiosity and social media providing visual confirmation. This finding provides
an important basis for KATAMA to develop communication strategies that encourage
customers to share their experiences voluntarily and make those experiences visible
within their own social networks.
 The community -based interpretation of the findings subsequently shifts the
orientation of KATAMA's digital communication from product promotion toward
customer engagement. A purely promotional approach would primarily emphasize menu
items, prices, discounts, and product availability. Such content may provide useful
information, but it does not fully represent the broader meaning that customers associate
with KATAMA. If customers already perceive the venue as a space for studying, working,
socializing, and p articipating in activities, then digital communication can be designed to
reflect these experiences. For example, Instagram and TikTok content can present a day at
KATAMA, show students completing assignments, document customers working
remotely, introduce different areas of the venue, highlight the 24 -hour operating concept,
and present community activities. The objective is not simply to tell audiences, KATAMA
sells coffee, but to communicate, KATAMA is a space in which particular activities and
experiences can take place. This distinction is important because customer engagement
involves a relationship between the audience and the brand or business rather than
simply exposure to a promotional message.
 The fifth aspect concerns the opportunity to transform customers from passive
audiences into active participants in KATAMA's digital communication ecosystem. The
findings open opportunities for user-generated content, customer testimonials, interactive
posts, community collaborations, and other forms of participatory communication. For
instance, KATAMA could encourage customers to share photographs of their study
sessions, work activities, community meetings, or social gatherings at the venue and tag
the co ffee shop's social -media account. Students or local communities could also be
invited to collaborate on events, discussions, creative activities, or digital campaigns.
Customer testimonials could provide experiential narratives that are more personal than
conventional advertising messages. In this model, communication flows in multiple
directions: KATAMA communicates with customers, customers respond through
engagement and content creation, and the resulting content can subsequently reach new
audiences through customers' own social networks. The strategic opportunity therefore
lies not merely in increasing the number of promotional posts but in building a
participatory communication environment in which customers become part of the story of
KATAMA.

3.3. Community-Based Digital Marketing Strategy for KATAMA
 The final finding of the community -service activity demonstrates that the digital
marketing strategy developed for KATAMA Coffee & Space was not formulated solely from
a promotional perspective, but emerged from the intersection between customer
experiences, business assets, and community participation. This distinction is important
because the CBR approach positions community members not merely as objects from
whom information is collected, but as participants whose experiences contribute to
defining problems and formulating relevant solutions. Racine et al. (2022) emphasize that
community-based participatory research should connect knowledge generated from the
community with practical action and should be developed through collaborative
relationships among researchers, community members, and relevant organizations. In the
KATAMA context, this principle is reflected in the formulation of a positioning direction as
a Student & Productivity Hub , which emerged from the observed pattern of customers
using the venue not only for consuming food and beverages but also for studying, working,
meeting, and conducting productive activities. Thus, the proposed positioning is not an
arbitrary branding concept created by the activity team; rather, it represents an
interpretation of the existing relationship between customer activities and KATAMA's
physical and operational assets. The community -service intervention therefore
transforms empirical customer knowledge into a strategic communication direction that
is more closely connected to the actual character of the business. This is consistent with
the broader principle of community -engaged research that findings should be translated
into contextually meaningful action rather than remaining merely as descriptive
information.
 The proposed positioning of KATAMA as a Student & Productivity Hub also illustrates
the importance of developing a distinctive value proposition in digital marketing. The
finding indicates that KATAMA possesses several assets that can support this positioning,
including a comfortable atmosphere, facilities suitable for study and work, affordable
offerings, and a 24 -hour operating concept. In digital marketing, these assets need to be

and behavioral activation in consumer -brand interactions. In practical terms, this means
that a customer who photographs a study session at KATAMA, uploads it to Instagram,

developing through processes including connection, interaction, satisfaction, retention,
commitment, advocacy, and engagement. For KATAMA, therefore, digital engagement
should not be measured only through likes or views. It should ideally be connected with
broader indicators such as customer interaction, repeat visits, participation in community
activities, testimonials, recommendations, and customer advocacy.

4. Conclusions
 The community -service activity demonstrates that the Community -Based Research
(CBR) approach can provide a context -specific foundation for strengthening digital
marketing and customer engagement at KATAMA Coffee & Space, Bandar Lampung. The
findings indicate that customers perceive KATAMA not merely as a coffee shop but as a
comfortable, affordable, and productive space for studying, working, socializing, and
spending time. Friends, Instagram, and TikTok were identified as important channels
through which customers discovered KATAMA, while the 24 -hour operating concept,
atmosphere, facilities, menu variety, and customer experiences represented key
communication assets. Based on these findings, the activity formulated a digital marketing
direction positioning KATAMA as a “Student & Productivity Hub,” supported by
experiential and interactive content, student -oriented programs, community activities,
and customer -generated content. The principal contribution of the activity lies in
connecting customer experiences with the business's existing assets and translating these
insights into practical recommendations for strengthening KATAMA's digital presence and
community engagement.
 This activity has several limitations that should be considered when interpreting the
findings. First, the customer data were obtained from a relatively small group of ten
respondents, consisting of eight students and two private-sector employees; therefore, the
findings represent the experiences of the participating customers and should not be
generalized to all KATAMA customers or coffee -shop consumers in Bandar Lampung.
Second, data collection was conducted within a specific observation period and relied
primarily on structured interviews and direct observation, without longitudinal
measurement of changes in customer engagement. Third, the proposed “Student &
Productivity Hub” positioning and digital marketing strategies were formulated as

 Future community-service activities should therefore move beyond the formulation of

the proposed digital marketing strategy. Subsequent activities could involve
implementing selected Instagram and TikTok content programs, developing user -
generated content campaigns, facilitating student and community collaborations, and
evaluating changes in measurable indicators such as reach, engagement, customer
participation, repeat visit s, testimonials, and digital interactions before and after
implementation. Future CBR activities should also involve a broader and more diverse
range of customers, including students, employees, community groups, and other
customer segments, to obtain a more comprehensive understanding of KATAMA's market
and community relationships. In addition, future activities could integrate periodic
customer feedback with improvements in menu innovation, facility cleanliness, and
community-oriented services. Such an it erative process would strengthen the connection
between community knowledge, business improvement, digital communication, and
measurable outcomes, allowing KATAMA's digital marketing strategy to develop from an
initial context -specific recommendation into a continuously evaluated and community -
responsive practice.
