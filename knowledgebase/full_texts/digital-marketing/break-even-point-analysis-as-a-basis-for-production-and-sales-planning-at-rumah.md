ABSTRACT
This study aims to analyze the application of Break -Even Point (BEP) as a
basis for production and sales planning at Rumah Kali Laya MSME in Bogor
Regency. Using a descriptiv e qualitative approach with a single -case study
method, data were collected through observation, in -depth interviews, and
documentation of financial reports for the November 2025 period. The results
show that the two product lines, Emping Beras Kencur and Keripik Kimpul,
have the same selling price of Rp10.000 per unit but different variable cost
structures and contribution margins. Emping Beras Kencur generates a
contribution margin of Rp5.315 per unit with a BEP of 0,88 units (practically
1 unit), while Keripik Kimpul generates a contribution margin of Rp1.200 per
unit with a BEP of 3,88 units (practically 4 units). The actual sales volumes
of both products are far above their break-even points, indicating a high margin
of safety. Nevertheless, the recordi ng of fixed costs remains very limited;
therefore, systematic cost separation and stronger digital marketing through
social media are still required to support business sustainability and efficiency.

1. INTRODUCTION
Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) play an important role in the Indonesian
economy. Based on data from Statistics Indonesia reported by the Ministry of MSMEs in July 2025,
there were approximately 65.5 million MSMEs contributing 61.9% of gross domestic product (GDP)
and absorbing around 97% of the national workforce (Aprionis, 2025). According to Government
Regulation (PP) Number 7 of 2021, a microenterprise is a business with capital of no more than Rp1
billion (excluding land and buildings used for business premises) or annual sales of no more than
Rp2 billion (Pemerintah Pusat Republik Indonesia, 2021).
Despite their major role, many MSMEs still manage their finances in a simple manner.
Business owners often determine sales and profit targets based on estimates without separating fixed
costs from variable costs (Silitonga et al., 2026 ). As a result, owners find it difficult to answer the
most basic question: how many products must be sold for the business to avoid a loss? This question
can be answered using break-even point (BEP) analysis, which identifies the condition in which total
revenue equals total costs so that the business neither earns a profit nor incurs a loss (Garrison et al.,
2021). BEP analysis has been applied to various food MSMEs, for example, a cracker MSME in
Karawang (Widyasari et al., 2024) and a shrimp-paste cracker business in Lamongan (Arrizqi et al.,
2020).
Rumah Kali Laya MSME is located at Jalan Raya Susukan, Gang Swadaya No. 57, Susukan
Village, Bojonggede District, Bogor Regency. The business produces two traditional snacks, namely
Keripik Kimpul and Emping Beras Kencur. Its products are halal -certified, have a shelf life of
approximately three months, and are sold through consignment points at local shops, direct sales at
Car Free Day events, and orders via WhatsApp.
Initial data from Project Based Learning (PBL) activities in November 2025 showed that
Rumah Kali Laya produced 540 units of Emping Beras Kencur and 276 units of Keripik Kimpul at a
selling price of Rp10.000 per unit. The cost of production (HPP) was Rp4.6 94 and Rp8.814 per unit,
respectively, resulting in a much thinner profit margin for Keripik Kimpul (11,86%) than for Emping
Beras Kencur (53,06%). The initial calculation produced BEP values of 0,88 units and 3,88 units per
month. These figures are very l ow and need to be reassessed because the only recorded fixed cost
was equipment depreciation of Rp9.333 per month. Other costs that may be fixed, such as electricity,
gas, premises rent, or owner wages, had not been separated. If fixed costs are incomplete , the BEP
will appear too low and the owner may consider the business "very safe" when this may not actually
be the case.
Based on the description above, the research questions are as follows:
a. What is the structure of fixed costs and variable costs for each Rumah Kali Laya product?
b. What is the BEP in units and rupiah for each product?
c. What are the contribution margin and margin of safety at the current sales level?
d. How does the BEP change when the selling price, raw material costs, or sales volume
changes?
This study aims to classify business costs into fixed and variable costs, calculate the BEP in
units and rupiah, calculate the contribution margin and margin of safety, and test changes in BEP
under several simple scenarios. The results are expected to help the owner determine the minimum
sales target, evaluate selling prices, and control costs.
In practical terms, this study provides an example of a simple BEP procedure that can be
replicated by other MSME operators with limited financial records.
2. LITERATURE REVIEW

capital or annual sales revenue. For microenterprises, business capital may not exceed Rp1
billion (excluding land and buildings used for business premises) or annual sales may not

exceed Rp2 billion. This regulation updates the criteria previously stipulated in Law
Number 20 of 2008 concerning MSMEs (Pemerintah Pusat Republik Indonesia, 2008, 2021).
To assist MSMEs in preparing financial statements, the Indonesian Institute of
Accountants issued the Financial Accounting Standards for Micro, Small, and Medium
Entities (SAK EMKM), which are simpler  than the standards for larger entities (Ikatan
Akuntan Indonesia, 2016) . These standards regulate the presentation of statements of
financial position, income statements, and notes to the financial statements. Their focus is
reporting rather than analyzing the relationship among costs, volume, and profit. Therefore,
MSMEs need additional analytical tools, one of which is BEP.
2.2 Fixed Costs, Variable Costs, and Semi-Variable Costs
Based on their behavior in relation to product ion volume, costs are divided into
three types (Garrison et al., 2021). Variable costs change in proportion to volume, while the
amount per unit remains constant. Examples include raw materials, cooking oil, and
packaging. Fixed costs do not change within a certain volume range (relevant range), such
as equipment depreciation, premises rent, and fixed salaries. Semi -variable costs co ntain
both fixed and variable components, such as electricity and gas.
In home -based businesses, cost separation is more difficult because equipment,
electricity, and premises are often shared with household needs. However, profit planning
that does not di stinguish between fixed and variable costs tends to produce less accurate
targets (Silitonga et al., 2026).
2.3 Cost of Production (HPP)
HPP comprises all costs incurred to produce a product, namely raw material costs,
direct labor, and factory overhead (Garrison et al., 2021) . HPP per unit is obtained by
dividing total production costs by the number of units produced. Full HPP (full costing)
already includes fixed costs, whereas variable cost per unit includes only costs that change
with volume. Therefore, full HPP should not be used directly as the variable cost per unit in
the BEP formula unless fixed costs are very small.
2.4 Break-Even Point (BEP)
BEP is the sales level at which total revenue equals total costs (Garrison et al., 2021;
Horngren et al., 2015) . Below the BEP, the business incurs a loss, while above the BEP, the
business begins to earn a profit. BEP can be expressed in units and in rupiah:
 BEP (unit) = FC ÷ (P − V) (1)
 BEP (Rp) = FC ÷ CMR (2)
 CMR = (P − V) ÷ P (3)
where FC is the total fixed cost per period, P is the selling price per unit, V is the
variable cost per unit, and CMR is the contribution margin ratio. Because products are sold
in whole units, BEP results in units are rounded up.
BEP calculations are based on several assumptions: (1) the selling price per unit
remains constant; (2) the variable cost per unit remains constant; (3) fixed costs do not change
within the analyzed volume range; (4) all products produced are sold; and (5) the sales mix
does not change when more than one product is involved (Garrison et al., 2021) . Because
these assumptions are rigid, BEP is only an estimate and should be ev aluated periodically
when prices or costs change (Silitonga et al., 2026).

2.5 Contribution Margin, Margin of Safety, and Target Profit
The contribution margin per unit is the difference between the selling price and variable cost
per unit (P − V). This margin is first used to cover fixed costs; the remainder becomes profit. The
margin of safety (MoS) indicates how far sales may decline before the business incurs a loss:
 MoS = [(Actual sales − BEP sales) ÷ Actual sales] × 100% (4)
The higher the MoS, the safer the business. However, the MoS figure is meaningful
only when fixed costs have been fully identified. To plan profit, the number of  units that
must be sold is calculated as follows:
 Q target = (FC + Target profit) ÷ (P − V) (5)
According to Rukman et al. (2025), selling price, fixed costs, and sales volume
determine the BEP; a change in any one of these factors will shift the BEP. Th erefore,
sensitivity analysis is conducted by changing one factor and then recalculating the BEP and
MoS so that the owner can identify which factor has the greatest influence.
2.6 BEP in a Multi-Product Business
Rumah Kali Laya sells two products. Under this condition, there are two ways to
calculate BEP. The first method calculates the BEP of each product after fixed costs are
allocated to the products using a clear basis. The second method calculates a combined BEP
using the weighted -average contribution margin ratio based on the sales mix. The first
method is easier for the owner to understand and shows which product is weaker, so it is
used as the main analysis. The second method is used for comparison.
3. RESEARCH METHODS
This study uses a descriptive qualitative approach with a single-case study method
at Rumah Kali Laya MSME. This approach was selected because the study aims to
understand the phenomenon in depth, supported by quantitative data analysis
(mathematical calculations of costs, sales volume, and the break-even point/BEP) (Sugiyono,
2019). The case study method was selected because the research seeks to understand one
business in depth under real-world conditions (Yin, 2018). Interviews and observations were
used to support the numerical data, for example, to determine whether a cost is fixed or
variable. The results of a single-case study are not intended to be generalized to all MSMEs.
The study was conducted at Rumah Kali Laya MSME, located at Jl. Raya Susukan, Gg.
Swadaya No. 57 RT 002/001, Susukan Village, Bojonggede District, Bogor Regency, during
October–November 2025.
Data were collected using three complementary techniques. First, field observations
were conducted at the production site to examine the production process, record -keeping
practices, and daily operational activities. Second, in-depth interviews were conducted with
Mrs. Selviana, the owner, and MSME members to obtain data on raw material costs, revenue,
business challenges, and marketing channels. Third, documentation was collected from the
company’s informal financial records t o reconstruct structured financial statements. This
triangulation approach ensured the validity and completeness of the data (Sugiyono, 2022).
This study uses two types of data. Primary data consist of direct observations and
interview findings, including details of raw material costs, production volume, and sales
records for November 2025. Secondary data consist of informal financial records maintained
by the company, which serve as the basis for structured financial reconstruction.
Data analysis was conducted in four sequential stages: (1) reconstruction of the
financial statements, including calculation of Cost of Goods Sold (COGS), preparation of
income statements using variable costing and full costing methods, a statement of changes

4. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Rumah Kali Laya MSME in Susukan Village has an advantage in that its products are halal-
certified and have a shelf life of up to three months. However, from a managerial perspective, several
areas still require improvement, particularly the separation of pers onal assets from business assets.
The cost analysis shows a difference in efficiency between the two products. Emping Beras Kencur
has a variable cost per unit of approximately Rp4.685,19 with a contribution margin of Rp5.314,81,
whereas Keripik Kimpul has a variable cost per unit of approximately Rp8.797,10 with a contribution
margin of only Rp1.202,90. This difference results in a lower BEP for Emping Beras Kencur than for
Keripik Kimpul, although both have the same selling price of Rp10.000 per unit.
The BEP analysis shows that the break -even points of both products are below four units,
meaning that actual sales for both products are far above their break-even points. This indicates that,
based on the data and cost classifications used, the MSME has a re latively high margin of safety.
However, differences remain in the cost classifications used in the report, so cost recording should
be carried out more consistently to ensure that subsequent BEP analyses provide a more accurate
picture of the business con dition. In addition to cost considerations, market development is also

5. CONCLUSION
Based on the Br eak-Even Point (BEP) analysis for November 2025, Emping Beras Kencur
reached a break -even point of 0,88 units, or practically 1 unit, with a sales value of approximately
Rp8.781. Meanwhile, Keripik Kimpul reached a break-even point of 3,88 units, or 4 units, with a sales
value of approximately Rp38.798. This difference is influenced by the variable cost per unit, with
Emping Beras Kencur having a lower variable cost and a higher contribution margin than Keripik
Kimpul.
Compared with actual sales of 540 unit s of Emping Beras Kencur and 276 units of Keripik
Kimpul, both products are far above their break -even points. This shows that, based on the cost
structure used, actual sales have been sufficient to cover the costs incurred. The low BEP values also
indicate that the minimum sales required to reach the break -even condition are relatively small.
However, these results should be interpreted with caution because differences remain in the cost
classifications in the MSME report. Therefore, fixed and variable cos ts should be recorded and
separated more consistently so that BEP analysis can produce more accurate results.
From a business-development perspective, the sustainability of Rumah Kali Laya needs to
be supported by more orderly financial record -keeping and stronger digital marketing. The use of
social media and plans to expand the market through campus or university cooperative networks
can help broaden consumer reach and support sales stability.
