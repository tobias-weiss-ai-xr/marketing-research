Abstract
Digital marketing has become an essential strategy for improving the competitiveness of micro, small, and medium-sized enterprises
(MSMEs). However, existing studies have predominantly examined digital marketing adoption as a general organizational
capability, while limited empirical evidence is available regarding whether the intensity of digital marketing engagement is
associated with sales performance among culinary micro-enterprises. This study addresses this gap by investigating the relationship
between digital marketing intensity and sales volume using a quantitative explanatory design based on data from 4,546 culinary
micro-enterprises. Descriptive statistics, Pearson correlation analysis, simple linear regression, independent -samples t-tests, and
one-way ANOVA were employed to evaluate the proposed relationships. The results reveal a significant positive association
between digital marketing intensity and sales volume, with regression analysis indicating that higher digital marketing inten sity
significantly increases sales performance (b = 22.188, p < .001) and explains 27.8% of the observed variation. Consistent findings
from the independent-samples t-test further demonstrate that enterprises with high digital marketing intensity achieve substantially
greater sales than those with low intensity. In contrast, no significant differences in sales volume were identified across nano, micro,
macro, and mega promotional-partner tiers, suggesting that the consistency of digital marketing engagement, rather tha n the scale
of promotional partners, is more strongly associated with commercial performance. These findings contribute to the digital
marketing and MSME literature by demonstrating that digital marketing intensity constitutes a distinct explanatory dimens ion
beyond simple technology adoption. The study also provides practical implications for policymakers and training providers by
emphasizing sustained digital engagement as a more effective strategy for enhancing the sales performance of culinary micro -
enterprises.

Keywords: Digital marketing intensity, sales performance, culinary micro-enterprises, MSMEs, digital engagement

1. Introduction
Digital transformation has fundamentally reshaped contemporary marketing practices, including within the micro,
small, and medium-sized enterprise (MSME) sector. The increasing accessibility of digital platforms has created new
opportunities for MSMEs to e xpand market reach, strengthen customer engagement, and improve sales performance
beyond the limitations of conventional marketing channels. Empirical evidence from developing -country contexts
indicates that digital marketing adoption can contribute signif icantly to MSME performance by facilitating broader
market access, enhancing customer interaction, and supporting more efficient promotional activities ( Djakasaputra et
al., 2021; Bruce et al., 2023; Sharabati et al., 2024). This development is particularly relevant in Indonesia, where
MSMEs constitute a substantial component of the national economy, while the level of digital readiness and
technological capability among business owners remains heterogeneous (Badri & Amrina , 2023). Consequently, the
availability of digital technologies alone does not necessarily guarantee effective digital transformation at the firm level,
particularly when entrepreneurs lack the competencies required to utilize these technologies strategically.
This issue is especially important in the culinary sector, which represents one of the largest and most competitive
segments of the Indonesian MSME landscape. The high intensity of competition within the culinary industry requires
business owners to continuously strengthen their ability to attract customers, communicate product value, and maintain
market visibility. Digital marketing provides considerable potential for achieving these objectives through social media,
digital content, online marketplaces, and other technology -enabled promotional channels. However, evidence from

culinary MSMEs in Malang, Karawang, and Toba Regency suggests that many business owners continue to face
substantial limitations in their understanding and technical skills related to digital marketing, including content creation,
social media management, online promotion, and the use of digital information for marketing decisions ( Afifa et al.,
2024; Maulana, 2024; Marbun & Simanjuntak, 2021). These limitations may prevent businesses from converting the
availability of digital channels into measurable commercial outcomes.
The challenge, therefore, is not merely whether MSMEs use digital marketing, but whether business owners possess
sufficient capabilities to implement it effectively. Previous studies have demonstrated that appropriate digital marketing
practices can improve customer engagement and sales performance among MSMEs ( Khaerani & Sudarmiatin, 2022;
Erwin et al., 2023). Nevertheless, the effectiveness of digital marketing is likely to depend on the knowledge and
practical skills of the individuals responsible for its implementation. In this context, digital marketing training can be
viewed as an important capacity-building intervention that bridges the gap between entrepreneurs’ intention to adopt
digital technologies and their actual ability to use these technologies effectively for business development. Training may
enhance participants’ understanding of digital platforms, improve their ability to develop relevant promotional content,
strengthen their capacity to manage online interactions, and ultimately support more effective conversion of digital
exposure into purchasing activity.
Existing literature provides considerable evidence regarding the relationship between digital marketing and MSME
performance, but an important research gap remains. Positive effects of digital marketing adoption on MSME
performance have been identified in Jordan and Ghana, respectively (Sharabati et al., 2024; Bruce et al., 2023), while
comparable findings have been reported among small and medium -sized enterprises in Nepal (Dhungana , 2024). In
Southeast Asia, digital marketing strategies for expanding the market of Indonesian creative MSME products have been
examined through a systematic literature review (Lubis et al., 2025), while the contribution of e -marketing to SME
performance in the post-pandemic new-normal context has been emphasized (Etim et al., 2022). However, these studies
have generally focused on digital marketing adoption, implementation, or strategy as the primary explanatory factor
rather than examining training itself as a measurable intervention.
The gap becomes more apparent when considering the culinary MSME context. Digital marketing practices among
culinary MSMEs in Malang have been investigated from a phenomenological perspective (Afifa et al., 2024), while the
transformation of online marketing among culinary businesses in Karawang has been explored (Maulana, 2024). The
role of marketing content and viral marketing in generating customer engagement in culinary businesses has been
examined (Erwin et al., 2023), while the role of digital marketing in developing promotional activities among culinary
MSMEs has similarly been emphasized (Hasvia  et al., 2023). Meanwhile, the relationship between digital marketing
implementation and sales volume has been directly examined and reported to show positive and significant effects,
although these studies focused on non -culinary business settings (Taufik urrahman et al., 2023; Reken  et al., 2020).
Furthermore, a systematic literature review of social -media-based MSME marketing strategies highlighted that much
of the existing literature continues to emphasize general digital marketing strategies and adoption rather than structured
training interventions (Ariyati et al., 2024). Methodologically, previous studies have also frequently employed
qualitative descriptive approaches (Afifa et al., 2024; Maulana, 2024) or multivariate structural equation modeling
(Djakasaputra et al., 2021), leaving limited empirical evidence on whether participation in digital marketing training
produces measurable changes in sales volume at the micro-business level.
Accordingly, the present study addresses this research gap by specifically examining micro-enterprises in the culinary
sector that have participated in a digital marketing training program. Rather than treating digital marketing adoption as
a broad organizational capability, this study positions digital marketing training as the independent variable and sales
volume as the dependent variable. The study further incorporates the comparison of average monthly sales before and
after participation in the digital marketing training program to provide a more concrete assessment of changes in
business performance. A quantitative explanatory approach is employed to examine the relationship between training
and sales volume, with statistical testing used to assess the strength and significance of the observed relationship. This
design is intended to provide a relati vely straightforward and replicable empirical framework that can be applied by
training providers, local governments, and other institutions involved in MSME development.
Based on this research gap, the study aims to analyze the implementation of digital marketing training among micro-
enterprises in the culinary sector, examine differences in sales volume before and after participation in the training, and
empirically test whether digital marketing training significantly influences sales volume. The study is expected to make
both theoretical and practical contributions. Theoretically, it extends the digital marketing and MSME literature by
positioning training as a distinct explanatory factor rather than treating digital marketing adoption as a general and
undifferentiated construct. In doing so, it complements previous research that has primarily examined digital marketing
strategies, adoption, and utilization (Sharabati et al., 2024; Etim et al., 2022; Ariyati et al., 2024). Practically, the findings
may provide evidence for evaluating the effectiveness of digital marketing training programs implemented by local
governments, cooperatives and SME agencies, and other development institutions. The results may also  assist
policymakers and training providers in designing more targeted capacity -building programs while helping culinary
micro-entrepreneurs assess the potential business value of investing time and resources in digital marketing training.
Overall, the study seeks to clarify whether strengthening digital marketing capabilities through structured training can
translate into measurable improvements in sales performance among culinary micro-enterprises.

2. Materials and Methods
2.1. Research Design and Approach
This study employed a quantitative explanatory research design to empirically examine the causal relationship
between digital marketing training and sales volume among micro -enterprises in the culinary sector. The explanatory
approach was selected because the study aimed to test the hypothesized effect of digital marketing training as the
independent variable (X) on sales volume as the dependent variable (Y) through statistical analysis. This design enables
the study to quantify the relationship between the two variables and determine whether participation in digital marketing
training is associated with a statistically significant change in sales performance. The quantitative approach is
particularly appropriate because the study relies on measurable indica tors of training implementation and sales
outcomes, including sales volume before and after participation in the training program. The analytical framework is
consistent with previous empirical research examining the relationship between digital marketing practices and MSME
sales performance ( Reken et al., 2020; Taufikurrahman et al., 2023), while extending the analysis by focusing
specifically on digital marketing training as the explanatory factor.
2.1 Population and Sampling
The population of this study comprised micro-enterprise owners operating in the culinary sector who had participated
in a digital marketing training program within the selected study area. A purposive sampling technique was employed
to ensure that the selected participants met the substantive requirements of the research. The inclusion criteria were that
participants: (1) were actively operating a micro -enterprise in the culinary sector; (2) had participated in at least one
digital marketing training program; and (3) maintained sales records for periods both before and after the training. These
criteria were established to ensure that respondents had direct exposure to the training intervention and could provide
measurable information regarding changes in sa les performance. Accordingly, the sampling process prioritized the
relevance and completeness of participant information rather than relying solely on a probabilistic selection procedure.
The required sample size was determined with consideration of the number of eligible participants available in the
study population and the requirements of the quantitative analysis. Because the study specifically targeted business
owners with documented pre- and post-training sales data, all eligible participants were included when the accessible
population was relatively limited. This approach maximized the use of available observations and reduced the potential
loss of relevant empirical information. The  resulting sample therefore represented micro -enterprises that satisfied the
predefined eligibility criteria and possessed sufficient longitudinal sales information to support the assessment of
changes in sales volume following digital marketing training. Such an approach is particularly appropriate for
intervention-focused studies in which the availability of respondents with complete and relevant observations may be
more restrictive than the size of the broader target population. This sampling strategy is  also consistent with
methodological guidance indicating that the appropriateness of a sample size for quantitative instrument-based research
should be evaluated against the study's specific design and objectives rather than a fixed universal threshold (Wh ite,
2022).
2.2 Variables and Operational Definitions
This study examined two main variables: digital marketing training as the independent variable (X) and sales volume
as the dependent variable (Y). Digital marketing training refers to a structured learning intervention designed to improve
the knowledge and  practical capabilities of micro -enterprise owners in utilizing digital platforms for marketing
activities. The variable was operationalized through three dimensions: training content, training delivery, and training
intensity. The indicators included the relevance and comprehensibility of the training materials, the practicality and
applicability of the training methods, and the frequency and duration of participation in the training. Each indicator was
measured using a five -point Likert scale ranging from  1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree), with higher scores
indicating a stronger perception of the quality and intensity of the training received.
Sales volume (Y) refers to the monetary value of sales generated by the participating culinary micro-enterprises and
was measured using the average monthly sales recorded before and after participation in the digital marketing training
program. The measurement consisted of average monthly sales before and after participation in the digital marketing
training program. Sales data were measured on a ratio scale, allowing direct comparison of business performance across
the two observation periods. The use of actual sales records provides an objective measure of business performance and
reduces reliance on subjective assessments by respondents. The operational definitions and measurement scales of the
variables are presented in Table 1.

2.3 Data Types, Sources, and Data Collection Techniques
This study employed both primary and secondary data to generate comprehensive empirical evidence regarding
digital marketing intensity and its relationship with sales performance among culinary micro -enterprises. The use of
multiple data sources was intended to improve the completeness of the dataset while enabling quantitative findings to
be interpreted within their operational context. Primary data were collected directly from eligible enterprises through
structured questionnaires to measure digital marketing training, documented sales records to measure sales volume, and
semi-structured interviews to support the interpretation of the quantitative findings. The structured questionnaire served
as the principal instrument for measuring digital marketing intensity using a five -point Likert scale, capturing key
aspects of enterprises' digital marketing activities, including the frequency, consistency, and extent of engagement across
digital promotional channels. To complement the quantitative responses, semi-structured interviews were conducted to
obtain qualitative insights into participants' digital marketing practices, implementation challenges, and perceived
business outcomes. These interviews were not used for hypothesis testing but rather to support the interpretation of the
statistical findings.
Objective indicators of business performance were obtained from documented sales records and transaction reports
provided by participating enterprises. These records were used to measure sales volume independently of respondents'
subjective perceptions, thereby reducing the potential influence of self -reporting bias and strengthening the reliability
of the dependent variable. In addition, secondary data were collected from institutional reports, MSME development
documents, program records, and relevant scie ntific literature to provide contextual information regarding the
characteristics of the participating enterprises and the broader digital marketing environment. The integration of primary
and secondary data within a single analytical framework enhances da ta triangulation, improves the credibility of the
empirical evidence, and provides a stronger methodological foundation for the subsequent statistical analyses.

3. Results and Discussion

relationship between digital marketing intensity and sales performance among the sampled culinary micro-enterprises.
The results are organized sequentially, beginning with descriptive statistics and data diagnostics, followed by correlation
analysis, regression modeling, group comparisons, and exploratory analysis. This analytical sequence provides a
comprehensive assessment of the proposed hypothese s while also offering additional evidence regarding the factors

into the subsequent analyses while ensuring that all statistical procedures were performed using complete and internally
consistent data.

3.2 Normality Assessment
Both distributions presented in Figure 2 exhibit noticeable departures from symmetry, with a pronounced right -
skewed pattern characterized by a longer tail toward higher values. This distributional pattern indicates that a relatively
small proportion of cu linary micro-enterprises achieved substantially higher levels of digital marketing intensity and
sales volume than the majority of the sample, reflecting the heterogeneous nature of commercial performance within
the sector. Such asymmetry is frequently obs erved in enterprise -level datasets, where differences in managerial

capability, market reach, customer engagement, and business scale often generate a small group of high -performing
firms alongside a much larger proportion of enterprises operating at more modest levels.
The visual evidence was confirmed by the Shapiro –Wilk normality test, which indicated statistically significant
departures from normality for both digital marketing intensity (W = 0.960, p < .001) and sales volume (W = 0.956, p <
.001). These results indic ate that the observed variables do not strictly follow a normal distribution. Nevertheless,
statistical significance in formal normality testing should be interpreted cautiously in large datasets because the Shapiro–
Wilk test becomes increasingly sensitive  as sample size grows. With 4,546 valid observations, even relatively small
and practically unimportant deviations from normality are likely to produce statistically significant results.
Consequently, rejection of the null hypothesis of normality does not necessarily imply that the data are unsuitable for
parametric statistical analyses.

consider the objectives of the analysis and the robustness of the statistical procedures employed. The principal analyses
undertaken in this s tudy Pearson product moment correlation, ordinary least squares (OLS) regression, independent -
samples t-tests, and one -way ANOVA primarily require the sampling distributions of the estimated statistics, rather
than the raw variables themselves, to approxim ate normality. According to the Central Limit Theorem, this condition
is generally satisfied when sample sizes are sufficiently large, allowing parameter estimates and associated significance
tests to remain reliable despite moderate departures from normality in the observed variables.
Furthermore, the absence of extreme irregularities in the empirical distributions, together with the substantial sample
size, provides additional support for the application of parametric methods in the present study. Consequently, the
observed deviations from normality were not considered sufficient to compromise the validity, precision, or robustness
of the subsequent inferential analyses. The statistical results reported in the following sections can therefore be
interpreted with confidence as reliable e stimates of the relationships between digital marketing intensity and sales
performance within the sampled culinary micro-enterprises.

3.7 Discussion and Synthesis of Findings
Taken together, the descriptive, correlational, and inferential analyses provide consistent evidence that digital
marketing intensity is positively associated with sales performance among the culinary micro -enterprises examined in
this study. Unlike previo us studies that mainly conceptualize digital marketing based on adoption status, this study
highlights digital marketing intensity as a continuous dimension reflecting the extent and consistency of digital
engagement. The regression analysis presented in T able 4 and Figure 4, together with the comparison between high -
and low-intensity groups in Table 5 and Figure 5, demonstrates that enterprises with higher levels of digital marketing
engagement tend to achieve better sales outcomes. These findings provide  empirical support for H1 and H2 and
reinforce previous research indicating that digital marketing contributes positively to MSME performance (Sharabati et
al., 2024; Etim et al., 2022; Ariyati et al., 2024). Furthermore, the findings suggest that the effectiveness of digital
marketing depends not only on technology adoption but also on the intensity and strategic utilization of digital resources.

                Azkiyah et al. / International Journal of Research in Community Service, Vol. 7, No. 4, pp. 263-276, 2026                               275

The positive association between digital marketing intensity and sales performance can be explained by the ability
of digital platforms to improve market accessibility, customer interaction, and promotional efficiency. For culinary
micro-enterprises, which often face limited resources and strong local competition, intensive digital marketing activities
can enhance brand visibility, facilitate customer communication, and support more responsive marketing strategies.
From a theoretical perspective, these findings extend the digital transformation literature by demonstrating that digital
marketing intensity represents a strategic capability rather than merely a technological tool. Consistent with the
resource-based view, digital marketing capability may function as a dynamic resource that enables MSMEs to identify
market opportunities, strengthen customer relationships, and adapt to changing consumer preferences. Thus, this study
contributes by shifting the discussion from whether MSMEs adopt digital technologie s toward how effectively they
utilize these technologies to generate business value.
However, the moderate explanatory power of the regression model (R² = .278) indicates that digital marketing
intensity represents only one of several factors influencing sales performance. This result reflects the complex nature
of MSME performance, where business outcomes are shaped by multiple internal and external determinants, including
product quality, innovation capability, pricing strategies, operational efficiency, service quality, entrepreneurial
competence, and market conditions. The overall regre ssion results presented in Table 6 further suggest that a more
comprehensive model incorporating additional business capabilities may be required to explain sales variation more
effectively. Moreover, the relationship identified in this study should be int erpreted cautiously because the cross -
sectional design limits causal inference; enterprises with stronger sales performance may also have greater resources to
invest in digital marketing activities.
From a practical perspective, these findings imply that MSME development programs should move beyond
promoting basic digital platform adoption and focus on strengthening sustainable digital marketing capabilities.
Training initiatives should emphasize stra tegic content development, customer engagement management, digital
analytics, and evaluation of promotional effectiveness, while simultaneously improving complementary business
capabilities such as product innovation, operational management, and service quality. Overall, this study demonstrates
that digital marketing intensity is an important but partial determinant of sales performance among culinary micro -
enterprises. The findings provide a more nuanced understanding of MSME digital transformation by show ing that
competitive benefits emerge not simply from having digital channels, but from the consistent and strategic utilization
of those channels to create measurable business outcomes.
4. Conclusion
This study examined the relationship between digital marketing intensity and sales performance among culinary micro -
enterprises using a large observational dataset and multiple complementary statistical techniques. The empirical findings
consistently demonstrate that greater digital marketing intensity is associated with higher sales performance. Significant positive
relationships were identified through correlation and regression analyses, while the comparison between high - and low-intensity
enterprises confirmed that businesses engaging more intensively in digital marketing achieve substantially higher average sales.
Together, these findings provide robust empirical support for the proposed hypotheses and reinforce the importance of digital
marketing as a strategic capability for MSME development.
The study further demonstrates that the effectiveness of digital marketing is determined more by the intensity and consistenc y
of engagement than by the nominal scale of promotional partners. Although digital marketing intensity explained a meaningful
proportion of the variation in sales performance, the moderate explanatory power of the regression model indicates that commercial
outcomes are also influenced by other factors, including product quality, operational capability, pricing strategies, and mar ket
conditions. Moreover, the absence of significant differences across promotional -partner tiers suggests that collaborations with
larger-scale promotional actors do not necessarily generate superior sales outcomes.
The primary contribution of this study lies in demonstrating that digital marketing intensity represents an independent
explanatory dimension of MSME sales performance, extending previous studies that have largely focused on digital marketing
adoption or i mplementation. Practically, the findings suggest that government agencies, MSME development institutions, and
training providers should prioritize programs that encourage sustained digital engagement, continuous content creation, and a ctive
customer interaction, while integrating complementary support for operational and product development to maximize commercial
performance.
