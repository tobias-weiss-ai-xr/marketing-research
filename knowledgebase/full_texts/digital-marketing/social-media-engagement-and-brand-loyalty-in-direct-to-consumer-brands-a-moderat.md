Direct-to-Consumer brands are using social media as a vital avenue to connect, engage,
and create enduring connections with customers in today's quickly c hanging digital
marketplace. This research investigates how consumer’s attitude towards direct -to-
consumer brands is shaped by social media engagement which therefore affects brand
loyalty. Drawing upon, Theory of Planned Behavior this study tests an empir ical model in
which the relationship between social media engagement and brand loyalty is mediated
by attitude towards direct -to-consumer brands, while the relationship between attitude
towards direct -to-consumer brand and brand loyalty is moderated by bra nd trust. An
online survey was used to collect primary data (N=337) in three waves from a sample of
direct-to-consumer -versed Pakistani social media users. Time -1 respondents were asked to
rate their attitude towards direct -to-consumer brands. Social medi a engagement
(mediator) was tapped at time 2 while study’s moderator (brand trust) and dependent
variable (brand loyalty) were measured at time 3. The PROCESS Macro in SPSS by Hayes
was used to examine mediation and moderation effects. The results show tha t social media
engagement influences the consumer's attitude towards direct -to-consumer brand, which
results in brand loyalty over time. Furthermore, the relationship between brand trust and
loyalty is qualified by brand trust, highlighting the importance of brand trust in sustaining
customers' relationship in the direct -to-consumer market. By offering actual data on the
behavioral and psychological processes via which social media interaction fosters brand
loyalty, this research adds to the expanding corpu s of expertise in digital marketing. The
findings offer valuable insights for direct -to-consumer marketers aiming to improve
customer relationships by targeting engagement strategies and building trust.

Introduction
The digital age has revolutionised customer engagement with brands, particularly within the
direct-to-consumer (DTC) sector. As competition escalates, understanding the impact of social
media involvement on customer attitudes an d behaviours is crucial for marketers aiming to foster
brand loyalty. DTC brands are those that exclusively operate in the digital realm for the goal of
selling.  They just own physical retail establishments that provide conventional enhancement to
clients.  The DTC brands are distinguishing themselves on the disruptive virtual platforms and
challenging the traditional linear sales patterns (Prasad &  Ghosal, 2022) . Social media
engagement is the degree of connection, participation, and involvement that customers
demonstrate with companies on social media platforms. It includes behaviours such as like,
commenting, sharing material, following brand pages, and engagin g in discussions relevant to the
brand. In the digital age, merchants increasingly utilise social media platforms like Facebook,
Twitter, Instagram, Pinterest, and YouTube to actively engage consumers. Retailers utilise Social
Media Marketing (SMM) to deli ver real -time information through newsfeeds, vlogs, tweets,
photographs, and posts regarding the advent of emerging items or services (Pentina et al., 2018).
Research indicates that there is a difference in the level of customer interaction on various social
media platforms, such as Instagram and Twitter  (Godey et al., 2016), Facebook (Wang & Yu,
2017). Using Instagram as an example, the platform, du e to its specific properties and functions
about superior quality imagery content, appears to be able to engage the user on the deeper level
as compared to other types of social networking, such as Facebook or Twitter. It is also more likely
to lose agains t a team based on friendly games (with a 0.286 frequency of loss) rather than one
based on official matches (0.263 frequency of loss) . Digital marketers therefore need an inclusive
understanding of the effects that some of the social media characteristics  such as media exposure
and trustworthiness could have on changes in consumer engagement behaviour. Consequently,
digital marketers need a comprehensive awareness of how social media qualities, including
media exposure and trustworthiness, might influence variances in consumer engagement
behaviour (Machleit et al., 1993)  perceive brand attitude as a singular dimension. For instance,
brand attitude differs from the emotions provoked by the brand. Emotions are ephemeral, whereas
attitudes are more persistent. Emotions are self -referential; they do not provide information about
the external environment; instead, they reflect how the external environment influences us (Batra
& Ahtola, 1991) . Moreover, emotions are fundamentally distinct from cognitive or evaluative
reactions, which encompass a respondent's expressions of approval or disapproval regarding the
attributes of t he message itself (Breckler, 1984) . The former is an assessment, whereas the latter
represents a psychologicalattribute of the individual or a condition elicite d in the respondent
(Gardner, 1985).
Brand trust refers to the amount of confidence and dependability that people attribute to a

Trust influences customer interactions by enhancing their commi tment and propensity to support
loyalty despite competing options. Trust is the readiness to depend on another entity despite

Theory and Hypotheses Development
Social Media Engagement and Brand Loyalty
Social media engagement is becoming a critical parameter to brand loyalty to direct -to-
consumer brands in the current digital -first marketplace.  The scenario is different with the
traditional retail models as they main focus is the face-to-face brand experience whereas with the
direct-to-customer brands, the relations and loyalty have to be developed and formed online.
This has led to the evolution of social media as an important tool in attracting attention of the
consumer, building long lasting loyalty and enabling engagement. A growing body of empirical
literature supports why brand loyalty is connected to small and medium -sized enterprises (SMEs).
With this, loyalty does not only imply intending to buy again, but it should entail a strong
emotional attachment towards the brand such that it is evident in the form of advocacy behavior
such as referring the brand to others as well as a desire not to defect. (Chatzi et al., 2024). Research
has indicated that intense engagement with the brand through social media has a significant
influence on the brand attachment and loyalty results (Mobini & Sharbatian, 2024) . When
people comment under the posts, updates, or live sessions of some brand, they can easily feel
connected to such a comm unity. This has the ability to create an emotional commitment that is
more reliable indicator of a lasting retention than any transactional source of satisfaction can be.
The dynamic is especially utilized by brands that sell directly to the consumer give n that their
business has a basis on offering customized and one -on-one digital experiences. These moves help
to minimize the psychological gap between the brand and the customer and strengthen the
position of the brand as an element of the lifestyle of th e consumer. Besides, an interpersonal
connection between the consumer and the brand is further reinforced due to the mutual exchange
of engagement behaviors. In a world of oversaturation with choices, the brands, which fail to
participate in the social med ia in a significant way, often experience problems in attracting and
maintaining the interest of consumers. Also longitudinal studies have shown that social media
engagement is a predictor of long term behavioral loyalty beyond the short term brand recall and

Attitude Towards Direct-to-Consumer Brands and Brand Loyalty

pressures or constraints (Peter & Olson, 2010) . Having this mind with a positive expectation will
result into the development of successful and sustainable brand loyalty behaviors. Several things
can contribute towards a positive perception including quality of t he product offered, clarity of
brand values, availability of good digital channels, and fulfilling customer service. These are

loyalty together with behavioral brand loyalty whereby attitudinal brand loyalty could be
characterized as a precursor to behaviora l brand loyalty. Further, this is especially so where the
brands develop engaging customized and engaging social media marketing. This is significantly
important in direct -to consumer context where digital communication may be the main means
through which the brand communicates. The consumer attitude has a direct impact on loyalty in
the online fashion retailing industry, which is highly dominated by DTC organizations, whereas
satisfaction and emotional connection were used as mediators.
The results indica te that when consumers maintain strong and positive perceptions, they have a

representations of the internal forces that reinforce behavioral intentions, including word -of-
mouth advocacy, zone purchases, considered within the frame of the Theory of Planned Behavior.

Mediating Role of Attitude Towards Direct-to-consumer Brands
Within the context of the current direct-to-consumer (DTC) brand market, consumer attitude as a
mediating variable between soci al media use and brand loyalty has come to occupy a rather
significant level of importance. Such attitude will in turn make the consumer remain loyal to the
brand. Thus, one can say that the particular relationship between brand loyalty and engagement
with social media is beyond direct, being highly affected by the perception of the consumer
towards DTC brand. Consumers are likely to develop a positive attitude towards a brand when
they find the content of the brand authentic, interactive, informational, an d lean towards the
values. This positive attitude that has been described by TPB then determines the intention of the
consumer to do certain activities such as a repeat purchase or referring the brand to other
shoppers and these have been the main focus of brand loyalty. Engagement essentially results into
cognitive and emotional evaluation of the brand which consequently forms the foundation upon
which action behavior is categorized. The mediating role of attitude is well evidenced due to the
current empirical results. Based on the findings of the research by Ali et al. (2025), social media
marketing campaigns impact positively on the attitude toward a brand by increasing consumer
involvement to a significant extent. The research also identified that brand attitude is a major
mediational factor that helps in converting digital engagement in brand loyalty outcomes.
According to the authors, alone, engagement initiatives may not achieve long-term loyalty unless
the attitudinal response is a positive one. In ac cordance with their findings, the suggestion that
engagement may result into loyalty holds true provided that it results in positive attitudinal
changes.
This intermediary role is particularly significant in the world of the DTC brands, where digital
interaction is the most common, and sometimes the only, source of contact with the brand. This is
because social media is the primary platform that customers use to cultivate attitude in the event
that they do not have physical experiences and intra -store relationships. This mediating pathway
is affirmed under the theory of planned behavior which opines that behavior does not only
emerge because of exposure or interaction but also due to inner evaluations and intentions.
Attitudinal component of the TPB is used as a perception means of explaining behaviors such as
engagement. Consumer loyalty can ’t be built only by content interaction. The establishment of

Moderating Role of Brand Trust
For a long time, trust has been understood to be essential to the establishment and upkeep of
strong customer -brand connections. In  the context of digitally native direct -to-consumer (DTC)
brands, brand trust is even more important because the consumer's relationship with the company
is often built entirely through online channels.  The consumer's conviction that a brand is
trustworthy, acts with integrity, and consistently fulfills its commitments is embodied by trust. The
initial touchpoint between a DTC brand and its consumer base is typically social media
engagement. Consumers de velop impressions of the brand through the consumption of content,
likes, shares, remarks, and user -generated content. Nevertheless, the degree to which this
engagement results in loyalty is significantly influenced by the presence of brand trust.
Regardless of the frequency of their interactions with content, consumers who lack trust in a brand
are less likely to convert engagement into meaningful commitment. Conversely, individuals who
have confidence in the brand are more inclined to interpret their enga gement favorably and to
fortify their emotional and behavioral loyalty over time. Consequently, trust serves as a
moderator by increasing the impact of engagement on loyalty outcomes. Whereas it does not
have physical stores, physical service, and tangible  assessment of products, in the case of DTC
brands perceived trust is more important.
When trust has been created, then the consumers have greater likelihood of implementing the
intentions that are generated by the engagement activities because they have greater confidence
in their conclusions.. In their analysis of e -commerce websites, consumers more likely to transform
their social media use to brand advocacy and repeat purchase. To some degree, participatory

H5: Brand trust moderates the relationship between social media engagement and brand loyalty,

Study Participants and Procedure
The research focuses on individuals who engage with direct -to-consumer (DTC) brands via social
media platforms such as Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, YouTube, and brand websites. Participants
must possess prior experience with DTC brands to effectively assess psychological aspects like
engagement, brand trust, and loyalty. A purposive sampling method yielded a sample of 337
respondents for the study. Three waves of surveys were conducted using a time -lag methodology
to gather primary data and address reverse causality in causal investigations. The study took
place in a natural environment with minimal researcher involvement, focusing on consumers of
direct-to-consumer (DTC) brands. Data were collected through online platforms including email
and social media. At Time -1, attitudes towards DTC brands were assessed, social media
engagement was measured at Time-2, and brand trust and brand loyalty were evaluated at Time-
3.

Measurement Scales
All Responses for all variables were obtained on a 5 -point Likert scale. Social media engagement.
This construct is assessed with the instrument given by Baldus et al. (2015) and Lamberton and
Rose (2012). The participants were requested to examine their interactions with DTC brands
through analyzing such activities as viewing, enjoyment, sharing, com ments on posts, and brand
content creation. Attitude towards DTC Brands . This construct is measured using items adapted
from Ajzen (1991). Participants were asked to evaluate their overall feelings and evaluations
toward DTC brands, including perceptions o f favorability, positivity, and desirability. Brand
Trust: The items of brand trust is measured with the help of a comprehensive scale, developed
based on the ideas of Chaudhuri, Delgado -Ballester et al. (2003). This scale measures the
perception of consum ers on the reliability, honesty and ability to deliver on a promise of a brand.
The items evaluate what the respondents think about the brand that it is providing the quality
consistently, acts in the best interest of the respondent and is reliable both in  communications and
performance. Brand Loyalty : The tool established by Bennett and Rundle - Attitudinal loyalty
reflects emotional and psychological commitment, while behavioral loyalty indicates the intent
to make future purchases, remain engaged, and ref er the brand to others. Respondents were asked
about their likelihood of remaining loyal to a direct -to-consumer (DTC) brand despite
alternatives, as well as their sense of community with the brand.
Analytical Method
The research utilizes regression analysis to validate hypotheses regarding social media
engagement's effect on brand loyalty, mediated by attitude towards DTC brands and moderated
by brand trust. The PROCESS macro facilitated the analysis, employing Model 4 for mediation
testing and Model 1 for moderation evaluation

Discussion
The ultimate aim of this research is to investigate and elucidate the correlation among brand
loyalty and social media engagement in  context of DTC brands. The primary focus will be on the
mediating role of consumer attitude and the moderating role of brand trust. This study is
predicated on the Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB), which underscores the influence of
subjective norms, atti tudes, and perceived behavioral control on individual behavior. In the
context of DTC branding and digital consumer behavior, this study adapts the TPB framework to
evaluate the process by which social media behaviors transform into brand loyalty through
attitudinal and trust-related mechanisms
This study confirm that brand loyalty is significantly predicted by social media engagement. This
confirms that online consumer interactions, whether passive, such as viewing or favoring content,
or active, such as r emarking or sharing, significantly accord to the progress of long -term
consumer-brand relationships. These findings validate the current corpus of research that posits

Journal of Social Sciences Development, Volume 05, Issue 08, October, 2026 117

117
that consumer loyalty is no longer solely determined by product satisfaction or traditio nal
advertising, but is instead increasingly influenced by interactive and participatory digital
experiences. As an example, DTC brands' social media interactions are typically the only touch
points with consumers in their domain, as many of these brands d o not have a physical store
presence.  It is important to emphasize that the findings also reveal how consumer attitudes
towards direct -to-consumer (DTC) brands also have a strong impact on the link between social
media engagement and brand loyalty. This m eans that consumer engagement does not directly
and linearly translate to loyalty, but rather, directly and indirectly affects the consumer's
attitudinal disposition toward the brand, both cognitively and emotionally, which can foster
loyalty behavior. Fav orable attitudes are formed when consumers' engagement experiences are
genuine and rewarding and align with their values. These attitudes are indicative of emotional
attachments and symbolic associations, in addition to assessments of brand functionality. This
finding confirms the TPB's claim that attitude is an important antecedent to the intention to
engage in a behavior, in this case, to act in a brand loyalty way.
In conclusion, these insights enable brand managers to approach digital inter action with greater
emotional intelligence. In this setting, loyalty is not the result of discrete marketing strategies but
rather of meaningful connections, emotional alignment, and long -term trust, all of which need to
be carefully fostered in a digital economy that is becoming more and more competitive and value
driven.
Theoretical Contributions
There are numerous considerable contributions to the existing literature.  For one, it gives a
modernization of (TPB) that incorporates digital marketing and direct to client (DTC) brand
strategies. Secondly, attitude is a mediating variable, which adds to the complexity of the current
engagement-loyalty models. Thirdly, the theoretical contribution of brand trust as a moderator is
very wide. The study brings another factor into the relationship between engagement and loyalty:
the strength of the engagement -loyalty relationship is contingent upon the level of trust that the
consumer has in the brand. Moreover, the study lays the foundation for ongoing investigat ions of
how social and cognitive aspects interact in algorithm -based contexts such as social media.
Moreover, the study can aid in advancing current theories and can motivate further research in
the fields of psychological constructs that impact brand loyalty in the digital era.
Managerial Implications
The research is very helpful to marketers a nd brand managers operating in the direct -to-
consumer (DTC) field. DTC brands must understand that the practice of social media engagement
is not just a vanity metric. The state of engagement must not be discussed in the context of likes,
shares, or the number of followers; engagement should be perceived and used as a strategic tool on
the quest to build stronger relationships with consumers. The brands should target producing
interactive and high -value content that makes an impression on their clients base d on the
emotional and intellectual levels.  Second, brand perceptions are also of high importance as far as

the development and maintenance of the positive ones is concerned. Marketers must maintain
consistency in the delivery of brands messages, be transparent in communicating, and must also be
relevant in the offerings that they provide. Stories can significantly influence perceptions that
work well with their values - authenticity, inclusiveness, and sustainability - as consumers. These
results depict the psychological bridge connecting engagement and loyalty as these attitudes.
Third, active nurturing of trust also needs to be practiced. In direct -to-consumer relationships,
building trust is an important responsibility of the brand since in this case brand  is likely to deal
with consumers without dealing with an intermediary. This also entails the ability to handle issues
as soon as they arise, ensuring integrity of data, and a regular adherence to brand promise, in
addition to the provision of high -quality goods and services. It is something that cannot be
achieved in a short time; trust must be nurtured by means of ethical actions, reputation, and
consistency. Finally, direct -to-consumer brands are advised to split their audience based on the
levels of tru st and adjust their engagement strategies based on the obtained results. As an
example, those who demonstrate a strong interest to it but are characterized by low scores in terms
of trust can also be interested in re -assurance campaigns, product guarantees , or those aimed at
increasing the level of transparency behind the curtains. Alternatively, members that possess
some loyalty and trustworthiness may become the best actors of programs designed to promote
community involvement. Additionally, marketers should use data analytics to continuously assess
how social media involvement affects their clientele's attitudes and behaviors. Brands can quickly
adjust their strategy with the use of sentiment analysis, behavioral tracking, and real -time
monitoring of cons umer responses. Purchasing technologies that enable A/B testing of content
can provide information on the kinds of postings that foster greater trust and engagement.
Additionally, including consumer input into company decision -making procedures may
empower customers, increasing their advocacy and loyalty. In order to humanize their brand and
demonstrate transparency, particularly to groups who lack trust, brands should also prioritize
instructional material and behind-the-scenes narrative.
Limitations and Future Research Directions
The results of the study show validity but one should note a few limitations. Initially, the study
employs non -probability purposive sampling, which may restrict the generalizability of the
results beyond the defined sample. Futu re studies might use random sampling in various cultural
settings and geographic areas to improve external validity. Secondly, the study is cross -sectional
as it does not track real behavior over time, even though it uses a three -wave time-lagged data
collection methodology. To measure the changes in engagement, attitudes, and loyalty when
there are changes in brand strategy or market conditions, longitudinal studies could be used.
Thirdly, this study focuses solely on social media engagement, but consumers  also interact with
DTC brands via other digital channels such as websites, email, influencers and mobile apps. These
channels can be included in the future research to establish Omni -channel engagement-loyalty
model. In addition, emotional branding, perce ived value and brand authenticity can be used as

the market in Pakistan. Consumer attitudes and behaviors can be shaped differently in other
countries due to t he cultural differences, digital adoption rates and maturity of brands.
Comparative studies done in developed and emerging markets can provide a more holistic
picture of how the loyalty models based on engagement can be applied around the world.
Moreover, relying on self -reported data could lead to social desirable bias or common method
bias that could impact validity. Although future research could employ multi -source data
gathering (behavioural monitoring or observational analytics), the use of temporal s eparation and
anonymity as procedures has helped reduce these biases. Last, although this study provides
primary focus on the planned behavior approach, the findings may be complemented with other
theories such as the Self -Determination Theory (SDT) and/or  the Technology Acceptance Model
(TAM), to gain a deeper understanding of the motivational process that leads to customer loyalty
and engagement in the digital era.
Conclusion
In the context of direct -to-consumer (DTC) brands, the results of this study in dicate the
substantial impact of social media involvement on brand loyalty, offering empirical support for
the suggested model. The findings show that consumers' perceptions of direct -to-consumer (DTC)
brands are positively and statistically significantly impacted by social media engagement. This
suggests that more positive customer impressions of the brand result from more engagement with
brand material on social media platforms, including like, commenting, reading stories or postings,
and sharing content.  These favorable opinions, in turn, strongly predict brand loyalty, indicating
that customers who have a positive opinion of a direct -to-consumer (DTC) brand are more likely
to stick with it, buy from it again, and refer it to others. Additionally, the med iation analysis shows
that brand loyalty and social media engagement are mediated by attitudes toward DTC brands.
This suggests that social media use affects customer views, which in turn affects loyalty both
directly and indirectly. As a result, interesti ng brand content influences consumers' perceptions of
a company and increases the possibility that they will stick with it over time.
The association between attitude toward DTC brands and brand loyalty is considerably
moderated by brand trust, according t o the moderation research. In particular, the beneficial
effect of attitude on loyalty increases with brand trust. This implies that when consumers believe a
brand to be reliable, consistent, and credible, they are more likely to achieve a positive attitud e
into devoted behavior. All things considered, these results are consistent with the Theory of
Planned Behavior, which indicates how attitude and trust influence customer behavior.
