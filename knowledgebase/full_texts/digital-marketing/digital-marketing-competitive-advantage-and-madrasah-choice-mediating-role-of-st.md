 Purpose: This study examines the role of Stoic rationality in prospective
students’ school selection decisions and its mediating effects on the
relationships between digital marketing, competitive advantage, and school
selection decisions.
Research Method: An explanatory quantitative approach was employed
using PLS -SEM with SmartPLS 4. Data were collected through purposive
sampling from 96 prospective students of MA Ma’arif 7 Sunan Drajat,
Lamongan Regency.
Results and Discussion: Digital marketing significantly enhances Stoic
rationality but has no significant direct effect on school selection decisions.
Competitive advantage positively influences both Stoic rationality and
school selection decisions. Stoic rationality significantl y influences school
selection decisions, fully mediating the effect of digital marketing and
partially mediating the effect of competitive advantage.
Implications: The findings highlight rational evaluation as a mechanism
linking institutional strategies to educational choices. Islamic schools should
strengthen competitive differentiation and develop digital marketing that
supports informed decisions. The single -institution setting and limited
sample constrain generalizability.
Originality: This study integrates Stoic rationality into educational choice
research, demonstrating that digital marketing influences school selection
indirectly through rational evaluation rather than directly.

Keywords: stoic rationality; digital marketing; competitive advantage; school
selection decisions; islamic education.

2. Literature Review and Hypothesis Development
2.1 Literature Review
2.1.1 Theoretical Foundation: Stimulus–Organism–Response (S-O-R) Theory
The Stimulus –Organism–Response (S -O-R) theory, originally developed by Mehrabian and Russell
(1974) and subsequently widely adopted in consumer behavior research, serves as the primary
theoretical framework of this study. Mu & Yi (2024) applied this framew ork in the context of digital
marketing and demonstrated that digital marketing characteristics as stimuli influence consumers’ value
perceptions as the organism, ultimately leading to loyalty responses. Kotler & Keller (2016) further
argued that consumer decision-making processes invariably begin with external stimuli that are
internally processed before resulting in behavioral outcomes. In the present study, digital marketing
and competitive advantage function as the stimuli (S), Stoic rationality represents the organism (O), and
school selection decisions constitute the response (R).
In the present study, digital marketing activities and institutional competitive advantage are
conceptualized as the stimuli (S). These external factors are processed through Stoic Rationality, which
operates as the organism (O) by enabling emotion regulat ion, objective information evaluation, self -
control, and reasoned judgment aligned with long -term values rather than transient affective or social
pressures. The resulting behavioral response (R) is the school selection decision. While prior S -O-R
applications in digital marketing and higher education consistently confirm the mediating role of
internal psychological states, existing studies predominantly employ conventional mediators such as
perceived value, brand equity, or general attitudes. Limited attention has been directed toward culturally
attuned rationality constructs such as Stoic Rationality, particularly within the specific context of
prospective students educated in pesantren and Nahdlatul Ulama traditions in Indonesia. This research
gap provide s the theoretical rationale for examining the sequential relationships among digital
marketing, competitive advantage, Stoic Rationality, and school selection decisions, and thereby
constitutes the foundation for the hypotheses developed in subsequent sections.

2.1.4 Stoic Rationality
Stoicism is a Hellenistic philosophical school founded by Zeno of Citium around 300 BCE and later
developed by three prominent Roman Stoics: Epictetus, Seneca, and Marcus Aurelius. Irvine (2009)
conceptualizes the essence of Stoicism as the art of distingu ishing between what lies within an
individual's control thoughts, decisions, and responses and what lies beyond it. Robertson (2019)
illustrates how the Stoic principles practiced by Marcus Aurelius can be applied to contemporary
strategic decision-making. Pigliucci (2017) contextualizes Stoicism within modern life and highlights its
relevance as a practical guide for decision -making in an era characterized by information overload.
Similarly, Sellars (2019) summarizes the practical lesson of Stoicism by arg uing that true freedom
consists of liberation from impulsive reactions to external stimuli.
Empirical evidence further supports the relevance of Stoicism to decision -making processes.
Hirsch et al., (2023) demonstrated that the three disciplines of Stoicism cultivate individuals’ capacity to
make sound decisions under conditions of uncertainty and external pressure. Karani & Saravanan (2024)
found that consumers with stronger Stoic orientations tend to make more reflective decisions and
prioritize long-term values. From a psychological perspective, Kahneman (2011) dual -process theory
provides additional support, suggesting that Stoic rationality encourages individuals to engage System
2 thinking, characterized by slow, analytical, and rational processing, when evaluating digital marketing
stimuli and the competitive advantages offered by educational institutions.
Despite these converging theoretical and empirical strands, existing research has largely
examined Stoic Rationality within general consumer or clinical decision domains, leaving its mediating
function in educational choice processes particularly the pathw ays through which digital marketing
exposures and institutional competitive advantages shape enrollment decisions insufficiently specified.
Prior studies have not adequately articulated how the capacity for emotional regulation, objective
information appraisal, and long-term value prioritization intervenes between external promotional and
competitive cues and the final educational selection. This omission constitutes a clear research gap.
Accordingly, the present study hypothesizes that Stoic Rationality fu nctions as a positive mediating
mechanism linking digital marketing stimuli and competitive advantage to educational decision -
making, such that higher levels of Stoic Rationality strengthen the translation of these external factors
into reasoned, value-congruent institutional choices.

2.2.2 Competitive Advantage and Stoic Rationality
According to the Stimulus-Organism-Response (S-O-R) framework, competitive advantage constitutes
an external stimulus that shapes prospective students’ cognitive evaluation by supplying objective
institutional cues such as academic reputation, accreditatio n status, qualified faculty, technological
infrastructure, graduate employability, and tuition affordability (Hart & Rodgers, 2024). Educational

evidence-based enrollment decisions among prospective students (Nagoya et al., 2021).
H2: Competitive advantage has a positive and significant effect on Stoic rationality.
2.2.3 Digital Marketing and School Selection Decisions
According to the Stimulus -Organism-Response (S-O-R) theory, external digital marketing stimuli can
directly shape behavioral responses by increasing institutional visibility, improving information
accessibility, strengthening trust, and elevating perceptio ns of institutional quality among prospective
students (Kharisma & Agzumi, 2026). Empirical research demonstrates that digital marketing strategies
particularly social media engagement, influencer marketing, and paid advertisements exhibit significant
positive associations with students’ decisions to select private educational institutions, accounting for
63.1 percent of the variance in choice behavior (Tuna et al., 2024). Quantitative analyses further confirm
that core digital determinants such as online content and institutional websites exert statistically
significant effects on college selection decisions, supported by standardized path coefficients and
acceptable structural model fit indices in a sample of 267 respondents (Gaur et al., 2024). Collectively,
these findings establish that digital marketing produces a positive and significant effect on school
selection decisions, with partial regression tests yielding significance values below 0.05 and overall
models explaining approximately 62 percent of the variance in student choice (Putri et al., 2026).
H3: Digital marketing has a positive and significant effect on school selection decisions.

2.2.5 Stoic Rationality and School Selection Decisions

2.2.6 The Mediating Role of Stoic Rationality in the Relationship between Digital Marketing and School
Selection Decisions
Within the Stimulus-Organism-Response (S-O-R) framework, digital marketing operates as an external
stimulus that activates internal cognitive mechanisms, specifically Stoic rationality, characterized by
deliberate information evaluation and critical apprai sal before behavioral responses emerge (Al -
Abdallah & Ababakr, 2025). Empirical investigations demonstrate that effective digital marketing
enhances prospective students’ rational information processing by increasing the accessibility and
credibility of in stitutional data, which in turn improves the quality of subsequent school selection
decisions (Kharisma & Agzumi, 2026). Quantitative analyses further establish that this internal rational
evaluation functions as a significant mediating pathway, with digit al communication tools producing
both direct and indirect effects on educational choice through strengthened cognitive deliberation in
samples of more than 1,000 respondents (Tuna et al., 2024). Therefore, Stoic rationality mediates the
relationship between digital marketing and school selection decisions, transforming external stimuli into
more informed and deliberate behavioral outcomes (Gaur et al., 2024).

2.2.7 The Mediating Role of Stoic Rationality in the Relationship between Competitive Advantage and
School Selection Decisions
Competitive advantage provides objective institutional information that prospective students evaluate
before making enrollment decisions. However, the influence of competitive advantage may depend on
the extent to which individuals process such information  rationally. Prospective students possessing
stronger Stoic rationality are expected to interpret institutional strengths more objectively, resulting in
higher-quality educational decisions. Within the S -O-R framework, Stoic rationality acts as the
psychological mechanism through which competitive advantage is translated into behavioral responses.
This indirect pathway explains how external institutional characteristics ultimately affect school selection
decisions by enhancing rational judgment. Theoretical  reasoning and previous empirical findings
suggest that cognitive evaluation mediates the relationship between institutional attributes and
behavioral intentions (Desembrianita et al., 2024; Mu & Yi, 2024). Accordingly, the following hypothesis
is proposed:
H7: Stoic rationality mediates the relationship between competitive advantage and school
selection decisions.

3. Research Method
3.1 Research Design and Study Area
This study employed an explanatory quantitative approach using a survey design. The research was
conducted at MA Ma'arif 7 Sunan Drajat and several junior secondary schools (SMP/MTs) located in the
Paciran, Brondong, and Solokuro districts of Lamongan Rege ncy, East Java, Indonesia. These locations
were selected based on the substantial decline in the number of locally domiciled students, which
constitutes the primary issue addressed in this study.
3.2 Population and Sample
The population comprised all prospective local students, defined as ninth -grade students enrolled in
SMP/MTs located around MA Ma'arif 7 Sunan Drajat who were considering their upper secondary
school options. Based on data from the Lamongan Regency Statist ics Office, the population was
estimated at approximately 2,500 students. A sample of 96 respondents was determined using Slovin's
formula with a 10% margin of error, which satisfies the 10 -times rule requirement for Partial Least
Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM) (Hair et al., 2019). Purposive sampling was employed
with four inclusion criteria: (1) respondents were ninth-grade students enrolled in SMP/MTs and residing
in neighboring districts; (2) they had not yet made a definitive school choice; (3) they had previously
received information about MA Ma'arif 7 Sunan Drajat; and (4) they were willing to complete the
questionnaire honestly.
3.3 Variables and Measurement
The study involved four variables. Digital marketing (X1) was measured using five indicators adapted
from Chaffey and Smith (2022) and Ruangkanjanases et al., (2022). Competitive advantage (X2) was
measured using four indicators based on Hearn (2023) and Porter,(1985). Stoic rationality (Z) was
assessed through four indicators derived from Irvine, (2009), Hirsch et al., (2023), and Alfianti et al.,
(2024). Finally, school selection decision (Y) was measured using four indicators adapted from Kotler &
Keller (2016) and Rozenkowska, (2023). All measurement items employed a five -point Likert scale
ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree).
3.4 Data Analysis
The data were analyzed using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM) with
SmartPLS version 4 software (Ghozali & Latan, 2020; Hair et al., 2019). The analysis was conducted in
two stages. First, the measurement model (outer model) was evaluated through factor loading (≥ 0.70),
Average Variance Extracted (AVE ≥ 0.50), Composite Reliability (CR ≥ 0.70), Cronbach's alpha (≥ 0.70),
and the Heter otrait-Monotrait ratio (HTMT < 0.85). Second, the structural model (inner model) was
assessed through hypothesis testing using a bootstrapping procedure with 5,000 iterations. The direct

4.2.3 The Absence of a Direct Effect of Digital Marketing on School Selection Decisions (H3 Not Supported)
The finding that digital marketing does not exert a statistically significant direct influence on school

prospective students' initial interest into actual e nrollment decisions. Rather than indicating the
ineffectiveness of digital marketing, this result implies that digital communication primarily functions as
an information dissemination mechanism, while the final decision -making process requires deeper
cognitive evaluation and personal judgment. In the context of Islamic educational institutions,
prospective students and their families appear to assess not only the promotional attractiveness of
online content but also the credibility, educational quality, in stitutional values, and long-term benefits

The rejection of H3 represents one of the most noteworthy findings of this study. Digital
marketing was found to be incapable of directly transforming content exposure into school selection
decisions without first undergoing an internal rationalization pro cess. This finding addresses the gap
identified by (Hearn, 2023), who argued that the mere availability of digital media is insufficient in the
absence of careful deliberation by prospective students. Mahjudin et al., (2024) further emphasized that

5. Concluding Remarks and Recommendation

This study was conducted to examine the direct effects of digital marketing and competitive advantage
on school selection decisions among prospective local students at MA Ma’arif 7 Sunan Drajat, while also
investigating the mediating role of Stoic rational ity within the Stimulus –Organism–Response (S-O-R)
framework. The findings demonstrate that both digital marketing and competitive advantage positively
and significantly enhance prospective students’ Stoic rationality. Furthermore, competitive advantage
and Stoic rationality exert significant positive effects on school selection decisions, whereas digital
marketing does not directly influence those decisions. Instead, digital marketing affects school selection
decisions indirectly through Stoic rationality, indicating a full mediation effect, while Stoic rationality
partially mediates the relationship between competitive advantage and school selection decisions.
These findings confirm that prospective students’ educational decisions are shaped not merely by
external marketing stimuli but by their capacity to evaluate institutional information rationally,
objectively, and in accordance with long-term educational values.
This study contributes to the educational  marketing literature in several important ways.
Theoretically, it extends the application of the Stimulus –Organism–Response (S -O-R) framework by
introducing Stoic rationality as a novel organism construct that explains the internal cognitive
mechanism lin king external institutional stimuli with school selection decisions. To the best of the
authors’ knowledge, this represents one of the earliest empirical applications of Stoic rationality within
the context of Islamic secondary education in Indonesia. Practically, the findings suggest that madrasah
administrators should not rely solely on increasing the intensity of digital marketing activities. Instead,
they should prioritize the development and communication of sustainable competitive advantages
while pro ducing transparent, evidence -based, and value -oriented digital content that encourages
prospective students to engage in reflective and rational decision-making. Such an approach is expected
to strengthen institutional credibility, improve public trust, and ultimately enhance student enrollment.
Despite these contributions, this study has several limitations. First, the research was conducted
in a single Islamic secondary school, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to other
educational contexts. Second, the sample consisted exclusively of prospective local students within one
geographical region, thereby restricting broader demographic representation. Third, the cross-sectional
research design captures decision-making behavior at a single point in time and therefore cannot fully
explain changes in students’ preferences over time. Future studies are therefore encouraged to employ
larger and more diverse samples across different types of educational institutions and regions, adopt
longitudinal research designs to examine changes in de cision-making behavior, and incorporate
additional psychological and contextual variables such as institutional trust, perceived educational
quality, parental influence, religious commitment, or electronic word -of-mouth to further enrich the
explanatory power of educational marketing models in Islamic education.
