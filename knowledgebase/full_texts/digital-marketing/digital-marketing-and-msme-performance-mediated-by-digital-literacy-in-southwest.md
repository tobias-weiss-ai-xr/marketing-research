  Abstract
 This study examines the influence of digital marketing on MSME performance in
Southwest Papua, with digital literacy as a mediating variable using the Unified Theory
of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT) approach. The study applies a
quantitative explanatory research design through a survey method, using structured
questionnaires distributed to MSME actors who have used or have the potential to use
digital platforms for marketing activities . A total of 100 MSME respondents were
selected through purposive sampling based on criteria such as active business
operation, use of digital platforms, and business location in West Papua Province . Data
were analyzed using validity and reliability tests, descriptive analysis, and inferential
analysis through SEM-PLS to examine direct and indirect relationships among variables
. The results show that the research instruments are valid and reliable, with item-total
correlations ranging from 0.522 to 0.744 and Cronbach’s Alpha values between 0.834
and 0.849 . Digital marketing has a positive and significant effect on digital literacy,
while digital marketing and digital literacy jointly  explain 40.2% of the variance in
MSME performance . The mediation analysis confirms that digital literacy partially
mediates the relationship between digital marketing and MSME performance, with a
significant indirect effect of 0.290 and a 95% bootstrap c onfidence interval of [0.150;
0.431] . These findings indicate that MSME performance in Southwest Papua can be
improved not only through wider adoption of digital marketing tools but also through
strengthening digital literacy, supported by facilitating conditions, social support, and
technology acceptance as emphasized in the UTAUT framework.

INTRODUCTION
MSMEs are the main drivers of local economies, including in Southwest Papua Province, because they
generate employment opportunities, increase household income, and reduce regional economic disparities while
supporting regional economic transformation (Kraus et al., 2021; P . Verhoef et al., 2015; P . C. Verhoef et al., 2021).
However, MSMEs in eastern Indonesia continue to face structural constraints, including limited market access, weak
managerial capability, and low adoption of di gital technology, which hinder business competitiveness and growth
(Nuseir & Aljumah, 2020; Renaningtias, Hidayat, & S.T.   Aan, 2024) . This condition has made it difficult for many
UMKM actors to improve their bus iness performance not only in terms of sales but also customer growth and
competitiveness against outside products (Renaningtias, Hidayat, & Erlansari, 2024).

Information technology, artificial intelligence, and internet development have fundamentally transformed
marketing activities, shifting MSMEs from conventional promotion toward digital marketing through social media,
marketplaces, and integrated online platforms (Bharadwaj et al., 2013; Dwivedi et al., 2021; Laila et al., 2024). Several
studies have demonstrated that digital marketing expands market reach, improves promotional effectiveness,
strengthens customer engagement, and supports innovation -driven business transformation among MSMEs  (Amin
et al., 2025; Effendi et al., 2023; Mulyono & Ramadani, 2026; Nambisan et al., 2019) . In some regions, digital
marketing has been proven to have a  positive impact on business orientation and resilience of UMKM so that the
business is able to survive the competition and changes in the environment (Razie et al., 2022).
Although digital marketing offers considerable opportunities, its effectiveness depends largely on the digital
literacy of MSME owners, namely their ability to access, understand, evaluate, and utilize digital technologies
effectively for business purposes (Susanto et al., 2026; UNESCO, 2021). Previous studies indicate that digital literacy
strengthens entrepreneurial orientation, inn ovation capability, and business sustainability among MSMEs  (S.
Rachmadana et al., 2024). In other regions, financial literacy, digital entrepreneurship, and product innovation have
been shown to increase digital marketing utilization and digital technology adoption, which in turn significantly
improve MSME performance (Muhtadin et al., 2026; Sholicha & Sukmana, 2025).
Previous empirical studies indicate that digital marketing directly influences MSME performance while
simultaneously acting as a mediating mechanism between innovation capability, financial literacy, and organizational
performance (Hazzam et al., 2026; Pasaribu et al., 2025; Thy, 2026). In addition, there is a study linking digital literacy
specifically in the marketing field to entrepreneurial orientation and UMKM business resilience in Papua, which
emphasizes the importance of digital skills in safeguarding business sustainability. This indicates that the relationship
between digital marketing, digital literacy, and UMKM performance is complex, therefore it is interesting to research
further in the context of a particular area such as Southwest Papua (Pasaribu et al., 2025).
Although previous studies have investigated digital marketing and digital literacy among Indonesian MSMEs,
most research has focused on western Indonesia, while evidence from eastern Indonesia remains limited (Elkhoudary
et al., 2026). The existing researches in Papua only capture marketing digital literacy and its effect on entrepreneurial
orientation and business resilience, however, they have not yet integrated UMKM performance comprehensively
with a technology adoption model framework such as UTAUT. Therefore, research is needed which specifically
investigates the impact of digital marketing on UMKM performance in Southwest Papua while making digital literacy
a mediating variable, thus it can provide a more complete picture of the determining factors of successful UMKM in
the digital economy era (Patria et al., 2023).
Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT) is one of the models that is widely used to
explain technology acceptance and use based on factors such as performance expectancy, effort expectancy, social
influence, and facilitating conditions. In the context of MSMEs, UTAUT explains how performance expectancy, effort
expectancy, social influence, and facilitating conditions en courage the adoption of digital marketing technologies,
which subsequently improve digital literacy and business performance (Puriwat & Tripopsakul, 2021). The use of the
UTAUT approach is expected to provide a strong theoretical basis for analyzing the relationship between digital
marketing, digital literacy as a mediating variable, and UMKM performance in Southwest Papua (Venkatesh et al.,
2019).
From the above discussion, it appears that there is still a gap between the great potential of digital marketing
and digital literacy and the real performance of UMKM in Southwest Papua. Besides, there are not many studies that
use the UTAUT approach to examine how technology acceptance factors influence the use of digital marketing, digital
literacy, and finally UMKM performance. This situation underlines the importance of the researc h entitled, "The
Influence of Digital Marketing on MSME Performance in Southwest Papua via Digital Literacy as a Mediating Variable:
A UTAUT Approach" to make theoretical and practical contributions for the development of UMKM in the region.

METHOD
Research Method
This study applies quantitative method since its main focus is testing the relationships between variables
and measuring the influence of digital marketing on the performance of MSMEs with digital literacy as the mediating
variable based on the UTAUT framework. Quantitative methods were chosen as they allow statistical processing of
numerical data to produce objective, systematic, and generalizable findings to the population of MSMEs in Southwest
Papua.
Research Approach
The approach used was explanatory research with the survey design, i.e. collecting data through structured
questionnaires to explain causal relationships between the variables under study. Survey design was selected as it is
effective to reach a large number of MSME respondents and is capable of depicting the influence of digital marketing
and digital literacy variables on the performance of MSMEs in the UTAUT framework.
Population and Sample
The population of this study is all Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) actors who operate in the
area of West Papua Province and have or are potential to use digital media in their marketing activities. The
population also covers a variety of business types such as culinary, trade, handicraft, and services that are important
parts of the local economy.  The study sample was determined at 100 respondents UMKM actors who meet the
criteria that have been set. The sample technique used was non -probability sampling with purposive sampling
approach, which means the respondents are chosen  deliberately by consideration that those are in line with the
research needs. The main inclusion criteria of the sample are: (1) UMKM actors who have been actively running their
businesses for at least one year; (2) have used at least one digital platform  (for example social media, marketplace,
or messaging application) for promotion or sales; and (3) residing and doing business in the West Papua Province
area. Meanwhile, the exclusion criteria include: (1) UMKM which at the time of data collection is no l onger in
operation; and (2) business actors who refuse or are not willing to fill out the research questionnaire.
Technique of Data Collection
Primary data were collected by closed questionnaires with Likert scale (e.g. 1 -5) which were distributed to
MSMEs both directly and online. Questionnaires contained statements that measured digital marketing variable,
digital literacy, MSME performance, as well as the UTAUT constructs such as performance expectancy, effort
expectancy, social influence, and facilitating conditions. Besides that, secondary data can be obtained from relevant
reports of the related department, official publications, and previous studies which are relevant to support the
analysis and discussion.
Operational Definition and Instrument
Every variable in a research must be explained through an operational definition which contains the
measurable indicators. For example, digital marketing operationalized by the indicators of intensity of social media
use, utilization of marketplace, and the freq uency of digital promotion; digital literacy through ability to use the
application, understanding of digital marketing features, and digital security; MSME performance through sales
growth, number of customers, and business continuity. Questionnaire instr ument is developed based on those
indicators and tested for validity and reliability before being used extensively.
Data Analysis Technique
Data analysis was done in stages, starting with data quality test (validity and reliability test), descriptive
analysis to profile respondents and variables, then inferential analysis to test research hypotheses. Considering the

academic calendar applied.

RESULT AND DISCUSSION
Instrument Validity and Reliability
The validity test indicates that all questionnaire items used to measure digital marketing, digital literacy, and
MSME performance are statistically valid. The item –total correlations (r -values) range from 0.522 to 0.744 and
consistently exceed the critical r-table value of 0.195, confirming that each item appropriately reflects its underlying
construct. This suggests that  respondents’ answers reliably capture their actual level of digital marketing usage,
digital literacy, and perceived business performance. Reliability analysis further supports the robustness of the
measurement instruments. The three main variables show C ronbach’s Alpha coefficients between 0.834 and 0.849,
which are above the commonly accepted threshold of 0.70. These results indicate strong internal consistency for all
scales, meaning that the items within each variable measure the same underlying concep t in a stable and coherent
manner.
Direct Effect of Digital Marketing on Digital Literacy
Regression analysis reveals a significant and positive effect of digital marketing on digital literacy. The
standardized coefficient for this relationship is 𝑩 = 𝟎. 𝟔𝟑𝟒 (p < 0.001), demonstrating that higher levels of digital
marketing utilization are associated with higher levels of digital literacy among MSME owners in Southwest Papua.
Practically, this means that MSMEs that more actively use social media, marketplaces, and other digital platforms for
marketing are also more likely to develop better skills and understanding in using digital technologies.
Joint Contribution to MSME Performance
The results show that digital marketing and digital literacy together explain 40.2% of the variance in MSME
performance. This proportion indicates a moderate to strong explanatory power, suggesting that these two variables
are important determinants of performance, although other factors outside the model still account for the remaining
59.8% of the variance. In other words, while digital marketing and digital literacy significantly shape MSME outcomes,
aspects such as product quality, pricing, financial management, and local market conditions may also influence
performance.
Indirect Effect and Mediation Analysis
The mediation analysis shows that the indirect effect of digital marketing on MSME performance through
digital literacy is 0.290, with a 95% bootstrap confidence interval of [0.150; 0.431]. Because this confidence interva l
does not include zero, the indirect effect is statistically significant. This confirms that digital literacy functions as a
meaningful mediating variable in the relationship between digital marketing and MSME performance. In addition,

DISCUSSION
Digital Marketing as a Driver of Digital Literacy
The strong and significant effect of digital marketing on digital literacy (B = 0.634; p < 0.001) highlights an
important dynamic in Southwest Papua: using digital marketing tools is not only a way to reach customers, but also
an informal learning process for MSME owners. As MSMEs experiment with social media posts, marketplace listings,
online advertising, and customer interactions through digital channels, they gradually develop greater familiarity and
competence with digital technology. This finding is consistent with UTAUT theory and previous technology adoption
studies (Tamilmani et al., 2019). When MSME owners perceive that digital marketing improves sales and is relatively
easy to use, they are more likely to engage with digital platforms. Over time, this engagement enhances their digital
literacy, enabling them to better understand features, analytics, and security aspects of digital tools.
The Role of Digital Marketing and Digital Literacy in MSME Performance
The fact that digital marketing and digital literacy jointly account for 40.2% of the variation in MSME
performance suggests that these two variables are central components of competitive advantage in the digital era,
even in regions with infrastructural and market challenges like Southwest Papua. MSMEs that actively utilize digital
marketing and possess higher digital literacy can reach broader markets, communicate more effectively with
customers, and adapt more quickly to changes in consumer behavior. From a practical standpoint, this means that
efforts to improve MSME performance should not focus solely on providing digital channels, but also on ensuring
that MSME owners have the skills and confidence to use these channels effectively. Digital literacy  is what enables
MSMEs to design appropriate content, interpret engagement metrics, and refine their strategies based on data rather
than intuition alone (Bisnis, 2021; Khairilisani et al., 2026).
Partial Mediation of Digital Literacy: Dual Pathways of Influence
The evidence of partial mediation —where both the indirect effect (0.290, CI [0.150; 0.431]) and the direct
effect (B = 0.258; p = 0.016) are significant—indicates that digital marketing affects MSME performance through two
pathways. First, direct pathway: digital marketing itself can improve performance by making products more visible,
simplifying customer communication, and opening access to new markets. Even without high digital literacy, simply
being present online and using basic promotional techniques may yield some performance improvements. Second,
indirect pathway via digital literacy: as MSME owners become more literate digitally, they can optimize the way they
use digital marketing tools —selecting the right platforms, targeting the right segments, a nalyzing insights, and
protecting their digital assets. This optimization amplifies the impact of digital marketing on performance, producing
stronger and more sustainable improvements in sales, customer retention, and business growth. The partial
mediation result implies that interventions in Southwest Papua should target both increased adoption of digital

UTAUT Perspective: Acceptance and Use of Technology
Although the detailed UTAUT coefficients are not reported here, the overall pattern of results is consistent
with the UTAUT framework. MSME owners’ willingness to adopt and use digital marketing appears to be influenced
by their expectations of improved performance (performance expectancy), perceptions of ease of use (effort
expectancy), social norms (social influence), and enabling conditions such as internet access and training (facilitating
conditions) (Puriwat & Tripopsakul, 2021).
These UTAUT constructs help explain why some MSMEs in Southwest Papua embrace digital marketing and
develop strong digital literacy, while others remain hesitant or passive. For example, MSMEs that receive strong
encouragement from peers or local business groups, and that have access to training and reliable connectivity, are
more likely to use digital marketing intensively and thus improve their performance. This highlights the importance
of building a supportive ecosystem around MSMEs, not only providing technology but also strengthening social and
institutional support (S. L. Rachmadana et al., 2024) . Implications for Policy and Practice in Southwest Papua. The
findings have several implications for stakeholders seeking to improve MSME performance in Southwest Papua:
Integrated training programs. Training should combine practical digital marketing skills (e.g., creating content,
managing social media accounts, using marketplaces) with broader digital literacy elements (e.g., understanding
analytics, data privacy, and cybersecurity). This integrated approach will strengthen both the direct and indirect
pathways from digital marketin g to performance. Supportive infrastructure and facilitating conditions. Consistent
with UTAUT, improving facilitating conditions —such as internet connectivity, access to devices, and local support
centers—will encourage MSME owners to adopt digital marketi ng and sustain its use. This is particularly critical in
remote areas where technical barriers remain significant. Data quality and performance monitoring. The note about
inconsistent turnover categories underscores the need for better data management pra ctices among MSMEs.
Encouraging MSME owners to record and categorize financial data consistently will not only improve research quality
but also help them monitor their own performance and make more informed business decisions. Community-based
digital literacy initiatives Given the partial mediation role of digital literacy, community -based initiatives—such as
workshops organized by local governments, universities, or NGOs —can play a key role in accelerating the
development of digital skills among MSME owne rs. These initiatives should be tailored to the local context of
Southwest Papua, using simple language, practical examples, and culturally appropriate approaches.

CONCLUSION
The study concludes that digital marketing and digital literacy, viewed through the UTAUT lens, jointly and
partially mediate the improvement of MSME performance in Southwest Papua. 1) Measurement instruments are
valid and reliable. All questionnaire items for digital marketing, digital literacy, and MSME performance are
statistically valid, with item –total correlations exceeding the critical r value, and show strong reliability with
Cronbach’s Alpha between 0.834 and 0.849. This indicates that the constructs in the study are measured accurately
and consistently. 2) Digital marketing significantly enhances digital literacy. Digital marketing has a strong, positive,
and significant effect on digital literacy (B = 0.634; p < 0.001). MSME owners who more intensively use digital
platforms for promotion and sales tend to develop higher levels of digital skills and understanding. In practice, active
engagement with social media, marketplaces, and other online tools becomes a pathway for learning and
strengthening digital literacy. 3) Digital marketing and digital literacy substantially explain  MSME performance.
Together, digital marketing and digital literacy explain 40.2% of the variance in MSME performance, showing that
these factors are important drivers of sales growth, customer reach, and business continuity. Although other

Implications for policy and practice in Southwest Papua
The findings imply that strategies to improve MSME performance should simultaneously: Promote wider
adoption of digital marketing tools. Strengthen digital literacy through targeted training and support programs.
Improve facilitating conditions, such as connectivity, access to devices, and local mentoring. Encourage better
recording and categorization of turnover data to support more accura te performance monitoring. Taken together,
the study underscores that in Southwest Papua, digital transformation for MSMEs is most effective when technology
adoption (digital marketing) goes hand in hand with capability development (digital literacy), supp orted by a
conducive ecosystem as described by UTAUT.
