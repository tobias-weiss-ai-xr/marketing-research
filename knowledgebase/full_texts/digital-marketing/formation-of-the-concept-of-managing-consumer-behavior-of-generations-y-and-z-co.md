Abstract
 Digital technologies have fundamentally transformed consumer behavior, particularly among Generations Y
and Z, whose consumption patterns are shaped by permanent online connectivity and social interaction. Despite
extensive research on digital consumption, an integrated understanding of how digital tools and social influence can be
used to manage consumer behavior across generations remains limited. This research aims to develop and empirically
substantiate a conceptual approach to managing the consumer behavior of Generations Y and Z in the context of digital
technologies. We used a combination of theoretical analysis, systematization of existing literature, and a quantitative
survey-based research design. Empirical data were collected through a structured  online questionnaire (n = 62).
Descriptive and comparative analysis was applied to test a set of hypotheses examining the effects of smartphone usage,
mobile applications, social influence, platform convenience, and promotional incentives on consumer beha vior. The
main results show that intensive smartphone use significantly increases the frequency of online purchases among both
generations. Platform convenience strongly influences Generation Y’s choice of shopping channels, while social
influence and online promotions play a decisive role in shaping Generation Z’s purchasing motivation. The presence of
mobile shopping applications alone does not guarantee brand loyalty, indicating the need for additional engagement
mechanisms. The novelty of this study lie s in the integrated empirical analysis of digital, social, and generational
determinants of consumer behavior. The findings contribute to consumer behavior theory by demonstrating the
differentiated impact of digital management tools across generations and  provide practical insights for developing
targeted digital marketing strategies.
Keywords: consumer behavior management, consumer preferences, consumer behavior, consumption crisis,
economic vulnerability.

Introduction
Consumer behavior constitutes one of the central research domains within modern marketing
and economic science, reflecting the complex mechanisms through which individuals and social
groups make purchasing and consumption decisions. Historically, the study of consumer behavior
developed at  the intersection of economics, sociology, and psychology, evolving from classical
rational-choice models toward more nuanced approaches that account for social influence, cultural
context, and behavioral irrationality. Contemporary research increasingly r ecognizes consumption
not merely as an economic act, but as a socially embedded and technologically mediated process
shaped by values, identities, and communication environments.
At the present stage, the rapid expansion of digital technologies has fundame ntally
transformed consumer behavior. Mobile devices, online platforms, social networks, and algorithm-
driven recommendation systems have altered information search processes, decision-making logic,
and the structure of market interaction. In this context,  Generations Y and Z attract particular
scholarly attention, as their consumption patterns were formed under conditions of continuous
digital connectivity. Existing studies actively examine mobile commerce, social media marketing,
influencer effects, and online shopping convenience; however, this body of research remains largely
fragmented. Most works focus either on technological instruments or on generational characteristics
separately, without developing an integrated conceptual understanding of how cons umer behavior
can be systematically managed in a digital environment [1].
The research problem, therefore, lies in the absence of a coherent and empirically grounded
concept for managing the consumer behavior of Generations Y and Z under conditions of pervasive
digitalization, where conventional marketing stimuli no longer produce predictable or uniform

Consumer behavior attracts sustained sc holarly and practical interest in marketing research, as it
enables the analysis and prediction of how consumers make purchasing decisions under the
influence of multiple interacting factors. Understanding these mechanisms allows marketers to
assess not only which products consumers choose, but also how and why these choices are formed
in everyday consumption, including the purchase of essential goods and food products [8].
Contemporary approaches to consumer behavior emphasize the role of need recognition and
problem awareness as the initial stage of the decision-making process. This stage is followed by the
influence of situational factors, including the social and physical environment, time constraints, and
the consumer’s prior emotional or physiological state. Psychological determinants, such as
personality traits, values, beliefs, lifestyle, perceptual selectivity, learning processes, and brand
attachment, further shape individual consumption patterns. In parallel, socio -cultural
influencesincluding opinion leaders, family structures, reference groups, social class, and word-of-
mouth communication play a significant role in reinforcing or modifying consumer preferences.
Informational factors, particularly access to relevant information and the ability to assess the
perceived value of goods and services under different purchasing conditions, also contribute to the
final decision [9-11].
In the digital economy, these traditional determinants are increasingly mediated by digital
space and technological platforms. Social networks and online environments function as interactive
communication systems in which consumers simultaneously receive information, exchange
opinions, and construct collective perceptions of products and services [12]. As a result, consumer
decision-making processes become embedded within complex multichannel ecosystems that
integrate offline and online experiences. Preferences and attitudes formed in physical retail
environments are often transferred to digital contexts, and vice versa, reinf orcing the continuity of
consumer behavior across channels [13]. Consequently, the study of consumer behavior
management in the digital era requires  a comprehensive understanding of evolving consumer
beliefs, values, and motivational structures, which are increasingly shaped by digitally mediated
interactions and the pursuit of need satisfaction.
Materials and methods of research.
The methodological framework of this study is based on a combination of theoretical and
empirical research methods aimed at identifying and testing key factors influencing the consumer
behavior of Generations Y and Z in the context of digital technologies.
The theoretical stage of the research involved the systematization and critical analysis of
contemporary academic literature on consumer behavior, generational theory, and digital marketing.
At this stage, the method of analysis was applied, whereby the collected sources were decomposed
into conceptual units in order to examine individual theoretical positions, behavioral mode ls, and
explanatory approaches. Subsequently, the method of synthesis was used to integrate the analyzed
elements into a coherent conceptual understanding of consumer behavior management under digital
transformation [14].
To structure and organize the identified factors, the classification method was employed. This
method made it possible to group digital, social, and psychological determinants of consumer
behavior based on common characteristics and functional roles. In addition, the modeling method
was ap plied to conceptually represent the relationships between digital technologies, social
influence, and consumer decision-making processes among different generations [15]. The method
of analogy was used to identify similarities and differences in behavioral  patterns across
generational cohorts by comparing observed consumer responses under comparable digital

The empirical stage of the study was designed to test a set of hypotheses derived from the
theoretical analysis. These hypotheses reflect the assumed relationships between digital
technologies, social influence, and c onsumer behavior patterns among Generations Y and Z. The
hypotheses were formulated prior to data collection and are grounded in existing literature on digital
consumption and generational behavior [16].
The study tests the following hypotheses:
Hypothesis 1a posits that the intensity of smartphone use affects the frequency of online
purchases among representatives of Generations Y and Z.
Hypothesis 1b assumes that the availability of mobile shopping applications increases
consumer loyalty to brands among Generations Y and Z.
Hypothesis 2a suggests that social influence, expressed through friends’ reviews on social
media, affects Generation Z’s intention to purchase products from a specific brand.
Hypothesis 2b proposes that social influence in the form of r ecommendations from social
media influencers affects the purchasing preferences of Generation Y.
Hypothesis 3a assumes that the level of convenience of online platforms influences
Generation Y’s choice between traditional and digital shopping channels.
Hypothesis 3b posits that the availability of online promotions and discounts is directly related
to Generation Z’s motivation to make purchases.
To test these hypotheses, a quantitative research design was employed using a structured
online questionnaire. The survey served as the primary source of empirical data and was conducted
among representatives of Generations Y and Z. The questionnaire included demographic variables
as well as indicators measuring smartphone usage intensity, frequency of online purchas es, use of
mobile applications, perceived platform convenience, exposure to social influence, and
responsiveness to promotions and discounts. The survey instrument was developed based on
validated questions used in prior international studies and adapted t o the objectives of the present
research.
The collected data were analyzed using descriptive statistical methods and comparative
analysis to identify patterns, relationships, and differences in consumer behavior across generational
groups. The study confirmed or rejected each hypothesis by examining the consistency between the
observed empirical distributions and the expected relationships formulated in the hypotheses.
This methodological approach ensures logical consistency between the theoretical framework,
hypothesis formulation, data collection, and empirical analysis, thereby providing a reliable basis
for interpreting the results and drawing scientifically grounded conclusions.

across generations and manifested through consumption patterns. In the digital era, culture
increasingly operates within online environments, where social interaction, symbolic meanings, and
consumption norms are rapidly formed and transformed. Consumer culture encompasses both
objective elements, such as product characteristics and purchasing conditions, and subjective
