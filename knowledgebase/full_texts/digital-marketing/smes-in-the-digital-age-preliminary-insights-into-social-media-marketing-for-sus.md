In the digital era, small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) have
opportunities and challenges when adopting social media for marketing.
Grounded in the Resource-based View (RBV) and Diffusion of Innovations
(DOI) theories, this study aims to explore the use of social media
marketing strategies by SMEs in response to the unprecedented business
disruption caused by the COVID-19 pandemic. A qualitative approach was
employed, involving three in-depth interviews with a marketing manager
and two SME owners from three different SMEs. The data were analysed
thematically using NVivo software. This study found that SMEs prefer to
use Facebook, Instagram, and TikTok to increase brand awareness, engage
with consumers, and increase revenue. Preliminary findings also show that
SMEs have improved their business performance while reducing marketing
expenses using social media marketing. However, SMEs face difficulties in
navigating the digital marketing landscape due to internet access problems,
limited resources, lack of new ideas for creating attractive promotional ads,
and changes in consumer buying behaviour. These region-specific and non-
generalizable insights offer practical guidance for SMEs seeking to leverage
digital tools for resilience and growth. Moreover, the findings provide
preliminary implications for policymakers in supporting sustainable SME’s
development through initiatives such as digital literacy training and targeted
financial assistance.

Digital Marketing Strategies
Digital marketing was defined by Mandal et al.
(2016) as brand promotion using all forms of
digital advertising media available to reach the
target audience. The modern digital landscape
for online organisations includes Social Media
Marketing (SMM), Search Engine Marketing
(SEM), and Search Engine Optimization (SEO)
as essential elements (Lies, 2019; Palos-Sanchez
et al., 2019). While SEM deals with strategies
for sponsoring search engines and website
banner ads, SEO concentrates on improving
search engine results. Conversely, SMM uses
techniques to interact with consumers on
social media platforms through social ads. To
engage with consumers and give them a way to
interact with the brand through digital channels,
marketers utilise electronic media to promote
products or services in the market (Yasmin et
al., 2015).
Chinakidzwa and Phiri (2020) found that
the performance of a business is impacted by
marketing strategy and execution, which are
linked to digital marketing strategy. Therefore,
if a company does not have an effective
digital marketing strategy, it might miss out
on opportunities to revolutionise its digital
marketing, achieve only partial integration of
modern technology into its current business
system, create a process that is not entirely
seamless, and unable to create indicators to
monitor its digital marketing efforts. Chaffey
(2011) agreed that social media marketing is an
integral part of digital marketing. When done
through websites, online advertisements, opt-in
emails, interactive kiosks, interactive TVs, or
mobile devices, digital marketing is also referred
to as online marketing (Chaffey & Smith, 2008).
A study by Gao et al . (2023) found that
SMEs may maintain their sustainability by
implementing digital marketing. Digital
marketing is the fastest method to brand
products and services. Using the internet is also
the most practical mean to transfer information
and business ideas, hence it is a technology
enabler for a sustainable competitive edge
(Sidek et al., 2020). The best way to understand
how customers behave and interact with
businesses online is through digital marketing,
which has become crucial for promoting
and communicating businesses’ sustainable
initiatives today (Saura et al., 2020).
According to Chaffey and Ellis-Chadwick
(2019), a growing number of businesses,
including those in manufacturing, retail,
wholesale, and others, are integrating smart
digital marketing into their overall sustainable
marketing strategies. Many contemporary SMEs
in developed countries have already incorporated
sustainability initiatives into all their advertising
initiatives, including traditional and digital
marketing strategies, communications, and
other strategies, as sustainability has emerged as
one of the most important issues in recent years
(Dumitriu et al., 2019).

Social Media Marketing
Strategically, social media involvement
facilitates the flow of resources between both
companies and consumers. Hollebeek et al .
(2019) argued that consumers may allocate
intangible resources (such as knowledge)
and tangible resources (such as equipment)
while engaging with companies. Social media
marketing provides a unique chance to reach a
worldwide audience, interact with customers in
real time, and establish brand loyalty without
the huge expenditures that major organizations
require (Kedi et al ., 2024). Social media
marketing is important for SMEs since it is easily
accessible and cost-effective. Platforms such
as Facebook, Instagram, Twitter, and LinkedIn
provide a variety of tools and statistics that
enable firms to adjust their marketing efforts,
target certain demographics, and track campaign
effectiveness (Adewumi et al ., 2024). This
highlights the crucial importance of network
interaction in integrating resources. Customer-
to-customer contacts are very important,
particularly for more involved engagement
behaviours (Fehrer et al., 2018).
Therefore, the interconnection and
interactions on social media platforms, both
between firms and customers and amongst
customers themselves, may be seen as
valuable resources that can be transformed into
marketing capabilities, as stated by Morgan and
Hunt (1999). An example of this is the ability
of a company to use information obtained
from interactions on social media platforms to
discover and foster loyal clients, which is known
as CRM skills (Trainor et al., 2014).
As social media has evolved from a simple
communication medium to a valuable source
of consumer and market insights, marketers
may strategically use social media to create
unique resources that align with their existing
organisational resources and skills. Positive
marketing outcomes can be achieved by
businesses through strategic engagement with
the customers. Thus, social media provides
digital opportunities that, when integrated
with fundamental marketing principles, enable
organisations to develop and implement
effective social media marketing strategies.
One of the advantages of using social media
marketing is its impact on brand reputation and
awareness. Social media can increase a brand’s
reputation through the widespread dissemination
of thoughts and reviews by customers. This
occurs through interaction between consumers
in expressing their support or criticism of a
brand, therefore spreading positive or negative
feedback about a firm.
Meanwhile, brand awareness refers to the
level of familiarity and recognition of a brand
in the minds of consumers. It directly affects the
consumers’ ability to remember and recall the
brand, as well as their likelihood of purchasing
the product’s brand (Lal et al ., 2019). Word-
of-mouth marketing is successful when the
message is positive. Yet, negative reviews have
the potential to quickly spread, and this will
damage the trust that exists between a business
and its customers. Hence, it is important
to formulate an efficient strategy for social
media communication to create trust. This is
because the rapid spread of information on
online platforms may have negative effects on
relationships and reputation, resulting in long-
term effects on the firm (Dolega et al ., 2021).

Challenges in Social Media Marketing
One key aspect that affects adoption is
the proficiency of employees in handling
technological complexity (Bharati et al., 2015).
Bharati et al . (2015) also demonstrated that
proficiency in using information technology
has an impact on the rate at which organisations
embrace new technologies. SMEs need to
allocate their resources, such as employees and
time, to properly manage their social media
presence (Rugova & Prenaj, 2016). It is important
for employees to possess the necessary expertise
and receive appropriate training to effectively
respond to consumer feedback and concerns,
as well as proactively manage unfavourable
remarks before they gain wider public
attention. SMEs need to consistently provide
fresh information regarding their business
to maintain customer engagement and foster
ongoing connections. However, SMEs should
refrain from spreading inconsequential material
since it has the potential to be bothersome. It
is very important for SMEs to regulate and
assess the results of social media marketing to
comprehend its efficiency and efficacy. Besides
that, SMEs could face several threats, including
bad reputations, legal problem, and operational
risks. So, to successfully execute social media
marketing in business activities, SMEs need to
have a clear understanding of the advantages
and disadvantages of each social media platform
to not waste valuable resources such as time and
money.

significant challenge in social media marketing is
the awareness of high costs. Technological social

Strategies for Effective Social Media Marketing
The success of social media marketing is
dependent on the role that consumers attribute
to firms and brands in social media (Felix et al.,
2017). Consumers may see firms and brands as
intruders, gate crashers, or unwelcomed visitors.
Nevertheless, studies indicate that many users
anticipate companies to engage in social media
discussions and may intentionally include
companies in the debate by referencing the brand
or using hashtags. This disparity may indicate
a diverse customer base, with some consumers
being receptive to proactive and involved
companies while others resist the breach.
Meanwhile, according to a study conducted
by Chatterjee and Kumar (2020), SMEs have a
significant potential to enhance and expand their
offerings to effectively fulfil customer demands.
SMEs can easily accomplish this task by using
the consumers’ comments and recommendations
found on social media platforms. This is
facilitating the collaborative development of the
business operations of SMEs. It will ultimately
lead to the enhancement of the general economic
well-being of the SMEs.
One of the main advantages that SMEs gain
from social media marketing is cost efficiency.
Rugova and Prenaj (2016) pointed out that social
media marketing is a cost-effective marketing
method that requires little advertising expenses
and time investment. It is a cost-effective
method for successful business marketing. If
a business knows the specific platforms where
its target audience engages in social media, a
little investment is sufficient to start a social
media strategy, which may provide remarkable
benefits. For example, Facebook provides a
very economical method of focused marketing.
This is very important for SMEs, as they lack
the financial resources to adopt expensive
marketing plans like big companies. This
advantage is particularly significant for SMEs
and SME start-ups in developing countries, as
it allows them to increase their brand visibility,
while at the same time reducing costs and saving
time. In addition, Kumar et al . (2016) agreed
that content creation through a company’s social
media affects consumers’ purchasing decisions.

Materials and Methods
Underpinning Theory
This study is theoretically supported by two
main theories: Resource-based View (RBV)
theory and the Diffusion of Innovations (DOI)
theory. According to Ulrich and Barney (1984),
the RBV originated in the field of economics
(Penrose, 2009). They argued that the services
that resources generate are dependent on how
they are used; the same resource can generate
different services depending on the use, purpose,
method, and combination of other resources.
Penrose (2009) posited that an organisation’s
uniqueness stems from the way it integrates
its abilities and assets. The RBV theory states
that for businesses to obtain a competitive edge,
they must contribute to business rivalry with
distinctive assets and competencies (Ulrich &
Barney, 1984). Furthermore, RBV also suggests
that SMEs can gain a competitive advantage

in social media marketing strategies by
utilising their unique resources, which include
innovation, personalised customer interactions
through customer relationship management
(CRM), and adaptability to new technologies.
With that, SMEs can produce engaging content,
interact with their audience, and react quickly to
changes in the market. Thus, through the RBV
framework, certain SMEs can take advantage of
digital technology advances even in resource-

Meanwhile, the DOI theory developed
by Rogers (2003) looks at how new ideas and
innovations can be adopted within businesses.
According to the DOI theory, variables
including compatibility, complexity, trialability,
observability, and relative advantage influence
how quickly innovations can be adopted in an
organisation. When applied to social media
marketing, the DOI outlines the rationale and
ways to facilitate SMEs’ use of social media
tools and platforms. It suggests that SMEs are
more than ready to adopt social media marketing
if they believe it to be beneficial, aligned with
their current operations, easy to implement, and
offers positive outcomes.
Therefore, the RBV and DOI integration
provides this study in a theoretical realm to
determine why SMEs adopt and use social media
tools as part of their marketing offerings. From
the RBV theory perspective, various digital
competencies (content creation, social media
facilitating customer engagement, and branding
storytelling) emerge as a digital resource to
render SMEs competitive advantages (Trainor et
al., 2014; Ritz et al., 2019). Within the scope of a
few resources available, such competencies are
a distinguishing factor against larger companies
(Dwivedi et al ., 2021). Simultaneously,
integration with DOI theory for technology
adoption intention occurs naturally due to the
innovations’ perceived advantage and the degree
of adaptability or complexity of use. For social
media tools to be investigated in this study,
they must be embraced after being perceived as
beneficial, aligned with what can be facilitated,
and generally, perceived as useful (Gao et al .,
2023; Tarazona-Montoya et al., 2024).
For example, the study reveals that the
tools with low costs and easy to observe (such
as social media platforms) and aligned with
branding and engagement efforts are more
likely to be adopted by SMEs (Chatterjee &
Kumar Kar, 2020; Rizvanović et al., 2023). Yet
most of the international literature thus far has
assessed SMEs in well-established, urbanised
areas where digital capabilities flourish. Little
research has been conducted on those with
limited capabilities and inconsistent digital
access. Thus, this work is positioned as a
contribution to the literature gaps where the RBV
and DOI theoretical approach substantiates the
ability of SMEs on the East Coast of Malaysia to
develop marketing advantages despite resource
limitations and inadequate infrastructure
access. The international, theoretical appeal
of the findings will further justify a more
contextualised approach to understanding
similar SMEs undergoing digital transformation
in developing areas.

This study adopted a qualitative research
design using semi-structured interviews to

Theme One: The Effects of Social Media
Marketing Strategies on Business Performance
The participants recognised that social media
marketing strategies used during the COVID-19
pandemic improved their business performance.
In alignment with the RBV , SMEs leveraged
their internal capabilities such as flexibility,
creativity, and personalised engagement to
gain digital visibility despite limited resources.

Theme Three: Overcoming the Obstacles
There may be several challenges when putting
social media marketing strategies into practice,

approaches. P1 mentioned that:
“A lot of businesses enter the world
of social media without measurable
targets. The most important thing that
you need to do is to establish your
specific objectives. In simple words,
you must have measurable and relevant
targets. For example, set a six-month
goal to increase brand awareness
by 20%. Or you set monthly key
performance indicators (KPIs) such
as engagement rates and click-through
rates. This will help your company to
measure your achievement.”
(P1)
P1 added:
“Our company also sends employees
to a skill-building class, so they are
capable of operating the packaging
machine and social media, such as
Facebook ads.”
(P1)

“SMEs often suffer lack of resources,

Limitations and Future Research
This study is not without limitations. In terms
of generalizability, the interviews were only
conducted in East Coast Malaysia. The findings
of this study can only be applied to the SMEs in
East Coast Malaysia. Care should be exercised
before applying these findings to other SMEs as
they are not generalised to the whole population
in Malaysia. Therefore, other researchers should
be cautious in generalizing the findings of the
study to other SMEs in general. In fact, the
observations and interviews with only three
respondents may limit the findings and cannot
be used to generalise the whole SMEs in East
Coast Malaysia. This is because the culture and
IT readiness that each SMEs have, for example,
might be different if compared to the SMEs
in the West Coast or South Coast Region in
Malaysia.
Therefore, future studies should consider
employing mixed-method or quantitative
approach, by using a survey approach through
a questionnaire distributed to the SME
population in Malaysia. This will provide more
understanding of the impacts of social media
marketing strategies on the performance of
Malaysian SMEs. Additionally, further research
could focus on specific social media platforms,
such as TikTok Shop or Facebook Marketplace
and explore how SMEs use these integrated
e-commerce tools not just for promotion but
for direct sales. Exploring platform-specific
strategies will enhance the understanding of how
multi-functional digital ecosystems shape SME
marketing behaviour and business performance.

comprehensive analysis to be made on the
adoption of digital marketing amongst SMEs
in a regionally developing environment. These
social media websites not only provided an
economical innovation in technology, which
was subsequently adopted due to its ease (DOI),
but are also viewed in this research as strategic
resources for these companies, differentiating
their competitiveness in markets via branding,
relationships, and market responses (RBV).
In addressing the aforementioned
barriers such as limited internet connectivity,
inadequate resources to support content
creation, and lack of content development
ideas, three recommendations are proposed for
policymakers in East Coast Malaysia. The first
is targeted digital literacy training involving the
delivery of workshops and training sessions on
content creation, reporting social media metrics,
and engaging customers online, especially in
places where SMEs have less access to digital
connection. The second is subsidised internet
provision that has the potential to improve a
reliable and affordable connection to individuals
and businesses in less connected or rural
areas to help alleviate the divide that exists
because of connectivity. Finally, providing
digital transformation grants or other financial
incentives to SMEs to allow them to invest
in digital tools, receive specific training, and
potentially hiring external help (e.g., freelancers
or agencies as P2 suggested).
