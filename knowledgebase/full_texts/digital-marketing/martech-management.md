# Martech Management

Martech Management
Martech Management: Aligning Marketing Technology with Business Strategy  offers
a clear and practical guide to navigating the complex world of Marketing Technology
(Martech). This book empowers marketers to harness Martech tools and data effectively,
aligning their marketing strategies with business objectives to drive measurable growth
and revenue.
Offering a step-  by- step process for identifying objectives, understanding stakeholder
requirements, and creating business process maps, this guide helps readers confidently
navigate the Martech landscape and make informed decisions. Each chapter includes
key definitions to clarify technical concepts and terms, extensive notes to provide back -
ground and context, and discussion questions to encourage critical thinking and concep-
tual understanding. Real- world mini- cases and a thoroughly developed case study about
an Indian pharmaceutical firm that successfully implemented a Martech makeover help
students relate theory to practice.
Providing the knowledge and tools needed to harness Martech effectively, this text is
recommended reading for postgraduate and executive education students studying digital
marketing strategy, business process management, and digital business more generally,
as well as marketing professionals. It is rooted in the author’s extensive background in
managing marketing in big firms and small.
Supplementary resources include an Instructor’s Manual, Lecture Slides, Exam
Questions, and In- class Exercises, and are available at martechmgt.com.
Tim P. McMahon  is Associate Professor of Practice Marketing and Management at
Creighton University, Heider College of Business, and a member of adjunct faculty at
New York University School of Professional Studies.

Introduction
Martech Management
Addressing a key paradox in modern marketing, this book depicts a situation in
which, despite unprecedented access to thousands of technological tools, marketers are
challenged to link their Martech capabilities to reliable revenue generation. The main
argument is that the problem lies not in technology but in the frameworks that transform
functionality into revenue. Specifically, it seeks to empower people to bridge functional
gaps within the organization to deliver fruitful customer experiences across the journey
from engagement to consideration, conversion, loyalty, and evangelism.
The book is broken down into three parts:
Diagnostic Foundation (Chapters 1 – 4): Martech has evolved through distinct
paradigms, from addressability to automation, orchestration, and artificial intel-
ligence (AI). While aimed at implementing effective tools, each advancement
introduced new complexities and failed to deliver predictable revenue outcomes.
Process mapping helps firms identify causes, such as workflows that fragment
across silos, leading to revenue loss at handoffs among strategy, execution, and
learning. We learn that revenue impact depends on four multiplicative factors,
including Technology, Adoption, Integration, and Insight, where a shortcoming in
any of these impairs system performance.
Operational Infrastructure (Chapters 5– 7): Three core systems contribute to Martech
success. First, there must be a solid infrastructure that delivers disciplined data
collection, reliable identity resolution, quality management practices, information
accessibility, and well- informed governance. These are the pillars of effective data
management and governance, which directly contribute to accuracy, reliability, and
successful outcomes. Second, the system must be positioned to facilitate dynamic
decision- making through shared definitions, clear ownership (accountability), pre -
dictable cadences, and action-  producing protocols. Third, the firm must develop
a mature, strategic approach to AI. This comes from shifting a tool acquisition
mindset to engineering an AI-  compatible infrastructure built on purposeful data
collection, adaptive systems, and coherent, value-  producing policies. Specifically,
that means building universal data layers, Application Programming Interface-
ready infrastructure, and governance frameworks that enable experimentation
without chaos.
Execution Discipline (Chapters 8– 11): The challenge of translating capability into rev -
enue lies in four critical activities: (a) process mapping shrinks the strategy- execution

gap by documenting actual workflows, exposing bottlenecks, and transforming
abstract strategy into repeatable operations; (b) vendor selection through a stra -
tegic procurement process that aims to use capabilities to generate revenue through
a scenario- based evaluation that contracts for effective adoption and integration
rather than features; (c) implementation through phased development that succeeds
using a prove- stabilize- scale sequencing, practice of continuous integration, closed-
loop data activation, and disciplined optimization; and finally (d) marketing- sales-
data alignment  that requires cross-  functional coordination to convert Martech
complexity into competitive advantage through shared definitions, clear decision
rights, and governance preventing drift.
Martech generates revenue through interdependent capabilities functioning as a unified
system. This relies on members of the organization shifting focus from “what to buy” to
“what to fix” and “how to coordinate?” We learn that it is not about gadgets and shiny
new objects; it demands building operational coherence, measurement discipline, and
cross- functional alignment that convert technological potential into measurable business
outcomes. The strategic imperative is to treat Martech as an evolving organizational cap-
ability that requires continuous stewardship, disciplined governance, and a systematic
link between technical capability and revenue generation.
This book is about technology and how we use it to generate revenue and profit. More
specifically, as one concept reviewer put it, it aims to provide a “robust and structured
guidance on how to implement Martech effectively.” In creating this book, technology
was used at every step, including AI, scholarly search platforms, writing assistants,
interviews with experts, transcription and note- taking platforms, and databases.
While I enjoy a long, highly engaged career in business spanning decades, my published
work has focused on organizational performance and the skills and characteristics that
drive buy- in through effective leadership communication. As I wrote in my first book,
leadership aims for real change that reflects the mutual purposes of leaders and followers.
This assumes that the follower is as much a part of success as the leader. It is not inde -
pendence, but interdependence. As such, three actions drive this concept: “vigorously
confront reality, expand individual and organizational knowledge, and create safety for
members to take bold action.”
