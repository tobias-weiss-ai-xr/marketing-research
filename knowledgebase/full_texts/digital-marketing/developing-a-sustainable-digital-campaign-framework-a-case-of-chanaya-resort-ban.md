 As sustainability becomes increasingly important in tourism industry.
Destinations are expected to communicate sustainability values effectively
through digital channels. However, existing studies on sustainability
communication and digital marketing provi de limited guidance on how
sustainability can be systematically integrated into digital campaign
planning. This study aims to develop a framework for integrating
sustainability into digital campaign planning for tourism destinations.
Using a Design Science  Research approach, Chanaya Resort Bandung was
employed as a case study. Data were collected through observations,
interviews, and document analysis, followed by stakeholder validation
involving academic, industry, and destination representatives. The stud y
resulted in a Sustainable Digital Campaign Framework consisting of four
interconnected layers: Strategic Foundation, Campaign Design, Campaign
Execution, and Campaign Evaluation. The framework integrates audience
persona and sustainability value as key i nputs for developing campaign
objectives, narratives, content strategies, activation activities, and
evaluation mechanisms. Validation results indicate that the framework is
relevant, practical, and applicable for tourism destinations. This study
contributes to sustainable tourism marketing by providing a structured
approach for translating sustainability values into digital communication
and campaign planning.

INTRODUCTION
Digital marketing has become an important driver of tourism competitiveness
by enabling destinations to build awareness, shape destination image, and engage
potential visitors through social media and other digital platforms (Oka & Subadra,
2024; Veseli et al., 2025) . At the same time, growing environmental awareness has
increased tourists’ expectations for destinations that demonstrate sustainable
practices and communicate environmental, socio -cultural and economic values
transparently (Chang et al., 2025) . Consequently, sustainability communication has
emerged as an essential component of destination marketing because it strengthens
destination positioning while supporting long-term competitiveness (Tölkes, 2018).
Recent studies indicate that sustainability communication, destination
storytelling, and value-based communication positively influence destination image,
visitor trust and travel intentions  (Alkhofaily & Noureldin, 2026; Chen et al., 2025) .

Likewise, digital campaign planning emphasizes the importance of aligning campaign
objectives, audience understanding, implementation activities  and evaluation within
a structured communication process (Liu & Mačerinskienė, 2016) . Previous research
also highlights that effective tourism marketing should be developed based on
audience characteristics and supported by consistent content strategies and systematic
campaign evaluation (Papageorgiou & Marneros, 2023; Veseli et al., 2025) . These
studies collectively suggest that sustainability communication and digital campaign
management are closely related in achieving effective destination marketing.
Despite these advances, previous studies have largely examined sustainability
communication and digital marketing as separate research streams. Existing literature
explains what sustainability communication should convey  (Tölkes, 2018) and how
digital campaigns should be managed  (Liu & Mačerinskienė, 2016) , but provides
limited guidance on how sustainability values can be systematically integrated
throughout the digital campaign planning process. Furthermore, few studies connect
audience persona, sustainability values, campaign objectives, communication
narratives, content strategies, campaign execution, and evaluation within a single
planning framework. This limitation reduces the practical applicability of existing
knowledge for tourism destinations seeking to communicate sustainability
consistently through digital channels.
Addressing this gap is particularly important as tourism destinations
increasingly rely on digital communication to strengthen competitiveness and
promote sustainable tourism experiences. A structured framework can assist
destination managers in translatin g sustainability values into campaign objectives,
communication strategies, implementation activities  and evaluation mechanisms
while maintaining consistency across digital platforms. However, such an integrated
framework remains underdeveloped in the existing tourism marketing literature.
Therefore, this study aims to develop a Sustainable Digital Campaign
Framework for tourism destinations using Chanaya Resort Bandung as a case study.
A Design Science Research (DSR) approach is adopted to design, demonstrate  and
evaluate the proposed framework through expert validation involving academics,
industry practitioners, and destination representatives. The study contributes
theoretically by integrating sustainability communication and digital campaign
planning into a  unified framework and contributes practically by providing a
structured guideline for tourism destinations to design sustainability-oriented digital
campaigns.

METHODS
This study employed a Design Science Research (DSR) approach to develop a
Sustainable Digital Campaign Framework for tourism destinations. DSR was selected
because the objective of this study was to develop an artifact in the form of a
framework that addre sses a practical problem while contributing to academic
knowledge. Following (Peffers et al., 2007) the research adopted five stages: problem
identification, objective definition, design and development, demonstration and
evaluation.
Chanaya Resort Bandung was selected as the case study. Data were collected
through semi-structured interviews, observations and document analysis to identify
existing digital campaign practices, communication challenges, audience
characteristics and sustai nability values relevant to the destination context. The
framework was developed following five stages of the DSR process: problem
identification, objective definition, design and development, demonstration  and
evaluation. The evaluation stage involved expert validation by tourism and digital
marketing professionals. The experts were selected based on their experience in
tourism management, destination marketing, and digital campaign planning.
Feedback was co llected through semi -structured discussions focusing on the
relevance, clarity, completeness, and applicability of the proposed framework
Following the DSR process, the study began with problem identification
through an analysis of Chanaya Resort’s existing digital communication practices. The
identified problems were then translated into framework objectives aimed at
supporting more structu red and sustainability -oriented digital campaign planning.
The framework was subsequently designed and developed by integrating empirical
findings with concepts from sustainability communication, digital marketing and
digital campaign management literature . To demonstrate its applicability, the
proposed framework was applied through a campaign planning simulation using the
“Return to What Matters” campaign. Finally, the framework was evaluated through
expert validation involving academic and industry experts, and the feedback obtained
was used to refine and finalize the framework. Figure 1 illustrates the Design Science
Research process adopted in this study.

FINDINGS AND DISCUSSION
Existing Conditions and Framework Development
The analysis of Chanaya Resort Bandung revealed that Instagram has been
actively utilized as the primary digital communication platform to promote
accommodation, recreational activities, culinary experiences, and nature -based
tourism. The findings indicate  that existing communication primarily emphasizes
destination experiences and promotional content, while sustainability -related values
are communicated inconsistently and without a coherent campaign structure.
Although environmental and well -being messages occasionally appear, they are not
systematically incorporated into campaign objectives, communication strategies, or
evaluation activities.
These findings suggest that the destination’s digital communication remains
content-oriented rather than strategy -driven. Campaign activities are developed
without explicitly linking audience understanding, sustainability values, campaign
objectives, implementation activities, and performance evaluation. Consequently, the
intended positioning of Chanaya Resort as a meaningful nature -based destination is
not consistently reflected across its digital communication. The empirical analysis

The proposed Sustainable Digital Campaign Framework was therefore
developed by integrating the empirical findings from Chanaya Resort with the
theoretical synthesis presented in Table 2. Empirically, the destination already
possessed sustainability -related values reflected in its tourism activities however,
these values had not been translated into a structured communication process.
Theoretically, previous studies identify the essential components of effective digital
campaign planning but do not explain h ow these components should be integrated
into a sustainability-oriented planning framework.
To address this gap, the framework adapts the digital campaign management
model proposed by Liu and Mačerinskienė (2016) while extending it through the
introduction of a Strategic Foundation layer. Unlike the original model, which begins
with campaign plan ning, the proposed framework starts by identifying audience
personas and sustainability values as strategic inputs for campaign development.

The demonstration illustrates how audience personas and sustainability values

The framework was subsequently evaluated through expert validation
involving academics, tourism practitioners, and destination representatives. Overall,
the evaluators agreed that the framework provides a logical and systematic approach
for integrating sus tainability into digital campaign planning. The Strategic
Foundation layer was considered particularly valuable because it encourages
destinations to identify audience characteristics and sustainability values before
designing communication activities. Likewise, the Campaign Design layer effectively
connects strategic insights with communication implementation.
The evaluators also emphasized the importance of maintaining an iterative
relationship between campaign execution and campaign evaluation. Although
campaign performance indicators were considered useful for evidence -based
decision-making, they recommended that these indicators remain adaptable to
different destination characteristics and campaign objectives. Only minor refinements
were required following the validation process, indicating that the framework is
conceptually coherent and practically applicable.
These findings reinforce previous research emphasizing the importance of
destination storytelling and value-based communication in shaping destination image
and visitor perceptions  (Alkhofaily & Noureldin, 2026; Chen et al., 2025) . However,
unlike previous studies, the present research integrates audience understanding,
sustainability values, campaign objectives, communication narratives,
implementation strategies, and evaluation mechanisms into a single planning
framework. Consequen tly, sustainability communication is positioned not as an
additional promotional message but as the strategic foundation that guides the entire
digital campaign planning process.
This study therefore contributes both theoretically and practically.
Theoretically, it extends the digital campaign management model of Liu and
Mačerinskienė (2016) by incorporating Audience Persona and Sustainability Value as
foundational planning components. Practically, the framework provides destination
managers with a structured guideline for designing sustainability -oriented digital
campaigns that align destination identity, visitor expectations, communication
strategies, and campaign evaluation within a continuous improvement process.

CONCLUSIONS
This study aimed to develop a framework for integrating sustainability into
digital campaign planning for tourism destinations using Chanaya Resort Bandung as
a case study. The findings indicate that existing digital communication activities at
Chanaya Res ort primarily focused on destination experiences, facilities, and
promotional content, while sustainability -related values were communicated
inconsistently and were not integrated within a structured campaign planning
process. Furthermore, audience understanding, campaign objectives, communication
activities, and evaluation mechanisms were not systematically connected.
To address these challenges, this study developed a Sustainable Digital
Campaign Framework consisting of four interconnected layers: Strategic Foundation,
Campaign Design, Campaign Execution, and Campaign Evaluation. The framework
integrates audience perso na and sustainability value as key inputs for developing
campaign objectives, narratives, content strategies, activation activities, and
evaluation mechanisms. By connecting these components within a single planning
process, the framework provides a struct ured approach for translating sustainability
values into digital communication activities.
The evaluation results indicate that the framework is conceptually relevant and
practically applicable for tourism destinations. Validation involving academic,
industry and destination stakeholders confirmed that the framework can support
more consistent sustainability communication and strengthen destination positioning
through digital campaigns.
Theoretically, this study contributes to sustainable tourism marketing
literature by integrating sustainability communication and digital campaign planning
within a single framework. Practically, the framework may assist destination
managers and tourism ma rketers in designing digital campaigns that align
sustainability values with visitor expectations and communication objectives.
This study is limited to a single case study and stakeholder validation involving
a limited number of participants. Therefore, the findings should be interpreted within
the context of Chanaya Resort Bandung. Future studies may apply and evaluate the
framework across different tourism destination types and geographical contexts to
further assess its adaptability and effectiveness. In addition, future research may
explore the relationship between framework implementation and campaign
performance outcomes using quantitative or mixed-method approaches.
