# The Mechanism and Implications of Social Media Marketing Strategies on Contemporary Consumer Behavior in the Digital Marketing Environment

Yiling Luo
Adelaide Business School, Adelaide University, Adelaide, SA5000 , Australia

Abstract: Social media has become a platform that combines content creation, social interaction and e -commerce transactions,
and has revolutionised the way customers obtain information and make purchase decisions . In this work, we aim to study the
mechanism of mark eting strategies affecting customer behaviour, using the data of digital marketing development and social
media development in China in 2025 –2026. The research results show that the evolution of participation in information
dissemination has made consumers  rely more on content created by users rather than one -way brand promotion; algorithmic
recommendations have narrowed the scope of consumer choice and improved the efficiency of information matching; and the
decentralisation of social trust has made peer e valuation and opinion leader recommendations become important pillars for
purchase decisions. These processes show distinct features in different platform environments. Reflexivity tells us that
algorithmic recommendations undermine consumer autonomy, that  users' contributions to platform content are expropriated
without recompense under commercial rationality, and consumer behaviour moves further away from its use value under
symbolic provocation. Social media marketing is an ignition. But consumers are do minated by systemic hazards. The commercial
value realisation should be based on the protection of consumer subject.
Keywords: Social media marketing, Consumer behavior, Algorithm recommendation, Social trust, Digital labor .

1. Introduction
China 's digital economy is transforming the core logic of
consumption behaviours . The data released by China Internet
Network Information Center showed that the domestic digital
consumption in the first half of 2025 reached 9.37 trillion
yuan. The number of users topped 958 million, accounting for
85.3% of the total number of internet  users. The count of
online purchasing users meanwhile reached 937 million, or
83.2 percent of the overall internet population. The
boundaries between online and offline consumption have
blurred, and customers can now make purchases while they
are browsing  social media, watching content, and connecting
socially. A survey by China Youth Daily found 65.08 percent
of consumers examine user reviews on social media, and
84.75 percent of respondents indicated they 've been
"convinced to buy " by social media influencers. The above
indicates that social media has become not only a channel of
commercial communication but also the basic arena of
consumption activities.
It has attracted a lot of scholarly attentions. Previous
research has shown that social media has altered the
effectiveness and reach of traditional marketing by being able
to mobilise  the attention of vast numbers of users in a short
period of time and allow for concentrated consumer releases.
Some scholars have also observed that social media has
transformed from a communication medium to a complex
ecosystem of content, social interac tion and e -commerce,
leading to new models such as interest -based e-commerce and
content -based e-commerce [1]. However, the existing studies
focus more on the mechanism of social media marketing, and
there is less critical examination on the structural impact on
consumers' subjectivity, for example, the invisible limitation
of algorithmic selection range, the commercialisation of
users' content contributions, and the symbolic shift of
consumption motivations.
Thus the article asks the following questions: What is the
effect of social media marketing on customer behaviour ?
Other than verifying its positive role in activating intake, are
there any alarming unwanted effects? This essay will discuss
three key mechanisms in a layered discussion and reflection
on the ethical limits of digital marketing in relation to the
distin ct environment of Chinese social media platforms.
2. The Participatory Turn in
Information Dissemination and the
Reconfiguration of Consumer
Cognition
In the good old days of traditional media, marketers pushed
content to their audience one way and consumers were
passive recipients. This tendency was overwritten when social
media exploded. The production of information was no longer
the exclusive domain of institutions. Communication pattern
changed from one -way to network distribution. Consumers
become into commentators, selectors and re -transmitters of
information.
In the pre -social media period, consumers ' perception of
the new brands was mostly focused on corporate
advertisements and the information source was highly
concentrated, which placed firms in a good position in
information transmission. That changed with social media. In
Xiaohongshu, consumers ca n see real usage records of
products ; in Douyin, they can see the opening box reviews of
amateur bloggers ; in WeChat groups, they can get suggestions
from friends. These alternative communication outlets are
much more numerous than the official brand publicity.
Studies have demonstrated the direct impact of opinion
leaders and content provided by users on cust omer purchase
intentions, with the mediating role of trust and perceived

44
value [2]. Brand publicity is less compelling than regular
users. They are normal users in the same situation as
themselves and have no sales posture. They share what
they 've used, not hyped marketing points. This peer -
credibility based information filtering has permitted dispersed
control over brand image among a large number of ordinary
consumers.
Social media also allows the consumer to react to and
influence information on the brand. Sichuan Tianfu Bank
adopted the giant panda Fufu for life in 2017. In 2025,
Sichuan Tianfu Bank has innovatively opened exchanges on
Douyin and Xiaohongshu . Indeed, the public image of the
brand was created in the conversations in the comment area
and secondary productions [3]. The brand was no longer a
sender to a receiver. The public meaning of the brand was
created and changed through the continuous inter action
between the brand, the users and the platform.
Another result of participatory dissemination is to reduce
the threshold of information diffusion, where accurate and
fraudulent information are distributed together. Assessments
may contain secret business participation, for example,
implicit marketing, e xaggerated effect displays, and phoney
consumer experiences. According to the Procuratorial  Daily,
food vloggers have been posing as ordinary consumers to take
commissions from merchants and distribute content, blurring
the boundaries between sharing of experience and
commercial promotion, violating consumers ' right to know.
But more sources of information does not mean consumers
have gotten more discriminating at the same time - the more
information, the harder it is to sift truth from deception.
3. The Cognitive Training of Algorithm
Recommendation and the
Constriction of Consumer Choice
Popular social media platforms predominantly use
algorithms to distribute content. Platforms build interest
profiles of users by tracking their surfing histories, stay time,
likes and collections, skipping behaviours, etc. and select
information from a big  amount of content to push. Algorithms
are also modifying the information that consumers view and
helping them decide what is worth buying. On the surface,
this makes matching more efficient. The user does not
typically see that procedure firsthand.
The first manifestation of algorithms influencing
consuming behaviour is the imperceptible limitation of the
border of information perception. The recommendation
algorithm considers the commercial goals of the platform of
higher retention time and transact ion conversion. News
stream will constantly present content linked to beauty in the
news feed of people who have shown interest in beauty
products, and other product categories will eventually fade
away. Users' perception of information is gradually restricted
and into "information cocoon " [4]. The algorithm is trained to
choose from the pre -defined range, it seems to be free
selection, but actually inside the range provided by the system.
A 2025 Dentsu Creative research found 87% of global
marketing professionals thought algorithms were pl aying a
larger role in consumers ' decision -making, compared with 97%
of Chinese respondents.
Interest-based e -commerce just exacerbates the problem.
On platforms like Douyin, it has transitioned from "people
find goods " to "goods find people. Products are developed to
actively push to the users ' perspective through short films or
live broadcasts [5]. The price of searching has been decreased
to where there are no longer the procedures of "active
comparison" and "prudent weighing". When people are
presented with algorithm -suggested products they often
complete transactions swiftly, in an emotional state. User
experiences smoothness and happiness. But it is this very
smoothness that conceals the necessity for serious scrutiny.
Even more dubiously, algorithms are not merely directing
purchasing but changing consumers ' perception of their wants.
Pushing some products all the time, the persistent exposure of
information can stimulate consuming intention that does not
exist previously. "What do I need? " is no longer the question,
it's "What was pushed to me? " If the need itself is predicted
by an algorithm, how much autonomy remains in
"independent choice "? The more users use the site, the more
accurate the forecast of the algorithm is. The smaller the range
of alternatives available to consumers. The scenario is
different on various platforms, the difference of the user
structure and the content form, the logic of the algorithm
manifests in different forms in the real operation.
4. Platform Ecological Differentiation
and Consumer Segmentation
As for the platform difference, the user composition and
content forms of Douyin, Xiaohongshu, Bilibili  and WeChat
Video Channel are different. But when this strategy is utilised
across platforms, the client reactions are radically different.
This differentiation is also an element of the remaking of
consumer behaviour: consumers on different platforms are
being remade into different sorts of customers.
Each platform 's differentiation is already rather apparent.
Douyin has more over 750 million daily active users, with
main content including short films and live streaming, from
first-tier cities to lower -tier towns. Xiaohongshu  has roughly
320 million monthly active users, and the primary content is
graphic notes and short movies. It has a user base that is over
70% female and has created a distinct ecosystem in sectors
like cosmetics, fashion and home décor. The feature is
medium and lengthy films, and the ratio of users under 24
years old is the highest. Bilibili  has more over 340 million
monthly active users. The WeChat Video Channel is based on
social relationship chains and covers 30+ user groups and
lower-tier locations. Businesses that advertize on several
channels simultaneously are targeting client groups w ith
varied preferences [6].
Content structure affects customer decision making.
Douyin information is scattered and complex, and a short film
may have stirred up emotions before the notion is understood.
The language of the live -streaming greatly reduces the time
taken to make decisi ons. According to studies, Douyin is a
high -incidence scene of impulse consumption. Xiaohongshu
mostly relies on graphical evaluation and has more time for
comparison during reading, thus it is somewhat careful.
Bilibili  guides horizontal comparison content to more
systematic information processing. The type of material on
the platform strongly impacts the course of customer decision
making.
Brand-customer connections also differ among platforms.
On Douyin, brand connection is largely event -driven, and
when the information flow renews, the relationship cools
down. WeChat stays in touch via official accounts and mini -
programs. Xiaohongshu  is in the center where brands
constantly promote word -of-mouth content, and consumer

attitudes are created more slowly but also more securely.
Differentiation of platforms leads to segmentation of
customers, and allows corporations to target particular
demographics. The product information environment that
users are exposed on different platforms is very different,
which weakens the consumers' ab ility to compare and select,
and makes them more easily influenced by the information
ecosystem of a single platform instead of making independent
judgements based on diverse information [7].
5. Decentralization of Social Trust and
Dependence on Consumption
Decisions
Social media has altered the way consumers trust a brand.
In the conventional world, trust was built on the company's
reputation and advice from friends. Social media has
generated a new trust mechanism. The trust object has shifted
from brands to nodes in  the social network. Consumers are
more likely to trust "people who are similar to themselves "
[8].
The substitution effect of user generated material for the
brand official information is the tangible realisation of such a
transformation in the consumer decision. Normal users '
authentic sharing is often more compelling than the brand
campaign. People will check reviews and comments before
buying. The primary reason is that buyers believe that normal
consumers are in the same boat as they are, with no desire to
beautify the prod ucts. What they share is closer to "truth"
itself. The results show that opinion leaders and types of user
created content directly effect on users ' purchase intention
while trust and perceived value have a moderating role.
Evaluation material has become an important information
source before purchase decision in real customer behaviour.
And opinion leaders have a special role to play in this
trusted network. They are different from brand endorsers,
they have their own independent personal IP and fan
following, they are different from pure regular users, there is
a business tie between the m and the brand. This intermediary
identity, "semi-commercial, semi -personal ", positions
opinion leaders as the bridge between brand exposure and
consumer trust [9]. Research in the industry reveals that the
market size of KOL marketing in China will reach tens of
billions of yuan by 2025. The commercial success of this
intermediat e model of trust is evidenced by the continuous
investment of brands in the opinion leader channel.
But this decentralisation  of the trust mechanism also
produces structural fragility. As consumers increasingly use
social media information to make purchase decisions, the
weakest link in the trust chain is perceived authenticity of this
information. Some review accounts get commi ssions from
merchants, and publish content disguised as "real sharing",
intentionally not identifying it as a commercial advertisement,
blurring the line between experience sharing and commercial
advertising, and infringing on consumers' right to know,
Procuratorial Daily reported. "Social trust " no longer is a
means of empowering customers, but rather a mechanism for
capturing consumers more efficiently when trust is founded
on insufficient or misleading information.
6. Alienation Effects and Reflections on
the Boundaries of Marketing Ethics
Social media is reshaping customers through platform
distinction. More importantly, this structural arrangement
deconstructs consumers' subjectivity from inside, which may
be examined in three dimensions, including consumer
autonomy, user labour and consumer motivation.
Algorithms manage customer autonomous decision -
making, steer preferences, and filter information to constrain
consumer choices within the limits of commercial logic. That
free choice is to a good extent a result of the tightening
feedback loop between the algorithm forecast and the user's
conduct. Continuous exposure to homogeneous content can
trigger wants that were not there before and the ability t o
evaluate about one's own true needs is compromised. The
more algorithmic the system becomes, the more behavioural
data it learns and the more accurate the forecasts become, yet
the options space for customers is constantly narrowing.
Convenience comes at the price of some liberty [10].
As for the valuation of user content contribution, the
platform content ecosystem is founded on everyday material
provided by users. Reviews, unboxing videos and interaction
with comments increase the platform's content reserves and
provide raw materials f or algorithm optimisation . Users are
not paid content creators but feel that they are engaged in
ordinary social contact while in fact they are involved in the
labour process of value creation for the platform and
businesses.
In terms of motivating customers, social media marketing
is the industrialised  of attention production. Brands and
platforms keep churning out more sensually arousing content
and more appealing symbols of consumption to seduce people
all the time. Consumption is moving away from the
calculation of utility value and toward the desire  of symbolic
significance. People don't buy the functional aspects of the
products, but the lifestyle identity and the sensation of
belonging to a social circle that comes with brand names.
Consumption that meets needs is replaced with consumption
that exp resses identity. Every purchase is a statement of
identity for consumers. But once they are fulfilled, they
require new symbols to fill the void.
The three alienation effects are embedded in the operating
logic of the platform economy [11]. Thinking about these
things is not anti -technology, anti -commerce, but a question:
how can we, in our admiration of the market force created by
social media marketing, create institutional and cultural space
for consumer autonomy? This is not a simple question to
answer, but it should be a basic issue for companies, platforms
and regulatory regulations to work together on.
7. Conclusion
This research investigates the mechanism of social media
marketing on consumer behaviour in four aspects: the change
of communication paradigm, the algorithmic mechanism, the
reconfiguration of social trust and the differentiation of
platform ecology. The research shows it's not a one-way affect.
It is not empowering, it is not disciplining. The complexity
can be viewed at the level of mechanism and at the level of
expense.
And these three mechanisms are interlinked.
Communication is shifting from one -way distribution to
participatory co -creation ; customers become co -constructors
of brand meaning, and UGC gradually replaces brand
marketing as the main source in consumption decision -
making. Algorithmic recommendations boost the efficiency
of matching, but restrict the width of choice horizons for
customers and transform their sense of needs. The

46
decentralisation  of social trust has increased the dependence
of consumers on peer judgements and the recommendations
of opinion leaders ; however the cost of decision making has
been reduced, and new information asymmetry has also arisen.
Together, the three mechanisms supply the structural
circumstances of consumer behaviour. The route of action is
examined at the level of the mechanisms. But it has its costs.
Consumers are challenged simultaneously by three types of
subjectivity loss. Algorithmic aid erodes consumer autonomy
and there is a disconnect between the perception of "free
choice " and the real choice set. Users' efforts and interactions
are not paid adequately, and digital labour is veiled by phrases
like "sharing " and "participation ". Marketing symbols induce
a shift in customer behaviour  from utility value to the pursuit
of symbolic meaning. Consumers are caught in a never -
ending identification search cycle. These three losses are
baked into the working logic of the mainstream social media
business model.
Brands must look after the trust of the consumer. Platforms
have to draw the line between commercial efficiency and user
rights. Regulators should establish norms for hidden
advertising and information asymmetry. Another major test is
the digital proficien cy of consumers. The present study did
not extensively compare the differences across different
platforms and groups. Further in -depth study can be carried
out. Marketing cannot be based on the permanent loss of
subjectivity of the consumer. A key issue fo r the expansion of
the sector is how to achieve a dynamic balance between
efficiency and rights, technology and humanity.
