ABSTRACT
The digital transformation of agricultural marketing has emerged as a critical pathway for rural revitalization and high-
quality agricultural development in China. However, existing research often examines digital marketing strategies from
a single disciplinary perspective, neglecting the intricate interplay among information credibility, legal compliance, and
brand value. This paper proposes a three -dimensional analytical framework —Information Credibility, Legal
Compliance, and Brand Value (ILCB)—that integrates insights from information science, regulatory law, and marketing
management to examine digital marketing practices for agricultural products. Drawing on signaling theory, institutional
theory, and brand equity theory, this study employs a qualitativ e case study approach to investigate Yunnan small -lot
coffee, one of China’s most geographically indicated agricultural products with rapidly growing digital market presence.
The analysis reveals that: (1) information transparency mechanisms, including tra ceability systems and content
marketing, significantly enhance consumer trust and purchase intention; (2) legal compliance frameworks, particularly
food safety regulations and geographical indication protection, serve as foundational institutional safeguar ds that
reduce information asymmetry; and (3) regional brand value is co -constructed through the synergistic interaction of
credible information delivery and regulatory adherence. The study contributes to the literature by offering an
integrated cross -disciplinary framework that explains how digital marketing outcomes for agricultural products are
shaped by the convergence of technological, legal, and market forces. Practical implications for policymakers, platform
operators, and agricultural enterprises ar e discussed, emphasizing the need for coordinated governance that aligns
information disclosure standards, regulatory enforcement, and brand development strategies.

The global agricultural sector is undergoing a profound digital transformation, with e -commerce platforms, social
media, and live streaming reshaping how farmers and agribusinesses connect with consumers. In China, agricultural e -

revitalization, widespread mobile internet adoption, and the proliferation of digital marketing channels (Wang, 2023).
Among the myriad agricul tural products marketed through digital channels, specialty coffee from Yunnan Province
represents a particularly compelling case study. Yunnan accounts for over 98% of China’s coffee production, with small-

Despite the opportunities presented by digital marketing, agricultural products face unique challenges in the online
environment. Unlike standardized manufactured goods, agricultural products are characterized by experience and
credence qualities —consumers often cannot assess quality before purchase, and certain attributes such as organic
cultivation methods or geographical authenticity remain difficult to verify even after consumption (Wu & Yuan, 2022).
This information asymmetry creates a “lemon market” problem, where low-quality products can drive out high-quality
ones, undermining consumer trust and discouraging investment in quality improvement (Akerlof, as cited in Wei et al.,
2024).
Existing research on agricultural digital marketing tends to approach the topic from disciplinary silos. Information
systems scholars focus on technology adoption and platform design (Cang & Wang, 2020); legal researchers examine
regulatory frameworks and food safety compliance (Zhou et al., 2011); marketing scholars investigate brand building
and consumer behavior (Lin & Wang, 2023). While each perspective offers valuable insights, the isolated treatment of
information, legal, and brand dimensions fails to capture their interdependent relationships in practice. For instance, a
well-designed traceability system may enhance information credibility but lose effectiveness without legal enforcement
mechanisms; conversely, robust regulations may provide necessary  safeguards but remain inert without effective
communication channels to convey compliance information to consumers.
This paper addresses this gap by developing and applying an integrated three -dimensional framework—Information
Credibility, Legal Compliance, and Brand Value (ILCB)—that bridges information science, regulatory law, and marketing
management. Through an in -depth case study of Yunnan small -lot coffee, this research examines how these three
dimensions interact and collectively shape digital marketing outcomes. The study seeks to answer three interrelated
research questions:
• How do information credibility mechanisms function in the digital marketing of agricultural products, and what
are their effects on consumer behavior?
• In what ways do legal compliance requirements—including food safety regulations and geographical indication
protection—influence digital marketing strategies and outcomes?
• How do information credibility and legal compliance jointly contribute to the construction and enhancement
of brand value for regional agricultural products in digital markets?
By addressing these questions, this study makes both theoretical and practical contributions. Theoretically, the ILCB
framework advances cross -disciplinary understanding of agricultural digital marketing by articulating the causal
mechanisms through which technological, legal, and market forces converge. Practically, the findings offer actionable
insights for policymakers, platform operators, and agricultural enterprises seeking to optimize digital marketing
strategies while maintaining regulatory compliance and building sustainable brand equity.
2 LITERATURE REVIEW
2.1 Information Credibility and Consumer Trust in Agricultural E -Commerce
Information asymmetry has long been recognized as a fundamental challenge in agricultural markets. Because
agricultural products possess significant credence attributes —such as production methods, origin authenticity, and
safety standards —consumers face su bstantial uncertainty when making online purchase decisions (Fu et al., 2022).
Signaling theory provides a foundational lens for understanding how market participants can communicate
unobservable quality attributes to reduce information asymmetry. In agric ultural contexts, effective signals must be
costly to fake and difficult for low-quality producers to imitate (Wei et al., 2024).
Research has identified multiple mechanisms through which information credibility can be enhanced in digital
agricultural markets. Fu et al.  (2023) demonstrate that product environmental information transparency —including
soil and water quality information —significantly influences online consumers’ purchasing behavior for green
agricultural products, with competence trust and benevolence trust playing mediating roles. Similarly, Fu et al.  (2022)
find that production technology information transparency and p roduction means information transparency have
differential effects on consumer trust dimensions, with trust in competence positively impacting trust in benevolence,
both of which influence online green purchase behavior.

Institution-based trust represents another critical dimension, particularly in online environments where direct

2.2 Legal Compliance and Regulatory Frameworks for Agricultural E -Commerce
The legal and regulatory environment constitutes the institutional foundation upon which digital agricultural markets
operate. Food safety regulations represent the most fundamental compliance requirement, as agricultural products are
directly consumed and  pose potential health risks if improperly handled. Zhou et al.  (2011), in their study of the
vegetable processing industry in Zhejiang, identify firm size, expected premium, export market orientation, brand name,
e-commerce adoption, training frequency, a nd traceability implementation as key determinants of food safety and
quality standard adoption behavior. Their findings underscore the reciprocal relationship between regulatory
compliance and market orientation —firms that engage in e -commerce and export markets are more likely to adopt
stringent quality standards.
China’s 2015 Food Safety Law (FSL) represents a landmark regulatory development requiring most food sellers on e -
commerce platforms to obtain valid online licenses for retail food handling. Jin et al. (2021), using proprietary data from
Alibaba, examine the progressive implementation of the FSL and find that buyers are more willing to transact with
sellers who display their FSL licenses, particularly for younger and less reputable sellers. Their synthet ic control
analysis reveals that the average quality of  surviving food sellers improved after partial and mandatory licensing, as
unwilling sellers exited the platform. Importantly, the FSL did not negatively impact platform gross merchandise value
or significantly increase average sales prices, suggesting that regulatory enforcement can enhance market transparency
without hampering long-term performance.
Quality and safety management systems provide a more comprehensive framework for compliance. Ma et al.  (2018)
develop an index system for agricultural products sold via e -commerce comprising six first -level and thirteen second -
level indicators, applying Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Point (HACCP) principles to identify key control points
throughout t he supply chain. Their work emphasizes that regulatory oversight must span production sources,
processing and manufacturing, warehousing and transportati on, and regulatory measures (Li, 2023), reflecting the
multi-stage nature of agricultural product quality assurance.
Geographical indication (GI) protection represents another critical legal dimension, particularly for regional specialty
agricultural products. Mao and Görg (2023) find that Chinese agricultural GIs have heterogeneous effects on firms’
export quality: GIs without quality supervision actually reduce export quality due to free -riding incentives, while GIs
with effective quality supervision prevent such degradation. Their findings highlight that legal designation alone is
insufficient—enforcement mechanisms ar e essential to ensure that GI protection delivers on its quality -signaling
promise. Qian and Dong (2023), comparing Chinese and EU GI protection systems, identify gaps in China’s operational
systems and operational status, suggesting that the China -EU Geographical Indications Agreement may play a crucial
role in strengthening quality supervision.
2.3 Brand Value and Regional Brand Equity for Agricultural Products
Brand building has emerged as a strategic priority for agricultural products seeking to differentiate themselves in
increasingly competitive digital markets. Regional public brands, which leverage geographical indications and collective
reputation, have be come particularly prominent in China’s agricultural development strategy. Ren et al.  (2023)
characterize agricultural geographical indications as carriers of unique product value accumulated through history,
arguing that building regional brands is a power ful mechanism for stabilizing farmer income increases and achieving
sustainable agricultural development. They identify branding dilemmas including the loss of GI resources, weak
supervision, and insufficient investment, proposing a three -stage branding pa th encompassing germplasm discovery,
quality control, and brand promotion.
The economic value of regional brands manifests through multiple channels. Lin and Wang (2023), analyzing user
reviews from JD.com and China’s Specialty Products Pavilion, find that regional brand agricultural products enjoy

rather than account-level reputation in agricultural product markets.
2.4 Research Gaps and the Need for Integration
The literature review reveals three notable gaps. First, most studies examine information credibility, legal compliance,
and brand value in isolation, with limited attention to their interactive effects. Second, research on agricultural digital
marketing has predominantly focused on general or generic products, with relatively few studies investigating specialty
products like Yunnan coffee that possess distinct geographical and cultural characteristics. Third, the cross-disciplinary
nature of agricultural digital marketing—spanning information science, law, and marketing —has not been adequately
theorized. The present study addresses these gaps by developing an integrated three -dimensional framework and
applying it to the specific context of Yunnan small-lot coffee.
3 THEORETICAL FRAMEWORK
3.1 The ILCB Framework: Information Credibility, Legal Compliance, and Brand Value
This study proposes the Information Credibility –Legal Compliance –Brand Value (ILCB) framework as an integrative
analytical lens for understanding agricultural digital marketing. The framework posits that effective digital marketing
for agricultural product s results from the dynamic interaction of three interrelated dimensions: (1) information
credibility mechanisms that reduce information asymmetry and build consumer trust; (2) legal compliance structures
that establish institutional legitimacy and enforce quality standards; and (3) brand value accumulation that translates
trust and compliance into competitive market advantage.
3.2 Information Credibility Dimension
Drawing on signaling theory (Spence, as cited in Wei et al., 2024) and cue utilization theory (as cited in Xu et al., 2022),
the information credibility dimension examines how various signals and cues transmitted through digital channels
influence consumer  perceptions of trustworthiness. In agricultural digital marketing, credibility signals operate at
multiple levels:
• Product-level signals: These include product descriptions, quality certifications, traceability information, and
environmental disclosures. Fu et al.  (2023) distinguish between soil information transparency and water
information transparency, demonstrating that different types of environmental information have differential
effects on consumer purchasing behavior.
• Seller-level signals: Seller reputation, user reviews, credit ratings, and credit codes represent signals about
the seller’s trustworthiness. Wei et al.  (2024) find that credit codes on packaging boost perceived brand
authenticity, with stronger effects on prevention-focused consumers.
• Platform-level signals:  Platform supervision mechanisms, user feedback systems, and certification
mechanisms provide institutional guarantees that enhance trust in online transactions (Wu & Yuan, 2022).

The effectiveness of these signals depends on their credibility —signals must be perceived as truthful, accurate, and
difficult to manipulate. In the digital environment, this credibility is shaped by both the content of the information and
the trustworthiness of its source.
3.3 Legal Compliance Dimension
Grounded in institutional theory (North, as cited in Wu & Yuan, 2022), the legal compliance dimension encompasses the
formal regulations, standards, and enforcement mechanisms that structure agricultural digital markets. Key
components include:
• Food safety regulations:  The Food Safety Law and associated standards establish mandatory requirements
for food production, processing, and online sales. Jin et al.  (2021) demonstrate that mandatory licensing
improves average seller quality by removing non-compliant sellers from the market.
• Geographical indication protection:  GI laws and regulations protect regional brand names and establish
quality standards for designated products. Mao and Görg (2023) emphasize that GI effectiveness depends
critically on the presence and enforcement of quality supervision mechanisms.
• E-commerce platform regulations:  Platform-specific rules and regulatory requirements govern seller
behavior, product listings, and consumer protection. Ma et al.  (2018) identify warehousing and transportation
regulatory measures as one of four major factors influencing agricultural product quality and safety on B2C
platforms.
Legal compliance serves both protective and signaling functions. Protectively, it establishes minimum quality standards
and penalizes non -compliance, reducing the likelihood of market failures. As a signal, visible compliance with
regulations—such as displayed licenses or certification marks —conveys quality assurance to consumers, particularly
when direct product verification is impossible.
3.4 Brand Value Dimension
The brand value dimension, informed by brand equity theory (Aaker, as cited in Tang et al., 2024), examines how
information credibility and legal compliance contribute to the intangible value of agricultural brands. Regional
agricultural brands derive their value from multiple sources:
• Brand awareness and recognition:  Digital marketing channels significantly enhance brand visibility and
consumer recognition (Wang, 2023).
• Perceived quality: Credible information and regulatory certification collectively shape consumer perceptions
of product quality (Hu et al., 2023).
• Brand trust and loyalty:  Consistent delivery of credible information and adherence to legal standards build
long-term trust and repeat purchase behavior (Xu et al., 2022).
• Brand authenticity: For geographical indication products, authenticity is a core value driver, closely linked to
origin verification and traditional production methods (Wei et al., 2024).
3.5 Interrelationships Among the Three Dimensions
The ILCB framework emphasizes that the three dimensions are not independent but dynamically interrelated:
• Legal compliance → Information credibility:  Regulatory requirements mandate certain information
disclosures and provide independent verification of product claims, thereby enhancing the credibility of
information transmitted through marketing channels. For example, a displayed food safety license ( legal
compliance) serves as a credible signal of quality (information credibility).
• Information credibility → Brand value:  When consumers perceive marketing information as credible, they
develop trust and positive brand perceptions, contributing to brand equity. Effective communication of quality
attributes is essential for building brand differentiation.
• Legal compliance → Brand value: Compliance with regulations and GI protection establishes the institutional
foundation for brand value by ensuring consistent quality and preventing free -riding. Without legal
enforcement, regional brands may suffer from reputation dilution due to low -quality entrants.
• Synergistic effects: The three dimensions interact synergistically—legal compliance provides the foundation,
information credibility provides the communication mechanism, and brand value represents the market
outcome. Weaknesses in any dimension undermine the effectiveness of  the others.

4 METHODOLOGY
4.1 Research Design
This study employs an exploratory qualitative case study design to investigate the three -dimensional framework in
practice. The case study method is particularly appropriate for this research because it allows for an in -depth
examination of complex, contex t-dependent phenomena within real -life settings (Yin, as cited in Lin & Wang, 2023).
Specifically, a single-case design with embedded units of analysis is adopted, focusing on Yunnan small-lot coffee as the
primary case while examining multiple dimensions (information, legal, brand) and multiple actors (farmers,
enterprises, platforms, regulators).
4.2 Case Selection Rationale
Yunnan small-lot coffee was selected as the case study for several reasons. First, it represents a high -value agricultural
product with significant geographical indication characteristics, making it an ideal context for examining brand value
and GI protect ion issues. Second, Yunnan coffee has experienced rapid digital marketing adoption, with increasing
presence on e-commerce platforms, live streaming channels, and social media. Third, the Yunnan coffee industry faces
genuine challenges related to informati on asymmetry (consumers cannot easily verify coffee origin and quality) and
regulatory compliance (food safety, GI protection). Finally, as a product from western China, Yunnan coffee embodies
the broader rural revitalization and agricultural modernization agenda that motivates much of the policy and academic
interest in agricultural digital marketing.
4.3 Data Sources
This study draws on multiple data sources to ensure triangulation and comprehensive understanding:
• Academic literature: Peer-reviewed journal articles, conference papers, and research reports on agricultural
digital marketing, food safety regulation, geographical indications, and Yunnan coffee industry development.
• Policy documents and regulations:  Chinese national laws and regulations including the Food Safety Law,
Geographical Indication protection regulations, and e -commerce laws, as well as local Yunnan provincial
policies supporting the coffee industry.
• Industry reports and statistics: Reports from industry associations, market research firms, and e -commerce
platforms providing data on Yunnan coffee production, consumption, and digital marketing trends.
• Digital marketing content analysis:  Analysis of representative Yunnan coffee brand websites, social media
accounts, live streaming sessions, and e -commerce storefronts to examine how information credibility, legal
compliance, and brand value are communicated in practice.
4.4 Data Analysis
Data analysis follows a thematic analysis approach aligned with the ILCB framework. The analysis proceeds through
several iterative steps: (1) organizing and familiarizing with the data; (2) initial coding aligned with the three
framework dimensions; (3) d eveloping thematic categories within each dimension; (4) examining cross -dimensional
relationships and interactions; and (5) synthesizing findings to address the research questions. This deductive -
inductive approach allows for both framework-driven analysis and emergent insights from the data.
5 CASE ANALYSIS: YUNNAN SMALL-LOT COFFEE
5.1 Context: Yunnan Coffee Industry Overview
Yunnan Province, located in southwest China, is the country’s primary coffee -producing region, accounting for over
98% of national coffee production (Liu et al., 2019). The main coffee-growing areas include Pu’er, Baoshan, Dehong, and
Xishuangbanna, situated in the tropical and subtropical highlands ideal for arabica cultivation. Yunnan small -lot coffee
(Coffea arabica) is characterized by its mellow but not bitter taste, fragrant but not overwhelming aroma, and slight
fruity acidity—qualities that have gained increasing recognition among specialty coffee consumers.
The Yunnan coffee industry has undergone significant transformation over the past two decades. Historically focused
on commodity green bean exports, the industry has increasingly shifted toward domestic market development and
value-added processing (Xu, 20 13). This transition has been driven by multiple factors: growing domestic coffee
consumption in China, fluctuating international coffee prices, government support for rural specialty industries, and the
rise of digital marketing channels that enable direc t-to-consumer sales. The “Yunnan small -lot coffee” (Yunnan xiaolü
coffee) geographical indication has become an important regional brand, with multiple local GI designations including
Pu’er coffee, Baoshan small-lot coffee, and others.

5.2 Information Credibility Dimension in Yunnan Coffee Digital Marketing
5.2.1 Information Transparency and Traceability
In the Yunnan coffee digital marketing context, information credibility is primarily challenged by the difficulty
consumers face in verifying coffee origin, processing methods, and quality attributes. Unlike single -origin specialty
coffees from established  origins like Colombia or Ethiopia, Yunnan coffee has historically been perceived as a
commodity product, with consumers often uncertain about the specific farm, processing method, or quality grade of the
beans they purchase.
To address this information asymmetry, Yunnan coffee producers and marketers have implemented various
transparency mechanisms. Traceability systems represent the most prominent approach. Many Yunnan coffee brands
now provide detailed information about the specific fa rm or cooperative where the coffee was grown, the processing
method (washed, natural, honey), the harvest year, and cupping scores. This information is communicated through
product packaging labels, e-commerce product pages, and digital storytelling on social media platforms.
The effectiveness of these traceability mechanisms aligns with findings by Wu and Yuan (2022), who identify product
traceability mechanisms as one of four key institutional mechanisms that enhance consumer trust. For Yunnan coffee,
traceability information  serves as a quality signal that helps consumers distinguish between commodity -grade and
specialty-grade beans, enabling price differentiation. Fu et al.  (2022) similarly find that production technology
information transparency and production means informa tion transparency have positive effects on consumer trust
dimensions, which is directly applicable to coffee processing method disclosures.
5.2.2 Content Marketing and Digital Storytelling
Content marketing has emerged as a particularly important channel for building information credibility in Yunnan
coffee digital marketing. Niu et al.  (2020), in their study of Yunnan plateau specialty agricultural products, identify “e -
commerce + content marketing” as a dominant model that connects brand concepts with consumer communication. For
Yunnan coffee, content marketing takes multiple forms:
• Farmer and origin storytelling: Many brands produce content about the farmers, farming communities, and
mountainous landscapes where Yunnan coffee is grown. This narrative approach helps consumers connect
emotionally with the product and perceive the authenticity of its origin.
• Educational content:  Coffee brewing tutorials, tasting notes, and explanations of processing methods help
consumers understand quality differences, thereby making marketing claims more credible.
• Live streaming demonstrations:  Live streaming sessions that show coffee roasting, brewing, or farm visits
provide real-time visual evidence that enhances information credibility.
Fan and Zhang (2025), studying social platform content marketing for local specialty green agricultural products, find
that product trust evaluations and source credibility significantly contribute to sales growth. Their finding that account
trust evaluation does not significantly affect purchase behavior is particularly relevant for Yunnan coffee: consumers
appear to base decisions on product -specific evidence (tasting notes, origin information) rather than general account
reputation, suggesting that detailed product information is more critical than influencer popularity.
5.2.3 User Reviews and Social Proof
User reviews and ratings on e -commerce platforms represent another important credibility mechanism for Yunnan
coffee products. Consistent with Wu and Yuan’s (2022) findings on user feedback mechanisms, review systems allow
consumers to share their consumpt ion experiences, providing social proof that supplements seller -provided
information. For relatively new products like Yunnan specialty coffee, user reviews play a particularly important role
in reducing perceived risk, as consumers have less pre-existing knowledge about the product category.
However, the credibility of user reviews is itself a concern, as fake reviews and review manipulation are known issues
in Chinese e-commerce. This connects to the legal compliance dimension, as platform regulatory oversight (discussed
below) is necessary to maintain the integrity of review systems.
5.3 Legal Compliance Dimension in Yunnan Coffee Digital Marketing
5.3.1 Food Safety Regulations
Food safety compliance constitutes the foundational legal requirement for Yunnan coffee sold through digital channels.
As a food product, Yunnan coffee must comply with China’s Food Safety Law, which was significantly revised in 2015.
Jin et al. (2021) demonstrate that the 2015 Food Safety Law’s licensing requirements have had measurable effects on e-

commerce food sellers: sellers displaying their FSL licenses are more attractive to buyers, and the average quality of
surviving food sellers has improved as non-compliant sellers exit the market.
For Yunnan coffee enterprises operating online, food safety compliance involves multiple requirements: obtaining food

throughout the supply chain,  and maintaining proper records for traceability. Smallholder coffee farmers and small
processing enterprises often face particular challenges in meeting these regulatory requirements, as compliance costs
can be relatively high for small operations.
The implementation of these regulations in the Yunnan coffee context illustrates the interaction between legal
compliance and information credibility. When coffee producers display their food safety licenses on e -commerce
storefronts, this visible compliance serves as a credible signal of quality (Jin et al., 2021). Consumers who may not have
the expertise to evaluate coffee quality directly can rely on regulatory certification as a heuristic cue.
5.3.2 Geographical Indication Protection
Geographical indication protection is of particular importance for Yunnan coffee, as the product’s identity is closely tied
to its regional origin. Multiple GI designations exist for Yunnan coffee at different administrative levels, including
“Yunnan small-lot coffee” as a general regional designation and more specific GIs for Pu’er coffee, Baoshan coffee, and
other producing areas.
Mao and Görg (2023) provide important insights into the dynamics of GI protection, finding that GIs without effective
quality supervision can actually reduce export quality due to free-riding incentives. This risk is salient for Yunnan coffee:
without robust quality enforcement, producers outside the designated region or producers of lower-quality coffee could
potentially misuse the “Yunnan coffee” designation, diluting its reputation. Qian and Dong (2023) note that China’s GI
protection system has historic ally suffered from overlapping management departments and imperfect quality
supervision systems—issues directly relevant to Yunnan coffee.
The China-EU Geographical Indications Agreement, which entered into force in 2021, has important implications for
Yunnan coffee. As a product that may be listed under the agreement, Yunnan coffee could benefit from enhanced GI
protection both domestically and in the EU market. This aligns with Mao and Görg’s (2023) finding that international GI
agreements can serve as quality supervision mechanisms that prevent free -riding.
5.3.3 E-Commerce Platform Compliance Requirements
Beyond national laws, e -commerce platforms impose their own compliance requirements on sellers. Ma et al.  (2018)
identify regulatory measures as one of four major factors influencing agricultural product quality and safety on B2C
platforms, encompassing platform supervision, seller qualification review, and post-sale dispute resolution. For Yunnan
coffee selle rs on major platforms like Taobao, JD.com, and Pinduoduo, compliance with platform rules includes:
providing business licenses and food circulation pe rmits, accurately describing product attributes, adhering to
advertising standards, and maintaining customer service standards.
The interaction between platform-level and national-level regulation creates a multi-layered compliance environment.
Platforms, motivated by their own reputation and legal liability, often enforce standards that exceed minimum legal
requirements. This priv ate regulation complements public regulation, contributing to the overall governance of
information quality and product safety in digital agricultural markets.
5.4 Brand Value Dimension in Yunnan Coffee Digital Marketing
5.4.1 Regional Brand Development
The brand value of Yunnan coffee has evolved significantly from its origins as a commodity export product to its current
position as a recognized regional specialty brand. Ren et al.  (2023) describe agricultural geographical indications as
carriers of historically accumulated product value, and Yunnan coffee exemplifies this transformation. The “Yunnan
small-lot coffee” brand has gradually built recognition among Chinese consumers, mov ing beyond the perception that
Chinese coffee is merely cheap commodity beans to one where specific origins and processing methods are appreciated.
Lin and Wang (2023) find that regional brand agricultural products enjoy higher reputation on e -commerce platforms
compared to non -brand products, with government service support playing a positive moderating role. For Yunnan
coffee, government support has  been substantial: local governments in major producing areas have invested in brand
promotion, industry infrastructure, and quality standardization. This government support aligns with Zhang et al.’s

5.4.2 Brand Differentiation and Premium Pricing
The development of Yunnan coffee’s brand value is evident in the emergence of premium pricing tiers. Hu et al.  (2023)
find that consumers are willing to pay approximately 17% more for branded eco -agricultural products, and Yunnan
specialty coffee shows similar patterns. While commodity -grade Yunnan coffee beans sell at relatively low prices
comparable to internatio nal commodity markets, specialty -grade Yunnan coffee with specific origin, processing
method, and cupping scores can command significantly higher  prices in direct-to-consumer digital channels.
This premium pricing is enabled by the interaction of information credibility and legal compliance. Detailed product
information (information credibility) allows consumers to understand quality differences, while GI protection and
quality certifications (legal compliance) provide assurance that the product claims are genuine. Together, these factors
justify price premiums and contribute to brand equity.
5.4.3 Digital Marketing and Brand Building
Digital marketing channels have been instrumental in building Yunnan coffee’s brand value. Wang (2023) argues that
agricultural enterprises can effectively enhance brand value by leveraging new media and digital technologies, and
Yunnan coffee provides a compelling example. The digital environment enables several brand -building mechanisms:
• Direct-to-consumer connection: Digital channels allow coffee producers to bypass traditional intermediaries
and build direct relationships with consumers, facilitating brand loyalty development.
• Storytelling and brand narrative: Social media and content marketing enable the construction of rich brand
narratives around origin, tradition, and quality—elements that are particularly important for regional specialty
products.
• Community building: Specialty coffee consumers form communities around shared appreciation for specific
origins and flavors, and digital platforms facilitate these communities, strengthening brand attachment.
Tang et al. (2024) identify innovation in branding as a key dimension of agricultural brand competitiveness, and Yunnan
coffee brands have demonstrated innovation in their use of live streaming, short video content, and interactive e -
commerce formats. Zheng et al.  (2023) find that anchor characteristics including influence, sales promotion, and
interactive entertainment positively impact consumers’ purchasing behavior for green agricultural products through
live streaming, with green consumption cognition p laying a mediating role —findings directly applicable to Yunnan
coffee live streaming marketing.
5.5 Cross-Dimensional Integration: The ILCB Dynamics in Yunnan Coffee
The Yunnan coffee case illustrates the dynamic interactions among the three ILCB dimensions. Several key cross -
dimensional relationships emerge:
• Legal compliance as foundation for information credibility. Food safety licensing and GI protection provide
institutional backing that makes marketing information more credible. When Yunnan coffee brands display
their food business licenses and GI certifications on e -commerce platforms, these legal compliance mark ers
function as high-credibility signals that complement product descriptions and user reviews. Without this legal
foundation, even well -crafted marketing content might be viewed with skepticism by  consumers concerned
about product authenticity.
• Information credibility as mechanism for brand value creation.  The detailed information about origin,
processing, and taste that Yunnan coffee brands provide through digital channels is essential for translating
legal compliance into brand value. Legal certifications alone do not create brand meaning —they need to be
communicated effectively to consumers. Content marketing, storytelling, and transparent product information
transform abstract compliance marks into meaningful brand attributes that consumers can appreciate and for
which they are willing to pay premiums.
• Brand value as incentive for legal compliance and information disclosure.  As Yunnan coffee brands
develop stronger brand equity, they have greater incentives to invest in legal compliance and transparent
information disclosure. A valuable brand has more to lose from compliance failures or quality scandals, creating
reputational incentives for maintaining high standards. This virtuous circle —brand value rewarding
compliance and credibility—helps explain why leading Yunnan coffee producers tend to be more tra nsparent
and more compliant than smaller, less established operators.
• Challenges and tensions.  The case also reveals tensions and challenges. Smallholder farmers and small
enterprises may struggle to invest in both information disclosure systems and legal compliance, creating a
divide between larger branded enterprises and smaller producers. The fr ee-riding problem identified by Mao
and Görg (2023) remains a concern: low -quality producers who benefit from the “Yunnan coffee” regional
brand without investing in quality improvement can undermine brand value over time. Addressin g these

6 DISCUSSION
6.1 Theoretical Contributions
This study makes several theoretical contributions to the understanding of agricultural digital marketing. First, the ILCB
framework provides an integrative cross -disciplinary lens that bridges information science, regulatory law, and
marketing management. By articulating the dynamic relationships among information credibility, legal compliance, and
brand value, the framework moves beyond single-discipline approaches to capture the complexity of agricultural digital
markets. The framework’s emphasis on inte ractive effects —how legal compliance enhances information credibility,
how information credibility builds brand value, and how brand value incentivizes compliance —offers a more nuanced
understanding than siloed analyses.
Second, the case of Yunnan coffee enriches our understanding of how geographical indication products navigate the
transition from commodity to specialty markets in digital environments. The findings support Mao and Görg’s (2023)
argument that GI protection effectiveness depends critically on quality supervision mechanisms, and extend this insight
by showing how digital information channels can both support and challenge GI systems. On one hand, digital
traceability and storytelling enhance the credibility of GI claims; on the other hand, the ease of online marketing creates
opportunities for misuse and free-riding.
Third, the study contributes to the literature on live streaming and social media marketing for agricultural products.
Consistent with Fan and Zhang (2025), the findings suggest that product -level credibility signals matter more than
account-level reputati on in agricultural product markets. This has implications for theories of influencer marketing,
which often emphasize the importance of source credibility at the persona level. In agricultural contexts, where product
attributes are central and consumers ar e making functional purchase decisions, product -specific evidence may
outweigh influencer popularity.
6.2 Practical Implications
The study offers several practical implications for different stakeholders. For policymakers, the findings underscore
the importance of coordinated governance across information, legal, and brand domains. Effective agricultural digital
marketing strategy cannot rely on regulation alone—it must also support the development of information infrastructure
(traceability systems, quality standards) and brand building initiatives. Strengthening GI quality supervision, as
recommended by Mao and Görg (2023) and Qian  and Dong (2023), is particularly important for preventing free -riding
and maintaining regional brand value.
For e-commerce platforms, the research highlights the dual role platforms play in governing information credibility
and facilitating brand value creation. Platforms that invest in robust seller verification, transparent review systems, and
quality certification displays will crea te more trustworthy market environments. The finding that displayed licenses
increase buyer willingness to transact (Jin et al., 2021) suggests that platforms should make compliance information
more prominent and accessible to consumers.
For agricultural enterprises and farmer cooperatives , the case demonstrates that investing in information
transparency and legal compliance is not merely a cost center but a brand -building strategy. Yunnan coffee producers
that effectively communicate their origin, processing methods, and quality certificat ions through digital channels can
build stronger brand equity and command price premiums. The content marketing approaches identified by Niu et
al. (2020) and Fan and Zhang (2025) offer actionable models for small producers seeking to differentiate themselves in
crowded digital marketplaces.
6.3 Limitations and Future Research
This study has several limitations that suggest directions for future research. First, the single -case study design, while
enabling in-depth analysis, limits generalizability. Future research could apply the ILCB framework to other agricultural
product categories and reg ional contexts to test its broader applicability. Second, the analysis is primarily qualitative,
based on document analysis and secondary data. Future research could employ quantitative methods to empirically test
the relationships among the three dimensio ns—for example, surveying consumers to measure how different types of
information credibility signals and compliance certifications jointly influence brand perceptions and purchase
intention.

Third, the study focuses on the Chinese context, where specific regulatory frameworks (such as the Food Safety Law
and China’s GI system) and platform ecosystem (Taobao, JD, Douyin) have unique characteristics. Comparative research
examining how the ILCB dynamics play out in different national contexts would enrich the framework. Finally, as digital
marketing technologies continue to evolve —particularly with the emergence of AI -generated content, blockchain
traceability, and virtual influencers —future resea rch should investigate how these new technologies reshape the
relationships among information credibility, legal compliance, and brand value.
7 CONCLUSION
This paper has developed and applied a three -dimensional ILCB framework —Information Credibility, Legal
Compliance, and Brand Value—to analyze the digital marketing of agricultural products, using Yunnan small -lot coffee
as a case study. The analysis demons trates that effective digital marketing for agricultural products emerges from the
dynamic interaction of three interrelated dimensions: credible information mechanisms that reduce consumer
uncertainty, legal compliance structures that establish institutio nal legitimacy, and brand value accumulation that
translates trust and compliance into competitive advantage.
The Yunnan coffee case illustrates how these dimensions operate in practice. Information credibility is enhanced
through traceability systems, content marketing, and user reviews; legal compliance is enforced through food safety
regulations, geographical i ndication protection, and platform rules; and brand value is built through regional brand
development, premium pricing, and digital storytelling. Crucially, these dimensions do not operate independently —
legal compliance provides the foundation that makes i nformation credible, effective information communication
transforms compliance into brand value, and growing brand equity creates incentives for continued compliance and
transparency.
As digital marketing continues to transform agricultural markets worldwide, the integrated perspective offered by the
ILCB framework becomes increasingly important. Policymakers, platform operators, and agricultural enterprises must
recognize that success in digital agricultural markets requires coordinated attention to information quality, regulatory
compliance, and brand development —addressing any one dimension in isolation will yield suboptimal results. For
Yunnan coffee and similar regional specialty pr oducts, the path to sustainable digital marketing success lies in
strengthening the virtuous circle among credible information, robust compliance, and growing brand value.
