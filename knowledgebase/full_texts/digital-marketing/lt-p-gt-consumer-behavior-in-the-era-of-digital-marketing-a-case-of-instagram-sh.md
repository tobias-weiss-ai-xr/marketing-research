Abstract
This study examined consumer behavior in the era of digital marketing by focusing on Instagram shoppers in Tanzania,
guided by the Theory of Planned Behavior and Theory of Perceived Risk. Specifically, the study sought to identify the
factors that drive consumers to engage with Instagram-based sellers, examine how trust is established between
consumers and sellers on Instagram, and analyzed the challenges that aﬀect consumers’ confidence in Instagram-based
shopping. A convergent mixed-methods design was employed, involving 384 respondents selected through snowball
sampling. Data were collected using a structured questionnaire comprising both closed- and open-ended questions and
were analyzed using descriptive statistics and thematic analysis. The findings revealed that Instagram is used not only for
social interaction but also as a commercial platform, with a substantial proportion of participants (79.4%) reporting
having purchased products through Instagram. Key factors driving consumer engagement and purchase behavior
included the attractiveness of product images, the availability of product reviews and testimonials, and quick responses
from sellers. Consumer trust and confidence in Instagram-based sellers were primarily enhanced through direct
communication, previous personal purchasing experiences, and collaborations with influencers. However, the major
challenge aﬀecting consumers’ confidence was the mismatch between product descriptions and the actual products
received, which discouraged repeat purchases among many consumers. This study contributes to the limited empirical
literature on social media-based consumer behavior in developing economies by providing context specific insights from
Tanzania. It extends understanding of how visual presentation, responsiveness, and trust-building mechanisms influence
consumer engagement on Instagram. Additionally, the findings oﬀer practical implications for Instagram-based sellers by
highlighting the importance of transparency, honest communication, product authenticity, and reliable delivery in
building consumer trust and sustaining long-term customer relationships in digital marketplaces.

Literature review
Theoretical Literature
Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB): This study is grounded in the TPB (Ajzen, 1991), which explains consumer behavior as a
function of behavioral intentions shaped by attitude, subjective norms, and perceived behavioral control. In the context
of Instagram shopping in Tanzania, consumers’ attitudes toward digital marketing content, perceived social influence
from peers and influencers, and their perceived ability to transact online collectively determine their intention to
purchase through Instagram. These intentions ultimately influence actual purchasing behavior. Thus, TPB provides a
suitable theoretical lens for understanding consumer behavior in the era of digital marketing.
Theory of Perceived Risk (TPR): This study is also grounded in TPR (Cox and Rich, 1964), which explained how uncertainty
and the possibility of negative outcomes influence consumer behavior. Cox and Rich proposed that consumers make
purchasing decisions under conditions of uncertainty when they are unable to fully predict the consequences of their
choices. This uncertainty generates perceived risk, which is defined as the consumer’s subjective expectation of loss or
adverse outcomes associated with a purchase decision. In the context of Instagram, where transactions are often
conducted without physical product inspection and with limited regulatory oversight, consumers tend to rely heavily on
available digital cues, including reviews, influencer endorsements, and seller credibility, to reduce uncertainty. The theory
posits that higher levels of perceived risk negatively aﬀect purchase intention, prompting consumers to engage in risk-
reduction strategies such as seeking additional information or avoiding transactions altogether. Therefore, this study
integrates Perceived Risk Theory to explain how Tanzanian Instagram shoppers navigate digital purchasing
environments, shaping their attitudes, trust levels, and ultimately their buying behavior in the evolving landscape of
digital marketing.

Research Method
Research design
This study aimed to examine how digital marketing influences consumer behavior among Instagram shoppers in
Tanzania. The research was conducted in the Iringa Region of Tanzania. The study adopted a convergent mixed-methods
design, which is a central approach within mixed-methods research. The convergent mixed-methods design involves the
simultaneous collection and analysis of both quantitative and qualitative data, followed by the integration of results to
compare and corroborate findings from the two data strands (Creswell and Creswell, 2023). This design was selected
because it enables researchers to obtain a comprehensive understanding of a research problem by combining the
strengths of both quantitative and qualitative approaches.
Context of the study
The study was conducted in Iringa Region, Tanzania, an area experiencing increasing internet penetration and growing
engagement in social media platforms such as Instagram, particularly in urban centers where digital infrastructure and
connectivity are relatively more developed (Tanzania Communications Regulatory Authority, 2023) (National Bureau of
Statistics, 2022).
Target population
The target population of the study comprised all Instagram users in Iringa Region who engage in online shopping
activities. These individuals represent consumers who interact with digital marketing content and make purchasing
decisions through social commerce platforms.
Sample size determination
Since the exact number of Instagram users who engage in online shopping in Iringa Region is unknown and therefore
considered infinite, the researcher determined the sample size using Cochran’s formula for an infinite population.
According to Cochran, this formula is widely used in survey research to estimate the appropriate sample size when the
population size is large or unknown (Cochran, 1977). The calculated sample size is therefore 384 respondents, which is
considered adequate for representing the large population of Instagram users engaged in online shopping in Iringa
Region. All respondents completed the survey; hence, the response rate was 100%. The questionnaire comprised both
closed-ended questions (for quantitative data) and open-ended questions (for qualitative data). Although all 384
respondents completed the questionnaire, only 22 respondents responded to the optional open-ended questions.
Consequently, the qualitative thematic analysis was based on these 22 responses.  Although the questionnaire initially
indicated that respondents could select more than one option, the analysis in Table 5 considered only the primary
response for each respondent. Consequently, each respondent contributed one response, and the percentages sum to
100%.
Sampling technique
The study employed a snowball sampling technique to recruit participants. The initial respondents were purposively
identified from active Instagram users who met the study's inclusion criteria, namely individuals residing in Tanzania,
aged 18 years and above, and who had previously used Instagram to search for, evaluate, or purchase products and
services. These initial participants were identified through the researchers' professional and social networks and through
Instagram communities related to online shopping. After completing the questionnaire, the initial respondents were
requested to share the survey link with other Instagram users who met the study criteria. This referral process continued
until the desired sample size was achieved. Snowball sampling is particularly useful when studying populations that are
diﬃcult to identify or reach directly (Etikan, 2016). Through this method, existing Instagram users who participated in
online shopping referred other users with similar experiences.

Data collection methods
Data were collected using a structured questionnaire consisting of both closed-ended and open-ended questions. The
questionnaire was designed to capture respondents’ demographic characteristics, purchasing behavior, and experiences
related to Instagram shopping. The questionnaire was developed and administered using Google Forms, and the survey
link was shared with Instagram users through various online channels to enhance accessibility and improve response
rates. According to Bougie and Sekaran, questionnaires are an eﬀective tool for collecting standardized data from a large
number of respondents in survey research (Bougie and Sekaran, 2019). All completed questionnaires were carefully
reviewed and verified for completeness and consistency before being entered into the statistical software for analysis.
Data analysis techniques
Quantitative data were analyzed using descriptive statistics through IBM SPSS Statistics version 20. The analysis helped
summarize respondents’ demographic characteristics and purchasing behavior patterns using frequencies and
percentages. Responses from open-ended questions were analyzed using thematic analysis. Thematic analysis is widely
used in qualitative research for identifying patterns within textual data (Braun and Clarke, 2006). The researcher first read
all responses several times to gain familiarity with the data. Key ideas and recurring statements related to consumers’
experiences, perceptions, and motivations for purchasing products through Instagram were identified. The responses
were then coded, where meaningful words, phrases, or sentences were labeled according to their relevance to the study
objectives. Similar codes were grouped together to form themes representing common patterns in consumer behavior
within the context of digital marketing on Instagram. These themes were interpreted and supported with selected
respondents’ statements to provide deeper insights that complemented the quantitative findings.
Ethical considerations
As the study involved human participants, ethical principles were strictly observed. Respondents were informed about
the purpose of the study, and informed consent was obtained prior to their participation. The confidentiality and
anonymity of respondents were maintained throughout the study. All information provided by participants was used
strictly for academic purposes, and respondents were assured that their identities would not be disclosed.

making purchasing a form of “gamble."  In this context, several types of risk act as barriers to purchase decisions.

expected quality; and psychological or ego risk, reflected in frustration and dissatisfaction after a poor purchase decision.
These risks directly aﬀect consumers’ confidence and willingness to proceed with a transaction. With the advancement of
digital technologies and the growth of online platforms, researchers argue that perceived risk has evolved and
intensified, particularly in Internet-based commerce. Beyond traditional economic, psychological, and physical risks,
consumers now experience additional concerns, including privacy risk, financial risk, and delivery risk. These risks are
especially pronounced in social media-based commerce environments such as Instagram, where transactions often occur
informally and outside structured e-commerce systems.

of fraud, misuse of personal or financial information, and non-delivery or delayed delivery of products heighten
perceived risk and may inhibit purchase intentions. Consequently, consumers rely on cues such as customer reviews,
testimonials, seller responsiveness, and visible social proof to reduce uncertainty and manage perceived risk. Therefore,

Managerial implications
The study provides a roadmap for Instagram-based entrepreneurs and digital marketers in Tanzania to change from
casual selling practices to sustainable social commerce operations as presented hereunder:
Visual Integrity and Authenticity: Managers should ensure strong alignment between digital catalogues and actual
physical inventory. Since product mismatch emerged as the primary barrier to consumer confidence, sellers are
encouraged to use unedited videos, live demonstrations, or behind-the-scenes footage to verify product quality and
authenticity.
Response Infrastructure: As quick responses were identified as a key driver of customer trust; sellers should adopt
automated chatbots for frequently asked questions (FAQs) or establish dedicated customer service support to facilitate
continuous engagement. This can help reduce the perceived risk associated with unresponsive sellers.
Strategic Use of Social Proof: Sellers should actively encourage and curate user-generated content (UGC), including
customer reviews, ratings, and testimonials. Collaborating with credible influencers can further strengthen trust, as
consumers often perceive peer recommendations as more reliable than paid advertisements.
Transparency as a Business Policy: Clear communication regarding pricing, delivery timelines, payment procedures, and
return policies should be standardized. Such transparency can minimize the uncertainty and “gambling” perception often
associated with shopping on informal social commerce platforms.
Limitations and future research directions
Despite the valuable insights generated, this study is subject to certain limitations concerning sampling bias and
platform specificity, which conversely present valuable opportunities for future scholarly investigation. First, the
utilization of snowball sampling may have introduced homophily bias, meaning that respondents were likely drawn from
closely linked social networks and consequently shared similar demographic and behavioral characteristics. Because of
this network overlap, the final sample may not fully capture the diverse experiences, varying socio-economic
backgrounds, or lower levels of digital literacy present among the broader population of Tanzanian consumers. To
address this constraint, future research should employ probability sampling methods, such as stratified random
sampling, to engage a more diverse and representative sample. This approach would allow researchers to systematically
capture the digital purchasing behaviors of consumers from varied socio-economic strata and distinct geographical
regions outside major urban centers. Second, the scope of this research was restricted exclusively to Instagram, leaving

Conclusions
This study set out to examine consumer behavior in the era of digital marketing by focusing on Instagram shoppers in
Tanzania, drawing on the TPB as the guiding framework while also incorporating insights from Perceived Risk Theory. The
findings confirm that Instagram has evolved beyond a social networking platform into a dynamic commercial
marketplace, where purchasing behavior can be meaningfully explained through the interplay of attitude, subjective
norms, and perceived behavioral control. Consistent with the TPB, consumers’ attitudes toward Instagram-based
shopping were positively shaped by visually appealing product images, credible reviews and testimonials, and prompt
seller responsiveness, all of which strengthened purchase intentions. The study further demonstrates the importance of
subjective norms, as influencer collaborations, peer recommendations, and visible social proof acted as powerful social
approval cues that encouraged purchasing behavior. Moreover, perceived behavioral control emerged as a critical
determinant of both intention and repeat purchasing behavior, with direct communication, positive prior experiences,
and confidence in payment and delivery processes enhancing consumers’ sense of control.
However, when viewed through the lens of Perceived Risk Theory, the findings reveal that various forms of risk
particularly product risk, financial risk, and trust-related concerns moderate this relationship by weakening both
perceived behavioral control and purchase intentions. Discrepancies between product descriptions and actual deliveries,
uncertainty about payment security, and lack of seller accountability heightened perceived risk, reduced trust, and
discouraged repeat purchases. Overall, the study aﬃrms that consumer behavior in Instagram-based marketplaces is not
only intention-driven, as proposed by the TPB but also risk-sensitive. Positive attitudes, supportive social influence, and
strong perceptions of control increase the likelihood of purchasing behavior only when perceived risk is suﬃciently
minimized. By providing context-specific evidence from Tanzania, this study extends the application of both theories to
social media commerce in developing economies, highlighting that eﬀective digital marketing strategies must
simultaneously foster favorable attitudes, reinforce positive social norms, enhance consumers’ perceived control, and
actively reduce perceived risks through transparency, authenticity, secure payment systems, and reliable delivery
mechanisms.
