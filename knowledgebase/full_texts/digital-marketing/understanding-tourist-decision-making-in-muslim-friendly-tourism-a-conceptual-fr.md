relevant theoretical framework within the tourism domain, as UGC can serve as an external stimulus that influences a travelers
internal cognitive process (tourism expectancy), and in turn affects travel decision behavior (visit decision). This the oretical

The steps in the methodology start from a systematic literature review. Four components guided the study design:

Following this stage, a critical analysis and synthesis process was conducted to identify theoretical gaps and

4. Results and Discussion

This section does not present empirical findings, but instead develops theoretically grounded propositions based on an
extensive review of prior literature . As a conceptual paper, the discussion aims to elaborate the underlying mechanisms that
explain the relationships between user -generated content (UGC), tourist expectations, and visit decisions. Drawing on Social
Cognitive Theory (SCT), this study moves beyond a purely descriptive application of the theory by analytically explaining how
environmental stimuli (UGC) interact with cognitive proce sses (tourist expectations) to shape behavioral outcomes (visit
decisions) within the context of Muslim-friendly tourism.

4.1. Factual User-Generated Content and its Impact on Tourist Expectations in Halal Tourism

Within Social Cognitive Theory, factual user -generated content (UGC) functions as an environmental stimulus that
shapes tourists’ cognitive evaluation processes . Factual UGC conveys factual and experience -based information about
characteristics of the destination, including transport access, accommodation quality, halal food availability, prayer facult ies,
prices charged by service providers and safety. Tourists de code this information through observational learning in order to
ascertain if a destination can be used to satisfy their travel needs and expectations.
Firm-generating promotional content is often viewed with suspicion whilst factual UGC more accurately represents the
reality of the direct experiences of tourists. This is why tourists depend on facts disseminated over various social networks  so
as to lowe r uncertainty levels and shape cognitive evaluations of destination quality and compatibility. Factual knowledge
surrounding halal facilities and religious convenience becomes especially salient in Muslim-friendly tourism contexts as Muslim
tourists are likely to assess destinations from both functional quality as well as Islamic -compatible perspectives.
Following this line of thought, one of the main insights SCT is that environmental stimuli exert indirect influences on
behavior through cognitive processing mechanism . Accordingly, factual UGC does not immediately generate behavioral
responses; rather, tourists first process and interpret destination -related information before forming expectations regarding
the anticipated travel experience. Tourist expectations therefore emerge from cognitive evaluations shaped by exposure to
reliable and relevant destination information.

4.2. Factual User-Generated Content and its Impact on Visit Decisions for Halal Tourism.

Within SCT, behavioral decisions are influenced by the interaction between environmental information and cognitive
evaluation processes. In tourism contexts, factual UGC serves as an environmental source of information that helps tourists
evaluate destination feasibility, credibility, and expected benefits before making visit decisions. Exposure to factual
destination-related information reduces perceived uncertainty and increases tourists’ confidence in choosing a destination .
With factual UGC, tourists gain access to pragmatic information about destination circumstances and factors like how
accessible a location is for tourism, halal services, prices or charges incurred while visiting the area as well as accommoda tion
options w ith safety precautions in place. In the context of Muslim -friendly tourism, this information becomes even more
relevant, as tourists tend to look for reassurances that the destinations they are visiting can support their religious pract ices
while traveling . Visitors feel more committed to visiting the destination provided their information on it is consistent and
applicable.
From the SCT perspective, tourists actively process observational information before translating it into behavioral
outcomes. Visit decisions therefore result from cognitive interpretations of environmental stimuli rather than from passive
reactions to online information. Factual UGC contributes to this process by strengthening tourists’ perceptions of destination
trustworthiness and reducing perceived travel risks.
Previous studies have demonstrated the influence of UGC on tourist behavior . Yamagishi et al. (2023) Research has
shown that user  generated content (UGC) affects tourist behaviour, as Generation Z tourists are significantly influenced in
visiting a destination due to their intentions being enticed by UGC  the credible and emotional appeal of authentic travelers
strengthens the credibility and attachment of a destination. Similarly, Xu et al. (2023) reported that informative UGC enhances
tourist loyalty and revisit behavior by shaping positive destination perceptions.
Factual information about the destination may have an even higher importance for Muslim-friendly tourism, as Muslims
will evaluate the destinations especially on factors related to compatibility with their religious beliefs and demands for services.
Thus, factual UGC can directly bolster tourists' decisions to travel whe re the destination fits the profile of satisfying both its
travel and spiritual needs. From these arguments, the following proposition is put forward :
P2: Factual user-generated content positively influences visit decisions in Muslim -friendly tourism.

4.3. Emotional User-Generated Content and its Impact on Tourist Expectations in Halal Tourism

In contrast to factual UGC, emotional UGC transmits experiential and symbolic meanings through affective pathways
associated with a destination. In SCT, this type of content acts as an environmental trigger that broaches emotional response s
that subsequently impact cognitive appraisal processes. Emotional narratives, images, and videos shared by other tourists can
evoke imagination, empathy, and anticipated satisfaction, thereby shaping affective expectations. This suggests that
expectation formation is not purely cognitive but also emotionally driven, where individuals internalize others’ experiences as
a reference point for their own anticipated outcomes. There has been limited research examining the positive impact of UGC
on tourists’ feelings and emotions toward a destination. The emotional UGC is posting experiences, emotion, and satisfaction
through images, videos, or narratives (Xu et al., 2023).
Positive Emotional UGC creates a good  feeling about a destination(Wang et al., 2017). Emotional UGC covers any type

4.6. Moderating Effect of Religiosity on Tourist Expectations towards Visit Decisions in Halal Tourism.

Religiosity functions as a boundary condition within the SCT framework by shaping how individuals interpret and
evaluate information. Information is filtered through the lens of religious values and norms, especially by those who are highly
religious so that expectations become visit decisions to varying degrees. By this token, religiosity does not determine behavior
in and of itself; it buffers or amplifies the cognitive-behavioral link when furthering a fit or misfit between destination attributes
and religious principles. Nugraha & Widyaningsih, (2022) finding that visit expectations and visit motivation have a significant
positive relationship with attitudes towards the visit. Additionally, previous studies conclude that gender significantly
moderates the relationship between Umrah visit expectations and attitudes toward the visit. Religiosity also significantly
moderates the relationship between visit expectations and visit mot ivation.
Meanwhile Rafiki et al. (2023) state that religiosity significantly acts as a moderating variable in the relationship between
awareness and purchase decisions, as well as habits and purchase decisions. According to Muslichah et al. (2020), religiosity
positively moderates the relationship between awareness and purchase decisions. Religiosity plays an important role in
individual cognition and behavior. To be more precise, the degree to which religion influences a person beliefs and actions
depends on how religious that person is. It is thus expected that religiosity will be significant in regulating behavior at t he
individual level.
With the growing need for Muslims to better understand the principles of Sharia, there is a greater demand for high -
quality services that comply with Islamic values. It follows that, to strategically service Muslim clientele within the touri sm
sector, indu stry practitioners and policymakers must supply services with a more strategic perspective available. Muslim
tourists and travelers have their own requirements as they adhere to religious duties which is something of a separate market
from other types of tourists. To fulfil the needs as well as varying facets of Muslim travellers, both domestic and foreign Muslims

5. Conclusions

This conceptual paper develops an integrated multistage framework that examines the antecedents and outcomes
associated with UGC in supporting travel decisions. As a contribution to the tourism marketing literature, this paper advances
an integrated conceptual framework that theorises user-generated content, on cognitive expectations and behaviour decision-
making, towards Muslim-friendly tourism.
 In this study, we hypothesize that UGC has an effect on tourist expectations and visit decision and that religiosity
moderates the effect of tourist expectation on visit decision. By taking tourist expectations as a mediator, you can achieve this
more nuanced perspective into the psychological mechanisms underlying visitation decisions to be targeted when designing
UGCs.
 This framework highlights the important role of UGC in aligning various environmental factors that influence tourist
behavior. It also provides new insights by incorporating religiosity as a moderating variable in the relationship between tourist
expectations and visit decisions . Considering the conditional effects across all tourist segments improves the model's
explanatory power. Overall, this multistage conceptualization presents a more holistic view of the complex decision -making
processes underlying sustainable consumption compared to existing discrete models.
 This research contributes to the development of Social Cognitive Theory (SCT) by explaining that user -generated
content (UGC), whether factual or emotional, can serve as an environmental factor that shapes tourists’ cognitive expectations
and influences their visit decision-making behavior. Within the domain of halal tourism, these expectations become crucial in
a tourist's decision -making, with Muslim travelers usually checking on religious values prior to the selection of destinations.
Therefore, apart from the application of SCT in tourism literature, this conceptual model incorporates a multistage modeling
approach that includes mediation (tourist expectations) and moderation (religiosity) variables, which have been relatively le ss
studied previously. T his framework provides a more solid theoretical underpinning for future research that seeks to study
Muslim consumer behavior from a holistic approach.
