Abstract
The increasing adoption of digital technologies has transformed the marketing landscape,
creating opportunities for small and medium -sized enterprises (SMEs) to improve their
competitiveness and performance. Despite the growing relevance of online marketin g,
many SMEs in Calabar Metropolis continue to face challenges linked to limited digital
skills, inadequate technological infrastructure, and uncertainty regarding the
effectiveness of emerging digital marketing tools. This study examined the effect of
online marketing on the performance of SMEs in Calabar Metropolis, Cross River State,
Nigeria, focusing on social media marketing, online advertising, content creation, internet
payment systems, and online ordering and delivery. A cross-sectional survey design was
adopted, targeting registered and unregistered SMEs in Calabar Municipality and Calabar
South Local Government Areas. Using the Topman formula for an unknown population,
a sample size of 169 respondents was determined, and data were collected through  a
structured questionnaire with reliability coefficients ranging from 0.703 to 0.831.
Descriptive statistics were analysed using SPSS version 27, while Partial Least Squares
Structural Equation Modelling (PLS-SEM) with SmartPLS and 5,000 bootstrap samples
tested the hypotheses. The findings showed that all dimensions of online marketing had
positive and significant effects on SME performance: social media marketing (β = 0.297),
online advertising, the strongest predictor (β = 0.324), content creation (β = 0.265),
internet payment systems (β = 0.299), and online ordering and delivery (β = 0.309), all
significant at p < 0.001. The model fit indices confirmed adequacy (SRMR = 0.054; NFI
= 0.822). The study concludes that online marketing is a critical driver o f SME

Theoretical Framework
This study is anchored on the theory of change, developed and published by Kurt Lewin in the
1940s. The theory is based on the fundamental idea that change is inevitable in people,
organizations, and other things; therefore, good change management and plan ning are
essential. It is assumed that the process of bringing about change consists of first establishing
the idea that change is necessary, then moving towards the new, ideal level of behaviour, and
lastly keeping the new behaviour as the standard course of action (Etim et al, 2021). Lewin’s
model offers a foundational perspective on the dynamics of organizational change, structuring
the complex process into a systematic approach that can be easily understood and
implemented. Lewin posited that change is not an autonomous occurrence but rather the result
of a dynamic interplay between forces for change and forces resisting change (Burnes, 2019).
The proponent of the theory conceptualized this interplay through the lens of his seminal three-
stage model known as "Unfreeze, Change, Refreeze."
Unfreeze: The initial stage of the model, unfreezing, involves preparing an organization or
group to accept that change is necessary. This phase requires a significant shift in mindset and
the dismantling of established s tructures and norms. Lewin emphasized the importance of
addressing the status quo, creating an environment where members of the group recognize the
need for change (Jalagat, 2016). niques such as open discussions, feedback mechanisms, and
awareness programs may be instituted to foster this recognition. The critical aspect of this
phase is the identification and reduction of counterproductive behaviors or resistance that may
hinder the subsequent change process (Furxhi, 2021). To successfully implement the
unfreezing stage, leaders must communicate the reasons behind the change initiative
effectively. This involves presenting data, evidence, and arguments that elucidate why the
existing status quo is no longer viable and why change is essential for future suc cess. This

communication must be clear, transparent, and supportive to mitigate feelings of uncertainty
and anxiety that often accompany change initiatives.
Change: This phase embodies the actual implementation of new behaviors, processes, and
attitudes. Unlike the unfreezing stage, which focuses on preparing for change, this stage is
characterized by action and transformation. The changing phase involves several key
components, including the design of the new procedures, establishing new organizational
cultures, and providing training to equip employees with the necessary skills to navigate the
transition (Barrow et al, 2022). It is imperative that leaders maintain robust communication
throughout this stage, reiterating the benefits of the change initiative  and reinforcing the
importance of adaptability. During this stage, resistance may still be prevalent, as individuals
wrestle with uncertainty regarding their roles and the overall impact of the changes on their
daily workflows. Hence, it is critical for l eaders to foster an environment where feedback is
encouraged, and concerns can be voiced openly (Mushtaq, 2011). By acknowledging and
addressing these concerns, leaders can facilitate a smoother transition, foster trust, and
enhance employee morale.
Refreeze: The final stage of Lewin's Change Model is refreezing, which is crucial to ensure
that the changes implemented during the second stage become an integral part of the
organizational culture. After the transition has taken place, it is essential to stabi lize and
solidify new behaviors and processes to avoid regression to outdated practices. Refreezing
involves codifying the changes into the organizational fabric through methods such as new
policies, revised organizational charts, and updated job descripti ons (Ahmady et al, 2016).
This ensures that the changes are not merely temporary adjustments, but rather a new way of
functioning within the organization. Leaders must also celebrate successes and recognize
employee contributions to reinforce the new norms  and behaviors. In addition, refreezing
requires ongoing support and reinforcement to maintain momentum (Rousseau & Have,
2022). This can include regular training sessions, mentorship opportunities, and recognition
programs that encourage employees to perpetuate the new behaviors. It is essential for leaders
to continuously monitor the impact of the changes and be responsive to any need for further
adjustments (Adeniyi et al, 2024). Kurt Lewin’s theory of change is not without criticism.
Burnes (2019) posit that the theory lacks accountability for the interaction of the individual,
groups, organization, and society; and also fails to address the complex and iterative process
of change.
The Kurt Lewin’s theory of change is relevant to the study as there has been a paradigm shift
in the operations of small and medium scale enterprise. As rightly stated by the proponent that
there is no change unless there is a force that propels the change and a resisting force. The
advent of the internet has made the business  environment a global village where business
owners are able to reach to their target audience without being in the same locality. Customers
have aligned with the new trend of ordering and paying for goods online while waiting for the
goods to be delivered to them. Although initially, this mode of transaction was not welcomed
by most customer, the force for change was able to subdue the force that resist change. The

Concept of Online Marketing and SMEs Performance
As economies evolve and technological advancements  carve new pathways for
communication and commerce, small and medium -sized enterprises (SMEs) have found
themselves at a significant crossroads. The rise of digital marketing strategies offers a plethora
of opportunities for SMEs to enhance their visibilit y, engage with customers, and ultimately
improve their performance. Online marketing is a business effort to inform, converse, promote
and sell products and services over the Internet. Small and Medium-Sized Enterprises (SME’s)
are indispensable drivers of  the economic growth and development of most countries,
especially developing countries (Papadopoulos et al, 2020). According to World Bank, small
and medium enterprises play an important role in most economies especially in developing
countries (Thaha et al, 2021). The authors further posit that SMEs make up the majority of
businesses worldwide and contribute greatly to employment availability, SMEs represent
about 90 percent of businesses and more than 50 percent of job globally. This makes this sector
of the economy very crucial, as they leverage on the giant strid in the technological sector can
come in handy to help advance their business. One of the hallmarks of online marketing is its
capacity to transcend geographical barriers, enabling SMEs to reach  a global audience.
Traditional marketing models often confine businesses to regional markets, limiting their
growth potential. However, with digital marketing tools such as social media platforms, email
marketing, and search engine optimization (SEO), SMEs can engage with a diverse customer
base across various locations (Inuwa & Habib, 2023).
Social media, in particular, has emerged as a powerful tool for SMEs. Platforms like Facebook,
Instagram, and Twitter allow businesses to develop their unique brand i dentities while
facilitating direct interaction with customers (Jeswani, 2023). Such engagements can lead to
heightened brand loyalty and customer retention, as social media fosters a community around
a brand where customers feel valued and heard. The ability to respond to customer inquiries,
gather feedback, and showcase user -generated content further enriches this interactive
experience, creating a dynamic dialogue that is crucial for building strong customer
relationships (Lim & Rasul, 2022). Another major area in SMEs sector is the cost implication.
Cost considerations play a vital role in the operational strategies of SMEs, which often
function with limited budgets. Traditional advertising methods, such as print media and
television commercials, can req uire substantial financial outlays that may be prohibitive for
smaller enterprises. In contrast, online marketing offers a more accessible alternative (Manley,
2015). Many online marketing channels, including social media, online advertising, content
creation can be tailored to fit various budget constraints. Furthermore, the potential for virality
and organic reach in social media campaigns can result in significant exposure without
incurring exorbitant costs, making online marketing a particularly appealing option for smaller
enterprises that are eager to compete against larger entities (Khanom, 2023).
Another defining feature of online marketing is the ability to measure and analyze campaign
performance with unparalleled precision. In traditional marketin g, gauging the effectiveness
of advertising efforts often relies on anecdotal evidence or broad sales trends (Santiago, 2024).

Social Media Marketing and SME Performance
Social media is becoming an indispensable part of every business's marketing strategy in the
modern digital environment. The increasing prevalence of social media platforms such as
Facebook, X formerly called Twitter, Instagram, LinkedIn, Tik Tok, WhatsApp among others
has transformed the way org anizations communicate, engage, and build relationships with
their customers (Khanom, 2023). For small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), leveraging
social media marketing has become not only an option but a necessity for enhancing their
performance and competitiveness. Social media marketing refers to the use of social media
platforms and websites to promote a product or service. It encompasses a range of activities,
including community engagement, targeted advertisements, and performance analytics. For
SMEs, social media marketing presents a unique opportunity to expand their reach, connect
with a diverse audience, and foster brand loyalty without incurring prohibitive costs (Belas et
al, 2021). A primary advantage of social media marketing for SMEs is the level of accessibility
it affords. Unlike traditional advertising channels, which often require significant financial
investment and yield uncertain returns, social media platforms allow SMEs to effectively
reach their target audience with relatively low expenditure (Oyekan, 2022). By establishing a
robust online presence, SMEs can generate brand awareness and drive traffic to their websites
or physical locations. The viral nature of social media content further amplifies this effect, as

customer engagement (Nwali & Ntegeeh, 2022).
Furthermore, social media marketing facilitates a two -way communication channel between
businesses and their customers. SMEs can soli cit feedback, conduct surveys, and engage in
direct dialogues with their clientele, thereby fostering a sense of community and connection.
This engagement not only enhances customer satisfaction but also provides SMEs with
valuable insights into customer preferences and behaviors (Rosario & Dias, 2022). The authors
posit that such feedback loops are instrumental in informing product development, refining
marketing strategies, and ultimately augmenting performance. In addition to cost -
effectiveness and enhanced communication, social media marketing enables SMEs to employ
precise targeting strategies. Platforms like Facebook and Google Ads offer sophisticated

effectively manage social media accounts and create compelling content. This deficiency can
lead to inconsistent messaging and missed op portunities in engaging potential customers
(Patrizi, 2024).
Another challenge is the rapidly evolving nature of social media. With algorithms frequently
changing and new platforms emerging, SMEs must remain agile and adaptable in their
marketing strategie s. The risk of falling behind industry trends can be detrimental, as
consumers increasingly gravitate towards innovative and engaging content. As such, SMEs
must invest in continuous learning and upskilling to remain competitive in the dynamic digital
marketplace (Fan, 2023). Moreover, measuring the effectiveness of social media marketing
efforts can be quite complex. Unlike traditional metrics such as sales volume, social media
success is often gauged through engagement rates, reach, and impressions. SMEs may find it
challenging to translate these metrics into meaningful business outcomes, leading to
uncertainty regarding the return on investment (ROI) of their social media activities.
Implementing robust analytics tools is essential for SMEs to track performance accurately and
make informed decisions (Jani et al, 2021).

Engaging content facilitates interaction between the SME and its audience, fostering a strong
community around the brand. This interaction is i ncreasingly significant in the age of social
media, where consumers seek brands that resonate with their values and interests. Content that
is educational, entertaining, or inspiring not only captures audience attention but also
encourages sharing and participation (Qin, 2020). This organic engagement can enhance brand
loyalty, as customers are more likely to return to a business that consistently provides value
through thoughtful content. Moreover, the alignment of content creation with the overall
marketing strategy of an SME is critical in enhancing performance (Li, 2024). A well-defined
content calendar that outlines topics, publication dates, and distribution channels can ensure
that the content produced is not only timely but also relevant to seasonal trends and customer
interests. By leveraging analytics tools, SMEs can track the performance of their content,
gaining insights into customer preferences and engagement levels (Suleman & Abbas, 2024).
Even though creating content has many benefits, SMEs fr equently face a number of
difficulties. Limited resources both in terms of finance and personnel can hinder their ability
to produce high -quality content consistently. Moreover, the rapid pace of digital trends
necessitates that SMEs remain agile and adapt able, a demanding task for many with
constrained capacity (Hojnik & Hudek, 2023).

integrate these syste ms can lead to enhanced operational efficiency, improved customer
experience, and ultimately, bolstered performance. Internet payment systems refer to
electronic mechanisms that enable the transfer of funds over the internet in exchange for goods
or servic es (Singh et al, 2023). These systems encompass a broad category of payment
methods, including credit and debit card transactions, electronic wallets, mobile payment

and services but also account for transactions at physical storefronts through advanced point-

consumer preferences, and the need for businesses to adapt to the complexities of a globalized

Firstly, SMEs frequently grapple with limited resources, making efficient payment processing

customer expectations are continually evolving. Internet payment systems offer secure,
convenient, and diverse payment options that cater to varied consumer preferences. By
meeting these expectations, SMEs not only enhance customer satisfaction but also cultivate
loyalty, which is essential for their long -term viability (Gupta et al, 2023). In addi tion to
operational benefits, internet payment systems empower SMEs to engage in global commerce.

SMEs to transcend geographical boundaries, providing access to a wider customer base and
fostering growth opportunities that might otherwise remain unattainable (Schwaeke et al,

relationship management. By tracking payment preferences and behaviors, SMEs can tailor
marketing strategies, promotions, and customer engagement efforts to enhance loyalty and
retention. A deeper understanding of consumer preferences allows SMEs to build customized
experiences that resonate with their target audiences, ultimately leading to improved sales
performance (Rane et al, 2023).

have become essential for SMEs striving to remain competitive. Online ordering systems are
particularly beneficial for SMEs, as they eliminate geographical co nstraints, enabling these
businesses to serve customers beyond their localities (Costa & Castro, 2021). The ability to
process orders online allows SMEs to cater to a more extensive customer base. This has
significant implications for sales volume and over all revenue. For many small businesses,

One of the most immediate benefits of online ordering systems is the enhancement of customer

Methodology
This study adopted a cross -sectional survey research design to examine the effect of online
marketing on the performance of small and medium -sized enterprises (SMEs) in Calabar
Metropolis, Cross River State, Nigeria. The study area comprised Calabar Municipalit y and
Calabar South Local Government Areas, which together constitute Calabar Metropolis and
host a significant concentration of SMEs engaged in diverse economic activities. The target
population consisted of both registered and unregistered SMEs operating  within the
metropolis. Given the absence of reliable data on the total number of SMEs, the population
was considered unknown. Consequently, the sample size was determined using the Topman
formula for an unknown population. Following a pilot survey involving 40 SMEs, a response
rate of 88% and a non-response rate of 12% were obtained, resulting in a sample size of 169

408  africascholarpublications@gmail.com                                                                                MAY, 2026

respondents. A combination of purposive and simple random sampling techniques was
employed. Purposive sampling was used to identify eligible SME operators, while simple
random sampling was adopted in selecting participating enterprises. Of the 169 questionnaires
administered, 85 were distributed in Calabar Municipality and 84 in Calabar South. Primary
data were collected using a structured ques tionnaire consisting of two sections. Section A
captured respondents’ demographic characteristics, while Section B measured the study
variables using a five-point Likert scale ranging from Strongly Agree (5) to Strongly Disagree
(1). Content validity of the instrument was established through expert review and assessment
by the research supervisor. The reliability of the instrument was assessed using Cronbach’s
Alpha based on data obtained from the pilot survey. The coefficients ranged from 0.703 to
0.831 across all constructs, exceeding the recommended threshold of 0.70 and confirming the
reliability of the measurement scales. Data analysis was conducted using both descriptive and
inferential statistical techniques. Descriptive statistics, including frequencies and percentages,
were generated using SPSS version 27. To test the study hypotheses, Partial Least Squares
Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM) was performed using SmartPLS software. A
bootstrapping procedure with 5,000 subsamples was employed to ass ess the significance of
path coefficients as well as the reliability and validity of the measurement and structural
models.
Ethical approval for the study was obtained from the research ethics committee of the
researchers' institution, and participation wa s strictly voluntary. Prior to administering the
questionnaire, informed consent was sought from each respondent, with assurances of
anonymity and confidentiality of the information provided. Common method bias was
assessed using Harman's single -factor tes t, and the results indicated that no single factor
accounted for the majority of the variance, suggesting that common method bias was not a
serious concern in the data. PLS-SEM was preferred over covariance-based SEM (CB-SEM)
because the study is largely predictive and exploratory in nature, the sample size is relatively
modest, and the data did not fully satisfy the multivariate normality assumption required for
CB-SEM, conditions under which PLS-SEM is considered more robust and appropriate.

Hypothesis Testing Results
The structural model was assessed through bootstrapping using 5,000 subsamples to examine
the significance of the hypothesized relationships. The results confirm that all five hypotheses
proposed in the study are statistically significant at the 0.001 level, leading to the rejection of
the corresponding null hypotheses (Table 7). For Hypothesis HO1, which states that there is
no significant effect of social media marketing on the performance of SMEs in Calabar, the
analysis revealed a standardized path coefficient (β) of 0.297, with a t-statistic of 8.095 and a
p-value of 0.000. This result indicates a significant positive effect, thereby rejecting HO1 and
confirming that so cial media marketing contributes meaningfully to SME performance.
Hypothesis HO2 posited that online advertising has no significant effect on the performance
of Calabar SMEs. This was also rejected, as the path coefficient from online advertising to
SME performance was 0.324, with a t -statistic of 9.104 and a p -value of 0.000. The result
confirms that online advertising significantly enhances the performance of SMEs. Regarding
Hypothesis HO3, which proposed that content creation does not significantly influence SME
performance in Calabar, the findings show a path coefficient of 0.265, a t -statistic of 7.253,
and a p -value of 0.000. Thus, HO3 is rejected, providing evidence that effective content
creation positively impacts SME outcomes. Hypothesis HO4, which  stated that internet
payment systems have no significant effect on the performance of SMEs in Calabar, was also
rejected. The standardized coefficient for this relationship was 0.299, with a t-statistic of 7.983
and a p-value of 0.000, indicating a strong and significant influence. Finally, Hypothesis HO5
proposed that online ordering and delivery do not significantly affect SME performance. This
hypothesis was rejected as well, with the analysis yielding a coefficient of 0.309, a t -statistic
of 8.184, and  a p-value of 0.000. The result underscores the importance of efficient digital
fulfilment systems in improving SME performance. Overall, the statistical evidence supports
the conclusion that all five digital strategy components social media marketing, onl ine
advertising, content creation, internet payment systems, and online ordering and delivery
significantly and positively affect the performance of SMEs in Calabar.

Discussion of Findings
The results of the structural model reveal that all five hypothesized relationships were
statistically significant, supporting the influence of key digital strateg ies on the performance
of SMEs in Calabar. These findings are consistent with existing literature, affirming that
digital marketing tools and infrastructure have become essential drivers of small business
performance in today’s economy. The outcome of Hypo thesis 1 (H1) indicates a significant
positive effect of social media marketing on the performance of SMEs in Calabar (β = 0.297,
p < 0.001). This finding aligns with Etim et al. (2021), who examined e -marketing strategies
and found that social media marketing had a strong impact on SME performance in the post-
COVID "new-normal" era. Similarly, the result supports the work of Oyakhire (2020), who
found that social media strategies positively influenced customer patronage of online
businesses in Nigeria. In both cases, social media served as a cost-effective, customer-centric
tool that fostered engagement and business visibility mirroring the current study's conclusions.
The result of Hypothesis 2 (H2) confirms that online advertising has a significant positi ve
effect on SME performance (β = 0.324, p < 0.001), which is consistent with the study of Ejeh

415  africascholarpublications@gmail.com                                                                                MAY, 2026

and Omede (2020). Their research demonstrated that e-business applications, including online
advertising, significantly contributed to SME success in Delta Stat e, Nigeria. Similarly,
Olusegun et al. (2020) found that online marketing had a favorable impact on small business
performance in Lagos State by expanding self-employment opportunities and contributing to
local economic development. These findings parallel  the current study’s results, reinforcing
the role of digital promotions in driving growth.
Hypothesis 3 (H3) found a significant relationship between content creation and SME
performance in Calabar (β = 0.265, p < 0.001). This outcome supports the findings of Adam
et al. (2020), who investigated the role of digital marketing platforms in enhancing supply
chain management for SMEs in Indonesia. Their study showed that digital marketing through
online media significantly affected customer satisfaction, indir ectly influencing customer
loyalty. While their study focused on the broader supply chain and consumer response, both
results affirm that rich, engaging content serves as a key factor in attracting and retaining
customers. The result of Hypothesis 4 (H4) shows that the internet payment system also has a
significant effect on SME performance (β = 0.299, p < 0.001). This is in agreement with
Kwabena et al. (2019), who demonstrated that digital payment systems, when integrated with
other technological and organizational elements, positively impact SME trade and stakeholder
relationships. Their findings emphasized how SMEs can leverage such systems not only to
streamline transactions but also to build trust with trading partners an observation that
reinforces the present study’s findings within the Calabar context.
Finally, Hypothesis 5 (H5) confirms that online ordering and delivery systems significantly
affect the performance of SMEs (β = 0.309, p < 0.001). This supports the findings of Adam et
al. (2020), whose study on digital marketing and supply chain management found that online
systems enhance customer satisfaction and indirectly influence loyalty. Their study further
emphasized that customer satisfaction was influenced more by system efficiency and digita l
accessibility than by features such as product reviews an insight that corresponds with the
performance gains seen in this study’s SME sample. In summary, the findings from this study
reinforce the importance of adopting a comprehensive digital strategy incorporating social
media marketing, content creation, online advertising, digital payments, and ordering systems
to enhance the competitiveness and sustainability of SMEs in emerging economies like
Nigeria. Each digital lever plays a distinct but complementary role in strengthening customer
relationships, streamlining operations, and improving overall business performance.

Conclusion
This study set out to examine the effect of online marketing on the performance of small and
medium enterprises (SMEs) in Calabar Metropolis, Cross River State, Nigeria, specifically the
effects of social media marketing, online advertising, content creati on, internet payment
systems, and online ordering and delivery. The results showed that all five dimensions of
online marketing had positive and significant effects on SME performance, with online
advertising emerging as the strongest predictor, followed b y online ordering and delivery,

416  africascholarpublications@gmail.com                                                                                MAY, 2026

internet payment systems, social media marketing, and content creation. Theoretically, the
study extends the application of Lewin's theory of change to the domain of digital marketing
adoption among SMEs, demonstrating that the unfreeze-change-refreeze sequence offers a
useful lens for understanding how small businesses transition into digital marketing practice.
Practically, the findings suggest that SME operators in Calabar stand to gain measurable
performance improvements, in market coverage, customer patronage, customer retention, and
sales, by deliberately investing in these five online marketing dimensions rather than treating
them as optional or peripheral activities. The study is, however, limited by its cross-sectional
design, which restricts causal inference, its focus on a single metropolitan area, which may
limit the generalisability of the findings to other regions with different infrastructural and
socioeconomic conditions, and its reliance on self -reported data, which may be subject to
social desirability bias. Future studies should consider longitudinal designs, cross -regional
comparisons, and objective performance measures to validate and extend the present findings.
Overall, the study contributes to the growing  body of knowledge on digital marketing and
SME performance by providing empirical evidence, from an under-researched context, on the
roles of content creation, internet payment systems, and online ordering and delivery alongside
the more established dimensions of social media marketing and online advertising.
