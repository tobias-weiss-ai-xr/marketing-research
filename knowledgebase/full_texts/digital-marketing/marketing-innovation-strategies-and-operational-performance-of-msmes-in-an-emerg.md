Abstract
This study examines the effect of marketing innovation on the operational performance of Micro,
Small and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) in Taraba State, Nigeria. Marketing innovation was
operationalized through four dimensions —digital marketing innovation, branding innovation,
customer relationship innovation, and promotional innovation—within a conceptual framework
grounded in the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT).  A quantitative
survey design was adopted, and data were collected from 350 MSME employees across five Local
Government Areas in Taraba State using a structured, validated questionnaire. Data were
analyzed using descriptive statistics, Pearson correlation,  and multiple regression analysis. The
results show that the four marketing innovation dimensions jointly explain 70.6% of the variance
in operational performance [F(4, 345) = 207.55, p < 0.001]. Customer relationship innovation
emerged as the strongest in dividual predictor (β = 0.363, p = 0.001), followed by promotional
innovation (β = 0.295, p < 0.001) and digital marketing innovation (β = 0.256, p = 0.044).
Branding innovation was not a significant unique predictor in the regression model (β = −0.047,
p = 0.709), a result attributed to multicollinearity with digital marketing innovation, although its
bivariate correlation with operational performance remained strong and significant (r = 0.795, p
< 0.001). The findings indicate that marketing innovation fu nctions as an integrated, mutually
reinforcing system rather than a set of independent practices, and that customer -centric and
promotional strategies offer the most direct route to improved operational performance among
MSMEs in resource -constrained, emerging-economy settings. The study contributes to theory
by extending UTAUT beyond adoption intention to operational performance outcomes, and
offers practical guidance for MSME managers, industry associations, and policymakers seeking
to strengthen MSME co mpetitiveness through marketing innovation, particularly within
agribusiness-dominated value chains.

Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) play a vital role in Nigeria’s economy by
contributing to employment creation, rural income generation, and food security,
particularly in agricultural states such as Taraba. Despite this importance , many MSMEs

inefficient processes, limited market access, poor customer retention, and weak
competitiveness in increasingly dynamic markets.
In response to these challenges, marketing innovation has become a key strategic tool for
improving business outcomes. It involves the adoption of new or improved marketing
practices such as digital marketing, social media engagement, branding innovation,
customer relationship management, and value-driven communication strategies. Empirical
studies suggest that such innovations enhance market reach, improve customer
engagement, and strengthen firm performance and competitiveness (Singh & Sharma,
2026; Syaifuddin et al., 2026). However, adoption among MSMEs  remains low due to
inadequate infrastructure, limited digital literacy, and continued reliance on traditional
marketing methods.
In Taraba State, these constraints are more pronounced, as many MSMEs operate with
limited access to mode rn marketing tools and weak institutional support, thereby
restricting their ability to respond effectively to changing consumer demands and
competitive pressures.
Nationally, MSMEs are the backbone of the Nigerian economy: they account for the
overwhelming majority of registered businesses and the bulk of non -oil employment, and
are widely credited with driving local wealth creation, poverty reduction, and
entrepreneurship (SMEDAN & National Bureau of Statistics, 2017). In agrarian states such
as Taraba, M SMEs occupy an especially strategic position because they dominate
agricultural production, processing, trading, and distribution activities that sustain both
rural livelihoods and regional food security. Sustaining and improving the operational
performance of these enterprises is therefore not merely a firm-level concern but a matter
of broader economic and developmental significance for the state and the country.
Marketing innovation has emerged as a strategic mechanism for addressing these
challenges thr ough new or improved practices such as digital marketing, social media
engagement, innovative branding and promotion, customer relationship management,
and value-oriented communication. Such practices can enhance market reach, customer
engagement, brand vi sibility, and responsiveness, thereby improving operational
effectiveness and competitiveness. Empirical studies generally report positive effects of
marketing innovation on competitiveness and business performance (Amoa -Gyarteng et
al., 2024; Trianto et a l., 2025; Syaifuddin et al., 2026). However, Esubalew and Adebisi
(2024) found that marketing innovation did not significantly mediate the relationship
between entrepreneurial competency and MSME growth in Ethiopia, suggesting that its
performance effects may vary across institutional, technological, sectoral, and economic
contexts.
Despite this growing scholarly interest, empirical evidence remains fragmented in ways
that limit its usefulness for Nigerian MSMEs. Geographically, the bulk of the literature

Literature Review
Marketing Innovation
Marketing innovation is defined as "the implementation o f a new marketing method
involving significant changes in product design or packaging, product placement, product
promotion, or pricing" (OECD/Eurostat, 2018). Building on this foundation, it encompasses
innovative strategies and tools — including digital marketing, social media, eco -labeling,
and ESG communication — aimed at enhancing brand visibility, consumer trust, and
competitive advantage (Ustik et al., 2026). Scholars further characterize it as a dynamic

integrated with sustainability principles, to differentiate themselves, create customer
value, and sustain competitive advantage (Civelek et al., 2025; Gonchar et al., 2026). In this
study, marketing innovation is  operationalized through four key dimensions: digital
marketing, branding innovation, customer relationship innovation, and promotional
innovation, which collectively enhance market responsiveness, customer engagement, and

Digital Marketing
Digital marketing refers to the strategic use of online platforms and digital tools to promote
products, engage customers, and support innovation processes through real -time data,
personalization, and enhanced customer interaction (Makhmudov et al., 2026). It is further
conceptualized as a strategic capability that leverages digital technologies to strengthen
customer engagement, market sensing, and innovation, thereby improving SME
performance and competitiveness (Elkhoudary et al., 2 026). In the context of  MSMEs,
digital platforms such as WhatsApp, Facebook, and Instagram serve as vital channels for
improving market access, customer engagement, and price discovery. Empirical evidence
indicates that digital marketing enhances market r each and operational efficiency by
reducing information asymmetry and improving communication between firms and
customers (Singh & Sharma, 2026; Eshbayev et al., 2024). However, the effectiveness of
digital marketing in MSMEs  is largely dependent on the l evel of digital literacy and the
availability of adequate technological infrastructure.

Branding Innovation
Bello et al. (2026) define branding innovation as the strategic enhancement of brand
identity and communication through the use of digital techn ologies and improved
customer experiences to strengthen competitiveness and foster customer loyalty. The
study further highlights that branding innovation is significantly influenced by digital
transformation, which enables firms to redesign brand interactions and improve customer
engagement through enhanced digital experiences (Bello et al., 2026). Empirical evidence
also suggests that innovative branding strategies, particularly those linked to sustainability
initiatives and eco-friendly packaging, significantly increase consumers’ willingness to pay
and strengthen market competitiveness (Giri & Chaulagai, 2024; Ustik et al., 2026).
Consequently, branding innovation serves as a critical mechanism for building consumer
trust, enhancing perceived value, and  sustaining long-term customer loyalty, particularly
among MSMEs.

Customer relationship innovation refers to the continuous enhancement of customer
management practices through the application of digital technologies a nd electronic
customer relationship management (E -CRM) systems, aimed at improving customer

Promotional Innovation
Amoa-Gyarteng et al. (2024) describe promotional innovation as the use of new, creative,
and technology -driven approaches to influence customer behaviour and enhance firm
performance. Similarly, Purchase and Volery (2020) define it as the introduction of new or
significantly improved communication and promotional methods aimed at informing,
persuading, and building relationships with customers and other stakeholders. This
includes digital and social media communication, personalized and data-driven marketing,
influencer and peer-to-peer marketing, and interactive promotional strategies (Purchase &
Volery, 2020). Empirical evidence further indicates that innovative promotional strategies
significantly enhance consumer engagement, product visibility, and purchase intention ,
particularly in agribusiness markets (Bondarenko et al., 2025; Singh & Sharma, 2026). These
strategies are particularly beneficial for MSMEs because they offer cost -effective ways to
reach broader audiences and stimulate demand despite limited financial resources.

Operational Performance
Operational performance refers to the extent to which a firm achieves efficiency,
productivity, quality, cost, and delivery objectives, often enhanced through benchmarking
and effective performance management practices (Voss, Åhlström, & Blackmon, 1997; De
Leeuw & Van den Berg, 2011). It is commonly measured using indicators such as
productivity, efficiency, quality, cost performance, delivery reliability, flexibility, customer
satisfaction, sales and revenue growth, cost reduction, and market responsiveness (Amoa-
Gyarteng et al., 2024; Truong et al., 2017).

Theoretical Review
The Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT), developed by
Venkatesh et al. (2003), is an integrative framework used to explain users’ acceptance and
actual use of technology. The model posits that behaviora l intention and usage behavior
are primarily determined by four constructs: performance expectancy, effort expectancy,
social influence, and facilitating conditions. These constructs capture the extent to which
users perceive a technology as useful, easy t o use, socially supported, and backed by
adequate technical and organizational resources.
Recent studies (Banluesapy et al., 2026; Kumar & Siringoringo, 2026; Sadiq et al., 2026)
affirm the continued relevance of UTAUT in explaining technology adoption in digital and
innovation-driven contexts. Across these studies, adoption is consistently higher when
users perceive clear benefits, minimal complexity, positive social reinforcement, and
sufficient infrastructural support.
In the context of MSMEs , UTAUT pro vides a useful lens for explaining the adoption of
marketing innovation practices, including digital marketing tools, branding systems,
customer relationship innovations, and promotional strategies. Adoption is more likely
when operators believe these tool s enhance performance (performance expectancy), are
easy to use (effort expectancy), are encouraged by peers or market actors (social influence),
and are supported by enabling infrastructure such as internet access, mobile devices, and
training (facilitating conditions) (Kempen et al., 2026; Sadiq et al., 2026).
Within this framework, the four marketing innovation constructs in the study can be
theoretically explained as follows:
Digital marketing innovation (social media, mobile platforms, online advertising) is adopted
when MSMEs perceive that it improves market access and sales performance (performance
expectancy) and is easy to operate (effort expectancy). Social influence from successful
adopters and access to digital infrastructure further strengthens usage, leading to improved
operational performance.
Branding innovation (packaging, labeling, and product differentiation) is adopted when it is
perceived to enhance product visibility and customer preference (performance expectancy)
and is feasible within existing operational capacity (effort expectancy). Peer practices and
market competition also encourage adoption, improving competitiveness and
performance outcomes.

Customer relationship innovation (CRM systems, feedback mechanisms, personalized
communication) is adopted when it is viewed as beneficial for customer satisfaction and

Promotional innovation (social media campaigns, digital advertising, and online

(performance expectancy) and cost-effective and simple to implement (effort expectancy).
Competitive pressure and market trends (social influence) also drive adoption, enhancing
market reach and operational outcomes.
Overall, UTAUT (Venkatesh et al., 2003) explai ns that marketing innovation adoption in
MSMEs is driven by perceived usefulness, ease of use, social influence, and facilitating
conditions, which collectively translate into improved operational performance.

Empirical Review
The empirical literatur e on marketing innovation and MSME performance reveals
converging evidence across diverse economic contexts, though notable inconsistencies and
contextual limitations persist.
Trianto et al. (2025), in a study conducted in Indonesia, investigated the interplay between
marketing innovation, market orientation, competitive business advantage, and
government support in sustaining micro-business survival during the COVID-19 pandemic.
Employing a quantitative research design and Partial Least Square Structural E quation
Modeling (PLS -SEM), the study established that marketing innovation significantly
contributes to sustainable competitive advantage through differentiation, cost leadership,
and focus strategies. The findings further indicated that competitor orient ation and
government support were critical determinants of micro-business resilience during periods
of economic disruption. While methodologically robust, the study's scope was confined to
a single crisis period (the pandemic), raising questions about the generalizability of its
findings to non-crisis, steady-state business environments.
In a related but contrasting study, Esubalew and Adebisi (2024) examined the mediating
role of marketing innovation in the relationship between entrepreneurial competency a nd
MSME growth in Ethiopia. Drawing on data from 288 MSME owner -managers selected
through stratified random sampling, and employing structural equation modeling, the
study found that entrepreneurial competency exerted a significant positive direct effect on
MSME growth. However, contrary to theoretical expectations, marketing innovation did
not significantly mediate this relationship, as both its antecedent (entrepreneurial
competency) and consequent (enterprise growth) paths proved statistically insignific ant.
This finding diverges from the broader literature affirming marketing innovation's
mediating capacity, suggesting that contextual and sectoral factors within the Ethiopian
MSME landscape may moderate the innovation –performance relationship, an issue t he
study did not fully explore.

Adopting a systematic literature review methodology guided by the PRISMA framework,

Syaifuddin et al. (2026) empirically examined the joint  effect of product and marketing
innovation on MSME competitiveness and economic growth. Using quantitative analysis,
the study demonstrated that both innovation dimensions exert significant positive effects
on competitiveness, with marketing innovation —particularly digital platform utilization
and customer engagement strategies —emerging as the stronger driver of market
expansion and firm performance. The study's findings reinforce the strategic primacy of
marketing innovation over product innovation alone in resource -constrained MSME
contexts, although the specific industry and geographic scope of the sampled MSMEs was
not sufficiently detailed to allow sectoral generalization.
Focusing on sustainability -oriented marketing, Ustik et al. (2026) explored inno vative
marketing strategy tools for promoting green agribusiness brands in Ukraine. The study
found that digital platforms, social media engagement, eco -labeling, storytelling, and
green branding practices significantly enhanced consumer trust, brand visib ility, and
customer loyalty. The authors concluded that authenticity, transparency, and
environmental responsibility constitute essential pillars of effective green branding. While
insightful, this study's applicability is constrained to agribusiness enter prises operating
within advanced sustainability-conscious markets, limiting its transferability to MSMEs in
developing agrarian economies with weaker environmental regulatory frameworks.
Finally, Amoa-Gyarteng et al. (2024) investigated whether innovative marketing and sales
promotion function as catalysts or inhibitors of SME performance in Ghana. Their empirical
findings revealed that customer -oriented promotional strategies generally enhance firm
performance by improving market reach, customer engagement , and competitiveness.
However, the study cautioned that poor implementation and resource limitations could
attenuate these benefits, underscoring those positive outcomes are not automatic but
contingent on effective execution capacity.
Within Nigeria spec ifically, Awotunde, Oyedele, and Aregbeshola (2024) examined how
online marketing and ICT -based initiatives influence the competitiveness and growth of
small and medium enterprises, using a quantitative survey design and regression analysis.
Their findings  showed that online marketing significantly enhanced business

Research Gap
Collectively, these studies affirm a generally positive relationship between marketing
innovation and MSME performance outcomes —including competitiveness, growth, and
survival—across Indonesia, Ethiopia, Ukraine, and Ghana. However, three critical patterns
emerge. First, the relation ship is not uniformly significant; Esubalew and Adebisi (2024)
demonstrate that mediating effects can be statistically insignificant depending on
contextual and institutional conditions. Second, the moderating or interactive role of
government support (Tri anto et al., 2025) and implementation capacity (Amoa -Gyarteng
et al., 2024) suggests that marketing innovation alone is insufficient without
complementary institutional and firm-level enabling conditions. Third, the reviewed studies
are geographically conc entrated in Southeast Asia, East Africa, West Africa (Ghana), and
Eastern Europe, with methodological approaches ranging from PLS -SEM and structural
equation modeling to systematic literature review —yet none employs a comparative or
Nigerian-specific empirical lens, particularly within agrarian, sub-national contexts such as
Taraba State. This geographic, sectoral, and methodological concentration constitutes the
empirical gap this study seeks to address, by empirically investigating the role of marketing
innovation, in enhancing MSME performance within Taraba State.

Methodology
This study adopted a quantitative survey design to examine the relationship between
marketing innovation and MSME performance in Taraba State, Nigeria. The state, with a
projected population of approximately 3.1 million in 2021 (National Bureau of Statistics,
2021), was selected due to its high concentration of MSMEs. The target population
comprised 953,821 MSME employees in Taraba State (SMEDAN & National Bureau of
Statistics, 2017).

The extremely high inter-predictor correlations (particularly DMI–BI = 0.969 and DMI–CRI =
0.940) indi cate substantial multicollinearity in the regression model. Researchers are
advised to examine Variance Inflation Factors (VIF) — values above 10 would indicate
problematic collinearity. Despite this, the overall model's predictive power (R² = 0.706) and
the significance of CRI, PI, and DMI suggest that the regression results are interpretable,
though individual beta coefficients should be interpreted with caution given the
suppression of BI's unique effect.
It should be emphasized that this level of collinearity is more severe than the framing above
suggests. While VIF = 10 is a commonly cited maximum threshold, several methodologists
regard VIF values above 5 -- and certainly the VIF = 9.64 observed for Branding Innovation
and VIF = 8.92 for Digital Market ing Innovation, corresponding to tolerance levels of only
0.104 and 0.112 respectively -- as indicating problematic collinearity that materially
destabilizes individual regression coefficients, even where the overall model remains
statistically significant. In practical terms, this means the non -significant unique effect of
Branding Innovation (β = -0.047, p = 0.709) should not be read as evidence that branding is
unimportant; rather, its near -collinearity with Digital Marketing Innovation (r = 0.969)
makes it statistically impossible for ordinary least squares regression to isolate the
independent contribution of either variable with confidence. Given this, the standardized
beta coefficients in Table 8 are best interpreted qualitatively (i.e., as indicating the general
ordering of predictors) rather than as precise, stable point estimates. Future analyses of this
dataset, or replications in similar contexts, would benefit from alternative analytical
treatments better suited to highly correlated predictors, such as ridge regression, principal
component regression, or partial least squares structural equation modelling (PLS -SEM),
which can accommodate multicollinearity without discarding theoretically important
predictors.

This study investigated the influence of marketing innovation strategies on the operational
performance of MSMEs in Taraba State, Nigeria. The findings provide empirical evidence
that marketing innovation represents a significant strategic capability for improving MSME
operational outcomes in an emerging economy context. Specifically, the results
demonstrate that digital marketing innovation, customer relationship innovation, and

innovation, although positively associated with performance, does not exert a significant
unique effect when other innovation dimensions are controlled. Collectively, the marketing
innovation dimensions explain 70.6% of the variance in operational perfor mance,
highlighting the strategic importance of integrating multiple marketing innovation
practices rather than relying on isolated initiatives.
The findings contribute to the growing body of literature that conceptualizes marketing
innovation as a multidi mensional capability involving technological adoption, customer
engagement, market communication, and value creation. Consistent with the Unified
Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT) proposed by Venkatesh et al. (2003),
the results suggest that MSMEs are more likely to achieve improved operational outcomes
when they perceive marketing technologies and innovative practices as useful, accessible,
socially supported, and supported by enabling conditions. Unlike traditional applications of
UTAUT that primarily focus on behavioural intention and technology adoption, this study
extends the framework by demonstrating how the actual utilization of marketing
innovation practices translates into operational performance improvements.

Digital Marketing Innovation and Operational Performance
The findings indicate that digital marketing innovation has a significant positive effect on
operational performance (β = 0.256, p = 0.044), confirming the strategic importance of
digital capabilities among MSMEs. This suggests that enterprises adopting digital
platforms, mobile communication tools, and online promotional channels achieve
improved market accessibility, customer interaction, and operational efficiency.
This finding corroborates Makhmudov et al. (2026), who established that digital marketing
enhances customer engagement through real -time communication, personalization, and
improved market intelligence. Similarly, Elkhoudary et al. (2026) identified digital

competitiveness and innovation performance. The result also supports Singh and Sharma
(2026) and Eshbayev et al. (2024), who demonstrated that digital marketing reduces
information asymmetry and expands market opportunities for small businesses.
The significant influence of digital marketing innovation can be explained through the
UTAUT dimensions of performance expectancy and facilitating conditions. MSMEs in
Taraba State appear to adopt digital marketing tools because they p erceive them as
relatively affordable mechanisms for expanding customer reach and improving business
visibility. In resource -constrained environments, digital platforms provide a low -cost
alternative to conventional marketing channels, thereby enabling MSM Es to overcome
geographical and financial limitations.

Conclusion, Implications, Limitations, and Future Research
Conclusion
This study examined the effect of marketing innovation on the operational performance of
Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) in Taraba State, Nigeria, operationalizing
marketing innovation through four dimensions —Digital Marketing Innovation (DMI),
Branding Innovation (BI ), Customer Relationship Innovation (CRI), and Promotional
Innovation (PI) —and measuring operational performance across indicators of efficiency,
productivity, cost performance, delivery reliability, customer satisfaction, and revenue
growth.
The findings unequivocally establish that marketing innovation is a powerful and
statistically robust driver of operational performance among MSMEs  in Taraba State,
collectively accounting for 70.6% of the variance in operational outcomes [F(4, 345) =
207.55, p < 0.00 1]. Three of the four innovation dimensions —Customer Relationship
Innovation (β = 0.363), Promotional Innovation (β = 0.295), and Digital Marketing
Innovation (β = 0.256) —emerged as significant independent predictors. Branding
Innovation, while non -significant in the multivariate context due to high multicollinearity
with DMI (r = 0.969), demonstrated a strong bivariate association with operational
performance (r = 0.795, p < 0.001), confirming its substantive relevance within the broader
innovation ecosystem.
Critically, the study establishes that marketing innovation in the MSME context is not a set
of independent practices but rather an integrated, co -evolving system of capabilities.
Digital marketing facilitates brand communication; branding reinforces customer trust;
customer relationship management sustains promotional effectiveness; and promotional
innovation generates the demand visibility that validates and incentivizes continued
innovation. This systemic character explains both the high inter-predictor correlations
observed and the substantial joint explanatory power of the model.
The findings also confirm the applicability of UTAUT (Venkatesh et al., 2003) as a
theoretical lens for explaining marketing innovation adoption in MSMEs. Perfo rmance

Theoretical Implications
This study contributes to knowledge by conceptualizing marketing innovation as a
multidimensional construct comprising digital, branding, customer relationship, and
promotional innovation, thereby providing a m ore context -specific understanding for
MSMEs. It also extends the application of the UTAUT framework by linking marketing
innovation adoption to operational performance outcomes.Furthermore,the finding that
customer relationship innovation is the strongest  predictor of operational performance
highlights the importance of customer -centered strategies over purely technological
approaches. The study also emphasizes the need for appropriate analytical methods when
examining highly related innovation dimensions.

Managerial Implications
The findings have important implications for MSMEs, industry associations, and
policymakers in Taraba State and similar contexts.
For MSME Owners and Managers: Given that Customer Relationship Innovation was the
strongest unique predictor (β = 0.363), managers with limited resources should prioritize
low-cost relationship -building practices first -- structured customer feedback routines,
prompt complaint handling, and WhatsApp/SMS -based follow -up -- before investi ng
further in stand -alone advertising spend. Promotional Innovation (β = 0.295) is the next
most reliable lever and should be pursued through targeted, low -cost promotions
(discounts, social media campaigns) rather than broad, undirected advertising. Digit al

collinear with Branding Innovation (r = 0.969) in this sample, managers should treat digital
platform adoption and brand -building as a single integrated investment  rather than two
separate budget lines -- for example, using digital channels themselves as the vehicle for
consistent branding (uniform packaging, logos, and messaging across WhatsApp,
Facebook, and Instagram) rather than funding branding and digital marketing as unrelated
activities.

Limitations of the Study
This study has some limitations. The cross -sectional design restricts causal interpretation
between marketing innovation and operational performance. The study was limited to five
Local Government Areas in Taraba State, which may affect the generalizability of the
findings. In addition, high correlations among some in novation dimensions reduced the
precision of individual predictor estimates. The use of self-reported data may also introduce
response bias, while the study focused mainly on MSME operators, excluding the
perspectives of customers and supply chain partners.

Recommendations for Future Research
Future studies should employ longitudinal designs to establish causality and use Structural
Equation Modeling (SEM) to better examine relationships among variables. Comparative
studies across different regions, investigation of moderating variables, and mixed-method
approaches are also recommended. Additionally, future research should incorporate
customer perspectives and sector-specific analyses for broader understanding.
