Abstract
This study examines how linguistic cues in online review text are associated with the social value of online reviews. Using
more than 172,000 Yelp reviews of leading quick-service restaurant (QSR) brands in the United States, we examine how
sentiment polarity and social language are associated with social engagement, measured through Cool votes. We find that
positive sentiment is associated with more Cool votes, whereas negative sentiment is associated with fewer Cool votes.
Higher levels of social language are also positively associated with Cool votes. Comparing these findings with information
value, measured through Useful votes, reveals a valence asymmetry: positive sentiment is associated with greater social
value but lower informational value, whereas negative sentiment shows the opposite pattern, being associated with greater
informational value but lower social value. Overall, these findings demonstrate that sentiment valence is differentially
associated with the social and informational value of online reviews. The study contributes to research on online review
engagement by identifying valence asymmetry as a key feature of how sentiment relates to different forms of review value
and offers implications for digital marketing strategy, review platform design, and customer experience management in
the QSR sector.

systems into sophisticated social ecosystems in which con -
sumers engage with content through distinct voting mecha-
nisms. Yelp exemplifies this evolution by allowing users to
evaluate reviews as “useful,” reflecting informational value,
or “cool,” reflecting social value. This distinction suggests
that reviews may serve fundamentally different functions
for audiences seeking information versus social engage -
ment (Yin et al. 2017). Although prior research has exam -

For empirical analysis, we use more than 172,000 Yelp

with greater information value (i.e., greater number of help-

cues receive significantly more cool votes, highlighting the
association between socially oriented narratives and con -
sumer social engagement.
This study contributes to the online review literature in
several theoretical and empirical ways. First, it advances
a valence asymmetry perspective by showing that senti -
ment expressions are associated with distinct dimensions of
review value. Specifically, positive sentiment is associated
with greater social value, as reflected in cool votes, whereas
negative sentiment is associated with greater informational
value. Second, the study extends research on review lan -
guage by demonstrating that socially oriented cues in
review text are associated with greater social engagement.
Together, these findings highlight that online reviews serve
multiple functions beyond information exchange and that
the linguistic characteristics associated with informational
value are not necessarily the same as those associated with
social value.
The findings also offer practical implications for quick-
service restaurant (QSR) brand managers, review platform
designers, and customer experience professionals. The
results suggest that online reviews serve different purposes:
negative reviews provide valuable diagnostic information
for prospective customers, whereas positive reviews are
associated with greater social engagement. Recognizing this
distinction may help firms develop more targeted review
management strategies rather than treating all reviews uni -
formly. In addition, the findings suggest that cool votes
capture a distinct dimension of review value beyond infor -

an additional indicator of socially engaging content. More
broadly, these insights can inform review curation, cus -
tomer engagement strategies, and platform design decisions
that address both the informational and social functions of
online reviews.
Conceptual framework
Research has demonstrated that consumers not only value
information but also hedonic or social engagement from
the online review content (Yin et al. 2017; Hazari et al.
2017). Platforms such as Yelp provide the functionality to
let the prospective consumer express their evaluation about
a review content by enabling “useful”, “cool” and “funny”
features. While the useful vote describes the utilitarian or
informational value of the review (Mudambi and Schuff
2010; Yin et al. 2017), the cool vote has been described to
have hedonic value (Fan-Osuala 2023). Although no exact
definition of a “cool vote” has been established in the litera-
ture, recent studies have identified dimensions of coolness
in relation to people, brands, and concepts (Dar-Nimrod et
al. 2012; Warren and Campbell 2014; Warren et al. 2019).
What is coolness?
Coolness itself can be defined as a socially desirable qual -
ity characterized by autonomy, originality, nonconformity,
and aesthetic appeal among other qualities, often evoking
admiration (Warren and Campbell 2014). In relation to peo-
ple, Pezzuti et al. (2025) identified six traits of cool people:
extraversion, hedonism, power, adventurousness, open -
ness, and autonomy. When coolness relates to brands, the
sub-dimensions are extraordinary, aesthetically appealing,
energetic, high-status, rebellious, original, authentic, sub -
cultural, iconic, and popular (Warren et al. 2019). Warren
and Campbell ( 2014) argue that brand coolness is closely
tied to status, autonomy, or belonging in social contexts
and that a brand is seen as a cool brand when it deviates
from social norms, but only appropriately. The similar argu-
ment is seen in other studies; Rahman ( 2013) indicates that
some products are consumed as “social tools” to signal
“coolness” in social context. An empirical investigation of
coolness reveals that coolnesss is primarily driven by “Per -
ceptions of peer-relevant social desirability” (Dar-Nimrod
et al. 2012). The study also indicates that social desirability
lies at the core of the coolness construct with a contrarian
factor contributing to overall likability. Overall, coolness is
not an intrinsic property, rather a positive social perception
of a brand, people, object or concept (Warren et al. 2019).
Supporting this view, social desirability and coolness rat -
ing are shown to be strongly correlated (Dar-Nimrod et al.
2012). In online consumer reviews context, Fan-Osuala
(2023) indicated that the reviews that contain the charac -
teristics or subdivisions of coolness are perceived as cool

Lee (2018) examined reviewer and review characteristics

review length, and images) as predictors of Yelp’s Useful,
Funny, and Cool votes and found that reviewer network size
and review length were significant predictors of Cool votes

Osuala ( 2023) focused on perceived review coolness and
found that reviews using negative words to express posi -
tive product views, as well as reviews containing pictorial
images, were perceived as cooler. Building on this research,
we examine how sentiment polarity (positive and negative
relative to neutral sentiment) and social language are associ-
ated with both Useful and Cool votes. In doing so, we exam-
ine whether sentiment valence is asymmetrically associated
with the informational and social value of online reviews.
Valence asymmetry and votes
Prior research has extensively examined the factors asso -
ciated with review usefulness and consumers’ decision-
making processes (Hong et al. 2017; Mudambi and Schuff
2010; Siering et al. 2018; Pan and Zhang 2011). A consis-
tent finding in this literature is that negative reviews tend
to be perceived as more helpful and valuable than positive
reviews (Chen and Lurie 2013; Li et al. 2020; Sen and Ler-
man 2007). This phenomenon is consistent with the well-
established negativity bias, whereby negative information is
often perceived as more diagnostic and utilitarian compared
to positive information (Baumeister et al. 2001; Eslami et
al. 2018). In online review contexts, negative reviews often
provide diagnostic cues about potential risks or product
shortcomings, making them particularly relevant for infor -
mational evaluations. Prior research further suggests that
reviews containing negative sentiment receive more helpful
(or useful) votes than reviews containing neutral or positive
sentiment (Chen and Lurie 2013; Li et al. 2020; Sen and
Lerman 2007).
However, consumers may evaluate online reviews
based on multiple dimensions beyond informational value.
Another stream of research suggests that consumers seek
both utilitarian and hedonic or social value from online
reviews (Hazari et al. 2017). While prior research has estab-
lished the relationship between negative sentiment and
informational value, less is known about whether sentiment
valence corresponds with different forms of review evalua -
tion. Specifically, positive and negative sentiment may not
operate in the same manner across informational and social
dimensions of review value. This possibility suggests a
valence asymmetry perspective, whereby different forms of
sentiment expression may be associated with distinct types
of consumer responses.
Coolness represents an important dimension of social
value in online environments and is associated with enthu -
siasm, energy, and hedonic appeal (Pezzuti et al. 2025;
Warren et al. 2019). Positive reviews often communicate
excitement and enjoyment regarding a product or service,
which may correspond with greater emotional engage -
ment among readers. According to the Emotions as Social
Information (EASI) framework, emotional expressions can
influence observers’ attitudes and responses through social
signaling processes (Van Kleef 2009). Expressions of hap -
piness and enthusiasm are often associated with greater
liking and relational responses among observers, whereas
negative emotional expressions may be associated with less
favorable social reactions. In the context of online reviews,
positive sentiment may therefore be associated with greater
perceived coolness by signaling enthusiasm and hedonic
value, whereas negative sentiment may be associated with
lower social appreciation.
Taken together, we propose a valence asymmetry per -
spective in online review evaluation. Rather than assuming
that positive and negative sentiment have similar relation -
ships with all forms of consumer response, we suggest that
sentiment valence may correspond differently with informa-
tional and social value. Negative sentiment is expected to be
more closely associated with informational value because
of its diagnostic characteristics, whereas positive senti -
ment is expected to be more closely associated with social
value because of its expressive and hedonic characteristics.
Accordingly, while negative sentiment may be valued for
decision-related information, positive sentiment may be
valued for its social appeal. Thus, we examine whether
positive and negative sentiment exhibit distinct associations
with cool votes, representing social value. Therefore, we
posit that:
H1a: Reviews with positive sentiment will receive more cool
votes than reviews with neutral sentiment.
H1b: Reviews with negative sentiment will receive fewer
cool votes than reviews with neutral sentiment.
Social content and cool votes
As discussed previously, social desirability and coolness
ratings are strongly correlated (Dar-Nimrod et al. 2012).
Extending this concept to review content, reviews contain -
ing social content may be associated with greater perceptions

of coolness by signaling social cues and interpersonal expe-
riences (Warren and Campbell 2014; Warren et al. 2019).
Prior research suggests that social cues shape how consum-
ers interpret and respond to review content beyond its infor-
mational value. Consumer responses are not solely based on
information quality but are also associated with the social
context in which reviews are embedded (Moe and Trusov
2011). Social cues presented through images or textual
expressions have been shown to be associated with con -
sumers’ emotional responses and engagement with online
content (Poirier et al. 2024). Similarly, identity cues, which
can also function as social cues, have been associated with
greater perceived emotional and informational support and
review credibility, particularly for positive reviews (Li and
Liang 2022). Taken together, these findings suggest that
reviews containing stronger social cues may be associated
with greater social engagement.
Moreover, when a product review contains social con -
tent, it may take the form of a narrative involving charac -
ters, events, and interactions, which can encourage readers’
mental simulation of the experience (Van Laer et al. 2019).
Contemporary marketing strategies involving consumer
brand storytelling have been associated with consumers’
emotional and cognitive responses (Hong et al. 2022).
This process is grounded in narrative transportation theory
(Green and Brock 2000), which suggests that audiences
become immersed in stories, resulting in greater enjoy -
ment (Green & Brock 2004) and persuasion (Van Laer et al.
2014). Accordingly, reviews containing social content may
be perceived as cooler because they provide readers with
greater enjoyment and social relevance, regardless of senti-
ment valence.
Therefore, product reviews containing social content
(e.g., interactions, events involving others, and descriptions
of socially situated product experiences) are expected to be
associated with greater coolness perceptions. We hypoth -
esize that:
H2: Product reviews containing social events or interac -
tions will receive more cool votes than reviews without such
content.

Data descriptives
The dataset reveals substantial variation in both sentiment
characteristics and engagement outcomes. As shown in
Table 1, the mean V ADER compound sentiment score is
0.191 (SD = 0.722), indicating a slight overall positive skew
in QSR reviews. Approximately 57.4% of reviews are clas-

cool votes (SD = 1.652), indicating that most reviews gar -

ability across the sample.

The study hypothesized that reviews expressing posi -
tive sentiment would receive more cool votes than neutral
reviews (H1a), whereas reviews expressing negative senti -
ment would receive fewer cool votes than neutral reviews
(H1b). As reported in Table 7, the results indicate a signifi -
cant positive association between positive sentiment and
social value. Reviews with positive sentiment received sig -
nificantly more cool votes than neutral reviews (β = 0.422,
p < 0.01), supporting H1a. In contrast, reviews expressing
negative sentiment received significantly fewer cool votes
than neutral reviews (β = − 0.084, p < 0.05), supporting H1b.
These findings suggest that sentiment valence is differen -
tially associated with social value, with positive sentiment
corresponding to greater social appeal and negative senti -
ment corresponding to lower levels of social appreciation.
For better interpretation, we computed incident rate ratio
(IRR). Positive sentiment is associated with approximately
52.50% more cool votes relative to neutral sentiment,
whereas negative sentiment is associated with approximately
8.05% fewer cool votes. The substantially larger positive-
sentiment association indicates that social value is more
strongly aligned with expressions of positive sentiment than
the extent to which negative sentiment detracts from social
value. In other words, positive sentiment appears to have a
stronger association with social appreciation than negative
sentiment has with social disengagement. This asymmetric
pattern is consistent with the proposed valence-asymmetry
perspective.

The study further hypothesized that social language

sistent with expectations, the coefficient for social language
is positive and significant (β = 0.005, p < 0.01), supporting
H2. Together, these results indicate that sentiment polar -

review’s social value, with positive sentiment and socially
oriented language corresponding to higher levels of social
engagement.
As shown in Table 4, social language is negatively bivar-

rience, while both variables are positively associated with
cool votes. Thus, the negative bivariate association between
social language and cool votes may partly reflect shared
variance with other predictors of social engagement. After
accounting for these factors and other review, reviewer,
business, and geographic characteristics, the association
between social language and cool votes became positive.
This pattern suggests that socially oriented language is posi-
tively associated with cool votes, though the effect size is
small, above and beyond the contribution of sentiment and
reviewer characteristics.
We next discuss the results for the control variables.
Review length is positively associated with both useful
votes (β = 0.362, p < 0.01) and cool votes (β = 0.278, p <
0.01), indicating that longer reviews receive higher levels
of both informational and social engagement. Review age
exhibits contrasting relationships with the two engage -
ment outcomes. Older reviews receive more useful votes
(β = 0.118, p < 0.01), consistent with the idea that informa -
tional value accumulates over time as consumers continue
to consult reviews for purchase decisions. In contrast, older
reviews receive fewer cool votes (β = − 0.060, p < 0.01)
after controlling for other factors. This finding suggests that
social value may be more closely associated with immediacy
and contemporaneous engagement, whereas informational
value remains relevant over a longer period. Accordingly,
reviews that are newer may be more likely to attract social
appreciation, while older reviews continue to serve as useful
sources of information.

important contextual factor rather than as a central theoreti-

Discussion
This study hypothesized that reviews with positive sentiment
would receive more cool votes than neutral reviews (H1a),
whereas reviews with negative sentiment would receive
fewer cool votes than neutral reviews (H1b). Together with
the established relationship between negative sentiment and
useful votes, these hypotheses propose a valence asymmetry
perspective, suggesting that positive and negative sentiment
are differently associated with informational and social
value in online reviews. The study further hypothesized
that reviews containing social language would receive more
cool votes than reviews without such content (H2).
Analyzing over 172,000 Yelp reviews of quick-service
restaurant (QSR) brands, the findings support the proposed
valence asymmetry. Consistent with prior research, nega -
tive sentiment was associated with greater useful votes,
reflecting higher informational value. In contrast, positive
sentiment was associated with significantly more cool votes
(supporting H1a), whereas negative sentiment was associ -
ated with fewer cool votes than neutral reviews (supporting
H1b). Taken together, these findings indicate that positive
and negative sentiment are associated with different dimen-
sions of review value: negative sentiment corresponds more
closely with informational value, whereas positive senti -
ment corresponds more closely with social value.
The study further found support for H2. Reviews contain-
ing social language, received significantly more cool votes
than reviews without such content. This finding suggests
that socially oriented language is associated with greater
social engagement and extends prior research by identify -
ing social language as an important correlate of perceived
review coolness.
Overall, the findings provide empirical support for the
proposition that consumers seek both utilitarian and hedonic
value from online consumer reviews. While negative senti -
ment is more closely associated with informational value,
positive sentiment and socially oriented language are more
closely associated with social value. Together, these find -
ings demonstrate that informational usefulness and social
desirability represent distinct dimensions of online review
evaluation.

focused on review helpfulness, emphasizing the associa -

positive and negative sentiment are differently associated

associated with informational value, whereas positive senti-
ment is more closely associated with social value. This per-
spective extends existing work by suggesting that sentiment
valence does not operate uniformly across different forms of
review evaluation.
Second, the study identifies social language as an impor-
tant correlate of social value. Reviews framed around inter-
personal interactions and shared experiences are associated
with higher levels of cool votes, suggesting that socially
oriented language is relevant to how consumers evaluate
reviews in social contexts. This finding extends prior work
on linguistic cues by demonstrating that relational and
experiential language is associated with social engagement
beyond informational evaluation.
Third, by focusing on cool votes as a measure of social
value, the study broadens the conceptualization of online
review evaluation. Prior research has predominantly relied
on helpful votes as the primary indicator of review value,
implicitly emphasizing informational usefulness. The pres -
ent findings suggest that cool votes capture a distinct dimen-
sion of consumer evaluation reflecting social and expressive
value. Together, these findings indicate that linguistic char-
acteristics associated with informational value are not nec -
essarily the same as those associated with social value,
underscoring the importance of distinguishing between
multiple dimensions of review evaluation in future research.
Practical implications
The findings offer several implications for QSR managers
and online review platform designers. First, the results sug-
gest that positive sentiment is associated with greater social
engagement, whereas negative sentiment is associated with
greater informational value. Accordingly, managers should
recognize that online reviews serve different communica -
tion purposes rather than treating all reviews uniformly.
Reviews expressing positive experiences may foster cus -
tomer engagement and community interaction, whereas
critical reviews provide valuable diagnostic information for
prospective customers. Response strategies may therefore
be tailored according to the role a review appears to serve.
For example, managers may leverage positive reviews
to reinforce brand advocacy and customer engagement
while treating negative reviews as opportunities to address
consumer concerns and provide information that may ben -
efit future customers.
Second, the findings suggest that review platforms and
firms may benefit from recognizing informational and social
value as distinct dimensions of review evaluation. Rather
than relying exclusively on helpful votes to identify valu -
able content, platform designers may also consider cool
votes as indicators of socially engaging reviews. Incorporat-
ing both dimensions into review ranking, recommendation,
or filtering systems may better accommodate the diverse
ways consumers use online reviews.
Third, although the estimated association between social
language and cool votes is modest, the findings suggest
that reviews containing interpersonal and socially oriented
language are associated with slightly greater social engage-
ment. Managers encouraging customer-generated content
may therefore consider prompts that invite customers to
describe shared experiences, interactions with others, or
memorable consumption occasions. Such prompts may
encourage richer narratives that resonate socially with read-
ers, while recognizing that sentiment polarity remains the
stronger predictor of cool votes.
Overall, the findings suggest that informational useful -
ness and social engagement represent complementary rather
than competing dimensions of online reviews. Recognizing
these distinct forms of value may help firms and review
platforms develop more effective review management, con-
tent curation, and customer engagement strategies.
Methodological contributions
This study contributes methodologically by emphasizing
the importance of examining social engagement metrics
in addition to traditional measures of review helpfulness.
While prior research has focused primarily on useful votes,
the present study demonstrates that cool votes capture a dis-
tinct dimension of consumer response.
In addition, the use of V ADER sentiment analysis in
combination with LIWC-based social language measures
provides a scalable approach to analyzing linguistic features
in large-scale review data. This approach enables a more
nuanced understanding of how different aspects of review
text influence social engagement.
Limitations and future research directions
Several limitations provide opportunities for future research.
First, the analysis focuses on QSR reviews in the United
States, which may limit generalizability. Moreover, the
distribution of reviews across brands and states is uneven,
reflecting differences in Yelp usage and review activ -
ity. Future research could examine whether the observed

Social value in online reviews: the role of sentiment and social language in consumer engagement
patterns hold across different industries, more balanced
samples, and broader geographic and cultural contexts. Sec-
ond, this study is limited by its reliance on Yelp reviews.
Although Yelp provides a suitable setting for examining
social and informational engagement, review behaviors and
engagement mechanisms may vary across platforms. Future
research could examine data from multiple platforms, such
as Google Reviews and food delivery platforms, to assess
the generalizability of these findings and explore how plat -
form characteristics influence consumer engagement with
reviews.
Third, online reviews often contain additional content,
such as tags (e.g., vegan, family-friendly), emojis, pho -
tos, videos, and other multimedia elements. The present
study focuses primarily on sentiment valence asymmetry
and social language. Future research should examine how
these additional forms of review content contribute to social
value, as reflected in cool votes. Fourth, the study adopts a
cross-sectional design and does not account for temporal or
reviewer-level effects. Longitudinal research could explore
how social engagement with reviews evolves over time.
Fifth, although Yelp’s “Funny” vote may capture humor
appreciation and engagement, prior research distinguishes it
from “Cool,” which is more closely associated with social or
expressive value (Bakhshi et al. 2014; Bakhshi et al. 2015).
Accordingly, we treat Funny as a conceptually distinct form
of engagement and do not include it in the valence asymme-
try examined in this study. Future research could extend this
framework by incorporating Funny votes alongside Useful
and Cool votes to examine whether sentiment and rhetori -
cal features are differentially associated with these distinct
forms of consumer engagement. Although Funny votes may
be associated with relatively negative tone, their relation -
ship with review valence is not uniform across contexts
(Bakhshi et al. 2014; Lee 2018).
Sixth, this study has a measurement limitation regarding
LIWC Social category. The category is broad and may be
extract routine references to staff, customers, pronouns, and
other social actors, rather than explicit relational language.
Accordingly, the variable should be interpreted as reflecting
people-oriented social references in review content and not
as evidence of deep social connection or the depth of the
quality of the relationship. Seventh, a limitation of this study
is that V ADER is a lexicon-based sentiment tool and may
not fully capture sarcasm or other nuanced (mixed) expres -
sions. Future research could test whether similar results
hold with transformer-based sentiment methods and labeled
validation data. Finally, the correlational nature of the find-
ings limits causal interpretation, suggesting the need for
experimental approaches to establish causal mechanisms.
