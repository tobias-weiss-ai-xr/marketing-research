Contemporary organizations operate under an unprecedented dynamic
of social, economic, and technological change. Globalization, the digital-
ization of managerial processes, and the development of artificial intelli-
gence, coupled with growing geopolitical and economic uncertainty, mean
that effective management is no longer solely the domain of economic,
financial, or organizational knowledge. It is becoming increasingly evident
that sound managerial decisions rest on an understanding of human
beings – their emotions, motivations, cognitive limitations, and needs
related to security, dignity, and belonging. It is precisely this perspective –
combining theoretical reflection with practical experience – that forms
the guiding thread of this monograph, prepared on the basis of the 6th
Scientific Conference New Vectors of Management: Behavioral Economics
for Conscious and Diverse Organizations,  held as part of the series
The Digital Society and Concepts of Organizational Management.
As Daniel Kahneman observed, dispelling the myth of the perfectly
rational consumer, in his book Thinking, Fast and Slow: Our thinking
is far more prone to error and far more susceptible to emotion than we
tend to assume.  This observation, formulated over a decade ago, has
gained renewed relevance today. Behavioral economics, by integrating
the achievements of cognitive and social psychology with classical
economic theory, provides tools for better understanding decision-making
processes, social relations, and the mechanisms underlying the functioning
of contemporary organizations. It also teaches that the organizations of
the future should be built on a foundation of empathy, diversity, responsi-
bility, and conscious leadership – values that run through all the chapters
of this monograph.
The structure of the monograph reflects the multifaceted nature of the
issues addressed – ranging from risk and digitalization, through artifi -
cial intelligence, legal regulation, geopolitics, and marketing, to diversity,
employee well-being, and leadership from a cross-cultural perspective.

The opening chapter, by M. Barna and Z. Novosad, is devoted to risk
management in the context of digitalization. The authors present a compar-
ative analysis of two methods used to support decision-making under
conditions of uncertainty – correlation and regression analysis, and expert
assessment methods – highlighting their methodological foundations,
strengths, and limitations in application to higher education. On the basis
of a survey study, they identify the need to combine study with work as
the most significant educational challenge.
In the second chapter, A. Dağdeviren addresses the accessibility of
infrastructure for people with disabilities in the context of the Blue Flag
programme criteria, examining its application on beaches in Türkiye.
The author points to the need to ensure a continuous, safe route from
parking areas to the water, as well as regular monitoring of infrastructure
compliance with technical requirements, situating the chapter within
a broader reflection on the inclusivity of public space.
The chapter by J. Kloc shifts the discussion to artificial intelligence and
behavioral management. The author presents the theoretical foundations
of behavioral management and analyzes the opportunities and challenges
associated with the use of machine learning algorithms, predictive analytics,
and recommendation systems in monitoring and shaping employee behav-
ior. A significant part of the discussion is devoted to the ethical dimension
of these solutions – the protection of privacy, algorithmic transparency,
and the risk of decision-making bias. The chapter’s conclusion, pointing
to the need to maintain a balance between organizational effectiveness
and respect for individual autonomy, resonates with the central theme of
the entire monograph.
M. Nowikowska examines the relationship between legal regulation and
organizational culture as determinants of the effectiveness of compliance
systems. The author shows that the effectiveness of mechanisms ensuring
an organization’s compliance with the law and ethical standards does not
stem solely from the quality of control procedures, but to a significant
extent from the organizational culture within which they operate. It is

K. Palonka-Pulikowska addresses the issue of geopolitics and its grow-

United States and China. The chapter raises the fundamental question of
the limits within which a contemporary market economy can function in
the face of the growing politicization of economic decisions.
M. Róg-Giacosa shifts the discussion to the level of business relation -
ships, analyzing the determinants of exhibitors’ perceived value of partici-
pation in B2B trade fairs in a declining industry, using the example of the
Kids’ Time International Fair in Kielce. Using exploratory factor analysis,
correlation analysis, and multiple linear regression, the author demon -
strates that exhibitors’ marketing capabilities – rather than the quality
of event organization – have a stronger effect on the business outcomes
achieved, although organizational quality remains important for the overall
evaluation of the event and for exhibitors’ willingness to recommend it.
A. Banaszak addresses the behavioral shaping of attitudes toward old
age as a tool for building inclusive organizations. Using the example of an
original workshop programme, Understanding Old Age, which employs
a geriatric-experience simulator, the author analyzes the mechanisms
through which experiential education reduces ageism and stereotypes
concerning older people. The chapter emphasizes the role of direct expe-
rience of the limitations associated with the aging process in fostering
empathy and intergenerational solidarity.

W . Bobisz addresses the topic of mobbing as a contemporary threat in
the workplace, a subject of considerable importance for human resource
management. The author points out that a rapidly changing labor market,
pressure to meet performance targets, and evolving management styles
all contribute to the intensification of pathological behaviors, the conse-
quences of which affect not only employees’ physical and mental health
but also workplace atmosphere, staff turnover, and companies’ financial
performance.
L. Weldemichael presents the results of a study on the impact of loyalty
programmes on passenger satisfaction in the airline industry. Amid strong
competition from Middle Eastern and European carriers, the author
examines how individual elements of a loyalty programme influence
customer satisfaction.
H. Weldemichael, in turn, addresses the role of strategic management in
enhancing the effectiveness of Ethiopian civil society and non-governmen-
tal organizations. The author examines these organizations’ managerial
practices, the relationship between strategic planning and effectiveness,
the role of leadership, and the barriers that hinder the implementation of
strategic management in the non-governmental sector.

The monograph concludes with a chapter by J. Nowicka, devoted to the
analysis and comparison of logistics processes implemented at selected
historic sites in the Wałbrzych Agglomeration. The chapter examines
information flows, deliveries, warehousing, transportation, and stake -
holder service. Drawing on statistical methods, the author highlights
the importance of historical logistics practices both for the preservation
of cultural heritage and for the improvement of contemporary logistics
management practices.
Although the chapters included in the monograph vary in terms
of subject matter, methodology, and geographical scope, they share
a common concern with understanding the mechanisms underlying
human behavior in organizations and identifying ways to develop struc-
tures that are more aware, responsible, and inclusive. They contribute
to the broader field of behavioral economics, understood not merely as
a subdiscipline of economics but as an epistemological perspective that
integrates insights from psychology, law, marketing, and management.
