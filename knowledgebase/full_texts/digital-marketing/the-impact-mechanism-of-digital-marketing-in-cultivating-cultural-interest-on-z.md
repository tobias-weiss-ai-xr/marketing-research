Abstract: As the Z generation gradually becomes the main group of cultural tourism consumption, how to effectively attract
this group through digital marketing methods has become an important issue faced by cultural institutions. This study takes the
digital marketing practice of the Palace Museum as a case study , focusing on the Z generation in China and exploring the
relationship between their digital exposure, cultural interest formation, and tourism participation behavior. The research
integrates the use and satisfaction theory and the customer eng agement theory. Through a questionnaire survey of 208 Chinese
Generation Z respondents, reliability and validity tests, correlation analysis, and other methods were used to empirically test the
hypotheses. The results indicate that the algorithm recommended short videos and graphic content platforms are the main entry
points for Generation Z to obtain cultural information. Digital exposure and cultural interest significantly positively predict travel
intention and cultural and creative product purchase intention, with cultural interest having the strongest explanatory power. The
intention of social sharing plays a partial mediating role in both influencing paths, and its role is more prominent in the purchasing
intention path. This study suggests that cultural institutions should attach importance to the construction of platform native
content, prioritize cultivating audience cultural interests rat her than simply expanding exposure, enhance the shareability of
content, and develop differentiated content strategies for different groups within Generation Z. The research provides reference
directions for future longitudinal tracking, cross-border comparison, and multi case design.
Keywords: Digital Marketing; Cultural Tourism; Generation Z; Customer Engagement.

1. Introduction
The popularization of mobile and Internet technologies is
reshaping the way of communication between cultural
tourism destinations and audien ces. Printed advertisements,
brochures, and interpersonal recommendations are giving
way to social media, short video platforms, and virtual
exhibitions. Digital marketing has not only changed the way
tourists obtain destination information, but also the way they
form intentions and consume, making it a core force in the
cultural and tourism market. Museums and other cultural
institutions are increasingly relying on digital marketing to
reach their target audience, enhance cultural dissemination,
and maintain competitiveness.
These dynamics have the most profound impact on
Generation Z (born between 1997 and 2012). As "digital
natives" who have grown up in ubiquitous network
connections, Generation Z exhibits extremely high media
literacy, strong platform dependence, and unique
consumption logic [1] and is  becoming the main force of
cultural consumption [2]. Therefore, how to stimulate their
cultural interest, travel intention, and consumption through
digital marketing has become a c rucial core issue in cultural
tourism management.
This study takes the Palace Museum as a typical case,
aiming to comprehensively analyze the driving mechanism
and profound impact of digital marketing on the cultural
tourism participation of Generation Z in China.
2. Literature Review and Hypotheses
2.1. Use and Satisfaction Theory: Exposure
and Cultural Interest (UGT)
The Use and Gratification Theory (UGT) suggests that
audiences actively choose specific media content to meet their
own needs [3]. In the context of cultural and tourism
digitalization, digital marketing can stimulate positive
psychological tendencies among audiences by satisfying their
four major gratitude factors of information, entertainment,
social interaction, and identity recognition, specifically
manifested as an increase in cultural interest. Based on this,
the following assumptions are proposed:
H1. The frequency of exposure to digital marketing at the
Palace Museum is positively co rrelated with the increased
cultural interest of Generation Z.
2.2. Consumer Participation Theory: Direct
and Direct Driven Effects (CET)
The Consumer Engagement Theory (CET) views
participation as a multidimensional psychological investment
of cognition, emotion, and behavior [4]. In the integrated
model of motivation participation behavior, the frequency of
digital marketing exposure plays a direct information effect,
while cultural interests cultivated through digital media are
the most direct psychological driving force for subsequent
tourism and consumption behavior. Based on this, the
following hypothesis is proposed:
H2. Increased cultural interest is positively correlated with

14
(a) travel intention and (b) purchase intention of cultural and
creative products.
H3. The frequency of exposure to digital marketing is
positively correlated with (a) travel intention and (b) purchase
intention of cultural and creative products.
2.3. The Mediating Role of Social Sharing
In the consumer participation chain, there is an
interdependent relationship between the various dimensions
of behavioral participation. S ocial sharing, as a highly
interactive participation behavior, can further amplify and
guide actual cultural and tourism consumption and on-site
travel intentions through network diffusion and peer influence
[5]. Therefore, social sharing plays a crucial bridging role in
the entire transformation mechanism. Based on this, the
following hypothesis is proposed:
H4. Social sharing intention plays a mediating role in the
influence of "contact frequency" and "cultural interest" on
"travel intention".
H5. Social sharing intention plays a mediating role in the
influence of "contact frequency" and "cultural interest" on
"cultural and creative product purchase intention".
2.4. Within Generation Heterogeneity of
Generation Z
Although Generation Z shares the label of 'digital natives'
at a macro level, this group spans a time span of up to 15 years.
This means that individuals with  different birth years within
the group have significant differences in the process of digital
socialization, media dependen ce, and actual life stages, and
their cultural identity and consumption logic are not
completely homogeneous [1]. Based on this, the following
hypothesis is proposed:
H6. There are significant differences in cultural interests
and behavioral intentions (travel, purchasing, sharing) among
different birth generations (groups) of Generation Z.
3. Research Methodology
The study adopts a single case design based on quantitative
research, with the Palace Museum as a typical case [6]. The
reason for choosing the Palace Museum is that it is both the
flagship institution of Chinese cultural tourism and a pioneer
in digital marketing innovation. Its marketing practices are
highly compatible with the media habits of Generation Z,
which can fill the previous research's neglect of cultural
institution contexts and meet the heterogeneous needs of
tourists.
The study used purposive sampling (non probability
sampling) method [7] with the target population being
Chinese Generation Z (born between 1997 and 2012). The
questionnaire is managed online through the Qualtrics
platform, and data collection takes about two weeks. It is
distributed through social media platforms, cultural and
tourism interest communities, and researchers' personal
networks to enhance sample diversity. After data cleaning,
208 valid questionnaires were ultimately retained. The
research tool is divided into fi ve parts: research description
and informed consent form; Demographic characteristics
( b i r t h  g e n e r a t i o n ,  g e n d e r ,  p a st travel experience; Q1-Q3);
Digital marketing outreach situation (information source
platform and contact frequency; Q4 – Q5);
Motivation/Perception Based on Usage and Gratitude Theory
(UGT) (Q6-Q8); Participation behavior based on Consumer
Engagement Theory (CET) (Q9-Q11). Except for
demographic characteristics, all items were measured using
the Likert five-point scale [8].
This study used the Likert five-point scale to measure five
core concepts: frequency of digital marketing exposure (Q5),
increased cultural interest (Q6), travel intention (Q7),
intention to purchase cultural and creative products (Q8), and
social sharing intention (Q9). Channel preference (Q4) is used
to capture the audience's information source platform.
All data analysis in this study was conducted in SPSS
software, strictly following standard social science research
ethics. The research has obtained ethical approval from the
host organization; The interviewee's participation fully
follows the principles of voluntariness and anonymity, and
can freely withdraw at any stage; For a very small number of
respondents under the age of 18, the guardian informed
consent clause in the ethical agreement has been strictly
enforced.

6. Discussion
6.1. Channel Preferences and the Logic of
"Platform as Search Engine"
The findings of this study clearly reveal that Generation Z
exhibits a distinct channel preference shift in obtaining
cultural tourism information: algorithm-driven short video
and graphic platforms have t aken an absolute dominant
position compared to official websites and offline
recommendation channels. This result not only confirms Hu's
[9] assertion that Generation Z sees social platforms as the
primary search and inspiration engine, but also highly aligns
with Moni et al.'s (2025) observation that young users have a
deeper trust in authentic, inte ractive user generated content
(UGC).
6.2. Motivation Satisfaction and Participation
Chain
The empirical results of this  study support hypotheses H1
to H3, which suggest that the digital content output by the
Palace Museum can simultaneously satisfy the core
motivations of Generation Z users in terms of information
acquisition, entertainment, and social interaction. Specifically,
the Forbidden City transforms its profound and complex
cultural heritage knowledge into highly accessible and
visually appealing multimedia forms, making previously
distant and solemn cultural content more everyday,
perceptible, and emotionally warm. More importantly, this
study found that the cultural interest cultivated through
content exposure constitutes a stronger behavioral driving
force than simply the exposure effect. This conclusion
validates the logical chain between the Use and Satisfaction
Theory (UGT) and the Consumer Engagement Theory (CET)
[4]: the deep role of digital marketing lies not in the infinit e
expansion of reach, but in transforming shallow attention into
deep cultural interests, which are the psychological basis that
truly drive subsequent engagement behavior. At the same time,
the partial mediating role played by social sharing (supporting

17
H4-H5) further indicates that behavioral participation in
Generation Z has inherent interdependence - sharing behavior
is both an external expression of individual participation and
a reverse amplification and diffusion of participation effects,
forming a self reinforcing participation cycle [10].
6.3. Heterogeneity within Generation
A core contribution of this study is to reveal that the
participating groups within Generation Z are not
homogeneous, thus providing support for hypothesis H6.
Although the digital content of the Forbidden City has
achieved roughly the same reach  rate among young users of
all ages, data analysis shows that the youngest group (born
between 2009 and 2012) is significantly weaker than older
members of Generation Z in t erms of awakening cultural
interests and behavioral conversion intentions. In other words,
equal exposure of information does not automatically
translate into equal cultural participation; The formation of
cultural identity seems to be closely related to an individual's
cognitive maturity, life stage, and disposable autonomy.
Future researchers must be sensitive to the differences in
cognitive levels, cultural reference frames, and autonomous
decision-making abilities among different age groups and
develop differentiated content strategies based on this.
6.4. Cultural IP Background as a Boundary
Condition for Transformation
Although some existing studies have reported that cultural
digital marketing performs on average in terms of purchase
and sharing conversion rates, the individual behavioral
intention observed in this study is significantly higher. This
study believes that this difference is not a methodological
accident, but can be attribut ed to the unique cultural
representativeness carried by the Palace Museum - it is not
only a museum, but also a concentrated symbol of Chinese
civilization, which is greatly amplified and activated in the
rising "China-Chic" movement in China. The powerful
cultural IP endows digital marketing with an added value that
goes beyond the function of information transmission,
elevating it from a tool for informing and persuading to a
triggering mechanism for cultural resonance and identity
recognition. This means that the conversion effect of digital
marketing is not a universal constant, but highly dependent on
the IP strength and symbolic capital possessed by specific
cultural institutions; For general tourist destinations lacking
similar cultural status, it may not be realistic to simply copy
the content strategy and conversion expectations of the
Forbidden City.
7. Conclusion
This study takes the Palace Museum as a case study,
systematically models and empirically tests the intrinsic
mechanism of digital marketing driving the participation of
China's "Gen Z" cultural tourism. Research has found that in
the digital age where algorithms are deeply embedded in the
information environment, the younger generation's access to
cultural content has become highly dependent on the
intelligent recommendation mechanisms of social media
platforms; Digital marketing effectively cultivates the deep
interest of this group in cultural heritage by precisely meeting
their four needs of information acquisition, entertainment,
social interaction, and identity recognition, and further
stimulates their participation in cognitive, emotional, and
behavioral levels. It is worth noting that cultural interests
constitute the core driving force behind on-site visits and
consumer purchase intentions throughout the entire
conversion path, while social sharing intentions play an
important mediating role between marketing stimuli and
participation outcomes, especially in terms of the mediating
effect on purchase intentions. In addition, the study revealed
differential findings that compared to online content sharing
or consumption of cultural and creative products, digital
marketing has a stronger driving effect on stimulating offline
on-site visits. Although digital marketing generally covers the
entire 'Generation Z', there ar e still significant differences in
segmented groups based on interest preferences, media usage
habits, and cultural identity within the generation. This
suggests that academia and industry need to go beyond
homogeneous understanding of the 'Generation Z'.
At the same time, although this study provides a feasible
basis for preliminary explora tion, future research needs to
rely on larger sample sizes, adopt stratified sampling
strategies, and expand to cross-border and cross-cultural
contexts to test the stability and heterogeneity of digital
marketing effects in different socio-cultural contexts.
There may be systematic differences in the input-output
ratio, conversion path, and user  reach efficiency of digital
marketing among cultural institutions of different scales.
Future research can organically integrate these institutional,
social, and psychological variables into the model to construct
a more inclusive and explanatory comprehensive theoretical
system.
