This study aims to identify the obstacles and strengths of marketers, and to formulate
a capacity-building strategy for marketers in achieving quality financing targets at PT
Permodalan Nasional Madani Ventura Syariah (PNMVS). A qualitative case study
approach was employed, involving three managers and six Generation Y and Z
marketers from the Jakarta, Bandung, and Surabaya branch offices as the units of
analysis. Data were collected through interviews, observation, and documentation,
and analyzed using the Miles and Huberman interactive model of data reduction, data
display, and conclusion drawing. The findings show that marketers' main obstacles lie
in limited technical and digital competence, administrative burdens, limited access to
customer data, and pr essure to meet targets amid intense market competition.
Conversely, marketers possess strengths in the form of work enthusiasm, flexibility,
extensive social networks, and orientation toward Islamic values such as
trustworthiness and honesty. A SWOT analys is indicates that a Weakness –
Opportunities (WO) strategy is the most relevant, namely leveraging external
opportunities such as digital transformation and the growth of the sharia market to
address digital competency weaknesses through digital marketing tr aining, the
adoption of customer relationship management (CRM), and sustainable talent -
development programs. This study contributes theoretically to the literature on values-
based human resource development and the Resource-Based View, and practically to
PT PNMVS in designing a more contextual and sustainable marketer coaching
strategy.

INTRODUCTION
PT Permodalan  Nasional Madani Sharia
Venture (PNMVS) is a sharia -based venture capital
company, a subsidiary of PT PNM Ventura Capital,
which is itself a subsidiary of PT Permodalan
Nasional Madani (Persero). The main task of this
company is to provide financing soluti ons based on
profit-sharing principles to Small, Medium, and
Cooperative Enterprises (SMEs and cooperatives),
and it has expanded its service network to the Greater
Jakarta area, West Java, Central Java, East Java,
Sumatra, and Sulawesi.
The success of PNM VS in distributing
financing doesn’t just rely on the products and
services offered, but also on the capacity and
competence of the marketing team. In line with the
Resource-Based View (RBV) approach, which
emphasizes the importance of managing internal
resources as a basis for competitive advantage (El
Nemar et al., 2022), developing human resource
capacity becomes key to the company's sustainable
competitiveness. Trimulato (2018) highlights that
human resource excellence in Islamic financial
institutions cannot be separated from four
dimensions of competence: mindset, skills
(knowledge, expertise, abilities), character (sidiq,
amanah, fathonah, tabligh), and quality of work
motivation.

The current marketing workforce at PNMVS is
mostly made up of Generatio n Y and Z, who are
known to be tech -savvy, value flexibility, and look
for meaning in their work (Sutikno et al., 2023; Goh
& Lee, 2018). These generational traits demand an
adaptive and personalized HR management approach
(Sánchez-Hernández et al., 2019).  In practice,
marketers are responsible for identifying customer
needs, offering financing solutions that comply with
sharia principles —free from riba, gharar, and
maisir—and assisting throughout the process until
the funds are disbursed (Febrian & Budiant o, 2023;
Muslimin et al., 2022). However, marketers often
face challenges such as heavy workloads, pressure to
meet targets, and a work environment that isn’t fully
supportive, which can potentially hinder the
achievement of quality financing targets.
The gap between management's expectations
and the on -the-ground operational reality has
become the main urgency of this research. Therefore,
this study focuses on three research questions: (1)
how marketers deal with obstacles in achieving
quality financing ta rgets at PT PNMVS; (2) how
marketers strive to achieve excellence and meet
quality financing targets; and (3) how strategies to
enhance marketers' capacity at PT PNMVS can be
formulated. Accordingly, the purpose of this study is
to identify and analyze the  obstacles and efforts of
marketers in reaching quality financing targets, and
to formulate strategies for improving marketers'
capacity based on empirical findings from the field.
This study is expected to provide empirical
contributions regarding the rol e of human resources
in enhancing marketers' capabilities, while also
serving as a consideration for PNMVS management
in designing more contextual and sustainable
development strategies for Generation Y and Z
marketers.

METHODS
Research Design
This research uses a qualitative method with a
case study approach, which allows the researcher to
conduct an in-depth and comprehensive review of an
organizational phenomenon over a certain period
(Bungin, 2010). This approach aligns with Creswell's
(2009) view that a case study is a research strategy
where the researcher carefully investigates a
program, event, activity, or process by gathering in -
depth data through various data collection
procedures. The case study design used refers to
Yin's (2018) approach, which includes six stages: (1)
planning, (2) design, (3) preparation, (4) data
collection, (5) analysis, and (6) reporting.

Unit of Analysis and Research Settings
The unit of analysis in this study is Gen Y and
Z marketers at PT PNMVS, along with the managers
who supervise them. The research was conducted at
three PNMVS representative offices, namely Jakarta,
Bandung, and Surabaya, representing the company’s
operational context in different work areas.

Data Collection Techniques
Data was collected throug h three techniques:
(1) unstructured interviews with three head
representatives (managers) and six Generation Y
marketers, which allowed for in -depth and flexible
information exchange; (2) participatory observation
of marketers' daily work activities in th e field; and
(3) documentation in the form of company
supporting data. The manager informants consisted
of the Jakarta manager (45 years old, Master's in
Financial Management, 15 years of experience),
Bandung manager (42 years old, Master's in
Business Adm inistration, 13 years of experience),
and Surabaya manager (39 years old, Bachelor's in
Development Economics, 11 years of experience).
The six marketer informants had educational
backgrounds ranging from Associate's to Bachelor's
degrees in marketing mana gement, communication,
business administration, management economics,
and accounting, aged 25–32 years with 2–7 years of
work experience.

Data Analysis Techniques
Data analysis refers to the interactive model of
Miles and Huberman (1994), which consists of three
interconnected activities: data reduction, data
display, and conclusion drawing/verification. Data
reduction is done by summarizing and focusing the
interview results on main themes related to
challenges, strengths, and the development needs of
marketers. The data is then presented in narrative
form and comparison matrix tables before drawing
conclusions.

Data Validity
The validity of the data was tested through four
standards as proposed by Lincoln and Guba (1985):
credibility (through source and time triangulation),
transferability (clarity of the research context),
dependability (consistency of the research process),
and confirmability (tracing the alignment of analysis
results with field data). The researcher also applied
member checking, which is re -confirming the
transcripts and interpretation results with the
informants to ensure data accuracy (Creswell, 2009).

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Overview of the Research Object
PT PNM Venture Capital was established on
October 28, 1999, as a business entity focused on
venture capital activities through equity participation
or financing for a certain period, with a primary focus
as a venture debt corporation (VDC). The company
has partnered with nine Business Partner Companies
(PPUs) supported by 18 office networks. The
majority of shares are owned by PT Permodalan
Nasional Madani (PNM) at 99.9997%, with a vision
to become a leading venture capital financial
institution in Indonesia that develops empowerment
and entrepreneurship programs for SMEs. PT
PNMVS, as a Sharia-based business unit within this
group, carries out the mission of empowering
MSMEs through Sharia-compliant financing.

The analysis results show that the main
challenges for marketers come from external factors
(market competition, low Islamic financial literacy,
regulatory dynamic s) as well as internal factors
(target pressure, limited access to potential
customers, suboptimal digital technology support).
These findings align with organizational behavior
theory, which states that environmental and
organizational system factors significantly influence
individual performance (Griffin et al., 2020). On the
other hand, work commitment, communication
skills, and an orientation towards Islamic values like
trustworthiness and responsibility are differentiating
factors that strengthen the o rganization's
competitiveness (Wagner III & Hollenbeck, 2020).

Interviews with six Generation Y and Z
marketers show a complex work dynamic, especially
when it comes to hitting financing targets. Marketers
with longer tenure (6 –7 years) tend to have more
developed customer relationship strategies, while
newer marketers focus more on the challenges of
adapting to internal systems and communicating
with potential clients. A summary of the findings is
presented in Table 2.

they have and the increasingly complex demands of
the market.

The Gap Between Managers' and Marketers'
Perspectives
The comparison between the two groups of
informants shows a pretty clear gap in perspectives.
Managers emphasize the importance of technical and
digital skills to reach financing targets measurably,
in line with findings that technology -based
competence significantly boosts work performance
(Chen et al., 2022). Marketers, while showing
enthusiasm and flexibility, stress the need for
practical guidance and ongoing training focused on
real work practices, not just theory (Bernardin &
Wiatrowski, 2013).
When it comes to targets and workload,
managers see targets as realistic and administration
as part of the organization's control system, while
marketers feel bureaucracy and approval processes
as burdens that create psychological stress. This

1273

perspective can be explained using the Theory of
Planned Behavior (Ajzen et al., 2018), where
perceptions of workload affect individual attitudes
and behavioral intentions; if targets are seen as too
demanding, motivation and the quality of
achievements can potentially drop.
On the dimension of Islamic values, both
groups of informants showed a strong similarity in
views, in line with the assertion that Sharia
compliance builds legitimacy and customer trust
(Alam et al., 2017; Muslimin et al., 2022). However,
the expected coaching approach differed: managers
emphasized a formal reward system, while marketers
called for a practical, hands -on approach with
ongoing mentoring —a finding consistent with the
idea that religious values encourage entrepreneurship
when combine d with an approach suited to the
character of the younger generation (Sutikno et al.,
2023).

The gap between the  availability of digital

that technological support needs to be accompanied
by improving human resource skills.

strategy uses rewards and Islamic culture to maintain
motivation amid competition; the WO strategy
focuses on digital training and administrative

on the analysis, the WO (Weakness –Opportunities)
strategy is considered the most relevant for PT
PNMVS at the moment, given that the main
weaknesses lie in digital competence and limited
data, while external opportunities —such as digital
transformation, adoption of customer relationship

CRM systems, and continuous mentoring, the
organization not only bridges the e xisting
competency gaps but also positions itself to be better
prepared for competition based on digital technology
(Song, 2024; Qi et al., 2022).

CONCLUSION
Conclusion
This study found that the main constraints for
PT PNMVS marketers in achieving quality financing
targets lie in limited digital skills, low utilization of
technology-based information systems, suboptimal
ability to analyze prospective customer data, and
external challenges such as competition among
financing institutions. On the other hand, marketers'
strengths include a wide network, close social ties
with customers, institutional credibility, and a strong
orientation toward Islamic values. Based on these
conditions, the most suitable strategy to enhance
marketers' capabilities is a Weakness –Opportunities
(WO) approach, which means taking advantage of
external opportunities through digital marketing
training, implementing a CRM system, and
sustainable HR talent development programs, so
internal weaknesses can be minimized wh ile
strengthening marketers' position in facing market
challenges.

Implication
• Theoretical implications: these findings
enrich studies on human resource
development strategies based on
organizational values in the Islamic
microfinance sector, showing that the
integration of technical competence, work
experience, and Islamic values —non-
technical factors —also determines financing
quality.
• Practical implications: the research results
provide a basis for PT PNMVS to design more
contextual marketer capacity-building
programs, including digital marketing
training, stress management enhancement,
and internalization of Islamic work ethics.
• Policy implications: this study offers input for
management in developing more adaptive
policies regarding work tar gets, coaching
patterns, and reward mechanisms, so that the
balance between financial performance and
HR well-being is maintained.

Recomendation
• Strengthen the mapping of the marketer's
profile and experience through regular
assessments to better match the individual's
background, capacity, and potential.
1. Adjusting targets to the level of competence of
marketers, while integrating capacity building
programs based on digital marketing, financing
risk analysis, and the use of CRM technology.
2. Strengthening the internalization of Islamic
work ethics in financing activities as an
organizational identity as well as strengthening
public trust.
• Provide a continuous coaching mechanism in
the form of mentoring, peer learning, and
psychological support so that  workload can be
managed properly and career development
patterns are clearer.

Limitations and Suggestions for Further
Research
This research is limited to a case study at three
PT PNMVS representative offices with a relatively
small number of informants , so the findings are
contextual and not meant to be generalized
statistically. Further research is recommended to test
this WO strategy model quantitatively across a
broader range of representative offices, as well as to
explore the specific role of Gener ation Z in
developing human resource capacity in Islamic
financial institutions in the future.
