PARADIGMATIC FOUNDATIONS OF MARKETING IN THE MACHINE-TO-
MACHINE (M2M) MARKET

Machine-to-machine interactions increasingly mediate economic exchange: connected devices
and software agents can identify needs, evaluate offers, and initiate tra nsactions with minimal human
intervention. These shifts challenge marketing theory that presumes human motivation as the primary
driver of choice. This article synthesises evidence on digital transformat ion and M2M infrastructures
and proposes a paradigmatic framing of marketing in the M2M market as a cyber-physical, utility-
centred form of marketing. A comparative analysis of classical, i ndustrial, relationship and digital
marketing logics is used to derive key assumptions, constructs and implication s for strategic planning
and operational marketing.
Keywords: marketing paradigm, machine-to-machine marketing, digital market transformation,
economic relations system, agentic A I, cyber-physical systems, machine customers, uti lity-based
decision-making, cyberbrand.

Introduction .  Over the last decade, economic interaction has increasingly incorporated
autonomous technical systems that monitor conditions, evaluate alternatives, and execute
actions within connected infrastructures. In such settings, exchange is initiated and completed
not primarily through human perception, persuasion, or interpersonal negotiation, but through
digitally mediated rules, protocols, and optimisation procedures embedded in cyber-physical
systems. The relevance of this transformation is evidenced by the expanding scale of connected
device ecosystems and cellular IoT connectivity forecasts, which imply a continuing rise in
machine-initiated interactions and transactions [4]. This development creates a practical task
for firms and public institutions: to ensure that products, services, and procurement offers

The conceptual starting point is the broader literature on digital transformation, which
frames digital technologies as drivers of disruptions that trigger strategic responses and reshape
value creation paths [10]. In parallel, Industry 5.0 research and policy framing emphasise that
connectivity, automation, and cyber-physical integration should be directed towards human-
centricity, sustainability, and resilience, thereby reconfiguring production and exchange
systems and strengthening the infrastructural basis for machine-mediated coordination across
organisations [1]. From a standards and architecture perspective, the technical preconditions for
machine interaction in networked environments are increasingly codified through reference
architectures and interoperability frameworks that specify service enablement, connectivity,
and security requirements. A conceptual clarification is offered by NIST, which explicates the
relationship between cyber-physical systems and the Internet of Things and supports a unified
perspective on connected, digitally enabled physical devices [3, 5].
Within marketing scholarship, several established paradigms provide essential
foundations. Historically, Relationship Marketing argues for a shift away from transactional
logic towards relational exchange, commitment, and trust as central constructs of market
functioning [5]. Service-dominant logic further reframes marketing around value co-creation
and the primacy of service provision rather than the exchange of goods alone [7]. Within the
Digital Marketing paradigm, research contributes an integrative framework for understanding
how digital technologies reshape marketing processes and strategy, including the role of
connected products, data, and analytics [11].

Aim and Objectives. The purpose of this article is to substantiate the need for a
paradigmatic revision of marketing under M2M market conditions and to propose a conceptual
formulation of an M2M marketing paradigm. This entails, first, linking the genesis of the M2M
phenomenon to the long-run development of economic systems from mechanisation to
automation and digital transformation. Second, comparing the foundational assumptions of
classical marketing with those that characterise machine-mediated exchange. And third,
synthesising relevant theoretical streams into a coherent definition that foregrounds algorithmic
decision-making, utility maximisation, and data-driven optimisation as central principles of
marketing in M2M markets.
Research Methodology. The study adopts a conceptual and theory-building design. Its
methodological basis combines the systems approach, comparative conceptual analysis,
historical-logical analysis, scientific abstraction, synthesis, and structural-logical modelling.
Historical-logical analysis is used to position M2M interaction within the evolution from
mechanisation and automation to digital transformation and cyber-physical systems.
Comparative analysis examines how key marketing categories change when autonomous
technical agents become immediate decision-makers. Scientific abstraction and synthesis
integrate marketing concepts with decision theory, optimisation, and algorithmic systems to
formulate the proposed M2M marketing paradigm. The systems approach and structural-logical
modelling are applied to represent M2M economic relations and to synthesise technological,
decision-making, and marketing foundations, while SIVA and the extended marketing mix are
reinterpreted to demonstrate the practical implications of the proposed paradigm.
Presentation and Discussion of the Main Results. The emergence of the Machine-to-
Machine market can be interpreted as the late outcome of a long productivity driven trajectory
in which economic systems moved from mechanisation and automation towards comprehensive
digital transformation. The logic of such systemic evolution, including its implications for
marketing activity, is concisely represented in the Figure 1 providing an integrated view of the
historical forces and transformation stages leading to contemporary market conditions [9].
Along this trajectory, technical systems gradually extended their scope from production
tasks to coordination and decision-making, thereby reshaping the mechanism through which
exchange is initiated and completed. With the advent of Agentic AI, this shift has intensified,
as autonomous systems are becoming increasingly capable of initiating transactions, evaluating
alternatives, and executing exchanges without direct human intervention. In this sense,
comprehensive business digital transformation has not merely digitised existing marketing
activities. It has also contributed to the emergence of M2M exchange as a distinct market
phenomenon in which value propositions are assessed, selected, and enacted through machine-
mediated routines. This development implies a need to rethink marketing not only as
communication and relationship management with human customers, but as a strategic and
operational system that functions in environments where the decision subject may be an
algorithmic agent.

At the strategic level, this requires an adaptation of marketing planning and market
strategy formation to conditions of algorithmic evaluation, constrained optimisation, and
automated coordination. Core strategic questions such as segmentation, positioning,
competitive advantage, and channel design must be reframed around machine-relevant criteria.
These criteria include interoperability, verifiability, reliability, latency, and data governance.
They do not replace human-facing value, but they increasingly shape whether an offer is
discoverable, comparable, and executable within automated decision pathways. Competition
also shifts towards ecosystem architecture, because firms increasingly compete through their
ability to embed offerings into machine-executable workflows, standards, and platform
interfaces that structure selection and repeated transactions.

Conclusions and Directions for Further Research. The results of the study provide
grounds to interpret the M2M market as a marketing domain in which the core logic of exchange
is reorganised around machine-mediated selection and execution. The key empirical and
conceptual implication is that, as purchasing and operational choices are increasingly delegated
to autonomous technical agents, marketing analysis cannot be limited to models that treat
human motivation and interpretation as the primary drivers of choice. Instead, the centre of
gravity shifts towards how offers are represented, compared, and acted upon within algorithmic
decision environments.
This finding supports the paradigm formation advanced in the paper. Marketing in the
M2M market can be defined as a cyber-physical paradigm in which machines and their
embedded algorithms become the immediate decision-makers, shifting marketing logic from
influencing consumer motivation to optimising the utility-based criteria that guide automated
choice. This formulation is consistent with the comparative evidence provided in Tables 1 –3
and with the integrative scheme presented in Figure 2. Together, they show that the M2M case
entails a simultaneous transformation of marketing categories, including the decision subject,
the evaluative basis, the information environment, and the execution mechanism of exchange.
These changes are not explained by a simple extension of digital marketing practices, because
they alter the underlying assumptions of choice and the conditions of market participation.

needed to formalise a typology of M2M decision contexts, distinguishing degrees of autonomy,
delegation structures, and accountability regimes, and specifying when a machine agent is
