 Digital transformation has driven fundamental changes in marketing
practices, compelling companies to shift their focus beyond economic gain
toward broader sustainability goals. Paid advertising and organic reach
represent two primary approaches in digital marketing, each with distinct
strategic advantages. The objective of this study is to compare the
effectiveness of these two approaches in supporting sustainability
performance, while also exploring the potential  for their integration into a
sustainability-oriented hybrid strategy. Employing a Systematic Literature
Review (SLR) method based on the PRISMA 2020 protocol, a total of 20
articles were selected and analy zed from Scopus -indexed databases. The
findings indicate that paid advertising is highly effective in achieving rapid
reach and initial conversions, making it ideal for short -term objectives.
Conversely, organic reach excels in fostering long -term engagement and
customer loyalty, making it essential for s ustainable brand building. The
hybrid strategy, which combines both approaches, is identified as the
optimal solution, leveraging the speed of paid advertising with the
authenticity of organic reach. Theoretically, this study contributes to the
digital marketing literature by providing a novel conceptual framework that
emphasizes the strategic integration of paid and organic methods as a
holistic approach to achieving sustainable performance. Practically, the
study offers clear guidelines for marketers, ena bling them to design and
implement sustainability-oriented digital strategies that optimize both cost-
efficiency and customer engagement. By balancing short -term gains and
long-term brand sustainability, this study provides actionable insights for
industry practitioners seeking to achieve sustainable digital marketing
outcomes.

INTRODUCTION
The rapid development of digital technology has transformed marketing strategies,
pushing companies to shift their focus from purely achieving economic gains to prioritizing
sustainability. Digital marketing has become a crucial tool for communicating values, building
emotional connections, and fostering deeper consumer engagement, all while supporting
corporate sustainability goals (Asante et al., 2023; Reztrianti, 2024) As globalization increases

the demand for social and environmental accountability, the ability of marketing strategies to
effectively convey sustainability values has become essential (Abdullah & Sani, 2025).
Digital marketing strategies generally fall into two main categories: paid advertising and
organic reach (unpaid). Paid advertising refers to sponsored promotional methods, such as
Google Ads and Instagram Ads, which offer precise audience targeting and wide reach within
a short period (Coffin et al., 2022; Sobolewski et al., 2024) . Conversely, organic approaches
include search engine optimization (SEO), user -generated content, and social media
interactions, which are known for building authentic relationships and sustainable customer
loyalty over time (Asante et al., 2023; Roumeliotis & Tselikas, 2022) .These two approaches
offer distinct benefits but also present unique challenges, making it essential for companies to
understand how to optimize their use to achieve both immediate results and long -term
sustainability.

LITERATURE REVIEW
Each of these approaches offers distinct advantages and limitations. Paid advertising has
been shown to be highly effective in terms of speed and distribution scale. However, it often
requires a substantial financial investment and can lead to audience fat igue. Conversely,
organic strategies have been shown to be more cost -efficient and to engender more authentic
engagement (Grantham et al., 2021; Sakas et al., 2023; Sakas & Reklitis, 2021) . However, it
should be noted that these strategies require considerable time and consistent effort. This
creates a strategic dilemma for many companies in deciding whether to prioritise one approach
over the other or to integrate both optimally to enhance sustainability performance.
It has been demonstrated by preceding studies that digital marketing has the capacity to
function as a transformational instrument in the communication of sustainability messages and
the cultivation of more conscious consumer behaviour. The efficacy of social media campaigns
in reaching audiences that are otherwise difficult to engage through conventional means has
been well-documented (Grantham et al., 2021). These campaigns offer both cost-efficiency and
broader accessibility. The utilisation of two-way communication in these strategies serves not
only to disseminate information but also to establish participatory spaces (Abdullah et al.,
2024), thereby encouraging psychological and emotional engagement among consumers (Lim
et al., 2022; Ningrum et al., 2023) . In the context of the creator economy, influencers and
personal brands play a pivotal role in the delivery of sustainability values through more
personal and narrative-driven approaches (Dolbec & Smith, 2025).
Conversely, the effectiveness of paid advertising in the sustainability context is
increasingly reinforced by the use of incentives and targeted technology. Paid advertisements
designed to encourage consumer participation in social campaigns or research initiatives have
demonstrated substantial outcomes in terms of reach and cost efficiency (Sobolewski et al.,
2024). In the context of escalating digital competition, data -driven and personalised
approaches, particularly via Search Engine Optimisation (SEO) – have become vital tools for
increasing organic traffic and enhancing the reputation of socially responsible bra nds
(Roumeliotis & Tselikas, 2022).
Nevertheless, systematic comparative studies examining the effectiveness of paid
advertising versus organic reach in supporting sustainability performance remain limited. The
extant literature on this subject has been found to place greater emphasis on technical aspects,
such as algorithms and conversion rates, rather than on the manner in which these strategies
contribute to broader strategic sustainability goals (Dolbec & Smith, 2025; Lim et al., 2022) .

Moreover, there is a paucity of research that explores the hybrid integration of these approaches
in a holistic and strategic manner.
In response to this research gap, the aim is to conduct a systematic literature review to
address three core areas of focus : the effectiveness of paid advertising and organic reach in
enhancing business sustainability performance. It is anticipated that this approach will provide
theoretical and practical contributions to the development of digital marketing strategies that
are both commercially effective and socially and environmentally responsible.
METHODS
The research method employed in this study is a systematic literature review
(SLR).guided by the PRISMA 2020 (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and
Meta-Analyses) framework. The selection of this method was driven by the necessity to ensure
that the processes of identification, selection, and analysis of scholarly articles are conducted
in a systematic, transparent, accurate, and objective manner.
The methodological procedures followed in this study are detailed as follows:
2.1 Formulation of Research Questions
The SLR process commences with the formulation of research questions that function as
the focal point of the review:
RQ1: How does the effectiveness of paid advertising compare to organic reach in enhancing
sustainability performance?
RQ2: In what ways can the advantages and challenges inherent to each approach be identified
in the context of sustainable digital marketing?
RQ3: In what manner may a hybrid integration of both approaches be optimised in order to
provide comprehensive support for the achievement of sustainability goals?

2.2 Literature Search Strategy
The literature search was conducted systematically using a Boolean keyword combination
comprising “Paid Advertising” OR “Paid Ads” OR “Sponsored Content” AND “Organic
Reach” OR “Organic Traffic” OR “Organic Marketing” AND “Sustainability Performance.”
The sea rch was conducted across a number of reputable and Scopus -indexed academic
databases, including ScienceDirect, Elsevier, JMIR Publications, IEEE Xplore, Emerald
Insight, MDPI, Routledge,  SAGE, and Inderscience Enterprises. The inclusion criteria were
meticulously delineated to guarantee the relevance and quality of the selected studies. The
selection of articles was constrained by the following criteria: the articles in question had to
have been published between 2016 and 2025; written in English; openly ac cessible (either as
open access or open archive); and categori zed as either review articles or original research
articles. The strategy was conceived with the objective of ensuring that the collected literature
was not only thematically aligned with the re search objectives, but also reflected a high
standard of academic credibility and currency within the domains of digital marketing and
sustainability.

An initial search yielded 328 articles. A preliminary identification phase was conducted in
order to remove articles that did not meet the established criteria with regard to language, year
of publication, article type and open access status (see Table 1, adapt from Efendi et al. (2025).

2.5 Analysis and Synthesis of Findings
The final stage of the SLR process involved synthesizing findings from the 20 selected
articles, which were organized thematically and evaluated in relation to the formulated research
questions. The analysis was conducted using a narrative approach, integrating the findings to
create three overarching categories: the effectiveness of the strategies in relation to
sustainability performance (RQ1); the identification of the strengths and challenges associated
with each approach, including paid advertising and organic reach (RQ2); and the formu lation
of an optimal hybrid strategy (RQ3) ; the objective of this synthesis was twofold: firstly, to
present a comprehensive overview of the current scholarly landscape, and secondly, to identify
research gaps that could serve as the basis for future studies. A PRISMA diagram (see Figure
1) was also developed to visually depict the structured and transparent process of article

overlooked in single -strategy research, while offering actionable insights for practitioners

the distinct but complementary roles of paid advertising and organic reach. Paid advertising,

as evidenced by Bennetts et al. (2019) [P1], Sanchez et al. (2020) [P2], Belanche et al. (2019)
[P15], Ford et al. (2019) [P6], Murphy et al. (2018)  [P12], and Graham et al. (2019)  [P13],
consistently excels in generating rapid visibility, large initial conversions, and measurable
campaign results across platforms such as Facebook, Instagram, and Snapchat. These results
align with the broader marketing literature, which has long recognized the tactical advantages
of paid media for time-sensitive goals such as recruitment or health campaigns. However, the
review highlights that this rapid impact comes at a cost: studies such as Sobolewski et al. (2024)
[P3] and and Richardson et al. (2016)  [P14] demonstrate that excessive incentives or lack of
transparency in paid content can undermine data quality, raise the risk of fraudulent
participation, and ultimately erode audience trust , a challenge that is amplified in long -term
sustainability initiatives. Arshanapally et al. (2022)  [P20] further confirms that paid app
campaigns can deliver impressive initial impressions and click -through rates, but maintaining
ongoing engagement and behavioural change remains difficult without additional strategies.
Conversely, organic reach proves to be the most effective for sustained engagement and
long-term brand loyalty. As reported by Müller & Christandl (2019) [P8], Asante et al. (2023)
[P17], Cahill et al. (2019) [P4], and Huang & Depari (2021) [P19], strategies relying on user-
generated content (UGC), search engine optimization (SEO), and authentic community
interaction consistently foster deeper psychological and behavio ural engagement. These
findings extend prior literature by emphasizing that trust and loyalty  are critical elements of
sustainability performance  and more readily achieved when marketing strategies leverage
organic content and encourage active audience participation. However, the slow build and
algorithmic dependence of organic tactics mean that they are less suitable for campaigns
requiring immediate, quantifiable outcomes.
Addressing RQ2 (strengths and challenges of each approach), the review confirms that
paid advertising offers unparalleled speed, audience targeting, and scale (Finnell et al., 2017
[P5]; Lee et al., 2018 [11]), supporting previous scholarship that emphasizes its efficiency for
awareness and customer acquisition. Nevertheless, its sustainability is undermined by high
ongoing costs, the potential for perceived intrusiveness (Belanche et al., 2019 [P15]; Campbell
& Farrell, 2020 [P7]) , and the risk of diminishing returns without continued investment
(Gutiérrez & Otero, 2020 [P16]; Murphy et al., 2018 [P12]) . In contrast, organic approaches
offer enduring benefits for psychological and social engagement, as highlighted by Gao et al.
(2023) [P18] and Gutiérrez & Otero (2020)  [P16], but struggle with limited initial reach and
unpredictability in the short term. The evidence thus complements and extends existing
literature, confirming that each strategy is constrained by its respective trade -offs, and that a
single-mode approac h is rarely sufficient for achieving both commercial effectiveness and
sustainability.
With respect to RQ3 (integrative/hybrid strategies), multiple studies point to the hybrid
model as a critical advancement in digital marketing practice. The combination of paid and
organic strategies is shown by Coffin et al. (2022) [P9], Sakas et al. (2023) [P10], and Gao et
al. (2023)  [P18] to produce synergistic value, balancing the short -term strengths of paid
campaigns with the long-term loyalty and engagement generated by organic tactics. The review
supports the argument that hybrid strategies not only maximize initial visibility and rapid
conversions but also ensure brand resilience and sustainable performance. These insights serve
to reinforce, and in numerous ways to advance, the conceptual discourse in prior studies that
have called for integrated, multi-channel marketing models in the era of digital transformation.

The practical implications of the hybrid framework ( see Figure 2) are substantial. For
digital marketing practitioners, this model provides a dynamic roadmap for campaign design
and resource allocation. In practical terms, organizations are advised to deploy paid advertising
in the early stages of a campaign to achieve rapid awareness and measurable reach, especially
when launching new products, conducting recruitment drives, or targeting time-sensitive health
initiatives. As these initial goals are met, practitioners should shift focus to organic strategies,
investing in high-quality SEO, user-generated content, and ongoing community management
to build trust, deepen engagement, and foster lasting brand loyalty. The hybrid approach also
offers flexibility: the balance between paid and organic can be continuously adjusted based on
campaign objectives, audience feedback, and changing mar ket conditions, ensuring
adaptability in a rapidly evolving digital ecosystem.
Moreover, the framework outlined in this study provides not only a theoretical
framework but also an operational tool for managers to evaluate and adjust their marketing
mix. By systematically integrating both approaches, companies can avoid the pitfalls of over -
reliance on either strategy alone , maximizing short-term performance without sacrificing the
authentic engagement necessary for long -term sustainability. This hybrid strategy is
particularly relevant for organizations seeking to meet rising expectat ions for ethical business
practices, social responsibility, and environmental stewardship alongside commercial
objectives.
In summary, this review advances the field by providing empirical and conceptual
evidence that paid advertising and organic reach, when integrated as a hybrid strategy, form a
powerful and adaptive foundation for sustainable digital marketing. The results not only
support but also refine previous literature by demonstrating that the most resilient and impactful
approaches are those that embrace both the immediacy of paid media and the authenticity of
organic engagement.
It must be noted that the scope of this study is subject to several limitations. Firstly, the
systematic literature review was exclusively dependent on the Scopus database, which may
have resulted in the exclusion of relevant studies indexed elsewhere. Sec ondly, the inclusion
of only English -language articles may have introduced a language bias, thereby limiting the
generalizability of the findings. Thirdly, given that this SLR is exclusively based on secondary

CONCLUSION
This study significantly contributes to the theoretical understanding of sustainable digital
marketing by introducing a novel hybrid model that strategically integrates paid advertising
and organic reach. Unlike previous studies that primarily focused on the isolated effectiveness
of either paid or organic strategies, this research emphasizes the synergistic value of a
combined approach. The hybrid model is conceptualized as a strategic integration of paid
advertising, which is effective for generating rapi d reach and initial conversions and organic
reach, which excels in fostering long-term engagement and customer loyalty. This theoretical
advancement enriches the existing literature by providing a more holistic understanding of how
these two strategies can  complement each other to enhance sustainability performance.
Specifically, it highlights the importance of balance between short -term effectiveness (paid)
and long-term brand sustainability (organic), offering a comprehensive perspective on digital
marketing strategy in the sustainability context.
The proposed hybrid model is further refined into a conceptual framework (Figure 2),
which visually illustrates the dynamic relationship between paid advertising, organic reach,
and their combined impact on sustainability performance. This framework provid es a clear
theoretical foundation for understanding the conditions under which a hybrid strategy is most
effective, and it elucidates how the complementary strengths of paid and organic approaches
can be leveraged to achieve sustainable marketing outcomes.  By emphasizing the need for
strategic integration rather than isolated implementation, this framework extends the
theoretical discourse on digital marketing beyond the conventional dichotomy of paid versus
organic, positioning the hybrid strategy as a mor e adaptive and resilient approach in the ever -
evolving digital landscape.
From a practical perspective, the conceptual framework serves as a strategic guide for
marketing managers and digital strategists seeking to design sustainability -oriented digital
marketing strategies. The model provides clear guidelines for optimizing the  use of paid and
organic methods based on organizational objectives and available resources. For instance, it
suggests that paid advertising should be prioritized when rapid reach and initial conversions
are critical, such as during promotional campaigns o r product launches. Conversely, organic
reach should be prioritized in contexts where long -term engagement, brand loyalty, and
community building are paramount. More importantly, the hybrid strategy enables practitioners
to achieve a balanced outcome by in tegrating both approaches, maximizing initial visibility
through paid ads while maintaining brand credibility and sustainability through organic
engagement.
Furthermore, this study offers actionable insights for optimizing hybrid strategies in
response to the dynamic nature of the digital marketplace. It highlights the importance of
transparent communication, especially in paid campaigns, to maintain consumer trust, as well
as the need for consistent and authentic content in organic strategies to foster meaningful
engagement. The framework also encourages marketers to continuously evaluate the
performance of both approaches and adjust their strategies based on audience behaviour and
evolving digital platform algorithms.
