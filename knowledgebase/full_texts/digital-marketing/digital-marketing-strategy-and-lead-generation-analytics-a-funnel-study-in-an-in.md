Digital Marketing Strategy and Lead Generation Analytics: A Funnel Study
in an Indian Digital Agency

Akanksha Dhananjay Gabale, Dipanjay Bhalerao
School of Business (MBA), Indira University
Tathwade, Pune 411033, Maharashtra, India

Abstract—Small digital agencies must decide where to spend limited
effort on attracting and converting prospects, yet few studies  report
channel-level funnel evidence from such firms. This paper examines how
creative format and acquisition channel relate  to lead generation and
enrolment at Woxen Digital Pvt. Ltd., an Indian agency that also sells
practical digital marketing training.  A descriptive and exploratory field
study was carried out from 15 May to 15 July 2026, covering 150 tracked
prospect interactions and campaign data from Meta Business Suite and
Google Analytics. Two hypotheses were tested. Educational carousels
achieved a click -through rate (CTR) of 3.85% against 1.90% for single -
image graphics (p < 0.001), supporting H1. Organic search leads
converted at 30.0% against 24.4% for paid leads, but the difference was
not significant (p = 0.605), so H2 is directional only. Outbound messaging
produced the most inquiries (60 of 150) but the lowest conversion
(20.0%), while referrals converted at 53.3%. Price (41.8%) and time
clashes (26.4%) explained most non -enrolments. A costed 90 -day plan is
proposed, and the limits of a small sample are discussed.
Index Terms —digital marketing, lead generation, conversion funnel,
marketing analytics, content engagement, inbound marketing, India
I. INTRODUCTION
Indian marketing spend has moved steadily towards digital media. An
industry survey puts digital at 44% of total advertising spend in
FY2025 with a further rise projected [1], and  a second estimate places
it at 45–46% [2]. Large brands can absorb rising paid -reach costs, but
small agencies compete for the same audiences on limited budgets.
They need to know which creatives, channels and follow -up habits
actually produce paying customers.
Lead generation is often judged by volume, which says little about
quality. Funnel analytics, which follow a prospect from impression to
enrolment, therefore underpin sound decisions [3],  [4]. Published
work mostly studies large firms or consumer brands, and evidence
from a small agency with a dual model (services for businesses and
training for students) is scarce.
This paper reports a field study at Woxen Digital Pvt. Ltd., a
private limited company incorporated in 2021. It asks two
confirmatory questions, answered through two hypotheses: (Q1) do
creative formats differ in click -through engagement, and (Q2) do
acquisition channels differ in lead conversion? It also reports two
exploratory observations, on prospect background and response speed,
and on the barriers that explain lost prospects. The contributions are
channel-level funnel
evidence with confidence intervals, a candid account of what a
small sample can and cannot show, and a measurable 90 -day
improvement plan. Section II reviews the literature, Section III
sets out the framework and hypotheses, Section IV the method,
Section V the results, and Sections VI to IX the discussion,
implications, limitations and conclusion.
II. LITERATURE REVIEW
A. Digital Marketing and the Customer Journey
Digital technologies have changed how firms reach and learn
about customers. Leeflang et al. [5] argue that digitalization
forces firms to rethink how they use data, customer connectivity
and measurement. Reviews of two decades of research [6], [4]
show that digital, social and mobile marketing now touch every
stage of the consumer journey, and Stephen [7] reaches a
similar conclusion for consumer behavior. More recent
syntheses identify analytics, content and social engagement as
continuing research priorities [8]. Lemon and Verhoef [9]
describe the journey as a chain of touchpoints across which
friction accumulates, which implies that a firm should measure
each stage rather than only the final sale.
B. Content, Creative Format and Engagement
Success on social platforms depends largely on content that
users find useful and worth sharing [10], and social media has
shifted part of the control over brand messages to consumers
[11], [12]. Engagement is not uniform across content. De Vries
et al. [13] found that the characteristics of a brand post influence
how many likes and comments it attracts, and Ashley and Tuten
[14] link the creative strategies of branded social content to
consumer engagement. Work on content marketing similarly
reports that practitioners value relevant, educational content
[15]. These studies motivate comparing creative formats
instead of treating all posts as one category. They mostly
measure engagement and seldom follow it through to inquiries,
which is the step this study adds.
C. Inbound and Outbound Channels in Small Firms
Inbound marketing earns attention through content and search
visibility, so that prospects come to the firm, while outbound
marketing initiates contact directly [16]. For small and medium
enterprises (SMEs), Taiminen and Karjaluoto [17] report that
use of digital channels is often limited and that measurement
and skills remain constraints. Kotler et al. [18] suggest that
small firms can narrow the gap through data-led targeting and

D. Lead Management, Analytics and Response Speed
Research on lead management stresses speed and measurement.
Oldroyd et al. [19] found that the odds of qualifying a web -
generated lead fall sharply as time to first contact increases, and
that most firms respond slowly. Studies of web analytics and
social media return on investment show that firms struggle to
link activity metrics to business outcomes [20], [21].
Practitioner texts therefore recommend tracking each funnel
ratio and relating acquisition cost to enrolment [3], [22].
E. Research Gap
Three gaps emerge. First, creative-format studies rarely connect
engagement to inquiries. Second, evidence on the relative
conversion of inbound, paid and outbound channels in small
agencies is thin. Third, most studies come from large firms or
Western markets, with few funnel -level reports from Indian
agencies that train as well as serve clients. This study addresses
these gaps with a small but fully tracked funnel and reports its
uncertainty openly.
III. THEORETICAL FRAMEWORK AND HYPOTHESES
The study is organized around the Attention –Interest–Desire–
Action (AIDA) hierarchy [23], [24]. Creative format is treated
as an attention variable, acquisition channel as an interest and
desire variable, and response speed, social proof and price as
later-stage variables (Fig. 1). For an intangible service, people,
process and physical evidence such as portfolios and
testimonials reduce perceived risk [25]. Perceived usefulness
[26] and perceived control, including affordability and time
[27], help explain why interested prospects still fail to enrol.

Fig. 1. Conceptual framework: AIDA stages mapped to study variables. H1 and
H2 are tested; other variables are explored descriptively.
From the framework and the gaps identified above, two
hypotheses are tested.
H1. Educational carousel posts achieve a higher click -through
rate than single-image graphics. Carousels let a firm teach step
by step, which the literature links to useful, shareable content,
so they should earn more clicks.
H2. Organic search leads convert to enrolment at a higher rate
than paid-ad leads. Prospects who find the firm through search
express an existing need, so they should be more likely to enrol
than those reached through an advertisement.
IV. METHODOLOGY
A. Setting and Design
Woxen Digital sells social media, search and design services to
small businesses (B2B) and agency -guided, project -based
training to students and job seekers (B2C). The study used a
descriptive and exploratory mixed -methods design during a
virtual on -the-job placement (15 May –15 July 2026). The
researcher ran four work streams: a branded Facebook page and
Canva creatives, search -optimized blogs, outbound and
inbound lead handling, and market research. Only the B2C
course funnel is analyzed quantitatively.
B. Data and Sample
Primary data come from an Excel lead tracker of 150 prospect
records (source channel, education, response time, funnel stage,
main objection, outcome) and observation notes on message
scripts. Secondary data come from Meta Business Suite
(impressions, clicks, engagements by creative) and Google
Analytics and Search Console. Purposive and convenience
sampling gave 87 undergraduates (58.0%), 48 postgraduates
(32.0%) and 15 working professionals or career switchers
(10.0%). By location, 42.0% were from Tier -1 cities, 38.0%
from Tier-2 and 20.0% from Tier -3 or semi -urban areas. The
most common goals were live -project credentials (44.0%) and
skill building (30.0%).
C. Metrics
Funnel ratios follow standard definitions [3]:
CTR = (Clicks / Impressions) × 100 (1)
Conversion = (Enrolments / Inquiries) × 100   (2)
CAC = (Ad spend + direct costs) / Enrolments   (3)
D. Analysis
Proportions are reported with Wilson 95% confidence intervals
[28], [29]. Associations were tested with Pearson chi -square,
with Fisher’s exact test and a permutation check where
expected counts fell below five [30]. CTR was compared with a
two-proportion z -test. Cramér’s V gives effect size. The
significance level is 0.05, and any comparison defined after
viewing the data is labeled exploratory. All statistics were
recomputed from the counts reported here.
E. Ethics
Only aggregated results are reported. No personal identifiers or
confidential financial data appear, and follow -up used the
consent practices expected under India’s Digital Personal Data
Protection Act, 2023 [31].

Carousels recorded 323 clicks from 8,400 impressions against
80 from 4,200 for single images, a difference of 1.94 percentage
points (95% CI 1.36–2.52; z = 5.84, p < 0.001). H1 is
supported. Carousels also produced the most inquiries (22 of
60, or 36.7%) and, with testimonials, the highest engagement
rates (Table I, Fig. 2).

A nuance matters. Per 1,000 impressions, offer posters and
testimonials yielded more inquiries than carousels (3.16 and
2.95 vs. 2.62), and carousel clicks converted to inquiries less
often (6.8% vs. 10.0% for the other formats, using estimated
clicks). Carousels attract attention and clicks, whereas
deadlines and social proof prompt a message. Cost per click on
paid static creatives also rose by 15 –20% after seven to ten
days.

The exploratory grouping of intent -driven channels (organic
and referral) against outbound and paid channels was defined
after viewing the data. The chi -square test gives p = 0.044 but
the exact test gives p = 0.069, so the evidence is suggestive, not
conclusive. The referral advantage is the clearest pattern but

channel contrasts (threshold 0.017).
C. Exploratory Observations
Pharmacy and technology graduates converted at 35.7% (15 of
42), business undergraduates at 24.4% (11 of 45), postgraduates
at 25.0% (12 of 48) and working professionals at 13.3% (2 of
15), but these differences are not significant (Table III). Internal
tracking showed that prospects contacted within 15 minutes had
a 38% higher qualification rate than those contacted after four
hours, and personalized scripts reduced pushback by 22%.
Enrolment typically needed three to four touchpoints. The
response-speed and script figures are descriptive because group
sizes were not retained, and they are consistent with earlier
findings on lead response [19].

Price explained 41.8% of non -enrolments (95% CI 33 –51%)
and time or exam clashes 26.4%, together 68.2% (Fig. 4).
Placement expectations accounted for 16.4%, although the

VI. DISCUSSION
The results fit the AIDA logic and the literature. Educational
carousels do the work of attention by offering something useful
before asking for anything, in line with the emphasis on useful
content [10] and on creative strategy [14]. The finding that
carousels attract clicks while offers and testimonials yield more
inquiries per impression extends engagement studies [13] by
following engagement through to inquiries. On channels, the
pattern supports the inbound argument [16] only directionally:
intent-driven sources look better, but small groups prevent firm
conclusions, a caution that echoes the measurement difficulties
reported for small firms [17]. The weak education effect
suggests that messaging built on goals and affordability may
serve the firm better than segmentation by degree. Overall, the
data show where to look rather than prove which channel is
best.
VII. MANAGERIAL IMPLICATIONS
Six actions follow, each with a baseline from this study and a
proposed pilot target to be validated. (1) Automate first
response through a messaging -based CRM and set a five -
minute reply standard (qualification 63.3% to at least 70%). (2)
Pair carousels with a proof slide and a clear offer, rotate paid
creatives weekly and A/B test (inquiries per 1,000 impressions
2.67 to at least 3.2). (3) Pilot a two-part payment plan and a self-
paced entry tier, leading with outcomes before price (price -
related losses 41.8% to 30% or less). (4) Scale blogs and referral
incentives (organic share of enrolments 22.5% to at least 30%).
(5) Log timestamp, source and spend for every lead so that tests
and CAC are possible. (6) State clearly that placement is not
guaranteed and add consent text to lead forms. A 90 -day
sequence places items 1, 5 and 6 first, then 2 and 3, then 4.
VIII. LIMITATIONS AND FUTURE WORK
The sample is small, non -random and drawn over eight weeks
that overlapped with examinations, so findings are directional
and not generalizable. Several channel groups hold only 15 to
45 records, giving wide intervals. Ad -ledger, fee and lifetime -
value data were confidential, so return on investment could not
be computed. Response -speed and script effects come from
internal tracking without retained group sizes. Objections were
coded by one person, so inter -rater reliability is unknown.
Future work should follow cohorts for six to twelve months, use
at least 500 records, run controlled tests of creative format and
response time, and model multi-channel attribution.
IX. CONCLUSION
For a small digital agency, lead generation depends on more
than reach. Educational carousels earn clicks (H1 supported),
intent-driven channels appear to deliver better -qualified leads
though the organic -versus-paid difference is not statistically
confirmed (H2), and price is the largest barrier to enrolment.
Combining content -led inbound activity, rapid response and
flexible pricing offers a practical route to lower acquisition cost
and higher conversion, to be confirmed in larger, controlled
studies.
