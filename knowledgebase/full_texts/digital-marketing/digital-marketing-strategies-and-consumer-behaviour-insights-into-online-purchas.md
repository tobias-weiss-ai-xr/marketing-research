ABSTRACT
This study examines digital marketing strategies and consumer behaviour, focusing on online purchase intention for
halal cosmetics in Malaysia as purchasing shifts from physical stores to online platforms post-COVID. It introduces a
novel framework investigating the direct and indirect effects of social media engagement, halal brand awareness,
and halal brand quality on online purchase intention, with attitude as a mediating factor, based on the Theory of
Planned Behavior (TPB). An online survey was conducted in 2024 among 268 Muslim females aged 18 to 26 (Gen
Z) using 5-point Likert scale questions and convenience random sampling, with data analysed through partial
least squares structural equation modelling (PLS-SEM). Findings reveal that social media engagement, halal brand
awareness, and halal brand quality significantly influence online purchase intention, with attitude as a crucial
mediator in these relationships. These results highlight the importance of fostering positive consumer attitudes
through effective social media engagement, strong halal branding, and an emphasis on product quality. Future
research could expand the framework to include older demographics, additional regions, and moderating
factors like income level, education, and electronic word-of-mouth (eWOM) to provide deeper insights into online
purchasing behaviours for halal cosmetics.

1. INTRODUCTION
Over the past few decades, the halal industry has grown
exponentially. The term "halal" refers to consuming
products and services permitted in Islam to fulfil Muslims'
obligation toward their religion, including food and
non-food products (Alserhan, 2010). The increase in
demand for halal-certified products is substantially
contributed by the rapid growth of the Islamic religion
across the globe. Muslim population is projected to
grow by 45.7% from 2022 to 2060, an increase of over
70% from 2015, recording Islam as the fastest-growing
religion in the world (Dyvik, 2023). The increase in
aggregate purchasing power also contributed to the
growing expenditure on halal products and services
globally, including food, pharmaceutical, fashion,
tourism, cosmetics, and finance, which amounted to
USD 2.0 trillion in 2021 and is expected to rise to USD 2.8
trillion by 2025 (Tighe, 2023).
This unique positioning drives the focus on halal cosmetics
at the intersection of ethical, religious, and quality
considerations. Beyond meeting Islamic requirements,
halal cosmetics address growing consumer demand
for sustainability, safety, and cruelty-free products,
making them appealing to Muslim and non-Muslim
consumers (Shahid et al., 2018; Ahmed et al., 2019). The
global halal cosmetics market, valued at USD 61 billion
in 2017 and projected to reach USD 90 billion by 2023,
reflects its vast potential, especially in regions like Asia-
Pacific, where Muslim-majority countries dominate the
market (El Shafaki, 2022). In Malaysia, halal cosmetics
hold particular relevance due to the country's role as
a global halal hub and the rising demand for certified
products among younger consumers. This shift toward
halal-certified options aligns with broader lifestyle trends
emphasising health, ethical sourcing, and religious
compliance (Hidayat et al., 2021). Understanding
consumer attitudes and behaviours within this market is

The shift from physical to online shopping post-

branding. Online marketing is heavily utilised to reach
a bigger market while effectively disseminating the

al., 2021). The easy access to product information
through various social media platforms accelerates
consumers' awareness of halal cosmetics and highlights
its significance in promoting clean, healthy, and safe
ingredients and production (Shahid et al., 2018). But
most importantly, consumers' behaviour toward online
shopping persists post-COVID and beyond. This presses
the need for halal cosmetic companies to shift their
attention to online selling to grab this emerging market
opportunity and compete with non-halal cosmetic
companies that have been utilising online platforms way
before the COVID outbreak. Therefore, this paper aims
to support the growth of the halal cosmetic industry,
especially Malaysian halal cosmetic companies, to
accommodate the shifting trends from conventional
selling to online selling. Factors such as social media
engagement are applied to observe its influence on the
online purchase intention of halal cosmetic products to
cater to the changes in consumers' shopping behaviour
with the prevalence of online shopping platforms.
Past studies have continuously explored the purchase
intention of halal cosmetics (Briliana & Mursito, 2017;
Handriana et al., 2020; Hussain et al., 2023), but very few
of them directly explore the online purchase intention
of halal cosmetic products, except for one study by
Suparno (2020), which utilises SOR framework in the
context on Indonesian halal cosmetic market.
Despite the growing interest in halal cosmetics,
research focusing on online purchase intention in
this niche market remains limited. Prior studies have
primarily emphasised purchase intention through
physical channels (Briliana & Mursito, 2017; Handriana
et al., 2020). While Suparno (2020) utilised the Stimulus-
Organism-Response (S-O-R) framework to investigate
halal cosmetics in Indonesia, few studies have
specifically explored the applicability of the Theory
of Planned Behavior (TPB) for understanding online
purchase behaviours of halal cosmetics, especially in
the Malaysian context. Furthermore, existing research
rarely examines the combined effects of social media
engagement and halal brand attributes, awareness,
and quality on consumer attitudes and behaviours in
the post-COVID era. The pandemic has significantly
transformed consumer behaviour, emphasising digital
engagement, yet many halal cosmetic brands still need
robust digital strategies to capture the online market.
This gap necessitates a deeper understanding of how
these factors influence attitudes and online purchase
intentions, particularly for younger Muslim consumers
in Malaysia (Fatima et al., 2022). Addressing this gap,
this study integrates TPB with a focus on attitude as
the mediating variable, offering new insights into
consumer decision-making processes. This research
aims to provide actionable strategies for halal cosmetic
companies to thrive in a competitive digital landscape
by bridging these gaps.
Thus, incorporating the Theory of Planned Behaviour
(TPB) model, as suggested by many scholars within the
context of halal cosmetics (Haque et al., 2018; Khan et
al., 2020; Bhutto et al., 2022; Hussain et al., 2023), this
paper aims to identify factors contributing to the online
purchase intention of these products, with attitude
serving as the mediating factor. The TPB framework
is particularly relevant in understanding consumer
decision-making processes, as it links attitudes,
subjective norms, and perceived behavioural control
to behavioural intentions. By focusing on attitude as a
mediator, this study seeks to uncover how social media
engagement, halal brand awareness, and halal brand
quality influence consumers' intentions to purchase
halal cosmetics online. This is especially critical in a
post-COVID landscape, where consumer preferences
have increasingly shifted toward online shopping.
Furthermore, this research extends the TPB framework
by exploring its applicability to a niche market segment,
thereby contributing to the growing literature on halal
cosmetics and digital marketing strategies. In doing
so, it provides practical insights for marketers and halal
cosmetic companies looking to enhance their online
presence and appeal to the evolving needs of Muslim
consumers.
1.1 Theory of Planned Behaviour (TPB)
The Theory of Planned Behaviour (TPB) predicts
consumer behavioural intention toward a particular
object/goal and is comprised of subjective norms,
attitude, and perceived behavioural control (PBC)
(Ajzen, 1991). Behavioural belief in the TBP refers to one's
internal beliefs about taking certain actions that affect

their attitude toward actual behaviour (Handriana et
al., 2020), whereas the individual's future behaviour can
be determined by attitude, subjective norms, PBC, and
purchase intention toward the specific product (Ajzen,
1985; Chu & Chen, 2016; Garg & Joshi, 2018; Ngah et
al., 2019). TPB has been widely used in various fields,
including certified halal products (Iranmanesh et al.,
2019) and halal cosmetic merchandise (Khan et al.,
2020). Several scholars reported that buying behaviour
varies according to a particular group and conditions,
so Muslim consumers behave differently than other
consumers (Ali et al., 2018; Pratiwi, 2018). In this study,
we apply TPB to justify the model and investigate the
relationship between social media engagement, halal
brand awareness, and halal brand quality with online
purchase intention and attitude as the mediator.
While subjective norms and perceived behavioural
control (PBC) are integral components of the TPB, they
are excluded in this framework for several reasons.
Firstly, prior research has established their roles in
influencing purchase intention (Ajzen, 1991; Haque et al.,
2018), reducing the need for further validation in halal
cosmetics. Secondly, this study focuses on individual
consumer attitudes, which are a critical mediator
between brand-related factors and purchase intentions
(Mohd Nawawi et al., 2019). Lastly, in the specific
context of halal cosmetics, subjective norms may have
a diminished influence due to purchasing decisions'
personal and religious nature, where consumers
prioritise intrinsic motivations over external social
pressures (Hussain et al., 2023). This approach allows
a more focused analysis of how brand engagement
and quality perceptions shape consumer attitudes and
behaviours.
1.2 Online Purchase Intention (OPI)
Online purchase intention (OPI) is the degree of
consumers' willingness to buy a product online (Peña-
García et al., 2020). This includes any platform that
allows online purchase transactions, such as websites,
e-commerce, and social media. The prevalence of
online shopping due to restricted movement during
COVID lingers post-pandemic and has shifted the
aggregate trend from shopping physically to online
(UNCTAD, 2020). The increasing online shopping rate
in the cosmetic industry (Statista, 2023) also indicates
the relevance of online shopping as a new measure
to market halal cosmetic products. Thus, this study
concentrates on the intention to purchase halal
cosmetic products online rather than physically.
1.3 Social Media Engagement (SME)
Social media engagement (SME) refers to consumers'
willingness to engage with a brand's social media
touchpoints, such as Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, and
other platforms (McKay, 2017). It could be defined as
recurrent interactions between customer and brand
that foster emotional, psychological, or physical
commitment to that brand (Harrigan et al., 2018). When
halal cosmetic companies publish advertisements
or content through their social media platform, they
mainly intend to communicate their product to potential
customers and create brand awareness (Jamil et al.,
2022). While the context of this advertisement or content
could be for hedonic or utilitarian purposes (Calder et
al., 2009), this study focuses mainly on the utilitarian
function of disseminating product information (Chahal
& Rani, 2017). This includes how customers utilise social
media to gather information on halal products (Fatima
et al., 2022).
Due to the easy access to information through social
media, it has become a vital consideration in making
online purchase intention (Kim & Ko, 2012; Yulianto
et al., 2021) as consumers delve over the product
specifications, quality, and reviews before considering
buying the product (Yogesh & Yesha, 2014; Yulianto et
al., 2021). Precedent studies on social media marketing
revealed the connection between social media
engagement and both physical and online purchase
intention. A survey of physical and online purchase
intention of halal cosmetics positively impacts social
media engagement (Fatima et al., 2022). A study on
non-halal cosmetics also confirmed the influence of
social media on general purchase intention (Choedon
& Lee, 2020). Similarly, social media is found to have a
significant influence on purchase intention in different
contexts, such as in the context of general halal
products (Yulianto et al., 2021), the hotel industry (Yoong
& Lian, 2019), non-halal food products (Cui et al., 2019),
and green product (Sun & Wang, 2019). Thus, this paper
hypotheses that:
H1. Social media engagement has a significant
relationship with online purchase intention of halal
cosmetic products
1.4 Halal Brand Awareness (HBA)
Similar to brand awareness, halal brand awareness
(HBA) explains consumers' ability to identify and
remember brands under different conditions (Nurhayati
& Hendar, 2019). However, the expansion of the halal
dimension tackles any perceptions regarding halal
issues and commitment when it comes to halal cosmetic
products (Rachmawati et al., 2022). Thus, consumers
are deemed to have halal brand awareness if they
understand and know what halal is and its benefits,
which leads them to opt for the halal product instead
of the conventional one (Ahmed et al., 2019). It has a
crucial role in marketing halal cosmetic products as it
serves a competitive advantage against the abundant
cosmetic products in the market. Past studies have
shown that halal brand awareness positively influences
online purchase intention of halal cosmetic products
(Yulianto et al., 2021; Septiarini et al., 2022). In another

1.5 Halal Brand Quality (HBQ)
Halal brand quality (HBQ) refers to the authenticity
and trustworthiness customers expect upon buying
the product (Batey, 2008). While conventional brand
quality conveys the general quality of the product,
such as effectiveness and durability, halal brand quality
associates the basis of how the halal product meets
customers' needs for acquiring the halal product, such as
health purposes, ingredient safety, and religious needs
(Jaiyeoba et al., 2020). Precedent studies show that
halal brand quality significantly affects the purchase
intention of general halal-certified products (Jaiyeoba
et al., 2020). Another study on the purchase intention of
halal processed food indicates that halal brand quality
is important in consumer buying behaviour (Hamdan
et al., 2013). This suggests that when halal cosmetic
companies have halal brand quality, consumers will be
more willing to purchase the products than other non-
halal products. Thus, this paper hypothesises:
H3. Halal brand quality has a significant relationship with
the online purchase intention of halal cosmetic products
1.6 Attitude
Attitude (ATT) could be defined as the feeling or
perception toward something, either positive or
negative (Haque et al., 2018). It was first theorised
by Fishbein and Azjen (1975), describing attitude as
an evaluative response to act either favourably or
unfavourably towards performing certain behaviour
and most commonly used in the Theory of Planned
Behaviour (TPB) and Theory of Reasoned Action (TRA).
Hence, if the customer has a positive attitude toward
the products, they are more inclined to engage with
or even purchase them. Attitude could be categorised
into cognitive and affective attitude; cognitive attitude
refers to evaluation based on thinking or cognition
(Eagly et al., 1994; Ahn, 2023), whereas affective attitude
is solely based on feeling or emotion (Zajonc, 1980; Ahn,
2023).
1.6.1 Attitude as a Mediator Between Social Media
Engagement and Online Purchase Intention
Social media engagement (SME) significantly influences
attitudes by creating meaningful interactions between
brands and consumers. Platforms like Instagram and
TikTok enable halal cosmetic brands to showcase their
values, product quality, and certifications, fostering
trust and positive perceptions (Choedon & Lee, 2020).
Consumers access detailed product information and
real-life reviews through SMEs, shaping their cognitive
and affective attitudes toward halal cosmetics (Fatima
et al., 2022). This study hypothesises that attitudes
mediate the relationship between SMEs and online
purchase intention, emphasising the importance of
engaging content and consumer-centric strategies.
1.6.2 Attitude as a Mediator Between Halal Brand
Awareness and Online Purchase Intention
Halal brand awareness (HBA) establishes the foundation
for consumer trust by ensuring recognition of halal-
certified products. However, awareness alone may not
guarantee purchase intention unless complemented
by a positive attitude toward the product's attributes
(Ahmed et al., 2019). While HBA fosters initial interest,
factors like perceived quality and ethical sourcing are
often required to translate awareness into purchase
behaviour. This study explores how attitudes mediate
this relationship, shedding light on the nuanced role of
HBA in consumer decision-making.
1.6.3 Attitude as a Mediator Between Halal Brand Quality
and Online Purchase Intention
Halal brand quality (HBQ) directly impacts consumer
attitudes by aligning product attributes with expectations
of safety, efficacy, and religious compliance (Jaiyeoba
et al., 2020). High-quality products strengthen positive
attitudes, which, in turn, drive purchase intentions.
For halal cosmetics, attributes such as cruelty-free
certification and ingredient transparency are critical to
fostering favourable consumer perceptions (Shahid et
al., 2018). This study hypothesises that attitude mediates
the relationship between HBQ and online purchase
intention, emphasising the importance of quality-driven
branding.
Precedent studies have shown that attitude mediates
the purchase intention of halal cosmetic products,
specifically through physical purchases (Anubha,
2021; Septiarini et al., 2022). Another study adopting
the S-O-R model stated that affective and cognitive
attitudes mediate halal cosmetics purchase intention
(Suparno, 2020). Some studies claim that attitude
mediates the relationship between halal brand
awareness and purchase intention of halal cosmetics
(Septiarini et al., 2022) and halal transportation (Ngah
et al., 2019). Attitude also mediates halal brand quality
and purchase intention in the context of halal food in
Thailand (Maichum et al., 2017). A study also indicates
the mediation impact of attitude between social media
exposure and purchase intention in halal personal care
products (Mohd Nawawi et al., 2019). Electronic word-of-
mouth (eWOM), which is heavily related to social media

2. METHODOLOGY
The methodology employed in this research provides a
comprehensive explanation of the various components
that guided the study. It includes a detailed description
of the sample and procedure, outlining the criteria
for participant selection, the sampling technique
used, and the data collection process. The instrument
section elaborates on the tools and techniques used

2.1 Sample and Procedure
The population of this study is Muslim females aged 18 to
26 years old in Johor Bahru, Malaysia, a rapidly growing
population of over 1.5 million, where approximately 60%
are Muslim (Department of Statistics Malaysia, 2020).
The focus on Muslim females aged 18 to 26 is supported
by their significant engagement with social media and
online shopping. According to Statista (2021), 90% of
individuals in Malaysia aged 18-24 use social media,
with 78% relying on it for product discovery and shopping
decisions. This age group, part of Generation Z, shows
a strong interest in ethical and sustainable products like
halal cosmetics (Handriana et al., 2020), representing
a key driver of growth in the halal cosmetics market.

An online questionnaire via Google Forms was

data, respondents were carefully filtered using screening
questions to confirm their eligibility.
After one month of the data collection, 287 responses
were gathered (95.7 percent). Upon data tabulation
and cleaning, 268 responses were obtained for data
analysis (93.4 percent). All respondents fulfilled the
characteristics required for the survey, including Muslim,
female, aged around 18 to 26 years old, and currently
living in Johor Bahru to accommodate the objective
of this study. The online survey was distributed through
multiple digital platforms, including social media and
WhatsApp groups, to ensure broad reach among the
target demographic in Johor Bahru. Data collection
was conducted over one month to ensure adequate
representation and mitigate the risk of non-response
bias. The sample size of 287 respondents, with 268 valid
responses after cleaning, is appropriate based on the
guidelines for partial least squares structural equation
modelling (PLS-SEM). Hair et al. (2017) recommend a
minimum sample size of 200 for complex models with
multiple latent variables. The large sample size ensures
sufficient statistical power for analysing the relationships
between constructs while accounting for missing or
incomplete data. Furthermore, the sample size aligns
with other studies examining similar consumer behaviour
contexts, such as Handriana et al. (2020), which utilised
comparable sample sizes to validate their models.
2.2 Instrument
This study employed a structured questionnaire to
measure the constructs related to consumers’ purchase
intentions of halal-certified cosmetics. The instrument
was developed by adapting items from validated scales
in previous research, ensuring alignment with the study’s
context and objectives. Social media engagement
(SME), halal brand awareness (HBA), and halal brand
quality (HBQ) are the exogenous variables used in
this study. Attitude towards halal cosmetic products
(ATT) is the mediating variable, while online purchase
intention (OPI) becomes the dependent variable.
The measurement of each variable is adapted from
precedent studies.
Social Media Engagement is adapted from Fatima et
al. (2022), who explored how social media influences
consumer behaviour, particularly in product awareness
and engagement. Halal Brand Awareness refers to
consumers’ familiarity with and recognition of halal
certification and branding. The revised items for
this construct focus on consumers' ability to identify
certification symbols, distinguish halal cosmetic
brands from conventional ones, and understand
the benefits of halal-certified products. Halal brand
awareness is adapted from (Zhao et al., 2021), which
was also utilised previously by Ahmed et al. (2019) and
supported by Shabbir et al. (2022). Halal Brand Quality
(HBQ) reflects consumers’ perceptions of the quality
and trustworthiness of halal cosmetic products. Items
measuring HBQ address the safety of ingredients,
Islamic compliance during manufacturing, and the
perceived reliability of halal certification. Halal Brand
Quality is adapted from Ali et al. (2017), who emphasise
the importance of quality assurance in halal branding.
Attitude toward halal cosmetic products (ATT) is the
mediating variable in this study, representing the
consumer's overall evaluation of halal cosmetics. This
construct assesses how favourable or unfavourable
consumers' attitudes are toward using halal-certified
cosmetics based on their perceptions of quality, safety,
and compliance with Islamic values. The attitude
measurement is adapted from Suparno (2020), who
highlights how personal values, beliefs, and attitudes
toward halal products influence consumer decisions.
Online Purchase Intention (OPI) is the dependent
variable in this study, representing the likelihood
of consumers purchasing halal-certified cosmetic
products through online platforms. The construct
measures consumers' willingness to engage in online
transactions for halal products and is adapted from
Handriana (2020), who explored how various factors
influence online purchase behaviours. Three question
items were utilised for each variable, as shown in Table 1.
2.3 Analysis Technique
Partial least square structural equation modelling (PLS-
SEM) was applied to run this study's data analysis using
SmartPLS 4.0. This is because PLS-SEM is more robust
when analysing complex models as it could process
multivariate relationships simultaneously instead of
individually, such as in regular regression analysis. Due
to the small sample size of 254, data analysis is made
possible through a bootstrapping option in the PLS-
SEM method to ensure the data is normally distributed
(Ramayah et al., 2018).

In PLS-SEM, Ramayah et al. (2018) suggested two major
steps to conduct the analysis. Firstly, a measurement
model is performed to verify the questionnaire items'
internal consistency, validity, and collinearity. Thus, some
items would be discarded if found to have low internal
consistency and low validity by observing the value
of Cronbach's Alpha (CA), Composite Reliability (CR),
Average Variance Extracted (AVE), Outer Loadings,
Fornell Larcker's Criterion, and Cross Loading. The VIF

data analysis step, which analyses the relationship
between the latent variables. In the structural model,
this study would test the direct relationship between
social media engagement, halal brand awareness,
and halal brand quality with online purchase intention
of halal cosmetics. Additionally, since this study
incorporates a mediating variable, mediation analysis
would be conducted to measure the mediation impact
of attitude in the relationship between exogenous and
endogenous variables.
3. RESULTS
This section presents the results of the data analysis,
which includes the evaluation of the measurement
model, structural model, and mediation analysis.
The measurement model assesses the reliability and
validity of the constructs, ensuring internal consistency,
convergent validity, and discriminant validity through
indicators such as Cronbach's Alpha, Composite
Reliability (CR), Average Variance Extracted (AVE),
and factor loadings. The structural model examines
the hypothesised relationships between social media
engagement, halal brand awareness, halal brand
quality, and online purchase intention, analysing key
metrics like beta coefficients, t-values, and effect
sizes (f²) while checking for multicollinearity issues.
Additionally, the mediation analysis investigates the role
of attitude as a mediator, revealing how it influences
the relationships between the exogenous variables
and online purchase intention. Together, these analyses
provide a comprehensive understanding of the
proposed framework and its relevance in explaining
consumer behaviour in the halal cosmetics market.

The empirical findings of this study reveal that all
exogenous variables—social media engagement,
halal brand awareness, and halal brand quality—
significantly and positively influence the online purchase
intention of halal cosmetic products. Firstly, social media
engagement strongly affects online purchase intention,
highlighting its critical role in driving consumers toward
online platforms for halal cosmetics.

This finding is consistent with a growing body of literature
emphasising social media strategies' importance in
shaping consumer purchasing behaviour. Effective
social media strategies, particularly engaging content
and interactive campaigns, have significantly enhanced
online purchasing intentions (Kim & Ko, 2012; Fatima et
al., 2022). Engaging content, such as visually appealing
posts, storytelling, and user-generated content, is pivotal
in fostering emotional and cognitive connections
between consumers and brands (Harrigan et al., 2018).
When consumers feel emotionally invested in a brand,
their purchase intentions increase as they perceive
the brand as more relatable and trustworthy (Hudson
et al., 2016). Furthermore, interactive campaigns and
influencer endorsements are particularly effective tools
for halal cosmetic brands, as they capture attention
and facilitate direct engagement with the target
audience. Interactive campaigns, including polls,
contests, and live streaming, give consumers a sense
of participation, enhancing their attachment to the
brand (Kim & Ko, 2012). Research has shown that inviting
consumer participation through social media leads
to increased loyalty and favourable brand attitudes
(Hwang et al., 2019). Influencer marketing, in particular,
is crucial in halal cosmetics, as it helps build trust and
credibility among consumers. Influencers, especially
those who align with the values and lifestyle of the target
demographic, serve as trusted sources of information,
making their endorsements highly impactful (Choedon
& Lee, 2020). These endorsements help create a sense
of authenticity, vital in the halal cosmetic sector, where
consumers are often highly discerning and values-driven
in their purchase decisions (Nadeem & Kaur, 2020). By
leveraging influencers with similar cultural and ethical
values, halal cosmetic brands can establish stronger,
long-lasting relationships with their audience, fostering
trust and consumer loyalty (Tariq & Rahman, 2021).
Similarly, halal brand awareness significantly contributes
to online purchase intention, indicating that consumers
who recognise and trust a brand's halal certification are
more likely to buy its products online (Ali & Raza, 2018;
Ismail & Bakar, 2020). This reinforces the importance of
halal certification as a key factor in Muslim consumer
decision-making. Halal brand quality also exhibits a
strong positive effect, demonstrating that consumers
place significant value on high-quality halal cosmetics
when making purchasing decisions. Halal certification
reassures consumers, ensuring the product aligns with
their religious and ethical values, increasing trust and
purchase likelihood (Zailani et al., 2019). Furthermore,
halal brand quality is another critical determinant of
consumer purchasing behaviour. A high-quality halal
cosmetic brand meets religious standards and satisfies
the consumer’s expectations for product effectiveness
and safety. Consumers are increasingly placing a high
value on the quality of halal cosmetics, viewing them
as premium products that offer both ethical assurance
and superior performance (Yusof et al., 2018; Rahman
et al., 2020). This growing emphasis on quality reflects
a broader trend in which consumers, particularly in the
halal market, seek products that combine ethical and
functional benefits (Hassan & Riaz, 2017). The strong
positive effect of halal brand quality further reinforces
the notion that Muslim consumers are willing to pay
a premium for products that meet their religious and
quality standards, which is decisive in their purchase
intentions (Nadeem & Zaman, 2020).
The study findings also provide valuable insights into
the mediating role of attitude in the relationships
between social media engagement (SME), halal brand
awareness (HBA), halal brand quality (HBQ), and online
purchase intention (OPI) for halal cosmetic products.
Firstly, attitude significantly mediates the relationship
between SME and OPI and between HBQ and OPI,
indicating that positive consumer attitudes are crucial in
enhancing the effects of social media engagement and
brand quality on purchase intention. Specifically, SMEs
benefit from this mediation, as effective social media
strategies—such as engaging content, interactive
campaigns, and influencer endorsements—can shape
positive attitudes that, in turn, drive online purchases.
Similarly, the mediating effect of attitude in the HBQ-OPI
relationship underscores the importance of delivering
high-quality halal products that align with consumer
expectations of safety, ethical sourcing, and Islamic
compliance. These results align with prior studies (e.g.,
Mohd Nawawi et al., 2019; Anubha, 2021), emphasising
that fostering a positive attitude enhances the impact
of marketing efforts on purchase intention.
However, attitude does not mediate the relationship
between HBA and OPI, as the findings reveal an
insignificant and negative indirect effect. This suggests
that more than mere awareness of a brand's halal
certification may be needed to stimulate online
purchase intention, even with a positive attitude. It
implies that while halal certification is a critical factor
in building consumer trust, it may require reinforcement
through other means, such as brand differentiation,
enhanced marketing strategies, or stronger value
propositions. Simply knowing that a product is halal-
certified may be sufficient to motivate purchasing
behaviour with additional value-driven marketing
efforts or more apparent differentiation in a crowded
market. Therefore, brands may need to go beyond
certification and focus on emphasising their unique
selling points, offering superior customer experiences,
or leveraging influencer endorsements. Such strategies
could enhance consumer trust and, in turn, further
boost purchase intentions, particularly when there is no
direct mediation by attitude (Kim & Ko, 2012; Choedon
& Lee, 2020).

Halal cosmetic companies should promote their core

5. CONCLUSION
This research provides valuable insights for Malaysian
halal cosmetic companies and marketers seeking to
drive sales growth through online platforms. The findings
demonstrate that social media engagement, halal
brand awareness, and halal brand quality significantly
influence online purchase intention, with attitude
playing a key mediating role. These results contribute
to the halal cosmetics literature by offering a robust
framework highlighting the importance of fostering
positive consumer attitudes through well-executed
corporate and brand strategies. Halal cosmetic
companies are encouraged to invest in initiatives such
as strategic public relations, strong value propositions,
and excellent customer service to enhance consumer
trust and online purchase behaviour. The study's
limitations primarily stem from the sample, which
focused on Malaysian Muslim females aged 11 to 26
residing in Johor Bahru. Future research should test this
framework in different regions and include broader
demographics, such as Generation X and Y, who may
exhibit different behaviours due to heightened interest
in halal cosmetics. Moreover, examining moderating
factors like income level, education level, and electronic
word-of-mouth (eWOM) could yield more profound
insights into the nuances of online purchasing behaviour.
These expansions would further validate and refine the
proposed framework, offering greater applicability
across diverse contexts.
