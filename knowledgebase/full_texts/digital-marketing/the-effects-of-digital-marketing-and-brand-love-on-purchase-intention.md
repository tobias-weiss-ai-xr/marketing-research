 This study examines the eﬀects of digital marketing and brand love on consumer
purchase intention, with customer experience as a m ediating variable at CALF
Coﬀee Shop. A quantitative explanatory research des ign was employed,
involving 150 consumers in Jakarta and Bekasi selecte d through purposive
sampling. Data were collected using a structured qu estionnaire and analyzed
through partial least squares structural equation m odeling (PLS-SEM) using
SmartPLS 4.0. The ﬁndings reveal that digital market ing has signiﬁcant positive
eﬀects on customer experience (β = 0.461, p < 0.001) and purchase intention (β
= 0.437, p < 0.001). Similarly, brand love positive ly inﬂuences customer
experience (β = 0.396, p < 0.001) and purchase inte ntion (β = 0.371, p < 0.001).
Customer experience also has a signiﬁcant positive eﬀect on purchase intention
(β = 0.203, p = 0.013). These ﬁndings highlight the  importance of integrating
digital marketing strategies, emotional brand connec tions, and customer
experience management to strengthen consumer purcha se intention.
Practically, CALF Coﬀee Shop should prioritize enga ging digital content,
meaningful brand interactions, and consistent servi ce experiences to enhance
consumer interest in its products. Further analysis  of indirect eﬀects is required
to establish the statistical signiﬁcance of the proposed mediating relationships.

II.  Literature Review

2.1.  Digital Marketing

Digital marketing refers to the strategic use of di gital technologies and online channels to
communicate value, engage target audiences, and sup port marketing objectives. Unlike traditional
marketing, digital marketing enables interactive co mmunication, targeted content distribution, and ong oing
consumer engagement across multiple platforms (Kann an & Li, 2017). Chaﬀey and Ellis-Chadwick (2022)
emphasize that eﬀective digital marketing requires the integration of digital channels, content, customer data,
and measurable marketing activities. Accordingly, d igital marketing should be understood not simply as
online promotion but as a coordinated process of creating and maintaining meaningful interactions between
brands and consumers.
Social media provides an important setting for these interactions. De Vries et al. (2012) demonstrated
that characteristics of brand posts are associated with brand-post popularity, highlighting the import ance of
content design in encouraging consumer responses. S imilarly, Hollebeek et al. (2014) conceptualized
consumer brand engagement in social media as a multidimensional construct involving cognitive, emotional,
and behavioral dimensions. These perspectives sugge st that digital marketing may inﬂuence consumers by
increasing exposure to brand information, facilitating interaction, and creating opportunities for engagement.
However, digital exposure alone does not guarantee purchase intention. Consumers may encounter the same
promotional content but respond diﬀerently dependin g on their previous experiences, perceptions of bra nd
credibility, and emotional relationships with the b rand. Digital marketing therefore needs to be exami ned
alongside other consumer-related constructs to explain how marketing activities relate to purchase intention.
In the present study, digital marketing is conceptu alized as a marketing-related antecedent of custome r
experience and purchase intention.

2.2.  Brand Love

Brand love represents a strong emotional relationsh ip between consumers and a brand. Carroll and
Ahuvia (2006) examined brand love as an important c onsumer response associated with brand-related
outcomes. Batra et al. (2012) subsequently develope d a richer conceptualization of brand love, describ ing it
as a multidimensional construct that encompasses em otional, cognitive, and behavioral elements. Their
perspective indicates that brand love extends beyon d simple satisfaction or favorable brand evaluation .
Bagozzi et al. (2017) contributed to this literature by developing and validating a practical scale for measuring
brand love. Their work supports the importance of treating brand love as a distinct construct rather than using
it interchangeably with general brand attitude or s atisfaction. Langner et al. (2015) also examined th e
emotional nature of brand love in comparison with i nterpersonal love, emphasizing the complexity of
emotional bonds involving brands.
In the coﬀee shop context, brand love may emerge wh en consumers develop strong aﬀective
connections with a brand through its identity, products, communication, and accumulated experiences. Such
attachment may be associated with a greater willing ness to consider the brand when making purchase
decisions. Nevertheless, emotional attachment does not necessarily lead to a purchase on every occasio n
because purchasing behavior may also depend on price, accessibility, preferences, and situational constraints.

2.3.  Customer Experience

Customer experience encompasses consumers’ cognitiv e, emotional, sensory, social, and behavioral
responses to interactions with a ﬁrm and its oﬀerings. Verhoef et al. (2009) explained that customer experience
is shaped by multiple determinants, including the s ocial environment, service interface, retail atmosp here,
and consumer characteristics. Lemon and Verhoef (20 16) further emphasized the importance of
understanding customer experience across the customer journey, which includes interactions before, during,
and after a purchase. Customer experience is also related to, but distinct from, brand experience. Brakus et al.
(2009) conceptualized brand experience through sens ory, aﬀective, intellectual, and behavioral respons es
elicited by brand-related stimuli. While brand expe rience focuses on responses to brand stimuli, custo mer
experience provides a broader perspective on intera ctions across the customer journey. This distinctio n is
relevant to coﬀee shops because consumers may form impressions through social media content, product
presentation, outlet atmosphere, ordering processes , and employee interactions. Customer experience ma y
connect marketing activities with consumer response s because consumers interpret marketing messages
through their actual and anticipated interactions w ith a brand. Digital content that is informative an d
engaging may contribute to favorable perceptions of  the brand experience, while a strong emotional
connection may inﬂuence how consumers interpret sub sequent interactions. Nevertheless, the mediating
role of customer experience cannot be established s olely from these theoretical arguments; it requires
empirical testing of the indirect relationships spe ciﬁed in the research model.

2.4.  Purchase Intention

Purchase intention refers to a consumer’s stated li kelihood or willingness to purchase a particular
product or service. It is conceptually distinct from actual purchase behavior because intention does not always
translate into action. Ajzen (1991), through the th eory of planned behavior, explained that behavioral
intentions are inﬂuenced by attitudes, subjective n orms, and perceived behavioral control. This framew ork
provides a general theoretical basis for understand ing why consumers may develop intentions to purchas e
while recognizing that situational constraints can aﬀect the relationship between intention and behavi or.
Spears and Singh (2004) examined the measurement of  brand attitude and purchase intention, emphasizing
the need to distinguish consumers’ evaluations of a  brand from their intentions to purchase it. Dodds et al.
(1991) also demonstrated the relevance of price, br and, and retailer information to buyers’ evaluation s and
purchase intentions. Together, these studies suggest that purchase intention is a consumer response that can
be shaped by marketing information, brand evaluatio ns, and perceived value. In the present study, purc hase
intention is the focal outcome variable. It should be measured using items that directly reﬂect consum ers’
intention or likelihood to purchase from CALF Coﬀee  Shop. Items concerning recommendations, brand
preference, and future purchases may provide relate d information, but their conceptual alignment with
purchase intention should be carefully established during instrument development.

showed that brand-post characteristics are relevant  to social media responses. These perspectives prov ide a
basis for expecting digital marketing activities to  be associated with customer experience. In a coﬀee  shop

2.6.  Brand Love and Customer Experience

Brand love may shape customer experience because co nsumers’ emotional relationships with a

2.7.  Digital Marketing and Purchase Intention

Digital marketing may inﬂuence purchase intention b y providing product information, increasing

depend on the relevance and credibility of the info rmation, consumers’ existing brand perceptions, and  their
actual needs. A promotional message may attract attention without necessarily producing purchase intention.
Thus, the proposed relationship should be evaluated  in the speciﬁc context of CALF Coﬀee Shop.

H3: Digital marketing has a positive eﬀect on purch ase intention.

Brand love may be associated with purchase intentio n because emotional attachment can

love, while Langner et al. (2015) further examine i ts aﬀective characteristics. These studies support the
conceptual distinction between emotional brand atta chment and more general brand evaluation. In the
coﬀee shop industry, emotional attachment may encou rage consumers to consider a preferred brand when
choosing where to purchase beverages. Nevertheless,  purchase intention may also be constrained by pric e,
location, availability, and competing alternatives.  The relationship between brand love and purchase
intention should therefore be understood as a testa ble association rather than an inevitable behaviora l
outcome.

H4: Brand love has a positive eﬀect on purchase int ention.

2.9.  Customer Experience and Purchase Intention

Customer experience may inﬂuence purchase intention  by shaping consumers’ evaluations of the
brand and their expectations of future interactions . Verhoef et al. (2009) and Lemon and Verhoef (2016 )
emphasize the multidimensional nature of customer e xperience and the importance of interactions
throughout the customer journey. Brakus et al. (200 9) also demonstrate the relevance of consumers’
responses to brand-related stimuli. In a coﬀee shop , consumers may evaluate not only beverage quality but

also depend on other factors. Empirical research in volving coﬀee shop customers in Jakarta has examine d
customer experience in relation to repurchase inten tion, supporting the relevance of this construct in  the

the present study addresses the latter directly.

H5: Customer experience has a positive eﬀect on pur chase intention.

2.10.  The Mediating Role of Customer Experience

Customer experience is proposed as a mediating vari able because it may help explain how
marketing-related factors are associated with purch ase intention. Lemon and Verhoef (2016) emphasize t hat
consumers’ experiences emerge across multiple touch points, while Verhoef et al. (2009) highlight the r ange
of factors that shape those experiences. These pers pectives suggest that marketing activities and emot ional
brand relationships may be connected to purchase in tention partly through consumers’ experiences with a
brand. Digital marketing may inﬂuence the informati on and interactions consumers encounter, while bran d
love may aﬀect their emotional interpretation of th ose interactions. Customer experience may then be
associated with consumers’ willingness to purchase. This proposed mechanism does not imply that mediation
is established in advance; rather, it provides a th eoretical rationale for testing the indirect eﬀects  within the
research model.
The coﬀee shop context oﬀers a relevant setting for  this investigation because consumers may
encounter the brand through both digital communicat ion and physical service interactions. Recent work on
coﬀee shop customers in Jakarta has examined digita l marketing and customer experience in relation to
repurchase intention, but the present study focuses  on the combined roles of digital marketing and bra nd
love in explaining purchase intention through custo mer experience (Liando & Keni, 2025). The contribut ion
therefore lies in testing the proposed integrated m odel in the context of CALF Coﬀee Shop, rather than
claiming that the individual constructs or relation ships have never been studied.

H6: Customer experience mediates the relationship b etween digital marketing and purchase intention.
H7: Customer experience mediates the relationship b etween brand love and purchase intention.

3.2.  Population and Sampling

The target population consisted of consumers who had purchased beverages or other products from

based on the following eligibility criteria: (1) be ing at least 17 years old, (2) having made a purcha se at CALF

3.3.  Data Collection and Research Instrument

Primary data were collected using a structured questionnaire consisting of ﬁve sections: respondents’
demographic characteristics, digital marketing, bra nd love, customer experience, and purchase intentio n. All
constructs were measured using a ﬁve-point Likert s cale ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (stron gly
agree). The measurement items were adapted from previously published research instruments relevant to the
study constructs. A preliminary instrument test inv olving 30 respondents was conducted separately from  the
main study. These respondents were not included in the main sample of 150 respondents. The preliminary
test was intended to assess the clarity and consist ency of the questionnaire items before the main dat a
collection.

3.4.  Variable Operationalization

Digital marketing was operationalized through conte nt quality, personalization, engagement,
interactivity, and calls to action, drawing on the frameworks discussed by Kotler et al. (2021) and Ch aﬀey
(2022). Brand love was measured through brand passi on, emotional attachment, self-brand integration, a nd
brand commitment, based on Batra et al. (2012) and Langner et al. (2015). Customer experience was assessed
through sensory, emotional, cognitive, behavioral, and social dimensions, following Lemon and Verhoef
(2016). Purchase intention was operationalized usin g purchase interest, intention to recommend, brand
preference, and future purchase plans, as speciﬁed in the original research instrument.  e questionnaire items
and indicators were used to represent their respect ive latent constructs in the PLS-SEM model. The
operationalization of each construct was aligned wi th the study’s conceptual framework and the
measurement items administered to respondents.

3.5.  Data Analysis

The data were analyzed using SmartPLS 4.0 through t wo main stages: measurement model
assessment and structural model evaluation. The mea surement model was evaluated to assess the validity
and reliability of the constructs. Convergent validity was examined using outer loadings and average variance

their statistical signiﬁcance. Bootstrapping with 5,000 subsamples was conducted to evaluate the signiﬁcance
of the hypothesized relationships. The coeﬃcient of  determination (R²), eﬀect size (f²), and predictiv e
relevance (Q²) were also considered to assess the e xplanatory and predictive performance of the model.  The
mediating role of customer experience was examined by assessing the speciﬁc indirect eﬀects of digital
marketing and brand love on purchase intention through customer experience. The bootstrapping procedure
was used to evaluate the statistical signiﬁcance of  these indirect eﬀects. The mediation hypotheses we re

because coeﬃcient comparisons are speciﬁc to the constructs, measures, and sample used. From a managerial
perspective, CALF Coﬀee Shop may use digital platfo rms to communicate product information, announce
promotions, and maintain consistent brand messaging. Nevertheless, the study does not test the eﬀectiveness

f.  The Potential Mediating Role of Customer Experience
The proposed conceptual model positions customer ex perience as a mediator between digital
marketing and purchase intention, as well as between brand love and purchase intention. The reported results
show that digital marketing and brand love are posi tively associated with customer experience, and tha t
customer experience is positively associated with p urchase intention. These direct relationships are
theoretically compatible with the proposed mediatio n framework. Nevertheless, the signiﬁcance of the
individual direct paths does not establish that eit her indirect eﬀect is statistically signiﬁcant. Med iation must
be assessed by examining the speciﬁc indirect eﬀect  of digital marketing on purchase intention through
customer experience and the speciﬁc indirect eﬀect of brand love on purchase intention through custome r
experience. The corresponding bootstrap conﬁdence i ntervals and p-values should be reported.
Methodological guidance on PLS-SEM emphasizes the need to evaluate the estimated indirect eﬀects directly
rather than infer mediation solely from separate pa th tests (Hair & Alamer, 2022). Accordingly, the av ailable
results are insuﬃcient to conﬁrm whether customer e xperience signiﬁcantly mediates either relationship .
Once the speciﬁc indirect-eﬀects output is availabl e, the discussion should report the indirect coeﬃci ent,
bootstrap t-statistic, p-value, and conﬁdence inter val for each pathway. The direct and indirect eﬀects should
then be considered together to determine whether th e evidence supports indirect-only mediation,
complementary mediation, competitive mediation, or no statistically signiﬁcant mediation.
