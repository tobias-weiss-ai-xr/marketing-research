Abstract
Purpose: This study analyzes the social media marketing strategy of PT Speaking ID in acquiring clients through Instagram @speakingid,
with a focus on the implementation of Integrated Marketing Communications (IMC) elements in the era of media convergence. The
research explores how digital-only marketing, personal branding  of the organization leader, visual identity consistency, and co ntent
strategy collectively contribute to client acquisition and retention.
Methodology: This study employs a qualitative descri ptive approach with a case study strategy. Data were collected through in-depth
interviews with three key informants, Sara Neyrhiza (Director and Social Media Manager), Budi Pasadena (Video Content Team), an d
Nonik Retno (Social Media Team), as well as digital observati on of Instagram accounts @speakingid and @neyrhiza. Thematic
interpretive analysis was applied, and data validity was ensured through methodological and source triangulation.
Results: Five out of seven Integrated Marketing Communications (IMC) elements by Philips Kotler on Marketing 5.0: Technology for
Humanity were identified and validated in PT Speaking ID's communication  practice: Interactive/Digital Marketing, Direct Marketing,
Personal Selling, Advertis ing (limited), and Public Relation s & Events. Sales Promotion in c onventional form (price discounts) was
consciously avoided, replaced by consistent educational content as the primary attraction mechanism. Personal branding of Sara Neyrhiza
(@neyrhiza) functions as the primary trust layer for corporate cl ient acquisition, while visual identity consistency (red #9B1B 30, with
poppins typography) reinforces all IMC elements across platforms.
Applications/Originality/Value: This research fills a gap in the literature by integrating the personal branding of organizational leadership
with a digital-only marketing strategy in the training services sector. The findings contribute to the development of digital m arketing
communication studies, particularly the use of Instagram in educational service industries. The study demonstrates that a pull-marketing
approach grounded in educational content can effectively replace both traditional outreach and conventional promotional tactics.

Introduction
The landscape of marketing communication has entered a complex phase over the past two decades, marked by the convergence of
media. This era is characterized by comprehensive digitalization, shifts in audience motivation, and transformation of communic ation
policies and markets (Nugroho & Simangunsong, 2024). Media convergence is not merely a migration from print to digital, but a
systemic disruption of how media is produc ed, distributed, and consumed. Traditional me dia such as brochures, pamphlets, and ve rbal
promotions have been replaced by real-time, interactive, and algorithm-based digital platforms.
According to Mukti (2023), media convergence supported by technological development enables media to be used digitally, combining
previously separate platforms into integrated digital formats and transforming the production, distribution, and consumption of
information. In the marketing context, medi a convergence presents  both challenges and opportunitie s. On one hand, companies mus t
adapt to audience behavior that has become more critical, mobile, and community-based. On the other hand, social media platforms like
Instagram offer strategic spaces for buildin g brands holistically, not only attracting clients, but also shaping identity, cred ibility, and
loyalty.
According to the We Are Social & Data Reportal report published in 2025, Indonesia had 143 million active social media users,
representing 50.2% of the population, with Internet penetration reaching 72.7% or ap proximately 207 million people. Instagram i s one
of the most popular platforms, especially among younger generations. Dwivedi et al. (2021) argue that social media marketing ha s

PT Speaking ID's digital marketing strategy is demonstrably effective in building brand presence and acquiring clients. The @speakingid
Instagram account serves as the primary platform for educational content, class promotion, and audience interaction. Based on in-depth

channel proving highly effective for conversion.
This study is grounded in the Integrated Marketing Communications (IMC) theory of Philip Kotler, which emphasizes the importanc e
of message consistency and digital technology utilization in buil ding long-term client relationships. This research fills a gap  in the
existing literature by integrating personal branding of organizational leadership with digital-only strategy in the training services sector,
an area minimally explored in prior scholarship. The research que stion guiding this study is: How does PT Speaking ID implement  its
social media marketing strategy through Instagram @speakingid in the era of media convergence?
The novelty of this study becomes clearer when positioned against prior Instagram-based IMC research beyond the immediate case
context. Existing studies such as Rosita et al. (2023) and Chesaria & Sunaryo, (2023) examined IMC implementation on Instagram for
an established fashion e-commerce brand and a leadership-focused st artup respectively, both of wh ich retained multiple institut ional
marketing functions and a degree of formal promotional structure. Thoha Aziz et al. (2023) likewise found that most digitalizing training
institutions in Indonesia continue to operate hybrid marketing models that combine digital and conventional channels. PT Speaki ng ID
diverges from all three precedents by operating on a digital-only architecture from inception, with no transitional or residual traditional
marketing activity, and by anchoring corporate client acquisition specifically in the founder's personal brand rather than in i nstitutional
advertising or price-based promotion. This combination, a singl e-founder-led training institution that substitutes personal bra nding for
both traditional outreach and formal sale s promotion, has not been documented in prior IMC scholarship on the Indonesian traini ng
services sector, which is the specific gap this study addresses.
Literature Review
Era of Media Convergence
The era of media convergence represents a phase of communication transformation in which boundaries between print, electronic, and
digital media become blurred due to the integration of information technology. Nugroho & Simangunsong (2024) explain that media
convergence drives audiences to become more active in selecting, producing, and distributing content. Mukti (2023) adds that
digitalization enables all content forms to be encoded in digital formats, making information distribution faster, more interac tive, and
algorithm-based.
In Indonesia, this phenomenon is evidenced by increasing social media penetration. The We Are Social & Data Reportal report published
in 2025 recorded 143 millio n active social media users, equivalent to 50.2% of the population, with penetration reaching 72.7% or
approximately 207 million people. This indicates that social media has become the primary space for companies to build brands, attract
clients, and strengthen identity.
Integrated Marketing Communications (IMC)
Integrated Marketing Communications (IMC) is a strategic approach that integrates multiple communication channels, advertising, sales
promotion, public relations, direct marketing, and digital media, into a single consistent and relevant message (Kotler et al., 2021). IMC
was first introduced in the early 1990s as a response to media fragmentation and the need for uni form messaging. Chaffey & Elli s
Chadwick (2022) further note that digital channels have fundam entally extended the IMC toolkit, enabling organizations to integ rate
paid, owned, and earned media into a unified and measurable communication strategy.

4003
In the digital era, IMC has evolved into a strategy utilizing technology to create personalized and authentic customer experiences. Kotler's
Marketing 5.0 emphasizes that digital technology should be used to strengthen the human elements of communication, namely trust ,
relevance, and emotional connection. According to Kotler et al. (2021), the seven elements of IM C include: (1) Advertising, (2)  Sales
Promotion, (3) Events & Experiences, (4) Public Relations & Publicity, (5) Online & Social Media Marketing, (6) Mobile Marketin g,
and (7) Direct & Database Marketing.
Theodora (2021) demonstrated that IMC is directly linked to th e enhancement of brand equity, as cross-channel message coordinat ion
strengthens perceived quality and brand awareness. Thamara et al., (2025) further emphasized that in the social media era, IMC plays a
crucial role in reinforcing brand equity through message consistency and the use of social proof.
Personal Branding in the IMC Framework
Personal branding is the strategic process of building an authentic professional image that distinguishes an individual from competitors
in the digital ecosystem. Venciute et al. (2024) assert that pe rsonal branding is a proactive, planned, and strategic process o f
communicating one's unique value, especially through social media platforms. In the era of media convergence, the boundaries between
individuals and institutions increasingly blur; the personal br anding of organizational leaders often becomes a representation of the
values and identity of the organization.
Within the IMC framework, personal branding functions as a human element that bridges the strategic messages of a company with the
emotional needs of the audience. Rehman et al. (2022) emphasize that the consistency of personal narrative can strengthen trust  and
improve client retention. Rahayu et al. (2024) confirm that pe rsonal branding effectively communicates unique individual values  and
differentiates individuals from competitors.
Visual Identity in IMC
Visual identity is a non-verbal language that reinforces bran d narrative and facilitates brand recognition. Lelis & de A. Kreut z, (2024)
state that visual identity carries stories that help frame mean ing and brand recognition. Visual elements such as colors, typog raphy,
layout, and symbols function as implicit message conveyors that shape audience perception.
Within the IMC framework, visual identity serves as a connector between strategic messages and audience experience. Gunawan et al.
(2021) demonstrate that visual identity consistency on Instagram plays an important role in increasing engagement and strengthe ning
brand equity. (Chesaria & Sunaryo, 2023) similarly found that the use of structured and visually consistent content on Instagra m
positively contributes to brand awareness and improves overall marketing performance. At PT Speaking ID, the use of red (#9B1B3 0)
as the primary corporate color is not merely aesthetic but symbolic, red is associated with energy, courage, and confidence, aligning with
the values conveyed in public speaking training.
Method
This study employs a qualitative descriptive approach with a case study strategy, enabling a deep understanding of the digital
communication practices implemented by PT Speaking ID through its Instagram account @speakingid. The research focuses on
identifying and analyzing the implementation of Integrated Marketing Communications (IMC) elements within a digital-only marketing
environment. Data were collected through in -depth interviews and digital observation of the @speakingid and @neyrhiza Instagram
accounts, encompassing feed content, reels, stories, captions, and audience interaction patterns. Secondary data were drawn fro m prior
relevant studies on IMC, personal branding, and visual identity in the context of social media.
The selection of informants was deliberate and purposive, ensuri ng triangulation across strategic, operational, and executional
perspectives within the organization. The three informants are as follows.
Sara Neyrhiza (Zahra Noor Eriza) serves as Director and Social Media Manager of PT Speaking ID. As the founder and primary decision-
maker, Sara holds full authority over communication strategy, content direction, and brand positioning. Her involvement since t he
organization's inception in 2007 and her dual role as both organizational leader and public-facing practitioner make her the mo st
authoritative and strategically significant informant. She has been working with Speaking ID in its formal capacity since 2018, overseeing
all digital marketing activities. Sara was selected because her perspective encompasses the entirety of the organization's strategic logic,
creative decisions, and brand philosophy.
Budi Pasadena serves as the video content team. Having been involved with Speaking ID for several years, Budi is responsible fo r
producing and editing training documentation videos that are later repurposed as promotional content on social media. His role occupies
a critical operational layer, translating training sessions into multimedia assets that feed into the brand's digital presence.  Budi was
selected because his experience handling hundreds of training recordings provides a unique operational lens on how content is generated,
curated, and strategically deployed in the marketing funnel.
Nonik Retno serves as the social media team, responsible for designing visual content, uploading posts, and responding to audie nce
interactions across digital platforms. As the newest member of the team, Nonik represents the executional layer closest to the day-to-day

content pipeline and audience engagement. Her position at the interface between management direction and public interaction mak es
her a key informant for understanding how strategy is operationalized in practice. Nonik was selected to provide perspective on  the
consistency between leadership intention and actual execution at the platform level.
Data analysis was conducted through an interpretive thematic analysis approach as described by Lochmiller (2021) involving deep
reading of all data, initial coding based on Kotler's IMC elements, thematic grouping according to emerging communication strat egy
patterns, and contextual interpretation linking communication practices with marketing outcomes. Data validity was ensured by
comparing perspectives across all three informants and cross-referencing informant accounts with observable patterns on the
@speakingid and @neyrhiza accounts.
The coding procedure followed three sequential stages. First, open  coding was applied to intervie w transcripts and observation notes
line by line to identify initial descriptive labels grounded in the informants' own language. Second, axial coding grouped thes e open
codes into categories aligned with Kotler's seven IMC elements, allowing patterns such as digital-only channel use, personal br anding,
and the deliberate avoidance of sales promotion to emerge as distinct thematic clusters. Third, selective coding integrated these categories
into the core narrative themes presented in the Results section,  linking them back to the research question. To strengthen the
trustworthiness of these findings, four criteria were addressed. Credibility was supported through source and methodological
triangulation across the three informants and the Instagram observation data, together with informal member checking in which
preliminary interpretations were verbally confirmed with Sara Neyrhiza during follow-up communication. Dependability was maintained
through a consistent interview and coding protocol applied unif ormly across all three informants. Confirmability was supported by
anchoring every interpretive claim to direct interview excerpts or observable account features rather than researcher inference  alone.
Transferability is addressed descriptively by providing detailed contextual information about PT Speaking ID's organizational structure
and digital practices, enabling readers to judge the applicability of the findings to comparable training service contexts.
Results
Based on in-depth interviews with three informants and digital observation of the @speakingid and @neyrhiza Instagram accounts, five

following presents the findings organized by IMC element, supported by direct interview excerpts from each informant.

are carried out exclusively through digital platforms, includin g Instagram (@speakingid and @speaking.clinic), TikTok, website

Personal Selling (Digital)
Personal selling at Speaking ID manifests through direct interaction in Instagram DMs and WhatsApp, characterized by a needs-
identification approach rather than a sales pitch. Nonik Retno described the interaction process:
“From the DM or chat, we directly interact with the audience, we talk and interact according to the audience's
needs. From there we give direction: 'If your need is like this, then the training you shou ld join is like this.' We
provide according to the audience's needs.”
Sara Neyrhiza reinforced this with the after-sales dimension of personal selling that she personally manages:
“I provide after-sales service; after joining the training, participants feel free to discuss further. So many of them
trained five years ago, but they still keep in touch with me if they have difficulties.”
The high rate of repeat orders from both individual and institutional clients is a measurable indicator of the effectiveness of this approach.
Advertising (Limited Paid Advertising)
Instagram Ads are used in a limited and strategic manner, activa ted only for specific moments such as the opening of offline cl asses.
Sara Neyrhiza articulated the philosophy behind this restraint:
“We use ads only at certain moments. As a business owner, I don't like burning money just for ads. So if we're
burning money, or in other words promoting, it's usually only when there is an offline class, we promote it one or
two weeks before. But if there is no such need, we use organic channels.”
Both Budi and Nonik confirmed that paid advertising is used se lectively for broader reach, and not all content is boosted. This  reflects
the efficiency prioritization that characterizes Speaking ID's operations overall.

“I want to make a statement that Speaking ID has never given discounts. Speaking ID never does price promotions.
We tend to use what is safe, like educ ational content. Personally, I am a business owner who gives first, then takes

Cross-Informant Consistency: A Validation Narrative
Across all major aspects of Speaking ID's marketing communication strategy, the accounts of the three informants are notably consistent.
Sara Neyrhiza, Budi Pasadena, and Nonik Retno each independently confirmed that the institution has never distributed printed brochures
or proposals, and that social media content constitutes the sole channel for client acquisition. Sara described the guiding principle; Budi
confirmed the absence of traditional marketing from his operational vantage point; and Nonik validated it as the frontline executor.
Regarding content structure, all three informants corroborated the monthly thematic system. Sara initiates the theme and daily format;
Budi supplies the video raw material; and Nonik executes the visual design and posting schedule. The informants also agreed
unanimously on the absence of price discounts, with each providing a distinct framing: Sara presented it as a philosophical stance, Budi
as a factual operational reality, and Nonik as a user-facing policy.
On paid advertising, all three confirmed that Instagram Ads are used only at specific moments and not for general content. Rega rding
Sara's personal branding, Sara identified @neyrhiza as the corporate client entry point; Budi described Sara's physical and professional
durability as the primary value proposition; and Nonik spoke to the effectiveness of the @neyrhiza-@speakingid content collaboration.
Finally, concerning testimonials and portfolio as the primary PR m echanism, Sara emphasized visible credibility, Budi highlighted the
persuasive power of real testimonials over formal PR, and Nonik noted how consistent portfolio content elevates account credibility.
Discussion
The findings reveal a coherent and distinctive pattern of IMC implementation that departs fro m the conventional multi-channel
integration model documented in the broader literature. While Rehman et al. (2022) and Theodora (2021) established that IMC
effectiveness in the social media era depends on cross-channel me ssage consistency, the PT Speaking ID case demonstrates that t his
consistency can be achieved even within a radically compressed channel set, where Instagram and WhatsApp alone carry the full weight
of marketing communication. The institution's digital-only architec ture is not a constraint but a deliberate design choice, and  its
effectiveness challenges assumptions that broader channel diversification correlates with stronger IMC outcomes.
The most analytically distinctive finding is the role of the founder's personal brand (@neyrhiza) as the primary client acquisi tion
mechanism for corporate accounts. This finding extends Venciute et al. (2024) framework on leader personal branding in professi onal
platforms, which was developed primarily in the context of larg e organizations with separated marketing and leadership function s. At
Speaking ID, the leader is simultaneously the brand, the practitioner, and the primary trust signal. Budi Pasadena's observatio n that
Speaking ID sells Sara's durability rather th an a curriculum represents an unusual i nversion of conventional institutional mark eting,
where organizational attributes typically supersede individual ones. In the contemporary training sector, where service quality is highly
personal and difficult to evaluate before purchase, this person ification of brand equity functions as a potent risk-reduction m echanism
for prospective clients.
The deliberate non-application of Sales Promotion is equally theoretically significant. Conventional IMC literature, including Kotler et
al. (2021), positions sales promotion as a tactical tool for demand stimulation through short-term price incentives. Speaking ID's rejection
of this element in favor of sustained educational content repres ents what could be described as value-first pull marketing, an approach
more aligned with content marketing and relationship marketing ph ilosophies than with classical promotional mechanics. Rosita e t al.,
(2023) demonstrated that IMC strategies implemented through Inst agram, particularly through visually appealing content and hash tag
utilization, are effective in raising brand awareness and buildin g a positive brand image, even without reliance on conventiona l
promotional tactics. This approach echoes findings by Firmansyah  (2023), who demonstrated that social media marketing grounded in
authentic value delivery builds brand trust more effectively than promotional campaigns. However, the Speaking ID case pushes t his
finding further, not only does educational content build trust, it fully replaces the promotional function, suggesting that in high-credence
service markets where expertise and trust are the dominant purchase criteria, promotional discounting may actually undermine ra ther
than enhance brand positioning.
The visual identity system employed by Speaking ID also merits  analytical attention. The deliberate migration from yellow to re d
(#9B1B30) reflects a strategic repositioning from a cheerful, yout h-oriented aesthetic to a mature, professional, and corporate -oriented
identity. This transition is consistent with Lelis & de A. Kreutz (2024) argument that visual identity is not merely decorative but carries
symbolic and cultural meaning that frames audience perception. The alternating red-white posting schedule described by Nonik Re tno
creates a form of visual rhythm that makes the account immediately recognizable even in a scrolling feed, functioning as a form  of
ambient branding. Gunawan et al. (2021) correlation between visual consistency and brand equity on Instagram is strongly corroborated

here, with the additional observation that visual consistency also appears to function as a signal of organizational reliabilit y and
commitment, qualities highly valued in institutional procurement decisions.
Compared with other training institutions studied in the literature, Speaking ID's model is distinctive in three respects. First, it operates
without any traditional marketing whatsoever, a rarity even among digitally native SMEs in the Indonesian training sector, where Thoha
Aziz et al. (2023) found that most institutions maintain hybrid marketing approaches. Second, it achieves high-value corporate client
acquisition without a formal sales force or active prospecting, relying entirely on inbound pull dynamics. Third, it maintains brand
consistency across hundreds of posts and multiple years without a dedicated marketing team, a testament to the head-direction model in
which the founder's personal oversight substitutes for institutional marketing infrastructure.
A closer critical comparison with the Instagram-based IMC studies cited earlier sharpens this distinctiveness. Rosita et al. (2023) found
that ERIGO, an established fashion brand, achieved brand awareness through visually appealing content and hashtag strategy layered on
top of an already-formalized multi-channel marketing structure,  meaning Instagram functioned as one component within a broader
promotional apparatus rather than as the near-exclusive channel it represents for Speaking ID. Similarly, Chesaria & Sunaryo, ( 2023)
documented Instagram-based brand awareness building at Pemimpin.id, a startup in the leadership ecosystem, but did not report a
comparable substitution of institutional advertising with a single leader's personal brand as the primary trust mechanism for c orporate
acquisition. Set against Thoha Aziz et al. (2023) finding that most  LKP-type institutions retain hybrid marketing structures pr ecisely
because digital conversion remains unreliable, Speaking ID's case is theoretically significant because it demonstrates that full conversion
reliability can be achieved without  hybridization, provided personal branding an d educational content jointly substitute for th e trust-
building functions that formal advertising and sales promotion typically perform. This suggests that the digital-only, personal ity-
anchored model may be more readily transferable to other small, expertise-driven service providers than to larger or product-ba sed
brands, where consumer trust rests less on an identifiable individual.
The (Estaswara & Said, 2024) review of thirty years of IMC practice in Indonesia noted an evolution from tactical coordination toward
technology-based strategy. The Speaking ID case represents a cont emporary instantiation of this trajectory: a digital-first, technology-
dependent, yet fundamentally human-centered IMC system in which algorithmic reach is the vehicle but personal authenticity is t he
engine. This synthesis may represent an emerging paradigm partic ularly well-suited to knowledge-intensive service SMEs in post-
pandemic Indonesia, where the collapse of geographic constraints (accelerated by the 2020 online training boom) has opened nati onal
markets to regional specialists who can project credibility digitally with the same authority previously reserved for institutions with large
physical footprints.
Conclusion
This study analyzed the implementation of PT Speaking ID's soci al media marketing strategy thro ugh Instagram @speakingid within
the framework of Philip Kotler's Integrated Marketing Communications (IMC). Based on in-depth interviews with three informants and
digital observation, five of the seven IMC elements were identified as actively implemented.
Interactive/Digital Marketing is the most dominant element, manifested through monthly theme-based structured content on Instagram,
TikTok, website, and WhatsApp, with no use of traditional media whatsoever. Direct Marketing is implemented through differentiated
WhatsApp Business channels and a bio link as a direct conversion bridge. Digital Personal Selling occurs through need-oriented DM
and WhatsApp interactions, strengthened by Sara Neyrhiza's person al after-sales service. Paid Ad vertising is applied in a limit ed and
strategic manner only for the opening of specific classes. Public Relations & Events are built through website and social media portfolio
documentation, participant testimonials, and Sara Neyrhiza's active involvement in various offline events and communities.
Two elements not applied conventionally are Sales Promotion, co nsciously avoided as Speaking ID has never offered price discoun ts,
and Mobile Marketing as an independent channel. In their place, educational content value serves as the primary promotional instrument,
consistent with the philosophy of "giving first, then taking results" upheld by Sara Neyrhiza.
Sara Neyrhiza's personal branding (@neyrhiza) functions as the primary trust layer driving corporate client acquisition, while Speaking
ID's visual identity consistency (red #9B1B30, Poppins typography, real human photographs) reinforces all IMC elements across
platforms. The client retention mechanism is supported by repeat orders based on institutional vendor lists, word of mouth, and personal
after-sales service.
For future research, it is recommended to combine qualitative approaches with quantitative analysis of Instagram engagement dat a to
obtain a more comprehensive picture of the effectiveness of each  IMC element. A comparative study with similar training institu tions
could also provide a broader perspective on best practices for digital-only marketing in the educational services sector.
