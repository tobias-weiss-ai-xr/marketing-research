 A B S T R A C T
Increasing competition among coffee shops in Indonesia has
encouraged businesses to adopt effective marketing strategies to
sustain consumer purchase intention. This study examines the
influence of digital marketing and brand image on customers'
purchase i ntention at Janji Jiwa Coffee Shop, Pontianak, West
Kalimantan, using the Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) as its
theoretical foundation. Within the TPB framework, brand image
represents consumers' attitudes toward the brand, while digital
marketing contributes to shaping social perceptions that influence
purchase intention. This quantitative study collected data from 100
respondents selected through purposive sampling using structured
questionnaires distributed at the Janji Jiwa outlet on Jl. Purnama 1
between January and February 2026. The hypotheses were tested
using Partia l Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -
SEM) with SmartPLS 3.0. The measurement model met all
reliability and validity criteria, with outer loadings above 0.70, AVE
above 0.50, composite reliability above 0.90, and HTMT values
below 0.90.  The results indicate that digital marketing has the
strongest positive effect on purchase intention (β = 0.546, t = 5.629,
p < 0.001), followed by brand image (β = 0.389, t = 4.102, p <
0.001). Together, both variables explain 71.2% of the variance in
purchase intention (R² = 0.712), while the model demonstrates
satisfactory predictive relevance (Q² = 0.483). These findings
suggest that integrating digital marketing with strong brand imag e
development can effectively enhance consumer purchase intention
in the competitive coffee shop industry.

INTRODUCTION
Coffee shop going has turned into a routine part of urban life across Indonesia,
and this shift has driven a nationwide boom in new outlets. The trend is not confined
to the largest metropolitan centers secondary cities such as Pontianak in West
Kalimantan have also seen a growing variety of coffee shop concepts aimed at

different customer segments. Because this growth is now widespread rather than
isolated, brands find themselves competing in a crowded marketplace where value
propositions must be constantly updated to keep consumers engaged.
Among the brands competing in this space, Janji Jiwa is notable for having expanded
quickly since its 2018 launch, relying on affordable pricing, a clean modern look, and
an active social media presence to become a familiar name to consumers. Even so,
conditions in Pontianak reveal how easily loyalty can erode in this industry: the brand
once operated three outlets in the city but now runs only one (Kompas.Id, 2024). Such
a decline invites a meaningful question, namely whether Janji Jiwa's present digital
marketing efforts and brand image remain strong enough to retain, and ideally expand,
its customer base in this particular market.
Two constructs frequently appear at the center of marketing discussions on what drives
consumers to buy digital marketing and brand image. Digital marketing refers broadly
to the use of online tools, including social media, content marketing, search engine
optimization, and targeted advertising, to build awareness, engage audiences, and
prompt purchasing behavior (Kotler, n.d. 2021). Brand image, meanwhile, describes
the mental picture and evaluative judgments that consumers form about a brand as a
result of exposure, direct experience, and communication  (Keller, 2021) . Prior
research links both constructs to purchase intention, but most studies have treated them
separately, and few have explored their combined effect within secondary Indonesian
cities, a context that remains underrepresented in the literature.
Researchers investigating comparable settings  (Zulfikar et al., 2022)  have reported
positive links between these two constructs and purchase intention. A gap nonetheless
persists: relatively few studies place both variables inside a single structural model,
and even fewer focus specifically on the coffee shop sector in provi ncial Indonesian
cities, where consumer behavior can differ substantially from that observed in larger
metropolitan areas. Comparable joint models tested outside the coffee shop sector,
however, support the plausibility of a combined effect (Kamilla, 2026)  found that
digital marketing significantly improves both brand image and purchase intention
among Ultra Milk consumers in Semarang, (Mutiara et al., 2024)  reported a partial-
mediation pattern for local fashion products in Surabaya explaining 55 percent of the
variance in purchase intention, and (Putra et al., 2022) found that digital marketing and
brand image jointly predicted 59.5 percent of purchase interest for Footstep Footwear
consumers in Bandung.
Addressing this gap, the present research applies the Theory of Planned
Behavior (TPB) as a lens for understanding how marketing -related factors shape
consumers' willingness to purchase. TPB holds that intention arises from three
components is attitude, subjective norms, and perceived behavioral control. Here,

LITERATURE REVIEW AND HYPOTHESIS DEVELOPMENT
This study is about the Theory of Planned Behavior. The Theory of Planned
Behavior says that what people want to do is affected by three things: how they feel
about it what they think others will think and how easy or hard it is to do. The way
people feel about something is what we call attitude. It is like their opinion, about a
behavior. Then there are norms, which is what people think others will think of them
if they do something. And there's perceived behavioral control, which is how easy or
hard peopl e think it will be to do something. The Theory of Planned Behavior is
important to understand how people make decisions about what they want to do. In the
study, brand image is conceptualized as the attitudinal camponent, as acaptures
consumers’ overall evaluation and preception of the coffee shop. Digital marketing,
perticularly through social media and online platfroms, plays a dual role by shaping
subjective norms through peer influence, reviews, and socal visibility, and by
enhancing percived behavioral control through easier access to information and
purchasing channels. Purchase intention is thus treated as the direct behavioral
outcome influenced by these psychological factors.
Digital marketing is broadly understood as the strategic deployment of
internet-based technologies and platforms to communicate value propositions, nurture
consumer relationships, and drive commercially relevant behaviors (Chaffey & Ellis-
Chadwick, 2019). For businesses operating in the food and beverage sector, where
physical visibility is inherently localized, digital channels extend market reach, enable
real-time consumer dialogue, and provide cost -efficient mechanisms for stimulating
purchase interest (Kotler, n.d. 2021).
A growing body of empirical work attests to the purchase -activating role of
digital marketing.  (Zulfikar et al., 2022) , established that social media marketing
positively predicts purchase intention among young Indonesian consumers,

highlighting the particular salience of platform -based engagement for this
demographic.(Nainggolan, 2024) , similarly found that digital marketing enhances
purchase interest by raising brand visibility and fostering parasocial connections
between brands and their online audiences. Such digital engagement helps cultivate
favourable attitudes and emotional responses toward the brand, which in turn translate

on purchase intention have also been reported in other Indonesian retail contexts,
including dairy products (Kamilla, 2026) . local fashion (Mutiara et al., 2024) , and
footwear (Putra et al., 2022), reinforcing the generalizability of this relationship across
product categories. On this basis, the following hypothesis is advanced:
H1: Digital marketing exerts a positive and significant effect on consumers'
purchase intention.
Brand image encapsulates the configuration of consumer beliefs, associations,
and evaluations attributable to a specific brand, formed cumulatively through direct
consumption experiences, marketing communications, and social context (Keller,
2021). A well -constructed brand image serves as a cognitive shortcut that reduces
decision-making complexity, signals product quality, and attenuates perceived
purchase risk  all of which facilitate a favorable disposition toward buying  (Aaker,
1991). In markets characterized by high product homogeneity, such as the coffee shop
sector, the distinctiveness and warmth of a brand's image can constitute a decisive
competitive advantage.
Evidence from the Indonesian market corroborates the purchase-enabling role
of brand image. (Saputro et al., 2024), documented a significant brand image effect on
purchase intention among student consumers, a segment that closely mirrors the
demographic profile of Janji Jiwa's customer base. (Kristinae, 2025), extended this line
of inquiry to show that brand image not only exerts a direct effect on purchase intention
but can also amplify the impact of digital marketing when the two are aligned.
Connecting these findings to the attitudinal component of TPB, it follows that positive
brand evaluations predispose consumers toward purchase action. This positive
relationship is echoed across other Indonesian consumer categories: (Kamilla, 2026)
reported that brand image significantly strengthens purchase intention among Ultra
Milk consumers, (Mutiara et al., 2024) found brand image to be the stronger of the two
predictors of purchase intention among Surabaya fashion consumers, and (Putra et al.,
2022), similarly showed that brand image exerts a significant positive effect on
purchase interest for Footstep Footwear products. Accordingly, the second hypothesis
is stated as:
H2: Brand image exerts a positive and significant effect on consumers' purchase
intention.

RESEARCH METHODS
A positivist, quantitative research design was adopted, consistent with the
study's objective of testing theoretically derived hypotheses through statistical analysis
of numerical data. The cross -sectional survey method was chosen as it provides an
efficient means of gathering attitudinal and behavioral intention data from a defined
consumer population at a single point in time.
The accessible population comprised all individuals who had patronized the
Janji Jiwa outlet situated on Jl. Purnama 1, Kecamatan Akcaya, Pontianak Selatan
during the data collection window. Because no population register exists for walk -in
coffee shop customers, a non-probability approach was necessary. Purposive sampling
was applied, restricting participation to individuals who (1) had completed at least one
purchase at this specific outlet within the preceding six months and (2) were aged 17
years or abo ve. Consumers unable to satisfy these criteria were not invited to
participate.
Sample adequacy was assessed against the ten-observations-per-indicator rule
advocated (Ringle & Sarstedt, 2017) . With 20 indicators distributed across three
constructs, this rule establishes a floor of 100 respondents. Complementarily, a
statistical power analysis conducted using G*Power 3.1 (Faul et al., 2009) for a
multiple regression with two predictors, a medium effect size (f² = 0.15), α = 0.05, and
desired power of 0.80 indicated a minimum required sample of 68 observations. The
minimum R² method proposed by Cohen (1992)  similarly estimated the minimum
sample size at 85 respondents for detecting a minimum R² of 0.10 with 80% power at

the 5% significance level. The obtained sample of 100 thus satisfies all three criteria
by a comfortable margin. Data collection yielded 110 completed questionnaires; after
screening for incomplete responses, 100 valid instruments were retained for analysi s,
representing a retention rate of 90.9%.
Survey instruments were administered face -to-face at the outlet premises
across a six -week window spanning January to February 2026. Trained research
assistants approached customers at the point of transaction, briefly outlined the study's
academic purpose , confirmed eligibility, and invited voluntary participation.
Anonymity and confidentiality were assured throughout. Before full deployment, the
questionnaire underwent a pilot trial with 20 consumers to identify ambiguous
wording; minor revisions were incorporated on the basis of pilot feedback.
All latent constructs were captured using multi-item reflective scales anchored
to a five-point Likert continuum (1 = strongly disagree; 5 = strongly agree). The digital
marketing scale comprised 10 items adapted from Saputro et al., (2024), covering
social media content appeal, information reach, and audience interaction. Brand image
was assessed with 4 items drawing on  (Keller, 2021), tapping perceptions of brand
distinctiveness, trustworthiness, quality, and recognition. Purchase intention was
measured via 6 items adapted from  (Sasono, 2025) , capturing willingness to buy,
repurchase intention, and likelihood of recommending the brand.
PLS-SEM, executed through SmartPLS 3.0  (Zambrano & Escobar -sierra,
2026), served as the primary analytical tool. This variance-based estimation technique
was preferred over covariance -based alternatives given its established suitability for
modest sample sizes, its tolerance for non -normality in observed data, and its dual
capacity for simultaneous measurement and structural model assessment (Ringle &
Sarstedt, 2017) . Analysis followed a two -stage protocol: the outer (measurement)
model was evaluated first to establish reliability and validity, whereupon the inner
(structural) model was assessed to examine hypothesized paths and quantify predictive
capacity.

Effect Size (F Square)
Effect size estimates reported in Table 7 provide a construct -level supplement
to the path coefficient results. Using Cohen’s (1988) benchmarks as adapted for PLS-

category. These values indicate that the omission of either construct from the structural
model would produce a substantial reduction in the explained variance of purchase
intention, underscoring the practical indispensability of both variables. The
comparatively larger effect size for Brand Image relative to its path coefficient (β =
0.389) warrants note: f² captures the unique variance contribution of each predictor
after controlling for the other, and the elevated f² for Brand Image signals that its
explanatory contribution is particularly distinctive rather than redundant with the
variance alread y explained by Digital Marketing. This pattern suggests a
complementary rather than substitutive relationship between the two constructs,
reinforcing the theoretical rationale for incorporating both within a unified model.

Model Predictive Power
 Table 5 consolidates the R² and Q² statistics used to characterize model -level

The preponderant role of digital marketing relative to brand image likely
reflects the composition of the study's sample. With two -thirds of respondents aged
between 20 and 25, and the majority identifying as students, this is a population whose
consumption decisions are heavily mediated by online information environments. For
such consumers, a brand's digital presence constitutes a primary touchpoint for product
discovery, social validation, and ultimately purchase activation. This aligns with the
argument advanced (Kotler, n.d. 2021) that digitally native generations experience the
marketing funnel primarily through screen -mediated interactions, making online
engagement strategies disproportionately influential relative to traditional brand -
building mechanisms.
The second hypothesis also received empirical support: brand image
demonstrates a meaningful positive association with purchase intention (β = 0.389, p
< 0.001), confirming H2. This finding resonates with the broader theoretical argument
that a cohesive and positively valenced brand image reduces decision uncertainty and
instills the consumer confidence necessary to initiate purchase behavior (Keller, 2021).
For Janji Jiwa, whose brand identity centers on accessibility, community orientation,
and a contemporary local character, these qualities appear to generate sufficiently
favorable brand associations to meaningfully elevate purchase intention among its core
audience.
When considered alongside the work of (Kristinae, 2025), the current findings
extend evidence for brand image as an important antecedent of purchase intention
across various Indonesian market contexts and product categories. The comparatively
smaller coefficient for brand image relative to digital marketing ma y not signal
weakness per se, but rather reflects the particular stage of brand development: as an
expanding local brand competing against many visually similar alternatives, Janji
Jiwa's brand image may not yet carry the same degree of differentiation as its digital
engagement strategy. This reinforces the strategic imperative to invest in brand
distinctiveness as a longer-term competitive buffer.

Behavioral in explaning purchase intention eithin a digital marketing context. Digital
marketing contributes to shaping subjectif norms by exposing consumers to peer

This study extends the literature by applying the Theory of Planned Behavior
to digital marketing within emerging local markets. It shows that digital marketing can
shape both subjective norms and perceived behavioral control at the same time, while
brand image functions as a reflection of consumer attitude. This finding highlights the
evolving role of digital environments in shaping multiple psychological drivers of
behavior within a single interaction space. The high combined R 2 (0.712) and
confirmed predictive relevance (Q 2 = 0.483) validate the choice of digital marketing
and brand image as theoretically motivated and empirically potent predictors of
purchase intention within this industry and geography.
For practitioners, these results translate into several concrete guidance points.
Coffee shop operators should allocate substantial resources to maintaining a high -
quality, high-frequency digital marketing presence  particularly on platforms popular
with younger consumers  with emphasis on authentic storytelling, community
engagement, and visually compelling content. Concurrently, sustained investment in
brand image differentiation is essential: consistent visual identity standards, excellence
in service interactions, and di stinctive experiential design can collectively strengthen
brand associations in ways that outlast any single promotional campaign. A strategic
alignment between digital marketing output and brand image messaging is likely to
generate reinforcing effects that compound over time.
This study demonstrates that digital marketing and brand image significantly
influence purchase intention within the framework of the Theory of Planned Behavior.
Digital marketing strengthens purchase intention by shaping social influence and
perceived ease of purchasing while brand image reinforces positive consumer attitudes
toward the brand. Drawing on data from 100 consumers at Janji Jiwa Coffe Shop in
Pontianak, digital marketing emerged as the more influential predictor ( β = 0.546),
with brand image contributing a secondary but nonetheless important effect (β =
0.389). The structural model accounted for 71.2% of the variance in purchase intention
(R² = 0.712) and exhibited strong predictive relevance (Q² = 0.483), underscoring the
theoretical and practical value of the two-predictor framework examined here.
Several constraints qualify the scope of these conclusions. The single -outlet,
single-city data collection restricts generalizability to other Janji Jiwa locations or to
the broader Indonesian coffee shop market. The cross -sectional design captures
consumer perceptions at a single moment and is therefore unable to illuminate how
these relationships might evolve across different competitive or seasonal conditions.
Only two independent variables were modeled, leaving open the possibility that

90

unmeasured factors such as perceived value, service quality, or customer satisfaction
account for some portion of the residual variance in purchase intention. Finally, the
exclusive reliance on self -report survey data introduces the possibility of common
method variance, a limitation that future research should seek to mitigate through
multi-method designs.
Future investigations would benefit from widening the geographic scope of
sampling to encompass multiple Janji Jiwa outlets across different Indonesian cities,
thereby enabling cross-market comparisons and strengthening the generalizability of
findings. Longitudinal panel designs would afford the opportunity to trace how digital
marketing exposure and brand image evaluations evolve over time and whether their
purchase-activating effects persist, diminish, or intensify with repeated consumer
contact. The exi sting model could also be enriched through the incorporation of
mediating constructs such as brand trust, consumer engagement, or emotional brand
attachment and moderating variables such as consumer involvement level, prior brand
experience, or digital media literacy  that might clarify the boundary conditions
governing the observed relationships.
