Abstract

This community service article develops a practical digital marketing strategy for Dwi Kristy
Mutiara, a pearl-jewelry small and medium enterprise in Mataram, Lombok, to strengthen
its international marketing performance. The program is designed as an applied case-based
intervention using a preliminary digital -footprint review, partner needs assessment,
literature synthesis, participatory training, strategic co -design, and a performance -
monitoring plan. The initial diagnosis indicates that international growth cannot be achieved
merely by increasing social-media posting. The enterprise needs a clearly prioritized foreign
market, a bilingual and trust-building digital catalogue, consistent product specifications,
stronger Lombok-origin storytelling, an integrated conversion system, export-ready pricing
and logistics, and measurable customer data. The proposed strategy integrates international
segmentation and positioning, a digital customer funnel, social commerce, cross -border
marketplaces, the official InaExport business-to-business directory, international buyer
development, and a weekly marketing dashboard. A phased 180-day roadmap is presented
to build digital foundations, validate foreign-market demand, conduct limited channel
experiments, and scale only the combinations of market, product, message, and channel that
demonstrate acceptable conversion and contribution margins. The article contributes an
implementable framework for craft SMEs seeking to transform local cultural value into
credible, discoverable, and measurable international market offerings without requiring
complex technology or excessive promotional spending.

INTRODUCTION
Digital technologies have changed the internationalization options available to small and
medium enterprises (SMEs). A small firm can now display products, communicate with prospective
buyers, test market responses, receive inquiries, and build international relationships without first
establishing a physical branch abroad. Online platforms can reduce information and search costs,
widen access to networks, and support the acquisition of foreign-market knowledge. However,
technology does not automatically generate international sales. Digital adoption must be supported
by market focus, reliable product information, organizational capability, customer trust, logistics,
and disciplined measurement (OECD, 2021; Da Rocha et al., 2024).
The strategic challenge is particularly important for craft and jewelry SMEs. Jewelry is a visually
attractive product category, but an online buyer cannot directly inspect the material, color, size,
workmanship, comfort, or authenticity before purchasing. The digital interface must therefore
perform several functions simultaneously: attract attention, explain the product, communicate
cultural and aesthetic value, reduce perceived risk, provide evidence of quality, enable convenient

Dwi Kristy Mutiara is an SME located in Mataram, Lombok, that markets Lombok pearl jewelry.
Its publicly visible digital profile presents products such as sea-pearl bracelets, while public
promotional posts show participation in national exhibitions, including an event in Jakarta in May
2026 (Dwi Kristy Mutiara Lombok, 2025, 2026). These activities indicate that the enterprise already
has product and promotional experience that can support broader market development.
Nevertheless, participation in exhibitions and social-media visibility do not by themselves create a
repeatable international marketing system. International performance requir es the ability to
identify priority markets, communicate a distinctive value proposition, convert attention into
qualified inquiries and orders, deliver products reliably, and use data to improve decisions.
Indonesia’s SME-development institutions increasingly connect digitalization with export
readiness. Bank Indonesia emphasizes that SMEs seeking global markets need market intelligence,
strong branding, international e-commerce, digital literacy, production planning, and sustainable
supply-chain capability (Bank Indonesia, 2024). The Ministry of Trade’s InaExport platform similarly
provides an official business-to-business directory intended to connect Indonesian suppliers with
worldwide buyers and trade representatives (InaExport, 2026). These facilities create
opportunities, but an SME can benefit from them only when its product data, business profile,
communication process, and operational readiness meet buyer expectations.
This article aims to: (1) identify the main digital and international-marketing challenges that
may constrain Dwi Kristy Mutiara; (2) develop a target-market and brand-positioning strategy; (3)
design an integrated digital funnel and cross -border channel architecture; (4) formulate
international-marketing performance indicators; and (5 ) provide a phased implementation
roadmap suitable for an SME with limited resources. The practical novelty lies in integrating cultural
storytelling, digital conversion, export readiness, B2C and B2B channels, and performance
governance in one operational framework for a Lombok pearl-jewelry enterprise. The proposed
framework is intended for community service and managerial application; it does not claim post-
intervention sales effects that have not yet been measured.
LITERATURE REVIEW AND HYPOTHESIS FORMULATION
This article is an applied community service case and does not test statistical hypotheses. The
literature review is used to construct the diagnostic framework and justify the proposed
intervention.
Digital Marketing Capability and SME Performance
Digital marketing is more than the use of social media. It is the coordinated management of
digital channels, content, customer data, technology, and measurement to achieve marketing
objectives. For SMEs, its value lies in scalable market access, direct customer interaction, rapid
experimentation, and the possibility of measuring customer responses at relatively low cost.
Empirical research generally associates digital marketing activities with improvements in customer
engagement and SME performance, but the effect depends on the firm’s capacity to integrate
online advertising, search visibility, social media, customer relationships, and analytics (Sharabati
et al., 2024).
A weak digital capability produces activity without learning. Firms may post frequently while
failing to know which country, audience, product, message, or channel contributes to inquiries and
profitable orders. A stronger capability links every digital asset to a strategic function: discovery,
evaluation, conversion, retention, market intelligence, or partner development. It also assigns
responsibilities, standardizes product data, and uses performance indicators to guide resource
allocation.

Digital Platforms and SME Internationalization
Digital platforms can help small firms overcome some disadvantages of limited size by
providing access to foreign customers, buyer networks, payment infrastructure, market
information, and reputation mechanisms. A recent review of small-firm internationalization shows
that digital platforms can reduce entry barriers and support international opportunity
development, but adoption alone does not guarantee internationalization. Positive outcomes are
stronger when firms invest in digital skills, international marketing capability, organizational
processes, websites, and foreign-market relationships (Da Rocha et al., 2024).
Social media contributes to export performance indirectly through the quality of international
business contacts, improved understanding of customer preferences, competitive intelligence, and
brand awareness (Eid et al., 2020). Similarly, international performance can benefit from digital
presence and network signals, yet followers and visibility should not be treated as final outcomes.
They are intermediate assets whose business value depends on their conversion into trustworthy
relationships and transactions (Kromidha & Robson, 2021).
For market entry, a hybrid logic is appropriate. Digital channels can support prospecting,
market testing, communication, and transactions, whereas relationships w ith distributors,
boutiques, trade representatives, hotels, tourism operators, and business-matching institutions can
provide local knowledge and credibility. International entry is therefore not a choice between
online and offline methods; it is a coordin ated combination of relational, digital, and hybrid
approaches (Watson et al., 2018).
Brand Storytelling, Trust, and Product Evidence
Social-media marketing can shape brand equity when it provides relevant interaction,
customization, trend awareness, word of mouth, and entertainment value (Godey et al., 2016). For
pearl jewelry, the most important digital task is to translate intangible product and origin attributes
into visible evidence. Buyers need information concerning pearl type, dimensions, color variation,
materials, craftsmanship, product care, packaging, authenticity, price, shipping, and return
conditions. Cultural narratives concerning Lombok can strengthen differentiation, but they should
not substitute for factual product specifications.
Trust is particularly important in cross-border transactions because buyers face uncertainty
concerning seller identity, product quality, payment security, delivery, and dispute resolution. The
brand should therefore combine emotional storytelling with operationa l proof: consistent
photography, transparent specifications, buyer testimonials, business identity, clear policies,
response standards, traceable quotations, and documented quality controls. A credible brand story
must be supported by evidence rather than by broad claims that buyers cannot verify.
International Marketing Performance
International marketing performance is multidimensional. Revenue and export-sales growth
are important, but they are lagging indicators and may conceal high acquisition costs, low margins,
excessive returns, or dependence on one buyer. SMEs also need leading indicators that reveal
whether their international marketing system is developing: target -country reach, qualified
inquiries, quotation acceptance, website or marketplace conversion, repeat orders, distributor
prospects, response speed, and delivery reliability.
A useful performance system links the customer funnel to financial and operational outcomes.
Awareness metrics show whether the brand reaches the intended foreign audience. Engagement
and lead metrics indicate relevance and buyer interest. Conversion metrics evaluate the ability to
generate orders. Financial metrics determine whether growth produces contribution margin.
Relationship and operational metrics assess repeat business, B2B pipeline quality, complaints,
shipping problems, and customer retention. This approach prevents the enterprise from mistaking
popularity for international marketing success.

RESEARCH METHODS
The program uses an applied case-based community service design with a participatory
problem-solving approach. The unit of analysis is the international digital marketing system of Dwi
Kristy Mutiara, including target markets, brand positioning, product information, digital content,
sales channels, customer communication, pricing, order processing, shipping readiness, and
performance measurement.
The intervention is organized into five stages. First, the needs-assessment stage reviews the
enterprise’s product catalogue, social-media presence, current customer segments, previous
promotional activities, available digital analytics, sales process, and managerial constraints. Second,
the diagnostic stage evaluates the digital customer journey from discovery to repeat purchase and
identifies gaps in market focus, trust, conversion, and operations. Third, the training stage covers
international segmentation, bilingual content, digital storytelling, social commerce, marketplace
selection, buyer communication, export pricing, and marketing metrics. Fourth, the co-design stage
converts the material into concrete enterprise assets, including a priority -market matrix,
international product catalogue, content calendar, buyer-response templates, channel plan, and
dashboard. Fifth, the monitoring stage reviews implementation indicators and determines which
experiments should be continued, revised, or stopped.
The preliminary assessment reported in this article is based on publicly visible business
information, the enterprise’s digital footprint, relevant institutional guidance, and academic
literature. It is not an internal financial, legal, quality, or export -compliance audit. During
implementation, primary data should be collected through management interviews, product and
process observation, customer-message review, digital-platform analytics, cost and price records,
and structured feedback from prospective buyers. Sensitive business information should be
accessed only with partner authorization.
The analytical tools include: (1) international segmentation and target-market scoring; (2)
value-proposition analysis; (3) a digital funnel covering reach, engagement, inquiry, conversion,
retention, and advocacy; (4) a channel-role matrix; (5) an export-readiness checklist; and (6) a
weekly performance dashboard. The intervention is evaluated through completion of strategic
assets, improvement in digital-process discipline, and subsequent pre-post indicators. Because the
program has not yet produced longitudinal data, the results section presents a strategic diagnosis
and proposed solution rather than causal claims regarding international sales.

The diagnosis is consistent with internationalization research showing that digital-platform
adoption does not guarantee foreign-market entry. Digital tools generate value when they are
combined with capabilities, organizational processes, market knowledge, and external relationships
(Da Rocha et al., 2024). Consequently, the recommended intervention begins with strategic focus
and export readiness rather than with an immediate increase in advertising expenditure.
Strategic Objective and International Value Proposition
The strategic objective is to build a repeatable system that converts the cultural and product
value of Lombok pearls into profitable international customer relationships. The enterprise should
not attempt to serve every country or every jewelry buyer simultaneously. It should identify a
narrow initial market, develop a credible offer, conduct controlled experiments, and scale only after
obtaining evidence of demand and operational feasibility.
A proposed value proposition is: “Authentic Lombok pearl jewelry presented with transparent
product information, gift-ready design, and responsive personal service.” This wording is a working
proposition and must be validated against the enterprise’s actual production process, material
specifications, and customer feedback. Its purpose is to unite four dimensions: origin, product
evidence, aesthetic value, and service reliability.

Excessive segmentation creates a hidden execution cost because every segment requires different
product combinations, content, pricing, service, and follow-up. A focused pilot allows the firm to
discover whether the main barrier is awareness, trust, price, shipping, product fit, or response

Global-Ready Digital Brand Assets
Before entering a new platform, Dwi Kristy Mutiara should create a reusable digital asset
system. The core asset is a bilingual master catalogue covering the priority products rather than
every available item. Each product should have a unique code, standardized English and Indonesian
name, clear photographs, short descri ption, pearl and supporting -material information,
dimensions, weight, color-variation notice, care instructions, packaging, available customization,
production or preparation time, stock status, consumer and wholesale price basis, and shipping
limitations. Claims concerning authenticity, materials, or production should be documented and
used consistently.
Photography should support both emotional appeal and buyer verification. Each priority
product requires a clean main image, close-up detail, scale reference, wearing or usage image,
packaging image, and short video. The visual identity should use consistent backgrounds, lighting,
typography, and product codes. International buyers should be able to recognize that listings on
different channels refer to the same product.
The brand story should be organized into content that buyers can verify: the relationship
between pearls and Lombok, the people behind the enterprise, design inspiration, selection and
production processes, product-care education, and evidence of participation in exhibitions or buyer

Content Strategy for International Trust and Conversion
The content calendar should be organized around six pillars: (1) Lombok and pearl -origin
education; (2) product specification and care; (3) design, styling, and gifting situations; (4)
craftsmanship and people; (5) trust evidence, testimonials, exhibitions, and delivery proof; and (6)
product offers and calls to action. Each post should have one objective and one audience. A content
item aimed at awareness should not be evaluated only by direct sales, while a product offer should
not be evaluated only by reach.
English content should not be a literal translation of Indonesian promotional language. It
should answer the questions of an unfamiliar international buyer. Product captions should be
concise, specific, and consistent with the catalogue. Calls to action should direct the buyer to a
product page, catalogue, inquiry form, marketplace listing, or business contact. Tracking
parameters or unique campaign codes should identify which content and channel generated the
inquiry.
A practical monthly mix is 30 percent education and origin, 25 percent product evidence, 20
percent styling and gifting, 15 percent trust and customer proof, and 10 percent direct offer. This
is an initial allocation, not a fixed rule. The enterprise should revise it based on the content formats
that generate qualified inquiries and profitable orders in the selected target market.
International Pricing, Logistics, and Risk Control
International pricing must begin with contribution margin rather than a simple conversion of
the domestic price into foreign currency. The calculation should include product cost, packaging,
labor, platform and payment fees, promotional discounts, shipping materials, international
delivery, insurance where appropriate, expected return or damage risk, taxes and duties allocation,
currency fluctuation buffer, and partner margin. The firm should distinguish retail, wholesale,
corporate, and reseller prices and establish a minimum acceptable contribution margin.
Operational readiness should be assessed before large-scale promotion. Product packaging
must protect the jewelry, present the brand professionally, and minimize international shipping
weight without compromising safety. Order confirmation should specify the product code,
materials, size, quantity, customization, packaging, price , currency, delivery method,
responsibilities for duties or taxes, and expected timeline. The enterprise should verify applicable
customs classification, precious-metal or material declarations, hallmarking, consumer-protection,
marketplace, and destination-country requirements with competent authorities or qualified export
advisers. This paper does not replace legal, customs, or compliance advice.
Cross-border risk also includes fraud, chargebacks, lost shipments, counterfeit claims, account
dependency, and platform-policy changes. The enterprise should use verified payment processes,
retain transaction records, photograph packed orders, apply secure account access, back up
customer and product data, and avoid dependence on a single platform. OECD (2021) emphasizes
that SMEs face digital-security and capability risks when digitalization proceeds without
preparation.

because promotion amplifies both strengths and weaknesses. If product specifications, response
processes, pricing, or shipping are inconsistent, a successful campaign can produce complaints and
losses more rapidly. The first sixty days therefore build the capability required to convert and fulfill
demand. The next sixty days generate controlled market evidence, and the final period scales only
validated activities.
Expected Community Service Outputs and Evaluation
The immediate outputs should include: a market-priority scorecard, a bilingual international
catalogue, standardized product information, a digital content and campaign calendar, a structured
WhatsApp and email response system, one marketplace pilot, an InaExport profile, an international
buyer list, an export-pricing worksheet, and a weekly dashboard. Training should be evaluated not
only through participant satisfaction but also through the completion and consistent use of these
assets.
Subsequent outcome evaluation should compare the baseline and  post-implementation
periods. Relevant indicators include target-country reach, qualified inquiries, response time,
quotation acceptance, international orders, contribution margin, repeat customers, B2B meetings,
active partner accounts, complaint rate, an d on-time delivery. Because market conditions,
campaign intensity, product availability, and exchange rates can change, causal claims should be
made cautiously. The program’s first objective is to improve managerial capability and evidence-
based execution; sales growth should then be evaluated as a downstream result.
CONCLUSION
Dwi Kristy Mutiara has a credible foundation for international market development through its
Lombok pearl-jewelry products, digital presence, and experience in promotional exhibitions. The
principal constraint is not simply the size of its online audience. The enterprise needs an integrated
system that connects target-market selection, bilingual product evidence, brand storytelling, trust,
conversion, export operations, partner development, and performance measurement.
The proposed strategy begins with focus. Dwi Kristy Mutiara should select a limited number of
customer segments and market hypotheses, build a global-ready catalogue for priority products,
and establish a clear international value proposition. Social media should serve discovery and
engagement, while an owned information destination, structured messaging, selected cross-border
marketplace, InaExport profile, and B2B follow -up system should support evaluation and
conversion. International pricing must protect contribution margin, and promotion should not be
expanded before shipping, payment, documentation, and customer-service processes are reliable.
The practical implication is that sophisticated technology is not the fir st requirement. A
disciplined combination of bilingual product data, consistent visual assets, tracking links, customer
labels, quotation templates, a simple database, and a weekly dashboard can substantially improve
marketing governance. The enterprise should scale only those market-product-message-channel
combinations that demonstrate qualified demand, profitable conversion, and operational
reliability.
