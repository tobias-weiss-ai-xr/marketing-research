Abstract
In Nigeria's congested commercial hubs, business incubators aim to transition micro, small, and
medium enterprises (MSMEs) from traditional trading to the digital economy. However, a
significant post -graduation disconnect persists when entrepreneurs leave these controlled
environments. This qualitative study explored the role of incubators in promoting digital marketing
platform adoption among MSMEs in the Lagos Island Local Government Area (LGA),
investigating graduates' lived experiences and the operation al barriers they face.  Grounded in
Social Capital and Effectuation theories, the methodology involved 40 purposively sampled, semi-
structured interviews with MSME owner-managers across traditional markets (Balogun, Idumota)
and modern sectors (Ma rina, Broad Street). Data were analyzed using the Braun and Clarke
thematic framework via Nvivo software, with data saturation achieved by the 28th interview. The
findings revealed a stark  Hub-Reality Asymmetry. The artificial comfort of incubators (stable
power, high-speed Wi-Fi) failed to prepare graduates for the operational chaos of peak trading
hours. As a result, entrepreneurs discarded complex, high-data strategies like custom e-commerce
websites and SEO. Instead, they leveraged pragmatic, "Bird -in-Hand" tools overwhelmingly
WhatsApp Business and Instagram to bypass local consumer trust deficits via real -time video
validation. However, long -term sustainability is severely constrained by an "infrastructure tax"
(alternative power and data costs) and a sharp post-graduation resource cliff. The study concludes
that standardized digital training fails without accounting for hyper -local constraints. It
recommends that incubators deploy low -data, context-aware social commerce mini  course and
establish solar-powered, post-graduation micro-hubs directly within market clusters to mitigate
infrastructure deficits.

Introduction
In today's African economic environment, micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs) are
essential to local economies, fostering employment and grassroots innovation (Endris & Kassegn,
2022). In Nigeria, Lagos State serves as the commercial hub, featuring a vibrant yet intensely
competitive small business sector. To thrive in this congested marketplace, small businesses need
to move past traditional in -person transactions and embrace digital m arketing platforms such as
data-driven social commerce, localized Search Everywhere Optimization (SEO), and WhatsApp

Institutional solutions, such as company incubation environments, have quickly spread throughout

Digital Marketing Adoption Platform
Digital marketing has become a vital part of business strategy in the 21st century, fueled by the
swift advancement of internet technologies, mobile devices, and social media. It includes all
marketing efforts carried out through digital channels, such as social media, email, websites, search
engines, and mobile apps (Chaffey & Ellis-Chadwick, 2019). In contrast to traditional marketing,
which heavily depends on mass media, digital marketing focuses on interactivity, personalization,
cost-efficiency, and real-time engagement with customers (Rejouani, 2026). Syalfudin (2024), also
indicated that structural flexibility dramatically improves accuracy and customer retention while
cutting overall marketing expenditures.

Hub-Reality Asymmetry
Hub-Reality Asymmetry  conceptualizes the structural mismatch between the controlled, fully
provisioned environment of a business incubator (the "Hub") and the volatile, infrastructure -
deficient marketplace where entrepreneurs must actually operate (the "Reality"). Within the
protective cocoon of an incubator, micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs) are socialized
into digital marketing under idealized conditions, supported by uninterrupted electricity, high -
speed Wi-Fi, and immediate technical mentorship (Ayatse et al., 2017). This is in line with (Singh,
2024, stated that, once enterprises are isolated from incubators, they face severe infrastructural gap
and volatile local market dynamics entirely on their own
(Obinna-Azubuike, 2026) added that , upon graduation, entrepreneurs experience immediate
environmental shock when plunging into hyper -local commercial hubs like Lagos Island. The
asymmetry manifests as a sharp operational cliff: the complex, data-heavy strategies taught at the
hub—such as c ustom e -commerce websites and Search Engine Optimization (SEO) —collapse
under the weight of erratic power grids, high data costs, and the frantic chaos of peak trading hours.
To survive this, disconnect, graduates aggressively filter out theoretical frameworks. Grounded in
effectuation, they pivot from sophisticated corporate systems to pragmatic, low -data "Bird -in-
Hand" tools like WhatsApp Business and Instagram (Johanson et al., 2024). (Suleiman, 2024), in
his view stated that, enterprises consistently reject high -cost, high-maintenance web applications
in favor of free or low -cost social media tools to reduce marketing overhead and build localized
customer.

Mentorship in Lagos Island Local Government
The quality of mentorship programs within Lagos Island local government incubators is a critical
determinant of their effectiveness in fostering startup success. Key aspects of evaluating these
programs include the establishment of clear and specific objec tives for the mentorship program,
the essence of which is to align the mentorship with the specific needs and goals of the mentee;
and, in addition, ensuring that the mentor provides guidance and support. In view of this, LIC
carefully selects mentors based on their expertise, industry knowledge, and compatibility with the
needs of startups. Thoughtful matching ensures that entrepreneurs receive guidance from mentors
with relevant experience. (Greco, 2023, Nijenhuis, 2020). Ghulam & Imran (2021) added that the
role model function performed by the mentor is to enhance entrepreneurial intentions and to
persuade mentees that unpleasant emotions (e.g., uncertainties, anxieties, and fears) are
manageable, just as they have done themselves. This structured approach ensures that mentorship
is purposeful and aligned with the developmental needs of startups.  In Lagos Island Local
Government, high-quality mentorship programs are recognized, the inclusion of mentors from
various industries, back grounds, and expertise areas is  not left out. Incubators also enrich the
learning experience for startups and expose them to a broader range of insights.   Furthermore, the
evaluation process is important, this is built into the program design from the beginning, and based
on the mentor-mentee relationship. Furthermore, effective mentorship programs establish
feedback mechanisms to assess the impact of mentorship on startup growth, regularly track and
report on mentorship capacity, registration by the day and feedback from registration questions to
ensure the program is on track and make necessary adjustment. Mentorship p rogram in Lagos
Island is an on going program, therefore, regular evaluations from both mentors and mentees for
continuous improvement of the program are observe Successful programs provide continued
support, facilitating mentorship relationships beyond the initial stages of incubation and adapting
to the changing needs of startups (Aguiar, et. al., 2019, Schulz, 2020).

Theoretical Review
This study is anchored on two  major theori es: the social capital theory (SCT ) theory and the
Effectuation theory. These theories are considered appropriate because they collectively explain
resource utilization, technology adoption, and business adaptability in a competitive environment.

Social Capital Theory
In the 1980s and 1990s, three prominent academics Pierre Bourdieu, James Coleman, and Robert
Putnam—were the main proponents and developers of social capital theory. Although Lyda J.
Hanifan first used the phrase in 1916, these three political scientists and sociologists codified it
into a multidisciplina ry academic theory.  SMEs' operations and performance are enhanced by
social capital (SC) (Akintimehin et al., 2019). According to Analia, Syaukat, Fauzi, and Rustiadi
(2020), social capital is a resource that SMEs use to increase their performance.  Consequently,
social capital extends beyond the success of SMEs to include politics.
The framework that explains how network -driven resources facilitate the adoption of digital
platforms is Social Capital Theory. For traditional merchants, incubators serve as social capital
engines, transforming intangible connections into tangible company  assets. By physically
connecting Lagos Island SMEs with IT specialists, suppliers, and digital agencies, they close
structural gaps. They create a trustworthy atmosphere that reduces the perceived operational and
financial risks of switching from cash -and-carry arrangements. By converting digital tools into a

Effectuation Theory
Effectuation Theory, created by Saras Sarasvathy in 2001 focuses on how entrepreneurs truly think
and behave when faced with high uncertainty, Conventional business models rely on causation hat
is, establishing a specific objective and figuring out how to get there. Effectuation, on the other
hand, is an action -oriented paradigm in which business owners consider their present resources
("Who I am," "What I know," and "Whom I know") and envision a patchwork of potential results.
The future is rather uncertain for a Lagos Island SME choosing whether to implement sophisticated
digital marketing tools.  This is how the function of business incubators in promoting such
adoption directly relates to Effectuation Theory.  The incubators engage in five principles , there
are, Bird-in-Hand which includes a smartphone, physical customer base, and now, the incubator's

addition, incubators bring together tech startups, logistics providers, fintech partners, and fellow
SMEs, helps SMEs pivot, and teaches the SME of how to control their content consistency, their
customer engagement, and their data collection.

Discussion of Findings
The results of this research sup port the fact that business incu bators foster digital marketing
platform adoption among small and medium scale enterprises in Lagos Island LGA. Based on the
answers to the interview, it was clear that most business managers’ business incubators foster the
adoption of digital marketing platforms in Lagos Island . This is consistent with the findings of
Chetouane Hania (2023), that digital business incubators under the influence of digital incubation
centers in Qatar, have the significant impact on the success of startups, fostering innovation,
providing resources, mentorship, and a collaborative environment. Likewise, Esin et al., (2025),
this study pointed out  that incubators significantly strengthen the positive impact o f dynamic
capabilities on start up performanc e. These studies support the current findings that business
incubators are not only the driver of business growth but they foster the adoption of digital
marketing platform adoption in the highly competitive environment of Lagos Island.

Conclusion and Recommendations
This research examined how  business incubators contribute to the adoption of digital marketing
platforms by MSMEs in the rapidly changing , highly competitive  commercial environment of
Lagos Island LGA . The qualitative  results indicate  a distinct divergence, which is termed  Hub-
Reality Asymmetry, between the well-organized, resource-abundant setting of business incubators
and the unpredictable, infrastructure-challenged conditions of conventional markets like Balogun
and Idumota.  Though incubators  effectively guide  entrepreneurs from  relying on  passive, foot -
traffic-driven approaches to embracing  value-oriented digital branding,  mere standardized
technical training falls short. To thrive after leaving the incubator, entrepreneurs abandon intricate,
data-intensive strategies like customized  e-commerce websites and  advanced technical SEO in
favor of  more practical , relationship -focused approaches  specifically Wha tsApp Business and
Instagram. These platforms  function as digital  exhibition spaces  and tools  for building
trust, directly addressing the local consumer "trust gap" through immediate video interactions and
personal connections. Therefore, if the issues of resource scarcity faced after graduation and local
environmental challenges (like the "infrastructure tax"  related to  power instability and
data fluctuations) are not tackled, standardized incubation programs that do not cater specifically
to individual needs may lead to the development of skills that only last temporarily instead of
fostering enduring  market resilience.  Based on the empirical insights and structural frictions
identified, the study recommends that  managers of incubators ought to move  away
from expensive, data -heavy digital methods such as intricate  Google Ads or  complicated
website development .Instead, the focus of  training should  be on efficient , low -data social
commerce systems (for instance , sophisticated WhatsApp Business APIs, automated  catalog
management, and localized mobile video marketing) that  cater to  the specific buying
patterns of consumers on  Lagos Island . To avoid entrepreneurs facing a "resource  gap"
after completing their programs , incubators  ought to collaborate  with local  governmental
bodies to set up  decentralized, solar -powered micro -work facilities  or communal digital
kiosks right in high-density market areas like Balogun and Idumota.
