Abstract
	 This	study	investigates	the	influences	of	digital	marketing	capability	on	multiple	stages
of	marketing	outcomes	among	cosmetics	firms	in	Thailand.	Grounded	in	Dynamic	Capabilities
Theory and Contingency Theory, the study examines the effectiveness of digital marketing capa -
bility	in	enhancing	customer	acceptance	excellence	and	competitor	completeness	efficiency.	It
further	examines	how	customer	acceptance	excellence	and	competitor	completeness	efficiency
subsequently enhance continued marketing competitiveness, while continued marketing compet-
itiveness	significantly	improves	sustainable	marketing	performance.	Data	were	collected	from	151
cosmetics	firms	in	Thailand	and	analyzed	using	OLS	regression	to	examine	the	relationships	among
digital	marketing	capability,	marketing	outcomes,	and	continued	marketing	competitiveness.	The
results	indicate	that	digital	marketing	capability	has	a	significant	effect	on	strengthening	all	multiple
stages of marketing outcomes and directly improves sustainable marketing performance (β	=0.324,
p	<	.05).	Customer	acceptance	excellence	(β	=	0.169,	p	<	.05)	and	competitor	completeness	effi-
ciency (β	=	0.597,	p	<	.001)	significantly	enhance	continued	marketing	competitiveness.	Continued
marketing	competitiveness,	in	turn,	significantly	improves	sustainable	marketing	performance	(β =
0.657,	p	<	.001).	These	findings	demonstrate	that	digital	marketing	capability	contributes	to	firm
performance through a multi-stage causal mechanism involving continued marketing competitive-
ness.	The	study	contributes	to	the	literature	by	clarifying	how	digital	marketing	capability	drives
sustainable marketing performance across multiple stages of marketing outcomes and provides
practical	implications	for	firms	seeking	to	achieve	competitive	and	sustainable	marketing	success
in	dynamic	market	environments.

Introduction
 The rapid pace of digital transforma -
tion has changed competitive landscapes
across	 industries,	 forcing	 firms	 to	 adapt	 to
increasingly volatile, technology-driven envi -
ronments	(Zahra	and	Mudambi,	2025,	p.	289).
These external pressures, from changing con -
sumer expectations to increased digital com -
petition,	demand	that	organizations	develop
capabilities that will allow them to continually
sense	and	seize	market	opportunities.	Within
the marketing domain, such capability-based
responses	are	conceptualized	as	marketing	ca-
pabilities,	which	enable	firms	to	integrate	mar-
ket knowledge, respond to customer needs,
and maintain competitive relevance (Tshuma,
et	al.,	2023,	pp.	1-10).
 Among these capabilities, digital mar -
keting	capability	(DMC)	has	become	a	strategic
imperative.	 DMC	refers	 to	the	capacity	 of	a
firm	to	utilize	 digital	 technologies,	 real-time
analytics, and interactive platforms to increase
customer engagement and create value, and
to modify marketing activities with speed and
precision (Apasrawirote, Yawised, and Munee -
sawang,	2022,	pp.	477-496).	Through	this	dy-
namic and technological enabled orientation,
firms	 can	 minimize	 information	 asymmetry,
enhance customer relationships, and achieve
higher	 levels	 of	 market	 responsiveness.
Grounded	in	Dynamic	Capability	Theory,	DMC
is formulated as a higher order capability that
allows	 for	 the	 reconfiguration	 of	 marketing
resources and practices in turbulent environ -
ments	(Teece,	2007,	pp.	1322-1328).
 Although digital marketing capability
has received considerable attention in recent
years,	 empirical	 findings	 remain	 somewhat
fragmented.	Many	prior	studies	tend	to	focus
on direct relationships, particularly between
digital	 marketing	 capability	 and	 firm	 perfor-
mance	or	customer-related	outcomes.	While
these studies provide useful insights, they
offer limited explanation of how such capa -
bility is translated into performance through
a	sequence	of	marketing-related	processes.	In
particular, relatively little attention has been
given to how process-level outcomes, such
as customer acceptance excellence and com-
petitor	 completeness	 efficiency,	 evolve	 and
contribute to continued marketing competi -
tiveness, and ultimately to sustainable mar -
keting	performance.	As	a	result,	the	underlying
mechanisms that connect digital marketing
capability with both short-term effectiveness
and long-term sustainability are still not fully
understood.	In	response	to	this	issue,	the	pres-
ent study proposes a capability-based model
that links digital marketing capability with
multiple marketing outcomes across different
stages, and examines how these outcomes are
associated with sustainable marketing perfor -
mance through continued marketing compet -
itiveness.	By	taking	this	perspective,	the	study
aims to provide a more integrated explanation
of how digital marketing capability contributes
to	sustained	competitive	advantage.
 The cosmetics industry in Thailand
offers a particularly suitable context for exam-
ining these relationships due to its high level
of digital engagement, intense competition,
and	rapidly	evolving	consumer	behavior.	Con-
sumers are highly responsive to digital content,
social media interactions, and online reviews,

determinant	of	firm	success.	Moreover,	the

Objectives

	 3.	To	examine	the	influence	of	contin-
ued marketing competitiveness on sustainable

Literature Review
	 Drawing	 on	 the	 Dynamic	 Capability
Theory, this study proposed that digital mar -
keting	capability	 helps	firms	to	sense,	seize,
and	reconfigure	resources	to	improve	both	in-
termediate and long-term marketing outcomes
(Teece,	2007,	pp.	1322-1325).	Digital	marketing
capability is expected to enhance customer
acceptance excellence and competitor com -
pleteness	 efficiency	 by	 enhancing	 market
sensing, customer engagement, and value
alignment.	These	intermediate	capabilities	in
turn lead to further marketing competitiveness,
enabling adaptive responses in dynamic envi -
ronments.	Furthermore,	digital	marketing	capa-
bility and marketing competitiveness to sustain
marketing performance are both contributing
factors to sustainable marketing performance
reflecting	 the	 long-term	 strategic	 value	 of
digitally enabled agility and customer-centric
renewal.
Digital Marketing Capability (DMC)
 The concept of digital marketing ca -
pability	 has	increasingly	 been	recognized	 as
a	strategic	 foundation	 that	enables	 firms	to
compete effectively in rapidly evolving digital
environments.	 Based	 on	Dynamic	 Capability
Theory,	digital	marketing	capability	reflects	a
firm’s	ability	to	integrate	digital	technologies,
data-driven insights, and interactive platforms
to generate valuable market intelligence,
engage stakeholders in real time, and deliver
marketing value without spatial or tempo -

89
ral constraints (Apasrawirote, Yawised, and
Muneesawang,	 2022,	 pp.	 477–496).	 From	 a
dynamic capability perspective, this capabil -
ity operates through three key mechanisms,
namely	 sensing,	 seizing,	 and	 reconfiguring
(Teece,	 2007,	 pp.	 1322–1328).	 First,	 digital
marketing capability enhances sensing capabil-
ity	by	enabling	firms	to	continuously	monitor
changes in customer preferences and market
trends	through	digital	data	and	analytics.	Sec-
ond,	it	supports	seizing	capability	by	allowing
firms	 to	 respond	 rapidly	 through	 targeted
digital	 campaigns	 and	 value	 propositions.
Third,	 it	strengthens	 reconfiguring	 capability
by facilitating the continuous adjustment of
marketing processes and resource allocation in
response	to	environmental	changes.	Through
these	 mechanisms,	 firms	 are	 able	 to	 align
their marketing strategies with evolving market
conditions and sustain adaptive performance
in	dynamic	environments	(Yin,	et	al.,	2024,	pp.
2750–2772).	In	this	research,	digital	marketing
capability	is	theorized	as	a	higher-order	stra-
tegic	capability	that	enables	firms	to	establish
meaningful digital engagement, provide cred -
ible and relevant information, and develop
long-term relationships with customers across
digital platforms (Apasrawirote, Yawised, and
Muneesawang,	2022,	pp.	477–496).	Its	strategic
value lies in its ability to reduce information
asymmetry, enhance customer understanding,
and	strengthen	the	firm’s	competitive	position
in	digital	markets	(Su,	et	al.,	2023,	p.	2).
 Building on this theoretical founda -
tion, digital marketing capability contributes
to	marketing	outcomes	through	specific	pro-
cess-based	mechanisms.	By	enhancing	sensing
capability,	firms	are	better	able	to	understand
customer needs and reduce uncertainty, which
leads to higher levels of customer acceptance
excellence	(Santy	and	Anwar,	2021,	pp.	31–36).
At	the	same	time,	through	seizing	capability,
firms	 can	 utilize	 digital	 insights	 to	 monitor
competitors, benchmark performance, and re-
fine	value	propositions,	thereby	strengthening
competitor	 completeness	 efficiency	 (Smith,
2024,	pp.	63–74).	In	addition,	the	reconfiguring
aspect of digital marketing capability enables
firms	 to	continuously	 refine	 their	 marketing
strategies and processes, which supports the
development of continued marketing compet-
itiveness	over	time	(Yin,	et	al.,	2024,	pp.	2750–
2772).	 This	 ongoing	 capability	 development
allows	firms	to	maintain	alignment	with	market
dynamics and sustain competitive positioning
in the long run, ultimately contributing to sus-
tained marketing effectiveness and long-term
performance	 (Ranjan,	 2023,	 pp.	2836–2856).
Taken together, these arguments indicate that
digital marketing capability plays a central role
in shaping marketing outcomes across multi -
ple	stages,	linking	firm	capabilities	with	both
process-level outcomes and long-term per -
formance.	Firms	with	strong	digital	marketing
capability are better able to enhance customer
understanding, strengthen competitive posi -
tioning, and improve market responsiveness,
which collectively contribute to improved
marketing 	 outcomes. 	 Accordingly, 	 digital
marketing capability is expected to positively
influence	 customer	 acceptance	 excellence,
competitor	completeness	efficiency,	and	sus-
tainable	 marketing	 performance.	 Therefore,
the following hypothesis is proposed:

Customer Acceptance Excellence (CAE)
 Customer acceptance excellence
refers to the inclination and readiness of
customers to collaborate, embrace, and con -
tinuously	utilize	a	firm’s	product,	service,	or
technology.	It	reflects	not	only	initial	adoption
but also the depth and continuity of custom -
er engagement, including loyalty of use and
active interaction across digital or physical
interfaces (Hassan, Nassar and Kamal, 2022,
pp.	 64–76).	 This	 concept	 extends	 beyond
initial	acceptance	by	emphasizing	the	quality
and	durability	of	customer	involvement.	The
establishment of such acceptance depends
on	a	firm’s	ability	to	deliver	digital	experiences
that are clear, credible, and psychologically
reassuring, enabling customers to make deci -
sions	with	confidence	(Velasco,	et	al.,	2024,	p.
2026).	Firms	that	communicate	transparently,
respond promptly, and reduce perceived risk
are more likely to foster trust, satisfaction, and
relational	 commitment	 (Okeke,	 et	al.,	 2024,
pp.	3517–3540).	Consequently,	customer	ac-
ceptance excellence represents a sustained
and mutually reinforcing relationship between
the	firm	and	its	customers,	grounded	in	trust,
clarity,	and	positive	experience.
 From a dynamic capability perspective,
customer acceptance excellence contributes
to competitive capability through continuous
learning	and	adaptation.	When	customers	ac-
tively engage, provide feedback, and maintain
ongoing	relationships	with	the	firm,	they	gen-
erate valuable market knowledge that enables
firms	to	refine	offerings	and	adjust	marketing
strategies in response to evolving customer
expectations	(Wang,	Aggarwal	and	Wu,	2020,	p.
1603).	Such	customer-driven	insights	support
the	reconfiguring	capability	of	the	firm,	allow-
ing	continuous	modification	of	marketing	pro-
cesses and resource allocation in dynamic en-
vironments	(Taçoğlu,	Ceylan	and	Kazançoğlu,
2019,	 pp.	 648–673). 	This	perspective	 shifts
the focus from transactional outcomes to a
process-oriented view, where customer accep-
tance is not merely an outcome but a strategic
input that supports ongoing capability devel -
opment.	Moreover,	higher	levels	of	customer
acceptance foster loyalty and advocacy, as
satisfied	customers	are	more	likely	to	repur-
chase and recommend the brand, thereby
strengthening	the	firm’s	market	position	(Ma-
tute,	 Palau-Saumell	 and	 Occhiocupo,	 2019,
pp.	360–376).	These	reinforcing	mechanisms
enable	firms	to	strengthen	relational	stability,
reduce customer acquisition costs, and sustain
competitive	positioning	over	time.	As	a	result,
customer acceptance excellence enhances a
firm’s	ability	to	maintain	continued	marketing
competitiveness in dynamic market condi -
tions.	 Therefore,	 the	following	 hypothesis	 is
proposed:
 H2:  Customer acceptance excellence
has	a	positive	influence	on	continued	market-
ing	competitiveness.

Continued Marketing Competitiveness
(CMC)
 Continued marketing competitiveness
refers	to	a	firm’s	ability	to	acquire,	sustain,	and
expand	profitable	market	share	by	delivering
superior	 value	 relative	 to	its	competitors.	 It
encompasses the capability to outperform
competitors through lower costs, faster
product development, higher quality, greater
reliability, and more effective delivery (Chikan,

Journal of Business, Innovation and Sustainability (JBIS) Volume 21, Issue 2 (April - June 2026)
92
et	al.,	2022,	pp.	1–13).	This	concept	reflects	a
multidimensional capability achieved through
the integration of strategic, operational, and
resource-based	competencies,	enabling	firms
to	 create	 value	 more	 efficiently	 than	 their
rivals	(Alonso-Ubieta,	Mora-Esquivel,	and	Leiva,
2021,	 pp.	 420–438).	 Continued	 marketing
competitiveness	also	captures	a	firm’s	ability
to transform resources into sustained eco -
nomic gains through ongoing improvements
in	efficiency	and	effectiveness,	consistent	with
the competitiveness cycle described in prior
literature	(Lijun,	et	al.,	2024,	pp.	1–28).	In	dy-
namic business environments, this capability
becomes	increasingly	important,	as	firms	must
remain agile, adaptive, and responsive to main-
tain	competitive	advantage	(Pang,	Wang	and
Wu,	2022,	pp.	1421–1443).
 From a dynamic capability perspec -
tive, continued marketing competitiveness
represents the outcome of effective capability
reconfiguration,	where	firms	continuously	align
their resources, processes, and value proposi -
tions with changing market conditions (Pang,
Wang	 and	 Wu,	 2022,	 pp.	 1421–1443).	 This
alignment	 enables	 firms	 to	sustain	 superior
performance by maintaining relevance and
responsiveness in competitive environments
(Lijun,	 et	 al.,	 2024,	 pp.	 1–28).	 Rather	 than
being a static indicator of market position,
continued	marketing	competitiveness	reflects
an	ongoing 	capability 	that	allows	firms	to
consistently deliver superior value relative to
competitors	 (Alonso-Ubieta,	 Mora-Esquivel,
and	Leiva,	2021,	pp.	420–438).	This	perspective
emphasizes	competitiveness	as	a	dynamic	and
process-oriented construct, rather than a one-
time	outcome.	Such	capability	enables	firms
to translate strategic and operational strengths
into tangible performance outcomes, includ -
ing improved customer satisfaction, stronger
market positioning, and enhanced value cre -
ation.	Empirical	evidence	indicates	that	firms
with higher levels of competitiveness tend to
achieve better marketing performance through
stronger 	price–value 	propositions, 	product
quality,	 and	customer	 loyalty	(Gunawardana
and	Wedage,	2020,	pp.	46–72;	Moradi,	et	al.,
2024,	pp.	46–53).	In	addition,	sustained	com-
petitiveness	allows	firms	to	maintain	differen-
tiation advantages, either by increasing sales
volume at existing margins or by commanding
premium prices, thereby improving overall
performance 	(Tolossa, 	Singh	and	Gautam,
2025,	pp.	26–44).	Furthermore,	the	ability	to
sustain competitive advantage through con -
tinuous innovation, agility, and responsiveness
strengthens long-term marketing outcomes
and	organizational	resilience	(Suherman,	2021,
pp.	191–197;	Pang,	Wang	and	Wu,	2022,	pp.
1421–1443).	Taken	together,	these	arguments
suggest that continued marketing competitive-
ness functions as a critical driver of sustainable
marketing	 performance	 by	enabling	 firms	to
maintain superior value delivery, adapt to
changing market conditions, and sustain com -
petitive	advantage	over	time.	Therefore,	the
following hypothesis is proposed:
 H4: Continued marketing competitive-
ness	has	a	positive	influence	on	sustainable
marketing	performance.
Sustainable Marketing Performance (SMP)
 Sustainable marketing performance
refers	to	a	firm’s	ability	to	translate	its	market-

Journal of Business, Innovation and Sustainability (JBIS) Volume 21, Issue 2 (April - June 2026)
93
ing capabilities into long-term outcomes that
sustain and strengthen competitive advan -
tage	over	time.	It	reflects	the	transformation
of short-term marketing success into a more
enduring and resilient form of performance,
supported by strong customer relationships,
consistent value delivery, and a credible
and lasting brand image (Cao and Tian, 2020,
pp.	1289–1299).	 Prior	research	 suggests	 that
sustainable marketing performance emerges
when	 firms	 effectively	 integrate	 digital	 and
strategic marketing activities to enhance cus -
tomer satisfaction, strengthen brand loyalty,
and	maintain	performance	over	the	long	term.
Rather than focusing solely on acquiring new
customers,	firms	with	sustainable	performance
tend to invest in developing existing customer
relationships, trust, and emotional attachment,
which encourage continued engagement and
voluntary	brand	advocacy	(Alani,	et	al.,	2019,
pp.	232–242).	 These	 long-term	 relationships
create more predictable revenue streams,
reinforce market positioning, and support stra-
tegic continuity in dynamic and competitive
environments.
 From a dynamic capability perspective,
sustainable marketing performance represents
the ultimate outcome of effective capability
deployment	 and	 reconfiguration	 over	 time.
Firms that continuously align their marketing
capabilities with evolving customer needs
and competitive conditions are better able
to sustain performance beyond short-term
gains.	 In	this	 context,	 sustainable	 marketing
performance is not merely an outcome of
isolated marketing activities, but a cumu -
lative result of ongoing capability develop -
ment	and	competitive	positioning.	Firms	with
strong continued marketing competitiveness,
reflected	in	differentiation, 	responsiveness,
and effective value delivery, are more likely
to achieve higher levels of customer loyalty,
perceived value, and long-term marketing
success	(Gunawardana	and	Wedage,	2020,	pp.
46–72;	Moradi,	et	al.,	2024,	pp.	46–53).	When
such competitive advantages are consistently
renewed and strategically leveraged, they
enable	 firms	 to	 move	 beyond	 short-term
performance cycles and achieve sustained
market	relevance	and	organizational	stability
(Suherman,	 2021,	 pp.	 191–197;	 Pang,	 Wang
and	Wu,	2022,	 pp.	1421–1443).	 Accordingly,
sustainable	 marketing	 performance	 reflects
the	long-term	effectiveness	of	a	firm’s	ability
to integrate digital capability and competi -
tive strength to continuously deliver superior
value and maintain strategic advantage over
time.	 Collectively,	 these	 arguments	 support
the role of sustainable marketing performance
as	the	final	outcome	in	the	capability-based
framework, capturing the long-term impact of
both digital marketing capability and continued
marketing competitiveness, as illustrated in the
conceptual	framework	presented	in	Figure	1.

Sample Selection Procedure and Data
Collection
 The sample for this study consisted
of	cosmetics	firms	in	Thailand,	identified	from
the	Department 	of	Business 	Development
(Department	of	Business	Development,	2024).
The	 unit	 of	 analysis	 was	 the	 firm.	 Data
were collected from key informants directly
responsible for marketing activities, including
marketing directors, marketing executives,
and marketing managers, as these individuals
possess the most relevant knowledge of the
firm’s	marketing	strategies	and	performance.
Respondents were purposively selected
based on their involvement in marketing deci-
sion-making processes, ensuring that the infor-
mation	provided	accurately	reflects	the	firm’s
marketing	activities.	Each	firm	was	represented
by a single respondent to ensure consistency
and	reliability	of	the	data	collected.
	 A	total	of	713	cosmetics	firms	in	Thai-
land	were	targeted	using	a	postal	survey.	The
questionnaire	included	the	study’s	objectives,
demographic information, measurement items
for each construct, submission deadlines, and
response	 tracking	 mechanisms.	 The	 surveys
were distributed between November 2024 and
January	 2025.	Due	to	relocation	 and	invalid
addresses, the effective mailing list consisted
of	698	firms.	A	total	of	153	responses	were
received,	of	which	151	were	deemed	usable
for	analysis.	The	effective	response	rate	was
approximately	21.63%	(151	×	100	/	698).	This
response rate is considered acceptable for
organizational 	survey	research, 	particularly
when appropriate follow-up procedures are
implemented	(Aaker,	Kumar	and	Day,	2001,	pp.
234–235).	 To	assess	 potential	 non-response
bias, a comparison between early and late
respondents	(first-wave	and	second-wave	re-
sponses) was conducted following the proce -
dure	recommended	by	Armstrong	and	Overton
(1977,	pp.	396–402).	The	results	indicated	no
statistically	 significant	 differences	 between
the	two	groups	at	the	95%	confidence	level,
suggesting that non-response bias is unlikely to
affect	the	findings	of	this	study.
Research Instrument
 All measurement items were assessed
using	 a	5-point	 Likert	 scale	 ranging	 from	 1
(strongly	disagree)	to	5	(strongly	agree),	except
for	firm	demographic	variables.	The	measure-
ment scales were adapted from prior studies
and	refined	based	on	the	conceptual	defini-
tions presented in the literature review to en -
sure	content	validity	and	contextual	relevance.
Digital	 marketing	 capability	 was	 measured

using	five	items	reflecting	the	firm’s	ability	to
utilize	digital	technologies,	data-driven	insights,

deliver offerings that align more effectively
with	customer	expectations	(Smith,	2024,	pp.
63–74).	Continued	marketing	competitiveness
was	measured	using	five	items	evaluating	the
firm’s	ability	to	acquire,	maintain,	and	expand
profitable	market	share	through	superior	value
delivery, responsiveness, differentiation, and
continuous	 improvement	 in	 efficiency	 and
effectiveness	(Chikan,	et	al.,	2022,	pp.	1–13).
Sustainable marketing performance was mea -
sured	 using	 five	 items	 reflecting	 the	 firm’s
ability to translate marketing capabilities into
long-term outcomes, including customer loy -
alty, consistent value delivery, brand strength,
and sustained competitive advantage (Cao and
Tian,	2020,	pp.	1289–1299).	Firm	age	(FA)	was
measured using a dummy variable based on
the	number	of	years	the	firm	has	been	in	oper-
ation,	where	0	represents	firms	operating	for	10
years	or	less	and	1	represents	firms	operating
for	more	than	10	years,	while	firm	size	(FS)	was
measured as a dummy variable based on the
number	of	employees,	with	0	indicating	firms
with	150	employees	or	fewer	and	1	indicating
firms	 with	 more	 than	 150	employees.	 Such
categorization	is	consistent	with	prior	empiri-
cal	research	that	distinguishes	firms	based	on
organizational	maturity	and	resource	capacity
(Phornlaphatrachakorn	and	Khajit,	p.	314).

the	 measurement	 model,	 confirmatory	 fac-
tor analysis (CFA) was conducted to examine
whether the observed variables adequately
represent	 their	 respective	 latent	 constructs.

loadings	ranged	from	0.648	to	0.908,	exceeding
the	recommended	threshold	of	0.50,	thereby
supporting	 convergent	 validity	 (Hair,	 et	 al.,
2019,	 pp.	 708–710).	 In	 addition,	 composite
reliability	 (CR)	 values	 ranged	 from	 0.835	 to
0.909,	surpassing	the	acceptable	level	of	0.70,
indicating	strong	construct	reliability.
	 The	average	variance	extracted	(AVE)
values	ranged	from	0.504	to	0.668,	all	exceed-
ing	the	recommended	threshold	of	0.50,	fur-
ther	confirming	adequate	convergent	validity
(Hair,	et	al.,	2019,	pp.	708–710).	Reliability	was
also	assessed	using	Cronbach’s	alpha,	with	val-
ues	ranging	from	0.812	to	0.902,	indicating	high
internal consistency across all constructs (Nun-
nally	and	Bernstein,	1994).	Collectively,	these
results demonstrate that the measurement
model exhibits satisfactory levels of validity
and reliability and is appropriate for testing the
hypothesized	relationships	in	this	study.

Statistical Technique
	 Ordinary	 Least	 Squares	 (OLS)	 regres-
sion was employed to test the proposed
hypotheses and examine the relationships
among	the	study	variables.	Although	struc-
tural	equation	 modeling	 (SEM)	is	commonly
used	 in	 similar	 studies,	 OLS	 regression	 was
selected	 due	 to	 the	 sample	 size	 and	 the
study’s	focus	on	examining	direct	linear	rela-
tionships	among	constructs	(Hair,	et	al.,	2019,
pp.	 157–160;	 Wooldridge,	 2016,	 pp.	 65–72).
The	 model	 specifies	 that	 digital	 marketing
capability predicts customer acceptance ex -
cellence,	competitor	completeness	efficiency,
and sustainable marketing performance, while
customer acceptance excellence and compet-
itor	 completeness	 efficiency	 further	 predict
continued	marketing	competitiveness.	In	this
study, composite mean scores were calculated
for each construct and treated as continuous
variables, which is a widely accepted practice
in	behavioral	research	using	Likert-scale	data
(Hair,	et	al.,	2019,	pp.	157–160;	Field,	2018,	pp.
269–270).	 The	 assumptions	 of	linearity,	 ho-
moscedasticity, and normality were examined
and	found	to	be	reasonably	satisfied,	consis-
tent with standard regression requirements
(Wooldridge,	2016,	pp.	65–72).	Multicollinearity
was	 assessed	 using	 variance	 inflation	 factor
(VIF)	values,	which	ranged	from	1.322	to	1.606,
well below the recommended threshold of
5,	 indicating	 that	 multicollinearity	 is	 not	 a
concern	(Hair,	et	al.,	2019,	pp.	203–204).	To
address potential common method bias, Har -
man’s	single-factor	test	was	conducted.	The
results indicate that no single factor accounted
for the majority of the variance, suggesting that
common method bias is unlikely to affect the
findings	of	this	study	(Podsakoff,	et	al.,	2003,
pp.	879–880).

Conclusion and Discussion
	 The	 empirical	 findings	 of	 this	 study
provide strong support for the role of digital
marketing capability in shaping marketing out -
comes across multiple stages, from interme -
diate	 capabilities	 to	long-term	 performance.
Firstly,	digital	marketing	capability	significantly
influences	 customer	 acceptance	 excellence
(β	=	0.250,	p	<	.05),	competitor	completeness
efficiency	(β	=	0.253,	p	<	.05),	and	sustainable
marketing performance ( β	=	0.324,	p	<	.001).
These	findings	are	consistent	with	prior	stud-
ies showing that digital capability enhances
customer	engagement	and	firm	performance
(Santy	and	Anwar,	2021,	pp.	31-36;	Ranjan,
2023,	pp.	2836-2856).	However,	unlike	previous
research that often examines these relation -
ships in isolation, this study demonstrates that
digital marketing capability simultaneously
strengthens both customer-related and com -
petitive	capabilities	within	a	unified	framework.
Firms with stronger digital capabilities are
better able to reduce customer uncertainty,
deliver relevant information, and respond
effectively to evolving market conditions,
thereby enhancing customer acceptance and
improving competitive positioning (Santy and
Anwar,	2021,	pp.	31-36;	Smith,	2024,	pp.	63-74).
This integrated effect highlights the broader
strategic role of digital marketing capability in
influencing	multiple	dimensions	of	marketing
outcomes.	In	the	context	of	cosmetics	firms
in Thailand, where digital platforms dominate
customer interaction and preferences shift rap-
idly,	firms	with	strong	digital	capabilities	quickly
adapt to emerging trends and customer feed -
back, further reinforcing customer acceptance
and	competitive	positioning.
 Secondly, customer acceptance ex -
cellence (β	=	0.169,	p	<	.05)	and	competitor
completeness	efficiency	(β	=	0.597,	p	<	.001)
both	have	significant	positive	effects	on	con-
tinued	marketing	competitiveness.	While	prior
studies	emphasize	the	importance	of	customer
relationships in driving performance (Hassan,
Nassar	and	Kamal,	2022,	pp.	64-76),	the	pres-
ent	findings	reveal	that	competitor	complete-
ness	efficiency	exerts	a	substantially	stronger
influence.	This	indicates	that	firms’	ability	to
interpret competitive signals, benchmark ef -
fectively, and adjust strategies plays a more
dominant role in sustaining competitiveness
in	dynamic	environments.	Although	customer
acceptance contributes to relationship stabil -
ity, competitive responsiveness and strategic
adaptation are more critical in maintaining

Journal of Business, Innovation and Sustainability (JBIS) Volume 21, Issue 2 (April - June 2026)
101
long-term	competitive	advantage	(Pang,	Wang
and	Wu,	2022,	pp.	1421-1443;	Taçoğlu,	Ceylan
and	 Kazançoğlu,	 2019,	 pp.	 648-673).	 In	 the
highly competitive Thai cosmetics industry,
where product differentiation is short-lived and
trends	evolve	quickly,	firms	must	continuously
monitor competitors and digital signals, making
competitive responsiveness a key determinant
of	sustained	advantage.
	 Lastly,	 continued	 marketing	 compet-
itiveness	 significantly	 influences	 sustainable
marketing performance ( β	=	0.657,	p	<	.001),
highlighting	its	critical	role	in	translating	firm
capabilities	 into	 long-term	 outcomes.	 This
finding	 reinforces	 the	 dynamic	 capability
perspective	 (Teece,	2007,	pp.	1322-1328)	by
demonstrating that sustained competitive
positioning is a key mechanism through which
firms	convert	capabilities	into	long-term	per-
formance	 outcomes.	 Unlike	 prior	 research
that	focuses	primarily	on	direct	capability–per-
formance relationships, this study provides
evidence of a multi-stage relationship in which
competitiveness plays a central role in sustain-
ing	performance	over	time.	Firms	that	maintain
strong competitiveness are more likely to
achieve customer loyalty, perceived value,
and long-term engagement, thereby reinforc -
ing	sustainable	marketing	success	(Gunaward-
ana	and	Wedage,	2020,	pp.	46-72;	Moradi,	et
al.,	 2024,	 pp.	46-53).	 In	a	digitally	 intensive
and	trend-driven	cosmetics	industry,	firms	that
continuously adapt their strategies and engage
customers through digital channels are more
likely to sustain relevance, build loyalty, and
achieve	long-term	marketing	performance.
Contributions
Theoretical Contributions
 This study makes several important
theoretical contributions to the literature in
digital marketing capability and marketing
performance.	 Specifically,	 this	study	contrib-
utes to the literature by introducing a multi-
stage	capability–performance	framework	that
integrates relational (customer acceptance
excellence) and competitive (competitor
completeness	 efficiency)	 mechanisms	 within
the	dynamic	capability	perspective.	Firstly,	the
findings	 empirically	 demonstrate	 that	digital
marketing	 capability	 significantly	 influences
customer acceptance excellence ( β	=	0.250),
competitor 	 completeness 	 efficiency 	 (β =
0.253),	and	sustainable	marketing	performance
(β	=	0.324),	thereby	confirming	 its	role	as	a
higher-order	dynamic	capability.	These	results
support the dynamic capability perspective by
showing	how	firms	leverage	digital	resources	to
sense	and	respond	to	market	changes.	Impor-
tantly, this study provides integrated empirical
evidence across multiple outcomes, rather
than treating these relationships in isolation as
in	prior	research.
	 Secondly,	the	study	identifies	compet-
itor	completeness	efficiency	(β	=	0.597)	and
customer acceptance excellence ( β	=	0.169)
as key drivers of continued marketing com -
petitiveness, with competitor completeness
efficiency	 exerting	 a	 substantially	 stronger
effect.	This	result	indicates	that	competitive
responsiveness plays a more dominant role
than customer-related factors in sustaining
competitive	advantage.	By	explicitly	comparing
effect	sizes,	the	study	provides	stronger	empir-

ical grounding for understanding how different

are	translated	into	long-term	outcomes.	This

by	illustrating	a	multi-stage	capability–perfor-

Managerial contributions
 The results of this study provide several
practical implications for managers seeking to
enhance digital marketing capability and long-
term	marketing	performance.	Firstly,	given	the
significant	effects	of	digital	marketing	capabil-
ity on customer acceptance excellence ( β =
0.250),	 competitor	 completeness	 efficiency
(β	=	0.253),	 and	 sustainable	 marketing	 per-
formance (β	=	0.324),	managers	should	treat
digital	capability	as	a	core	strategic	investment.
In	the	context	of	cosmetics	firms	in	Thailand,
where digital platforms and real-time consum-
er engagement are central, managers should
leverage	 social	 media,	 influencer	 marketing,
and data analytics to respond quickly to chang-
ing	consumer	preferences	and	market	trends.
 Secondly, since competitor complete-
ness	 efficiency	 has	the	strongest	 impact	 on
continued marketing competitiveness ( β =
0.597),	managers	should	prioritize	competitive
intelligence	capabilities.	In	the	Thai	cosmetics
industry, where competition is intense and
trends	change	rapidly,	firms	must	continuously
monitor competitors, track digital trends, and
adjust strategies in real time to maintain com-
petitive	advantage.
	 Lastly,	the	strong	effect	of	continued
marketing competitiveness on sustainable
marketing performance ( β	=	0.657)	 suggests
that managers should focus on sustaining long-
term competitiveness through continuous ad -
aptation.	In	a	digitally	driven	cosmetics	market,
this involves maintaining product relevance,
engaging customers through digital channels,
and leveraging real-time feedback to build
loyalty	and	ensure	long-term	performance.
Limitations and Future Recommendations
	 Despite	providing	meaningful	empirical
insights, the present study has a number of lim-
itations	that	offer	avenues	for	future	research.
Firstly, the study is based on a relatively small
sample	size	of	151	firms,	which	limits	the	use
of	more	advanced	analytical	techniques.	Given
the	multi-stage	relationships	identified	in	this
study, future research could employ structural
equation	 modeling	 (SEM)	 to	simultaneously
test these pathways and provide stronger vali-
dation	of	the	proposed	model.
 Secondly, digital marketing capability
is	conceptualized	as	a	higher-order	construct.
However,	 the	findings	indicate	 that	different
mechanisms contribute unevenly to out -
comes,	as	competitor	completeness	efficiency
(β	=	0.597)	has	a	stronger	effect	than	customer
acceptance excellence ( β	=	0.169).	This	sug-
gests that different aspects of digital marketing
capability	 may	 influence	 competitive	 and
relational	 outcomes	 differently.	 Therefore,
future research could examine its underlying
subdimensions to identify which components

 Thirdly, this study focuses on cos -
