The rapid adoption of artificial intelligence (AI) has transformed marketing through
personalised recommendations, chatbots, predictive analytics, automated
advertising, and customer -data analysis. However, further evidence i s needed to
explain how these applications influence customer experience and translate into
improved business performance. This study examines the effects of AI -based
marketing on customer experience and business performance, including the
mediating role o f customer experience. A quantitative explanatory design was
employed using survey data collected from 200 Indonesian customers who had
interacted with AI -enabled marketing services. The proposed relationships were
analysed using partial least squares structural equation modelling (PLS-SEM) with
SmartPLS. The measurement model demonstrated satisfactory convergent validity,
discriminant validity, and internal consistency. The structural model explained
46.4% of the variance in customer experience and 43.5% of the variance in business
performance. AI-based marketing had a positive and significant effect on customer
experience (β = 0.681, t = 11.210, p < 0.001). Customer experience also positively
influenced business performance ( β = 0.460, t = 5.678, p < 0.001 ), while AI-based
marketing had a significant direct effect on business performance ( β = 0.253, t =
3.470, p = 0.001). Furthermore, customer experience significantly mediated the
relationship between AI -based marketing and business performance (indirect
effect: β = 0.314, t = 4.915, p < 0.001). These findings demonstrate that AI -based
marketing improves business performance both directly and indirectly by creating
more personalised, responsive, convenient, and efficient customer interactions. The
study highlights customer experience as an important mechanism connecting AI -
enabled marketing practices with organisational outcomes. Managers should
therefore evaluate AI investments not only by their automation capabilities but also
by their capacity to create cu stomer value, strengthen relationships, improve
marketing effectiveness, and sustain business competitiveness.

1. INTRODUCTION
Artificial intelligence (AI) has become an increasingly important marketing capability through
applications such as personalised recommendations, chatbots, predictive analytics, automated
advertising, and customer -data analysis. These applications can improve targeting, responsiveness,
decision-making, and serv ice efficiency. However, the business value of AI -based marketing depends
not only on technological implementation but also on how customers interpret and experience AI -
enabled interactions. Customer experience (CX) captures customers' cognitive, emotional, behavioural,

Existing studies have predominantly examined either the direct performance consequences of AI
or the customer -related outcomes of specific AI applications . Wu and Monfort (2023) , for example,
demonstrated that implementing an AI marketing strategy affects organisational performance among
food-sector firms (C. W. Wu & Monfort, 2023) . Similarly, Baabdullah et al. (2021)  examined the
antecedents and consequences of AI -based business-to-business practices among small and medium -
sized enterpri ses (Baabdullah et al., 2021) . These studies establish AI as a performance -enhancing
strategic resource, but they primarily emphasise organisational adoption and performance
consequences. They do not fully explain whether performance improvements occur because AI creates
more valuable customer experiences.
A separate stream of research concentrates on customer responses to AI. Ameen et al. ( 2021)
showed that trust and perceived sacrifice mediate the formation of AI -enabled customer experience,
while relationship commitment directly affects that experience (Ameen et al., 2021) . Their study
explains why customers may accept or reject AI -enabled services but does not extend the model to
business-performance outcomes. Liu-Thompkins et al. (2022)  focused on artificial empathy,
demonstrating how empathic AI can reduce the human–AI gap in affective and social CX (Liu-Thompkins
et al., 2022) . However, their model concerns a particular relational characteristic of AI agents rather
than a broader configuration of AI marketing practices encompassing personalisation, recommendation
accuracy, automation, and predictive analytics.
Customer Experience ( CX) studies provide additional insight but leave the AI –performance
pathway unresolved. Sahhar et al. (2023)  conceptualised CX as dynamic and multidimensional and
developed the GraphEx framework to help m anagers understand and influence customer journeys
(Sahhar et al., 2023). Although the framework connects CX management with customer loyalty and value
creation, it does not empirically test AI -based marketing as an antecedent or business performance as
an outcome. Moreover, Hollebeek et al. (2024) identified considerable variation in how CX is
conceptualised and measured, highlighting the need for theoreticall y coherent models and carefully
specified CX constructs  (Hollebeek et al., 2024).
Taken together, these studies reveal a fragmented literature. AI-marketing research demonstrates
direct performance benefits but often leaves the customer -level value -creation mechanism
implicit(Gimeno-Arias et al., 2021). Conversely, AI-enabled CX research explains customer reactions to
specific technologies but rarely connects these experiences to broader performance outcomes (Hidayat
et al., 2022). Therefore, the research gap is not simply whether AI improves CX or whether CX improves
performance. The unresolved issue is how a multidimensional set of AI -based marketing practices
translates into business performance through customers ' evaluations of r elevance, convenience,
responsiveness, trust, satisfaction, and service quality.
The theoretical rationale for CX mediation is based on a customer -value transmission mechanism.
AI-based marketing represents a firm -controlled technological input that change s the quality of
customer touchpoints  (Expo  sito et al., 2023). Personalisation and recommendation systems can
increase relevance; chatbots and automation can improve responsiveness and convenience; and
predictive analytics can help firms anticipate custo mer needs. Nevertheless, these technical functions
do not create business value automatically  (Baidya et al., 2023) . Customers must perceive them as
useful, reliable, trustworthy, and supportive. CX therefore represents the evaluative mechanism through
which technological capabilities are converted into customer value.
A favourable CX subsequently encourages repeat purchasing, loyalty, retention, advocacy, and
stronger customer relationships. At the aggregate level, these behaviours support sales growth,

The novelty of this study does not rest solely on introducing CX as a mediator. Instead, it lies in

business performance as the resulting organisational outcome. The model simultaneously tests the
direct performance effect of AI-based marketing and the indirect pathway operating through CX. It also
examines these relationships among Indonesian customers, providing evidence from a digitally
expanding emerging -market context that remains underrepresented in the dominant AI -marketing
literature.
Accordingly, this study examines four relationships: AI-based marketing's effect on CX, CX's effect
on business performance, AI-based marketing's direct effect on business performance, and the indirect
effect of AI -based marketing on business performance through CX. The proposed model position s AI-
based marketing as the independent construct, CX as the mediating construct, and business
performance as the dependent construct.

2. METHOD
2.1 Research Design
This study adopts a quantitative, explanatory design because it tests hypothesized relationships
among variables using numerically coded responses and aims to explain why and how variables are
related (i.e., causal pathways) (Draucker et al., 2020) . The model specifies AI -based marketing as the
independent construct, customer experience as a mediator, and business performance as the outcome,
enabling estimation of both direct and indirect (mediated) effects within a single multivariate
framework (Hall et al., 2022). To evaluate the proposed causal paths and mediation simultaneously, the
analysis can be implemented using structural equation modeling (SEM), which is explicitly suited to
testing complex causal relationships and mediating mechanisms, including constructs measured
indirectly via indicators (Wang et al., 2020). If the study prioritizes prediction and explained variance
and/or expects non -normal data or a complex model, PLS -SEM is commonly justified as a causal -
predictive approach widely used in marketing and social sciences and capable of estimating mediation
paths in complex models (Zhang et al., 2023).
2.2 Research Object and Scope
The object of this research is customers who have interacted with businesses that use artificial
intelligence-based marketing. The scope of AI-based marketing in this study includes the use of digital
technologies such as chatbots, product recommendation systems, personalized advertisements,
automated customer service, predictive analytics, and customer data-based marketing communication.
The research focuses on customers of digital-based businesses, especially e-commerce, online retail, and
other companies that use AI -based marketing tools in their marketing activities. This scope is selected
because customers in digital business environments are more likely to experie nce AI-based marketing
interactions directly.

2.4 Data Collection Technique
This study uses primary data collected via an online questionnaire administered  to respondents who
meet the predetermined screening criteria, consistent with prior marketing and management studies
that gathered survey data online (Dewi et al., 2023) . The questionnaire is structured to capture (i)
respondent identity/demographics and (ii) respondents' experience with AI-enabled marketing/service
features (e.g., chatbot use), reflecting common survey designs that include demographic items alongside
AI-use/experience measures (Deshen & Noa, 2024) . All items are measured using a five -point Likert
scale anchored from 1 = strongly disagree to 5 = strongly agree, a scale format widely used in digital
marketing/AI-related questionnaire studies to quantify respondents' agreement with construct
statements.

2.6 Data Analysis Technique
Survey data are analyzed using variance -based SEM (PLS -SEM) in SmartPLS, as this approach is
widely used to estimate complex causal chains with latent constructs and to test mediation effects within
a single model (Sugiarto et al., 2025) . PLS -SEM is also frequently justified for predictive model
assessment and for situations where multivariate normality cannot be assumed, making it suitable for
explanatory–predictive research designs (Toyon, 2021).

3.5 AI-based marketing as a driver of customer experience and business performance
The study's results align with prior evidence that artificial intelligence (AI) –based marketing can
improve organizational outcomes and service -related performance. Empirical work on AI marketing
strategy reports positive associations with organizational performance outcomes such as profits, sales
growth, service quality, and customer satisfaction, supporting the claim that AI-enabled marketing can
be a performance driver rather than merely an operational tool (C. Wu & Monfort, 2022) . Related
evidence from B2B SMEs shows that AI -based practices contribute to performance outcomes (e.g.,
competitive advantage, customer retention, market share, sales volume, and profitability) by enhancing
firms' ability to understand customers' behaviors, attitudes, and experience s and by enabling data -
intensive decision-making.
3.6 Why AI-based marketing improves customer experience: efficiency, personalization, and reduced
customer effort
The finding that AI -based marketing positively affects CX is consistent with the literature,  which
indicates that AI enhances shopping and service experiences through personalization and convenience,
while also making trust a central determinant of whether these improvements are realized (Dimitrios
et al., 2023). In practice, AI applications such as chatbots and virtual assistants are repeatedly identified
as tools that can enhance customer service responsiveness and streamline customer interactions,
thereby improving the experience when deployed effectively. Broader digital customer interfaces (e.g.,
interactive kiosks, AI-enabled retail technologies) are likewise discussed as mechanisms to improve CX
and help firms remain competitive, suggesting that customers value accessible, efficient digital
touchpoints that deliver useful, accurate interactions (Jyoti et al., 2024).
3.7 Customer experience as a strategic resource for business performance
The study's finding that CX improves business performance is consistent with evidence that CX functions
as a pathway to relationship and brand outcomes t hat are strategically valuable. In B2B contexts, CX
quality is discussed as mediating between experience quality and relationship outcomes, and CX is
positively linked to brand equity dimensions (including brand loyalty), suggesting that improvements
in CX  can translate into downstream competitive benefits (Behera et al., 2022) . In consumer -facing
contexts, CX has been empirically positioned as a mediator that amplifies the effects of marketing
activities on loyalty -related outcomes; for instance, work in  the beauty industry finds that customer
experience significantly mediates the effect of content marketing on customer loyalty, supporting the
interpretation that CX is not merely an outcome but a mechanism that strengthens loyalty formation
and retention-relevant behaviors (Rosdiana et al., 2024). Evidence from banking in the "Marketing 5.0"
era similarly emphasizes that customer relationships and loyalty formation depend on customer
experience and perceived service quality, and that AI-enabled digital banking can contribute to feelings
of securit y and comfort, attributes plausibly connected with retention and relationship strength
(Sihombing & Zulfitri, 2024).

diverse, large -scale data, thereby facilitating improved decisions related to customer demand and
broader operational considerations. These mechanisms plausibly extend to marketing productivity and
more accurate targeting/strategy design (Sudarwati & Prianto, 2025).
3.9 Mediation: why customer experience makes AI -based marketing's performance impact more
meaningful
The mediation result reported in the study is consistent with evidence that CX frequently operates
as an intermediary mechanism translating marketing and technology inputs into loyalty, relationship,
and brand outcomes. CX is explicitly described as mediat ing between experience quality and
relationship outcomes in B2B settings, reinforcing the plausibility of CX as a central channel through
which improvements translate into business -relevant effects (Trusova et al., 2021) . Similarly, CX has
been empirically shown to mediate the relationship between marketing activities (e.g., content
marketing) and loyalty in consumer markets, indicating that marketing's effectiveness is amplified
when it delivers a superior experience rather than mere exposure  (Kramer et al., 2021). In AI-enabled
shopping contexts, the role of trust and perceived sacrifice as mediators shaping AI-enabled CX further
supports a layered mediation logic: AI features influence CX through trust/cost perceptions, and CX then
influences downstream outcomes(Mbama & Ezepue, 2018).

4. CONCLUSION
This study examined the relationships among AI-based marketing, customer experience, and customer-
reported business performance using data from 200 Indonesian customers. AI -based marketing was
positively associated with customer experience (β = 0.681, t = 11.210, p < 0.001) and perceived business
performance (β = 0.253, t = 3.470, p = 0.001). In contrast, customer experience was positively associated
with perceived performance (β = 0.460, t = 5.678, p < 0.001). Customer experience also mediated the
relationship between AI-based marketing and perceived performance (β = 0.314, t = 4.915, p < 0.001).
The model explained 46.4% of the variance in customer experience and 43.5% in perceived
performance. These findings suggest that favourable evaluations of AI-enabled marketing are associated
with better customer experiences and stronger perceptions of business outcomes. Managers should
therefore emphasize personalisation, responsiveness, convenience, trust, and service quality rather than
viewing AI solely as an automation tool. However, the cross-sectional design, purposive sampling, single-
source data, and perceptual performance measures limit causal inference and generalisability. Future
studies should use longitudinal or experimental designs, incorporate objective performance data, and
examine factors such as trust, privacy concerns, digital literacy, perceived usefulness, and customer
loyalty.
