Abstract
Against the backdrop of the deep integration of the “Healthy China” strategy and the digital economy, the
digital communication of ecotourism destinations is undergoing a paradigm shift from “landscape
attraction” to “health value co-creation.” Based on the AISAS model (Attention-Interest-Search-Action-
Share), this study conducts a case exploration of the current digital marketing practices for ecotourism
at Sayram Lake in Xinjiang, China. Utilizing the location map of the Sayram Lake Scenic Area
downloaded from the standard map service system of the Ministry of Natural Resources, combined with
ﬁeld research and online big data, this paper employs the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) and indicator
selection sources to conduct a comprehensive evaluation. The study systematically examines the
communication effectiveness of digital marketing in ecotourism areas and innovatively introduces the
analytical dimension of “wellness value communication” to reveal the interactive mechanisms between
tourists’ health perceptions and digital communication pathways. The ﬁndings indicate that: (1) The
comprehensive evaluation score of Sayram Lake’s ecotourism resources is 79.9 (rated as “good”), with
“ecological environment” (87.4) and “tourism resource value” (88.2) performing notably well, while
“digital communication capability” scores relatively low (68.3), constituting a critical bottleneck
constraining brand inﬂuence expansion; (2) Among online tourist reviews, keywords related to
“health/wellness” themes account for 62.4% of total valid feature words, with positive sentiment
reaching 75.03%, of which over 60% are directly associated with physical and mental health experiences,
indicating that tourists’ perceptions of Sayram Lake have exhibited signiﬁcant “therapeutic tourism”
characteristics; (3) Wellness value communication demonstrates a structural mismatch characterized by
“UGC-driven spontaneity and oﬃcial content absence,” with oﬃcial health narratives markedly
insuﬃcient across the Attention, Interest, and Search stages, a pronounced “health information supply-
demand disconnect” in the Search stage, and lagging wellness product development in the Action stage
constraining the conversion eﬃciency from health interest to health consumption. Based on these
ﬁndings, this study proposes the theoretical construct of a “digital-intelligent ecotourism wellness
communication system,” advocating that western ecotourism regions should transcend traditional
marketing logic by repositioning ecological resources as publicly accessible “health assets” and “social
welfare.”
1. Introduction
Globally, the digital economy is profoundly reshaping industrial structures and consumption patterns,
with the digital transformation of the tourism industry having become an irreversible global trend. Data
from the United Nations World Tourism Organization (UNWTO) indicate that digital tourism service
platforms now cover over 60% of global tourism transaction processes, with social media emerging as
the primary information channel inﬂuencing tourists’ destination choices. Against this global backdrop,
China’s digital economy has entered a phase of comprehensive deepening in recent years. The 14th Five-
Year Plan for Digital Economy Development (2022) explicitly proposes promoting the widespread
application of digital technologies across society, accelerating the digital transformation of traditional
industries, and particularly emphasizing the reshaping role of digital technologies on production
Page 2/35

methods, lifestyles, and governance systems (State Council, 2022). In the tourism sector, the State
Council’s 14th Five-Year Plan for Tourism Development (2022) further advocates actively advancing
smart tourism development and expanding digital marketing pathways to enhance tourism service
eﬃciency and destination image communication capabilities (Zheng, 2022). These national-level policy
orientations provide institutional support for improving the institutional mechanisms promoting
consumption and enhancing the foundational role of consumption, while also driving the evolution of
tourist consumption patterns toward greater dependence on new media, intelligent platforms, and online
content. As digital commercial communication methods gradually emerge in public view, optimizing
information communication pathways and improving consumer decision-making paths through
communication studies and marketing theories have become current priorities.
As digital technology application scenarios continue to expand, tourism destination marketing has
gradually shifted from traditional media advertising and oﬄine promotion toward full-link digital
marketing, content community operations, and user interaction experience optimization. Domestic
scholar Qi (2020) noted that the rapid development of digital tourism indicates that border regions such
as Xinjiang are gradually entering a “golden decade” window period, with destinations effectively
enhancing tourist awareness and travel willingness through multi-platform integrated marketing
strategies. Cheng (2024) further emphasized that relying on the construction of the Silk Road Economic
Belt, promoting tourism digital transformation in Xinjiang is not only a necessary path to enhance
competitiveness but also an important component of regional coordinated development. These studies
indicate that domestic tourism marketing research is gradually evolving from traditional media
dominance toward digital marketing mechanisms, demonstrating a shifting trend toward precision
targeting, content operations, and user interaction. Concurrently, Abdurrahim et al. (2019), in
constructing the AISAS model (Attention-Interest-Search-Action-Share), emphasized that destination
visibility and interactivity on social platforms in new media environments profoundly inﬂuence tourists’
cognitive formation, information search, and ﬁnal actions, providing a new theoretical perspective for
studying the relationship between “destination image” and “tourist behavioral decisions.”
From a practical perspective, digital marketing has not only expanded the communication pathways of
tourism information but also reconstructed tourists’ cognitive structures regarding travel experiences.
Within the collaborative framework of multimedia platforms, online distribution systems (OTA), and
telecommunications operators, tourism enterprises and management institutions can more precisely
reach potential customers, achieving rapid information dissemination and product conversion. Gao and
Lu (2023) proposed that in smart tourism service systems, digital transformation has become the core
pathway for optimizing tourism public services and eﬃcient resource allocation. Taking Xinjiang as an
example, although its tourism digital layout started relatively late, it has achieved certain results in recent
years through self-media communication, government guidance, and platform promotion. Particularly in
the Sayram Lake Scenic Area, local government and tourism management units have launched a series
of online-oﬄine collaborative promotion activities under the theme “Encounter Sayram Lake: The
Promise of Blue Ice,” constructing a tourism brand with regional identity. By creating a “social media
hotspot destination” image through platforms such as Xiaohongshu and Douyin, they have effectively
Page 3/35

extended the tourism off-season period while enhancing consumption enthusiasm in the winter tourism
market. Xiao and Huang (2020), taking Sayram Lake as their research object, proposed that in
constructing tourism destination images, attention should be paid to the dynamic balance between
supply and demand perspectives to achieve rational utilization of tourism resources and continuous
optimization of brand image.
However, against the backdrop of continuously released digital dividends, the development of
ecotourism destinations still faces multiple challenges. Particularly under the policy context of
ecological civilization construction and high-quality development as national priorities, promoting
tourism industry development while protecting ecosystems has become a core issue in contemporary
tourism planning and regional coordination. Tian and Wu (2011), in their study of the Louguantai Scenic
Area in Xi’an, pointed out that tourism attractiveness derives not only from natural resources themselves
but is also closely related to market promotion mechanisms, transportation accessibility, and supporting
service levels, imposing higher requirements on the systematic integration capacity of ecotourism
destinations. Wang and Guo (2022) noted that under the “Revitalizing Xinjiang through Tourism” strategy,
Xinjiang’s ecotourism development should take digital transformation as the entry point to construct a
comprehensive system coordinating resource protection, community participation, and market
promotion. In fact, ecotourism destinations often exhibit signiﬁcant seasonal characteristics, with tourist
reception volumes heavily inﬂuenced by climatic conditions and holiday systems, forming structural
contradictions of “peak season overload, off-season weakness” (Wei & Mihala, 2024). It is projected that
by 2025, national ecotourism annual reception will reach 2.5 billion person-times; without effective
regulation and scientiﬁc guidance, this will not only exacerbate ecosystem burdens but also weaken the
sustained attractiveness of destination brands. Notably, the other side of these challenges contains
important opportunities: as tourist motivations shift from mere “sightseeing gaze” toward pursuing
physical and mental health and self-actualization, the unique natural healing resources of ecotourism
destinations structurally align with wellness tourism market demands, opening new strategic space for
digital marketing transformation centered on “health value co-creation” in western ecotourism regions.
2. Theoretical Analysis and Conceptual Framework
2.1 Theoretical Development of the AISAS Model and Its
Application in Tourism
The AISAS model was initially proposed by the Japanese advertising agency Dentsu to explain the
complete behavioral chain of consumers from Attention to Share in the digital age, emphasizing user
proactivity and interactivity in information acquisition and action (Abdurrahim et al., 2019). Combined
with the logic of the digital marketing “funnel model,” it constructs a research framework centered on
tourist behavioral pathways, as illustrated in Fig. 1.
Within this framework, digital tourism marketing research indicates that users undergo behavioral
pathways from interest stimulation, information search, to actual action and social sharing, with
Page 4/35

conversion characteristics that can be revealed through the funnel model (Javed et al., 2022). Relevant
literature emphasizes the use of quantiﬁable indicators to monitor performance at each stage, such as
keyword heat and platform search volume in the Search stage, booking and click conversion data in the
Action stage, and UGC output and interaction rates in the Share stage (Li & Xu, 2025). By comparing data
performance across each link, critical loss points in the marketing chain can be identiﬁed, providing a
basis for optimizing digital marketing strategies. Therefore, the AISAS model demonstrates strong
explanatory power in studying ecotourism marketing effectiveness.
Domestic scholars have gradually applied AISAS to tourism marketing in recent years, though the overall
application remains in the exploratory stage. Zhang et al. (2025) combined AISAS with the HOE model to
analyze the impact of KOC marketing on tourist Attention, Interest, Search, Action, and Share, validating
the model’s effectiveness in revealing digital communication pathways. Yang et al. (2025) constructed
an antecedent conﬁguration model of online purchase behavior for social commerce users based on
AISAS, further demonstrating the critical roles of Search, Action, and Share stages in digital scenarios. Li
(2025) emphasized that AISAS is highly compatible with the “strong interaction and emphasis on
sharing” characteristics of short videos, proposing marketing pathways for cultural tourism short videos.
Ding (2025) applied AISAS to regional tourism consumption behavior research, demonstrating its
practical value in tourism behavior analysis.
Wellness tourism has risen from a niche market to one of the fastest-growing segments of the global
tourism industry. According to the Global Wellness Institute (GWI), the wellness tourism market grew
from USD 438 billion in 2012 to USD 894 billion in 2024, exceeding pre-pandemic levels by 36% and
projected to surpass USD 1.4 trillion by 2029. The core driving force lies in the deep awakening of
consumer health awareness: McKinsey’s 2025 survey revealed that 84% of American consumers and
94% of Chinese consumers rank “health” as “important” or “most important” among their life priorities.
Wellness tourism consumption demonstrates notable economic resilience, with consumers showing
lower willingness to cut health-related expenditures than clothing or entertainment spending during
budget constraints. The academic community generally recognizes that the connotation of wellness
tourism has transcended traditional “medical intervention,” emphasizing the achievement of holistic well-
being through natural healing, physical-mental balance, and active lifestyles. In this context, tourist
motivations have undergone profound shifts: from seeking “sightseeing gaze” to pursuing “self-care,”
and from passive leisure to active health practices. Valente-Mosqueda et al. (2026) further revealed the
positive correlation between mental health and wellness tourism participation, demonstrating that
tourists’ mental health levels directly inﬂuence their interest in and willingness to participate in wellness
tourism. Tiwari et al. (2026) emphasized that wellness tourism is transitioning from individual healing
toward a “regenerative tourism” paradigm that considers destination sustainability, with the core logic
lying in the co-creation of “tourist physical and mental health” and “destination ecological well-being.” To
systematically evaluate the potential of wellness tourism destinations, Jittamai et al. (2025) proposed
the 6AsTD evaluation framework, encompassing Attractions, Accessibility, Amenities, Activities,
Available Packages, and Ancillary Services, with natural landscapes, cultural signiﬁcance, safety, and
facility quality identiﬁed as priority sub-criteria inﬂuencing wellness tourist decisions. Concurrently,
Page 5/35

research based on Identity-Based Motivation Theory (IBMT) further reveals that “health identity” serves
as a critical psychological mechanism linking tourist motivations and behavioral intentions: when
tourists perceive themselves as “individuals pursuing a healthy lifestyle,” their destination choices and
experience evaluations tend to align more closely with wellness values. Based on the above theoretical
connection, this study constructs an integrated analytical framework of “AISAS Model × Wellness
Tourism Value”: in the Attention and Interest stages, the focus is on examining whether social media
content effectively communicates Sayram Lake’s wellness imagery as a “soul healing destination” and
“natural oxygen bar”; in the Search stage, the study investigates whether tourists can conveniently
access information on wellness services such as forest bathing, meditation, and Kazakh traditional
healing experiences; in the Action and Share stages, attention is directed toward whether tourists gain
health beneﬁts and spiritual satisfaction from their experiences, thereby facilitating more compelling
word-of-mouth sharing and value co-creation.
3. Study Area and Research Methods
3.1 Study Area Overview
Following the principle of theoretical sampling, this study selects Sayram Lake as the case site. The
Sayram Lake Scenic Area is located in a high-mountain basin of the western Tianshan Mountains, over
90 kilometers southwest of Bole City, Bortala Mongol Autonomous Prefecture, Xinjiang Uygur
Autonomous Region. It is a natural and cultural treasure of northwestern Xinjiang, adjacent to the Wu-I
Highway of National Highway 312, and is a semi-open scenic area (Fig. 2). With its high altitude,
expansive lake, and abundant natural resources, Sayram Lake is not only an ideal destination for
ecotourism but also possesses rich historical and cultural heritage (Tian et al., 2015). However, despite
its superior natural conditions and strategic geographical location, the scenic area faces challenges
during development, including short tourism cycles and declining economic returns. Therefore, rational
planning and product innovation are essential to promote the sustainable development of Sayram Lake’s
tourism industry (Wang & Guo, 2022).

359.542 billion, a year-on-year increase of 21%, marking historic highs and ranking second nationally in

3.2 Research Methods
3.2.1 Analytic Hierarchy Process Principles and Indicator
System Construction
To scientiﬁcally evaluate the attractiveness level of ecotourism resources in Sayram Lake National
Wetland Park, this study employs the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) to construct a comprehensive
evaluation indicator system for ecotourism resources. AHP is a multi-criteria decision-making method
combining qualitative and quantitative approaches, suitable for comprehensive evaluation problems with
multi-level and complex structures. Taking Sayram Lake National Wetland Park as the research object,
this study references the lake wetland ecotourism evaluation frameworks proposed by Zeng et al. (2010)
and Zhong et al. (2012), integrating dimensions reﬂecting tourist behavioral characteristics from the
AISAS marketing model (Attention-Interest-Search-Action-Share) to construct a comprehensive
evaluation system for ecotourism resource attractiveness adapted to the digital communication context.
Page 7/35

This method builds a multi-level indicator system and determines indicator weights through expert
scoring and consistency testing, effectively reﬂecting the inﬂuence of different factors on overall
evaluation results, and enabling quantitative expression and visualization of certain spatially related
indicators (such as ecological integrity, scenic spot distribution density, and transportation accessibility),
thereby enhancing the empirical foundation and operability of the evaluation (Ou et al., 2012).
(1) Ecotourism Resource Attractiveness Evaluation System
This study constructs a three-tier evaluation system:
Target Layer (A): Comprehensive evaluation of the ecotourism resource attractiveness of Sayram
Lake National Wetland Park.
Criteria Layer (B): Six ﬁrst-level factors: Ecological Environment (B1), Lake Wetland Characteristics
(B2), Tourism Resource Value (B3), Development Conditions (B4), Digital Communication Capability
(B5), and Tourist Experience Feedback (B6).
Indicator Layer (C): Twenty-seven quantiﬁable second-level indicators covering dimensions of
naturalness, uniqueness, accessibility, online communicability, and tourist perception (see Table 2).
(2) Indicator Design Principles and Data Sources
The indicators adhere to the following principles:
Scientiﬁc Validity: Reference to domestic and international ecotourism evaluation studies.
Quantiﬁability: Each indicator has clear data sources.
Spatial Relevance: Priority given to indicators compatible with GIS data.
Behavioral Orientation: Integration of AISAS model reﬂecting tourist behavioral pathways.
Regional Adaptability: Representation of the resource characteristics of Sayram Lake Wetland Park.

4. Results and Analysis
4.1 Construction of the Evaluation Indicator System
Based on the theoretical framework of AHP, this study constructed a three-tier comprehensive evaluation
system for the ecotourism resources of Sayram Lake National Wetland Park. The system includes the
Target Layer (A Layer: Comprehensive Attractiveness of Ecotourism Resources), the Criteria Layer (B
Layer: Ecological Environment, Lake Wetland Characteristics, Tourism Resource Value, Development
Conditions, Digital Communication Capability, Tourist Experience Feedback), and the Indicator Layer (C
Layer: 27 speciﬁc evaluation factors), as shown in Table 2. The selection of indicators comprehensively
considered the ecological environment foundation, tourism resource attractiveness, infrastructure
conditions, and digital communication potential, while integrating the ﬁve-stage AISAS model (Attention-
Interest-Search-Action-Share) to embed tourist behavioral logic into the indicator system. This design
ensures the system serves both the scientiﬁc evaluation of ecotourism resources and the practical
guidance for digital marketing optimization.
4.2 Evaluation Factor Weights
Eight experts from relevant ﬁelds at the university (including tourism geography, ecological conservation,
and marketing) were invited to conduct pairwise comparisons of the indicator layer, constructing
judgment matrices. Consistency veriﬁcation and weight calculations were performed using Yaahp
software. The judgment matrix for the Criteria Layer B against Target Layer A yielded a consistency ratio
of C.R. = 0.067 < 0.1, indicating satisfactory consistency and reliable evaluation results. Furthermore, the
judgment matrices for the Indicator Layer C against each Criteria Layer all yielded consistency ratios < 
0.1, demonstrating good logical consistency. The weight allocations for each ﬁrst-level indicator of

5. Conclusions and Recommendations
5.1 Conclusions
This study, employing the AISAS model as the analytical framework and integrating the Analytic
Hierarchy Process (AHP) with online text analysis methods, systematically examines the digital
marketing practices of ecotourism at Sayram Lake along the tourist behavioral chain of “Attention-
Interest-Search-Action-Share.” Innovatively introducing the analytical dimension of “wellness value
communication,” the study reveals the communication patterns, structural deﬁciencies, and social value
realization pathways of western ecotourism regions in the digital economy era. The main conclusions
are as follows:
First, the ecotourism resource endowment is superior, but digital communication capability constitutes
the core bottleneck constraining brand spillover. The comprehensive evaluation results show that
Sayram Lake’s overall ecotourism resource attractiveness score is 79.9 (rated as “Good”), with
“Ecological Environment” (87.4) and “Tourism Resource Value” (88.2) performing notably well, reﬂecting
its strong regional appeal in natural landscapes and cultural endowments. However, the “Digital
Communication Capability” score is only 68.3 (rated as “Fair”), signiﬁcantly lower than other dimensions.
The spatial distribution of social media followers exhibits characteristics of “Xinjiang agglomeration
(over 40%) and insuﬃcient eastern diffusion,” with low penetration rates in economically developed
provinces such as Guangdong, Beijing, and Jiangsu, indicating that the scenic area’s national visibility
still relies on hotspot events and seasonal content, without yet forming normalized cross-provincial and
cross-demographic communication capabilities.
Second, tourists demonstrate highly spontaneous cognition and positive evaluation of Sayram Lake’s
“health/wellness” value, but the scenic area’s oﬃcial wellness narratives lag signiﬁcantly behind tourist
perceptions. Online text analysis indicates that feature words directly related to physical and mental
health account for 62.4% of tourist online reviews, with positive sentiment reaching 75.03%, of which
over 60% relates to experiences such as “healing,” “relaxation,” and “soul puriﬁcation.” Tourists’
perception of Sayram Lake has transcended the traditional sightseeing level, exhibiting distinctive
“therapeutic tourism” characteristics. However, “wellness” themed content accounts for less than 15% of
the scenic area’s oﬃcial promotional content. The Attention stage lacks clear health brand positioning;
the Interest stage fails to actively guide wellness topic interaction; and the Search stage lacks
Page 25/35

authoritative information on negative oxygen ion concentration and suitable meditation locations on
oﬃcial channels, creating a structural mismatch of “strong spontaneous tourist perception, systematic
absence of oﬃcial content.”
Third, wellness value communication exhibits typical characteristics of “uneven distribution and chain
fragmentation” across each AISAS stage. In the Attention stage, new media marketing has achieved
initial results but precision targeting remains insuﬃcient, and health brand narratives have not yet been
established. In the Interest stage, UGC content is rich but uneven in quality, lacking in-depth wellness
experience content. In the Search stage, a serious mismatch exists between health information supply
and tourist demand, with SEO optimization and intelligent customer service systems remaining
imperfect. In the Action stage, the digital booking process requires further optimization, and wellness
products (such as forest bathing, Kazakh traditional healing experiences, and guided meditation) are
underdeveloped, constraining the conversion eﬃciency from “health interest” to “health consumption.” In
the Share stage, tourists show strong willingness to share “healing experiences” but lack systematic
word-of-mouth management mechanisms; high-popularity content concentrates on “seasonal scenery,”
“cycling,” and “camping,” with insuﬃcient communication drive for cultural, ecological, educational, and
wellness-oriented in-depth content.
Fourth, the dilemma of wellness value communication at Sayram Lake reﬂects the universal tension
within the digital transformation of western ecotourism regions — the “high value of ecological resource
endowment” versus the “low eﬃciency of health value communication.” During the policy window period
characterized by the overlapping of strategies such as “Belt and Road,” “Cultural Enrichment of Xinjiang,”
and “Healthy China 2030,” Sayram Lake possesses the foundational conditions and geographical
advantages to build a high-quality “ecological wellness destination” brand. However, its digital marketing
remains in a transitional stage from “scale expansion” to “quality improvement.” This study further
reveals that the digital communication of ecotourism destinations should not be limited to the technical
level of “how to sell beautiful scenery,” but should reconstruct the communication logic from the value
dimension of “health social welfare,” transforming ecological resources into publicly perceivable and
accessible health assets and social beneﬁts.
In summary, by incorporating the dimension of “wellness value communication” into the AISAS analytical
framework, this study extends the model’s application boundaries in ecotourism research, providing a
new human geography interpretation framework and empirical reference for the digital transformation
and health value communication of western frontier tourism destinations under the “Belt and Road”
initiative. The ﬁndings also offer policy implications for the implementation pathways of the Healthy
China strategy in western ecotourism regions and the enhancement of social welfare in frontier areas
through digital technology empowerment.

5.2.1 Enhancing Wellness Brand Exposure and Precision
Targeting in the Attention Stage
In the Attention stage, Sayram Lake should transcend traditional landscape marketing logic and
establish a clear “ecological wellness destination” brand positioning. Recommendations include: (1)
Leveraging big data user proﬁling to identify target populations with clear needs for “health tourism,”
“stress relief,” and “natural healing” (such as high-stress professionals from ﬁrst-tier cities, middle-aged
and elderly wellness groups, and families) and implementing precision content delivery on platforms
such as Douyin, Xiaohongshu, and WeChat Moments; (2) Integrating the emotional imagery of “The Last
Tear of the Atlantic” with health functional narratives of “natural oxygen bar” and “soul healing
destination” to construct a national-level brand narrative of “frontier ecological security barrier — health
value provider”; (3) Planning the “Sayram Lake Wellness Season” annual theme campaign to create a
complementary off-season wellness communication strategy alongside peak-season landscape
marketing, extending the tourism communication cycle.
5.2.2 Strengthening Wellness Content Attractiveness and
Interaction Mechanisms in the Interest Stage
A three-tier wellness content ecosystem of “oﬃcial guidance + KOL demonstration + UGC co-creation”
should be constructed: (1) At the oﬃcial level, plan the “Sayram Lake Healing Plan” series of content,
covering composite scenarios such as “lake cycling,” “high negative oxygen ion deep breathing routes,”
“starry sky meditation,” and “Kazakh traditional cultural healing experiences,” providing tourists with a
menu of participatory wellness activities; (2) Invite health and wellness, outdoor sports, and
psychological healing KOLs for in-depth experience creation, producing content with both aesthetic value
and health knowledge; (3) Establish cycling supply stations and stamp collection check-in points on-site,
redeemable for Xinjiang specialty IP cultural and creative gifts, using gamiﬁcation mechanisms to
stimulate users’ active creation and sharing of wellness experience content.
5.2.3 Optimizing Health Information Supply and
Authoritativeness in the Search Stage
An “authoritative health information matrix” should be established: (1) Uniformly launch a “Wellness
Healthy Travel” special section on the oﬃcial website, mini-program, and OTA ﬂagship stores,
systematically publishing health-related information such as real-time negative oxygen ion concentration
monitoring data, recommended meditation and hiking locations, and wellness activity schedules; (2)
Implement SEO optimization for long-tail health search terms such as “Sayram Lake healing,” “Is Sayram
Lake suitable for relaxation,” and “Sayram Lake wellness guide,” ensuring oﬃcial authoritative
information ranks at the top of search results; (3) Deploy intelligent customer service systems with a
“wellness travel recommendation” dialogue module that automatically pushes personalized wellness
routes and products based on user needs.

5.2.4 Advancing Wellness Product Development and
Digital Service Experience in the Action Stage
The transformation of wellness products from “concept” to “bookable services” should be accelerated:
(1) Develop “Sayram Lake Three-Day Wellness Journey” packages integrating lake cycling, lakeside
meditation, Kazakh herbal bath experiences, and eco-photography therapy, available for sale on
platforms such as Ctrip and Fliggy; (2) Add a “Wellness Service Booking” entry in the “One Mobile Phone
Tour Sayram Lake” mini-program, supporting one-click reservation of wellness guides, customized
itineraries, and rental of wellness equipment (such as trekking poles and yoga mats); (3) Improve on-site
wellness supporting facilities within the scenic area, including meditation viewing platforms, wellness
trail signage systems, and health supply stations, enhancing the comfort and sense of security of oﬄine
services.
5.2.5 Strengthening Wellness Word-of-Mouth
Communication and Social Empowerment in the Share
Stage
A systematic “wellness word-of-mouth communication mechanism” should be established: (1) Launch
the “Sayram Lake Healer Project” social sharing incentive campaign, encouraging tourists to publish
wellness experience short videos or photo guides using oﬃcial uniﬁed hashtags (e.g.,
#SayramLakeHealingDiary), with monthly selection of quality content rewarded with scenic area annual
passes and cultural and creative gift boxes; (2) Establish a “word-of-mouth monitoring and rapid
response” mechanism for wellness experiences, promptly responding to and rectifying negative
evaluations concerning hygiene, service quality, and health safety to maintain health brand trust; (3)
Regularly publish the Sayram Lake Wellness Experience Annual Report, presenting tourist health
satisfaction and wellness service coverage through data visualization, reinforcing the public perception
and social responsibility of the scenic area as a “health value provider.”
In summary, Sayram Lake should seize the dual opportunity window of the “Healthy China” strategy and
digital economy development, taking the construction of a “digital-intelligent ecotourism wellness
communication system” as the lever to promote the paradigm shift of digital marketing from “traﬃc
acquisition” to “health value co-creation,” providing a replicable practical model for the high-quality
development of western ecotourism destinations and the supply of health social welfare.
