Abstract:
Background: The declining interest in agro-maritime higher education has become
a strategic concern for Indonesia’s sustainable development agenda. As universities
increasingly use digital marketing communication to attract prospective students, limited
attention has been given to how digital communication, career-related perceptions, and the
Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) constructs jointly shape students’ educational choices.
Purpose: This study investigates students’ intentions toward agro-maritime higher
education by examining the role of digital marketing communication within an extended
Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) framework.
Design/methodology/approach: A cross-sectional survey was administered to 1,424 Grade
XII science-track students from 44 senior high schools across six regions in Indonesia. The
proposed model was analyzed using Structural Equation Modeling-Partial Least Squares
(SEM-PLS).
Findings/Results: The model explains a substantial proportion of the variance in students’
intentions (R² = 0.588). Digital marketing communication was identified as the strongest
determinant of intention (β = 0.447, total effect), influencing students both directly (β =
0.345) and indirectly through perceived career and employment prospects and attitude.
Subjective norms and perceived behavioral control also showed significant positive effects
on students’ intentions.
Conclusion: The findings highlight the strategic role of digital marketing communication
in shaping students’ educational choices by influencing their perceptions of future career
opportunities and attitude toward agro-maritime study programs.
Originality/value (State of the art): By integrating psychological, economic, and
informational factors within a single framework, this study offers a more comprehensive
explanation of higher-education choice behavior. The findings extend the TPB by
demonstrating a communication-driven mechanism in which digital marketing
communication shapes perceived career and employment prospects, which subsequently
influences attitudes and intentions toward agro-maritime higher education.
Keywords: agro-maritime higher education, career and employment prospects, digital
marketing communication, higher education choice, students’ intention

INTRODUCTION
The agro-maritime sector plays a strategic role in
supporting food security, natural resource management
and sustainable economic development in Indonesia. As
an agrarian and archipelagic country, Indonesia relies
heavily on agriculture, fisheries, livestock, and forestry
as key economic drivers, contributing significantly to
its economic stability and environmental sustainability
(FAO, 2017; World Bank, 2021). Consequently, the
sector requires a competent and adaptive workforce
capable of addressing global challenges, including
climate change and sustainable resource management
(FAO 2017).
However, the agro-maritime sector is currently facing
serious challenges related to workforce regeneration.
Recent data indicate a growing trend in labor aging,
which may threaten the long-term sustainability of
agriculture and related subsectors (BPS, 2023). This
phenomenon is consistent with global findings showing
declining interest among younger generations in
agricultural and related fields, leading to concerns about
future shortages of skilled labor (FAO, 2014; OECD,
2019). Previous studies have also highlighted that the
younger generation tends to perceive agriculture as
less attractive than other modern sectors (White, 2012;
Jayne et al. 2014).
The declining interest of young people in agro-
maritime fields is also reflected in higher-education
choices. Agro-maritime study programs tend to be less
competitive than disciplines such as health sciences,
engineering, and business. Prior studies suggest that
students’ choice of study program is largely influenced
by perceived career prospects, economic returns, and
alignment with future career goals (Chapman, 1981;
Cabrera & La Nasa, 2000; Montmarquette et al. 2002;
Altonji et al. 2016). A similar pattern was observed
at IPB University, where agro-maritime programs
consistently showed lower application rates than non-
agro-maritime programs (DAPPMB IPB, 2024). These
conditions indicate a mismatch between the strategic
importance of the agro-maritime sector and students’
educational preferences.
Students’ intentions to choose a study program
are shaped by a complex process involving both
psychological and contextual factors. The Theory
of Planned Behavior (TPB) explains that intention
is influenced by attitude, subjective norms, and
perceived behavioral control (Ajzen, 1991, 2005). This
framework has been widely applied in educational
decision-making contexts (Suharti and Sirine, 2012;
Paul et al. 2016). Recent applications of the TPB
demonstrate its continued relevance and adaptability
in explaining decision-making processes in evolving
educational environments (Broadbent et al. 2025).
However, students’ decision-making is not solely
driven by internal factors.
From the perspective of Human Capital Theory,
education is viewed as an investment that generates
future economic returns, including job opportunities,
career advancement, and income potential (Becker,
1993; Psacharopoulos & Patrinos, 2018). Empirical
studies consistently show that perceived economic
returns significantly influence students’ educational
choices (Altonji et al. 2013; Altonji et al. 2016). In
the context of agro-maritime education, the limited
visibility of career prospects may reduce students’
interest in choosing such programs. For many
prospective students, educational choices are evaluated
not only based on academic interest but also on how
clearly a field of study is associated with future career
opportunities, professional identity, and long-term
career development.
The rapid advancement of digital technology has
fundamentally changed the way students search for,
evaluate, and interact with information. Rather than
acting as passive recipients, prospective students
increasingly engage with personalized and interactive
content on multiple digital platforms (Chong et al.
2024).
Against this backdrop, digital marketing communication
has become an important source of information for
higher education decision-making (Constantinides &
Zinck Stagno, 2011; Rutter et al. 2016; Dwivedi et al.
2021). Beyond increasing information accessibility,
effective digital communication can foster customer
engagement, which in turn shapes perceptions,
preferences, and subsequent decisions (Lim et al.
2022).
Recent evidence shows that social media marketing
influences university choice by shaping institutional
image, trust, and educational preferences (Arief &
Brabo, 2025). This trend is particularly evident among
Generation Z, who rely heavily on social media
platforms to explore educational opportunities. For

METHODS
A quantitative cross-sectional survey design was
adopted to investigate the factors influencing students’
intentions toward agro-maritime higher education.
This approach allows for a systematic examination of
the relationships among variables within a structural
model (Creswell & Creswell, 2018). Data collection
was conducted across multiple regions of Indonesia
between November and December 2025, during which
Grade XII students actively explored higher education
options.
The data used in this study were primary data collected
directly from the respondents through a structured
questionnaire. The respondents consisted of Grade
XII science-track (IPA) students, as they represent
the primary population eligible to enrol in agro-
maritime study programs. The questionnaire was
designed to measure students’ perceptions of digital
this generation, message credibility, relevance, visual
attractiveness, and interactive features play important
roles in shaping preferences and information-processing

Despite the extensive use of TPB in explaining
educational choices, prior studies have generally
examined psychological, economic, and
communication-related factors independently (Juhaidi,
2024). While TPB studies primarily focus on attitude,
subjective norms, and perceived behavioral control as
predictors of intention, research on career prospects
and digital communication often treats these variables
as separate external influences. Consequently, little
is known about the mechanism through which digital
communication shapes students’ perceptions of
future career opportunities and how these perceptions
subsequently translate into favorable attitudes and
educational intentions. Understanding this process
is particularly important in agro-maritime education,
where career pathways are often perceived as less
visible than those in more popular fields of study.
This limitation is particularly relevant in the context
of agro-maritime education. Although the agro-
maritime sector plays a strategic role in national
development, student interest in agro-maritime study
programs is relatively low. This suggests the existence
of a perception gap, where the strategic importance of
the sector is not necessarily accompanied by equally
positive perceptions of future career opportunities and
professional prospects among students.
To address this gap, this study proposes an extended TPB
model by integrating digital marketing communication
and perceived career and employment prospects into
the intention-formation process. In this model, digital
marketing communication is positioned not only as
a promotional tool but also as an upstream cognitive
antecedent that shapes students’ perceptions of future
career opportunities in the field. These perceptions
subsequently influence attitudinal evaluation and
ultimately affect students’ intentions toward agro-
maritime higher education. This integrative approach
provides a more comprehensive explanation of
how internal psychological factors, career-related
perceptions, and external informational stimuli interact
to shape students’ educational decisions.

Data analysis was conducted using SEM-PLS, which
is suitable for analyzing complex models involving
multiple constructs and latent variables (Hair et al.
2021). The analysis was conducted using a two-
stage approach. First, the measurement model was
evaluated to assess reliability and validity, including
indicator loadings, composite reliability, average
variance extracted, and discriminant validity using the
Fornell-Larcker criterion. Second, the structural model
was assessed to examine the relationships among
constructs using path coefficients, the coefficient of
determination (R²), and bootstrapping procedures to
test the significance of both direct and indirect effects.
The hypotheses in this study were developed based on
established theoretical foundations and were supported
by prior empirical findings. Within the TPB framework,
attitude, subjective norm, and perceived behavioral
control are recognized as direct determinants of
behavioral intention (Ajzen, 1991). Accordingly, these
constructs are expected to positively influence students’
intentions toward agro-maritime higher education.
Drawing on Human Capital Theory, educational choices
are often evaluated based on expected economic returns.
Therefore, perceived career and employment prospects
are expected to positively shape students’ attitudes
toward agro-maritime study programs (Becker, 1993).
From a marketing communication perspective, digital
marketing communication functions as an external
informational stimulus that shapes belief structures and
influences decision-making processes (Kotler & Keller,
2016; Sumarwan 2014). Effective communication is
expected to enhance perceived value, reduce uncertainty,
and strengthen evaluative judgments. Consequently,
marketing communication, perceived career and

All constructs were measured using a five-point Likert
scale ranging from strongly disagree to strongly agree,
which is widely used in behavioral research to capture
individual perceptions and attitudes (Hair et al. 2021).
The questionnaire items were adapted from established
theoretical constructs and previously validated
measurement scales. To ensure conceptual consistency
and contextual relevance to agro-maritime higher
education, the research supervisors reviewed the initial
instrument. A pre-test involving more than 30 Grade
XII students was subsequently conducted to assess item
clarity, readability, and comprehension. The instrument
was further evaluated through a pilot study in a senior
high school in Bogor to examine the data collection
procedures and overall readability. Feedback from
these stages informed revisions to the item wording and
questionnaire structure prior to nationwide distribution.
This study applied a multistage sampling technique
to ensure adequate representation. Indonesia was
first divided into six major regions, followed by the
selection of schools using a cluster sampling approach.
Subsequently, respondents were purposively selected
from Grade XII science-track students in each selected
school. A total of 1,424 valid responses were collected
from 44 senior high school students.
Data were gathered using a self-administered online
questionnaire distributed through KoBoToolbox, with
prior coordination and supervision from the school
counselors (Guru Bimbingan Konseling). Providing
respondents with brief exposure to digital promotional
content ensured a shared understanding of agro-
maritime study programmes and reduced the likelihood
of response bias resulting from differences in prior
knowledge.

students’ educational decision making.
Taken together, these findings suggest that students’
intention formation follows a sequential process, where
digital marketing communication shapes perceived
career prospects, which in turn influences attitude, and
ultimately intention. This process is further reinforced
by social influence, highlighting that students’ decisions
are not purely individual but socially embedded and
information-driven.
The findings of this study provide strong empirical
support for the extended Theory of Planned Behavior
(TPB) models in explaining students’ intentions
toward agro-maritime higher education. The model
demonstrates substantial explanatory power, indicating
that students’ intentions are shaped not only by core
TPB constructs but also by contextual informational
factors, particularly digital marketing communication.
This finding is consistent with prior studies suggesting
that behavioral intention is influenced not only by
internal psychological factors but also by external
informational stimuli that shape belief structures
(Ajzen, 1991; Conner and Armitage, 1998).

A key insight from this study is the central role of digital
marketing communication in the formation of intention.
Rather than functioning solely as a promotional tool,
digital communication has emerged as a critical
antecedent that shapes how students evaluate the
value and outcomes of higher education. This finding
suggests that digital marketing communication plays
a fundamental role in influencing students’ cognitive
evaluation, particularly regarding perceived career and
employment prospects.
One possible explanation for this result is that most
senior high school students have limited direct
exposure to agro-maritime professions and the realities
of the labor market. Consequently, their understanding
of career opportunities is largely constructed through
mediated information sources. In such circumstances,
digital communication becomes more than a
promotional instrument; it serves as a mechanism
through which students develop expectations regarding
future occupations, professional identities, and career
opportunities associated with agro-maritime education.
This result aligns with prior research highlighting
the importance of digital media in shaping students’
perceptions and decision-making processes in higher
education (Constantinides & Zinck Stagno 2011;
Rutter et al. 2016). This finding reinforces the growing
recognition that digital communication plays a central
role in shaping consumer decision-making, particularly
in information-intensive contexts such as the higher
education sector. Emerging evidence suggests that
digital communication influences decision-making
by increasing information accessibility and fostering
customer engagement through interactive and
personalized content experiences (Kulikovskaja et
al. 2023). In the context of higher education, such
engagement may strengthen students’ emotional
connections with academic programs and enhance the
perceived relevance of future career opportunities.
This role is particularly important in agro-maritime
education, where career pathways are often perceived as
less visible, making digital marketing communication
essential for reducing uncertainty and enhancing
perceived value.
The descriptive findings suggest that the issue is not a
lack of access to information. Most respondents actively
sought university-related information and regarded
social media as an important source of educational
information. Therefore, the challenge facing agro-
maritime higher education may lie less in information
availability and more in the ability to communicate
future career relevance and professional opportunities
in ways that resonate with younger generations.
This study further extends the TPB framework by
demonstrating that informational exposure, particularly
through digital channels, can shape the underlying
belief structures that influence intention formation.
While TPB traditionally emphasizes attitude,
subjective norm, and perceived behavioral control as

upstream factors that shape these cognitive evaluations.

Managerial Implications
From a strategic marketing perspective, the findings
emphasize that digital marketing communication
should be positioned as a core driver of student
acquisition strategies in higher education. Rather
than merely functioning as a promotional tool, digital
marketing communication acts as a behavior-shaping
mechanism that influences how students construct
their perceptions, evaluations, and intentions. Given
its dominant total effect, universities should reposition
digital communication as a central strategic function in
student recruitment rather than as a peripheral activity.
First, universities should strengthen career pathway
visibility by explicitly linking academic programs to
tangible career outcomes and professional trajectories.
Communication strategies should clearly demonstrate
how students’ progress from specific study programs
to future occupations, professional roles, and career
opportunities. The use of career pathway maps, alumni
career stories, industry partnerships, and real-world
applications can help students understand the long-
term value and relevance of agro-maritime education.
Second, considering the strong influence of subjective
norms, communication strategies should extend beyond
student-centered messaging to actively engage key
social influencers, particularly parents, teachers, and
school counselors. This can be implemented through
targeted informational campaigns, testimonial-based
narratives, and collaborative outreach programs with
schools, thereby reinforcing positive social support for
agro-maritime education.

engagement digital platforms, particularly Instagram
and TikTok, which serve as primary information

Recommendation
Several limitations should be considered when
interpreting these findings. First, the cross-sectional
design does not capture changes in students’ intentions
over time, limiting the ability to examine causal
dynamics in educational decision-making. Second, the
study focused exclusively on Grade XII science-track
students, who represent the primary target population for
agro-maritime higher education programs in Indonesia.
This sampling focus may limit the generalizability of the
findings to students from other academic backgrounds,
educational tracks or institutional contexts.
From a practical perspective, universities are encouraged

programs with future professional opportunities.

CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS
Conclusions
This study demonstrates that students’ intentions
toward agro-maritime higher education are shaped by
the interaction of digital marketing communication,
perceived career and employment prospects, and the
core constructs of the Theory of Planned Behavior.
Among the factors examined, digital marketing
communication emerged as the most influential
determinant of students’ intentions, both directly and
indirectly, through career-related perceptions and
attitudinal evaluation. In addition, social influence
remains an important consideration in students’
educational decision making.
From a theoretical perspective, this study extends the
Theory of Planned Behavior by incorporating digital
marketing communication and perceived career and
employment prospects into the intention formation
process. The findings demonstrate a communication-
driven mechanism in which digital marketing
communication shapes career-related perceptions,
which subsequently influence attitudes and educational
intentions. By integrating psychological, economic,
