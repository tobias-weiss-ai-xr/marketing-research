Abstract
Real estate developers and brokerages increasingly invest in digital marketing channels such
as social media, property listing websites, and online review platforms, yet converting digital
exposure into actual sales remains a persistent challenge in a mark et characterised by high
financial value, long decision cycles, and significant information asymmetry between buyers
and sellers. Although prior research has examined how digital tools and platforms influence
property purchase intention, the psychological mechanisms throu gh which digital marketing
stimuli translate into sales outcomes remain underexplored. Drawing on the Stimulus -
Organism-Response (SOR) framework and signaling theory, this paper develops a conceptual
model that explains how three digital marketing stimuli (Social Media Marketing Quality,
Website Quality, and Online Reputation and Reviews) influence Real Estate Sales
Performance. Buyer Trust is conceptualised  as the key psychological mechanism through
which these digital marketing stimuli are translated into measurable sales outcomes. Five
propositions are advanced, and a methodology for future empirical testing using partial least
squares structural equation modelling (PLS-SEM) is outlined. The framework offers testable
guidance for property developers, brokerages, and digital marketing teams seeking to
optimise their digital marketing investments. The propositions require future empirical
validation.

Literature Review
This section reviews digital marketing in real estate, the SOR framework, signaling theory, and
the role of trust in high-involvement property purchases.

Digital Marketing in Real Estate Sales
Digital marketing in real estate refers to the strategic use of digital channels and platforms to
promote property listings, communicate with prospective buyers, and facilitate the property
search and evaluation process. Property technology, often termed P ropTech, represents the
broader digital infrastructure through which the real estate industry is undergoing digital
change (Al-haimi et al., 2025). Within this infrastructure, three categories of digital marketing
stimuli are particularly prominent in property sales. First, developers and brokerages use
social media platforms to publish property-related content, interact with prospective buyers,
and build brand awareness (Ho et al., 2024; Kim & Ko, 2012; Prasad et al., 2026) . Second,
property listing websites and developer portals serve as the primary digital interface through
which buyers access listing information, compare options, and initiate enquiries, and
information systems research offers a basis for considering the q uality of these platforms
(DeLone & McLean, 2003). Third, online reviews and reputation signals generated by previous
buyers and third-party platforms provide social proof that influences prospective buyers’ trust
and purchase decisions (Cheung & Thadani, 2012; Filieri, 2015) . These three stimulus types
differ in their predominant sources of information (organisation -generated, platform -
mediated, and peer -generated), a distinction that becomes central to explaining how each
builds buyer trust through different informational pathways.

Two features distinguish the digital marketing environment in real estate from general digital
retail. First, the high financial value and long decision cycle of property purchases mean that
buyers may engage in extensive information search and evaluation across digital and offline
touchpoints (Lemon & Verhoef, 2016; Payne & Frow, 2005). Second, information asymmetry
between buyers and sellers is particularly acute in property markets, where buyers cannot
fully assess construction quality, neighbourhood conditions, or developer credibility from
digital information alone (Broxterman & Zhou, 2023; Gefen et al., 2003). These features mean
that the effectiveness of digital marketing in real estate depends not merely on reach and

Stimulus-Organism-Response Theory
The Stimulus-Organism-Response (SOR) framework was proposed by Mehrabian and Russell
(1974) in their seminal work on environmental psychology. The framework posits that
external environmental stimuli (S) influence an individual’s internal cognitive and affective
states (O), which in turn drive behavioural responses (R) in the form of approach or avoidance
behaviour. The SOR model has since become one of the most widely applied theoretical
frameworks in consumer behaviour and marketing research (Jacoby, 2002).

In marketing, the SOR framework explains how environmental cues shape consumers’ internal
responses and subsequent behaviour. Eroglu et al. (2003) examined online store atmospherics
and shopper responses. Ha and Lennon (2010) investigated pleasure and arousal in response
to online visual merchandising cues, while Kim and Lennon (2013) examined emotion and
perceived risk in relation to reputation, website quality, and purchase intention. These studies
illustrate that the organism component can encompass different psychological states. In short-
video commerce, Luo et al. (2025) identified consumer trust as a mediator between content
characteristics and purchase intention. In residential real estate, Prasad et al. (2026)
incorporated developer trust and perceived usefulness into an SOR -based account of
purchase intention. Building on this evidence, the present study proposes extending the
outcome of interest to real estate sales performance.

In this study, the SOR framework is applied to the real estate digital marketing context. The
three digital marketing stimuli (Social Media Marketing Quality, Website Quality, and Online
Reputation and Reviews) represent the stimulus variables (S). Buyer T rust represents the
organism variable (O), capturing the internal psychological state that buyers develop in
response to these digital stimuli. Real Estate Sales Performance represents the response
variable (R), reflecting the behavioural outcome of the buyer’s psychological processing. The
parsimony of using a single organism variable is deliberate: in high -involvement, high -risk
property purchases characterised by acute information asymmetry, trust is proposed as a
focal psychological me chanism through which digital marketing stimuli may influence
purchase behaviour (Gefen et al., 2003; McKnight et al., 2002).

Signaling Theory in Information-Asymmetric Markets
While SOR theory explains the overall process through which digital marketing stimuli shape
buyer psychology and drive behavioural responses, signaling theory provides the
complementary explanation of why these digital stimuli are able to build trust in the first
place. Signaling theory, originally proposed by Spence (1973) in the context of job market
economics, explains how one party (the signaler) communicates credible information about
unobservable qualities to another party (the receiver) through observable actions or
attributes. Connelly et al. (2011) provided a comprehensive review and assessment of
signaling theory in management research, identifying four core elements: the signaler, the
signal, the receiver, and the signaling environment.

In property markets, information asymmetry between developers and buyers is particularly
severe: buyers cannot directly observe construction quality, verify the accuracy of listing
information, or assess developer credibility before committing to a purchas e (Broxterman &
Zhou, 2023). In this environment, digital marketing activities function as quality signals that
developers send to prospective buyers. Social media marketing quality signals the developer’s
willingness to engage transparently and responsively with the market. Website quality signals
the developer’s organisational  competence and investment in professional infrastructure.
Online reputation and reviews may provide complementary third -party signals when buyers
perceive their sources as independent and credible (Cheung & Thadani, 2012; Connelly et al.,
2011).

The integration of signaling theory with the SOR framework provides a more complete
theoretical account of the proposed relationships. SOR explains the process: external stimuli
shape internal states that drive behaviour. Signaling theory explains the mechanism : in
information-asymmetric property markets, digital marketing activities function as quality
signals that reduce buyer uncertainty and build trust. Together, the two theories explain both
the psychological pathway (SOR) and the informational logic (signaling) through which digital
marketing may influence real estate sales performance.

Trust in High-Involvement Purchases
Trust is a central construct in understanding consumer behaviour in markets characterised by
information asymmetry and high perceived risk. McKnight et al. (2002) developed a
comprehensive trust model for e -commerce that identifies three dimensions of
trustworthiness: competence (the ability to fulfil promises), benevolence (genuine concern
for the buyer’s interest), and integrity (adherence to ethical principles). In property markets,
where buyers cannot fully verify listing accuracy, construction quality, or developer credibility
before purchase, trust in the developer and the digital platform serves as a critical
psychological gateway that determines whether buyers progress from information search to
serious enquiry and commitment (Gefen et al., 2003; Pavlou, 2003).

The importance of trust in high -involvement purchases is well established. Pavlou (2003)
demonstrated that consumer trust reduces perceived risk and increases willingness to
transact in electronic commerce. Gefen et al. (2003) showed that trust, integrated with the
Technology Acceptance Model, is associated with online purchase intention. In the property
context, Prasad et al. (2026) found that developer trust significantly mediates the relationship
between digital marketing and purchase intention, and Ho et al. (2024) confirmed the

Research Variables
This study examines five research variables: three stimulus variables (Social Media Marketing
Quality, Website Quality, and Online Reputation and Reviews), one organism variable (Buyer
Trust), and one response variable (Real Estate Sales Performance). Each  construct is defined
below with its meaning, its real estate context, and its main dimensions.

Social Media Marketing Quality
Social Media Marketing Quality refers to the overall quality of a developer’s marketing
activities on social media platforms, as perceived by prospective buyers. Kim and Ko (2012)
identified five dimensions of social media marketing activities: entertainment, interaction,
trendiness, customisation, and word of mouth. In the real estate context, Social Media
Marketing Quality encompasses the relevance and visual appeal of property -related posts,
the responsiveness of developer accounts to buyer enquiries, the currency of listing
information shared on social platforms, and the extent to which content is tailored to
individual buyer preferences (Ho et al., 2024; Prasad et al., 2026) . Its main dimensions are
entertainment, interaction, trendiness, customisation, and word of mouth (Kim & Ko, 2012).

Website Quality
Website Quality refers to the overall quality of property listing websites and developer portals
as evaluated by buyers across information, system, and service dimensions. The DeLone and
McLean (2003) Information Systems Success Model identifies three core quality dimensions:
information quality (the accuracy, completeness, and relevance of content), system quality
(the usability, reliability, and speed of the platform), and service quality (the respon siveness
and helpfulness of online support). In real estate, Website Quality shapes the buyer’s ability
to efficiently search listings, compare property options, access pricing information, and
initiate contact with sales representatives. Qalati  et al. (2021) found that trust partially
mediates the relationship between website quality and online purchase intention, with
perceived risk moderating the relationship between trust and purchase intention. Its main
dimensions are information quality, system quality, and service quality (DeLone & McLean,
2003).

Online Reputation and Reviews
The construct Online Reputation and Reviews refers to the electronic word -of-mouth
(eWOM) information that prospective buyers encounter about a developer, a project, or a
property through online review platforms, social media discussions, and third -party r ating
sites. Cheung and Thadani (2012) reviewed eWOM research and developed an integrative
model of its influence on consumer responses. Filieri (2015) further demonstrated that the
diagnosticity and helpfulness of online reviews significantly influence consumer adoption of
reviewer recommendations. In real estate, buyers frequently consult online reviews, forum
discussions, and social media comments to assess developer credibility, construction quality,

Buyer Trust
Buyer Trust refers to the buyer’s overall assessment of the developer’s trustworthiness across
three dimensions: competence (the developer’s ability to deliver the property as promised),
benevolence (the developer’s genuine concern for the buyer’s interest ), and integrity (the
developer’s adherence to ethical standards and honest communication). This
conceptualisation follows McKnight et al. (2002), whose trust model has been widely
validated in e -commerce and information systems research. In real estate, Buyer Trust
reflects the buyer’s confidence that the developer will deliver accurate listing information,
honour pricing commitments, maintain con struction quality, and provide reliable after -sales
service (Prasad et al., 2026) . Its main dimensions are competence trust, benevolence trust,
and integrity trust (McKnight et al., 2002).

Real Estate Sales Performance
Real Estate Sales Performance is the response variable of the framework. It is conceptualised
as a multidimensional sales outcome that includes the sales conversion rate, the sales speed
from enquiry to signed agreement (Anglin et al., 2003; Castelli et al., 2020) , and the
achievement of transaction value relative to the asking price (Anglin et al., 2003; Zahirovic -
Herbert et al., 2020) . This proposed conceptualisation draws on research concerning
marketing time and sale prices, while extending the framework to include sales conversion.
The definition and measurement of each outcome must be aligned with the unit of analysis.

Digital Marketing Stimuli and Buyer Trust
In high-involvement purchase contexts characterised by information asymmetry, buyers rely
heavily on digital cues to form trust judgements about sellers (Gefen et al., 2003; Pavlou,
2003). From a signaling theory perspective (Connelly et al., 2011), the three digital marketing
stimuli identified in this framework each function as distinct quality signals that reduce buyer
uncertainty through different mechanisms.

Social media marketing provides interactive and personalised content that allows prospective
buyers to observe developer behaviour , communication responsiveness, and community
feedback in real time. Repeated positive interactions on social media platforms may build
relational familiarity and signal the developer’s willingness to engage transparently with
prospective buyers, thereby s upporting trust (Karunasingha & Abeysekera, 2022; Kim & Ko,
2012). Prasad et al. (2026) found a positive relationship between their multidimensional
digital marketing construct and developer trust in the residential property context, and Ho et
al. (2024) demonstrated that entertainment, interaction, customisation, and word-of-mouth
dimensions of social media marketing significantly influence purchase intention in the
property industry. Therefore, this study proposes the following.

Proposition 1: Social Media Marketing Quality positively influences Buyer Trust.

Website quality signals organisational  competence and professionalism. Accurate and
complete listing information, an intuitive user interface, and responsive online support
convey the developer’s capability and reliability, and may contribute to the buyer’s trust
assessment. The information sy stems success model provides a conceptual basis for these
quality dimensions (DeLone & McLean, 2003). Qalati et al. (2021) demonstrated that website
quality significantly influences trust in online purchase contexts, while Gefen et al. (2003)
examined trust alongside technology acceptance in online shopping. Kim and Lennon (2013)

Buyer Trust and Real Estate Sales Performance
Trust is associated with willingness to transact in online settings (McKnight et al., 2002;
Pavlou, 2003) . Wang et al. (2022) also reported a positive relationship between trust and
purchase intention in social commerce. Extending this reasoning to property transactions, this
study proposes that buyer trust may encourage progression from enquiry to a signed
agreement and reduce hesitation during negotiation. These mechanisms could improve
conversion, shorten the sales cycle, and support transaction value achievement. The effects
on these actual sales outcomes remain propositions for empirical testing, rather than
established find ings of the cited e -commerce studies. Therefore, this study proposes the
following.

Proposition 4: Buyer Trust positively influences Real Estate Sales Performance.

Mediation Effect
The SOR framework provides a theoretical basis for examining how internal psychological
states transmit the effects of external stimuli on behavioural responses (Jacoby, 2002;
Mehrabian & Russell, 1974) . Signaling theory complements this account by explaining how
credible signals may reduce uncertainty in information -asymmetric markets (Connelly et al.,
2011). In this study, Buyer Trust is proposed as a mediator between the three digital
marketing stimuli and Real Estate Sales Performance. Evidence for trust mediation is available
in social media marketing and short -video contexts (Karunasingha & Abeysekera, 2022; Luo
et al., 2025) . Prasad et al. (2026) also reported partial mediation by developer trust in the
relationship between digital marketing and residential property purchase intention. These
findings motivate the proposed mediation pathway but do not establish that all effects on
sales performance operate through trust. Therefore, this study proposes the following.

Proposition 5: Buyer Trust mediates the relationship between digital marketing stimuli (Social
Media Marketing Quality, Website Quality, and Online Reputation and Reviews) and Real
Estate Sales Performance.

Methodology
Sample and Data Collection
This section describes the sample and data collection procedures for the proposed empirical
test of the framework. The proposed study will employ a quantitative, cross-sectional survey
design, which is an established approach for capturing buyer perception s across multiple
constructs at a single point in time and is widely employed in marketing and digital
engagement research (Rindfleisch et al., 2008). Because cross-sectional data cannot establish
causal direction, any estimated mediation effects will be interpreted as associations
consistent with the proposed framework, rather than as proof of causality. Procedural steps
will be taken to reduce common  method variance, a recognised risk when all constructs are
measured from the same respondent using the same instrument (Podsakoff et al., 2003) .
Ethical approval will be obtained from the relevant institutional review board prior to data
collection. The following paragraphs specify the target population, sampling strategy,
recruitment channels, questionnaire procedures, and minimum sample size.

The target population is individual residential property buyers who have completed a digital
property purchase journey within the previous twenty -four months. This recall window
reflects the nature of residential property purchase as a high -involvement decision with a
lengthy search and negotiation cycle (Broxterman & Zhou, 2023; Prasad et al., 2026), and the
proposed twenty -four-month boundary balances recruitment feasibility against the risk of
recall error; its suitability will be assessed during pre-testing (Podsakoff et al., 2003). The unit
of analysis is the individual buyer, because all stimulus and organism constructs are buyer -
reported perceptions.

The study will use purposive sampling to reach qualified respondents who possess direct and
recent experience of a digitally mediated property journey (Etikan et al., 2016). To be eligible,
respondents must be of legal contracting age, must have actively searched for residential
property using at least one digital channel, and must have progressed to at least the
negotiation or offer stage. Qualified buyers will be reached through property listing platforms,
developer customer databases with institutional consent, and property -focused online
communities. Distributing recruitment across multiple channels reduces the risk of source -
specific selection bias (Dillman et al., 2014).

Self-administered online questionnaires will be the primary instrument. Participation will be
voluntary and anonymous. The questionnaire will undergo cognitive pre -testing with a pilot
group of fifteen to twenty respondents to check wording clarity and ite m comprehension
before full deployment (Collins, 2003) . To reduce common method bias, the survey will
employ procedural safeguards including clear item wording, assurances of anonymity, and
psychological separation of predictor and outcome sections (Podsakoff et al., 2003). Attention
check items will be embedded to identify inattentive respondents (Meade & Craig, 2012).

The minimum sample size will be estimated using the inverse square root method, with
assumptions about the smallest path coefficient of interest and the desired significance level
and statistical power specified in advance (Kock & Hadaya, 2018). A target of at least 300 valid

Measures
Each construct will be measured reflectively (Hair & Alamer, 2022) by several survey items on
a seven-point Likert scale from strongly disagree to strongly agree. Where suitable validated
scales are available, items will be adapted and reworded for the property context; newly
developed items will require content validati on and pilot testing. Social Media Marketing
Quality will be measured with items adapted from Kim and Ko (2012); Website Quality with
items informed by DeLone and McLean’s (2003) quality dimensions; Online Reputation and
Reviews with items informed by Cheung and Thadani’s (2012) integrative framework; and
Buyer Trust with items adapted from McKnight et al. (2002). Real Estate Sales Performance
will be measured using items capturing sales conversion rate, sales speed, and transaction
value achievement, with operational definitions informed by research on marketing time and
sale prices (Anglin et al., 2003; Castelli et al., 2020; Zahirovic-Herbert et al., 2020).

For constructs specified reflectively, the psychometric properties will be assessed following
established criteria. Internal consistency reliability will be examined through Cronbach’s alpha
and composite reliability, with values of 0.70 or above treated as acceptable (Hair & Alamer,
2022; Nunnally, 1978) . Convergent validity will be examined through the average variance
extracted, with values of 0.50 or above treated as acceptable (Fornell & Larcker, 1981) .
Discriminant validity will be examined through the heterotrait-monotrait ratio of correlations,
with values below 0.85 indicating satisfactory discriminant validity (Henseler et al., 2015) .
These criteria will inform the evaluation of reflective measures before structural model
testing; any formative or objective measures will require assessment appropriate to their
specification.

Data Analysis
The collected data will be analysed with partial least squares structural equation modelling
(PLS-SEM), using SmartPLS (Ringle et al., 2014)  for the structural model and SPSS for
descriptive statistics. This method suits a predictive conceptual model with several related
constructs and a mediation path (Hair & Alamer, 2022). The analysis will follow two steps.

First, the measurement model will be examined through indicator reliability, composite
reliability, average variance extracted, and the heterotrait -monotrait ratio for discriminant
validity (Henseler et al., 2015) . Second, the structural model will be examined through path
coefficients and their significance from bootstrapping (Hair & Alamer, 2022), the coefficient
of determination, and effect sizes (Cohen, 1988) , in order to assess the five propositions.
Direct paths from each stimulus to performance will also be examined to distinguish partial
mediation from an indirect-only pattern. The specific indirect effects through Buyer Trust will
be assessed using the sp ecific indirect effect procedure in PLS -SEM with bootstrapped
confidence intervals (Hair & Alamer, 2022) . Harman’s single -factor test and the full
collinearity approach will be used as supplementary diagnostics for common method bias
(Kock, 2015; Podsakoff et al., 2003) ; neither will be treated as definitive evidence that such
bias is absent. These analytical steps provide a rigorous basis for evaluating both the
measurement quality and the structural relationships proposed in the framework.

Discussion
Theoretical Contributions
This paper makes several contributions to the literature. First, it builds on applications of the
Stimulus-Organism-Response framework in digital commerce and residential real estate by
focusing on sales performance as the proposed response variable. Secon d, it integrates
signaling theory with the SOR framework to provide a dual -theoretical account that explains
both the psychological pathway (SOR) and the informational logic (signaling) through which
digital marketing may influence sales performance. Third , it identifies three categories of
digital marketing stimuli (social media content, website quality, and online reputation) that
correspond to different signal sources (organisation-generated, platform-mediated, and user-
generated) and examines their effects on buyer trust. Fourth, the framework positions buyer
trust as a focal mediator whose contribution to sales performance can be tested alongside
possible direct effects. This pars imonious approach provides a focused theoretical account
that identifies a specific psychological mechanism through which digital marketing may relate
to sales outcomes. Fifth, the framework advances the real estate marketing literature by
proposing links between digital marketing constructs and multidimensional sales
performance outcomes rather than purchase intention alone, addressing a measurement gap
identified in prior property digitalisation research (Al-haimi et al., 2025; Ho et al., 2024; Prasad
et al., 2026).

Practical Implications
For practice, the framework suggests that real estate sales performance may depend partly
on how effectively digital marketing investments build buyer trust. Developers and
brokerages should recognise that social media content, website quality, and online reputation
management are not isolated marketing activities but interconnected quality signals that
collectively shape the same psychological outcome: buyer trust (Connelly et al., 2011). Social
media marketing should prioritise interactive and responsive communication to build
relational trust, rather than focusing solely on content volume (Ho et al., 2024; Kim & Ko,
2012). Property listing websites should ensure information accuracy, system reliability, and
responsive online support, as these quality dimensions may signal organisational competence
to prospective buyers (DeLone & McLean, 2003; Qalati et al., 2021) . Online reputation
management should actively encourage credible buyer reviews and address negative
feedback transparently, as user -generated content serves as powerful third -party validation
in a market defined by information asymmetry (Cheung & Thadani, 2012; Filieri, 2015). Most
importantly, the trust-centred framework suggests that all digital marketing activities should
be evaluated not by reach or engagement metrics alone, but by their contribution to building
buyer trust and, subject to empirical validation, sales conversion.

Limitations and Future Recommendations
This study has several limitations. First, as a conceptual paper, its propositions are drawn from
theory and existing literature and have not yet been tested empirically. Second, the
framework is developed for high -involvement residential property in digit ally mature
markets, which may limit its generalisation  to other property types, emerging markets, or
low-digital-adoption contexts. The proposed cross-sectional survey is also vulnerable to recall
error and common method bias, and cannot establish causal mediation. In addition, the
relationship between buyer -level perceptions and sales performance depends on a
measurement design that aligns outcomes with the unit of analysis.

These limitations open several directions for future research. First, the framework should be
tested empirically using the survey design outlined above. Second, longitudinal designs can
track how digital marketing stimuli shape trust across the long proper ty decision cycle,
offering stronger evidence of temporal sequence than cross-sectional data alone (Rindfleisch
et al., 2008). Third, the framework could be tested across different countries, city tiers, and
market segments, including commercial property, to examine cultural and market differences
in the trust-building process. Fourth, future work can introduce additional organism variables,
such as perceived value or perceived risk, to determine whether trust is a sufficient mediator
or whether other psychological states provide additional explanatory power. Fifth, future
research can examine moderating variables such as buyer digital literacy, property price tier,
and market maturity to identify boundary conditions under which the proposed relationships
are strengthened or weakened. Finally, future studies can compare the relative importance
of the three digital marketing stimuli in building trust, providing actionable guidance on how
developers should allocate their digital marketing budgets across social media, website
development, and reputation management.

Conclusion
This conceptual paper has developed a framework proposing how digital marketing stimuli
may shape real estate sales performance through the mediating role of buyer trust. Grounded
in the Stimulus-Organism-Response framework and signaling theory, it addresses a context in
which purchases carry high value, high risk, and significant information asymmetry, and in
which the psychological mechanisms linking digital marketing to sales outcomes have
received limited theoretical attention. The framework proposes that Social Media Marketing
Quality, Website Quality, and Online Reputation and Reviews influence Real Estate Sales
Performance through Buyer Trust, and it advances five propositions and a method for future
testing. These propositions await empirical validat ion, but the framework provides a
structured basis for future research and practice. The framework suggests that credible digital
signals may support buyer trust and progression towards a signed agreement, providing a
basis for testing how trust relates to actual sales outcomes
