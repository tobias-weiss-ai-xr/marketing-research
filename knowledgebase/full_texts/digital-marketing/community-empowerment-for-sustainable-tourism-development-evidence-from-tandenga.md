 This study examines how community empowerment supports
sustainable tourism development in Tandengan Tourism Village,
Indonesia. A qualitative case study employed purposive sampling, semi-
structured interviews, observations, and document analysis, with data
analyzed using the Miles–Huberman–Saldaña interactive model and
triangulation. Results reveal four interconnected dimensions of
empowerment: political, psychological, economic, and social.
Community participation through Pokdarwis strengthened local
involvement in tourism governance, while capacity-building enhanced
confidence and tourism competencies. Tourism-based entrepreneurship
expanded MSMEs, diversified household livelihoods, and added value to
agricultural products, while collective tourism activities reinforced social
cohesion and environmental stewardship. However, unequal
participation, limited digital marketing, service standardization, and
financing remain constraints. The study concludes that integrated
community empowerment strengthens commun ity-based tourism
sustainability, provided that institutional capacity, inclusive
participation, entrepreneurship support, and continuous stakeholder
collaboration are systematically reinforced through locally grounded
governance mechanisms.

Introduction
Tourism has increasingly evolved from a growth -oriented economic sector into a development
mechanism expected to generate social inclusion, cultural preservation, environmental conservation,
and community welfare (Bramwell & Lane, 2021; Rasoolimanesh et al., 2023). This transition has
encouraged more participatory approaches that position local communities as active actors rather than
passive beneficiaries of tourism development (Dolezal & Novelli, 2022; Su et al., 2024). Community
participation is particularly important in rural destinations because tourism resources are closely
embedded in local livelihoods, cultural practices, and natural environments (Lee & Jan, 2019; Vujko et

constitute empowerment. Participation concerns community involvement in tourism activities,
whereas empowerment requires communities to develop the capacity, authority, and control
necessary to influence decisions and obtain meaningful benefits from tourism. This distinction is
critical because participatory tourism may remain externally driven when local communities have
limited influence over resources, institutions, and benefit distribution.
Community-Based Tourism (CBT) provides an important framework for addressing this challenge
by emphasizing community participation, local ownership, decision-making authority, and equitable
benefit sharing (Giampiccoli & Mtapuri, 2020; Dolezal & Novelli, 2022). Rather than prioritizing visitor
growth alone, CBT seeks to improve livelihoods, strengthen community capacity, and protect
environmental resources by positioning residents as entrepreneurs, managers, and decision-makers
(Okazaki, 2008; Rasoolimanesh et al., 2023). These principles are particularly relevant to rural tourism,
where agricultural resources, landscapes, local traditions, and community experiences can be
transformed into locally controlled tourism products while maintaining rural identity and resilience
(Lane & Kastenholz, 2015; Jepson & Sharpley, 2015; Ruiz-Ballesteros, 2017; Saxena et al., 2021).
Nevertheless, rural tourism frequently faces limited managerial skills, weak institutional
arrangements, inadequate financing, and insufficient marketing capacity, which can preve nt
communities from exercising meaningful control over tourism development and capturing its benefits
(Giampiccoli & Mtapuri, 2020; Bhatta et al., 2022).
In Indonesia, tourism villages have become a prominent mechanism for translating CBT principles
into local development by integrating natural attractions, cultural resources, traditional economic
activities, and community lifestyles into tourism experiences (Nuryanti, 1993; Kemenparekraf, 2023).
Previous studies demonstrate that tourism villages can suppor t income diversification, MSME
development, employment, and community empowerment (Sidiq & Resnawaty, 2017; Putri et al.,
2022). However, the availability of tourism assets does not automatically ensure inclusive community
benefits because local capacity, institutional effectiveness, entrepreneurship, and control over tourism
resources determine who participates and who benefits. Tandengan Village in Eris District, Minahasa
Regency, provides a relevant setting for examining these issues because its agricultural landscapes,
natural environment, and community-based cultural resources offer potential for nature -based
tourism, educational tourism, and agritourism (BPS Minahasa, 2024). The significance of this context
lies not merely in its tourism potential but in understanding how local resources can be converted into
sustainable attractions through community-controlled development.
Community empowerment therefore provides a more appropriate analytical lens for examining the
transformation of tourism potential into locally meaningful development outcomes. Empowerment
refers to the process through which individuals and communities increase their capacity and control
over decisions and resources affecting their lives (Perkins & Zimmerman, 1995; Aghazamani & Hunt,
2024). In tourism, this process may involve psychological confidence, skills and competencies,
economic opportunities, collective organization, and greater influence over tourism governance
(Scheyvens, 1999; Su et al., 2024). Institutional capacity is particularly important because local
organizations mediate participation, coordinate collective action, and mobilize resources. In
Indonesian tourism villages, Tourism Awareness Groups (Pokdarwis) perform these functions by
connecting residents with tourism man agement and other stakeholders, while effective local
institutions can facilitate knowledge sharing and collaborative action (Ansell & Gash, 2008; Nastiti et
al., 2023). At the economic level, empowerment can enable residents to develop homestays, culinary
businesses, handicrafts, transportation services, agritourism, and value-added agricultural products,
thereby diversifying livelihoods and strengthening local economic circulation (Rasoolimanesh et al.,
2023; Sutawa, 2019).
Community empowerment also depends on the broader institutional environment in which local
tourism operates. Collaboration among government, academia, businesses, communities, and media
can provide complementary resources for capacity building, innovation, financing, infrastructure, and
destination promotion (Carayannis & Campbell, 2010; Noviana et al., 2025). The Pentahelix perspective
has consequently gained attention in Indonesian tourism development, with evidence suggesting that
cross-sector collaboration can strengthen tourism village sustainability and community welfare
(Nastiti et al., 2023; Noviana et al., 2025) . Nevertheless, stakeholder collaboration should be

Despite extensive research on CBT, community participation, and tour ism villages, important
analytical gaps remain. Existing studies have often examined participation, institutional governance,
entrepreneurship, or sustainability separately, leaving limited understanding of how political,
psychological, economic, and socia l empowerment interact with local institutions, MSME
development, and agricultural resource utilization within a single community-based tourism setting.
Empirical evidence from Tandengan Tourism Village is also limited, particularly regarding how
empowerment processes transform local resources into tourism opportunities while simultaneously
strengthening community capacity and sustainable destination management. This study therefore
aims to examine how community empowerment supports sustainable tourism deve lopment in
Tandengan Tourism Village by identifying its forms and strategies, analyzing its implications for
institutional capacity and local economic development, and assessing the challenges that constrain
inclusive and sustainable outcomes. By integrating multiple dimensions of empowerment within the
CBT context, the study seeks to provide a contextually grounded explanation of how community
empowerment contributes to sustainable tourism development and to inform locally appropriate
strategies for strengthening community-based destination governance.

Method
This study employed a qualitative descriptive case study design to examine how community
empowerment supports sustainable tourism development in Tandengan Tourism Village, Eris District,
Minahasa Regency, North Sulawesi, Indonesia. A case study design was selected because it enables an
in-depth investigation of empowerment processes within their institutional, social, economic, and
environmental contexts (Yin, 2018). Tandengan Tourism Village constituted the bounded case, while
community empowerment practices and their implications for tourism development served as the
primary unit of analysis. Informants were selected purposively based on their direct involvement in,
knowledge of, or experience with tourism development. They included the Village Head (Hukum Tua),
Pokdarwis leaders and members, tourism-related MSME owners, community and traditional leaders,
and tourists. Data collection continued until thematic saturation was reached, defined as the point at
which subsequent interviews produced no substantively new information relevant to the research
questions (Guest et al., 2020).
Data were collected through semi -structured interviews, participant and non -participant
observations, and document analysis to capture multiple perspectives on community empowerment
and tourism development. Interviews explored participation in tourism gove rnance, capacity
development, entrepreneurship, community relations, environmental practices, and perceived
opportunities and constraints. Observations focused on tourism attractions and facilities, community
participation, resident–visitor interactions, tourism-related economic activities, and environmental
management practices. Documentary evidence included village profiles, tourist visitation records,
tourism-related village regulations, Pokdarwis organizational documents, meeting minutes, and
photographic records. Evidence from these sources was systematically compared to identify
convergence and divergence across informants and methods, thereby reducing reliance on a single
source of information and strengthening the credibility of the case study findings (Yin, 2018).
Data were analyzed using the interactive framework of Miles, Huberman, and Saldaña (2014),
comprising data condensation, data display, and conclusion drawing and verification. Interview,
observational, and documentary data were coded and progressively organized into categories and
themes related to political, psychological, economic, and social empowerment, while allowing
additional themes to emerge from the data. The resulting themes were compared across participant
groups and data sources to identify recurring patterns, divergent perspectives, and relationships

Results and Discussions
Political Empowerment Through Community-Based Tourism Governance
The findings indicate that community empowerment in Tandengan Tourism Village is manifested
through active participation in tourism governance and decision-making processes. Local residents are
involved in planning, implementing, and evaluating tourism development programs through village
meetings and the Tourism Awareness Group (Pokdarwis). This institutional arrangement enables
community members to voice their aspirations and contribute to the formulation of tourism-related
policies and activities.
The establishment and active involvement of Pokdarwis have contributed to a gradual transition
from a top-down development approach toward a more participatory and community -centered
governance model. Community members are no longer positioned merely as beneficiaries of tourism
development programs but have increasingly become active actors in determining the direction and
priorities of tourism initiatives.
This finding reflects the concept of political empowerment proposed by Scheyvens (1999), which
emphasizes the ability of local communities to participate in and influence decisions affecting their
livelihoods and resources. Through active involvement in tourism governance, residents gain greater
control over local development processes and strengthen their bargaining position in r elation to
external stakeholders.
The findings are also consistent with the principles of Community-Based Tourism (CBT), which
emphasize local participation, community ownership, and decentralized decision-making (Murphy,
1985; Timothy, 2007). Nevertheless, the study found that participation tends to be dominated by
certain active community members, particularly Pokdarwis leaders and village officials. Therefore,
future empowerment efforts should promote broader participation among women, youth, and
economically disadvantaged households to ensure more inclusive tourism governance.
Psychological Empowerment Through Capacity Enhancement
The study reveals that tourism development has contributed significantly to psychological
empowerment among local residents. Community members reported increased confidence, stronger
self-esteem, and a greater sense of pride regarding the tourism potential of their village. These positive
changes were attributed to a variety of empowerment activities, including training in hospi tality
services, tour guiding, tourism management, environmental conservation, and digital marketing.
Capacity-building programs have enabled community members to recognize the economic and
cultural value of local resources that were previously underutilized. Residents increasingly perceive
themselves as capable of managing tourism activities and interacting with visitors. Such confidence is
particularly evident among youth and Pokdarwis members who actively participate in tourism
operations and promotion.
According to Scheyvens (1999), psychological empowerment occurs when community members
develop self-confidence and pride in their culture, traditions, and local identity. The findings of this
study support this argument, as local residents expressed a stronger appreciation for their cultural
heritage and natural environment after becoming involved in tourism activities.
Furthermore, the findings align with Zimmerman’s (2000) empowerment theory, which highlights
the importance of enhancing individuals' perce ptions of competence and control over their
environment. Community members who previously viewed tourism as an external activity now
perceive themselves as capable contributors to local tourism development. However, the study also
indicates that capacity-building initiatives remain largely project-based. Sustainable empowerment

Economic Empowerment Through Tourism-Based Entrepreneurship
One of the most visible outcomes of community empowerment in Tandengan Tourism Village is the
growth of local entrepreneurship and tourism-related micro, small, and medium-sized enterprises
(MSMEs). Tourism development has created alternative sources of income for local households through
culinary services, souvenir production, homestay businesses, agricultural product processing, visitor
transportation services, and tourism-related retail activities.
The findings indicate that a growing number of residents are transforming local agricultural
products such as coffee, cloves, nutmeg, and other commodities into value-added products that can be
marketed directly to tourists. This transformation has increased the economic value of local resources
while reducing dependence on traditional agricultural income.
From the perspective of community empowerment, this phenomenon represents economic
empowerment because local communities gain direct economic benefits from tourism development
(Scheyvens, 1999). Economic empowerment is reflected not only in increased household income but
also in improved opportunities for entrepreneurship and livelihood diversification.
These findings support Goodwin and Santilli’s (2009) argument that successful community-based
tourism should generate tangible economic benefits for local communities and encourage local
ownership of tourism-related enterprises. Tourism in Tandengan Village has demonstrated its
potential as a local economic driver capable of stimulating communit y entrepreneurship and
strengthening local economic resilience.
Despite these achievements, several economic challenges remain. Limited access to financial capital,
insufficient business management skills, lack of product standardization, and restricted market access
continue to constrain business expansion. Therefore, sustainable economic empowerment requires
greater support in the form of entrepreneurial mentoring, financial assistance, product development
training, and market facilitation programs.
Social Empowerment and Community Cohesion
The findings reveal that tourism development has generated important social benefits beyond
economic gains. Community participation in tourism activities has strengthened social cohesion,
enhanced cooperation among residents, and revitalized traditional values of mutual assistance (gotong
royong). The management of tourism attractions, community events, and environmental conservation
activities has encouraged collective action and increased interaction among different social groups
within the village. The involvement of community leaders, youth organizations, women's groups, and
village institutions in tourism-related activities has contributed to stronger community networks and
collaborative problem-solving mechanisms. These developments indicate that tourism has become a
platform for strengthening local social capital and collective identity.
According to Scheyvens (1999), social empowerment occurs when tourism development fosters
stronger social relationships, enhances community cohesion, and increases residents' capacity to
organize collectively. The findings clearly demonstrate these dynamics, as tourism has encouraged
greater collaboration among residents and reinforced a shared commitment to village development. In
addition, tourism development has increased residents' sense of belonging to the destination. As local
people become more actively involved in tourism activities, they increasingly perceive the village's
natural and cultural resources as collective assets that must be protected and maintained. This sense
of ownership represents an important foundation for the long-term sustainability of community-based
tourism initiatives.
However, social empowerment should not be taken for granted. If tourism benefits become
concentrated among specific groups, social inequalities may emerge and weaken community
solidarity. Therefore, equitable benefit distribution and inclusive participation mechanisms are
essential to sustaining social cohesion and community support for tourism development.

Sustainable Tourism Development and Environmental Stewardship
The study found that community empowerment has also generated positive environmental outcomes.
Increased community involvement in tourism management has contributed to greater environmental
awareness and a stronger commitment to preserving natural resources. Residents now recognize that
environmental quality plays a crucial role in maintaining the attractiveness and competitiveness of the
tourism destination.
Community members have initiated various environmental conservation activities, including waste
management programs, regular clean-up campaigns, maintenance of tourism facilities, and the
preservation of natural landscapes. These initiatives indicate an increas ing understanding that
environmental sustainability is directly linked to the long-term success of tourism activities. This
finding is consistent with sustainable tourism theory, which emphasizes the integration of economic,
social, and environmental objectives in tourism development (Weaver, 2006). Local participation in
environmental protection not only contributes to ecosystem conservation but also enhances
destination image and visitor satisfaction.
Furthermore, the findings suggest that environmental stewardship has become embedded within
community empowerment processes. Tourism has motivated local residents to actively protect the
natural resources upon which tourism development depends. Consequently, community
empowerment can be viewed as a mechanism for promoting both environmental conservation and
sustainable destination management.
Nevertheless, environmental sustainability remains vulnerable to increasing visitor numbers and
infrastructure development. Therefore, tourism growth must be accompanied by effective
environmental management strategies, carrying -capacity considerations, and continuous
environmental education for residents and visitors.
Toward an Integrated Community Empowerment Model for Sustainable Tourism Development
Although the findings demonstrate significant progress in community empowerment, several
challenges continue to hinder the sustainable development of Tandengan Tourism Village. The study
identified three major constraints: limited digital marketing capabilities, insufficient service
standardization, and restricted access to business financing. Digital marketing remains a challenge
because many tourism actors still lack advanced skills in content creation, online promotion, and
destination branding. Similarly, service quality standards have not yet been fully institutionalized,
creating variations in visitor experiences. Moreover, financial constraints continue to limit the growth
and competitiveness of local MSMEs.
These challenges indicate that community empowerment should be understood as a continuous
process rather than a one-time intervention. Sustainable tourism development requires long-term
investments in human capital, institutional strengthening, entrepreneurship development, and
stakeholder collaboration. Based on the findings, this study proposes an Integrated Community
Empowerment Model that combines four dimensions of empowerment: political empowerment,
psychological empowerment, economic empowerment, and social empowerment. These dimensions
are mutually reinforcing and collectively contribute to the sustainability of tourism development.
To operationalize this model, a Pentahelix collaboration framework involving government agencies,
higher education institutions, private sector actors, local communities, and media organizations is
recommended. Such collaboration can support capacity building, innovation, destination promotion,
access to financing, and institutional strengthening. Through this integrated approach, Tandengan
Tourism Village can enhance its tourism competitiveness while maintaining social inclusiveness,
environmental sustainability, and community ownership of tourism resources.

Conclusions
This study concludes that community empowerment supports sustainable tourism development in
Tandengan Tourism Village through interconnected political, psychological, economic, and social
dimensions. Community participation in tourism governance, particularly through Pokdarwis, has
strengthened local involvement in decision-making and institutional capacity, while capacity-building
