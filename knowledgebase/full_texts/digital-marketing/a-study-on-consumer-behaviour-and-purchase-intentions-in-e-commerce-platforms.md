Abstract
The rapid growth of electronic commerce has significantly transformed consumer purchasing
behaviour and decision -making processes. E -commerce platforms provide consumers with
convenient access to a wide range of products, competitive prices, personalized
recommendations, and multiple payment options. However, consumer purchase intentions are
influenced by several psychological, technological, economic, and social factors. The present
study examines the major determinants of consumer behaviour and purchase i ntentions in e-
commerce platforms, with particular emphasis on perceived usefulness, ease of use, trust,
product quality, price sensitivity, customer reviews, website usability, and perceived risk. The
study proposes a quantitative research framework using  a structured questionnaire
administered to 100 online consumers selected through convenience sampling. Descriptive
statistics, correlation analysis, and multiple regression analysis are proposed to examine
relationships between consumer perceptions and pu rchase intentions. The conceptual
framework draws upon the Technology Acceptance Model and the Theory of Planned
Behaviour to explain how consumer attitudes and platform characteristics influence purchasing
decisions. The study also considers the importanc e of digital marketing, secure payment
systems, delivery services, and customer satisfaction in encouraging repeat purchases. The
proposed findings and analytical framework may help e -commerce businesses understand
consumer expectations and develop effecti ve strategies for improving customer engagement,
satisfaction, and purchase intentions. As primary survey data have not been supplied, the results
section presents an illustrative analytical framework rather than verified empirical findings.

1. Introduction
Electronic commerce has emerged as an important component of the global digital economy,
transforming traditional retail operations and consumer purchasing practices. The widespread
availability of smartphones, affordable internet connectivity, digital pay ment systems, and
online shopping applications has enabled consumers to purchase products and services without
visiting physical stores. E -commerce platforms such as Amazon, Flipkart, and Meesho  have
contributed to changing shopping habits by offering product variety, convenience, promotional
discounts, and home delivery services.

Consumer behaviour refers to the processes through which individuals identify their needs,
search for information, evaluate alternatives, make purchasing decisions, and assess their
experiences after consumption. In online environments, these processes are  influenced by
digital interactions, platform design, product information, customer reviews, advertisements,
and the perceived reliability of online sellers. Unlike traditional shopping, e -commerce
transactions frequently involve limited physical interacti on with products, making trust and
information quality important determinants of purchase decisions.

Purchase intention represents the likelihood or willingness of a consumer to purchase a product
or service in the future. It is an important indicator of potential consumer behaviour, although
intention does not always translate into an actual transaction. Factors such as perceived product
value, payment security, delivery reliability, promotional offers, and customer satisfaction can
strengthen or weaken purchase intentions.

Understanding consumer behaviour in e -commerce is essential for businesses seeking to
improve customer acquisition, conversion rates, and long-term retention. This study therefore
focuses on identifying and examining the major factors influencing consumer purchase
intentions in e-commerce platforms and proposes an analytical framework for evaluating their
relationships.

1.1 Statement of the Problem
Despite the increasing popularity of online shopping, consumers continue to experience
concerns regarding product authenticity, personal data protection, payment security, delivery
delays, misleading advertisements, and complicated return procedures. These  concerns can
influence consumer confidence and reduce the likelihood of completing online purchases.
E-commerce businesses must understand why consumers prefer certain platforms, how they
evaluate products, and which factors motivate them to complete or abandon purchases. The
present study addresses the need to examine the relationship between consumer pe rceptions,
online shopping experiences, and purchase intentions.

1.2 Objectives of the Study
1. To examine consumer behaviour and shopping preferences on e-commerce platforms.
2. To identify the major factors influencing online purchase intentions.
3. To analyse the influence of consumer trust, perceived usefulness, price, and customer
reviews on purchase intention.
4. To study the relationship between perceived risk and online purchasing decisions.
5. To examine the importance of customer satisfaction and shopping convenience in
encouraging repeat purchases.
6. To suggest strategies for improving consumer confidence and purchasing experiences
on e-commerce platforms.

1.3 Research Hypotheses
The following hypotheses are proposed:
• H1: Perceived usefulness has a significant positive influence on consumer purchase
intention.
• H2: Consumer trust has a significant positive influence on online purchase intention.
• H3: Price attractiveness has a significant positive influence on consumer purchase
intention.
• H4: Online customer reviews have a significant positive influence on consumer
purchase intention.

2. Review of Literature
Davis (1989) introduced the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), explaining that perceived
usefulness and perceived ease of use are important determinants of technology acceptance.
This model provides a theoretical foundation for understanding why consumers adopt online
shopping platforms. When consumers perceive an e -commerce website as useful and easy to
navigate, their willingness to use the platform may increase.
Ajzen (1991) developed the Theory of Planned Behaviour (TPB), which explains behavioural
intentions through attitudes, subjective norms, and perceived behavioural control. In the
context of online shopping, consumer attitudes toward digital transactions, recomm endations
from friends and family, and perceived control over purchasing activities may influence
purchase intentions.
Gefen, Karahanna, and Straub (2003) examined the importance of trust in online shopping and
demonstrated how trust contributes to consumer acceptance of e -commerce. Their research
highlights the role of trust alongside technology acceptance factors in understanding online
purchasing behaviour.
Pavlou (2003) developed an integrated framework connecting trust, perceived risk, and
technology acceptance in electronic commerce. The study emphasized that consumers consider
uncertainty and potential risks when making online purchasing decisions. Secure trans actions
and trustworthy platforms can therefore contribute to stronger purchase intentions.
Cheung, Lee, and Rabjohn  (2008) investigated the impact of electronic word -of-mouth on
consumer purchasing decisions. Their work explains how online opinions and
recommendations influence consumers' acceptance of product information and their decision -
making processes.
Kim, Ferrin, and Rao (2008) examined the relationships among trust, perceived risk, perceived
benefit, and consumer purchasing decisions in electronic commerce. Their findings support the
importance of both positive expectations and risk considerations in online consumer behaviour.

3. Research Methodology
3.1 Research Design
The study adopts a descriptive and analytical research design. A quantitative survey approach
is proposed to collect information regarding consumer shopping preferences, online purchasing
experiences, and factors influencing purchase intentions. The resear ch combines descriptive
analysis of consumer characteristics with statistical examination of relationships among
selected variables.

3.2 Population and Sample
The target population comprises consumers aged 18 years and above who have experience
purchasing products or services through e-commerce platforms. A sample of 100 respondents
is proposed using convenience sampling. Participants may include students, emplo yees,
business professionals, and other consumers who regularly use online shopping applications.

3.3 Sources of Data
Primary data are proposed to be collected through a structured questionnaire containing
demographic questions, online shopping behaviour questions, and Likert -scale statements.
Secondary data are drawn from published research articles, academic books, and relevant
literature on e-commerce and consumer behaviour.

3.4 Research Instrument
The questionnaire will use a five -point Likert scale, ranging from 1 (Strongly Disagree) to 5
(Strongly Agree), to measure consumer perceptions. Major constructs include perceived
usefulness, trust, price attractiveness, customer reviews, perceived risk, customer satisfaction,
and purchase intention.

5. Consumer Purchase Decision-Making Process
Consumer decision-making in e -commerce generally involves need recognition, information
searching, evaluation of alternatives, purchase intention, transaction completion, and post -
purchase evaluation.

During the information-search stage, consumers examine product descriptions, specifications,
customer ratings, seller information, and delivery conditions. They subsequently compare
available alternatives based on price, quality, convenience, and perceived reliability.
Purchase intention develops when a consumer identifies a suitable product and considers
buying it. However, the actual purchase may be influenced by additional factors such as
unexpected delivery charges, limited payment options, or concerns regarding prod uct
authenticity. Following the transaction, satisfaction with product quality, delivery, and
customer service may influence future purchasing behaviour.

6.3 Consumer Trust and Purchase Intention
Consumer trust is an important consideration in e -commerce because buyers often make
payments before physically inspecting products. Trust may develop through secure payment
systems, accurate product descriptions, reliable sellers, transparent return polic ies, and
consistent service delivery.
A consumer who believes that an e -commerce platform protects personal information and
fulfils purchasing commitments may demonstrate stronger purchase intention. Conversely,
negative experiences involving defective products, misleading descriptions, or ref und
difficulties may reduce consumer confidence. The proposed study will test whether trust is
positively associated with purchase intention.

6.4 Price Attractiveness and Purchase Decisions
Price attractiveness can influence consumers who compare products across multiple online
platforms. Promotional discounts, seasonal sales, cashback offers, and price-comparison tools
may encourage consumers to consider online purchases.
However, consumers do not necessarily select the lowest -priced product. They may evaluate
overall value by considering product quality, delivery charges, warranty coverage, seller
reputation, and return conditions. The study therefore treats price attractiveness as a perceived-
value factor rather than simply the lowest available price.
6.5 Customer Reviews and Online Purchasing
Online reviews provide consumers with information about product quality, usability,
reliability, and previous purchasing experiences. Positive reviews may strengthen consumer
confidence, whereas negative reviews may raise concerns about product suitability  or seller
credibility.
Review credibility is particularly important because consumers may encounter misleading or
manipulated ratings. Detailed, verified, and balanced reviews can help consumers make
informed decisions. The study proposes to examine whether the perceived usefuln ess of
customer reviews is positively associated with purchase intention.
6.6 Perceived Risk and Purchase Intention
Perceived risk refers to the consumer's expectation of possible negative outcomes associated
with online purchasing. Such risks may include financial loss, privacy violations, incorrect
products, delayed deliveries, and difficulties obtaining refunds.
The study hypothesizes a negative relationship between perceived risk and purchase intention.
E-commerce businesses may reduce consumer concerns through payment protection,
transparent product information, secure authentication, reliable logistics, and res ponsive
customer support.

6.8 Discussion
The theoretical framework suggests that online purchasing behaviour involves a combination
of technological convenience, economic value, social information, and perceived security.
Technology Acceptance Model constructs help explain the usefulness and acce ssibility of
shopping platforms, while trust and risk theories provide insight into consumer uncertainty.
Consumer purchase intentions may also depend on the quality of the overall shopping
experience. Clear product information, reliable delivery, responsive customer service, and
transparent refund procedures can contribute to consumer satisfaction. The relative importance
of these factors, however, should be established through empirical analysis rather than
assumed.
7. Major Findings and Expected Outcomes
Based on the literature review and proposed research framework, the study identifies the
following expected relationships, subject to empirical validation:
1. Shopping convenience and perceived usefulness are expected to be positively
associated with purchase intention.
2. Consumer trust is expected to play an important role in encouraging online purchases.
3. Price attractiveness and promotional benefits may influence consumers' evaluations of
e-commerce alternatives.
4. Customer reviews may contribute to product evaluation and purchasing confidence.
5. Perceived financial, privacy, and product -related risks may discourage online
purchases.
6. Positive post-purchase experiences may contribute to customer satisfaction and repeat
purchase intention.
These statements represent theoretically supported expectations and should not be presented as
confirmed findings until primary data have been collected and analysed.
8. Suggestions and Managerial Implications
E-commerce platforms should focus on improving the overall consumer shopping experience
by providing accurate product information, transparent pricing, secure payment methods, and
reliable delivery services. Website and mobile application interfaces should  be designed to
simplify product searches, comparisons, payment processing, and order tracking. These
improvements may reduce consumer effort and strengthen perceived usefulness.
Businesses should also establish effective customer relationship management practices.
Verified customer reviews, responsive support services, clear return policies, and timely refund
processing may help build consumer confidence. Personalized recommendati ons and
promotional offers should be relevant, transparent, and respectful of consumer privacy.
Marketing strategies should be developed with consideration for differences in consumer
preferences, purchasing frequency, and price sensitivity. Consumer feedback and platform

9. Limitations of the Study
The proposed study has several limitations. First, the sample size of 100 respondents may
restrict the generalizability of findings. Second, convenience sampling may introduce selection
bias because participants are not selected randomly from the target po pulation. Third,
questionnaire-based responses may be affected by social desirability, recall errors, and
differences between stated purchase intentions and actual behaviour.
Furthermore, the proposed cross-sectional design captures consumer perceptions at a particular
point in time and does not establish causal relationships. Consumer preferences may also differ
according to product category, income, location, and previous sho pping experiences. Future
research could use larger probability -based samples, longitudinal data, and actual transaction
records to improve the reliability and applicability of findings.
10. Conclusion
E-commerce platforms have transformed consumer purchasing practices by providing
convenient access to products, competitive pricing, digital payments, and personalized
shopping experiences. Understanding the factors that influence consumer behaviour and
purchase intentions is important for businesses seeking to improve customer engagement and
long-term relationships.
The present study proposes an integrated framework incorporating perceived usefulness,
consumer trust, price attractiveness, customer reviews, and perceived risk as major
determinants of online purchase intention. It also recognizes the importance of custo mer
satisfaction in encouraging repeat purchases. The framework draws on established consumer
behaviour and technology acceptance theories and offers a structured basis for quantitative
analysis.
The study concludes at the conceptual level that consumer purchasing decisions in e-commerce
should be understood as the outcome of multiple interacting perceptions rather than a single
factor. Empirical testing with actual survey responses is necessary to determine the magnitude
and statistical significance of these relationships. The proposed research may provide useful
insights for e-commerce businesses, digital marketers, researchers, and policymakers interested
in improving the effectiveness and reliability of online shopping environments.
