ABSTRACT
This review examines digital marketing tools used as
commercialization strategies by microenterprises in
Ecuador’s commercial sector. The problem emerges in a
context of expanding connectivity and e -commerce,
alongside persistent gaps in skills, planning, investment, and
performance measurement. The objective was to interpret
the available evidence in order to identify the tools with
the greatest commercial usefulness, the conditions that
support their implementation, and the limitations that
constrain their outcomes. A systematized documentary
review was conducted following PRISMA 2020 guidance
and drawing on academic and institutional sources
published mainly between 2018 and 2025. Information was
organized through a bibliographic matrix and thematic
categories concerning digitalization, competitiveness,
business adaptation, e-commerce, and the performance of
small economic units. Results indicate that social media,
WhatsApp Business, websites, marketplaces, content
marketing, search engine optimization, ta rgeted
advertising, email, analytics, and mobile marketing work
more effectively as an integrated system than as isolated
actions. Ecuadorian evidence associates digitalization with
greater visibility, business continuity, and sales
opportunities; nevertheless, it also reveals uneven
adoption, insufficient training, and decisions weakly

escenario de mayor conectividad y crecimiento del

INTRODUCTION
Economic and technological transformations have altered traditional forms of
negotiation and marketing. Mobile connectivity, social media platforms, payment
systems, and delivery services have changed the way consumers search for, compare,
purchase, and evaluate goods. In this context, digitalization has evolved from being
merely a promotional tool to playing a role in the entire relationship with the market.
ECLAC (2022) argues that Latin America’s digital development requires the integration
of infrastructure, capabilities, and innovation, while the World Bank (2024) warns that
its benefits continue to be distributed unevenly. Therefore, access to technology is a
necessary—but not sufficient—condition for improving productivity and commercial
inclusion.
This transformation takes on special relevance for microenterprises, which operate with
limited capital, staff, and time. Digital channels can expand reach with investments
proportional to their scale, strengthen customer interaction, and generate information
to inform decision-making. At the same time, they introduce new demands: timely
responses, public reputation, constant content production, payment security, and
logistical coordination. Bradač Hojnik and Huđek  (2023) explain that the
characteristics, resources, and competencies of small businesses determine technology
adoption. Consequently, digital marketing must be understood as an organizational
capability and not as a mere collection of applications.
In Ecuador, the pandemic accelerated the adoption of social media, messaging apps, and
online sales. Rodríguez et al. (2020) note that e-commerce became an alternative for
sustaining the operations of MSMEs, while Sampedro et al. (2021) show that social media
platforms and e-commerce applications facilitated business continuity during lockdowns.
However, progress has been uneven. Viteri-Rade et al. (2022), in a study of family-run
microenterprises in Guayaquil, identified a lack of awareness regarding the strategic
value of digital marketing, although they also noted a willingness to adopt it. The tension
between technological availability and effective utilization constitutes the central issue of
this review.
The guiding question of this study is: What are the main digital marketing tools that can
serve as marketing strategies for microenterprises in Ecuador’s retail sector, and what
conditions determine their effectiveness? The objective is to integrate theore tical

MATERIALS Y METHODS
A systematic literature review with descriptive and interpretive scope was conducted.
This design allows for the identification, organization, evaluation, and synthesis of
knowledge derived from previous research through explicit and reproducible decisions.
Snyder (2019) notes that a literature review constitutes a research methodology when
the search, selection, and analysis serve a defined purpose. To enhance transparency,
the PRISMA 2020 guidelines were followed, particularly regarding the definition of
sources, eligibility criteria, data screening, and presentation of the process (Page et al.,
2021).
The review combined scientific articles on digital marketing, e-commerce, and small
businesses with institutional reports necessary to contextualize connectivity, digital
transformation, and economic performance. The synthesis was qualitative because the
documents featured different designs, samples, indicators, and regions; therefore, it was
not appropriate to integrate their results as if they were equivalent measures. The
analysis sought convergences, differences, conditions of application, and gaps, whi le
exercising caution regarding unproven causal relationships.
The search focused on Google Scholar, SciELO, Redalyc, and academic or institutional
documents included in the original manuscript. Combinations of the terms “digital
marketing,” “digital commerce,” “e-commerce,” “social media,” “microenterprises,”
“SMEs,” “performance,” “Ecuador,” “digital marketing,” “e-commerce,” “SMEs,” and
“business performance” were used in both Spanish and English. The primary time frame
was 2018–2025, although directly relevant institutional and methodological documents
were retained. The review’s cutoff date was 2025, in accordance with the analyzed
bibliographic corpus.
Publications with full-text versions in Spanish or English were included if they addressed
at least one of the following relationships: digital tools and marketing; digitization and
performance; e-commerce and small businesses; social media and business adaptation;
or the Ecuadorian digital context. Qualitative, quantitative, and mixed-methods studies,
as well as reviews and reports from organizations, were accepted. Duplicates, works
unrelated to business outcomes, and documents whose information did not allow for
the identification of authorship, date, or thematic contribution were excluded.
A matrix was designed to include authorship, year, title, source, context, method, tools
analyzed, main findings, and limitations. The review was organized into ten categories:

RESULTS
Innovative alternatives integrate social commerce, digital catalogs, instant messaging,
marketplaces, electronic payments, and coordinated deliveries. Social commerce bridges
the gap between discovery and purchase: consumers view a product, check availability,
negotiate terms, and confirm their order all within the same ecosystem. Perdigón Llanes
(2020), while studying Cuban cooperatives, demonstrates that a strategy based on
Facebook can strengthen market relationships, innovation, and competitiveness. His
transferable insight lies in designing processes, assigning responsibilities, and creating
content, rather than limiting the strategy to simply posting offers.
For Ecuadorian microenterprises, WhatsApp Business is valuable due to its low barrier
to entry and because it integrates a product catalog, quick replies, tags, location, and
tracking. Instagram and Facebook facilitate discovery and social proof; TikTok an d
YouTube expand audiovisual demonstrations; and marketplaces drive traffic and build
transactional trust. Rodríguez et al. (2020) view e-commerce as an alternative for
streamlining operations and expanding markets, while Ballerini et al. (2023) explain that
performance improves when there is a genuine commitment to the channel and the
platform’s features become operational capabilities. Innovation, therefore, does not
consist of accumulating apps, but rather of reducing the difficulty customers face in
learning about, comparing, paying for, and receiving products.
Digitalization expands the potential market, reduces information costs, and makes part
of the consumer response observable. Loyola et al. (2024), based on an analysis of
companies in Azogues, link digitalization to business recovery and profitability. Baque
Cantos et al. (2021) also identify the contributions of digital marketing to the commercial
development of microenterprises in Manta. Overall, national evidence suggests a positive
association between digital capabilities, operational continuity, and bus iness

performance, although the study designs reviewed do not allow for attributing every
change in sales to a specific platform.
The impact is influenced by connectivity, logistics, trust in payment systems, content
quality, and response speed. The World Bank (2024) argues that digital progress creates
opportunities for productivity and inclusion but warns of gaps in connectivity, data, and
capabilities; the ITU (2023) confirms that internet access continues to grow unevenly.
The study by the Ecuadorian Chamber of E-Commerce and Espíritu Santo University
(CECE and UEES, 2025) presents e-commerce as an ecosystem where devices, financial
services, supply, and logistics converge. This perspective avoids a common pitfall:
increasing promotions without sufficient inventory or delivery capacity can harm the
customer experience and a company’s reputation.
Adaptation requires a shift from short-term responses to planned management. During
the pandemic, many companies incorporated online networks and sales to sustain
operations; they then had to transform that reaction into a sustainable competitive
advantage. Villalba (2023) describes the evolution from product -centric marketing
toward approaches focused on the consumer, experience, data, and personalization.
Current trends include omnichannel marketing, conversational commerce, short-form
video, accessible automation, and analytics; however, their adoption must align with the
needs of the audience and the business’s actual capabilities.
The OECD (2023) notes that SMEs need managerial capabilities, skills, and support
networks to navigate the digital transition. Castro Medina (2025) identifies Facebook,
Instagram, and WhatsApp as among the platforms most widely used by Latin American
SMEs, while also pointing out barriers related to training and resources. A reasonable
approach begins by standardizing brand identity, product catalog, pricing, and customer
responses; continues with tracking inquiries and sales; and only then incorporates
advertising, automation, or artificial intelligence. This gradual progression allows
businesses to assess costs, correct errors, and ensure that the promised online
experience aligns with actual availability and service.
Digital marketing strengthens competitiveness when it helps differentiate the offering,
understand the customer, and measure performance. Núñez and Miranda (2020) present
it as a strategic support linked to the organization’s overall objectives, not as a separate
activity. García et al. (2024), in their review of Ibero-American SMEs, observe uneven
adoption and a recurring relationship with sales management. Ukshini et al. (2025) find
that digital marketing has a positive effect on the performance of SMEs, although the
magnitude of this effect varies by sector and implementation conditions.
For a commercial microenterprise, competing does not mean matching the budget of a
large chain. The advantage can come from specialization, proximity, visible availability,
rapid response, and reliable delivery. Reach and engagement are initial indicators, but
they must be complemented by qualified leads, conversion, repeat purchases, margin,
and customer acquisition cost. This perspective prevents us from considering a campaign
successful merely because it accumulates followers. Competitiveness is sustained when

Microentrepreneurs need to prioritize because they have limited resources. Viteri et al.
(2018) describe social media, content, and digital media as relevant techniques, but their
value depends on consistent application. Caraballo et al. (2023) emphasize that adoption
requires skills, investment, and learning. An up-to-date catalog, organized responses,
verifiable information, and clear payment methods can generate more value than a
complex campaign that fails to hold the customer’s attention. After streamlining the
basic process, the business can expand its advertising, customer base, or its own store.
Strategic value also depends on human buy-in. Training should include content creation,
digital services, data protection, order tracking, and interpreting metrics. Acosta and
Rodríguez (2019) highlight the contribution of websites, social media, search engines,
advertising, and email to business performance. However, a tool only becomes an asset
when it addresses a need and fosters learning. Therefore, digital literacy constitutes the
intangible infrastructure needed to transform free or low-cost platforms into sustainable
commercial capabilities.
The Ecuadorian retail sector comprises neighborhood stores, family-run businesses,
specialized distributors, and chains with widely varying capabilities. The Central Bank of
Ecuador (BCE, 2025) highlights the significance of commercial activities within th e
national accounts and their sensitivity to fluctuations in consumption and production.
Zambrano et al. (2024) observe a recovery in the sector’s sales following the 2020
decline, but note that microenterprises continue to face financial constraints. These
differences in size, geographic scope, and formalization status influence the ability to
adopt technology and compete.
Digital commerce has shifted from simple informational ads toward processes that
integrate promotion, customer interaction, payment, delivery, and after-sales service.
López and Rivera (2020) describe how Ecuadorian entrepreneurs adapted during the
emergency, and Sampedro et al. (2021) highlight the role of online marketplaces and
shopping apps. Connectivity provides a favorable foundation but does not guarantee a
satisfactory transaction. Businesses need to align published information with inventory,
communicate clear terms, protect data, and resolve changes or complaints. Perdigón et
al. (2018) specifically highlight security, consumer beh avior, and search engine
optimization as key dimensions of a comprehensive strategy.
The national evolution can be divided into three stages. The first stage involved a basic
online presence through websites, social media profiles, and ads that transferred
traditional communication to the virtual environment. The second stage incorporated
interaction, messaging, and social commerce, all of which accelerated during the
pandemic. The third stage integrates payments, logistics, automation, analytics, and an
omnichannel experience. Molina et al. (2023) place mobile marketing within the
communication practices of Industry 4.0, an approach consistent with the central role
of the smartphone in business-to-consumer interactions.

Family-owned microenterprises are characterized by centralized decision-making, a
blurring of personal and business finances, close customer relationships, and scarce
resources. Viteri et al. (2022) found that a lack of knowledge limits the implementation
of digital marketing in Guayaquil, despite an interest in attracting customers and
improving performance. This finding suggests avoiding disproportionate
recommendations. The first level should ensure a complete business profile, a catalog
with prices, consistent photographs, delivery policies, hours of operation, frequently
asked questions, and basic order tracking.
A second level incorporates targeted campaigns, a content calendar, loyalty messaging,
and a dashboard of metrics. A third stage can integrate proprietary e -commerce,
automation, and predictive models, provided that volume and capacity justify it. Saltos
et al. (2023) propose coordinating email and CRM, websites, social media, inbound
marketing, SEO, mobile devices, and paid advertising. This sequence reduces waste and
prevents the increase in inquiries from exceeding service capacity. Zambrano et al.
(2024) report a positive relationship between marketing spending and sales, but caution
that investment remains low and its impact is still in its early stages; therefore, the budget
must be accompanied by planning and expertise.
Social media and social commerce. Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube facilitate
reach, segmentation, product demonstrations, and social proof. Each platform should

serve a specific function and lead to a concrete action. Castro Medina (2025) confirms
the regional prominence of Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp. Reposting the same
content across all platforms without adapting it to the audience and format dilutes the
message.
WhatsApp Business and conversational marketing. Messaging converts interest into a
sale through catalogs, direct customer service, quick responses, and follow -up. Its
strength is proximity; its risk, improvisation. It is advisable to define response times, tags
by stage, consent for communications, and flexible scripts. In Ecuador, it functions as a
lightweight commercial infrastructure that connects networks, payment, and delivery.
E-commerce, marketplaces, and digital payments. A company -owned store offers
control over the customer experience and data; a marketplace brings traffic and trust,
though it charges commissions and limits direct interaction. Ballerini et al. (2023) explain
that the platform’s capabilities improve performance when the company learns to use
them. The choice depends on volume, margin, and logistical capacity.
Content and video marketing. Photos, demonstrations, comparisons, testimonials, and
tutorials reduce uncertainty and communicate value. Viteri et al. (2018) consider content
a core technique. Its effectiveness increases when it answers real questions and links to
available products. A balanced content calendar combines education, trust, community,
and product offerings.
SEO, local presence, and targeted advertising. Search engine optimization improves
discoverability through names, descriptions, location, reviews, and helpful pages.
Advertising accelerates reach but requires clear objectives, target audiences, and testing.
Perdigón et al. (2018) link search rankings to security and consumer awareness.
Investment should be evaluated based on cost per conversation, acquisition, conversion,
and margin—not just impressions.
Email, CRM, and automation. Email remains useful for repeat purchases, updates, and
re-engaging identified customers. A CRM can start as a structured spreadsheet with
consent, history, and preferences. Automation should free up time from repetitive tasks
without dehumanizing the service. Automating disorganized processes merely replicates
their flaws more quickly.
Analytics, artificial intelligence, and mobile marketing. Metrics allow us to identify which
content attracts audiences, which channel converts, and which customers make repeat
purchases. Artificial intelligence can support copywriting, segmentation, and customer
service, but it requires human review and data protection. Molina et al. (2023) link
mobile marketing to new communication practices, and Ukshini et al. (2025) confirm the
general relationship between digital strategies and performance. The guiding principle
must be verifiable commercial utility.

The evidence converges on three assertions. First, digital marketing can enhance
visibility, performance, and business continuity. Second, social media and messaging
platforms serve as accessible entry points for small businesses. Third, gaps in knowledge,

resources, infrastructure, and measurement limit value capture. The alignment between
the meta-analysis by Ukshini et al. (2025), the reviews by Caraballo et al. (2023) and
García-Pujay et al. (2024), and the Ecuadorian studies from Manta, Azogues, Guayaquil,
and Guayas lends consistency to this interpretation.
There are also differences that must be taken into account. Findings from the service
sector or cooperatives cannot be directly applied to retail without adjustments. Similarly,
an association between digitalization and profitability does not prove that any given
campaign will lead to increased sales. The studies reviewed use diverse samples, time
periods, and indicators; some were conducted during the pandemic, when restrictions
altered consumer behavior. Therefore, the conclusion is conditional: these tools yield
better results when integrated with value propositions, inventory, customer service,
payment, logistics, and measurement.
Ecuador’s main problem does not appear to be a total lack of technology, but rather its
fragmented use. There is activity on social media, but the source of customers,
conversion, repeat purchases, and profitability are often not tracked. Business owners
monitor followers and reactions, yet they remain unaware of each channel’s marginal
contribution. This gap hinders learning and leaves investments vulnerable to algorithm
changes. The priority must shift from mere presence to a data -driven commercial
capability, without losing the personal touch that characterizes microenterprises.
In practical terms, a minimum sequence is proposed: assess customers and capabilities;
select one or two channels; organize the catalog and content; establish a conversation
protocol; enable reliable payment and delivery; track data; evaluate monthly; and scale
gradually. Universities, chambers of commerce, and public agencies can contribute
through applied training, consulting, connectivity, and digital security. Training should
incorporate industry-specific case studies and economic metrics to prevent dig ital
marketing from being reduced to the design of social media posts.
This review has limitations. The analysis was based on a heterogeneous set of
documents, and descriptive or cross-sectional studies predominate. Information about
platforms changes rapidly, and while several institutional reports provide context, they
do not offer causal analysis. Furthermore, the retail sector encompasses activities with
varying profit margins, purchasing cycles, and capabilities. These limitations do not
invalidate the identified patterns, though they require that these patterns be interpreted
as guidelines rather than a universal formula.

CONCLUSIONS
The main digital marketing tools for Ecuadorian retail microbusinesses are social media,
WhatsApp Business, websites, e-commerce, marketplaces, content marketing, video,
SEO, targeted advertising, email, CRM, analytics, automation, and mobile marketing.
Their value lies not in acting as standalone substitutes, but in forming a system that
accompanies the customer from discovery through repeat purchases. The selection

operational capacity.
The review identifies a positive correlation between digitalization and performance, but
the benefit depends on training, planning, trust, security, logistics, and measurement.
Simply creating online profiles does not guarantee competitiveness; value arises when
each interaction is linked to business objectives and results. For small businesses, the
most sustainable strategy is a gradual one: first organize the product offering and
customer service, measure conversions and complaints, and then expand investment or
technological sophistication.
Future research should measure sales, margins, retention, customer acquisition costs,
and return on investment using longitudinal designs and comparisons by activity,
territory, and level of maturity. It is also advisable to study data protection, the impact
of artificial intelligence, and dependence on platforms. This agenda would make it
possible to distinguish between short-term adoption and true, sustainable digital
capability.
