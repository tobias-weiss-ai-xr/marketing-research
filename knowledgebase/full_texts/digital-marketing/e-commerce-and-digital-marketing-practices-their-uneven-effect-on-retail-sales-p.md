ABSTRACT: Digital marketing is widely promoted as an equalizer for small retailers in emerging markets, yet evidence on
whether its returns are evenly distributed - across firms and across marketing channels - remains limited. Drawing on the
technology-organization-environment framework and the resource -based view, this study examined the adoption of five digital
marketing channels (email, social media, search engine, digital display, and mobile marketing) and their relationship to four  sales
performance indicators (revenue, sales volume, customer acquisition, and customer retention) among 100 retail businesses in
Surigao City, Philippines. Data from a validated survey were analyzed using weighted means, one -way ANOVA, and Pearson
correlation. Three findings stand out. First, adoption was shallow and uneven: retailers concentrated on free, low -skill
applications of social media (M = 2.82) while the channel most strongly associated with performance - search engine marketing
(r = .74 -.92 across indicators) - was the least practiced (M = 1.73), revealing a substantial adoption -effectiveness gap. Second,
returns to digital marketing were channel -contingent rather than uniform: social media, search engine, and mobile marketing
correlated positively with all performance indicators, email marketing showed no significant association, and digital display
advertising correlated negatively with all four indicators (r = -.33 to -.61), suggesting that poorly targeted investment can be
counterproductive. Third, both adoption and performance differed significantly by capitalization, technological infrastructur e,
organizational form, retailer type, and operational tenure - a within-market digital divide in which better -resourced firms adopt
more and benefit more. The findings caution against treating digital marketing as a monolithic, capacity -neutral intervention and
support resource- and channel-differentiated approaches to SME digitalization policy in emerging markets.

1. INTRODUCTION
Digital marketing is frequently framed as a great equalizer for small retailers: it lowers the cost of reaching customers, re moves
geographic constraints, and allows micro -enterprises to compete with far larger firms (Deku et al., 2024; Grewal et al., 2021).
This framing is especially prominent in emerging markets such as the Philippines, where exceptionally high social media
engagement and rapid e -commerce growth have made digital channels appear both accessible and essential (Nadeem, 2024).
Yet a growing body of evidence complicates the equalizer narrative. The OECD (2021) documents a persistent and in some
respects widening digital gap between smaller and larger firms, warning that the benefits of digital transformation may accru e
disproportionately to early and well-resourced adopters - deepening, rather than narrowing, existing inequalities among firms. If
that concern is valid, then two questions matter for any small -retail economy: whether the returns to digital marketing are
evenly distributed across firms of different resource endowments, and whether they are evenly distributed across the differen t
digital marketing channels a firm might invest in.
Two established theoretical lenses frame these questions. The technology -organization-environment (TOE) framework holds
that a firm’s adoption of technological innovation is shaped jointly by its technological context, organizational characteris tics,
and external environment (Tornatzky & Fleischer, 1990) - implying that adoption of digital marketing should vary systematically
with firm-level attributes such as capitalization, organizational form, and technological infrastructure. The resource -based view
(RBV) goes further, arguing that performance differences among firms stem from heterogeneously distributed resources and
capabilities that are valuable, rare, and difficult to imitate (Barney, 1991) - implying that even among adopters, the performance
returns to digital marketing should favor firms with the financial and technological resources to implement it well. Together ,

A parallel gap concerns channels. Most SME -focused studies treat digital marketing as a single construct or examine one
channel in isolation, leaving open the question of whether different channels - email, social media, search engine, display, and
mobile marketing - yield comparable returns for small retailers (Chatterjee & Kumar, 2021; Dinh et al., 2023). Channel -level
evidence from developed markets suggests they do not: search advertising effectiveness depends heavily on keyword and
campaign quality (Li & Yang, 2022; Wenning, 2021), while display advertising can backfire under poor targeting (Fernando &
Wahid, 2019). Whether this channel heterogeneity holds among small retailers in a peripheral emerging -market city - where
technical capacity is limited and adoption is recent - has not been established.
This study addresses both gaps using survey data from 100 retail businesses in Surigao City, a secondary Philippine city whos e
retail sector is dominated by small, sole -proprietor enterprises. It asks three research questions: (RQ1) To what extent, and how
unevenly, do small retailers practice the five major digital marketing channels? (RQ2) Do the extent of digital marketing pra ctice
and its perceived sales performance effects differ systematically by firm resources and characteristics - type of retailer,
organizational form, capitalization, operational tenure, and technological infrastructure? (RQ3) Are the associations between
digital marketing practice and sales performance uniform across channels, or channel -contingent? In answering these questions,
the study contributes a channel -disaggregated, resource -contingent account of digital marketing returns in an understudied
emerging-market retail setting, and offers evidence relevant to the design of SME digitalization support programs.

2. LITERATURE REVIEW AND THEORETICAL FRAMEWORK
2.1 Theoretical Anchors: TOE and the Resource-Based View
The TOE framework posits that a firm’s adoption of technological innovation is jointly shaped by its technological context
(available internal and external technologies), organizational context (size, structure, and slack resources), and environmen tal
context (industry and market conditions) (Tornatzky & Fleischer, 1990). Applied to digital marketing, TOE predicts that adopt ion
will vary with a retailer’s technological infrastructure, capitalization, organizational form, and sector - the business -profile
variables examined in this study. The RBV complements this adoption logic with a performance logic: firms differ in their bun dles
of valuable, rare, and imperfectly imitable resources, and these differences - not the mere availability of a technology - explain
performance heterogeneity (Barney, 1991). Recent empirical work substantiates this mechanism directly in the digital marketin g
domain: Zhang et al. (2025) found that digital marketing improves corporate performance primarily by building firms’
“information dynamic capability” - their capacity to acquire, integrate, and absorb market information - rather than through
digital marketing activity alone, with information-integration capability showing no mediating effect where firms lacked it. This is
precisely the capability -contingent mechanism RBV predicts, and it anticipates a central pattern in the present study’s results:
adoption and returns should both track firm resources, not run independently of them. Together, TOE and RBV predict not a
level playing field but a resource -contingent one, in which both the extent of digital marketing practice and its payoff differ
across firms. The broader scholarly trajectory of the field supports treating digital marketing as a multidimensional, capabi lity-
dependent construct rather than a single undifferentiated practice (Kannan & Li, 2017; Dwivedi et al., 2021).
2.2 Digital Marketing Channels and Their Expected Returns
E-commerce has expanded rapidly alongside internet penetration, allowing even small retailers to operate continuously and
reach previously inaccessible markets (Tarso et al., 2025), though adoption barriers related to infrastructure, cost, and dig ital
readiness persist in developing economies (Ulhaq et al., 2022). Within this environment, the five major digital marketing
channels differ substantially in their capability requirements and documented returns. Email marketing is low -cost and highly
measurable (Budac, 2016), with strong reported ROI when campaigns are segmented and personalized (Lee & Kim, 2022) - but
its returns depend precisely on those capabilities, and unsegmented, untracked campaigns yield weak effects on direct sales
metrics (Chatterjee & Kumar, 2021; Sabbagh, 2021). Social media - broadly defined as internet -based applications built on the
participatory, user -generated foundations of Web 2.0 (Kaplan & Haenlein, 2010) - combines free organic reach with paid
amplification; multi-platform campaigns measurably lift online sales (Wang et al., 2025), paid social advertising increases traffic
and conversion (Tiago & Veríssimo, 2014), and activity on these platforms has been linked to stronger customer equity even in
premium retail segments (Kim & Ko, 2012), making it the most accessible entry point for resource -constrained firms (Hazée et
al., 2017). Consistent with this, case -study evidence from fashion e -commerce shows social media content shaping consumer
decision-making directly at the point of purchase (Pei, 2024).
Search engine marketing (SEM) targets consumers actively searching for products (Key, 2017), and its effectiveness is tightly
coupled to technical capability: campaign structure, ad relevance, and keyword optimization significantly determine click -

low-capability end; SEM and targeted display at the rare, high-capability end.
2.3 Sales Performance and the Emerging-Market Evidence Gap
Sales performance in retail digital marketing research is commonly operationalized through revenue, sales volume, customer
acquisition, and customer retention (Grewal et al., 2021), though a large -scale review of marketing performance measurement
cautions that these outcome constructs are frequently only weakly related to one another across studies and should not be
treated as interchangeable (Katsikeas et al., 2016) - a caution the present study addresses by analyzing all four indicators
separately rather than collapsing them into a single index. Revenue is increasingly shaped by online -offline channel integration
(Yan, 2024; Zou, 2024); sales volume responds strongly to digital marketing and e -commerce adoption (Junaedi et al., 2023);
acquisition is supported by targeted content and incentives (García et al., 2019; Langen & Huber, 2022) as well as by satisfa ction-
driven word -of-mouth referral (Wangenheim & Bayón, 2007) and user -generated content (You & Joshi, 2020); and retention
responds to digital loyalty programs and sustained social media engagement (Kim et al., 2022; Santini et al., 2020; Thompson,
2024), consistent with earlier evidence that loyalty -program membership shapes how customers evaluate and remain with a
firm through repeated service experiences (Bolton et al., 2000). Underlying all four outcomes is a broader shift in consumer
decision-making itself: digital and social channels now shape the customer journey well before purchase, from information
search through post-purchase word-of-mouth (Stephen, 2016). However, this evidence base is drawn overwhelmingly from large
firms and developed or metropolitan markets. Channel -disaggregated evidence from small retailers in secondary cities of
developing economies is scarce, and the moderating role of firm resources - central to both TOE and RBV - is rarely tested across
multiple channels simultaneously. This study addresses that gap. Consistent with TOE, it expects adoption to differ significa ntly
by firm profile (RQ2); consistent with RBV, it expects performance associations to be strongest for capability -intensive channels
and weakest - potentially null or negative - for channels practiced without their complementary capabilities (RQ3).

3. MATERIALS AND METHODS
3.1 Research Design
The study employed a quantitative, descriptive -correlational research design. The descriptive component characterized the
profile of retail business respondents and the extent to which they implement digital marketing strategies, while the
correlational component tested whether a statistically significant relationship existed between the extent of digital marketi ng
practice and sales performance. This design allowed the identification of patterns and associations in real -world business
operations without manipulating any variable.
3.2 Participants
Respondents were 100 retail business owners and managers in Surigao City, Philippines, selected through purposive sampling.
Eligibility was determined via a pre -screening questionnaire requiring that respondents (a) currently operate a retail business
within Surigao City, (b) engage in some form of e -commerce or digital marketing practice, and (c) voluntarily agree to
participate. A sample of 100 was considered adequate to detect moderate relationships in correlational analysis while remaini ng
logistically manageable (Creswell & Creswell, 2018). Respondents represented a range of retail sectors, including food and
beverage, apparel, electronics, home and furniture, and beauty and personal care .
3.3 Research Instrument
A researcher -made survey questionnaire was administered in three sections: (a) business profile (type of retailer, form of
business organization, capitalization, years of operation, and technological infrastructure), (b) extent of digital marketing
practice across the five strategies, rated on a 4 -point frequency scale from Never to Always, and (c) perceived sales performance
across the four indicators, based on self -reported assessment. The instrument was reviewed by a panel of business and
marketing experts for content validity and was pilot-tested to assess internal consistency prior to full administration .

3.4 Data Collection and Analysis
Following institutional approval, data collection proceeded in four phases: pre -screening, informed consent, administration of
the main questionnaire (online and face -to-face), and monitoring/retrieval, over a four -week period. Data were analyzed using
frequency and percentage distribution (business profile), weighted mean (extent of digital marketing practice and sales
performance), one -way ANOVA (differences by business -profile variable), and Pearson product -moment correlation
(relationship between digital marketing practice and sales performance), all computed at the 0.05 level of significance.
Respondent participation was voluntary and anonymous, with informed consent obtained prior to data collection and the
research protocol reviewed by the appropriate institutional authorities.

4.2 RQ1: Shallow and Uneven Channel Adoption
Overall, digital marketing was practiced only to a limited extent (M = 2.26, SD = 0.33). Adoption followed a clear capability
gradient consistent with the RBV framing developed in Section 2: the freely available, low -skill channel - social media marketing -
was the most adopted (M = 2.82), while the most capability -intensive channel - search engine marketing - was the least (M =
1.73) (Table 2). The indicator -level pattern sharpens this picture (Table 3). Within social media itself, near -universal organic
promotion (M = 3.82) coexisted with almost no paid advertising (M = 1.54); within SEM, even basic keyword incorporation (M =
1.38) and paid search (M = 1.29) were essentially absent. Adoption, in other words, is not merely shallow overall - it is
systematically concentrated in the imitable, zero -cost applications of each channel, while the differentiating, capability -
dependent applications remain untouched. This is precisely the adoption profile that RBV predicts will yield little durable
competitive advantage (Barney, 1991), and it mirrors the OECD’s (2021) observation that SME digital uptake often stalls at ba sic
connectivity and communication uses without progressing to productivity -enhancing applications.

4.5 RQ3: Channel-Contingent, Not Uniform, Returns
Pearson correlation revealed that returns to digital marketing were sharply channel -contingent rather than uniform (Table 8),
directly answering RQ3. Three distinct patterns emerged. First, and most striking, search engine marketing showed the stronge st
associations with sales performance of any channel - a very strong correlation with sales volume (r = .92, p < .001) and a strong
correlation with revenue (r = .89, p < .001) - despite being the least -practiced channel of all. This adoption -effectiveness
inversion is the study’s central paradox: the channel with the highest apparent payoff is the one retailers use least, consis tent
with Zhang and Lichtenstein’s (2020) finding that SEM directly enhances conversion in competitive retail environments, and wi th
the RBV logic that rare, capability-intensive resources generate the largest returns (Barney, 1991). Second, mobile marketing (r =
.41 to .73) and social media marketing (r = .44 to .66) showed significant, moderate -to-strong positive associations across all four
indicators, aligning with Dinh et al.’s (2023) evidence on social media as a cost -effective driver of acquisition and retention
among Southeast Asian SMEs.
Third, and contrary to the equalizer narrative, two channels departed from the positive pattern. Email marketing showed no
significant relationship with any sales performance indicator (r = -.05 to .16, all p > .05), consistent with Chatterjee and Kumar’s
(2021) observation that email’s impact on direct sales is weak unless paired with personalization and automation - features that,
per Table 3, were largely absent here. Most notably, digital display advertising showed a significant negative relationship w ith all
four indicators (r = -.33 to -.61, all p < .01). Rather than dismissing this as anomalous, we read it through Fernando and Wahid’s
(2019) finding that poorly targeted, fatigue -prone display placements actively depress engagement and customer perception:
for capability -constrained small retailers, display advertising implemented without targeting or creative discipline appears not
merely ineffective but counterproductive. This negative association is the clearest single piece of evidence against treating
digital marketing as a monolithic good - some channels, practiced without their complementary capabilities, destroy rather than
create value.

5. DISCUSSION
The three findings converge on a single argument: in Surigao City’s retail sector, digital marketing is neither a monolithic
intervention nor a capacity -neutral equalizer, but a set of resource -contingent, channel -specific practices whose benefits are
unevenly distributed. This reframing carries both theoretical and practical implications, and - as a single-city case study - offers a
concrete empirical instance of the broader emerging -market SME digital divide that future multi -site research can test for
generalizability.
5.1 Theoretical Implications
The results jointly validate the TOE and RBV lenses while specifying how they operate in a small -retail context. Supporting TOE,
adoption of every capability -intensive channel varied significantly with firm context - retailer type, organizational form,
capitalization, and technological infrastructure - while only email marketing, the least capability -intensive channel, was largely
context-invariant (Table 6). Supporting RBV, performance returns were not conferred by the mere use of freely available tools
but concentrated in channels requiring rarer complementary capabilities: SEM, the scarcest capability in the sample, showed t he
strongest performance association, whereas organic social media - ubiquitous and easily imitated - showed weaker (though still
positive) associations. The negative display -advertising association extends RBV in a way rarely made explicit in the SME digital
marketing literature: a resource deployed without its complementary capabilities can generate negative returns, not merely
zero ones. Value destruction, not just value absence, is a possible outcome of undercapitalized digital marketing - a proposition
that warrants direct testing in future confirmatory work.
The study also contributes an emerging -market boundary condition to the “digital marketing as equalizer” thesis. The
equalizer narrative assumes that low access cost translates into broadly shared benefit. Our within -market evidence instead
shows the OECD’s (2021) macro -level SME digital divide reproduced at the level of a single secondary city: better -resourced
firms adopt more and benefit more, so equal access to platforms coexists with unequal returns. Access is necessary but not
sufficient; capability is the binding constraint.
5.2 The Adoption-Effectiveness Gap
The most actionable finding is the inversion between adoption and effectiveness for search engine marketing: the channel with
the strongest performance association (r = .74 -.92) was the least practiced (M = 1.73). Under a simple returns -seeking model,
retailers should invest most where payoffs are highest; the observed inversion therefore signals a market failure rooted in
capability and awareness rather than in the technology’s value. Because SEM requires technical skills - keyword research,
campaign structuring, bid management - that are scarce among sole-proprietor retailers, the channel remains unused despite its
apparent payoff. This gap is where targeted intervention (training, shared agency services, platform -provided automation) could
yield the largest marginal return, and it reframes the policy question from “how do we increase digital adoption?” to “how do
we close the capability gap on the highest-return channels specifically?”
5.3 Practical and Policy Implications
For retailers, the results argue against undifferentiated “go digital” advice and in favor of channel prioritization matched to
capability: consolidate the low -cost channels already yielding returns (organic social media, mobile/SMS), approach display
advertising with caution or avoid it until targeting capability exists, and treat SEM as the highest -value capability to acquire. For
business-support organizations and local government, the within -market divide implies that generic digitalization campaigns
may inadvertently widen inequality by benefiting already -capable firms; support should be resource -tiered, channeling
capability-building (not just access) toward the smallest and least -capitalized retailers. For platform providers, the SEM
adoption-effectiveness gap represents both an unmet need and a market opportunity for simplified, automated search -
advertising tools designed for micro-enterprises.

6. CONCLUSION
This study set out to test whether the returns to digital marketing are evenly distributed across firms and across channels i n
Surigao City’s retail sector. They are not. Adoption among Surigao City retailers was shallow and concentrated in freely avai lable,
low-skill applications, while the channels and capabilities most strongly associated with performance remained largely
untapped. Both adoption and returns tracked firm resources - capitalization and technological infrastructure above all -
reproducing a within -market digital divide in which better -resourced firms adopt more and benefit more. And returns were
sharply channel -contingent: strongly positive for search engine, mobile, and social media marketing; null for email; and
significantly negative for digital display advertising. Interpreted through the technology -organization-environment framework
and the resource -based view, these findings reframe digital marketing not as a capacity -neutral equalizer but as a resource -

7. RECOMMENDATIONS
Building on the channel -differentiated, resource -tiered logic developed in Section 5.3, the following operational
recommendations are organized by form of business organization to match guidance to typical resource levels:
For sole proprietors:
• Adopt free or low -cost digital tools such as Facebook Marketplace, TikTok, and Messenger for regular product promotion,
capitalizing on the near-universal existing use of basic social media promotion (Table 3).
• Integrate cashless payment options (e.g., GCash, Maya) to complement the already  widespread use of payment terminals
and improve transaction convenience.
• Use direct, personalized channels such as community group chats to notify customers of new stock and promotions, building
the customer-engagement habits that this study found underdeveloped in email and acquisition -focused practices.
For partnerships:
• Pool resources for professional -grade digital advertisements or boosted posts, addressing the low uptake of paid social
advertising identified as the weakest social media indicator (M = 1.54).
• Assign a partner to monitor digital inquiries and sales data specifically to close the ROI -tracking gap identified across email,
revenue, and sales-volume indicators.
For corporations:
• Invest in dedicated e -commerce platforms and professional digital marketing staff to move beyond payment -terminal-level
digital adoption toward the fuller e-commerce integration this study found still rare (8% of respondents).
• Implement CRM and analytics tools to enable the systematic ROI tracking, keyword optimization, and audience targeting that
this study found were the most consistently underutilized capabilities across all five digital marketing practices.
• Given the negative correlation found for digital display advertising, prioritize creative quality and audience targeting audi ts
before scaling any display-ad investment, rather than assuming increased spend will improve outcomes.

8. LIMITATIONS AND DIRECTIONS FOR FUTURE RESEARCH
Several limitations bound these conclusions and define a forward research agenda. First, both digital marketing practice and
sales performance were self -reported and cross -sectional, so the associations reported here - including the striking SEM and
display-advertising findings - are correlational and cannot establish causal direction; a longitudinal or quasi -experimental design
linking channel adoption to objective, time -stamped sales data (point -of-sale records, platform analytics) is the natural next
step. Second, the negative display -advertising association, while theoretically interpretable as value destruction under poor
targeting, is based on perceptual measures and a modest sample; it should be treated as a hypothesis for confirmatory testing
rather than a settled result. Third, purposive sampling of 100 retailers in a single secondary city limits statistical genera lizability;
replication across other emerging -market cities would test whether the within -market digital divide and channel -contingent
returns observed here hold more broadly. Finally, the study did not measure the specific capabilities (analytics skill, campa ign-
management expertise) that the RBV interpretation implicates; directly measuring these mediators would allow future work to
test the capability mechanism rather than infer it. Despite these limits, the convergence of the adoption pattern, the resour ce-
graded differences, and the channel -contingent correlations provides a coherent, theory -consistent account that meaningfully
advances understanding of SME digital marketing in emerging markets.
