 Abstract
Introduction: The aim of this study is to determine the knowledge and usage status of micro
enterprises regarding digital marketing applications, to identify the difficulties they face and to
reveal their support needs. In addition, the challenges these businesses face in digital marketing,
the tools they use most frequently, their performance measurement methods and their support
needs were also examined.
Method: A qualitative exploratory-descriptive research design was employed in this study. The
sample comprised 12 micro-enterprises in the Bozüyük district of Bilecik. Data were collected
through face -to-face interviews using a semi -structured interview guide administered to
voluntary participants identified through purposive and snowball sampling.
Results or Findings: The findings indicate that all 12 micro -business representatives
interviewed actively use digital marketing, although this usage is largely limited to social media
platforms. The vast majority of participants prefer visually -oriented platforms, particularly
‘Instagram’, and view social media advertising as the cornerstone of digital marketing. Whilst
this finding may indicate that digital marketing is replacing traditional marketing methods, it is
evident that micro-businesses are operating without strategic depth in this area.
Discussion or Conclusion: The process by which micro-enterprises adopt digital marketing is
progressing through a superficial engagement driven by necessity. For this reason, in order for
micro-enterprises to remain effective by going beyond merely having a digital presence, it is
vital that they are supported by an institutional training and support mechanism, rather than
relying on individual initiative. Future research could contribute to the literature by using
quantitative methods to test the impact of digital marketing investments on financial outcomes.
Keywords: digital marketing, micro business, qualitative research

Introduction1
 In today’s competitive business environment, digitalisation has become an increasingly
important strategic consideration  not only for large -scale companies but also for micro -
enterprises struggling to survive on limited resources. Given the high costs and regional
limitations associated with traditional marketing methods, digital marketing stands as a key tool
that provides cost-effectiveness for small-scale businesses, enables direct customer interaction,
and offers equal opportunities to overcome market barriers (Sharabati et al., 2024). In addition
to these market barriers, the current technological revoluti on demands a significant
restructuring of business operational efficiency, performance strategies, and client relationship
networks (Bermeo-Giraldo et al., 2022). Particularly for resource -constrained small business
entities, adopting advanced information communication technologies (ICTs) through  web-
based networking channels has evolved from a simple option into an important tactical and
strategic vehicle to ensure long -term viability (Bermeo -Giraldo et al., 2022; Teixeira et al.,
2018). Prior literature on organizational innovation adoption emphasizes that digital
transformation requires small firms to deeply balance multi -dimensional determinants such as
perceived industrial pressure, top management support, and expectation patterns agai nst
technological barriers (Bermeo -Giraldo et al., 2022; Teixeira et al., 2018). Within this
framework, integrating digital marketing channels serves as a strategic axis for micro -
enterprises to optimize scarce resources, reduce coordination costs, and sec ure an efficient
market positioning layout relative to large corporate entities (Bermeo -Giraldo et al., 2022).
Thus, the decision to engage in digital business environments is heavily moderated by the
baseline capabilities of the entrepreneur to transform technical constraints into dynamic market
opportunities (Teixeira et al., 2018).
However, despite the high potential offered by digital marketing, the extent to which
micro-enterprises can adopt this process and how they manage it strategically remains a subject
of debate in the  literature. Recent research indicates that  even when micro -enterprises in
emerging markets enter digital channels, they are unable to fully integrate these tools into their
long-term business plans due to a lack of technical know -how and digital literacy issues
(Alharthey, 2023). Similarly, although digital technologies may be more cost -effective
compared to traditional marketing, small businesses in particular may face challenges in
adopting them, such as limited financial resources for investing in digital infrastructure  and
tools. Furthermore, they may lack the necessary knowledge and experience to select and
implement digital solutions tailored to their specific needs (Zeitschel et al., 2022; Restrepo
Morales et al., 2024).
Despite the potential offered by digital marketing, issues such as the extent to which
micro-enterprises have adopted this process, the channels they use, and the challenges they face
in this process have not been adequately addressed, particularly in the national literature.
Adopting a qualitative exploratory-descriptive design, this study examines the digital marketing
practices of micro -enterprises, the tools they use, the challenges they encounter, and their
expectations for the future. The data for this study were collected through in -depth interviews
with 12 micro-enterprises located in the Bozüyük district of Bilecik province. The study aims
to fill a gap in the literature by identifying micro -enterprise owners’ perspectives on digital
marketing, the platforms and methods they commonly use, the time and knowledge constraints

Conceptual Framework
Digital Marketing
The widespread adoption of the internet, coupled with the proliferation of fast, cost -
effective communication channels and the ability to reach global audiences, has compelled
marketing managers to fundamentally restructure their strategic frameworks (Lamb erton &
Stephen, 2016). Moving beyond traditional paradigms, digital marketing encompasses all
promotional and transactional activities executed through interactive platforms, mobile
applications, and interconnected digital networks (Chaffey & Ellis -Chadwick, 2019).
Essentially, it represents a comprehensive approach that migrates core marketing processes into
dynamic digital environments. In this context, the digital transformation of marketing
operations is initiated through continuous web -based market re search and consumer insight
generation (Verhoef et al., 2021). Consequently, corporate digital ecosystems and web
interfaces fulfill entire marketing functions—ranging from automated distribution management
and dynamic pricing policies to customized after -sales services—within virtual environments
(Ryan, 2020).
Digital platforms bypass traditional red tape, making business -to-consumer exchanges
far more direct. This speed gives digitally agile firms a sharp edge: they enter new markets
rapidly and capture untapped value (Stephen, 2016). When tied to E -CRM, the domain shifts
from broad outreach to precise relationship building. As Chaffey and Ellis -Chadwick (2019)
conceptualize it, digital marketing is less about isolated channels and more about orchestrating
digital touchpoints to pull in and keep high-value customers.
This perspective aligns closely with the foundational definition provided by Kannan and
Li (2017), who argue that digital marketing has transitioned from a novel tactical term into an
overarching, multidimensional concept. It encapsulates the holistic proc ess of leveraging
cutting-edge digital technologies to attract prospective buyers, establish strong brand
preferences, promote corporate identity, cultivate long -term customer loyalty, and ultimately
accelerate sales performance (Kannan & Li, 2017). Businesses that successfully integrate these
digital strategies can project their offerings onto expansive global audiences at minimal
operational costs, while simultaneously exploiting real-time customer feedback loops (Dwivedi
et al., 2021). Driven by rapid te chnological velocity and the exponential rise of consumer
reliance on mobile platforms, modern enterprises are systematically reallocating substantial
proportions of their total marketing budgets away from legacy media and toward sophisticated
digital marketing instruments (Lamberton & Stephen, 2016; Verhoef et al., 2021).
In another definition provided by Kannan and Li (2017), digital marketing has evolved
from a simple term at its inception into a comprehensive concept that describes the process of
using digital technologies to attract customers, build brand preference, pr omote brands, foster
customer loyalty and increase sales (Kannan & Li, 2017). Digital marketing also enables
consumers with shared interests and views to form virtual communities. In this way, consumers
can make their voices heard more effectively on digit al platforms and come together to pool
their strength. Furthermore, companies have now recognised the critical importance of
establishing a ‘digital relationship’ with customers. Digital technologies and devices such as
smartphones, smart products, the Internet of Things (IoT), artificial intelligence and deep

learning are expected to bring about significant transformations in consumers’ lives in the near
future (Kannan & Li, 2017; Alkara, 2020).
Unlike traditional brick -and-mortar approaches, digital marketing channels refer to
internet-based systems capable of simultaneously creating, promoting, and delivering unique
value from producers to consumers via interactive digital networks. The rapid evolution of fast
and cost-effective online infrastructures has altered the way businesses discover, learn, and
manage their strategic interfaces with target markets. In the contemporary digital econom y,
managing a corporate web presence enables small -scale operations to optimize scarce
resources, reduce coordination costs, and secure an efficient market positioning layout relative
to large corporate entities (Bermeo -Giraldo et al., 2022). Furthermore, utilizing technology-
based marketing strategies allows smaller businesses to target niche consumer segments with
personalized content, thereby creating real -time interactive experiences that foster trust and
customer retention without requiring heavy capital investments. Pereira et al. (2025) conducted
a bibliometric analysis of 300 scientific articles examining customer loyalty and retention in
the digital environment, finding that emerging technologies —including artificial intelligence
and big data—significantly shape customer experience, trust, and engagement. Their findings
indicate that personalization and digital innovation, rather than the scale of capital investment,
emerge as the fundamental drivers of customer retention, offering businesses o f any size
strategic insights to strengthen their competitiveness in the digital marketplace (Pereira et al.,
2025).
Digital Marketing Tools
 Nowadays, digital marketing is carried out using a wide variety of tools. The most
popular methods include social media marketing, e-mail marketing, mobile marketing, affiliate
marketing, PPC advertising, search engine optimisation (SEO), display advertisi ng, viral
marketing and location -based digital marketing. These digital marketing tools are briefly
summarised below under the following subheadings (Alkara, 2020).
Mobile Marketing
The Mobile Marketing Association (MMA) defines mobile marketing as " a set of
applications that enables organisations to establish interactive and relevant communication
and engagement with their target audiences via any mobile device or network." Aydin-Gokgoz
et al. (2022) traced how these vehicles have developed into essential tools for contemporary
business communication and advertising over the past decade, enabling organizations to
establish interactive engagement with their target markets through wireless networks. Drawing
on a database of 178 articles published in leading marketing journals, their review demonstrates
that as handheld smart devices and smartphone applications become integrated into consumers'
daily lifestyles, mobile -oriented campaigns offer advertisers a highly persuasive and cost -
effective vehicle to send direct responses, streamline internal operations, and connect with ideal
clients regardless of time and place parameters, while also reshaping consumer -to-firm, firm-
to-consumer, and firm -to-firm marketing relationships more broadly (Aydin -Gokgoz et al.,
2022).
Social Media Marketing
With the advancement of information technology, social media has provided consumers
with ease and convenience in communication in their daily lives, whilst also becoming the most
effective and convenient communication tool for businesses in their corporate  marketing
activities. Platforms such as YouTube, Facebook, Twitter and Instagram enable businesses to

reach potential customers from various demographic groups through corporate posts, regardless
of time and place, to develop existing customer relationships and to acquire new customers
(Dwivedi et al., 2021). Kotler and Keller (2016), on the other hand, describe it as "a platform
where consumers can interactively share text, visual, video and audio content both with one
another and with businesses" (Kotler & Keller, 2016; Alkara & Çalışkan, 2023).  In summary,
whilst socialising and self-expression are among the primary motivations for people’s use of
social media, there are also other objectives such as networking, accessing information and
economic benefits (Kavak et al., 2020).
E-Mail Marketing
Companies use email to inform their customers and target audiences about their
products, services, and other corporate developments. As Eze et al. (2021) note, e-mail
marketing is an important digital marketing tool —particularly for micro -scale businesses—
that enhances basic customer loyalty and makes it easier to offer special deals directly to the
target audience.
Content Marketing
Content marketing encompasses systematic corporate efforts to create, distribute, and
automate multimedia -focused, interactive brand content that directly addresses specific
consumer questions. Du Plessis (2022) conducted a scoping review of twelve years of academic
literature on content marketing's effect on online consumer behavior, synthesising evidence on
how digital brand content —including blogs, infographics, and multimedia assets —shapes
consumer engagement across various touchpoints. The review found that by leveraging
innovative web-based assets and visual storytelling frameworks, this strategy effectively shifts
the emphasis of market competition from intrusive advertising behaviours toward building
reliable consumer relationships, fostering trust, and capturing high -quality organic prospects
(Du Plessis, 2022).
Search Engine Optimisation (SEO)
SEO means improving the ranking of a web page in Internet by optimizing digital
content to increase visibility, traffic, and brand authority across search engines.  By optimizing
web content, selecting relevant keywords, and building a robust backlink profile, micro -
businesses can benefit from organic consumer searches; this allows them to direct highly
interested prospects to the sections of their web platforms tailo red to their target audience and
enables them to gain a sustainable competitive advantage (Kovalenko & Kuzmenko, 2020).
PPC Advertising
PPC (Pay -Per-Click) is a digital marketing tool in which advertisers design website
advertising campaigns targeting specific audiences via other websites or search engines, and
pay per click for the traffic generated. Rezaei et al. (2024) examined this tool empirically within
a retail banking context, finding that PPC advertising enables advertisers to swiftly target
specific customer segments, enhance online and localized visibility, schedule ads for particular
times and places, and maintain better budget and cost control while generating immediate,
measurable results. Their path analysis further revealed that customers' attitude toward PPC
advertising significantly affects both their continuous search intentions and their intentions to
use the advertised services, underscoring PPC's effectiveness as a performance -based digital
marketing tool (Rezaei et al., 2024). Consequently, this mechanism provides resource -
constrained small and medium -sized enterprises (SMEs) with  granular, real -time autonomy
over the temporal distribution, messaging frequency, and operational budgetary thresholds of

their digital promotional campaigns, enabling them to maximize their market efficiency despite
significant financial and human resource constraints (Ritz et al., 2019).
Display Ad
Display advertising involves the presentation of visual brand content—such as banners,
images, and video —on third-party websites and applications, enabling firms to reach broad
audiences through behavioral targeting (Bayer et al., 2020). As one of the most popular types
of online advertising, display ads can target specific consumer characteristics to enhance
advertising effectiveness. The first online advertisement was published in 1994 as an AT&T
banner in HotWired magazine, at a time wh en there were approximately 30 million internet
users (Wang et al., 2017).  Display advertising utilise interactive tools, banners, visual formats,
and short video structures to function as a virtual counterpart to traditional billboard advertising,
across content-rich websites to capture consumer attention. Giombi et al. (2022) conducted a
scoping systematic review of studies examining how interactive advertising features —
including website banners, pop-ups, and social network ads—influence consumer engagement,
recall, and comprehension. Their findings indicate that while interactive features can enhance
consumer engagement, evidence regarding their effect on ad recall and comprehension remains
mixed, suggesting that  micro-enterprises must carefully calibrate interactivity levels when
tailoring brand messaging to different consumer segments and communication channels in
order to meaningfully improve baseline ad recall outcomes (Giombi et al., 2022).
Location-Based Digital Marketing
Location-based marketing utilizes advanced technological infrastructures to capture a
consumer's real -time geographical position and behavioral data to deliver highly relatable,
localized marketing notifications. Garcia (2022) explored this proximity-oriented approach
through the development and evaluation of a geomarketing application employing geofencing
technology, whereby immediate, context-specific commercial updates are transmitted directly
to consumers' mobile devices when they enter p redefined geographical target zones. The
usability analysis revealed that while performance expectancy, effort expectancy, and hedonic
motivation were significant factors driving user acceptance of such location -based marketing
applications, businesses must also carefully manage issues related to security, privacy,
advertisement relevancy, and notification overload in order to effectively transform the
customer journey without alienating targeted consumers (Garcia, 2022).
Viral Marketing
Online marketplaces have rapidly transformed traditional sales methods through the
interactive relationships offered by the internet. The development of the internet has altered the
dynamics between source and recipient in communication, transforming consumers into active
participants and making the process reciprocal andKanna interactive. Dinh and Lee (2025)
demonstrated that viral marketing leverages established social communication networks to
promote exponential brand growth, product acceptance, and cost -effective message
dissemination, finding that emotional engagement moderates the relationship between
consumers' social and personal motivations and their participation in consumer online brand -
related activities (COBRAs). By encouraging consumers to act as organic brand advocates who
actively share their personal purchase experiences and product reviews online, this word -of-
mouth strategy allows firms to trigger favorable customer behaviors, reduce marketing costs,
and capture broad digital markets rapidly through authentic, emotionally resonant content (Dinh
& Lee, 2025).

Literature Review
This section examines the concept of micro-enterprises and outlines the objectives and
findings of previous international and national studies in this field.
Thirtawati et al. (2025) examined the marketing communication strategies of six
agricultural social enterprises in Indonesia through a qualitative descriptive approach
combining in-depth interviews with SWOT analysis. The findings revealed that limited funding
and d ifficulties in sustaining customer engagement were the most significant barriers to
effective marketing communication, while opportunities emerged through digital media
utilization and stakeholder collaboration. The study further emphasized that social enterprises
often struggle to move beyond ad -hoc, resource-constrained communication efforts toward a
more strategic and consistent use of digital channels (Thirtawati et al., 2025).
Luo et al. (2025) employed a quantitative research design to examine the impact of
short-form video content on consumer purchase intention on social media platforms, utilising
structural equation modelling (SEM) via SPSS and AMOS. In the study, data collec ted via an
online questionnaire from 372 Chinese consumers were analysed to test the effects of short
video content characteristics—usefulness, ease of use, and entertainment —on consumer trust
and purchase intention, drawing on the Stimulus –Organism–Response (S–O–R) framework.
The results of the analyses revealed that all three content characteristics significantly influenced
both consumer trust and purchase intention, with entertainment emerging as a particularly
strong driver of engagement on platforms such as TikTok and Douyin (Luo et al., 2025).
Fu et al. (2024) investigated  the adoption of social media marketing among small and
medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) and its impact on business performance, employing the
Technology-Organization-Environment (TOE) framework. Survey data were collected from
508 SMEs in Indonesia and ana lyzed using Structural Equation Modeling. The findings
revealed that technological factors—particularly perceived advantage, complexity, and cost —
created notable hesitancy among SME owners toward adopting social media marketing. In
contrast, organizational  factors such as top management support and employee capability,
together with environmental pressures from consumers and vendors, were found to significantly
drive adoption (Fu et al., 2024).
Matosas-López (2024) examined whether customer engagement (CE) on social media
can serve as a predictor of operating revenue among small and medium -sized enterprises,
drawing on a sample of 100 Spanish SMEs analyzed over an eight -year period. Employing
Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA) time -series modeling, the study
distinguished between enterprises managing their social media internally with non-specialized
staff and those outsourcing this function to specialized professionals. The findings revealed that
CE on social media could not reliably forec ast operating revenue when social networks were
managed internally by non -specialized personnel. However, when social media management
was outsourced to specialists and combined with complementary digital marketing actions such
as SEO, SEM, and email marketing, CE emerged as a significant predictor of revenue growth
(Matosas-López, 2024).
Buvar and Gati (2023) examined the adoption processes of digital marketing tools
among 181 micro-enterprises with limited resources in Hungary, using an online survey method
within the framework of the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM). The study also
investigated the impact of external information sources (such as Facebook groups and blogs),
which represent informal, problem -based learning methods, and the trust placed in digital
marketing professionals on the use of digital tools. The data obtained were t ested using

JATSS Volume 8 Issue 3 429

Structural Equation Modelling (PLS-SEM), and unlike other studies, no linear relationship was
found between the ‘perceived ease of use’ and the ‘perceived usefulness’ of digital marketing
tools. However, trust in digital marketing consultants and professio nals was identified as the
factor that most significantly accelerates adoption by making the technology perceived as both
easier to use and more beneficial (Buvar and Gati, 2023).
Ritz et al. (2019) examined the motivations of small business managers to run their own
digital marketing campaigns by integrating the ‘Do -It-Yourself (DIY)’ behaviour model with
the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM). Data from an online survey of 250 small business
managers who manage their own promotional activities was analysed using Structural Equation
Modelling (SEM). The research found that the DIY model, developed for consumer behaviour,
is also applicable to commercial activities. It was determined that businesses undertake these
activities in-house because they find external professional services costly or because packaged
services do not fully align with the business ethos; furthermore, a strong sense of control and a
desire for self-development were found to be present during the execution of the process (Ritz
et al., 2019).
Taiminen and Karjaluoto  (2015) examined the current level of use, objectives and
barriers to the use of digital marketing tools in SMEs in Finland using a mixed -methods
research design. Qualitative data obtained from semi -structured interviews with 16 different
marketing managers in the first phase was supported by quantitative data collected via an online
survey from 421 SME representatives in the second phase. According to the results, despite the
general prevalence of digitalisation, it was found that approximately 46% of SMEs admitted to
misusing digital marketing. It was found that most businesses use social media as a one -way
announcement tool rather than an interactive dialogue tool; time constraints, staff shortages and
a lack o f expertise were identified as the major internal barriers delaying technological
integration (Taiminen & Karjaluoto, 2015).
A review of the international literature reveals that research on digital marketing in
micro-enterprises has primarily focused on topics such as the adoption of new technologies,
integration with technological and digital developments, strategy development , and the
measurement of performance success; it has been established that these studies have been
unable to implement effective digital marketing strategies due to obstacles such as insufficient
resources, inadequate levels of digital marketing knowledge,  and the limited capabilities of
businesses, preventing them from implementing effective digital marketing strategies.
Subsequent studies have focused on the adoption of emerging digital technologies, such as
artificial intelligence, and the various factor s influencing them; however, recent research has
been observed to focus more on the factors influencing digital marketing success in micro and
small businesses, strategic improvements aimed at enhancing competitive strength, and specific
methods used to achieve measurable results.
The contemporary strategic configuration of digital and interactive marketing
capabilities within the micro-enterprise ecosystem often exhibits a highly reactive, superficial,
and short -term operational posture (Eze et al., 2021). Because micro -scale operations are
consistently bedeviled by acute financial limitations, poor institutional infrastructure, and gaps
in technical know -how, these firms traditionally target digital platforms that promise
instantaneous sales conversions or quick, low -cost customer  interactions (Gao, 2025).
Consequently, Gao (2025) enhanced the traditional Technology Acceptance Model by
incorporating consumer engagement as a critical mediating construct —reflecting the resource
constraints and rapidly evolving market conditions chara cteristic of firms in developing
economies such as China and Kazakhstan. Empirical investigation using structural equation

modelling and regression analysis across 390 firms in these emerging countries revealed a
highly fragmented outcome: while the implementation of digital marketing tools and
investment in digital marketing technologies related directly and positively to business
performance, the effect sizes for direct tool implementation remained comparatively modest,
with consumer engagement and the resulting capacity for digital transformation emerging as
far stronger and more consistent predictors of firm success than the mere adoption of any single
marketing channel (Gao, 2025).
In particular, limited research has been found in the national literature in recent years
that examines the experiences of micro -enterprises in digital marketing processes using a
qualitative research approach. Unlike studies in the national literature, this study focuses on
digital marketing processes in micro-enterprises. The primary aim of the study is to determine

Method
Research Design, Sample and Data Collection Process
This study employed a qualitative exploratory-descriptive research design. Qualitative
research is a process that centres on participants’ perspectives, aims to uncover truths through
in-depth analysis, and is conducted using techniques such as observation, interviews and
document analysis. The main designs of qualitative research include narrative, ethnography,
theory building, case study and phenomenology (Creswell, 2017).  Sample sizes vary
significantly depending on the design; for instance, Creswell suggests a range  of 5 to 25
participants for phenomenological studies. In this study, the qualitative exploratory-descriptive
research design  was chosen in order to obtain valid data in line with the study objectives
and purposive sampling was employed to select participa nts who possessed direct, in -depth
experience regarding the phenomenon under study (Lincoln & Guba, 1985).  The final sample
comprised 12 participants. Given the qualitative, descriptive nature of the study and the rich,
extensive narratives obtained from individual interviews, this group size was deemed adequate
to achieve sufficient descriptive depth without imposing predetermined numerical thresholds
(Malterud et al., 2016)
 The research design has been structured around the defined research questions and
theoretical framework; through the selected qualitative method, the aim is to gain an in -depth
understanding of the subject under investigation by drawing on the participant s’ own
experiences. In line with this, a semi-structured interview guide was developed to serve the
research objective.  In addition, closed-ended questions were asked separately in the interview
guide to obtain current demographic information about the businesses. The flexible structure
offered by this method enabled participants to express their views freely. Thanks to this design,
which is fundamentally exploratory in nature whilst also possessing descriptive characteristics,
comprehensive answers were sought to the research questions outlined below.
•   What is the level of knowledge regarding digital marketing practices among micro -
enterprises?
•    Which digital marketing tools are most commonly used by micro-enterprises?
•  What challenges do micro -enterprises face when implementing digital marketing
processes?

Data Analysis
The data were analysed using a deductive thematic analysis informed by Braun and
Clarke’s (2006) analytical stages. Initial themes were derived from the research questions, while
additional patterns emerging from the data were considered during repeated re adings of the
transcripts. The analysis phase began with the careful listening to and transcription of the
interview audio recordings; during this process, the statements were transcribed verbatim. To
protect the participants’ confidentiality, each was assigned a unique code number. In
accordance with the research questions and the structure of the semi-structured interview form,
a deductive approach was adopted, and the main themes were established prior to the analysis.
Taking into account the volume of data and the scope of the study, and without the need to use
any software packages, the transcribed texts were read repeatedly;  the coding was conducted
manually by the researcher. Following the initial coding, the transcripts and theme
classifications were reviewed again to assess consistency between the raw data, codes, and
themes. An audit trail documenting the coding and theme -development decisions was
maintained to ensure the scientific validity and reliability of the research, the study did not rely
on a single method; in line with Lincoln and Guba’s (1985) four fundamental criteria, strategies
of member validation, detailed (thick) description, audit trail and reflexivity were integrated not
only into the data collection and analysis stages but throughout the entire research process.
Validity and Reliability
The research process was conducted with great care, taking into account four
fundamental elements (credibility, transferability, reliability and confirmability) to ensure the
validity and reliability of the study. In qualitative research, the primary aim  is to explore in

(Kocaman, 2024). Participants’ statements were supported by direct quotations, and
interpretations were presented in a manner consistent with the d ata, thereby strengthening the
reliability and credibility of the research in the study. These procedures were used to strengthen
the credibility, transferability, dependability, and confirmability of the study. Furthermore, the

Discussion and Limitations
Discussion
When compared to studies based on SMEs’ digital marketing practices within the
Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) framework, the findings of this study are generally
consistent. While previous studies have shown that perceived ease of use and perceived benefits
are key determinants of technology adoption, this study found that participants’ perception of
Instagram as a digital marketing tool and their active use of it indicated a high perceived ease
of use. However, the study also revealed that email and local SEO were not used for marketing
purposes, and that the perceived benefits of more complex tools remained limited among
participants. This finding supports the results of a study by Alharthe y (2023) conducted on
micro-enterprises in emerging markets.
From a Technology -Organization-Environment (TOE) perspective, the technological
barriers (inadequate digital literacy), organisational constraints (time, budget, single -person
operations), and environmental factors (limited peer adoption within the district) identified in
this study collectively explain why adoption remains confined to low -complexity, high -
visibility platforms. This resonates with Zeitschel et al. (2022), who found that cost sensitivity
and knowledge gaps remain persistent adoption barriers for micro -firms regardless of
geographic context.
The thoughts and opinions of business representatives participating in the study
regarding digital marketing reflect a needs -based approach rather than an opportunity -based
one, as seen in the study by Restrepo Morales et al. (2024). However, the findings of the study
suggest that, in order to develop and implement a more strategic approach to digital marketing
in micro-enterprises located in relatively smaller regions, not only must individual capacities
be developed, but institutional and structural support may also be necessary.
Limitations
 There are various limitations that must be taken into account when interpreting the
findings of this study. As the sample consists of micro-businesses from the Bozüyük district of
Bilecik province, this may create selection bias because the participating businesses may not

JATSS Volume 8 Issue 3 440

represent all micro -enterprises in Bozüyük.  The findings are context -specific and are not
intended for statistical generalisation. However, the detailed description of the research setting
and participant characteristics may help readers assess their potential transferability to similar
contexts. In addition, the data obtained through interviews is based on the responses to the
questions provided by the the business owners or an employee representing the participating
enterprise. it has not been possible to verify the data using other sources. This situation limits
internal triangulation. Another limitation of the study is the sample size. Whilst a sample size
of 12 businesses may be sufficient for a qualitative study with an exp loratory aim, it is not
possible to generalise the findings.
Conclusion
This research offers an in-depth account of the digital marketing experiences of micro-
enterprises, spanning their current practices, operational dynamics, and lived experiences. The
findings reveal that all 12 micro-enterprise participants actively engage with digital marketing.
But this adoption is predominantly confined to social media platforms  (especially Instagram).
The vast majority of participants exhibit a strong preference for visually oriented channels,
particularly Instagram, appearing to associate digital marketing predominantly with social
media advertising.  Although this shift indicates that digital avenues are supplementing
traditional marketing methods, it simultaneously underscores a an important limitation: micro-
enterprises operate without strategic depth or long -term planning. This pattern is shaped by
well-defined internal barriers: insufficient time, notable gaps in specialized knowledge in
specialized knowledge, and inadequate budget allocation. Excessive daily workloads prevent
micro- enterprises from sustaining consistent efforts, resulting in irregular content deployment
and tactical improvisation, while half of the participants lack a dedicated budget and the rest
operate under tight financial constraints . These findings are consistent with the existing
literature on the resource constraints faced by micro -enterprises. This narrow perception also
impedes the development of robust omni -channel strategies, despite recent emphasis by
Febriani et al. (2025), who demonstrated that even when MSMEs recognize the strategic value
of integrating online and offline channels, limited resources and managerial capacity constrain
their ability to progress beyond fragmented, early-stage omnichannel adoption.
 Perhaps the most striking finding concerns what micro -enterprises do not use. Low -
cost, high-return channels remain almost entirely absent from their repertoire: eleven of the
twelve participants had made no attempt at local SEO (Search Engine Optimization), and not a
single participant had ever run an email marketing campaign. This limited engagement indicates
that micro-enterprises equate popular, consumer -facing platforms with the digital marketing
ecosystem as a whole, leaving instruments that are decisive for competitiveness in localized
markets largely unused. The pattern points less to deliberate strategic choice than to restricted
technical know-how and digital literacy —an interpretation reinforced by the dual bottlenecks
of limited time and limited expertise, which closely mirror the internal organizational barriers
reported by Taiminen and Karjaluoto (2015).
When contextualized within the specific socio -economic landscape of the Bozüyük
district in Bilecik, these empirical insights underscore a broader phenomenon documented
across the SME literature: a relatively limited and necessity-driven form of digital engagement
rather than a knowledge -based strategic posture. This is reflected in a discernible hesitancy
among SMEs to adopt social media marketing strategies, driven primarily by technological
apprehensions concerning perceived advantage, complexity, and cost (Fu et al., 2024). This
pattern reinforces international evidence concerning small-scale structures in peripheral or non-
metropolitan settings, where perceived cost remains a significant negative predictor of the

JATSS Volume 8 Issue 3 441

intention to adopt social media marketing (Abbasi et al., 2022). In these rural or semi -urban
environments, smaller firms overwhelmingly rely on scarce, internal human resources to
manage digital activities. Structurally, organizational performance in SMEs has been shown to
depend heavily on knowledge management capability, financial literacy, and problem -solving
skills that are typically in short supply among small, resource -constrained firms. These
dynamics align with broader findings that digital marketing capabilities carry significant value
relevance for firm performance (Homburg & Wielgos, 2022), underscoring why superficial,
low-capability digital engagement prevents firms from converting visual traffic into premium
sales conversion rates or sustainable brand awareness.
Moving micro -enterprises from a passive digital presence to genuine digital
performance may require a gradual shift  from individual improvisation to structural support.
Regional policymakers, chambers of commerce, and development agencies should therefore
carefully develop low-cost digital literacy training and technical advisory services tailored to
the constraints of  rural enterprises. Future research could extend these findings through
longitudinal, cross-sectoral studies that quantify the returns of local SEO and comparable low-
cost tools, and could examine how—or whether—micro-enterprises measure and evaluate their
digital marketing investments over time.
