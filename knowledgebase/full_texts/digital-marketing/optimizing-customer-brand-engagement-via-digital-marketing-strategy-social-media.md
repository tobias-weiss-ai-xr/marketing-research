ABSTRACT
The growth of digital technology has established social media as a primary consumer touchpoint for brands
utilizing influencer endorsements. This study examines how social media influencers and brand equity jointly
affect customer brand engagement with Ort useight running shoes in Indonesia. To address this objective, a
survey was conducted with 202 current Ortuseight users, and the data were analyzed using Partial Least Squares
Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM). The results indicate that both social media influencers and brand
equity positively and significantly influence customer brand engagement, though brand e quity exerts a
substantially stronger effect. Specifically, brand equity dimensions serve as primary drivers of long -term
engagement, whereas influencer credibility and communication prompt active consumer interaction. Overall,
these findings provide valua ble strategic insights, suggesting that long -term brand equity building exerts a
stronger influence on engagement than influencer campaigns alone, offering clear guidance for local brands’
digital strategies.
Keywords: social media influencers (SMIs); brand equity; customer brand engagement.

determining how customers conn ect with brands because they encourage interactions in the
form of likes, shares, and comments on posts about the brand (Arora et al., 2019).
The popularity of running leads to the growing running shoe market segment with
various shoes adapted for different age groups, from beginner shoes prioritizing comfort and

strategies. In digital marketing literature, the definition of a Social Media Influencer (SMI)
encompasses three primary perspectives: audience reach, the role of content creation, and the
concept of the "human brand." At a fundamental level, an SMI is defined as a di gital figure
possessing a massive follower base and an extensive social network across platforms such as
TikTok, Instagram, and YouTube (Albadri, 2023; Goanta & Ranchordás, 2020; Veirman  et

SMIs as content creators who leverage specialized expertise in specific fields such as lifestyle
and health to cultivate interactive communities (Sokolova & Kef i, 2022). This expertise and
interaction serve as the foundation for SMIs to influence their followers' decision-making
processes (Hii & Ong, 2025). Diverging from this functional perspective, (Liang et al., 2025)
expand the SMI concept through a strategic lens by viewing the influencer as a human brand.
From this perspective, an SMI's standing is measured not only by cont ent but also by unique
attributes such as post, communicability, credibility, and word -of-mouth effects that shape
audience perceptions. Synthesizing this literature, the present study defines an SMI as a
specialized content creator who commands a massive digital audience and operates as a human
brand capable of influencing consumer behavior.
In the modern marketing landscape, brand equity is generally understood as the
intangible added value inherent in a product or service beyond its basic utility (Chahal, 2020;
Keller, 2018). From a business performance perspective, this added value is refle cted in
superior marketing outcomes compared to unbranded products (Keller, 2018). The formation
of brand equity relies heavily on consumer cognitive processes, wherein the accumulation of
positive perceptions, strategic associations, and loyalty builds th e brand's credibility and

Customer Brand Engagement (CBE) is widely understood as a multidimensional
construct encompassing the cognitive, emotional/affective, and behavioral engagement of
consumers when interacting with a brand (Matute et al., 2019; Ooi et al., 2022; Pereira et al.,
2024). More than a mere momentary reaction, this engagement reflects active cognitive
processing, strong emotional bonds, and sustained personal participation (Ooi et al., 2022).
From the perspective of service -dominant logic, CBE emerges as a psycholog ical and
behavioral manifestation stemming from the process of shared value creation through resource
integration and service exchange between the consumer and the brand (Ndhlovu & Maree,
2022). This engagement transcends routine transactional relationship s, wherein deep
psychological bonds and active consumer participation in various brand initiatives ultimately
serve as key drivers of long-term loyalty and brand advocacy (Salvietti, 2025). A synthesis of
these perspectives indicates that CBE is a form of multidimensional, interactive engagement
that creates shared value and strengthens long -term relational bonds between consumers and
brands. Far exceeding routine transactional exchanges, CBE reflects a process of shared value
creation and resource integration, typically manifested through conscious attention, enthused
participation, and social connection (Henni & Rufaidah, 2025; Salvietti, 2025 ). A  study
conducted by (Galdón et al., 2024), found that SMI had a positive and significant impact on
customer engagement. This finding is supported by previous research social media influencers

& Aljuhmani , 2025). Research (Ao et al., 2023) SMI traits, which encompass eight

customer engagement. Due to customers' significant activity on social media and their

to engage customers and stimulate buy intention. Consequently, the researchers proposed the
subsequent hypothesis for this study:
H1: Social media influencers have a positive effect on customer brand engagement.
Investigations into the correlation between brand equity and client brand engagement
remain comparatively scarce. Most prior research has investigated the negative link, namely
the impact of customer brand engagement on brand equity. Customer -Based Brand E quity
positively influences customer satisfaction and reputation. Moreover, it yields effects like as

equity. Consequently, the researchers articulated the subsequent hypothesis for this study:
H2: Brand equity has a positive effect on customer brand engagement.

Grounded i n these considerations, this study synthesizes social media influencer (SMI)

brand equity on CBE.
This research offers three distinct contributions to the marketing literature. First, it
enriches consumer engagement theory by disentangling the multidimensional effects of SMI
and brand equity, providing empirical proof that brand equity plays a far more  decisive role
than influencer communications in building long -term customer engagement. Second, it
elevates digital marketing literature by embedding influencer campaigns within a relational
brand equity architecture, recasting influencer tactics from standalone promotional tools into
integrated assets for brand building. Third, by offering empirical insights from an emerging
market context specifically the booming local running shoe segment in Indonesia, this study
strengthens the external validity and co ntextual adaptability of integrated CBE, SMI, BE
models.
METHODS
This study employs a quantitative approach with a causal-explanatory research design
to examine and validate the hypothesized causal relationships among the variables. This
design was selected because the primary objective of the study is to provide an emp irical
confirmation of a theoretically constructed consumer behavior model. Data collection was
carried out utilizing a cross-sectional survey design, enabling the efficient capture of consumer
trends, perspectives, and attitudes at a single point in time.  This approach is deemed highly
appropriate for observing the fast -paced and dynamic nature of social media user behavior.
Furthermore, incorporating an explanatory framework allows for a deeper extraction of
structural paths, ensuring that the underlying predictors of customer interactions are not
merely described but robustly quantified. Consequently, this systemic configuration provides
the necessary analytical rigor to validate complex structural frameworks within contemporary
digital marketing ecosystems.
The target population for this study consists of active consumers and running
enthusiasts in Indonesia. Specifically, users of the local footwear brand, Ortuseight, were
selected as the primary target respondents. The selection of Ortuseight is justified by the fact
that this brand represents one of the most rapidly growing domestic sportswear companies in
the market, which aggressively leverages digital marketing strategies and running influencers
to maintain its competitive edge. Furthermore, the explosive growth of running as a lifestyle
trend among Indonesian youth provides an ideal backdrop for this investigation. By focusing
on this specific demographic, the study captures the behavioral nuances of a highly engaged

A total sample of 202 respondents was successfully gathered for this study.  The
sampling procedure relied on a non -probability convenience sampling technique combined
with a self-administered questionnaire approach. The utilization of convenience sampling was
justified based on practical feasibility and the researcher's ease of ac cess to the running
community within a limited timeframe (Nunan et al., 2020). However, it is explicitly
acknowledged that this non -probability approach entails inherent methodological trade -offs,
particularly regarding restricted generalizability to the broader population of casual footwear
consumers and potential self -selection bias among respondents with stronger initial brand
affinity or higher social media engagement. To mitigate potential sampling bias and maintain
data relevance, two strict screening  criteria (purposive filters) were applied at the beginning
of the questionnaire: (1) respondents must actively follow running influencers on social media,
and (2) respondents must have seen or interacted with Ortuseight promotional content within
the last three months.
The primary data collection process was carried out over a 30-day period using a dual-
channel distribution approach. First, the questionnaires were distributed physically at various
sports hubs and recreational venues, such as running tracks and sports complexes. Second, the
questionnaires were administered digitally via Google Form links, which were shared across
numerous running community groups on social media platforms, including Threads,
Instagram, Facebook, and X. The characteristics of the captured r espondents were then
comprehensively mapped based on their demographic profiles (gender, age, educational

Furthermore, rigorous ethical considerations were maintained throughout the data

Data analysis was executed using SmartPLS  4.0 via Partial Least Squares -Structural
Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM) following the two -stage analytical procedure recommended
by (J. F. J. Hair et al., 2022). PLS-SEM was selected due to the presence of complex second-
order constructs, the primary objective of maximizing explained variance in Customer Brand
Engagement, and its robustness with moderate sample sizes (n = 202) without requiring
multivariate normality. The first evaluation focused on the measurement model (outer model)
to establish the validit y and reliability of the research instrument. At this stage, indicator
reliability was evaluated using outer loadings (≥ 0.708) to ensure that each indicator explained
more than 50% of its construct variance. Internal consistency reliability was subsequent ly
assessed via Cronbach’s Alpha and Composite Reliability (≥ 0.70), indicating that all items
consistently measured the same latent construct without redundancy. Meanwhile, convergent
validity was established through Average Variance Extracted (AVE ≥ 0.50 ), demonstrating
that the latent construct accounted for more than half of its indicators' variance on average.
Finally, discriminant validity was tested using the Fornell -Larcker criterion and cross -
loadings, where the Fornell -Larcker criterion required t he square root of the AVE for each
construct to be greater than its correlations with other constructs, while cross -loadings
required an indicator's loading to be highest on its intended construct. This procedure
confirmed that each construct uniquely shared more variance with its own indicators than with
any other construct in the model.
The second evaluation stage examined the structural model (inner model) to test the
hypothesized relationships and the model's predictive capability. The assessment began with
multicollinearity analysis using Variance Inflation Factor (VIF < 3.0) values to guarantee the
absence of excessive correlation among independent variables, ensuring that path coefficient
estimates were unbiased. A non-parametric bootstrapping algorithm with 5,000 resamples was
then applied to evaluate structural path coefficients (β), where the magnitude and direction of
the coefficients reflected the degree of influence between constructs, while t-statistics (> 1.96)
and -values (< 0,05) determined the statistical significance of the hypotheses. Furthermore,
the model's explanatory p ower was evaluated through the coefficient of determination (R 2),
reflecting the percentage of variance in Customer Brand Engagement explained collectively
by the predictor variables. Effect size (f2) was also calculated to assess the relative contribution
of each predictor variable, categorized as small (0.02), medium (0.15), and large (0.35).
Concluding the structural model assessment, predictive relevance (Q 2 > 0) was evaluated to
confirm the model's out-of-sample predictive accuracy.  Consequently, the application of the
PLS-SEM approach through the evaluation of both outer and inner models proves to be
appropriate and highly relevant for testing the relationships among variables in this study.

A VIF value of 5 or higher indicates a significant collinearity problem. Early signs of

to the criteria, a model is declared ideal and free from multicollinearity constraints if it has a
VIF score below 3. Conversely, the emergence of early indications of collinearity disturbances
should be watched out for if the value is in the range of 3 to 5. Based on the test results in this
study, the VIF values for the first (H1) and second (H2) hypotheses are all below 3. Thus, it
can be concluded that both relationship models are completely safe from collinearity
problems.

CONCLUSIONS
This study concludes that digital marketing strategies via social media influencers
(SMIs) and brand equity simultaneously exert a positive and significant effect on customer
brand engagement (CBE) among Ortuseight running shoe users in Indonesia. Although both
variables demonstrate a positive impact, brand equity exhibits a substantially more dominant
and fundamental direct effect (β = 0.507; p = 0.000) compared to the influence of social media
influencers (β = 0.246; p = 0.007). Multidimensionally, brand equity is most strongly formed
by brand association, brand awareness, brand loyalty, and perceived quality. Meanwhile, the
role of influencers is predominantly reflected by credibility, communicability, post qualit y,
and word -of-mouth. Overall, the integration of brand equity and influencer marketing
accounts for 49.9% of the variance in customer engagement toward the local brand,
demonstrating adequate out-of-sample predictive accuracy (R2 adjusted = 0.499; Q2 = 0.329).
These findings offer significant theoretical implications by enriching the literature on
customer engagement in emerging markets, where brand equity serves as the primary
foundation for long -term relationships, while social media influencers act as short -term
amplifiers. From a managerial perspective, brand managers and marketers are advised against
over-reliance on standalone influencer campaign tactics. Although influencers are effective in
capturing initial conscious attention and driving interaction, su stainable emotional
engagement and customer loyalty ultimately originate from the strength of brand equity
inherent in product reputation and quality.
Despite offering comprehensive insights, this study possesses several methodological
limitations. First, reliance on a non -probability convenience sampling approach limits the

generalizability of the results, as the sample predominantly represents accessible running
community members and may not fully capture the broader population of casual athletic
footwear consumers or non-community runners across different geographic regions. Second,
the self-administered survey design introduces potential self -selection bias, wherein
individuals with stronger brand affinity, higher social media engagement, or greater emotional
involvement with Ortuseight  may have been more motivated to complete the questionnaire
compared to average consumers. Third, the study relies on a cross -sectional survey design
with a sample size of 202 respondents and focuses exclusively on a single brand within the
sportswear industry. To enhance external validity, future research should consider employing
probability sampling designs and expanding the sampling frame to encompass broader
demographic and regional cohorts. Additionally, future studies are encouraged to employ
longitudinal designs to observe the evolution of customer engagement over time, replicate the
model across different product categories or industries, and compare the effectiveness of
various influencer tiers (such as nano/micro-influencers versus macro-influencers).
As a practical recommendation for local brands and Micro, Small, and Medium
Enterprises (MSMEs) operating under budget constraints, resource allocation should prioritize
strengthening brand equity foundations, such as maintaining consistent product quality  and
building a strong brand perception. When leveraging influencer marketing, MSMEs are
advised to shift away from merely pursuing costly macro -influencers for reach toward
strategic partnerships with micro-influencers or local community advocates who possess high
credibility and authenticity. Furthermore, local brands need to integrate digital promotions
with offline community activations (online -to-offline) to foster meaningful and sustained
enthused participation and social connection among consumers.
