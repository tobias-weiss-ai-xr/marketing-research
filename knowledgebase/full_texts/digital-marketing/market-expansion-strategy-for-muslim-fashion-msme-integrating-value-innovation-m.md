Abstract:        Rapid expansion of the Mus lim fashion industry has led to fierce competition
an create a red ocean market for M SMEs across Java Isl and. This study
investigates how V alue Innovation and Market Positioning drive Market
Expansion while evaluating the mediating role of Digital Marketing Capability.
In this intense digital lands cape, small busin esses must  adopt efficient,
innovation-driven marketing approaches to survive. By using quantitative
explanatory method, data were gathered through a cross sectional survey of 220
respondents selected via purposive s ampling. The conceptual model tested
using Partial Least Squares Structural Equ ation Modeling (PLS-SEM) in
SmartPLS. The empirical results demonstrate that both Value Innovation and
Market Positioning exert a direct, positive, and significant influence on Digital
Marketing Capability as well as Market Expansion. Also, the analysis indicates
that Digital Marketing C apability serves as a partial medi ator in this
relationship. These insights emphasize that digital capability is far more than an
operational tool, it acts as a strategic catalyst that successfully translates product
innovation and brand image in to actual business growth. To sus tain a
competitive advantage, MSMEs are highly encouraged to continuously upgrade
their digital services, standardize promotional efforts, and invest in data
analytics literacy.

Local business has to shift toward fresh strategies rooted in the Value Innovation principles
of the blue ocean strategy to escape this destructive pricing spiral (Kim & Mauborgne, 2005).
Rather than competing directly within saturated spaces, entrepreneurs should focus on carving
out entirely new, un contested market territories. Value Inno vation directly disrupts the
conventional tradeoff between differentiation and low cost. Instead, firms are urged to deliver
novel buyer value while keeping operational overhead minimal. For capital constrained small
firms, Latifi et al. (2021) suggest three practical approaches. The first is value capturing, which
pairs original product designs with highly responsive digital customer support. Second is value
delivery, a step where brands collaborate with distribution channels to target overlooked online
segments. Lastly, value creation focuses on building affiliate networks that offer flexible,
profitable pricing models. By supplying apparel that is both distinctive and budget friendly,
merchants can seamlessly attract new demographics. Once a standout product is developed,
anchoring the brand identity through a solid Market Positioning str ategy b ecomes the next
essential phase (Kotler & Keller, 2021). This deliberate effort aims to embed the product’s core
image and value deeply within the consumer's mind. Based on the insights of Tolstoy et al.
(2021) and Makarim et al. (2026), positioning breaks down into three distinct elements. Brand
differentiation stands as the first, emphasizing unique visual  aesthetics, stitching quality,
attractive packaging, and compelling brand storytelling. Next comes positioning consistency,
which demands strict alignment of marketing campaigns across every digital storefront. The
third element involves a niche market focus, allowing small businesses to bypass generalized
price competition by dir ecting m arketing str eams toward sp ecialized audience s egments.
Consequently, aligning product differentiation with structured positioning facilitates scalable
customer acquisition.

2.1 Population, Sample, and Sampling Technique
This study centers on Java-based Muslim fashion entrepreneurs who actively leverage
digital marketing. Given the lack of official registries tracking these specific businesses, we
treated the study  group as an infinite population.  Participants were s elected via pur posive
sampling, filtering for individuals who run a Java-based enterprise, have used digital platforms
for a minimum of six months, and serve as either the owner or marketing manager. Screening
questions at the start of the online questionnaire filtered out ineligible respondents on the spot.
To determine sample size, we applied the PLS-SEM rule of thumb from Hair et al. (2019), which
suggests multiplying the number of research indicators by a factor of five to ten. With 22 distinct
indicators in this model, the baseline target was set at 220 participants, a figure successfully met
during data collection with exactly 220 valid responses.

2.3 Data Analysis Technique
Data analysis in this research employs Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling
(PLS-SEM), executed through SmartPLS software. This technique is highly suited for complex
structural frameworks with mediating effects, as it accommodates no n-normal data and
emphasizes predictive capability (Hair et al., 2019; Sarstedt et al., 2020). The evaluation
follows a two-tier process. The analysis begins with assessing the outer model to ensure
measurement validity and reliability and this requires outer loadings exceeding 0.70, an AVE
above 0.50, an HTMT ratio under 0.90 for discriminant validity, alongside Cronbach's Alpha
and Composite Reliability scores above 0.70. Subsequently, the inner model is evaluated using
the coefficient of determination (R²), predictive relevance (Q²) via blindfolding, and effect
size (f²). To test the hypotheses, a bootstrapping routine was implemented, applying a critical
threshold of a T-statistic over 1.96 and a P-value under 0.05. Lastly, the mediating function of
Digital Marketing Capability was quantified through the Variance Accounted For (VAF)
approach (Hair et al., 2019), classif ying mediation as non -existent (under 20%), partial
(between 20% and 80%), or full (exceeding 80%).

3.2 Discussion
Statistical evaluation of the fi rst hypothesis confirms that Value Inno vation exerts a
positive and significant effect on Digital Marketing Capability. This outcome supports the
premise that firm-level value creation requires the optimal integration of digital technologies
to effectively communicate competitive advantages to the public (Hajar et al., 2022; Khin &
Ho, 2019). From the Resource -Based View (R BV) perspective, Value Innovation can be
regarded as a strategic intangible resource because it enables MSMEs to create distinctive
customer value through new products, services, and cost -efficient value configurations.
However, the possession of such resources alone does not automatically generate competitive
advantages. Their value depends on the firm's ability to effectively deploy and organize them
through relevant organizational capabilities. In this context, Digital Marketing Capability
represents an organizational capability that enables Muslim fashion MSMEs to translate the
value created through innovation into effective digital content, customer engagement, and
targeted online promotion. Therefore, the positive and significant effect of Value Innova tion
on Digital Marketing Capability indicates that MSMEs with stronger innovation -oriented
resources are more likely to develop and utilize digital marketing capabilities to communicate
and commercialize their distinctive value. This finding reinforces th e RBV argument that
competitive advantage depends not only on possessing valuable resources but also on the firm's
ability to effectively leverage those resources through organizational capabilities. Furthermore,
these findings align with the dynamic capability’s theory (Latifi et al., 2021), which suggests
that business model adaptations directly drive technology adoption. In this study, because many
micro-enterprise respondents are actively involved in digital marketing, highly innovative
products offer them tangible leverage for on line content creation, optimizing promotional
efficiency despite severe capital constraints. Similarly, our analysis v alidates the second
hypothesis, d emonstrating that  Market Positioning significantly driv es Digital M arketing
Capability. This link underscores how pre cise market targeting relies heavily on digital

Research Novelty
While prior literature often evaluates Value Innovation, Market Positioning, and Digital
Marketing Capability as isolated constructs (Purwanti et al., 2022; Jung & Shegai, 2023), this
study establishes its novelty through three distinct contributions. First, by consolidating these
variable into an integrated mediation framework, the analysis demonstrates that digital
capability exerts a more dominant influence on Market Expansion than isolated produ ct or
brand strategies. Second, focusing on Muslim fashion micro and small enterprises (MSEs)
provides a unique research context characterized by interwoven religious values, rapid trend
cycles, and hyper competitive market dynamics. This sectoral focus of fers a fresh empirical
application of the Resource -Based View (RBV) theory within Indonesia's creative economy.
The study concludes by demonstrating that a subjective, reflective approach to operationalize
Market Expansion offers a reliable alternative for measuring MSE performance. This approach
effectively mitigates the analytical biases associated with traditional financial indicators, which
are often distorted due to the widespread absence of standardized accounting practices among
small-scale operators.

4. Conclusion
In conclusion, the empirical findings confirm that Value Innovation and Market Positioning
significantly str engthen Digital M arketing C apability, ul timately dri ving su ccessful Market
Expansion for Java-based Muslim fashion enterprises. While both independent variables exert
a dir ect impact on expansion, their contributions are ma rginal compared to the indirect
mediation pathways. The findings demonstrate that digital capability functions as a critical
strategic catalyst and a p artial mediator, transforming foundational product innovation and
brand positioning into tangible ma rket growth amid intense competition. From a theoretical
standpoint, these ins ights enrich the Resource-Based View (RBV) literature by validating
digital capability as an essential str ategic asset for small-scale operators. From a practical
standpoint, entrepreneurs should balance unique prod uct development with competitive
pricing, maintain br and consistency, and upgr ade d ata analytical skills to survive s aturated
domestic markets and avoid destructive price wars. In addition to this point, policymakers and
mentoring institutions can leverage these  findings to d esign targeted digi tal literacy and
marketing training initiatives tailored for micro-enterprises.
Despite these insights, several limitations warrant consideration for future research. The
cross- sectional design constrains the ability to observe strategic shifts over time, and relying
on self- reported survey data introduces potential subjectivity bias. In the terms of geographic
scope, the sample is restricted to participants within Java. Furthermore, the structural model
leaves approximately 57.4% of the variance in Market Expansion unexplained, given that the
combination of predictors accounts for 42.6%  of the shifts. Future s tudies should adopt a
longitudinal approach to capture temporal dynamics and combine perceptual metrics with
objective digital performance data. Expanding the demographic scope beyond Java would also
enhance generalizability. Concludingly, incorporating alternative predictors, such as capital
availability and formalized Customer R elationship Management (CRM) pr actices, could
significantly improve the framework's overall explanatory power.
