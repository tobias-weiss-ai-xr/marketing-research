ABSTRACT

The packaged drinking water industry in Indonesia faces increasingly intense competition,
and PT XYZ, a demineralized water producer, has repeatedly failed to meet its sales volume
targets during the 2024–2026 period, with shortfalls reaching 37.48%. This issue motivated
a study on the influence of product quality and pri ce on sales volume, grounded in the
theoretical frameworks of product quality by Kotler and Keller (2022), pricing by Tjiptono
and Chandra (2021), and sales volume by Swastha (2022). A quantitative approach was
applied using total sampling involving 70 consumers as respondents. Data were collected
through Likert-scale questionnaires and company documentation, then tested for validity and
reliability before being analyzed using classical assumption tests, multiple linear regression,
t-tests, F-tests, and the coefficient of determination via SPSS 27. The findings reveal that
product quality (β = 0.631) and price (β = 0.498) each have a positive and significant effect
on sales volume, both p artially and simultaneously (F =  135.226; sig. 0.000), with an
Adjusted R² of 79.6%. The  study concludes that enhancing product quality alongside
implementing competitive pricing is a key strategy for sustainably increasing sales volume
of demineralized water.

INTRODUCTION

The bottled drinking water industry in Indonesia continues to show significant growth
as public awareness of the importance of consuming clean and healthy water increases. The
Central Statistics Agency (Badan Pusat Statistik, 2023) recorded that the packaged beverage
industry, including bottled water, is one of the processing industry subsectors that has grown
consistently in recent years, which has encouraged the emergence of  new producers and
intensified market competition. This condition requires every company to have an effective
marketing strategy to maintain its existence and sustainably increase its sales volume, given
that sales volume is directly related to revenue achievement and business continuity.
PT XYZ, a demineralized water producer, faces the problem of repeatedly failing to
meet its sales volume targets during the 2024–2026 period, with a shortfall reaching 37.48%
of the s et target. Th is uns table sales fluctuation ind icates a fundamental prob lem in the
company's marketing strategy, particularly related to two factors that are theoretically and
empirically proven to significantly influence sales volume: product quality and price.
In terms of product quality, technical data show that PT XYZ's demineralized water
has a very high purity level (Total Dissolved Solids of 0 ppm, purity of 99.9%) produced

effect on s ales volu me, while Wulandari and Kusumawati (2022) demonstrated the

segmentation, and pricing structures compared to conventional bottled water—remains very
limited, thus constituting a research gap addressed by this study.
Based on the above description, this study aims to determine and analyze the influence
of product quality and price, both partially and simultaneously, on the sales volume of
demineralized water at PT XYZ. This study is expected to provide an academic contribution
to the development of marketing management science, as well as practical recommendations
for the company in formulating more effective product quality and pricing str ategies to
encourage increased sales volume.

LITERATURE REVIEW

Product Quality
Various  experts  have proposed  definitions  of product  quality  from  diverse yet
complementary p erspectives. Prakosa and Tjahjaningsih (2021), as cited in Novelia and
Susanti (2026, p. 4), emphasize product quality as the ability of a product to perform its
functions, such as durability, reliability, precision, and ease of use. Philip Kotler and Kevin
Lane Keller (2022), as cited in the same source, extend this meaning by adding elements of
innovation and added value perceived by customers. Mursidi et al. (2020), as cited in Wijaya
et al. (2024, p. 1436), view product quality as something that has value in the target market
and is able to provide benefits and satisfaction. Ely (2021), as cited in Hilam (2024, p. 9),
explains that product quality is the totality of characteristics that enable a product to meet
needs, both stated and implied, while Sinulingga (2021), as cited in the same source, adds
that product quality relates to a company's efforts to maintain elements considered important

of these views, product quality in this study is understood as a product's ability to perform
its basic functions optimally, supported by superior characteristics, innovation, and added
value that provide satisfaction and meet consumer expectations, both explicit and implicit.
Based on the theoretical review above, several factors influence product quality, including:
(1) the production process and technology used, such as the combination of distillation,
reverse osmosis, deionization, and UV disinfection, which determines the product's purity
level; (2) the raw materials or water sources used in production; (3) packaging design and
durability, which affect ease of use and product protection during storage; (4) consistency in
applying quality and safety standards in accordance with applicable regulations, such as SNI
and BPOM; and (5) brand reputation and image, which shape consumer perceptions of
quality, as emphasized by Zeithaml, Bitner, and Gremler (2023, p. 79).
According to Kotler and Keller (2009), as cited in Sari and Avriyanti (2023,
pp.1283–1284), there are s ix indicators for measuring product quality: performance,
reflecting the b asic benefits of t he prod uct; features, referring to the completeness of
supporting facilities such as packaging design; reliability, reflected in the quality of the raw
materials used; conformance to specifications, relating to compliance with established safety
standards; durability, concerning the product's lifespan and shelf life; and perceived quality,
reflecting the brand image in the eyes of consumers.

Price
Price is an amount of value that consumers must give up to obtain a product or
service, which essentially reflects an exchange process between what is sacrificed and what
is received. Kotl er, Keller, and Chernev (2022), as cited in Wulansari (2025, p. 964),
emphasize that price relates to the comparison between perceived benefits and the sacrifice
incurred, making perceived value the primary determinant of consumer response. Cartwright
et al. (2022), as cited in Piyoh, Rahayu, and Dirgantari (2024, p. 2), highlight the suitability
of price with purchasing power, while Riyono and Budiharja (2020), as cited in Salsabilla
and Fasa (2024, p. 7704), regard price as a medium of exchange for obtaining goods or
services. Tjiptono (2015), as cited in Sabela (2023, p. 19), adds that price is a monetary unit
or other measure used to obtain the right of ownership or use of a product. Based on these
various views, price in this study is synthesized as an amount of sacrifice, in monetary or
other terms, that consumers must incur to obtain a product, reflecting a balance between the
benefits received, purchasing power, and value perceived by consumers.
Factors that influence a  product's pricing include: (1) prod uction costs that the
company must cover; (2) prices set by competitors offering similar products; (3) consumer
purchasing power and perceived value of the  product's benefits; and (4) market demand
conditions and consumer price sensitivity, which, according to R ahmawati (2023, p. 201),
tends to increase amid intensifying market competition.
According to  Kotler and Armstrong (201 9), as cited in  Oktaviani and Sumaryanto
(2024, pp. 188–189), pri ce indicators consist of price affordability, which is a consumer
expectation prior to purchase; price-quality conformity, reflecting the alignment between
price and the q uality received; price competitiveness relative to  similar products in the
market; and price-benefit conformity, reflecting how well the price matches the benefits
consumers perceive from the product.

RESEARCH METHODOLOGY

This study utilized both primary and secondary data. Primary d ata were collected
directly from consumers of PT XYZ through the distribution of structured questionnaires.
Secondary data were obtained from company documents, including sales volume records,
sales targets for the 2024–2026 period, product price lists, and documents related to product
quality, which were used to support the research analysis. The research was conducted at PT
XYZ, located at The Hive Tanamera, Waterfront River Drive, Hegarmukti, Cikarang Pusat
District, Bekasi Regency, West Java, Indonesia. The study was carried out from February to
June 2026,  covering preliminary obs ervation, proposal preparation, questionnaire
distribution, data processing, and research report preparation. The population of this study
consisted of all consumers of d emineralized water prod ucts at PT XYZ, totaling 70
respondents. Since the  population size w as relatively small, a non-probability sampling
technique with  a total  sampling  method  was  employed,  allowing  all  members of  the
population to be included as research respondents. Therefore, the sample consisted of 70
respondents. Data were collected using questionnaires and documentation. The
questionnaire employed a fiv e-point Likert scale ranging from Strongly Disagree ( 1) to
Strongly Agree (5) to measure respondents' perceptions of product quality, price, and sales
volume. Documentation techniques were also used to obtain supporting data, including sales
reports, sales targets, product price l ists, and other company documents relevant to the
research objectives. Data were analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics v ersion 27. The
analytical procedures included instrument testing through validity and r eliability tests,
descriptive statistical analysis, and classical assumption tests consisting of  normality,
multicollinearity, and heteroscedasticity tests. Furthermore, multiple linear regression
analysis was employed to examine the  influence of  product quality and price on  sales
volume. Hypothesis testing was conducted using the partial (t-test) and simultaneous (F-
test), while the coefficient of determination (Adjusted R²) was used to determine the
proportion of variance in sales volume explained by the independent variables.

DISCUSSION

Overall, the test results show that Product Quality has a positive and significant effect
on Sales Volume (t-value 11.234;  sig. 0.000; β = 0.631 ), Price also has a posit ive and
significant effect (t-value 8.870; sig. 0.000; β = 0.498), and both variables simultaneously
have a posit ive and significant effect on Sales Volume ( F-value 135 .226; sig. 0.000),
contributing 79.6% (Adjusted R²). Interestingly, this positive effect occurs even though the
descriptive responses for Price (mean 1.89–2.20) and Sales Volume (mean 2.31–2.86) are
relatively low, while Product Quality receives a very favorable assessment (mean 4.30–
4.76). This pattern indicates that superior product quality has not yet been able to fully
lift sales  volume,  as  it  remains  constrained by  a less  competitive price perception  and
a distribution system that is not yet optimal.
The finding  regarding the posit ive effect of Product Quality on Sales Volume is
consistent with the theory of Kotler and Keller (2022), which states that product quality
relates to a product's ability to perform its functions, including reliability, durability, and the
added value perceived by consumers, thereby driving purchasing decisions and increased
sales. This is reinforced by the view of  Tjiptono and Chandra (2021) that products which
meet or exceed consumer expectations create satisfaction, which in turn affects loyalty and
purchase frequency. Meanwhile, the positive effect of Price on Sales Volume is consistent
with the definition of Tjiptono and Chandra (2021) that price is an amount of money that
carries a certain utility for obtaining a product, in which appropriate pricing must consider
purchasing power and consumer-perceived value. Nevertheless, the low descriptive scores
on the Price indicators suggest that the application of this theory in the field is not yet fully
optimal, as the price set by PT XYZ is not yet perceived as commensurate with consumer
purchasing power and value expectations. The simultaneous effect of both variables is
consistent with the marketing mix theory of Kotler and Keller (2022) and Tjiptono and
Chandra (2021), which emphasizes that product quality and price are two complementary

significant  effect  on  sales  volume in  bottled  water companies,  and  is  consistent  with
Novitasari and Samari (2021), who found that various marketing factors significantly affect
sales volume opt imization. However, the  finding of a posit ive effect of Price on  Sales
Volume differs in direction from the results of Pratiwi and Haryono (2022), who found a
significant negative effect of price on  sales volume in  bottled water companies. This
difference indicates that consumer responses to price are context-dependent, varying with
market  segmentation,  product  characteristics  (demineralized  water versus  conventional
bottled water), and the degree of competitor benchmarking. The si multaneous effect of
Product Quality and Price is in line with the findings of Wulandari and Kusumawati (2022),
who showed that both variables jointly influence the purchasing decisions of bottled water
consumers in  Indonesia, and is consistent with Novitasari and Samari (2021), who
demonstrated the simultaneous effect of various marketing factors on sales volume. The
alignment of these find ings reinforces the empirical basis that sales volume improvement
strategies need to be m anaged through multiple factors rather than focusing on a sing le
variable alone.

CONCLUSION

Based on the results of the analysis and discussion, it can be concluded that Product
Quality has a positive and significant effect on the sales volume of demineralized water at
PT XYZ, as evidenced by a calculated t-value of 11.234 > table t-value of 1.9 9601 and
significance of 0.000 < 0.05, with a regression coefficient of 0.403 and a Beta of 0.631 as
the most dominant variable, although the mean score for the ease-of-carrying-and-using-
packaging indicator (4.30) remains relatively the lowest among the p roduct quality
indicators.
Price also has a positive and significant effect on Sales Volume, as evidenced by a
calculated t-value of 8.870 > table t-value of 1.99601 and significance of 0.000 < 0.05, with
a regression coefficient of 0.252 and a Beta of 0.498. However, the mean scores for the Price
indicators remain low (1.89–2.20), with the lowest score on price attractiveness relative to
competitors (1.89), indicating that PT XYZ's price is not yet perceived as competitive by
consumers.
Simultaneously, Product Quality and Price have a positive and significant effect on
Sales Volume, as evidenced by a calculated F-value of 135.226 > table F-value of 3.13 and
significance of 0.000 < 0.05, with an explanatory power (Adjusted R²) of 79.6%, while the
remaining 20.4% is explained by other variables outside this study, such as promotion and
distribution. Therefore, improving prod uct quality alongside i mplementing more
competitive pricing and enhancing distribution smoothness constitute key strategies for PT
XYZ in sustainably driving growth in demineralized water sales volume.
