Abstract
This study aims to analyze the marketing communication strategies of the @tumurunmuseum Instagram account in fostering the intention
to visit the Tumurun Private Museum in Solo. The main issue underlying this study is the  stigma of exclusivity often associated with
private museums, in addition to the limited literature on how the richness of media is strategically utilized by cultural institutions to reach
a wider audience. This study employs a descriptive qualitative method using a case study approach. Data were collected through in-depth
interviews with six informants comprising four members of the museum’s internal management staff and two active followers of the
@tumurunmuseum Instagram account, supported by direct observation and documentation of digital  content from December 2025 to
February 2026. Data  analysis followed the  interactive  model  by  Miles  and Huberman,  with the  application  of  source and  method
triangulation to ensure the validity of the findings. The research results reveal three main findings. First, the use of inclusive language in
photo captions effectively reduces ambiguity and the stigma of exclusivity associated with private museums by optimizing the linguistic
variation dimension in Media Richness Theory. Second, Instagram Reels serve as the primary conversion factor that transforms  digital
interest into a decision to visit in person through a dual-signal mechanism. Third, collaborative content management based on shared
values without written Standard Operating Procedures proved capable of maintaining brand identity cohesion, although this introduced
vulnerability to the risk of personnel turnover. This study confirms that the integration of Media Richness Theory and the Circular Social
Media Model  constitutes a relevant  and comprehensive analytical  framework  for examining  the digital marketing communication
strategies of cultural institutions in the era of social media.
Keywords: Instagram, marketing communication, Media Richness Theory, private museum, visit intention
Introduction
Museum marketing communication  today  goes  beyond simply  promoting  collections,  shifting toward  the  creation  of
symbolic experiences that shape the audience’s perception  of cultural spaces. Emilia  & Sumarlan (2025) emphasize that
visual elements,  narratives, and digital interactions on Instagram  function  as symbols  of communication  that construct
collective meaning. This interactive process influences the public’s assessment of a museum’s image and educational value,
a point reinforced by Zingone (2024), who asserts that the audience’s interpretation of digital content
strategically drives the intention  to make a physical  visit.  Recuero Virto et al.  (2024)  further solidify this relationship,
revealing through a study of 956 Instagram users that the cognitive and affective reactions triggered by the platform’s
content significantly shape behavioral responses, including the intention to visit renowned museums. These three studies
consistently confirm that digital content influences visitation intent, but they have not specifically elucidated the messaging
mechanisms that make such content effective an analytical gap that becomes increasingly crucial in the context of private
museums, which face different perceptual barriers than well-established public museums.
Technological evolution  has  fundamentally  redefined  how  organizations  interact  with  their  audiences.  Unlike
traditional, transaction-oriented marketing models,  social  media offers  high  efficiency  and  global  reach  without
geographical barriers, transforming communication from one-way information delivery into an interactive dialogue space.
Kaplan and Haenlein (2010)  define social media  as a group  of internet-based applications built on the ideological  and
technological foundations of Web  2.0, which  enable the  creation and exchange  of  user-generated content, thereby
fundamentally transforming  the  relationship  between organizations  and  their  audiences.  In  this context, Instagram  has
emerged as a crucial  platform whose visual characteristics allow for the delivery of aesthetic and persuasive messages,
thereby enabling museums to build more participatory and meaningful relationships with modern society.

The ambiguity in interpreting visual narratives especially when the general public is confronted with complex art
terminology presents a communication challenge that cannot be adequately explaine d solely through approaches focused
on media usage motives, such as Uses and Gratifications Theory, or on the processing pathways of persuasive messages,
such as the Elaboration Likelihood Model. Both of these perspectives place greater emphasis on the recipient’s side namely,
the audience’s motivations and cognitive processing but fail to adequately explain how
the technical characteristics of a communication channel its capacity to convey cues, the speed of feedback, and linguistic
variation also determine the clarity of the message itself. On that basis, this study adopts Media Richness Theory (MRT)
as its primary analytical framework, as this theory specifically focuses on the alignment between a medium’s capabilities
and the level of message complexity  a direct relevance to the challenges faced by private museums in translating esoteric
contemporary art discourse into narratives understandable to the general public. According to Daft and Lengel (1986),
effective communication is achieved when media richness aligns with the level of task uncertainty. In the museum context,
Instagram provides a variety of cues and instant feedback that offer a more realistic representation of art. Setyaningsih et
al. (2024) further assert that “rich” media can transform raw information into deep understanding, engaging both cognitive
and affective dimensions to foster visitation intent. Ocon et al. (2024), in their analysis of European museums’ Instagram
strategies, reinforce this view by demonstrating that actively listening to and interacting with user -generated content is a
key component in building a museum’s reputation in the digital space. However, these findings generally stop at identifying
correlations between digital activities and reputation, without elaborating on the mechanisms at the message level that
explain why such correlations occur it is this analytical gap that this study seeks to bridge through an MRT-based analysis.
This study builds upon the research by Emilia and Sumarlan (2025) on Instagram strategies at the Muhammadiyah
Museum, which employed a qualitative descriptive approach to map the application of the Circular Social Media Model
Share, Optimize, Manage, and Engage in the muse um’s content management. That study effectively documented the
operational stages of account management but did not explain why and how the characteristics of messages at each stage
influence the audience’s perception of the institution  a question that ind eed falls outside the scope of the circular model
itself, as it is designed to map the flow of the communication process, not to assess the alignment between the richness of
the message and the complexity of the information conveyed. A similar limitation i s evident in several other studies of
museum Instagram accounts that rely solely on the Social Media Circular Model (Triana, Hidayah, & Noerdjamal, 2021;
Nurcahyani et al., 2026): their focus tends to be descriptive-operational and does not address the psychological dimensions
of audience reception of the messages. It is this gap that underpins the integration of two theoretical frameworks in this
study: the Circular Social Media Model is used to map the content management process, while Media Richness Theo ry is
used to explain the underlying message mechanisms that drive the effectiveness of this process in reducing the stigma of
exclusivity. Thus, this study expands the “share, optimize, manage, and engage” framework applied to the
@tumurunmuseum account not merely as a description of stages, but as an analytical structure in which the appropriateness
of media richness is tested at each stage, thereby moving beyond technical metrics toward a more substantial understanding
of the mechanisms of communication strategies.
Unlike public museums, private museums often face psychological barriers in the form of the stigma of exclusivity.
This perceptual barrier generally arises from inappropriate communication patterns, not from institutional policies. This
study fills a gap in the literature by positioning social media not merely as a technical channel, but as an instrument for
deconstructing psychological barriers. Through an “inclusive language” strategy, private museums can shift their narrative
from merely announcing to actively inviting, thereby creating an emancipatory dialogue that meaningfully bridges the gap
in public perception.
This study aims to analyze the marketing communication strategies of the @tumurunmuseum Instagram account in
building visit intent through the integration of the Social Media Circular Model and the Media Richness Theory perspective.
Using a descriptive qualitative method, this research focuses on how these strategies are systematically applied to attract
audiences.

Literature Review

Media Richness Theory

Media Richness Theory (MRT), developed by Daft and Lengel (1986), focuses on the compatibility of media with message
complexity to reduce audience confusion. A medium is considered “rich” if it can effectively convey compl ex messages
through four main dimensions, which can be simply interpreted as follows:
a. Feedback Speed: This dimension relates to how quickly two -way communication occurs. For the layperson, this
means the ease with which the audience receives an instant re sponse from the administrator, so that information
uncertainty can be resolved immediately.
b. Diverse Signals: This dimension involves the simultaneous use of various types of communication signals.
Essentially, the audience receives information not only th rough text but also through a combination of images,
video movements, and sound that create a more vivid atmosphere.

  3852
c. Linguistic Diversity: This dimension refers to a medium’s ability to employ diverse linguistic styles. Simply put,
a rich medium can translate complex artistic terminology into a casual and inclusive narrative that is easily
understood by the general public without losing its original meaning.
d. Personal Focus: This dimension is a medium’s ability to create message s that feel personal to individuals. This
means the audience feels as though they are being spoken to directly for example, through direct interaction or
recognition of content created by users thereby building an emotional connection.
In the digital ecosystem, Instagram is classified as rich media because its integrated features can fulfill all four dimensions
simultaneously. Its interactive and visual capabilities have proven effective in building emotional bonds (Setyaningsih,
Hakim, & Romli, 2024; Ratih, Putra, & Wajdi, 2025). This is reinforced by Zingone (2024), who states that museums that
optimize visual features are far more successful in increasing audience engagement compared to static media. Hasim et al.
(2020) add that the higher the media richness of a given medium, the better the dissemination of information, because richer
media reduce a mbiguity and uncertainty in the communication process, which directly increases the recipient’s level of
understanding.
This study applied MRT to the @tumuru nmuseum account (December 2025 – February 2026) using four indicators: the
admin’s interactive response patterns (speed of feedback), the diversity of content formats such as Reels and Carousels
(variety of cues), the variation in language style and storyt elling in captions (linguistic diversity), and content
personalization through the incorporation of user -generated content (personal focus). This analysis focuses exclusively on
the Instagram platform to explore the effectiveness of its visual communicatio n strategies.

The Social Media Circular Model

The Circular Social Media Model, introduced by Regina Luttrell (2019), is a strategic framework in digital public relations
that emphasizes dynamic and iterative communication processes. Unlike linear mod els, this circular model enables the
formation of meaningful relationships between organizations and communities through structured two -way interactions
(Zaesar & Saidah, n.d.; Qurniawati, Indriastuti, & Nurohman, 2025). This model consists of four stages in a cycle (Luttrell,
2019):
a. Share: Strategic distribution of content to reach relevant audiences through the selection of appropriate narratives
and timing.
b. Optimize: The process of increasing content visibility through the use of hashtags, visual aesth etics, and
performance data analysis.
c. Manage: Consistent account management, including content scheduling and maintaining a cohesive brand
identity.
d. Engage: Efforts to build active two -way interaction with the audience by responding to comments and creat ing
participatory content. The evaluation results from the Engage stage will inform the next Share cycle, ensuring the
content strategy continues to adapt (Emilia & Sumarlan, 2025).
In this study, the model was applied to the @tumurunmuseum account using indicators such as post themes and frequency
(Share), the effectiveness of algorithmic reach (Optimize), the team’s internal planning system (Manage), and the depth of
audience interaction (Engage) (Triana, Hidayah, & Noerdjamal, 2021).
The synergy between Media Richness Theory (MRT) and the Circular Social Media Model creates a holistic analytical
foundation: MRT unpacks the qualities of Instagram as an emotionally rich medium, while the circular model systematically
maps its operationalization. The integ ration of these two frameworks enables a comprehensive analysis of
@tumurunmuseum’s strategy in fostering audience interest in visiting the museum (Setyaningsih, Hakim, & Romli, 2024;
Emilia & Sumarlan, 2025).

Marketing Communication

Marketing communication is a strategic process for conveying an organization’s messages to build awareness, influence
attitudes, and drive behavioral change among the audience. For cultural institutions, this practice goes beyond mere visitor
numbers; it e ncompasses the shaping of public image, values, and experiences through adaptive digital strategies
(Nurcahyani et al., 2026). As educational institutions, museums are required to strategically communicate their identity
and collections to remain relevant to modern audiences, particularly younger generations. In this regard, Lopes et al.
(2024), who studied Instagram strategies at five major European museums, found that the value of actively listening to
and engaging with user -generated content serves as a key component in building reputation and image, reflecting the
centrality of two-way communication in cultural marketing. Optimizing platforms such as Instagram @tumurunmuseum
has proven effective in strengthening the museum’s position as an interactive cu ltural destination while tangibly
stimulating visitor interest (Aurellia & Bachtiar, 2025).
The digital media revolution has transformed marketing communication from a one -way model into a dynamic
and interactive dialogue. Social media now enables institutions not only to disseminate information but also to engage in
meaningful conversations with their audiences, as underscored by the findings of Lopes et al. (2024) cited above regarding
the centrality of two -way communication in cultural marketing.  Therefore, today’s museum marketing communication
strategies

must now be able to integrate promotional narratives with genuine social interactions to foster more substantial public
engagement.

Research Methodology
This study employs a descriptive qua litative method with a case study approach to explore the marketing communication
strategies of @tumurunmuseum in depth and comprehensively (Kriyantono, 2014; Sagiyanto & Sulfiah, 2020). This
approach presents data in narrative and visual forms to enrich u nderstanding of the phenomenon (Nugrahani, 2014).
Through a constructivist paradigm, the researchers sought to reconstruct the meaning behind the management of the
@tumurunmuseum Instagram account, which now has 54,000 followers.
Data were collected using  purposive sampling to obtain representative and rich information (Sugiyono, 2020). The data
collection process began with direct observation and documentation of the digital content on the @tumurunmuseum
Instagram account from December 2025 to February 20 26, to map the patterns of visual and textual communication that
naturally emerged in the posts. In-depth interviews with internal stakeholders namely the Museum Manager, Social Media
Specialist, Photographer/Videographer, and Public Relations Staff as wel l as external stakeholders consisting of active
followers, were subsequently conducted as a confirmatory step to validate and deepen the researcher’s interpretation of the
media observation results. Thus, the interviews were not treated as a standalone dat a source but rather as a triangulation
tool to test the alignment between the patterns observed in the digital space and the meanings intended by the museum
management as well as those perceived by the audience. Secondary data included documents, photos, a nd the museum ’s
monthly insights reports.
The analysis process follows an interactive model that includes data reduction, presentation, and drawing conclusions
(Miles & Huberman in Mentari, 2021). To ensure the validity and consistency of the findings, the researcher applied source
triangulation by comparing the perspectives of management and the audience, as well as methodological triangulation by
cross-checking the results of interviews, observations, and written documentation (Alfansyur, 2020).

Results and Discussion
Reducing the Ambiguity of Private Museums Through Inclusive Language
Research findings indicate that @tumurunmuseum strategically utilizes Instagram to deconstruct the stigma of exclusivity
associated with private museums. The management employs an inclusive and casual style of language.
to transform the institution’s image into one that is more open and welcoming to everyone. This strategy is implemented
by simplifying complex art terminology into everyday language without compromising commun ication ethics. The
manager of the Tumurun Private Museum reaffirms this vision.

  3854
“We’re also building an image of ourselves as a warm, contemporary, and inclusive private museum
that offers a relaxed learning experience, so it doesn’t come across as stuffy. Through the narratives we
create, we hope the public will see Tumurun as an accessible, comfortable, and engaging art space.”
(A1, Manager of the Tumurun Private Museum).
This claim of inclusivity is confirmed by visitors through their firsthand experiences. Source B1 acknowledged that the
museum’s digital communication successfully alleviated doubts about the accessibility of the public space: “At first I
wondered, ‘Is this for the general public?’, but the content clearly shows that this museum is open to everyone. Although it
looks exclusive, the way they communicate feels friendly and not intimidating” (B1, Instagram follower of
@tumurunmuseum).
Informant B2 highlighted  the effectiveness of the word choice in the photo captions: “The photo captions are
informative but not complicated. I felt invited to understand, not lectured to. The exclusivity has more to do with quality
than with something that makes people reluctant to approach” (B2, Instagram follower of @tumurunmuseum).
The statement “invited to understand, not lectured to” theoretically reflects the success of the Language Variation
dimension in Media Richness Theory, as is also evident in the practice of writing  museum photo captions for example, in
posts that translate curatorial terms such as “installation” or “found object” into everyday narrative sentences, as in the

This mechanism for reducing the stigma of exclusivity can be explained further: the inherent complexity of contemporary
art terminology creates a cognitive distance between the institution and the general public, and it is this distance that is
often psychologically perceived as exclusivity. By reducing the linguistic complexity of the message without sacrificing
the substance of the information, museums effectively align the  richness of linguistic codes with the general audience’s
interpretive capacity, thereby reducing semantic ambiguity and, consequently, the impression of elitism. This interpretation
expands upon the argument by Daft et al. (1987) regarding the fit between  media richness and message complexity: in the
context of business organizations, this fit is generally measured by task -completion efficiency, whereas in the context of
private museum communication, the same fit functions as a mechanism to mitigate social  perceptions namely, the stigma
of exclusivity a function that has not been extensively explored in the applied MRT literature. This finding both reinforces
and expands upon the results of Setyaningsih et al. (2024), which demonstrated that rich media can transform raw
information into deep understanding in the context of Islamic philanthropy; in this study, a similar capacity was shown to
apply equally to the context of contemporary visual art which is inherently more esoteric indicating that the language
simplification mechanism in MRT is cross-domain and not limited to a specific type of message. Conversely, these findings
differ from the pattern observed by Ocon et al. (2024) in major European museums, which tend to rely on active listening
and user engagement as primary strategies for building reputation; at
@tumurunmuseum, language adaptation in photo captions actually serves as the primary entry point before two -way
engagement takes shape, indicating that the sequence of communication strategies for s mall-to-medium-sized private
museums may differ from that of large museums that already have an established audience base and reputation. The
management of User -Generated Content (UGC) further strengthens this process by creating an organic mechanism for
social proof, which collectively builds the museum’s inclusive image in the public eye, thereby transforming the audience
from passive visitors into co -creators of the brand’s identity (Kotler, Kartajaya, & Setiawan, 2017). Research on UGC
confirms that when audiences actively produce and share content related to a brand, this significantly strengthens consumer
trust and perceptions of brand authenticity, as narratives generated by fellow users are generally considered more credible
than messages produced by institutions (Mandal, 2021; Ngo et al., 2024).

Visual Optimization as a Key Conversion Factor for Visit Interest

Field findings indicate that visual elements play a crucial role in transforming digital interest into a decision to visit in
person. Among the various content formats used by @tumurunmuseum, Reels were deemed the most effective compared
to single-photo posts or carousels, due to their ability to combine motion, audio, and short duration to simulate the museum’s
spatial experience and atmo sphere more comprehensively  something that static visual formats struggle to achieve. The
superiority of this short-form video format aligns with the findings of Liu et al. (2023), which show that short videos excel
at stimulating travel intent because they can simultaneously combine informational and emotional cues within a single piece
of content something that, in static photo formats, must be conveyed separately through written captions. This strategy was
confirmed by the museum’s creative team: “Visual content like Reels and feeds is crucial because it conveys the atmosphere
and experience of the exhibition space more vividly. Viewers can imagine their visit based on what they see on Instagram”
(A2, Social Media Specialist).
For visitors, the richness of visual cues (various cues) determines the priorities of their visit. Source B1 noted:
“Personally, I’m more interested in Reels. Because through them, the visual experience truly captures the calm, aesthetic
atmosphere and that distinctive vibe. For me as a student, video content like that sparks a much greater curiosity to visit in
person” (B1, Instagram follower of @tumurunmuseum).
Informant B2, who is from outside Solo, emphasized: “Reels are the most effective because they  can immediately
showcase the experience without the need to read a lot of text. The visual experience and the uniqueness of the artworks.
Ultimately, I added Tumurun to my list of museums I want to visit” (B2, Instagram follower of @tumurunmuseum).
From an MRT perspective (Daft & Lengel, 1986), the effectiveness of Reels does not stem from any single dimension
of media richness in isolation, but rather from the interaction of all four dimensions working simultaneously within a single
unit of content. Various visual and audio cues create a realistic impression of the exhibition space’s atmosphere; the
linguistic diversity in the Reels’ narration translates that experience into an easily understandable framework;  the personal
focus achieved through intimate camera angles creates the impression that viewers are physically present; and the speed of
feedback via the comments section allows potential visitors to get immediate clarification on questions that arise while
watching. It is this combination of the four  mechanisms that simultaneously reduces ambiguity across three distinct
aspects—visual, linguistic, and interactional resulting in a stronger and more multidimensional intent to visit than if only
one dimension of media richness were fulfilled. In other wo rds, the power of Reels as a conversion factor does not lie in
the superiority of a single technical feature alone, but rather in the mutually reinforcing synergy across these dimensions
during a single moment of content consumption. Informant A2 also shar ed their perception of a correlation between viral
Reels posts and an increase in ticket booking demand during the same period, although this claim is observational from the
manager’s perspective and has not been verified through quantitative data, such as  a comparison of the number of views
with ticket booking volume before and after a piece of content went viral. Given these limitations, the findings should be
interpreted as a qualitative indication of the role of video content in driving visit decisions,  rather than as evidence of a
statistically confirmed cause-and-effect relationship; further quantitative studies linking Instagram insights data with ticket
booking figures will be necessary to confirm the strength of this relationship more convincingly. Despite these limitations,
the observed patterns remain consistent with the idea that video content serves as one of the triggers for decision -making
in the digital consumer journey. These findings are supported by Liu et al. (2023), who, in their open -access study in
*Frontiers in Psychology*, demonstrated that short -form videos effectively stimulate travel intentions by combining
information-oriented and emotional content cues, encouraging users to plan visits to destinations they encounter on social
media platforms. On a broader scale, Recuero Virto et al. (2024) found through an analysis of 956 Instagram users that
perceived enjoyment and user experience on museum Instagram accounts significantly predicted visit intent, underscoring
the role of immersive video content in transforming passive browsing into active behavioral responses.
A Collaborative Management Model Without Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs)
Content management at @tumurunmuseum is carried out through an organic and collaborative organizational model. Rather
than relying on rigid written SOPs, the team relies on shared values regarding the museum’s identity and aesthetic standards.
Although flexible, quality control is strictly maintained through tiered discussions among the creative te am, curators, and
managers before publication. In practice, this mechanism operates informally yet sequentially: content ideas initiated by
the creative team are first discussed with curators to ensure the accuracy of the artistic and historical substance of the
collection, then consulted with managers to align with the institution’s overall communication tone and image, before
finally being approved for upload. This tiered sequence serves as a functional substitute for a written SOP: rather than being
governed by procedural documents, quality consistency is maintained through the layered involvement of parties who each
act as gatekeepers over one dimension of the institution’s values  namely, substantive accuracy, appropriateness of
communication tone, and s trategic alignment. Thus, the normative integration in question is operationally realized not as
the internalization of abstract values, but as a practice of layered verification that occurs every time content is about to be
published. As expressed by the museum’s creative team:

“My role is flexible and adapts to the needs of the content. There is no fixed work schedule. Content ideas are
collaborative. Many ideas come from me, but they still must go through discussion and approval by
management, curators, and the promotions team” (A3, Videographer & Photographer).

Theoretically, the function of shared values as a substitute for formal SOPs can be understood through the concept of
normative integration in organizational theory. This coordination mechan ism relies on aligning team members’ behavior
through the internalization of shared values and goals (Mintzberg, 1983). Luttrell (2019) emphasizes that the “management”
phase is fundamentally concerned with the sustained maintenance of brand identity cohesion a function often fulfilled more
effectively by collective values than by static documents.
However, a management approach that relies too heavily on this organic aspect is open to criticism from the
perspective of organizational sustainability. The ab sence of procedural documents creates vulnerability to the risk of
personnel turnover. As noted by Quesenberry (2021), a lack of written guidelines can lead to the loss of institutional memory
when key practitioners leave the organization. This risk is particularly relevant for cultural institutions, as observed by Ocon
et al. (2024). In their study of European museums, the consistency of digital communication strategies over time was
directly linked to the quality of internal management systems, where plat forms such as Instagram require structured
governance to maintain long-term audience engagement. Therefore, standardization through SOPs remains crucial to ensure
that the effectiveness of marketing communications remains stable and is not disrupted by the  organization’s internal
dynamics. The implications of these findings are relevant to the broader management of communication in cultural
institutions: a collaborative model based on shared values can serve as an effective short-term strategy for small-to-medium-
sized cultural institutions with lean teams, as it supports speed and flexibility in responding to digital trends. However, a s
the audience base grows and operational complexity increases, cultural institutions need to consider a gradual transition
toward minimal procedural documentation for example, in the form of tone-of-voice guidelines or written content approval
workflows which can preserve creative flexibility while reducing overreliance on specific individuals to maintain long-term
communication consistency.

Conclusion
This study yielded three interrelated key findings, which should be interpreted within the constraints of a qualitative study
with a limited number of informants. First, the Instagram account @tumurunmuseum shows strong indic ations that it is
capable of deconstructing the stigma of exclusivity associated with private museums through the use of an inclusive and
approachable tone of voice in its digital narratives. Within the framework of Media Richness Theory, this strategy ref lects
the optimization of the “linguistic diversity” dimension, which appears to successfully translate the complexity of
contemporary art into messages that are more accessible to the general public, as perceived by the informants in this study.
Second, the optimization of visual elements through Instagram Reels was identified as the most decisive conversion
factor in transforming digital interest into a decision to visit in person. This finding confirms the “dual cue” principle in
Media Richness Theory, whereby the synergy between movement, audiovisual elements, and the simulation of spatial
experience offered by the video format reduces audience ambiguity regarding the actual visit experience.
Third, the @tumurunmuseum content management system which op erates organically and collaboratively without
written SOPs indicates that brand identity cohesion can be maintained through the internalization of shared values among
team members. Although this approach appears effective in supporting creativity and agil ity, the lack of procedural
documentation remains an institutional vulnerability that could potentially disrupt long -term communication consistency
in the event of a turnover of key personnel.
Overall, this study demonstrates that the integration of Media Richness Theory and the Circular Social Media Model
yields a comprehensive analytical framework for unpacking digital marketing communication strategies in cultural
institutions. Further research is recommended to test this integration on other digital platforms such as TikTok or YouTube,
as well as to apply a quantitative approach based on Instagram data insights to numerically measure the impact of
communication strategies on physical visits.
