coordinate digital communication with engaging on -site experiences to
encourage repeat visits and strengthen destination competitiveness.

urban tourism attractions.

A. Introduction
The development of digital technology has brought fundamental changes
to tourism marketing practices and tourists’ travel decision -making behavior.
Information and communication technology has transformed the process of
searching for tourism information, evaluating destination alternatives, and
forming tourists’ perceptions of a destination through various digital platforms
(Buhalis & Law, 2008). Social  media, online reviews, search engines, and digital
travel platforms have now become major sources of information that influence
tourists’ behavior prior to travel (Xiang & Gretzel, 2010). This condition
encourages destination managers to develop digital m arketing strategies that are
more interactive, responsive, and oriented toward tourist engagement (Gretzel
et al., 2015).
Digital tourism marketing no longer functions merely as a medium for
disseminating information but has evolved into a strategic instru ment for
building relationships between destinations and tourists. Through digital
content, destinations can build images, establish two -way communication, and
enhance tourists’ emotional engagement before and during their trips
(Hollebeek & Macky, 2019). Effective digital marketing strategies can increase
destination awareness, strengthen destination image, and influence tourists’
behavioral intentions (Kumar et al., 2022). However, the success of digital
marketing does not depend solely on the intensity o f promotional activities but
also on the destination’s ability to provide tourism experiences that meet
tourists’ expectations (Kim et al., 2012).
Changes in tourism consumption patterns indicate that modern tourists
increasingly seek experiences that are personal, authentic, emotional, and
capable of creating lasting memories. Memorable tourism experiences have
become one of the important factors influencing satisfaction, attachment to
destinations, and tourists’ intentions to revisit (Kim et al., 2012). T ourists do
not merely evaluate the quality of services or destination facilities but also
consider the emotional aspects and meanings derived from their experiences
during the journey (Pine & Gilmore, 1999). Therefore, successful digital
marketing should b e able to connect tourists’ expectations prior to travel with
the actual experiences they obtain while visiting a destination (Zhang et al.,
2018).
In the context of urban tourism destinations, the integration of digital
marketing and tourism experiences has become an increasingly important issue.
Urban destinations have complex characteristics because they combine
historical, cultural, creative economic, and local community activities within a
single tourism space (Ashworth & Page, 2011). Urban heritage destinations face
increasing competition from emerging destinations with strong visual appeal on
digital platforms (Timothy, 2018). Therefore, destination managers need to
understand that the success of digital marketing should not be measured solely
by the number of online interactions but also by the destination’s ability to

Previous studies have shown that digital tourism marketing h as a positive
relationship with tourist behavior, including revisit intention and destination
recommendation (Kumar et al., 2022). However, several studies have found
that this relationship is not always direct because tourists require positive
experiences that can strengthen the relationship between digital marketing
communication and post -visit behavior (Zhang et al., 2018). In other words,
digital marketing can attract tourists’ attention, but the actual experience during
the trip is an important factor in determining whether tourists will revisit or
recommend the destination to others (Kim et al., 2012).
Furthermore, most previous studies have focused on the direct effect of
digital marketing on tourist behavior, while research examining the moderating
mechanism of memorable tourism experience remains relatively limited,
particularly in the context of urban heritage destinations in developing countries
(Rather, 2021). Research on the relationship between digital tourism marketing,
memorable tourism experi ence, and revisit intention in Indonesian urban
heritage destinations still provides room for further development, particularly
because the characteristics of urban tourism in Indonesia combine historical
values, local culture, and digital transformation i n ways that differ from
destinations in other countries (Rahmawati et al., 2025). This research gap
indicates the need for an approach that can explain how digital marketing
strategies can generate tourist behavioral responses through experiences formed
during travel.

B. Literature Review
Digital Tourism Marketing and Tourist Revisit Intention
Digital tourism marketing plays a strategic role in connecting destinations
with tourists by facilitating information sharing, interactive communication, and
the formation of favorable destination perceptions (Hays et al., 2013; Kim et al.,
2017). The development of digital technology enables tourists to obtain
destination information rapidly throu gh social media, online travel platforms,
and various other digital channels before undertaking a trip (Buhalis & Law,
2008). Information available through digital media can influence tourists’
evaluations of destination attractiveness and shape their beha vioral decisions
after a visit (Xiang & Gretzel, 2010).
Digital Tourism Marketing uses digital platforms to promote destinations,
provide information, and engage tourists. Buhalis and Law (2008) emphasize
the role of technology in tourism marketing, while Xiang and Gretzel (2010)
highlight social media's importance in travel information searches. Based on
these studies, Digital Tourism Marketing is measured using five indicators:
information accessibility, content quality, interactive communication,
information credibility, and promotional attractiveness.
From the perspective of tourist behavior, digital marketing has the
potential to enhance trust, strengthen destination image, and increase the
likelihood of tourists revisiting a destination. Informative, e ngaging, and
credible digital content can establish a psychological connection between
tourists and destinations before the actual experience takes place (Gretzel et al.,
2015). Several studies have shown that the quality of digital interactions
influences tourist loyalty and future behavioral intentions (Kumar et al., 2022).
However, the influence of digital tourism marketing on revisit intention
remains an evolving area of research because tourists’ decisions to revisit are
not determined solely by digita l information but also by the actual experiences
obtained during their trips. Therefore, this study proposes the following
hypothesis:

Digital Tourism Marketing and Memorable Tourism Experience
Digital tourism marketing not only functions as a promotional tool but
also shapes tourists’ expectations and perceptions before their trips.  Digital
content in the form of photographs, videos, tourist reviews, and experience
stories can create an initial representation of a destination, which subsequently
influences how tourists evaluate their experiences during their visits (Hudson &
Thal, 2013).
From the perspective of experiential marketing, effective marketing
communication can est ablish emotional connections with tourists through the
creation of experience value (Pine & Gilmore, 1999). When digital marketing
strategies are able to communicate the authentic values, cultural uniqueness,
and emotional appeal of a destination, tourists  may be more likely to obtain
memorable experiences during their trips (Kim et al., 2012).
In the context of heritage destinations such as Kota Tua Jakarta and
Taman Mini Indonesia Indah, digital marketing can play an important role in
introducing historic al values, cultural heritage, and local identity to tourists.
Appropriate digital information can enhance tourists’ appreciation of the
destinations and strengthen the formation of meaningful tourism experiences.
Based on this argument, the following hypothesis is proposed:
H2: Digital Tourism Marketing has a positive effect on Memorable Tourism Experience.

Memorable Tourism Experience and Tourist Revisit Intention
Memorable tourism experience refers to tourism experiences that are
positively retained in t ourists’ memories and influence their behavior after the
trip has ended. Memorable experiences are formed through a combination of
emotional, cognitive, social, and sensory aspects experienced by tourists during
their visits to a destination (Kim et al., 2012).
In the study of tourist behavior, experience is one of the key factors
influencing tourist satisfaction, destination attachment, and tourist loyalty.
Tourists who obtain positive experiences tend to have more favorable
perceptions of a destination an d demonstrate a higher tendency to revisit
(Zhang et al., 2018).
In heritage destinations, tourism experiences play an increasingly
important role because the core value of a destination is derived not only from
its physical facilities but also from its ab ility to convey stories, culture, and
emotional connections with tourists (Timothy, 2018). Therefore, a memorable
tourism experience is expected to be an important factor in shaping revisit
intention. Accordingly, the following hypothesis is proposed:
H3: Memorable Tourism Experience has a positive effect on Tourist Revisit Intention.

The Moderating Role of Memorable Tourism Experience
Based on the Stimulus -Organism-Response (S -O-R) theory, individual
behavior is influenced not only by external stimuli b ut also by internal states
that mediate or strengthen responses to such stimuli (Mehrabian & Russell,
1974). In this study, Digital Tourism Marketing serves as an external stimulus,
while Memorable Tourism Experience represents an internal psychological
condition that determines the strength of the influence of digital marketing on
tourist behavior.
Although Digital Tourism Marketing can generate tourist awareness and
expectations regarding a destination, its influence may become stronger when
tourists obta in experiences that meet or even exceed their expectations. A
memorable tourism experience enables tourists to connect the digital
information encountered before the trip with their actual experiences during the
visit, thereby strengthening emotional attac hment and the intention to revisit
(Kim et al., 2012).
Conversely, when tourists’ actual experiences do not correspond to the
image established through digital media, the effectiveness of digital marketing in
fostering tourist loyalty may decrease (Zhang e t al., 2018). Thus, Memorable
Tourism Experience is expected not only to have a direct effect on Tourist
Revisit Intention but also to strengthen the relationship between Digital
Tourism Marketing and Tourist Revisit Intention. Based on this argument, the
following moderation hypothesis is proposed:
H4: Memorable Tourism Experience moderates the relationship between Digital Tourism
Marketing and Tourist Revisit Intention.

Research Location and Population
The research was conducted at Kota Tua Jakarta and Taman Mini
Indonesia Indah (TMII), two prominent cultural and heritage tourism
destinations in Jakarta, Indonesia. These destinations were selected based on
their historical and cultural significance, revitalization initiatives, and adoption
of digital marketing strategies. Although visitor statistics are available from
official tourism au thorities and destination management reports, these figures
represent the number of visits rather than unique individual tourists.
Furthermore, complete annual visitor statistics for 2026 were not yet available
during the research period. Consequently, the  exact population of individual
tourists who had visited both destinations could not be determined. Therefore,
the target population was defined as all tourists who had visited Kota Tua
Jakarta or TMII, while the accessible population consisted of eligible  tourists
who could be reached during data collection. A total of 200 respondents
participated in the study using purposive sampling based on predetermined
eligibility criteria.

Sampling Technique and Sample Size
The sampling technique employed in this st udy was purposive sampling,
which is a method of selecting respondents based on specific criteria relevant to
the research objectives. This technique was used because not all visitors have
the experience and characteristics required to evaluate the relatio nship between
digital marketing and revisit behavior.
To ensure that respondents met the established eligibility criteria, a
screening procedure was conducted before administering the main
questionnaire. Respondents were asked to confirm their previous vi sits to Kota
Tua Jakarta or Taman Mini Indonesia Indah, indicate their age, and identify the
digital platforms through which they had obtained information about the
destinations. Only respondents aged 17 years or older who had visited at least
one of the s elected destinations and had been exposed to destination -related
digital information were eligible to participate. Informed consent was obtained
before questionnaire completion, and the responses were checked for
completeness. Questionnaires that did not m eet the eligibility criteria or
contained incomplete responses were excluded from the analysis.

SEM analysis. According to Hair et al. (2022), sample size determination in

Data Analysis
Data analysis was conducted using the Partial Least Squares Structural
Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM) approach with the assistance of software such
as SmartPLS 4. The analysis consisted of two main stages: measurement model
analysis (outer model) and structural model analysis (inner model).
Measurement model analysis was conducted to assess the validity and
reliability of the research constructs. Convergent validity was evaluated based
on outer loadings > 0.70 and Average Variance Extracted (AVE) > 0.50.
Discriminant validity was assessed using the Fornell –Larcker criterion and the
Heterotrait–Monotrait ratio (HTMT). Internal consistency reliability was
evaluated using Cronbach’s Alpha > 0.70 and Composite Reliability > 0.70
(Hair et al., 2022).
Structural model analysis was conducted through the following
procedures: (1) The coefficient of determination (R²) was used to assess the
extent to which the independent variables explain the variance in the dependent
variable; (2) Significance testing, hypothesis testing  was conducted using the
bootstrapping procedure with a significance level of 5% ( p-value < 0.05); (3)
The moderating effect of Memorable Tourism Experience was examined using
the interaction effect approach, namely: Digital Tourism Marketing ×
Memorable Tourism Experience → Tourist Revisit Intention.

D.  Discussion
The Effect of Digital Tourism Marketing on Tourist Revisit Intention
The findings indicate that Digital Tourism Marketing h as a positive and
significant effect on Tourist Revisit Intention (β = 0.284; t = 4.126; p < 0.001).
This finding indicates that digital marketing activities involving the provision of
destination information, content quality, digital interaction, and info rmation
credibility are associated with a higher tendency among tourists to revisit Kota
Tua Jakarta and Taman Mini Indonesia Indah. In the context of urban heritage
destinations, digital media functions not only as a means of disseminating
information but also as an instrument for shaping tourists’ initial perceptions of
the historical, cultural, and experiential values offered by destinations. This
finding supports the Stimulus -Organism-Response (S -O-R) Theory, which
explains that external stimuli in the form of marketing communication can
influence individuals’ behavioral responses. In this context, Digital Tourism
Marketing serves as a stimulus that contributes to the formation of positive
perceptions, which may subsequently encourage tourists’ revisit intentions.
This finding is consistent with several previous studies demonstrating the
contribution of digital technology to tourist behavior. Xiang and Gretzel (2010)
explained that social media and digital information sources play an important
role in tou rists’ information -search processes and travel decision -making.
Buhalis and Law (2008) also demonstrated that information technology has
transformed communication patterns between destinations and tourists by
increasing information accessibility and facili tating digital interactions.
Furthermore, Kumar et al. (2022) found that digital marketing activities can
enhance tourists’ behavioral intentions by strengthening positive perceptions of
destinations. Hu (2026) also found that characteristics of digital pl atforms, such
as information accessibility, the quality of online reviews, and content
personalization, are associated with tourism experiences and revisit intention
through tourist behavioral mechanisms.

E.  Conclusion
This study examined the relationship between Digital Tourism Marketing,
Memorable Tourism Experience, and Tourist Revisit Intention in the context
of urban heritage destinations in Jakarta, Indone sia. By applying the Stimulus -
Organism-Response (S -O-R) framework, this research provides empirical
evidence that digital marketing activities implemented by destinations can
influence tourists’ behavioral responses, particularly their intention to revisit .
The findings demonstrate that Digital Tourism Marketing has a positive and
significant effect on Tourist Revisit Intention, indicating that effective digital
communication, accessible information, credible content, and interactive
engagement through digi tal platforms contribute to strengthening tourists’
future behavioral intentions.
Furthermore, this study reveals that Digital Tourism Marketing
significantly influences Memorable Tourism Experience, suggesting that digital
platforms have evolved beyond promotional tools into mechanisms for shaping
tourists’ expectations, emotional connections, and perceptions of destination
value. The strong relationship between Digital Tourism Marketing and
Memorable Tourism Experience highlights that tourists begin constructing their
destination experiences before actual visitation through digital exposure. In
addition, Memorable Tourism Experience was found to have a significant
positive influence on Tourist Revisit Intention, confirming that emotionally
meaningful and m emorable experiences are important determinants of post -
visit tourist behavior.
A key contribution of this research is the confirmation of the moderating
role of Memorable Tourism Experience in strengthening the relationship
between Digital Tourism Marketi ng and Tourist Revisit Intention. This finding
extends previous tourism marketing studies that primarily examine the direct
effect of digital marketing on tourist behavior. The results indicate that digital
marketing effectiveness is not solely determined by the quality of online
communication but also depends on the strength of tourists’ accumulated
experiences with the destination. In other words, digital marketing becomes
more influential when it is supported by authentic, meaningful, and memorable
destination experiences.
From a theoretical perspective, this study contributes to the development
of S-O-R Theory within the tourism marketing context by demonstrating that
internal tourist conditions can influence the intensity of behavioral responses
toward external marketing stimuli. While previous studies have commonly
positioned tourist experience as a mediating mechanism, this research provides
additional insight by demonstrating its moderating role in explaining variations
in revisit intention. This pers pective enriches the understanding of how digital
stimuli interact with tourists’ psychological experiences in shaping future travel
decisions.
From a managerial perspective, the findings provide important
implications for destination managers, particularl y those responsible for urban

This study has several limitations that provide opportunities for future
research. First, the research was conducted in two urban heritage destinations in
Jakarta, which may limit the generaliz ability of the findings to other tourism
contexts with different cultural and geographical characteristics. Future studies
may expand the research setting by involving rural tourism destinations, natural
attractions, or international heritage destinations.  Second, this research focused
on Digital Tourism Marketing, Memorable Tourism Experience, and Tourist
Revisit Intention. Future research may incorporate additional variables such as
destination image, tourist satisfaction, perceived authenticity, destinat ion
attachment, electronic word-of-mouth, and smart tourism technology adoption
to develop a more comprehensive behavioral model. Third, future studies may
employ longitudinal research designs to examine how digital exposure and
memorable experiences evolv e over time and influence repeated travel
behavior.

marketing initiatives but also by the ability of de stinations to transform digital
engagement into meaningful and memorable visitor experiences. The
integration between digital communication strategies and experience -based
destination management represents an important direction for strengthening
competitiveness in the contemporary tourism landscape.
