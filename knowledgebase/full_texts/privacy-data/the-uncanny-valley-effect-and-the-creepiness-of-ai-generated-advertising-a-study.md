ABSTRACT :
The aim of this study is to identify the relationship between advertising realism, AI disclosure, the human likeness of AI avatars, perceived creepiness of AI-
generated adsandcustomertrust andbrandloyalty. Primarydata were gathered using astructured questionnaireon103 social media usersofIndia, andanalysed
byusingchi-square test,one-wayANOVAandlinearandmultipleregression, theBaronandKennyprocedure, Sobel testandabootstraptocheckthe mediation.
The factors of human likeness (β = 0.76), realism (β = 0.16) and disclosure (β = 0.11) all significantly contributed to the variance in perceived creepiness,
accounting for82.6% ofthe variance. Contraryto the hypothesis, creepiness didnot negativelyinfluence the customertrust (β=−0.05, p=0.614),but positively
(β = 0.39, p < 0.001) influenced brand loyalty. Trust strongly predicted loyalty (β = 0.58, R² = 0.34). Contrary to the usual role of mediation, creepiness was a
suppressor rather than a classical mediator: realism and human likeness increased creepiness, which, in turn, was associated with higher trust, to some extent
counteracting their direct negative impact on trust. The results indicate that, forthis sample, discomfort with AI-driven adverts that resemble humans has not led
to any decrease in trust or loyalty, and that trust is the primary pathway to loyalty. Advertising agencies must navigate the moral and ethical landscape of AI-
generated content with great caution, as consumers are growing increasingly aware of these new methods as AI tools raise the bar for the ethics and morality of
advertising
Keywords: "AI disclosure", "customer trust", "brand loyalty", “perceived creepiness”, “ai-advertising”.
Introduction
With Generative AI, brands cannow create realistic advertising characters, voices and scenes at a fraction of the price. These characters reach greater
realism,whichmayleadtoafeelingofdiscomfort thatmanypeople describeasbeinginthe"uncannyvalley" (Mori,1970)astheaffinityincreases as
the artificial figure becomes more human-like, and then decreases when it starts to look almost, but not quite, human. The question from advertisers'
perspectiveis whether this discomfort hurtstrust andloyalty thatisessentialtorepeat purchase. Previous studies correlatethe realism of AI-generated
content, its eeriness, personalisation, privacy issues, transparency with consumer trust, acceptance and purchase intent and Indian Gen-Z audiences
reactwithamixtureofcuriosity,interest andsuspicion.ButthelinkbetweenAIcreepinessandconsumers' trustandloyaltyisstilllimited inresearch.
Thisstudyfillsthisvoid.
Review of Literature
The impact of Generative AI in advertising on consumer trust, acceptance, purchase intention and brand responses have been shown in previous
literature. Key drivers that have been identified are AI realism, perceived eeriness, personalisation, privacy concerns, and transparency. AI generated
content can be creepy and potentially detract from trust, as evidenced by some findings. The gen Z Indian consumers are a mix of curiosity and
suspicionwhenitcomestoAI-poweredads.Thetheoreticalanchoristheuncannyvalleyhypothesis(UCH)proposedbyMori(1970)andMacDorman
andIshiguro(2006)whichsuggeststhatartificialfigureswillbeuncomfortablewhentheyareclosetobutnotidenticaltoahuman.Fromtherelational
perspective,MorganandHunt's(1994)commitment–trusttheoryofrelationshipsplacestrustasakeyantecedentofcommitment–basedoutcomes,such
as loyalty outcomes, to support H2. There remains a significant lack of research on the connection between AI creepiness, customer trust and brand
loyalty.
Research Gap
MuchofthepreviousresearchhasbeencentredontheuncannyvalleyeffectandthecreepinessofAI-producedcontent,especiallywhenitcomestothe
feelingof discomfort consumers get when they see human-likeAI characters or avatars.But little researchhas been done on advertising andcustomer

Conceptual Framework
The framework defines the advertising realism, AI avatar and AI disclosure/transparency as independent variables. The mediator is the perceived
uncanniness/creepiness,andcustomertrustandbrandloyaltyarethedependentvariables,withcustomertrustalsoexpectedtoinfluencebrandloyalty.

10. Discussion
The close association between human likeness and realism with creepiness supports the first half of the uncanny valley logic: The more realistic the
advertisingcharactersoftheAIthemoreuncomfortabletheusersfeel.Thatuneasedidn'texistinthesecondhalf:nodoubtaboutit,theylosttheirtrust
andloyalty.Thefirstreadingishabituation:Ifyouareaheavyuserofsocialmedia,youmayhavebecomeaccustomedtothesyntheticcontentandyou
sense discomfort but not impact brand judgments. The other is measurement: The creepiness items ask about one's discomfort with AI characters in
general, while the trust and loyalty items are conditional on transparency and trusted brands, which means that people might be rating the brands that
they already trust. This positive creepiness–loyalty relationship may also be driven by those most aware of AI advertising having the highest brand
loyalty,beingthemostintunewiththealgorithm.Theseexplanationshavenotbeentestedhere,butshouldbelookedintoinfutureresearch.
Theresultofthedisclosureissusceptibletomisinterpretation.Thedisclosurescaleindicatesthesignificancerespondentsattributetotransparency,with
a positive correlation to creepiness indicating that those who findit more uncomfortable also believe thatbrands need to disclose AI use, highlighting
the need for a tailored approach. A positive association with creepiness suggests that the more uncomfortable people feel, the more significant they
believe disclosure is to brands, indicating the need for a tailored approach. Those who did notice a disclosure, however, reported a lower level of
creepiness,whichalignswithdisclosurereducingthecreepinessofAIadvertising,butcannotprovecausation.
