Abstract
This review examines Computational Advertising: Evolution, T ransformation, and the Cognitive
T urn (in Chinese࠹ෘؿ֥ܼۡᅚa ੀэაಪᆩሇཟ), edited by Duan Chunlin and published in 2025,
and assesses its attempt to reposition computational advertising through a cognitive orientation
that foregrounds human ⚶machine collaboration, semantic understanding, and mechanism‐based
explanation. The book traces the evolution from programmatic delivery to cognitive computational
advertising and articulates how data infrastructures, platform mechanisms, and generative AI are
jointly reshaping creative production, distribution coordination, and effect measurement. It fur‐
ther proposes conceptual and methodological pathways imost notably the TKLI framework ias
an alternative to linear knowledge hierarchies, arguing for closed‐loop processes of semantic in‐
teraction, knowledge collaboration, and reasoning‐based decision‐making. This review highlights
the book ns contributions to clarifying disciplinary boundaries and advancing a research agenda
that moves beyond correlation‐focused optimisation toward cumulative explanatory models. It also
identifies areas where the argument could be strengthened, including clearer specification of key
constructs, more explicit empirical validation strategies, and tighter discussion of governance im‐
plications under privacy, bias, and cross‐cultural constraints. The review concludes by highlighting
how the book ns cognitive turn provides a coherent agenda for future work on advertising com‐
munication mechanisms, methodological renewal, and the theory ⚶practice interface in intelligent
communication.

Structured around the trajectory from computational advertising
to cognitive computational advertising, the book is organized into
three parts (Parts I ⚶III), progressing from developmental evolution to
technological application and, finally, to cognitive transformation. This
tripartite structure establishes a research logic that moves from macro‐
level trends to micro‐level mechanisms, before returning to higher‐level
paradigmatic and methodological questions. Such an arrangement en‐
ables readers to grasp three interrelated dimensions: first, how compu‐
tational advertising has become embedded in contemporary communi‐
cation systems through data infrastructures and platform mechanisms;
second, how generative AI and intelligent agents are reshaping the
logics of programmatic creativity, distribution coordination, and effect
measurement; and third, how the incorporation of cognitive science
pushes advertising research beyond correlation‐focused optimization
toward mechanism‐oriented explanation, thereby laying the ground‐
work for subsequent theoretical development and algorithmic gover‐
nance frameworks. Part I focuses on the development and evolution of
computational advertising (Chapters 1 and 2). Beginning with the condi‐
tions of emergence and the developmental trajectory of computational
advertising, the authors revisit the structural transition from contract‐
based advertising and targeted advertising to programmatic buying,
highlighting how data, models, and algorithms jointly reconstruct adver‐
tising communication mechanisms. Advertising is no longer merely a
matter of media resource purchasing and scheduling; instead, it increas‐
ingly operates as a continuously running system of algorithmic decision‐
making within platform economies through automated transactions and
real‐time optimization. Within this framework, the book traces the
transformation of advertising strategies driven by big data, examining
the internal logic of data management platforms, user tagging systems,
interest‐matching algorithms, and intelligent decision mechanisms. This
analysis captures a shift in emphasis from advertisers purchasing expo‐
sure to systems automatically identifying and prioritizing users within
the digital advertising ecosystem. Importantly, Part I does not reduce
computational advertising to more precise targeting. By integrating in‐
telligent creativity and content optimization into the communication
mechanism, it clarifies the theoretical foundations and technical support
underlying the integration of brand building and performance, while also
outlining emerging trends such as the systematization of creativity, intel‐
ligent transactions, and multi‐screen integrated communication ( Duan
and Cui 2024).
Part II concentrates on the technological application of computa‐
tional advertising (Chapters 3, 4, and 5). This part centers on the imple‐
mentation logic of cognitive computational advertising and examines a
deeper shift from computational precision to cognitive understanding.
The authors argue that with the entry of generative AI and intelligent
agents into advertising systems, content production is moving from ma‐
terial assembly and rule‐based scheduling toward dynamic generation
and knowledge recombination. This transformation parallels new find‐
ings in platform governance and the ethical implication of the crowd
in value creation ( Lissillour and Sahut 2022, Sahut et al. 2023). At
the same time, the book addresses key industrial constraints iinclud‐
ing data bias, privacy protection, computational capacity limitations,
and cross‐cultural adaptation itreating them as boundary conditions
that cognitive computational advertising must navigate. This discussion
reflects a dual concern for technical feasibility and sustainable gover‐
nance, resonating with recent inquiries into power dynamics and ethical
transparency in AI implementation strategies ( Lissillour and Monod
2024).
Part III foregrounds a research paradigm shift grounded in cognitive
science (Chapters 6 and 7). Here, the analytical focus moves toward the‐
oretical consolidation and methodological refinement. The authors con‐
struct a modeling framework that integrates large language models with
knowledge‐collaboration mechanisms, proposing a multi‐layer research
structure and bidirectional coordination approach that emphasizes the
coupling of models, knowledge, reasoning, and decision‐making. This
approach anticipates the emerging management perspective that recog‐
nizes AI⚶human interaction as a dynamic system of ethical supervision
and cognitive augmentation ( Monod 2025, Singla 2024). Moreover, by
examining intelligent agent ⚶driven content generation and advertising
creativity, the book explores how cognitive dynamics, psychological
mapping, and affective interaction unfold within communication scenar‐
ios, addressing how understanding emerges, resonance is produced, and
relationships are systematically shaped. In doing so, it shows how cogni‐
tive computational advertising can enable practice paradigms centered
on value co‐creation and system coordination.
From a paradigmatic perspective, cognitive computational advertis‐
ing corresponds closely to what is often described as aofifth paradigm,p
namely a research logic oriented toward AI‐enabled knowledge gen‐
eration and knowledge collaboration. Rather than approximating truth
primarily through empirical induction or single‐model data fitting, it inte‐
grates multi‐source intelligent agents and cognitive reasoning through
knowledge‐generation mechanisms into an iterative, learning‐oriented
knowledge process. This reflects an epistemic shift where computa‐
tional advertising contributes to the broader management discourse on
sustainable innovation and organisational learning (Duan and Cui 2024).
The significance of this approach lies in how it repositions cognitive
computational advertising: no longer as a problem of technological ac‐
cumulation, but as a systemic question of how knowledge structures
are generated, coordinated, and rendered interpretable.
Although the book presents a well‐organized and insightful per‐
spective on the evolution of computational advertising, its discussion
remains largely theoretical, with relatively limited empirical evidence il‐
lustrating how cognitive mechanisms function in practice. The analysis
of algorithmic governance could be further developed to address trans‐
parency, fairness, and accountability in cognitive advertising systems. In
addition, as the argument is grounded mainly in China ns digital ecosys‐
tem, its applicability to other cultural and market contexts warrants
further examination.
