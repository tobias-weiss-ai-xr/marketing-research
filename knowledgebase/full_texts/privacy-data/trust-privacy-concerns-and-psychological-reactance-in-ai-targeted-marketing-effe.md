The rapid advancement of Artificial Intelligence (AI) has transformed
contemporary marketing by enabling personalized advertising,
predictive recommendations, and data -driven consumer engagement.
Despite these technological advancements, the increasing adoption of AI-
targeted marketing has generated growing concerns regarding consumer
trust, privacy protection, and psychological resistance toward
automated personalization. This study examined the effects of trust,
privacy concerns, and psychological reactance on consumer attitudes
toward AI -targeted marketing The quantitative research met hodology
was used with a cross sectional survey design, and data were gathered
from 250 respondents from five main cities of Punjab, Pakistan, using a
structured questionnaire, which had five -point Likert scale. Correlation
analysis, multiple regression, a nalysis of variance (ANOVA), and
independent samples t-test were used to analyses the collected data. The
results showed that trust has a positive impact on consumer attitudes
toward AI -driven marketing, while privacy concerns and psychological
reactance have strong negative impacts. Moreover, there were found to
be differences in consumer attitudes between gender and age groups.
The results indicate that to boost consumer acceptance and the impact of
AI-influenced marketing strategies, fostering consumer t rust,
transparency in AI -driven marketing practices, protecting consumer
data, and reducing psychological resistance play key roles. The study
adds to the existing literature on AI -powered marketing by offering
empirical insights from the Pakistani context  and practical implications
for marketers and organizations aiming to create AI marketing
strategies that are ethical, consumer-centric, and privacy-aware.

1. Introduction
Among the many game-changing innovations of modern technologies, Artificial Intelligence (AI)
has placed itself amongst the top two. (Chintalapati & Pandey, 2022). AI systems have empowered even
small companies with the ability to access, parse, and use marketing-related consumer data to construct
preferences and behaviors, and build individualized systems for marketing. For an applied example of
the AI systems at use, AI-targeted marketing pertains to the use of appetitive technologies for machine -
learning to construct marketing and messaged systems that are driven by the needs of the receiver to
an extent that the message is even likely to be delivere d in a highly individualized manner; (Wedel &
Kannan, 2016). There are a multitude of ways that AI systems have improved markers’ abilities to
target consumers with marketing. The use of marketing technologies driven by AI enables businesses to
go beyond the outdated marketing systems that focus on demographics and general trends. Companies
may instead focus on specific ways to meet the needs of the consumer through their purchasing history,
clicking history, online posts, and virtually any other online beh aviors. The advancements in AI -
augmented systems have improved marketing to the point where businesses can focus on the best way
to reach relatively specific individual consumers. (Davenport et al., 2020).

The use of AI technologies has deeply affected co nsumers' traditional relationships with brands
as companies now have access to marketing technologies that are more advanced, interactive, and, in a
sense, centered on their consumers. Among many channels, the use of recommendation tools, virtual
assistants, catboats, and automated customer service have now become staples in almost every digital
marketing Kaplan & Haenlein (2019). Businesses now have the ability to understand their consumers
more and, as a result, answer and fulfill their specific needs tha t are more individually tailored. The
different techniques and methods of AI have also helped consumers by simplifying the overwhelming
large amounts of information and by streamlining the processes of their decisions. When AI
personalization is employed and integrated into an organization, studies prove it to greatly increase the
happiness of the consumers and the organization’s brand, and, the most, increase the engagement of
the consumer.

The most relevant and impactful improvement AI marketing has on o rganizations is the ability
to forecast consumer activity and design and implement marketing strategies that target them. With the

Using AI technology, digital marketing can help build faster, easier, and more relevant

1.1 Role of Trust in Consumer Acceptance of AI Marketing
Trust is a major factor in consumer response to the use of AI in marketing. The inherent opacity
of many AI algorithms means that, for the most part, consumers must trust organizations and
electronic systems Martin & Murphy, 2017. Consumer trust indicates that the organization will handle
personal data responsibly and with care, and t hat AI -based personalization will yield helpful and
relevant recommendations. Thus, trust in AI -based marketing stems from a belief that the system will
privacy. Under these conditions, consumers are more likely to accept focused marketing and participate
in AI-based services even more so when these services are more interactive and engaging Aguirre et al.,
2015.

Moreover, ethical AI and transparency are necessary to build consumer trust. Gefen et al. (2003)
found that businesses that explain data practice s and provide means for consumer data management
build rapport with consumers. Trust decreases the perceived risk associated with technology and
increases its acceptance, therefore, trust is one of the most important elements of consumer attitude
and behavioral intention toward AI-based marketing (Shin, 2021; Pavlou, 2003).

Not all consumers readily adopt AI marketing technology, despite its convenience and the ability
to save time. AI marketing technology can trigger psychological reactance in consumers, which is the
emotional and behavioral response to threaten the consumer's sense of autonomy and control. In the
case of AI marketing, consumers can feel reactance when they feel that the marketing systems are
influencing their decisions, monitoring their b ehaviors, and limiting their options (Clee & Wicklund,
1980).

Negative perceptions of control and manipulation can result from overzealous personalization,
frequent advertising, and persistent data collection. This can cause consumers to forgo AI
recommendations and personalization of advertising and create an unfavorable attitude toward AI
marketing systems (Davis, 1989). According to psychological reactance theory, consumers can exhibit a
strong sense of reactance when they perceive their autonomy to sel ect and control choices has been
violated, therefore, reactance is an important consideration for the resistance toward AI -based
marketing behavior (Brehm, 1966).

1.2 Consumer Attitudes and Behavioral Intentions toward AI-Targeted Marketing
Consumer attitudes form a vital component that explains the psychological dynamics of the
perception and response of individuals towards AI -targeted marketing. Attitudes are embedded in
consumers’ perception, belief and judgment of the usefulness, trustwor thiness and acceptability of

marketing technologies that are driven by AI, Yaprak, 2025. With AI -targeted marketing, consumers
will hold positive attitudes when they view tailored advertising, suggestions and automated services as
helpful and as an added c onvenience and value. AI technologies can be beneficial to consumers by
saving them time, effort and by assisting them in making better buying choices, Ajzen, 1991. The
attitude of consumers can also be negative when they feel that AI systems are aggressiv e, and threaten

The development of consumer attitudes towards AI -targeted marketin g strikes a balance
between technological advantages and psychological factors. Consumers are inclined to hold positive
attitudes when AI systems are perceived to be transparent, accurate, and fair in their personalization
efforts (Gefen et al., 2011). For  instance, consumers are more accepting of AI -centered marketing
practices when they believe suggestions are made to enhance their shopping experience as opposed to
suggestions being made to manipulate purchasing decisions (Wang, et al., 2025). Conversely,  negative
attitudes can stem from excessive personalization anxiety, data sensitivity, and concern with the opacity
of algorithms. Previous research has established that the absence of the aforementioned concerns is a
significant factor in consumer positive evaluations of digital technologies (Shin, 2021).

Behavioral intentions are the consumer’s likelihood to perform various actions related to AI -
targeted marketing. These actions can be engaging with personalized advertisements, accepting AI -
based recomme ndations to purchase an item, providing personal data, and maintaining an online
relationship with a digital platform. Behavioral intentions are often a good indication of a consumer’s
actions, as people are inclined to act based on their attitudes and per ceptions (Aguirre-Camacho et al.,
2024). In an AI marketing context, consumers that have a positive perception of AI services are likely to
engage with personalization, as well as provide favorable feedback toward the associated brand.
Conversely, consumers that have a negative perception of AI services because of a lack of trust, privacy
concerns, and psychological oppositeness, are likely to avoid AI -centered advertisements and reduce
engagement with digital platforms (Jamal et al., 2023).

Moreover, in the context of AI-targeted marketing, examining the correlation between consumer
attitudes and behavioral intentions holds significant value. It is noted that positive consumer attitudes
reinforce behavioral intentions by bolstering confidence and satisfact ion and increasing willingness to
engage with AI technologies Ansari, et al., 2024. When consumers view AI marketing as value -
generating and privacy -respecting, they begin to more readily accept personalized recommendations
and even build enduring relation ships with the recommending firms. Contrary to the above, if privacy
concerns and/or psychological reactance create negative attitudes, consumer willingness to engage with
AI-based marketing will be significantly reduced Barbarossa et al., 2023. In light o f the above, in order
to foster positive attitudes and behavioral intentions regarding AI -targeted marketing, organizations
will need to focus on building AI responsibly, transparency, and consumer trust.

1.5 Problem Statement
AI is reshaping how businesses connect with their customers. Whether through targeted ads,
data-backed suggestions, or smart decision -making, businesses now have the tools needed to engage
with their customers on a more personal level. Using marketing AI, businesses are able to analyze
consumer trends and build a data-backed understanding on how to best engage their consumers. This is
seen as a progressive step in building consumer -business relationships. Like everything, AI marketing
has its challenges. One of its biggest is gaining the trust of consumers. With AI -based marketing, there
is a greate r chance of the consumer's data being involved without their knowledge. This leads to a
distrust of the marketing technique, an adverse opinion of AI, and discomfort from the consumer. There
is also the theory of psychological reactance, which suggests that consumers, when made to believe that
their privacy is being exploited or freedom is being taken away, will oppose the marketing AI
technology and its techniques. Except for a handful, most of the research that has been done has been
on the impact of privacy on consumer attitudes and behaviors. I intend to analyze the impact of privacy,
trust, and psychological reactance on consumers and their response to marketing AI.

This study examines the psychological and behavioral aspects of consumer acceptance of AI

2.1 Trust and Consumer Attitudes toward AI-Targeted Marketing
Trust, in relation to AI-targeted marketing, is one of the mo st important psychological factors in
consumer acceptance and engagement with the practice. In digital marketing, AI systems incorporate
automated processes and complex algorithms that, in most cases, operate beyond a consumer’s reach,
and thus, direct obs ervation. Because of this, in most cases, the consumer's knowledge of AI is limited.
Therefore, as a means of understanding AI marketing, consumers place their trust in businesses and the
systems that integrate technology. Along with the protection of pers onal information, responsible
actions, and the provision of accurate recommendations, trust in the systems is contingent on the
creation of value rather than harm. Consumers’ confidence in AI marketing systems results in their
willingness to engage with the systems, provide information, and use the systems to their full potential.
This means that confidence in AI marketing reduces uncertainty and the perceived risks associated with
the use of AI Business Marketing systems. (Badewi et al., 2023).

Trust is all the more vital in AI -centric marketing because consumers have more reasons to feel
apprehensive in this regard Claessens et al., 2023. As consumers are less likely to know the rationale

consumer control over their data, it helps foster trust in AI -based advertising. When consumers see

advertising. For this reason, the successful adoption and use of AI in advertising is dependent on the
trust of the consumers (Liyanaarachchi et al., 2024).

2.2 Privacy Concerns in AI-Based Personalized Marketing
Privacy challenges are now at the forefront of AI -targeted marketing, restricting the use of
marketing AI. To present tailored promotional content, machine learning technologies must capture,
analyze, and proces s huge volumes of digital consumer data (Mohiuddin, 2024). This includes online
purchase history, web search history, social media activity, engagement history, and location data.
Personalized marketing provides timely and relevant content to users and enh ances their digital
engagement. However, this kind of marketing raises concerns for the end user regarding the methods
(and the extent) that companies use to capture, store, and utilize their private data (Zhou, 2025).
Concerns for privacy challenges are: the ethical use of data, the level of data capture control retained by
the end user, and the commitment and level of protection for user privacy by the company. A decline in
end-user confidence will have a negative impact on the extent of their possible in teractions with
marketing AI (Bansal, et al. 2024).

As AI technologies get better at predicting customer needs and preferences, users of digital
services are getting more anxious about privacy concerns. Companies deploy advanced AI systems
which are really good at predicting what individuals are most likely interested in, what they are likely
going to need, and what they are likely to purchase next. This makes marketers really good at tailoring
their offers to customers’ needs and preferences; customers pe rceive this to be a result of the

2.3 Psychological Reactance and Consumer Resistance toward AI Marketing
Psychological reactance provides an explanation of consumer resistance to certain technologies
and marketing tools. When consumers feel their privacy, autonomy, and/or control are threatened,
psychological reactance explains the resistance as them exercising their autonomy and right to react. In
the case of AI marketing, consumers may see the decisions of AI as an overreach of its control and may
see the AI is watching them and is actively working to manipulate the consumers' online experience.
When consumers feel there is an algorithmic manipulation of their control, they may react negatively to
the personalization and marke ting of AI, even though the intent is to make the consumer feel more
valued and improve their experience. The reactance may take the form of a negative attitude, more
distrust toward AI marketing, avoiding the marketing, and less willingness to engage with  the AI
marketing.

When marketing messages seem overly focused, consumers can push back against AI
personalization efforts. Modern AI can evaluate almost anything about a consumer, even their purchase
history. This can help businesses deliver useful marke ting messages, but consumers can feel as if
organizations are monitoring them to the extent that they are being controlled. Consumers feel as
though companies have violated their privacy when they are shown ads that are too related to their
searches. Pushb ack against personalized marketing can be caused by companies violating consumer
privacy by personalizing marketing too closely. Companies that utilize AI to market to consumers can
be successful when they balance the closeness of marketing and the consume r's perceived closeness of
the company to them (Aguirre et al., 2015).

Psychological reactance is a dominating factor in consumer preference. Consumer preferences
are driven by the balance of emotionally perceived closeness to the company and the company’ s desire
to control them through marketing. Even though consumers may be shown too many personalized ads,
they don’t dislike the technology; they dislike seeing their privacy violated. Previous studies on
consumer preferences have focused on control, expli tativeness, and manipulation of the consumer.
Consumers are most wary of technology that can make automated decisions.

Psychological reactance dampens consumer acceptance of AI -driven marketing. Strong
resistance from consumers will deter them from using AI services, despite their awareness of the
benefits of such services (Miron & Brehm, 2006). Companies aiming to overcome psychological
reactance from consumers will need to strike the right balance between marketing personalization and
the consumer's sens e of control. Such companies should analyze the concerns of consumers and use
data control mechanisms. Psychological reactance will be diminished, and the attitudes of consumers
toward the acceptance of AI marketing will be more favorable, if consumers fee l they have some control
over their data and decisions. Hence, the analysis of psychological reactance is of utmost importance to
companies for the successful incorporation of AI in marketing services that consumers will accept.

2.4 Consumer Attitudes and Behavioral Intentions toward AI-Targeted Marketing
Consumer attitudes toward AI -targeted marketing are critical in forming the behavioral
intention to accept these marketing practices. Consumer attitudes are their evaluations, perceptions,
beliefs, and feelings toward the use of technologies to market and advertise in a more personalized and
consumer-specific manner. Consumer attitudes toward AI -targeted marketing will be positive if AI
systems are convenient, reliable, and transparent, and are characteriz ed by the enhancement of
Consumer satisfaction in their digital interactions (Shin, 2021). AI recommendations and personalized
ads will assist consumers through the product search and selection process. Such marketing systems
will be perceived as value -adding and efficient. AI marketing systems will be perceived as value -
reducing and inefficient if they are characterized by highly personalized marketing that is perceived as
intrusive and manipulation. Consumer concerns regarding data privacy, predatory syst ems, and the
perceived opacity of highly personalized marketing systems will negatively impact the adoption of AI
marketing systems (Venkatesh et al., 2003).

The formation of attitudes toward AI-targeted marketing will depend on whether consumers feel
that the advantages outweigh the drawbacks of AI technologies. Consumers are more likely to have
positive attitudes toward marketing activities that are based on AI when they feel that AI systems are
valuable and that they experience a level of personalizatio n. For example, consumers may view AI
recommendations positively because they are more likely to find a product that aligns with their
preferences, and they may spend less time looking for it (Martin & Murphy, 2017). Similarly,
personalized marketing messa ges may improve the consumer’s experience by replacing a generic
message with an advertisement that contains relevant information. Conversely, consumers are more
likely to exhibit a negative attitude toward AI systems, and their interactions with AI-based systems will
likely decrease when they feel that AI Systems are pervasive, that AI systems are collecting too much
personal data, and that they are manipulating their decisions.

Behavioral intentions describe the likelihood that a consumer will take a spe cific action related
to AI-targeted marketing (e.g., interacting with a personalized ad, clicking on an AI recommendation,
purchasing a recommended product, providing data to a digital platform, and establishing a long -term
relationship with a brand that u ses AI technologies) (Puntoni et al., 2021). Behavioral intentions are a
strong predictor of consumer behavior, as individuals will likely conduct an action based on their

attitudes and perceptions (Ajzen, 1991). In AI marketing, if consumers have a positi ve attitude, they are
likely to accept AI recommendations, engage with personalized marketing content, and have a positive
attitude toward organizations that employ AI marketing strategies (Davis, 1989).

Consumer attitudes heavily shape the acceptance and  adoption of AI marketing practices. With
positive attitudes, consumers’ confidence, satisfaction, and willingness to use AI increase, thereby
strengthening their behavioral intentions. Consumers are likely to perceive stronger AI services and
build brand relationships when AI marketing is perceived as useful and trustworthy. However, there
are detrimental effects of psychological reactance and privacy concerns. These factors decrease
behavioral intentions and increase resistance to AI personalization. For positive attitudes and greater
behavioral intentions, companies should ensure privacy control, promote transparency, and strengthen
the trustworthy nature of their AI systems.

2.5 Research Gap
Despite exploring how AI shapes and transforms marketing pract ices, limited research
investigates consumer responses to AI marketing. Most research has focused on the positive aspects of
AI-driven marketing's personalization, such as the improved customer experience and efficiencies
within marketing (Davenport et al. , 2020). More attention should be dedicated to the psychological
aspects of AI marketing, and to the interplay of consumer trust, privacy, and psychological reactance in
shaping consumer attitudes and behavioral intentions. Trust has been shown as one of t he important
constructs of technology acceptance, and privacy as an enabler of consumer engagement (Gefen et al.,
2003), yet limited studies have investigated the interplay of these constructs in the context of AI
marketing (Martin & Murphy, 2017).

Prior studies show what many already know: consumers find advertisements built on AI
intrusively tailored to the individual, especially when the AI operates with insufficient transparency and
consumer control (Aguirre et al., 2015). The gap that this study addre sses is the effect of psychological
reactance on the consumer push -back to AI -based personalization. This gap can partially be bridged
using Brehm's Psychological Reactance Theory (1966). This theory states that consumers will resist
anything that they perceive as an attack on their freedom and autonomy (in this case the use of AI). To
cover this gap in the literature, this study aims to add psychological reactance to the well -known
instrumental factors of trust and privacy, and thus, provide a fuller under standing of consumer
attitudes and behavioral responses to AI -based marketing. Huang & Rust (2021) also note the
importance of adding psychological reactance to give a fuller understanding of consumer behavior to
AI-based marketing.

3. Methodology
3.1 Research Design
A quantitative approach was used to study the relationships of trust and privacy, psychological
reactance, consumer attitudes, and behavioral intentions to AI-based marketing. The cross -sectional
survey method was used to collect data from the consumers at one point in time. This study used a
quantitative approach because it aimed to make statistical analyses to confirm the proposed hypotheses
and study the r elationships of the variables. The chosen research design was appropriate to measure
consumer attitudes and behavioral responses to AI-based Marketing (Creswell & Creswell, 2018).

3.2 Population of the Study
This study focused on consumers who encountered  targeted marketing advertisements after
using digital platforms. Data was collected from consumers residing in the cities of Multan, Lahore,
Faisalabad, Rawalpindi, and Bahawalpur, which represent digitally consuming cities of Punjab,
Pakistan. Respondents were people with access to social media, online shopping, search engines, and
services with marketing personalization based on AI. These cities are representative of consumers with
an increasing exposure of AI-based marketing.

3.3 Sample Size
This study focused on a sample of 250 respondents from Punjab, Pakistan. Sample sizes of this
order are of adequate size for quantitative research, and for this study, sample size was adequate to test
and generate a hypothesis. The sample provided the necessary dime nsion to the research to analyze
different quantitative relationships. The respondents were consumers with sufficient exposure to AI -
based advertisement systems and marketing personalization.

3.4 Sampling Technique
To gather responses, the researcher empl oyed a non -probability convenience sampling
technique. This sampling method was the best approach for the study’s interest in consumers who had
previously used digital platforms and AI -based personalized marketing. The researcher leveraged
online, social, and other digital communities to solicitation participation, which was totally voluntary.

3.5 Data Collection Procedure
A structured data collection tool in the form of an online questionnaire was used to gather
primary data from the consumers of Punjab, Pakistan. Based on the inclusion and exclusion criteria of
the respondents, the online questionnaire was circulated via the digital platforms, social media
networks, and online communities to the respondents who came across the AI -based promotional
advertisements. The respondents were made aware of the purpose of the study and the collection of
data was done anonymously. The questionnaire items of the present study were based on the previously
validated scales and adapted to the constructs of trust, privacy  concerns, psychological reactance,
consumer attitudes, and behavioral intention. The reliability and the validity and the psychometric
property of the items used in the study were measured using a five -point Likert scale (1 = Strongly
Disagree and 5 = Str ongly Agree) (Gefen et al., 2003; Malhotra et al., 2004; Hong & Kim, 2020). The
response data were processed for analysis.

3.6 Research Instrument (Questionnaire Design)
An adapted questionnaire and measurement scale were developed from other validated st udies
and incorporated into this study as the research instrument. The questionnaire included items related
to trust, privacy concerns, psychological reactance, consumer attitudes, and behavioral intentions
toward AI -based marketing, and was sectioned, acc ordingly. The adopted scales enhanced the
measurement instrument's validity and consistency. Each construct was measured with multiple items
using a five-point Likert type scale, where 1 = Strongly Disagree and 5 = Strongly Agree.

3.7 Reliability Test
Cronbach’s Alpha test measured the research instrument's reliability, and the internal
consistency of measurement items was assessed. Reliability analysis was conducted on all constructs
(i.e., trust, privacy concerns, psychological reactance, consumer attitu des, and behavioral intentions). A
Cronbach’s Alpha of 0.70 and above was considered reliable and consistent for measurement of the
variable (Hair et al., 2019).

3.8 Ethical Considerations
The research process was steered by ethical considerations. Inform ed voluntary participation
was achieved with an explanation of the research objective prior to survey completion. Participants'
information was de-identified and kept confidential. Data were used for research purposes only, and no
traceable information was collected. Participants maintained the right to withdraw from the study with
no consequence.

5. Discussion
The outcomes of this study reveal how trust, privacy concerns, psychological reactance, and
consumer attitudes impact AI -targeted marketing behavioral intentions. The results suggest that
consumer perceptions and psychological evaluations significantly impact the acceptance, engagement,
and resistance of AI -driven marketing. Even though AI -targeted marketing focuses on personalization,
ease, and efficiency of custom er engagement and advertising, consumer acceptance hinges on the
perception of the ethical data usage, opacity of AI systems, and consumer control over their data
(Dwivedi et al., 2021). The results provide additional support to the previous studies that s uggest the
adoption of AI in marketing has less to do with the technology itself and more with the psychological
and social dimensions of the consumer.

The results also show that trust and consumer attitudes toward AI -targeted marketing have a
strong positive correlation of 0.542 (p < 0.01). This means that consumers with higher trust in AI-based
marketing systems are more likely to have a positive attitude toward personalized marketing and AI -
based suggestions. Trust mitigates the discomfort that consumer s have with automated marketing,
especially when careful data usage is proven. This is in concurrence with Gefen et al. (2003), who
argued that trust is a critical component of technology acceptance as it lowers the perceived risk of
engagements on automated marketing. Also, Trust helps consumers navigate marketing systems when
they have no direct control of the processes (Pavlou, 2003).

The trust consumers have can have a positive impact on how they perceive things. Because of
this, Huang and Rust, 2021, believe that as long as certain strongholds like ethical AI practices and
marketing, and viable AI integration continue to remain unchallenged, consumer attitudes toward AI
adoption will remain positive. Trust concerning the protection of consumer data, an d the use of AI to
facilitate the provision of responsible, accurate, and AI -enabled marketing communications, will most
likely result in the acceptance of AI advertising. Previous research has shown the consumer perception
of explainability and transparen cy of responsible AI will increase consumer confidence and improve
attitudes to the application of AI (Shin, 2021).

It was also shown that concern for privacy has a strong and negative impact on consumer
attitudes to AI -enabled marketing (β = -0.431, t = -7.71, p < 0.001). This means that the more
consumers are concerned about the security of their data, the less they will support AI marketing
initiatives. The more the consumer feels that their data is being increasingly tracked and monitored, the
more dis comfort and insecurities will outweigh the conveniences and relevancies offered by AI

concerns dominated consumer reservations about digital technology. Simil arly, Bleier and Eisenbeiss

and use their personal data which in turn negatively affects their confidence and attitudes toward

The relationship between consumer attitudes and privacy concerns reveals the privacy paradox

data. When AI consumer behavior prediction reaches an intrusive level, advertisements that are served

Excessively personalized advertisements can have a negative effect on consumers. Organizations can
meet this challenge by developing a data protection framework that balances the benefits of
personalization with consumers' right to privacy by providing data protection and transparency (Martin
& Murphy, 2017).

Also, it was shown that psychological reactance had a significant, negative effect on consumers'
intentions to engage in AI marketing (t = -6.89, p < 0.001). This was the case for consumers whose
privacy, autonomy, and freedom to choose were viewed as being violated by the AI -oriented marketing
systems. This supports the psychological reactance theory which states that a violation of personal
control or freedom leads to the development of resistance. In the case of mar keting systems that utilize
AI, continuous tracking, and recommendation systems to serve highly personalized advertisements
create a perception of being manipulated by the AI marketing systems. This leads to avoidance of the
systems. (Clee & Wicklund, 1980).

Psychological reactance shows that consumer resistance to AI marketing goes beyond privacy
concern and includes emotional and behavioral dimensions. The absence of transparency and a lack
ability to exert control over AI algorithms that are assumed to dictate consumer choices may lead to
consumers actively rejecting the marketing that has been personalized to them. Studies have shown
that the perceived loss of autonomy plays a role in resistance to the adoption of technological
innovations and automated systems. Accordingly, to reduce resistance and to improve the acceptance of
AI-based marketing, choice, control, and most importantly, transparency should be viewed as priorities.

It was also demonstrated that consumer attitude has a strong impact on beh avioral intentions
toward AI-based marketing (F = 18.62, p < 0.001). Consumers that viewed AI marketing positively were
more likely to be involved with personalized ads, be supportive of the brand, and engage in buying
activities. This was in line with pre vious studies that supported the view that stronger predictive
elements of attitudes in the technology adoption field were more likely to be observed. In the same vein,
Ajzen (1991) stated that positive attitudes increase the likelihood that the individual  will perform the
behavior. Perceived usefulness of a technology and a positive perception of a technology will increase
the likelihood of acceptance and use of the technology.

Findings show that trust, privacy, psychological reactance, and attitudes shap e how consumers
respond to AI marketing. The result shows that consumers are more accepting of AI marketing when
they trust the AI system being used. Privacy concerns and psychological reactance work to hinder the
adoption of AI systems. The current resear ch shows the other side of the equation. For marketing to
consumers using AI to be effective, organizations that use AI in marketing must use AI with a
consumer-centric approach that addresses ethical and psychological concerns. Organizations must be

6. Conclusion
This study investigates the impact of trust, privacy concerns, and psychological reactance on
consumer attitudes and behavioral intentions regarding AI marketing. These results illustrate how trust
is a key factor in shaping positive attitudes among consumers, and emphasize the necessity of building
trust through credible, transparent, and responsible data handling pr actices in AI -driven marketing.
When users trust the organization and feel it is dedicated to their privacy, they will be more willing to
accept and interact with AI -generated personalized ads and recommendations. Building trust and
ensuring users' persona l information is safeguarded are essential for their willingness to accept and
engage with personalized ads and recommendations generated by AI. On the other hand, raised privacy
issues have adverse effects on consumer attitudes, such as uncertainty in the  area of data collection,
storage and use. In the same way, when customers feel their autonomy, privacy, or freedom of choice is
being restricted by the use of AI in marketing, their willingness to interact with the AI is diminished.
Likewise, when consume rs experience a threat to their autonomy, privacy, or freedom of choice when
using AI in marketing, their desire to engage with the AI is lowered.

The study also confirms that a positive attitude from consumers has a significant impact on their
behavioral intentions towards AI-driven marketing. Consumers with a positive view of AI marketing are
more likely to engage with ads that are customized for their needs, use AI -based services, and
appreciate personalized marketing efforts. These results highlight th ree points: the first is that
technological sophistication does not guarantee consumer acceptance; the second is that a small
number of variables can greatly impact consumer acceptance; and the third is that technological
innovation is not a deterministic process. However, trust in AI requires a complementary approach of
ethical data practices, transparency, consumer empowerment, and robust privacy protection
mechanisms. Incorporating consumer values into technological innovation can help build trust, reduc e
consumer resistance, and establish enduring consumer relationships. Overall, this study contributes to
the growing body of literature on AI -enabled marketing by providing empirical evidence from the
Pakistani context and offering practical insights for o rganizations seeking to implement effective,
ethical, and sustainable AI-targeted marketing strategies.

7. Future Implications
The results of this study serve as a basis for future research on consumer acceptance of AI -based
marketing as several directions for further analyses are suggested. Other technological, psychological
and ethical aspects including perceived usefulness, algorithmic transparency, perceived fairness, AI
literacy, perceived control, consumer innovativeness and ethical concerns  should be included in future
studies to gain a deeper understanding of consumer behavior in AI -driven marketing environment.
Researchers are also encouraged to use longitudinal and experimental research designs to explore how
consumer trust and privacy co ncerns develops in relation to growing sophistication and penetration of
AI technologies in practice. In addition, comparative research between other industries, various
demographic groups and cultural communities would enhance the generalizability of the results and
identify possible differences in consumers' perceptions. In conclusion, the effectiveness of responsible
AI governance frameworks, privacy laws, and transparent data management practices in bolstering
consumer trust, mitigating psychological re actance, and fostering ethical and sustainable use of AI -
driven marketing strategies is worth exploring in future research.
