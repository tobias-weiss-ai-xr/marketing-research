Abstract
This systematic theory-oriented literature review synthesizes r esearch on the relationships between social media
advertising (SMA) and consumer attitudes, engagement, and purchase intentions. The review analyzes 582 journal articles,
with particular attention to the theoretical frameworks and pat hways used to explain consumer responses within the
broader media culture of algorithmically driven platform ecosys tems. This research found that the Theory of Planned
Behavior, Uses and Gratifications Theory, and Technology Acceptance Model were frequently applied frameworks. The
synthesis demonstrates that conventional communication antecedents—such as informativeness, entertainment value, and
trust—are structurally mediated and co-constructed by predictiv e recommendation algorithms  and specific platform
affordances. Conversely, platform-induced irritation and percei ved data privacy violations prompt active consumer
resistance. Positive attitudes were consistently linked to enga gement and purchase intention, although the effects of
subjective norms and perceived behavioral control were more context dependent. Cross-cultural evidence further indicates
that consumer responses vary across cultural and institutional settings and are shaped by differences in platform
ecosystems and affordances. Future research can pay more attent ion to the impact of technical elements of SMA on
consumer response. These insights offer a theoretically integrated account of SMA effectiveness and actionable strategies
for designing effective SMA campaigns.
Keywords: SMA; consumer attitude, engagement, purchase intention, platform capitalism, decentralization, ecosystem
1. Introduction
1.1 Background
With the continuous development of digital technology, social media is increasingly becoming a digital platform for online
users to interact with the outside world (Bhosale et al., 2020) . The use of social media is also becoming increasingly
popular (Kemp, 2023). The huge number of users and traffic on s ocial media platforms naturally attract advertising
(Michel & Gandon, 2024). The marketing strategy, advertising ch aracteristics, user usage, and user response to
advertisements in SMA are all worth exploring and studying.
Prior studies have shown that the content characteristics and marketing strategies of social media advertisements affect how
users respond to advertisements and their engagement and interaction behaviors in social media advertisements. Factors that
positively influence attitude towards online advertisement amon g digital natives include “the ease of use, usefulness,
entertainment gratification, credibility, design, and personalization” of the advertisement (Lim et al., 2024). Advertising may
also evoke both positive and negative emotions, which subsequently influence user behavior (Monesa & Simanjuntak, 2024).
Moreover, platform and advertis ement design can affect engageme nt and reduce psychological distance, thereby
strengthening purchase intenti on (Sun et al., 2024). In additio n, Ducoffe’s (1995) Web Advertising Model similarly
emphasizes informativeness, entertainment, and irritation as central determinants of advertising value and effectiveness, a
pattern also supported in later SMA research (Hamouda, 2018). These relationships are further shaped by platform-specific
interfaces and affordances across Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, WhatsApp, and other social media environments.
Some scholars have presented different results on advertising value's contents or explanatory factors, like environmental
awareness and Influencer Credibility (Pranata & Salehudin, 2024; Hanaysha, 2022; Hasbullah & Harun, 2022). In addition,
users' social motivations were also relevant to attitudes towar d SMA (Shareef et al., 2018; Pang et al., 2024). However,
relatively few studies integrate advertising-value perceptions, motivations for social media use, and trust in the platform
within a single explanatory framework. In addition to value per ceptions and motivation, there are also considerations of

1.2 Research Aims and Objectives
1.2.1 Aim
This theory-oriented systematic review aims to synthesize and c ritically evaluate existing academic literature regarding
the influence of SMA on consumer attitudes, engagement, and purchase intentions.
1.2.2 Objectives
• To contextualize social media-consumer dynamics within wider culture.
• To understand the impact of algorithmically driven platform ecosystems
• To evaluate the cross-cultural variations in consumer-advertisement interactions
1.3 Research Questions
RQ1: What does existing empirical literature identify as the key influence factors shaping users’ attitudes toward SMA ?
RQ2: How are the relationships between attitude, engagement, percei ved behavioral control, subjective norms, and
purchase intentions characterized and synthesized across current studies?
RQ3: How does current research evaluate the moderating role of cultural differences on consumer responses to SMA?
2. Methodology
2.1 Review Design
This study adopts a systematic theory-oriented literature review approach to examine how previous empirical research has
conceptualized and explained the influence of SMA on consumer attitudes, engagement, and purchase intentions. The purpose
of the review is to provide a structured theoretical synthesis of the major explanatory frameworks used in the literature.
A theory-oriented review design was considered appropriate beca use the central contribution of the article lies in
identifying and comparing the theoretical lenses through which SMA has been studied.
2.2 Data Sources and Search Scope
The literature search was conducted using two major academic da tabases: Web of Science and Scopus. These databases
were selected because they index peer-reviewed academic journals in communication, advertising, marketing, consumer
behavior, business, media studies, psychology, and the social sciences. The review focused on academic journal articles
published between 2016 and 2025, in line with the manuscript’s objective of synthesizing contemporary research on SMA
and consumer responses.
The search strategy was based on the core concepts of the study. The initial keywords used to identify relevant literature
included: SMA value, attitude towards SMA, motivation of using SMA, trust in SMA, customer engagement in SMA,
purchase intention in SMA, perceived behavioral control, subjective norms, and cultural differences in SMA. These terms
reflect the conceptual structure of the article and correspond to the main sections of the theoretical synthesis.
Because the review is theory-orie nted, the search was designed to retrieve studies that addressed SMA in relation to
consumer attitudes, engagement, purchase intentions, trust, motivation, behavioral control, subjective norms, and culture.
The search, therefore, prioritized conceptual relevance to the research questions over bibliometric comprehensiveness.
The selection of studies followed predefined inclusion and excl usion criteria. Articles were included when they met the
following conditions:

2.3 Identification and Screening Process
The search initially identified 1,371 records across the two databases: 666 records from Web of Science and 705 records
from Scopus. After applying the language criterion, 1,360 records remained. The publication-period criterion, limited to
studies published from 2016 onward, reduced the number of records to 1,260. After applying the document-type criterion
to retain academic journal articles, 1,085 records remained for title and abstract screening.
During the title and abstract screening stage, records were ass essed for relevance to the research questions. First, titles
and abstracts were assessed against the eligibility criteria. S econd, the full texts of poten tially eligible articles were
reviewed. Studies were excluded when they were not directly rel ated to SMA, did not examine consumer attitudes,
engagement, purchase intention, trust, motivation, subjective norms, perceived behavioral control, or cultural differences,
or appeared as duplicated records across databases. At this stage, 503 records were excluded. The final corpus consisted
of 582 publications, which were r etained for full-text review a nd theoretical synthesis. The screening process is
summarized in Figure 1.
The screening process was therefore conducted in four stages:
• Identification of records in Web of Science and Scopus;
• Application of language and publication-year criteria;
• Application of document-type criteria;
• Title and abstract screening for thematic and conceptual relevance.
2.4 Analytical Focus of the Review
The analysis of the selected publications focused on the theore tical frameworks and conceptual relationships used to
explain consumer responses to SMA. The manuscript therefore examines the literature through the following conceptual
domains:
• Theoretical grounding of SMA research;
• Social media advertisement value perception;
• Social interaction motivation;
• Dependence and trust in social media;
• Attitude toward SMA and customer engagement;
• Attitude toward SMA and purchase intention;
• Subjective norms and perceived behavioral control;
• Customer engagement and purchase intention;
• Cultural moderation in SMA responses.
These domains form the basis of the findings section and reflec t the major theoretical areas identified in the reviewed
literature.

3.2.1 Social Media Advertisement Value Perception
Advertisement value played an important role in previous research related to users’ attitudes toward ads (Crespo et al., 2025). The
Ducoffe Web Advertising Model, a widely used theoretical framework in the field of advertising value-attitude-behavioral intention,
examines the key factors affecting the value of online advertisements: informativeness, entertainment, and excitement (Ducoffe,
1995). Advertising value has an impact on consumer attitudes toward online advertising, which in turn affects consumer behavior
and intention (Ducoffe, 1996). The information contained in social media advertisements can satisfy users' needs for information
acquisition, including information on promotional activities, interactive reward activities, product information, product experience,
etc., which often makes consumers perceive that the advertisements have a certain practical value and can satisfy informational
needs, thus generating positive attitudes towards digital advertisements (Mahmut et al., 2022). However, in today's network society,
some scholars have suggested that information overload will also make the audience feel that the information is too complicated
and contradictory, increasing the risk of trust in the advertisements (Kusi et al., 2022), which in turn Informativeness -> attitude
may become Conditional relationship. Entertainment means users can get entertainment satisfaction and perceive the emotional
entertainment value of digital advertisements (Ünalmış et al., 2024). On the contrary, in digital advertising environments, this hyper-
focus on amusement can distract audiences from critical content evaluation, demonstrating that entertainment value does not
inherently produce favorable or media-literate attitudinal outcomes (Pineda et al., 2022). Also, there have been studies conducted
to prove that the concept of irritation can explain the value of advertisements in the research, and it is considered to be a negative
predictor of advertisement value. Although some recent research indicated Non-significant/no relationship between irritation and
attitude in special groups and modern platform ecosystems (Alkindi et al., 2025).

3.2.3 Dependence and Trust in Social Media
The dependency theory by Ball-Rokeach & DeFleur (1976) and trus t transfer theory by Stewart & Zhang (2003) state
that users' dependency on media plays an important role in thei r perceptions of the trustworthiness of the platform's
content (Wirtz et al., 2017). Trust in a social media platform primarily pertains to its credibility as a reliable source of
information (Sangwan et al., 2022), while trust in social media advertisements is related to not only trust in the brand but
also a connection with the advertisement’s content and even the creator or source of the message (Gupta & Sharma, 2022).
Empirical literature consistently underscores how psychological  and structural media dependency drives shifts in user
attitudes and behavioral intentions. For instance, high dependency on specialized brand channels or fan pages significantly
fostered positive consumer attitudes toward platform-hosted content, and heightened consumer engagement and purchase
intentions for products promoted within these ecosystems (Bianchi et al., 2017). Social media dependency fosters positive
attitudes and intentions to purchase products promoted on these platforms. . Users who trust the platform are more inclined
to believe that advertisements are personalized and aligned wit h their interests, positively associating with higher
perceptions of ad relevance, which can, in turn, enhance purchase intentions (Sombultawee & Wattanatorn, 2022)
3.2.4 Technology-Related Factors and Other Factors
According to TAM (Technology Accep tance Model), the perceived u sefulness of technology and ease of use mostly
positively related to attitude t oward SMA (Lin & Kim, 2016). So me recent studies have examined how technological
changes—such as algorithms and big data—affect usability and usefulness, which in turn influence users’ attitudes toward
these technologies. Additionally, the technological convenience provided by platforms that integrate entertainment and
shopping ecosystems is also linked to users’ intentions. Other factors also affect the attitude toward SMA, such as critical
media literacy. Critical media literacy enables consumers to see through the manipulative power of algorithmic targeting,
spot product placements by influencers, and deliberately resist ad tracking through privacy settings. For some specific SMAs,
environmental awareness and personal social media usage habits, and Privacy concerns were also associated with attitudes.

3.3.2 Subjective Norms and Perceived Behavioral Control
According to the model proposed by the Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB), subjective norms and perceived behavioral
control influence consumers' purchase intentions where subjective norms refer to the perceived social pressure to engage
in a behavior, based on the expectations of significant others, such as family, friends, or peers.
A study by Juliana et al. (2024) has demonstrated that the infl uence of friends and family strongly shapes consumer
decisions. Consumers tend to rely on the opinions of trusted ot hers when making purchase decisions, particularly in
cultures with high social interc onnection. At the same time, so me studies have found no si gnificant or decreased
correlation between subjective norms and purchase intention (Akar, 2024; Zayed et al., 2022).
Perceived behavioral control (PBC) refers to the perception of the ease or difficulty of performing a behavior, influenced by
past experiences and external factors that may facilitate or hinder the behavior. Research by Kim et al. (2019) suggests that
perceived control over financial resources significantly influences purchase intentions. When consumers perceive that they can
afford a product or easily access it, their intention to purchase increases. Similarly, A study by Iqbal et al. (2024) found that the
resources and chances enhance perceived behavioral control, leading to stronger purchase intentions. Improved online shopping
interfaces and security features increase consumers’ perceived control, thereby enhancing their intentions to make online
purchases. Studies by Tseng et al. (2021) have shown that when consumers perceive fewer risks associated with purchasing a
product, their sense of control increases, which in turn positively affects their purchase intentions. However, some studies have
found no significant correlation between behavioral control and purchase intention (Lončarić et al., 2021; Sarfraz & Bano, 2023).
In the relationship between subjective norms, perceived behavio ral control, and purchase intention, some scholars have
added moderating factors to their research, such as religious c ulture (Hassan et al., 2022), trust (Kong et al., 2021) and
moral obligation (Kumar, 2024).
3.3.3 Customer engagement and Purchase intention
It has been shown that the active participation of customers in SMA is a presentation of social relationships and influences that
will be mediated through the generation of initial trust, which in turn will positively influence the intention to buy from them
(Supotthamjaree & Srinaruewan, 2021). In the context of social media, Interactive ads that encourage user engagement (e.g.,
liking, commenting, or sharing) foster a sense of involvement, which strengthens positive attitudes and increases purchase
intentions. Customer engagement moderates the relationship between customer perceptions and behavioral intentions (Dabbous
& Barakat, 2020). Abdul-Ghani et al. (2019) suggest that custom er engagement behaviors drive consumers' behavioral
intentions as reflected in persistent search behaviors and repurchase intentions (Waltenrath, 2024).Some studies indicate that
there are other factors moderating the relationship between par ticipation and purchase intention, such as mindfulness
(Konstantoulaki et al., 2022). However, some scholars argue that online social engagement does not necessarily promote
purchase intention, as users may choose to comment or share to express their disapproval of the message or values conveyed
by the product or brand, which may have a negative impact on purchase (Aghekyan-Simonian et al., 2012).

4. Discussion
The findings indicate that consumers form attitudes toward SMA through a combination of message value, social motivation,
trust, and technological experience. Informativeness was the mo st consistently positive an tecedent due to reducing
uncertainty and supporting purchase decisions (Tuten & Solomon, 2021). However, information overload can weaken this
benefit, highlighting the need to balance informational richness with clarity and advertising frequency (Fu et al., 2020).
Entertainment was generally positive, but one-fifth of the code d relationships were nonsignificant, suggesting that
amusement alone may be insufficient when advertising lacks relevance or cultural fit. Peers and social factors also have an
impact on consumer attitudes and engagement. They may comment to express their opinion in seeking to align with specific
cultural groups, which may enhance their attitude and be convincing to buy, inspiring social marketing strategies to improve
electronic word-of-mouth. Trust in social media platforms means that SMA can be considered as a reliable resource to make
purchase decisions (Sombultawee & Wattanatorn, 2022). The advertiser needs to investigate which social media platforms
their target audience trusts to advertise effectively. Irritation, by contrast, remains a clear barrier, and consumer-specific
factors such as environmental concern, ethical evaluation, and media literacy may further differentiate responses.
Attitude has been shown to positively influence both engagement and purchase intention, while engagement fosters brand
interaction and often serves as an indirect driver of purchase decisions. Nevertheless, engagement should not be equated
with conversion because users can interact with advertising for exploratory, social, or critical reasons. However, as shown
in the findings and analysis section, the relationship between subjective norms, perceived behavioral control, and purchase
intentions remains inconsistent, with 24% and 16% no-significan t relationships, largely due to variations in cultural
contexts (e.g., collectivism and individualism), individual differences, and the complexity of SMA communication, such
as social media platforms. As shown in the findings, survey(que stionnaire) methods were mostly used in previously
reviewed studies to verify or explore these relationships; howe ver, few studies combined with other methods to deeply
understand their behavior mechanism or explore why they showed different responses. Cross-cultural research highlights
the role of cultural factors in influencing consumer engagement and purchase decisions across diverse markets. Kim et al.
(2019) found that cultural differences affect individual engagement motives, posing challenges for globalized marketing
strategies. Local culture and cultural differences should be taken into account in SMA strategies.
5. Conclusion
5.1 Summary of the Study
This theory-oriented systematic review synthesizes research on the relationships between SMA and consumer attitudes,
engagement, and purchase intentions. Across the reviewed studies, informativeness, entertainment, social motivation, trust, and
technology-related perceptions generally contributed to favorable attitudes, whereas irritation was predominantly detrimental.
Attitude was the most consistent predictor of both engagement and purchase intention. Engagement supported brand interaction
and operated as an indirect stimulator of purchasing, but it did not necessarily translate into conversion. In this regard, it is
critical to understand the major differences between Western and Chinese digital platforms (such as Instagram, TikTok, and
Rednote, Douyin). The effects of subjective norms and perceived behavioral control were less uniform and depended on
consumer, product, platform, and cultural conditions. Cross-cultural research further demonstrated that consumer responses are
shaped not only by national cultural values but also by the institutional organization and affordances of platform ecosystems.
By integrating Uses and Gratifications Theory, the Technology Acceptance Model, the Theory of Planned Behavior, advertising-
value models, and cultural frameworks, this review provides a more coherent explanation of how users move from advertising
exposure to evaluation, interaction, and purchase intention. It also identifies important gaps in research on platform-specific
mechanisms, actual purchasing behavior, and the causes of nonsi gnificant or conditional relationships. Overall, SMA

5.2 Limitations of the Study
While providing a comprehensive synthesis, this study recognizes certain limitations inherent in the compiled literature. The
study is limited by its selected volume of literature, mostly based on empirical self-reporting surveys, which risks mixing social
desirability and personal biases. The theory-oriented coding also synthesizes reported relationships rather than estimating pooled
effect sizes. Conversely, with digital media technologies evolving rapidly, existing academic models struggle to capture the real-
time changes brought by emerging generative artificial intelligence (AI) tools and synthetic influencer marketing.
5.3 Future Scope
In future research, the use of longitudinal studies and digital methods to track behavior conversion rates from advertising
engagement through to purchase, rather than simply purchase int ention statements, will be possible. The influence of
generative AI, automated content optimization, and deep-fake mo dels on users' perceptions of brand and platform
authenticity within platform news feed environments can be stud ied. Finally, the role of differing degrees of awareness
regarding user privacy and ad blocking as an attempt to subvert it can be investigated, along with media literacy.
