ABSTRACT
The phaseout of third-party cookies is reshaping digital marketing
communication by disrupting how advertisers target audiences,
allocate budgets, and justify media and messaging choices. While
firms in developed markets are investing in first-party data and AI-
driven tools, advertisers in emerging markets face infrastructural
and organisational constraints that complicate these decisions. This
study examines how advertisers in Bangladesh evaluate cookieless
alternatives, focusing on organisational role, work experience, and
perceived cost-effectiveness. Drawing on the heuristic-systematic
model and cognitive load theory, Study 1 surveys 196 advertisers
and shows that specialists rely more on systematic evaluation of
information quality, whereas managers place greater weight on
heuristic feasibility cues such as compatibility. Study 2 uses expert
interviews analysed through the Technology-Organisation-
Environment framework to contextualise these patterns and high-
lights regulatory pressure, skills gaps, and organisational silos. The
findings extend dual-process theory in marketing communication
by showing that evaluative cue weighting is role-embedded and
shaped by resource and organisational constraints rather than uni-
formly applied. Practically, they underscore the importance of
cross-role coordination and feasibility-oriented cost framing when
planning cookieless strategies in budget-constrained settings.

1. Introduction
The deprecation of third-party cookies represents a major disruption to contemporary
marketing communications, challenging established practices of audience targeting,
message personalisation, media planning, and performance evaluation (Geradin,
Katsifis, and Karanikioti 2020). Decisions that were previously supported by highly
detailed insights into individual online behaviour must now be made under heightened
privacy scrutiny and increased client and managerial accountability.

This disruption is particularly acute in emerging markets. Limited infrastructure, frag-
mented marketing technology ecosystems, skills shortages, and cost sensitivity constrain
both implementation and the ability to credibly evaluate and justify alternative targeting
approaches (El Hana, Mercanti-Guérin, and Sabri 2023; Rasul, 2018). Under these condi-
tions, targeting decisions directly shape which audiences can be reached, how messages
are personalised, and which channels can be defended within budgetary constraints.
Research on advertising technology adoption has largely relied on technology accep-
tance perspectives, emphasising perceived usefulness and ease of use as predictors of
behavioural intention (Davis 1989; Dwivedi et al. 2019). While informative, this work offers
limited insight into how evaluative judgments are formed within advertising organisa-
tions, where specialists and managers face distinct responsibilities, constraints, and
cognitive demands.
To address this gap, the present study draws on the heuristic-systematic model (HSM)
and cognitive load theory (CLT) to examine how organisational role and work experience
shape advertisers’ evaluation of cookieless targeting strategies. The HSM explains how
decision makers combine systematic cues, such as information quality, with heuristic cues,
such as compatibility and perceived cost effectiveness (Chaiken 1980; Chen and Chaiken
1999) and CLT (Kalyuga 2011; Sweller 1988) explains why reliance on these cues varies
with task demands and accumulated expertise, as cognitive resources differ across roles
and experience levels.
Focusing on Bangladesh as an emerging market context, this study examines how
advertisers evaluate and justify commonly discussed alternatives to third-party cookies in
the context of privacy regulations and resource constraints. Rather than treating cookie-
less advertising as a purely technological transition, the study addresses it as a marketing
communications management challenge, centred on how targeting options are assessed,
justified, and translated into communication and media strategy decisions. Accordingly,
this study addresses three research questions:
RQ1. What factors shape advertisers’ intentions to adopt alternative ad-targeting strate-
gies in a cookieless environment within an emerging market?
RQ2. How do advertisers balance systematic evaluation and heuristic judgment when
assessing these alternatives under conditions of privacy regulation and resource constraint?
RQ3. How do organisational role and work experience influence the weighting of these
cues and, in turn, the communication and media strategies that advertisers pursue?
The following review synthesises research on cookieless advertising, adoption, and dual-
process evaluation under organisational constraints.
2. Literature review
2.1. Cookieless advertising and marketing communications
The phaseout of third-party cookies increases uncertainty in digital campaign planning
and accountability because cross-site tracking and attribution practices are constrained

(Geradin, Katsifis, and Karanikioti 2020). Privacy regulation intensifies this shift by requir-
ing consent-based approaches, while advertisers still face pressure to deliver relevance
and measurable outcomes (Geradin, Katsifis, and Karanikioti 2020; Martin and Murphy
2017). In practice, cookieless decisions involve trade-offs among data quality, system fit,
implementation effort, and cost, and these trade-offs must be defensible to clients and
senior stakeholders.
2.2. Adoption, persuasion, and organisational constraints
Technology acceptance research highlights perceived usefulness and ease of use as
drivers of intention (Davis 1989; Dwivedi et al. 2019). However, such models offer limited
insight into how evaluations are formed within organisations, where roles, resources, and
institutional constraints shape decision-making (Lee, Ramasamy, and Subbarao 2025).
Dual-process perspectives provide a mechanism-based account of evaluation. The heur-
istic-systematic model distinguishes between systematic cues, such as information qual-
ity, and heuristic cues related to fit and feasibility (Chaiken 1980; Chen and Chaiken 1999),
while cognitive load theory explains how reliance on these cues varies with expertise and
task demands (Kalyuga 2011).
Despite this potential, key gaps remain. Adoption research prioritises intention
over evaluative judgement, while persuasion research rarely accounts for organisa-
tional roles and resource constraints. Privacy-focused studies foreground regulation
but pay limited attention to how advertising teams interpret, justify, and act on
targeting alternatives. These limitations are especially salient in emerging markets,
where infrastructural constraints and organisational capacity are likely to reshape
how advertising strategies are evaluated and defended (Balarezo, Foss, and Nielsen
2024).
2.3. Integrating dual-process perspectives in cookieless decision-making
Addressing these gaps requires theorising how evaluative judgements are formed under
organisational and resource constraints. In such settings, perceived cost-effectiveness
functions as a heuristic of feasibility under uncertainty (Mitra 1995), yet remains under-
theorised within dual-process frameworks despite its relevance for adoption in emerging
markets (Odoom, Anning-Dorson, and Acheampong 2017; Rahman and Sloan 2017).
Building on these insights, this study integrates dual-process theory with cognitive
load theory to conceptualise cookieless targeting as a marketing communication deci-
sion. Advertisers evaluate alternatives while managing demands related to data quality,
compatibility, budget constraints, and coordination. Organisational role and work experi-
ence shape cognitive demands and cue weighting during evaluation, motivating the
following role-based and experience-based hypotheses. Appendix A presents Table A1,
which summarises prior approaches, and Table A2, which provides a detailed synthesis of
the reviewed literature.

3. Hypothesis development
3.1. An integrative perspective on decision-making in cookieless advertising
Drawing on the heuristic-systematic model (HSM), advertisers are expected to rely on
both systematic cues, such as information quality, and heuristic cues, such as integration
compatibility and cost-effectiveness, when evaluating alternative targeting strategies
(Cacioppo et al. 1986; Chaiken 1980). Cognitive load theory (CLT) explains why reliance
on these cues varies with task demands and accumulated expertise, shaping the balance
between effortful systematic processing and simplifying heuristics (Kalyuga 2011; Sweller
1988).
In marketing communication contexts, these processes underpin judgments about
whether targeting approaches are useful, feasible, and defensible. Accordingly, perceived
usefulness is treated as the central evaluative judgment linking cue weighting to adoption
intention (Davis 1989; Dwivedi et al. 2019). Organisational role and work experience are
therefore conceptualised as sources of variation in cognitive load that shape how eva-
luative cues are weighted.
3.2. Systematic cues, organisational role, and perceived usefulness
Information quality is a core systematic cue in digital advertising because it underpins
targeting accuracy, message relevance, and performance evaluation (Delone and McLean
2003). High-quality data reduces uncertainty and supports informed optimisation, making
it particularly salient for professionals directly responsible for analytics and execution.
Prior research suggests that specialists place greater emphasis on information quality
when evaluating communication technologies, reflecting the diagnostic demands of their
roles (Wixom and Todd 2005; Zhang et al. 2014). Managers, by contrast, are less involved
in day-to-day analytical optimisation and may rely less on detailed assessments of
information quality when forming usefulness judgments (Hodgkinson et al. 2023).
Accordingly, perceived information quality is expected to exert a stronger influence on
perceived usefulness among specialists than among managers.
H1a: Organisational role moderates the relationship between perceived information
quality (PIQ) and perceived usefulness (PU), such that the relationship is stronger for
specialists than for managers.
Because perceived usefulness represents a key pathway linking evaluative judgments to
strategic intention, this role-based difference is also expected to extend indirectly to
adoption intention (Dwivedi et al. 2019).
H1b: Organisational role moderates the indirect relationship between perceived informa-
tion quality (PIQ) and adoption intention (AI) via perceived usefulness (PU), such that the
indirect effect is stronger for specialists than for managers.

3.3. Heuristic cues, organisational role, and perceived usefulness
Integration compatibility functions as a heuristic cue by signalling how easily a targeting
strategy can be aligned with existing systems, workflows, and organisational routines.
Under conditions of time pressure, coordination demands, and accountability, compat-
ibility offers a cognitively efficient shortcut for judging feasibility (Tornatzky and Fleischer
1990).
Managers are accountable for resource allocation, coordination, and client justification,
making organisational fit particularly salient in their evaluations (Odoom, Anning-Dorson,
and Acheampong 2017). Specialists, whose roles focus on technical execution, are
expected to rely less on compatibility cues when forming usefulness judgments.
H2a: Organisational role moderates the relationship between perceived integration
compatibility (PIC) and perceived usefulness (PU), with the relationship stronger for
managers than for specialists.
Given the central role of perceived usefulness in shaping communication strategy adop-
tion, these role-based effects are also expected to operate indirectly (Davis 1989).
H2b: Organisational role moderates the indirect relationship between perceived integra-
tion compatibility (PIC) and adoption intention (AI) via perceived usefulness (PU), such
that the indirect effect is stronger for managers than for specialists.
These hypotheses reflect dominant tendencies rather than absolute role differences,
recognising that organisational decision-making remains context dependent.
3.4. Work experience as a boundary condition
Work experience shapes evaluative processing by influencing the availability of cognitive
schemas and domain-specific knowledge. As experience accumulates, professionals pro-
cess complex information more efficiently, reducing extraneous cognitive load and
reliance on simplifying heuristics (Kalyuga 2011; Venkatesh et al. 2003).
Accordingly, more experienced advertisers are expected to rely more on systematic
cues, such as information quality, and less on heuristic cues, such as compatibility. Less
experienced professionals, facing greater cognitive demands, are more likely to depend
on heuristics when evaluating targeting strategies (Cacioppo et al. 1986).
H3a: Work experience strengthens the relationship between perceived information qual-
ity (PIQ) and perceived usefulness (PU).
H3b: Work experience weakens the relationship between perceived integration compat-
ibility (PIC) and perceived usefulness (PU).
Because perceived usefulness connects evaluative cues to strategic intention, these
effects are also expected to extend indirectly to adoption intention (Venkatesh et al.
2003).

H3c: Work experience strengthens the indirect relationship between perceived informa-
tion quality (PIQ) and adoption intention (AI) via perceived usefulness (PU).
H3d: Work experience weakens the indirect relationship between perceived integration
compatibility (PIC) and adoption intention (AI) via perceived usefulness (PU).
3.5. Perceived cost-effectiveness as a heuristic amplifier
In resource-constrained markets, perceived cost-effectiveness functions not only as
a budgetary consideration but also as a heuristic cue that supports feasibility judgments
and internal justification under uncertainty (Mitra 1995; Rahman and Sloan 2017).
When organisational resources and infrastructure are limited, cost-related cues
become particularly salient, increasing reliance on heuristics rather than effortful systema-
tic evaluation (Odoom, Anning-Dorson, and Acheampong 2017).
Accordingly, cost-effectiveness is conceptualised as a contextual amplifier that condi-
tions how compatibility cues translate into perceived ease of use and, in turn, adoption
intention, by strengthening heuristic-based evaluations of implementability (Davis 1989).
H4a: Perceived cost-effectiveness (PCE) strengthens the relationship between perceived
integration compatibility (PIC) and perceived ease of use (PEU).
H4b: Perceived cost-effectiveness strengthens the indirect relationship between per-
ceived integration compatibility (PIC) and adoption intention (AI) via perceived ease of
use (PEU).
3.6. Adoption intentions toward alternative targeting strategies
Industry discourse commonly identifies first-party data, direct media buying, and AI-based
targeting as leading responses to the phaseout of third-party cookies (Choi and Lim 2020;
El Hana, Mercanti-Guérin, and Sabri 2023; Geradin, Katsifis, and Karanikioti 2020). These
approaches are framed as offering transparency, control, and regulatory compliance.
This study examines advertisers’ reported adoption intentions toward these alterna-
tives, while recognising that actual adoption may be shaped by organisational and
contextual constraints.
H5a: Intention to use first-party data is positively associated with cookieless advertising
adoption intention (AI).
H5b: Intention to use direct media buying is positively associated with cookieless adver-
tising adoption intention (AI).
H5c: Intention to use AI-based ad serving and analytics is positively associated with
cookieless advertising adoption intention (AI).

4. Methodology
4.1. Research design and data collection
This research adopts a sequential mixed-methods design comprising a quantitative sur-
vey (Study 1) followed by a qualitative interview phase (Study 2). The design prioritises an
initial quantitative survey to test the hypothesised relationships, with the qualitative
phase used to provide contextual and interpretive depth (Onwuegbuzie and Teddlie
2003). Study 1 employs a role-based comparative survey to examine how advertising
professionals in different organisational roles evaluate cookieless ad-targeting strategies.
Study 2 complements this analysis by explaining how these evaluations are shaped by
organisational constraints, professional responsibilities, and industry practices. Ethical
approval was obtained in accordance with the University Research Ethics Board’s proce-
dures. Participants received an information sheet and consent form, provided informed
consent, participated anonymously, and data were stored securely on university systems.
4.2. Study 1
4.2.1. Sample and data collection

ence in digital advertising (Campbell et al. 2020). A total of 430 professionals in
Bangladesh were contacted via LinkedIn, Facebook, and WhatsApp. After excluding
incomplete responses and those not employed in advertising-related roles, 196 valid
responses were retained.

4.2.2. Measures and analysis approach
All constructs were measured with adapted multi-item scales from prior research, with
wording adjusted to the cookieless context while preserving conceptual meaning. Items
used a 7-point Likert-type scale from 1 (strongly disagree) to 7 (strongly agree). Full items,
sources, and factor loadings are in Appendix C.
Analyses followed a two-stage approach. Measurement validity was assessed using
confirmatory factor analysis, and composite scores were then used to test the structural
model hypotheses. Models were estimated in AMOS 28.0 using bootstrapping with 2000
resamples to obtain bias-corrected confidence intervals for direct, indirect, and moder-
ated effects (Chernick 2008). Continuous predictors were mean-centred and standardised,
and interaction terms were computed from the standardised composites following Hayes
(2017). Moderation and moderated mediation probing used AMOS procedures with user-
defined estimands following Crowson’s guidelines (Crowson 2019a, 2019b, 2019c).
4.2.3. Measurement validity, reliability, and common method bias
Data were suitable for factor analysis (KMO = 0.907; Bartlett p < .001) (Leguina 2015). The
measurement model demonstrated good fit (CFI = 0.971; RMSEA = 0.049; SRMR = 0.057;
PClose = 0.543) (Hu and Bentler 1999). Composite reliability exceeded the recommended
threshold of 0.70 for all constructs. AVE ranged from 0.494 to 0.727. Although the cost
effectiveness AVE was marginally below 0.50, reliability and loadings were acceptable
(Fornell and Larcker 1981). Discriminant validity met Fornell-Larcker and HTMT criteria,
with HTMT below 0.85 (Henseler, Ringle, and Sarstedt 2015). Variance inflation factor
values were below 3, indicating no multicollinearity concerns (Chatterjee and Hadi 2015).
Descriptive statistics, correlations of latent constructs, CFA results and validity statistics
are reported in Tables B2 - B6 (see Appendix B).
Common method bias was assessed with Harman’s test (largest factor 44.97 per cent)
and an unmeasured latent method factor in the CFA. The method factor improved fit (Δχ2
(1) = 50.13, p < .001), but explained less variance than the substantive constructs, and
substantive loadings and structural paths were substantively unchanged, suggesting that
common method variance is unlikely to materially bias the findings (Podsakoff et al. 2003).

and heuristic cues (perceived integration compatibility, PIC, and perceived cost-
effectiveness, PCE) influence adoption intention (AI) via perceived usefulness (PU) and
perceived ease of use (PEU), and how these relationships vary by organisational role and
work experience.

4.3. Study 2
4.3.1. Research design and sampling
Study 2 employed semi-structured expert interviews to deepen understanding of how

4.3.2. Data analysis and results
Qualitative data were analysed using reflexive thematic analysis (Braun and Clarke 2013),
allowing theoretically informed interpretation while remaining grounded in participants’
accounts. To support integration with Study 1, themes were organised using the
Technology-Organisation-Environment (TOE) framework (Tornatzky and Fleischer 1990),
linking individual evaluations to organisational capabilities and environmental con-
straints. The analysis was conducted for explanatory purposes in line with a realist mixed-
methods logic (Maxwell 2012), providing contextual insight into the role-based patterns
and non-significant effects observed in Study 1. A detailed coding framework is provided
in Appendix F.

5. Discussion
This study examined how advertising professionals evaluate and justify cookieless target-
ing strategies under conditions of privacy regulation and resource constraints. By inte-
grating the heuristic-systematic model with cognitive load theory, the findings show that
evaluative judgments in cookieless advertising are shaped by organisational role and
work experience. Rather than a uniform processing approach, advertisers rely on different
cues, depending on their responsibilities and cognitive demands, when translating eva-
luation into adoption intention.
5.1. Role-based processing in marketing communication decisions
The findings indicate that cookieless targeting strategies are evaluated through dis-
tinct role-contingent pathways. Specialists place greater weight on perceived informa-
tion quality when forming usefulness judgments, reflecting their responsibility for
analytics, optimisation, and performance monitoring. High-quality information sup-
ports diagnostic evaluation and iterative refinement of targeting decisions (Delone
and McLean 2003).
14
 S. RAHMAN AND Y. ZHANG

Managers, by contrast, rely more heavily on feasibility-oriented heuristics when eva-
luations translate into behavioural intention. Integration compatibility serves as
a practical screening cue for whether a strategy can be implemented within existing
workflows, platforms, and coordination structures, and whether it can be credibly
defended under organisational and budgetary constraints (Odoom, Anning-Dorson, and
Acheampong 2017; Tornatzky and Fleischer 1990).
Not all hypothesised moderation effects were supported. Organisational role did not
significantly moderate the compatibility to perceived usefulness relationship, suggesting
that in resource-constrained settings, compatibility functions as a baseline feasibility
condition rather than a differentiating cue during usefulness formation (El Hana,
Mercanti-Guérin, and Sabri 2023; Rasul, 2018). Conditional indirect effects nonetheless
reveal asymmetric pathways: feasibility-based heuristics are sufficient for managers to
translate evaluation into intention, whereas specialists require stronger systematic justi-
fication grounded in information quality. Overall, the findings indicate selectively acti-
vated, role-contingent evaluative pathways rather than broad differences in processing
style. In line with guidance on interpreting moderated mediation in context-sensitive
models, a non-significant global index does not preclude the presence of meaningful
conditional processes (Hayes 2017; Lakens 2022).
5.2. Work experience as a boundary condition for evaluative processing
Work experience reshaped how evaluative cues were weighted rather than uniformly
increasing adoption propensity. As experience increased, reliance on compatibility cues
weakened, consistent with cognitive load theory, which predicts reduced dependence on
simplifying heuristics as domain-specific expertise accumulates (Kalyuga 2011; Sweller
1988).
Importantly, experience influenced adoption intention indirectly through changes in
evaluative structure rather than through strong direct effects. This helps explain why
moderated effects emerged even when main effects were modest (Venkatesh and Bala
2008).
This pattern also clarifies the non-significant adoption intentions for direct media
buying and AI-based targeting. Both survey and interview evidence suggest that these
strategies can be evaluated favourably in principle, yet remain difficult to justify or
implement due to cost uncertainty, skills gaps, and infrastructure constraints (Choi and
Lim 2020; El Hana, Mercanti-Guérin, and Sabri 2023). In emerging-market settings, feasi-
bility and communicability limits therefore constrain behavioural commitment despite
positive evaluations.
6. Theoretical contributions and practical implications
This study makes three theoretical contributions to marketing communications and
technology adoption research under privacy constraints.
First, the study extends dual-process theory by showing that systematic and heuristic
processing in organisational decision-making is role-embedded rather than uniformly
activated. Whereas prior applications of the heuristic-systematic model focus on indivi-
dual motivation or ability (Chang and Tung 2008; Chen and Chaiken 1999), the findings

demonstrate that evaluative cue weighting is structured by organisational roles, reflecting
differences in responsibility and accountability.
Second, the study reconceptualises work experience as a structural moderator of
evaluative processing rather than a direct driver of adoption. While adoption research
often treats experience as facilitating acceptance (Dwivedi et al. 2019; Venkatesh et al.
2003), integrating cognitive load theory shows that experience reshapes how evaluative
cues are processed and combined, explaining why experience-related effects may emerge
through conditional pathways even when main effects are weak (Kalyuga 2011; Sweller
1988).
Third, the study theorises perceived cost-effectiveness as a communicative heuristic
within dual-process frameworks. Existing research positions cost primarily as an economic
constraint in emerging-market adoption (Odoom, Anning-Dorson, and Acheampong
2017; Rahman and Sloan 2017). This study shows that cost-effectiveness also serves as
a justificatory signal, determining whether feasibility cues translate into commitment,
helping explain why strategies may be evaluated positively yet remain difficult to adopt.
More broadly, by integrating the heuristic-systematic model, cognitive load theory,
and the Technology-Organisation-Environment framework (Tornatzky and Fleischer
1990), the study reframes cookieless advertising as an organisational judgement process
shaped by cognitive capacity and institutional constraint, responding to calls for a theory
that reflects managerial decision-making under evolving digital and regulatory conditions
(Stremersch et al. 2023).
Practically, cookieless strategy planning should align with role-specific evaluative
demands. Specialists benefit from support for systematic assessment of data quality
and measurement credibility, whereas managers prioritise compatibility, implementation
risk, and cost-effectiveness when committing resources.
The findings also clarify why AI-based targeting often struggles to translate into
adoption in emerging markets. Even when perceived as useful, integration burden, skills
gaps, and cost justification can outweigh performance considerations. Framing cookieless
solutions in terms of feasibility and organisational fit is therefore critical for managerial
uptake.
Finally, in privacy-constrained contexts, regulation serves not only as a compliance
requirement but also as a legitimising resource. Targeting strategies that can be credibly
implemented and justified through narratives of consent, transparency, and trust are
more likely to be sustained.
7. Limitations and future research direction
This research has several limitations that suggest directions for future work. Study 1
employed purposive sampling and featured a gender-imbalanced sample, which may
limit generalisability. Although diagnostic tests indicate that common method bias is
unlikely to explain the observed relationships, reliance on self-reported, single-source
survey data cannot be ruled out entirely. Future research could address this limitation
using multi-source designs or by combining survey responses with behavioural or archival
data.
Study 2 relied on a small number of expert interviews. While thematic saturation was
achieved and the qualitative phase was designed for explanation rather than representation,

future studies could extend this approach across additional emerging markets and organisa-
tional contexts to assess the robustness of the role-based patterns identified here.
Methodologically, future studies could employ experimental or longitudinal designs to
isolate the effects of intrinsic, extraneous, and germane cognitive load on communication
strategy adoption, strengthening causal inference. Further work could also examine how
emotional responses, habitual practices, and cultural norms interact with cognitive pro-
cessing in shaping advertisers’ responses to privacy regulation and technological change.
Finally, as cookieless technologies continue to evolve, future research could explore
whether role-based processing differences narrow or widen as organisational capabilities
mature, and how emerging tools reshape the balance between systematic and heuristic
evaluation over time.
