Abstract: As blockchain technology continues to advance rapidly, it's presenting
fresh possibilities to solve some of the enduring issues in digital advertising, such as
transparency, privacy, security, and trust.  The Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use
of Technology (UTAUT) is a prevalent model for understanding technology
adoption; however, limited research has investigated how blockchain technology
attributes influence consumer intentions to view blockchain ads. This study aims to
explore the linkage between UTAUT constructs and consumer behavioral intentions,
considering blockchain technology attributes as a higher-order construct mediator.
The proposed conceptual framework combines the major constructs of UTAUT
"performance expectancy, effort expectancy, social influence, and " facilitating
conditions ") and a multidimensional higher-order construct of BCT attributes
(perceived transparency, perceived immutability, perceived traceability, and
perceived anonymity). The research method employed is quantitative research.
Data were collected using a structured questionnaire, and the analysis was
conducted using PLS-SEM (PLS SEM), Partial Least Squares Structural Equation
Modeling. The higher-order construct is examined using a two-stage approach to
check the reliability of measurement, convergent validity, and discriminant validity.
The study reveals that the UTAUT constructs influence consumers' intent to view
advertisements through their perceptions of blockchain technology attributes. This
research extends UTAUT by presenting blockchain attributes as a multidimensional
construct, enriching the literature on blockchain-driven digital advertising. The
findings suggest valuable insights for advertisers, digital marketing platforms, and
blockchain developers aiming to leverage blockchain features to enhance consumer
engagement and acceptance of advertisements in digital environments.

Literature Review
Digital technology has revolutionized the advertising industry, allowing businesses to reach consumers
through highly personalized, data-driven, and interactive advertising platforms. Digital advertising has
become an indispensable part of modern marketing approaches due to its many advantages for marketers,
such as greater targeting accuracy, real-time campaign tracking, and measurable advertising performance
(Gupta et al., 2025). As companies increasingly seek to leverage digital channels to improve interaction
with customers and build brand awareness, global investments in online advertising keep rising due to the
channel's momentum (Gunduzyeli, 2025; Kotler et al., 2021). The digital advertising space is booming, but
still struggles with common problems that hinder efficacy. Consumer concerns regarding click fraud, fake
impressions, lack of transparency, privacy concerns, and unauthorized use of consumer information have
led to a lack of consumer confidence in online ads (Gunduzyeli, 2025; Khan, 2024). These challenges have
also hindered advertisers' capacity to precisely assess the performance of their campaigns and adjust
advertising spending (Casino et al., 2019; Peres et al., 2023). Recently, blockchain technology has come up
as a potential solution to these challenges. Blockchain is a decentralized distributed ledger that allows the
transparent, secure, and unalterable record of advertising transactions without the need for centralized
intermediaries (Antsipava et al., 2024; Khan, 2024). Its distinctive features, like transparency,
immutability, and security, can contribute to lowering the prevalence of advertising fraud, boosting
responsibility, shielding customer data, and granting greater trust in digital advertising and marketing
frameworks (Casino et al., 2019; Wallbach et al., 2020).

Digital Advertising
Digital advertising is the distribution of marketing messages via digital channels like web pages, search
engines, social media, mobile apps, video sharing sites , and other Internet-based communication
technologies. Digital advertising gives marketers more flexibility in the content they provide, the ability to
track consumer experience in real-time, and the ability to measure the effectiveness of the campaign with
sophisticated analytics. (Kotler et al., 2021).
Artificial intelligence, big data analytics, and programmatic advertising have transformed digital
advertising and brought an efficiency that was not possible before.  But the same technological
advancements have raised alarms about advertising fraud.(Sağlam & Kirçova, 2025)Click manipulation,
fake traffic (Neves, 2025)Privacy issues with data, or transparency in the advertising supply chain (ASC)
(Khan, 2024; Rasool et al., 2020). These problems can lead to mistrust among consumers and a decrease
in their interest in online ads. One of the potential solutions is the use of blockchain technology. (Khan &
Amin Ul Haq, 2025)which can provide the advertising records to be transparent and tamper-proof, and
can be verified independently. By using decentralized verification mechanisms and the immutability of
transaction records, blockchain technology can enhance the accountability of advertising systems,
minimize fraudulent practices, and boost trust in digital advertising platforms. (Casino et al., 2019). As a
result, blockchain has become a key innovation in recent years for enhancing the trustworthiness and
efficiency of digital advertising.

Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT)
The UTAUT is a popular theory in information systems research. The UTAUT theory, presented by
Venkatesh et al. (2003), is one of the most influential models explaining the acceptance and use of new
technology from individuals’ perspectives. The model incorporates previous technology acceptance
theories: the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) (Davis, 1989a), the Theory of Reasoned Action (TRA)
(Fishbein & Ajzen, 1975), the Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) (Ajzen, 1991), and the Innovation Diffusion
Theory (IDT) (Rogers, 1962), into a whole structure.
According to UTAUT, there are four constructs that influence the formation of behavioral intention:
“performance expectancy”, “effort expectancy”, “social influence”, and “facilitating conditions”.
Performance expectancy is the extent to which an individual thinks that a technology will help them
perform a task. Consumers are more inclined to interact with ads when they feel blockchain technology is
enhancing the transparency of ads, making ads more credible, and making data more secure. Performance
expectancy has been cited in numerous studies as one of the strongest indicators of technologies' adoption
in various technological settings (Venkatesh et al., 2003). Effort expectancy is the ease of using a
technology that is perceived. Familiar and easy-to-use technologies are more readily accepted. People who
find blockchain applications easy to understand and use are expected to form more positive perceptions
towards blockchain in advertising. It is believed that easy-to-understand and easy-to-use blockchain
applications in advertising will lead people to form positive perceptions of blockchain. Social influence is
how much individuals think that significant people (family, friends, coworkers, social groups, etc.) want
them to use a certain technology. Social recommendations and peer influence have always been
demonstrated to influence consumers' willingness to embrace emerging technologies, especially in the
early stage of technological diffusion (Venkatesh et al., 2003). Facilitating conditions are the extent to

The Blockchain Technology Attributes as a Higher-order Construct
Blockchain technology has proven to be one of the most transformative technologies of the digital
economy, creating secure, transparent, and decentralized information systems .(Abbasi et al., 2021).
Blockchain was launched as the technology behind cryptocurrency but has since expanded to other sectors
such as finance, healthcare, supply chain, government services, and digital marketing. (Casino et al., 2019).
Instead of using a centralized database, blockchain uses a distributed ledger that keeps all transactions
cryptographically secured and permanently recorded.  These qualities increase the difficulty in
manipulating data and enhance transparency and accountability of the stakeholders involved in the
process. (Yaga et al., 2018). In digital ads, blockchain offers solutions to various persistent issues that
plague the industry, such as click fraud, fake impressions, unauthorized data sharing, and lack of
transparency across the ad supply chain.(Khan, 2024). By removing the need for trusted intermediaries,
the blockchain decentralized system helps advertisers and publishers verify advertising transactions,
which enhances confidence in advertising performance measurements and the outcomes of advertising
campaigns. (Khan & Amin Ul Haq, 2025; Xu et al., 2019). Hence, blockchain is gaining acceptance as a
technological solution that can boost trust in digital advertising ecosystems. In recent information systems
research, the authors suggest using the higher-order construct (HOC) of blockchain technology attributes
instead of considering each technical characteristic, given that users tend to assess blockchain technology
as a whole, rather than as specific technical components. A higher-order construct represents the overall
effect of several related dimensions and is also able to minimize the number of dimensions in your model
and improve the parsimony of your theory . (Hair et al., 2022). Blockchain technology attributes
(characteristics) are conceptualized as a formative higher-order construct of perceived transparency,
perceived security, perceived immutability, and perceived anonymity in the present study.

Perceived Transparency
Perceived transparency is the degree of discoverability, visibility, and traceability of information that was
recorded in a blockchain and can be seen by authorized participants. Transparency allows all parties to
track advertising transactions, validate campaign results, and assess advertising spending without relying
on a central reporting system. (Gupta et al., 2025; Saberi et al., 2019). When consumers feel that ads on
these blockchain-powered platforms are accurate, transparent, and verifiable, they are more inclined to
rely on them.

Perceived Security
Perceived Security is blockchain’s protection of data from unauthorized access, cyber-attacks, and data
manipulation, using sophisticated cryptographic methods and distributed consensus. However, consumers
are becoming more aware of privacy breaches and the improper use of their personal information in digital
platforms, making data security one of the critical factors in technology adoption(Ahmad & Arif, 2022).
The decentralized nature of blockchain technology also minimizes the risks inherent in relying on a
centralized data repository, further boosting user trust that their personal information and advertising

Perceived Immutability
Perceived Immutability means that data cannot be changed, deleted, or manipulated after it is recorded
and verified in the blockchain network. Each transaction is cryptographically signed by the previous
transactions, forming an irreversible chain of transactions that cannot be tampered with by unauthorized
parties.(Yaga et al., 2018). Immutability is a significant benefit in digital advertising to ensure advertising
records are more reliable throughout the advertising process, with campaign data, consumer interactions,
and advertising metrics being authentic. Blockchain stores information in a permanent way, making it
harder for fraudulent reports of ads and increasing the trust stakeholders have in advertising performance.

Perceived Anonymous
Perceived anonymity is the feeling that the person's identity and information are hidden and that it can't
be associated with their transactions or activities online. Perceived anonymity boosts user trust and lowers
privacy concerns, with users able to engage with ad platforms without putting private details at risk, which
makes the blockchain advertising model even more trusted. Perceived anonymity helps users feel more
comfortable and confident, as they can interact with the advertising platform without disclosing
identifiable personal information, making the blockchain advertising model even more trusted. (Pavlou &
Gefen, 2004; Xu et al., 2019).
In total, transparency, security, anonymity, and immutability are the essence of blockchain,
distinguishing it from conventional digital information systems. These attributes do not act independently
of one another as they are a reflection of the overall quality and trust in blockchain technology. Thus,
modeling blockchain technology attributes as a higher-order construct affords a more complete account
of consumers' perceptions of blockchain-enabled digital advertising.

Behavioral Intention
These are the individual's intention or readiness to engage in a specific behavior, and have been
consistently found as one of the most potent predictors of actual use of technology. (Venkatesh et al., 2003).
Behavioral intention, in the digital advertising realm, is actually consumers' desire to view, interact with,
and react favorably to blockchain ads. Technology acceptance theories indicate that people are less inclined
to form positive behavioral intentions when they think the technological innovations are of no value,
untrustworthy or insecure, and difficult to use. Previous studies have shown that consumers' intentions
regarding emerging technology are greatly influenced by their perceived system quality, information
quality, transparency, and trustworthiness.(Saxena & Thakur, 2024). However, blockchain technology will
help reinforce these views as it reinforces the integrity and credibility of digital advertising transactions.
Consumers' positive behavioral intentions towards blockchain advertisements are greater when they think
that blockchain improves transparency, provides protection against personal information, and blocks
advertising fraud.(Gupta et al., 2025a; Khan, 2024). Therefore, the behavioral intention is a suitable
dependent variable for the assessment of the consumer acceptance of blockchain-based digital advertising,
as it represents consumers' willingness to engage with blockchain-based advertising.

use of blockchain technology in digital advertising, in combination with technology acceptance theories,
is still relatively limited (Khan, 2024; Marikyan et al., 2022). Previous blockchain research has concentrated
on the technical aspects of blockchain, cryptocurrency usage, supply chain, healthcare systems, and
financial services, with fewer studies investigating the impact of blockchain on consumer behavior in
digital advertising contexts(Casino et al., 2019; Xu et al., 2019). Also, prior research on UTAUT is mainly
focused on traditional information systems, mobile commerce, electronic government, and digital
payment technologies (Yu, 2012; Zhang et al., 2023). UTAUT does not explicitly include technology-specific
aspects that distinguish between blockchain and traditional digital systems. In addition, most of the

Conceptual Framework and Hypothesis Development
The UTAUT) consists of four main factors: performance expectancy (PE), effort expectancy(EE), social
influence (SI), and facilitating conditions (FC), which influence the acceptance of a technology.(Venkatesh
et al., 2003). These constructs together explain users' cognitive evaluation of technology and their
resultant intention to adopt or use it. But in blockchain-based digital advertising, consumers develop
behavioral intentions not just on general technology perceptions but also on unique technological features
that blockchain provides, such as transparency, security, and immutability.
The more consumers see blockchain technology as useful, easy to use, social, and technologically
accessible, the more likely they are to become aware of the advantages of blockchain.  Such positive
assessments will reinforce the consumers' trust in blockchain transparency, security, and immutability.
Past research has shown that technology acceptance beliefs have great effects on technology capabilities
and system quality perceptions. (Davis, 1989b; Venkatesh et al., 2003).
H1: UTAUT constructs have a positive influence on Blockchain Technology (BCT) Attributes.

There are some distinctive properties of blockchain that distinguish it from traditional websites.
Immutability means that advertising records can't be changed once they are verified. This technology

Conceptual Framework
The conceptual framework proposed in this paper builds upon the UTAUT with the addition of Blockchain
Technology Attributes as a mediating variable. According to UTAUT, the acceptance of technology by
consumers can be described by four first-order constructs: performance expectancy (PE), effort
expectancy (EE), social influence (SI), and facilitating conditions (FC), which are seen as a user's attitude
towards the blockchain-based advertising platforms in general. Blockchain Technology Attributes are a
second-order higher construct, which includes attributes of transparency, security, anonymity, and
immutability. These dimensions represent consumers' total perception of the capability of blockchain, and
are assumed to act as a mediator between UTAUT constructs and BI.

Methodology
The study was a cross-sectional, survey questionnaire-based quantitative research design to explore the
influence of the constructs of (UTAUT) on consumers' behavioral intention to watch blockchain-enabled
digital advertisements as a mediation model of Blockchain Technology (BCT) Attributes. A quantitative
approach was chosen since the study aimed to test the hypothesized relationships between latent
constructs through the use of (SEM) structural equation modeling. The cross-sectional design allowed for
obtaining consumer perceptions at one time and is frequently used in studies of technology acceptance
and digital marketing.(Hair et al., 2022).

Research Context
The study was carried out in a country like Pakistan, where Internet penetration, smartphone adoption, e-
commerce, and Social Network Sites use are growing at a very fast rate, making it an emerging digital
economy. Over the past few years, the rise of digital platforms has significantly boosted the amount of
money organizations put into online advertising. But advertising fraud, privacy, and transparency issues
persist and are undermining consumer trust in digital advertising. Given the aforementioned challenges,
Pakistan is a relevant setting to explore if blockchain technology attributes could be applied to improve
consumers' willingness to interact with digital advertisements.

Sampling and Data Collection
The primary data were collected using a structured questionnaire method from the population of internet
users in major cities of Pakistan. A purposive sampling technique was used due to the study's specific
interest in individuals who were knowledgeable about digital advertising and Internet-based services. This
is the identification and selection of people or groups who are knowledgeable and skilled about a certain
topic of interest. (Creswell & Plano Clark, 2011). Alongside knowledge and experience, (Bernard, 2006), and
(Spradley, 1979) emphasize the significance of availability and willingness to reply. In contrast to random
studies that intentionally include a varied demographic of ages, ethnicities, and cultures, purposive
sampling aims to focus on individuals with certain qualities that are more likely to contribute effectively
to the relevant study.
The surveys were administered online as a Google Form. Respondents were told that their responses
would be anonymous and confidential, and participation was voluntary. The questionnaire was pre-tested
with a small number of respondents before the main survey to evaluate the clarity and wording of the
questions (items) and the content validity of the questionnaire. The final survey was rolled out after
feedback was received from the pilot study.

Measurement of Constructs
Content validity and comparability with previous studies were achieved by adapting all measurement items
from previously validated scales. The UTAUT four constructs were adapted from (Venkatesh et al., 2003),
namely performance expectancy, effort expectancy, social influence, and facilitating conditions.
Blockchain Technology Attributes were designed as a formative higher-order construct that includes four
measurement items: Transparency, Security, Anonymity, and Immutability; items were adapted from the
work of (Siagian et al., 2022; Wallbach et al., 2020) and the BI scale was adapted from (Kaczmarek et al.,
2015). The validated technology adoption scales were used as instruments for measuring Behavioral
Intention, which was considered the respondents' intention to view digital advertisements with the help
of blockchain technology. All items were rated on a 5-point (Strongly Disagree to Strongly Agree) Likert
scale.

modeling. Data analysis was carried out in two steps. The measurement model is evaluated by confirmatory

Results
This study focused on the technology acceptance theory (UTAUT) with the combination of blockchain
technology (BCT) attributes to explore the factors affecting the behavioral intention to use blockchain-

the results from the structural model. In addition, BCT attributes were determined to have a significant
effect on behavioral intention. The results indicate that consumers' trust in blockchain technology and
their positive intention to interact with blockchain-based digital ads is influenced by their perception of
the technology's transparency, security, immutability, and privacy protection. Based on the mediation
analysis, it was found that the relationship between the UTAUT higher-order construct and behavioral
intention is partially mediated by blockchain technology.  The result suggests that the technology
acceptance factors not only directly affect the consumer's behavioral intention but also indirectly through
the perception of the characteristics of the blockchain. Partial mediation indicates that UTAUT is an
independent predictor of behavioral intention, but the blockchain attributes make another contribution
that reinforces consumers' adoption decision.

Discussion
The results reinforce the empirical evidence that can be used to support the integration of UTAUT with
blockchain technology attributes to explain blockchain-enabled digital advertising adoption by consumers.
These results are in line with UTAUT theory, which claims that Cognitive evaluations of users have the
most significant influence on users' intentions to adopt a technology (Venkatesh et al., 2003).The effect of
BCT attributes on behavioral intention emphasizes the important relevance of these core mechanisms
“transparency, security, immutability, and anonymity as they increase consumer trust in digital
advertising. The results align with existing research that blockchain enhances the institutional trust by
minimizing uncertainty and improving information integrity (Casino et al., 2019; Wang et al., 2021). Lastly,
the results of the mediation analysis show that blockchain technology mediates the relationship between
UTAUT higher-order construct and behavioral intention partially. This study deepens the understanding
of technology adoption theory by showing that UTAUT factors are not the only cause to influence consumer
adoption of technology and that blockchain-specific technology characteristics can also enhance their
acceptance. So, blockchain attributes are a significant explanatory factor that connects the technology
acceptance beliefs with the actual adoption intentions. The partial mediation further suggests that UTAUT
continues to have a direct effect on behavioral intention, with the addition of blockchain attributes to
increase trust and consumers' perception of the advertising ecosystem.

Managerial Implications
The results indicate that managers need to incorporate blockchain technology into digital advertising by
prioritizing transparency, security, immutability, and privacy to strengthen customer trust and
engagement. Blockchain technology attributes partially mediate the relations between UTAUT2 constructs
and behavioral intention; organizations must reconcile blockchain attributes with personalized platforms.
Introducing blockchain-enabled attributes, such as safe data management and verified advertising, helps
to minimize consumer distrust and enhance advertising efficacy. These techniques may enhance consumer
connections, improve advertising efficacy, and provide a lasting strategic advantage within the digital
advertising landscape.

Theoretical Contribution
This study expands the UTAUT by incorporating blockchain technology attributes as a mediating construct
to understand consumers' behavioral intention toward blockchain-based digital advertising. The results
indicate that the relationship between UTAUT2 constructs and behavioral intention is partially mediated
by transparency, security, immutability, and anonymity. This highlights the complementary role of
blockchain-specific attributes in technology adoption. This study offers a more comprehensive framework
for understanding consumer behavior in decentralized digital advertising by incorporating UTAUT with
blockchain literature.

Conclusion
By adding blockchain attributes into UTAUT, the model further clarifies both the psychological and
behavioral determinants of technology adoption among consumers, as well as how blockchain’s unique
technological attributes like transparency, immutability, anonymity, and security influence trust and
affect behavioral intention.

Limitations and Recommendations for Further Research
This paper, like any study, has certain limitations that must be acknowledged and discussed. First, this
study was limited to a survey questionnaire in a developing nation. Several qualitative studies must be
undertaken to fully understand the phenomenon in depth. Secondly, the questionnaire was completed by
a readily accessible population of students; perspectives of the general population should also be
incorporated. Conversely, the generalization of the findings should be treated with care. The outcomes of
this study can serve as a foundation for researchers to conduct more exploratory research on the role of
blockchain technology attributes as a mediator between the UTAUT model and behavioral intention to
watch digital advertisements. A more comprehensive approach can be used by incorporating trust and
attitude for the extension of the UTAUT model to predict the adoption of new technology in digital
advertising.
