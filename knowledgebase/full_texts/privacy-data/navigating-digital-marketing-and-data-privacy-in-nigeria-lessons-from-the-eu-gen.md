Abstract
Nigeria has witnessed exponential growth in digital marketing driven by increased
internet penetration and smartphone adoption. Businesses increasingly depend on
digital marketing and targeted advertising that relies on consumer data collection and
processing. While this data -driven approach creates unique opportunities for
businesses to target and engage consumers, it raises significant concerns about
consumer data privacy rights and protection. This need to protect  these rights has
prompted countries worl dwide, including Nigeria, to establish legal frameworks for
consumer data privacy protection. This study examined the complex relationship
between digital marketing practices and data privacy regulations within Nigeria's
legal framework, using the European  Union's General Data Protection Regulation
(GDPR) as a benchmark for comprehensive data protection legislation. The research
employed a mixed-methods approach, distributing online survey questionnaires to 787
randomly selected Nigerian respondents. The st udy used descriptive and analytical
statistics to analyze responses. The study revealed that while Nigeria has made
significant progress in developing data protection standards, improvements are
needed in areas such as specific authorization requirements f or marketing activities,
enforcement of data subject rights, and sanctions frameworks for non -compliance.
Additionally, while Nigerians are aware of digital marketing operations , including
targeted advertising, they largely lack awareness of existing data protection laws and
privacy policies. The study recommended enhancing public awareness of data
protection rights, implementing robust cybersecurity measures against data breaches,
and strengthening existing regulatory frameworks for data protection.

 Methods
This study used a hybrid research method that integrated both doctrinal and
non-doctrinal legal research. The doctrinal method involved content analysis of both
primary and secondary sources of law addressing digital marketing and data privacy
in Nigeria and generally. Primary sources included existing legislation, regulations,
and judicial pre cedents related to  the ethical and legal issues surrounding digital
marketing and data privacy in Nigeria. Similarly, secondary sources included
scholarly works on digital marketing and data privacy, which were thoroughly
examined to understand the current  state of knowledge on digital marketing and
data privacy generally and the dimensions of the global discussion on the subject.
The non-doctrinal method, on the other hand, involved the collection of data
through an electronic questionnaire, i.e., a Googl e Form, to interrogate the public
perception of digital marketing in Nigeria and the extent of awareness of the issue
of data privacy and the existing regulatory framework for the protection of data
privacy rights in Nigeria. This approach helped to obtain firsthand information on
the public response to digital marketing and the collection and processing of their
personal data. The data obtained were then subjected to descriptive and analytical
methods to draw logical conclusions about the operation of digi tal marketing and
the ethical issues surrounding the processing of personal data.

1) Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria, 1999
Section 4 of the 1999 Constitution confers on the National Assembly the power
to enact laws for the good governanc e of the country . Therefore, legislative action
by the National Assembly becomes pivotal in formulating comprehensive legal
frameworks that regulate the practice of telemedicine, ensuring ethical standards,
patient safety, healthcare professional accountab ility, and technological standards.
The Constitution, particularly Chapter IV, establishes fundamental rights, including
citizens' right to privacy .17 For clarity, Section 37 guarantees citizens' privacy,
including their correspondence and communications. In the context of data privacy,
this provision underscores the importance of maintaining the confidentiality and
data privacy of every person in Nigeria , especially information transmitted
electronically, necessitating robust data protection measures and s ecure
communication platforms in digital marketing practices.
Nigerian courts have consistently interpreted Section 37 of the Constitution of
the Federal Republic of Nigeria as encompassing data privacy violations. The Court
of Appeal in Emerging Market Telecommunication Services v Barr. Godfrey Nya Eneye
upheld the decision of the Federal High Court.18 In that case, the claimant instituted
an action against the telecommunications company for exposing his data to a third-
party company that constantly sends  unsolicited text messages to his phone
number. The court held that the defendant violated the claimant’s data privacy right
under section 37 of the Constitution.

Conclusions
This study has contributed significantly to the discourse on data protection in
digital marketing in Nigeria. These contributions are in three basic senses. First, this
study makes a normative contribution to scholarship in areas of law. By analyzing
the existing legal framework on data privacy in Nigeria and comparing it with the
GDPR, it found that normative gaps exist in Nigerian law in areas such as child data
protection, among others. Second, the study contributes empirically to the discourse
on data privacy right in the context of digital marketing. This study is the first
primary survey on the level of public awareness, data subjects' attitudes, and the
enforcement of data privacy rights in Nigeria as it relates to digital marketing. These
empirical findings justify the need for reform within the digital marketing sector of
the Nigerian economy. The third contribution of the study is policy contribution ,
which concerns the proposition of reform recommendations specifically grounded
in legally informed findings and empirical data.
